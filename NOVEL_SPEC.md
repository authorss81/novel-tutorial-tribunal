# Novel Specification

Title: The Tutorial Tribunal
Repository slug: novel-tutorial-tribunal
Genre: system mystery / legal fantasy
Male lead: A former game tester who becomes the first defendant before the System
Length target: 850 chapters
Relationship policy: One slow-burn relationship or no romance, decided in the bible phase.
Power rule: Growth must be earned through practice, cost, failure, relationships, and changed decisions. The lead must not become instantly overpowered unless the approved genre premise requires it.

## Premise
A System judges whether a fantasy world is fit to continue, and every challenge becomes evidence in a trial about who created it.

## Ending
The verdict decides whether the world is a game, a prison, or a place where people can refuse the rules.
## Status

Volume 01 is complete: Chapters 1–50 written, and the volume's resolution delivered in Chapters 41–50 — the ninth-bridge charter amended with an appeal, a visible exit, a named maintenance burden, recognized access, housing, and work for the Ro household, Ilyan's provisional standing restored, and Sera formally licensed. The Quiet Chamber's page is still missing and yielded one phrase.

Volume 02 is in progress. **Batch 0004, Chapters 81–90, is complete, and it is the half of the volume's resolution that is not about Sera: the appeal has to stop depending on one poor district's willingness to keep paying half a farthing on a pump. It advanced by three ordinary things and secured none of them — a definition a bench of four wrote into its own book about what its own rate is a rate for, a man's name under a rate that cannot be withdrawn, and a rule that any person of a district may ask for a page to be read aloud to them at a pump twice a week and not be asked why. All three cost somebody an hour, a day, or a bank of mud in the cold, and the batch's finding is that the cost of a durable thing in a poor district is always paid by a person and never by a body.**

**The bill was the engine, and it was never a notice.** A clerk of the ninth bridge's district sent four miles down a fen road a letter saying that the charter of the district of Halloway costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year out of a road fund of four hundred and ten that belongs to a bridge, and that the year as set out at the public review leaves forty-four pounds and four shillings, so the charter costs twelve shillings more than what is left, and that the hundred and five pounds which came back when the under-deck watch went to one hand is in a different line, and a clerk cannot move a line into another line without a bench's minute. **A poor district's law is paid for by another district's road fund four miles away, and the payer is not the district asked and cannot be asked.** A hauler of twenty-nine asked in public whether the rate for a haul could be appealed by any person and was told no, because the only person who asked for it is the person who asked, and a holder may not be a party or appeal or be named in one — **an appeal is a thing a person can carry, a rate is a thing a person has to be inside of, and the machinery has a procedure for the first and not one word for the second** — and she got the sentence instead of the money and then took on a boy of nineteen as a second pair of eyes at her own cost. A salt seller of Weir Lane withdrew his consent to a rate in his own hand and could not undo it and cannot un-say it, so a rate in that district now carries the name of a man who does not agree with it, and a rule the bench made for itself requires it to be read aloud at a pump four times a year by the woman who cannot appeal from it. And the one thing that changed on the ground in three weeks was a washer of sixty-one who walked four miles to another district's pump, because eight buckets a day is a penny and the rate falls on her at eight times what it falls on most of the rest of them, and she told one person and not the district.

**T. Blore has been told, in Oren Sedge's own mouth, at the cut banks, at the second bell, with no messenger and no page in it.** He is the only person in this case's record named as having lost something in it, and he was told that too, and the loss is four hours a day and one of the two seasons an apprenticeship in that trade wants. **The resistance was never the loss. It was that the loss is recoverable-looking — a fund with forty-four pounds and four shillings in it would pay him back about five months, a district can be written to, a chamber can be asked, and a man can be sent for — and none of those is a wage, and there is no job either.** And he was told something true nobody had told him, for his own reason, so that a false story does not get written about a trade in about four years: *you were not let go.*

**The carrier's clause was used a second time, by a bench of three eleven miles off a fen road that cannot pay, and it cost that district a day and four shillings and sixpence and cost the carrier a walk he was not obliged to take.** A recordwright refused to send him and gave the reason: a recordwright's carrier is a thing she is and not a thing she hands to the man the fourth term names. What came back was a finding nobody had stated — a clerk of twenty-nine had made herself a holder of a room nobody made her a holder of, and her bench's ordinary work had stopped — and at the foot of the ninth sheet, under the fifth term, in the copy and not in the sheet, a bench of three that has adopted nothing read out a line nobody entered, in public, because it was reading the sheet out word for word. **The continuity line appeared for the ninth time and was refused for the sixth, in the same words, nobody named, and the holder has still not read it.**

**Maren Kett came back after two months and asked the question nobody had thought of asking: what does it cost, in money, to make a matter ready, and who pays it.** A place on a docket board costs about four pounds fifteen in four months and the whole of twenty-two names on that board has cost this city less than the ninety-six pounds of timber bought for a bridge four miles away — **and a document is made by a person who can read, and this city has no line on which a person is paid for reading.** A magistrate said the other half and nobody liked it: a body that pays a person to prepare the paper has bought the paper; this city can afford eleven places and always could, and what it cannot afford is the work that makes a matter ready, because that work is done by people who are not paid, not named, cannot be produced, and whose names are in no column anywhere. The cost of the sitting was entered against the ninth bridge's district road fund, which is not the district asked.

**Ilyan's record cost him a seventh thing, and the only thing his body ever gave back.** Produced as a witness a second time about a number he put in a bench's book without the consent of the woman it was owed to, he entered that a witness can be produced twice and three times and that there is no line anywhere in this city that pays a man for having been produced — and, against himself, that he has stopped counting the gate hand's tide, so the number stands, is wrong, and is read out at a pump four times a year, and the only man in this city who could have made it more right by counting more carefully has stopped. He gave the reason out loud and it was in the body: counting was the only thing that ever cleared her weight out of his leg, and he had worked out that he has been holding a woman up for three hours a day so that a bench of four can read a number. **The leg is his own now and does not clear, and the token was not touched and the rendering is unchanged and unresolved.** A gate hand who still does not know what is in it told him he has made himself a man who cannot be produced on a number, and then told him the other thing, which he did not ask for: she has been counting her own tide in her own feet since she was fourteen, and there is no slate, and she would have gone on with it.

**The batch ends on a question nobody had ever asked out loud in this city.** At the sixth bell on a mud bank, with about two hundred people behind her, a hauler of twenty-nine said that nobody has ever told her what a page is for — not what it may be used for, which she has worked out twice a week, but what it is for — and sent it to a recordwright at her own cost, with the question at the top of the letter, her own name under it and the recordwright's at the bottom, because she is the holder and may not be in a support of anything. **A recordwright read it, did not answer it, put a licence with four terms on a table, and read the fourth one twice, and wrote four lines in the back of her own book that are in no record but hers, and did not go out and did not open the book.** The appeal is in use and is not secured. The Quiet Chamber's quarter sentence and the page over a pump are still side by side in a record and are not the same sentence, and nothing in this batch is better than that. The Bridgeward has not been touched. No new enemy has been introduced and the planned ending is untouched.
