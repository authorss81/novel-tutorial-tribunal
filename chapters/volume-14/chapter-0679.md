# Chapter 679: Second Day Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which Nobody Goes Down And A Man Of Forty-One Bails A Flight Nobody Is Going Down, And The Question Is Put Again And Has Changed, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Says He Is Not Going To Be A Keeper

Second day of the hundred and nineteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-nine morning. Two hundred and seventy-nine days after the settlement. The fever fifty-six weeks and four days. Two hundred and thirty-three days since the division. One hundred and seventy-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Nobody went down on the Wednesday and the man of forty-one bailed the flight anyway, and he said why before anybody asked him, and it was the reason and not a figure.

The man is forty-one and he keeps two carts, and one of them has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making on the way up. He has been in and out of the ninth of nine doors twice a day for nineteen years.

“*The water was off all ninety at the sixth hour and the drain puts back about two feet a day, and if I do not bail it this morning then nobody can stand on the bottom nine this afternoon, and there is nobody coming this afternoon.*”

“*And I want it said that a man bailing a flight that nobody is going down is not doing a job for anybody. I have been doing it on Wednesdays for about three weeks and I did not do it on the Saturday because the bank was full and I was not going to stand in it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for two hours in four feet of water that nobody was going to use.

Then the man of forty-three gave the reading and the pair of figures, and it is the twelfth morning running, and he said what he has been saying for four days.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour and about an inch on the second step at the seventh hour. That is four days since it came off and it fills in about four, and the pair of figures has not moved in twelve mornings.*”

“*And I am going to stop saying the pair of figures out loud now and not because I have stopped having them. I am going to stop saying them because a man of sixty-one is not writing them down and a figure that is said out loud twelve times and not written down is a figure that about nine people in this bank have and about four hundred miles off nobody has.*”

Then the man of thirty-one started to say a question he has said twice, and a woman of forty-four stopped him before the end of it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. On the Sunday of the hundred and seventeenth week she said that two is the correct number of times to say one thing.

“*No. You said that on the Friday of the hundred and sixteenth week and you said it on the Sunday of last week and I said the thing about the number of times both times and you told me you had learned one thing in about nine days.*”

“*And you have not learned it. You have learned that a woman of forty-four will stop you. That is not the same as learning it, and I would like about four of you to be able to tell the difference.*”

Then he asked another question, and he said out loud that it was a different one and that he had checked.

“*Then here is a different one and I have checked it twice and it is not the same question.*”

“*Not who is holding it up. What can about nine people hold. That is the whole of the difference and I am not going to pretend the two run into each other.*”

Then the man of forty-one gave the answer, and it was about thirteen sentences, and it took him less time than he has ever taken to say anything on that bank.

“*About nine people can hold about nine doors. One each.*”

“*And that is not the nine jobs. The nine jobs are how we get there and this is what is on the other end of it. I have said that once already this week and I am not going to say it a second time, because a woman of thirty-eight stopped a man of thirty-one putting the two in a line.*”

“*And the answer has changed and I am going to say how it changed, because nobody else in this bank is going to.*”

“*On the Sunday of last week a man of forty-one told about nine people that the answer was no. It is not no now. It is nine doors and nine jobs and about nine people, and one each, and about four of those nine said they would not first and came anyway.*”

“*And I have had the ninth one for nineteen years and I did not know it was the ninth one. I am not going to give it back. I am not going to be thanked for it. I am the only one of these nine who can say what it costs and I am not going to be asked.*”

Then a man of sixty-one did a count out loud that nobody had asked for, and it is a figure of a book and not of the doors, and he said which.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years, and there are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five.

“*I have eleven years of a book of heads and carts. It has about nine hundred heads in it and carts against about as many. There is not one name in it of anybody who did anything in those eight doors.*”

“*And that is eight doors and no names. It is a figure of a book and not of the doors. I have said so twice in a fortnight and I am going to keep saying it until somebody gives me a figure that is a figure of the doors.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine hundred heads and about nine of them had the eight doors.

Then the man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said the thing about himself, and it was ten sentences, and he said it before anybody thought of asking him.

“*I am not going to be a keeper of any of it. I am not going to keep the ninth line on that stone. I am not going to count the lamps. I am not going to be the man who goes and looks at the eight doors nobody has been in.*”

“*I said on the Sunday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a thing that is sent for is a thing people are sent for about. I have been right twice since and I am not going to be anything else.*”

“*The day I am a keeper of this, I am a thing that is sent for, and I will have said so myself.*”

“*And a man of sixty-one has a book, and a man of thirty-eight has a lamp, and a man of forty-one has a door. None of the three of them can be sent for about the other two.*”

“*I would like about four of you to have that shape in your heads before anybody says the word keeper again.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about that and nobody improved on it.

Then the man of thirty-one said the cost of it out loud, and it was not the cost of the nine doors and it was the cost of a man of thirty-one, and he said it in the first sentence.

“*The cost of this is that about nine people have to be told something, one at a time, by somebody, and I am not going to be the somebody for any of them.*”

“*And I am not saying that about nobody’s sake. I asked a man of sixty-six what a floor was on the Thursday of the hundred and fifteenth week and got four sentences about a floor, and I have not asked a woman of thirty-eight a question since and I am not going to.*”

“*And I offered to be the thing that is sent for three times and was refused three times. Every one of those nine people watched at least one of them.*”

“*That is on me and it is not going to come off, and nobody is to forgive me for it, and I am not going to say that again.*”

Then the man of forty-one said what he was going to do about it. Nobody thanked him and nobody improved on it.

“*And I am going to ask about eight doors and not one, on foot, one at a time. It is going to take me about a week, because two of them are four miles off and one of them is in a village I have never been inside a gate of.*”

“*And I am not going to do it on a bank with nine people standing about, and if anybody in this bank wants to come and stand behind me while I do it they can come and stand behind me. And if anybody wants to wait at the top of that flight and be told what I am saying to people, they can wait there.*”

About nine people on that bank had the week and about four of them had the sentence about the gate.

Then the man of thirty-one said the count of his own out loud with the subtraction, because he had said on the Tuesday that he would, and he said it last because he had decided it would be last.

“*One hundred and twenty-five days. One hundred and twenty-five is six hundred and seventy-nine less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am not going to say a hearing is a number today. A man of sixty-one gave the last of that number on the Friday of the hundred and seventeenth week and stopped, and he was right to stop.*”

“*I am not going to pick it up off the ground and put a new one in his hand in front of about nine people, because it is the day I would feel like doing it.*”

Nobody on that bank said the word.

---

The fever was fifty-six weeks and four days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-five days.

A man of forty-one bailed a flight nobody was going down for about two hours, and said out loud that a man bailing a flight nobody is going down is not doing a job for anybody. He said that nine people can hold nine doors one each, and that he has had the ninth one for nineteen years and did not know it was the ninth one.

And a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said out loud that he is not going to be a keeper of any of it. He gave the reason that the day he is a keeper of it, he is a thing that is sent for, and he will have said so himself.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud that about nine people now have to be told something one at a time and that he is not going to be the somebody for any of them, and that nobody is to forgive him for it.
