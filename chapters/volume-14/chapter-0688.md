# Chapter 688: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Friday, And On Which Nobody Goes Down Because A Bank Is At About Ten Inches Over The Top Step, And The Question Is Put A Third Time By A Man Who Has A Scar And Is Still Nobody, And The Answer Has Changed Again And What About Nine People Can Hold Now Has Two Halves

Fourth day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and thirty-eighth morning. Two hundred and eighty-eight days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks and six days. Two hundred and forty-two days since the division. One hundred and eighty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about ten inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped. And he has now stopped after the reading on five mornings running.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has two crossings of a block of limestone with a line cut round it nine miles up this water in his own hand and no figure for the gap between them.

“*About ten inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Three days since it came off those steps and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And that is the reading. I have stopped five mornings running after the reading and I am going to go on stopping, and a man of thirty-one can come and ask me for the pair of figures any morning he likes and I will say I do not give it out loud any more, and that is not me being clever, it is that it is in about nine heads and there is nowhere else it is.*”

About four people on that bank had the ten inches and about nine of them had a bank they could not get up past two hundred yards of road.

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count out loud with the subtraction. And then he said what he was not going to write this week, and it was three things and none of them was a person.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years and has four lines on the leaf after the last head and there are not five, and he wrote a line in that book seventeen days ago and has not written a number in it since he took it.

“*He has been in this county a hundred and thirty-four days. A hundred and thirty-four is six hundred and eighty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am not going to write a number in this book this week. I am going to write one before this week is out and I am telling this bank that now so that nobody says afterwards that I was surprised into it.*”

“*And I am not going to write the six inches, or the one boot, or the space in the wall, and I have been asked for two of those three by two different mouths and a woman of forty-four has said that two is the correct number of times to say one thing and she was talking about me.*”

Then a man of forty-one said what his week of asking has come to. And he gave the count the way he gives every count, and it was not a good week and he did not dress it.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and asked a woman of forty-four one question on the Saturday of last week and has been walking on foot for seven days about eight doors nine miles down a river.

“*I have asked six of the eight. Six in seven days, and I am not going to round that and I am not going to call it about a week, because it is not a week, a week is seven days and I have six.*”

“*Four of the six said they had never heard of a door under a bank and said it the way a person says a thing they have never once had to think about. One of them said nothing at all and shut a door, and I stood on that step for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading.*”

“*And one of them is a woman of about sixty who told me in a long speech that when she was a girl a man used to tell her he went in and out of a door under a bank, and I asked her his name and she gave me his name and he has been dead about thirty years.*”

About four people on that bank had the thirty years and about nine of them had a name.

Then a man of thirty-one said he was going to ask a question. And he said out loud the whole of the housekeeping before he asked it, and a woman of forty-four stopped him once and then let him go.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and he offered three times in about two weeks to be the thing that is sent for and was refused three times out loud by a man who cannot see well.

“*I am going to ask what about nine people can hold. I asked it on the Wednesday of the hundred and nineteenth week and it has been asked twice in this county before that, and a woman of forty-four has told this bank in front of about nine people that two is the correct number of times to say one thing, and I am asking it a third time and I am going to say why out loud before I ask it.*”

“*I am asking it because the answer changed once on the Wednesday of the hundred and nineteenth week and it has changed again since. And I am not asking it a third time to see whether it changes again. I am asking it because on the Tuesday of this week I put my hand on a wall and there is a cut across it from the base of the thumb to the first joint. And I have a scar and there is nobody to put it on.*”

Then the woman of forty-four said the condition, and she gave it in about five sentences and then she said she was not going to stop him.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. And there are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she said on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a list is a column.

“*You may ask it. And I am going to ask you one thing first and you are going to answer it in front of these people and I am going to hear the answer or I am going to stop you.*”

“*You are not asking it because it is a good question. You are asking it because you have got a hand and you want to know whether the hand goes on the list. And if that is the reason then say it, because a question asked to get a scar onto a list of nine things is not the same as a question asked to find out what nine people can do.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word to her about that, and about four of them had thought it.

Then he asked it, and it was the third time, and about nine people on that bank had heard it twice.

And the man of forty-one gave the answer, and it took him about eighteen sentences and he said the count of the people out loud before he said anything else, because that is the shape of his figures.

“*About nine people can hold about nine doors. One each. That has not changed and it is not going to change and I am not going to pretend it has.*”

“*What has changed is what a door is. On the Thursday of the hundred and eighteenth week a door was a door and a job was a job and you could put the two in a line and I let a man of thirty-one put them in a line and a woman of thirty-eight stopped him in about seven sentences.*”

“*A door is now a job with a name on it. And the name is not there. Nine of those, and not one name on any of the nine. And there has not been a name on the ninth one for as long as I have been in and out of it, which is nineteen years.*”

“*And here is the half I have not said on any bank before. About nine people can hold a door that somebody is doing. They cannot hold a name. A name has to be given by a person, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether they will still be doing it. And a man of sixty-one has said that he is not going to cut one.*”

“*So the answer is two halves and it is not one figure. About nine people can hold about nine doors, one each. And they cannot hold the nine names, and there are not nine names, and no names at all is not a smaller number. It is the other kind of number and I am not going to add it to anything.*”

The first half went round that bank and the second one did not go with the rest of them.

Then he said the other half of the answer, about the hand. And nobody had asked him for it and about four people on that bank did not expect a man of forty-one to have anything to say about a hand.

“*And about nine people can hold about one scar, and the one who can hold it is the man it is on. And there is no form in this county that says a man may be sent for about his own right hand.*”

“*I am the only one of these nine who can say what it costs and I am not going to be asked. And I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and I am going to say it once and I have said it.*”

Then the man of thirty-one said the cost of it out loud. And it was his and not the bank’s, and he said it in the first sentence, and he did not say the second sentence anybody was waiting for.

“*I have a cut across my right hand that a man of thirty-eight measured against a groove in a wall on the Tuesday of this week and about four of you have seen it and the rest of you have not.*”

“*I did not offer it. Nobody offered it to me. I offered to be the thing that is sent for three times in about two weeks and I was refused three times. And nobody in this county ever said to me that the ninth thing anybody does in this bank costs a hand.*”

“*And I am not going to say it was worth it, and I am not going to say it was not worth it, and if anybody on this bank says either of those two things to me I am going to stand here and let them, and then it will have been said and I will not have said it.*”

Nobody on that bank said either of those two things, and nobody said sorry. And nobody said the word brave, and about four people on that bank looked at the water.

Then about nine people stood about on a bank with ten inches over the top step, and the light went, and a man in an ash on the low side of a landing nine miles up this water did not say anything, and a woman of twenty-nine in the reed on the other side of that landing did not say anything either, and about nine people walked past both of those mouths about nine times.

---

The fever was fifty-seven weeks and six days old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty-four days.

What about nine people can hold was asked a third time and it has two halves and neither of them is the figure it was on the Wednesday of the hundred and nineteenth week. About nine people can hold about nine doors, one each.

And they cannot hold the nine names, because there are not nine names and no names at all is not a smaller number but the other kind of number. And a man of forty-one said it twice on that bank and would not add the two halves to anything.

And about nine people can hold about one scar, and the one who can hold it is the man it is on.
