# Chapter 694: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-First Week, And A Thursday, And On Which Nobody Goes Down Because A Man Of Thirty-Eight Told That Bank On The Tuesday Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week What His Shoulder Would Do On A Thursday, And Nobody Tells Him He Was Right, And A Man Of Thirty-One Who Wanted A Count Of Times Was Refused One By The Argument He Has Been Making On That Bank For Six Weeks

Third day of the hundred and twenty-first week. His four hundred and forty-fourth morning. Two hundred and ninety-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-eight weeks and five days. Two hundred and forty-eight days since the division. One hundred and ninety-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped, and nobody on that bank asked him for the pair of figures and he did not offer it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. It is seven days since the Thursday of last week that he has given a reading and stopped, and he said on the Friday after that Thursday that he was going to keep stopping, and he has, and he has said he has nothing to say about a night three times and has not said it since.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Three days since it came off those steps, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And it was about eight inches at this hour yesterday. That is the working, and I am not going to say anything else about water on this bank until somebody asks me a question they have not asked.*”

About four people on that bank had the eight inches and about nine of them had a Thursday.

Then a man of thirty-eight stood in the middle of that bank and said the thing he had said in a gallery nine days ago. Nobody looked surprised, and that was the whole of what a prediction is worth when it is about a shoulder.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has a lamp that is his.

“*It is a Thursday. I said on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that my shoulder would be no good for carrying a pole up that water on a Thursday, and I said it in a gallery with a lamp in my hand.*”

“*And I put that lamp down about four times to get the shoulder back in, and I have not been able to lift my right hand above that shoulder since, and I am not going down today.*”

“*And I am not going to be told I was right. I would like about four of you to notice that nobody has said it and I would like it noticed by whoever was going to.*”

Nobody on that bank said he was right. Nobody on that bank said he was wrong either, and about four of them had believed him nine days ago and about nine of them had not, and the two of those numbers had not moved all morning.

Then he said what a Thursday is and what not going down costs, and he gave the working, and a man of sixty-one wrote nothing down and said so out loud.

“*A Thursday is a boat and a pole nine miles up that water and nine miles down it, and about nine hours, and about nine minutes of reading two copies that are nine miles apart.*”

“*And those two copies are not going to be read today. Tomorrow is no better. The next reading of either of them is in seven days, and I have not got a figure for what a copy thinks about seven days.*”

“*And that is seven days, and seven days is a figure of a gap and not of a copy.*”

“*And the copies do not know. That is the true of it and I have not got a way of saying it that is better. Two copies in two rooms nine miles apart are lying there not knowing, and there is nobody in this county who could tell them.*”

About four people on that bank had the seven days and about nine of them had the two rooms.

Then the man of thirty-one asked the man of thirty-eight for a count, and it was a count he wanted and not a count he was going to use. He said why out loud before he asked it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has a cut across his right palm that a man of thirty-eight measured against a groove in a wall on the Tuesday of the hundred and twentieth week and would not round it.

“*How many times have you said a thing in advance and had it come true. I am asking because you have been right nine days running and I have not got a figure for that and I have been standing here for about a minute wanting one.*”

“*I am not going to use it. I am going to hear it and then I am going to do nothing with it, and I would like that said out loud before I say anything else about it.*”

Then a man of sixty-one refused, and he refused it in seven sentences, and the reason was the reason a man of thirty-one has been giving that bank for a fortnight.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. Five lines are on the leaf after the last head, the last of the five is a number, and he wrote that number on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week and has not written one since.

“*No. And I am not going to give it to you in a small voice either, so that you can hear how much it costs me.*”

“*How many times a man has been right is a count of times. It has no unit on it. You have spent a fortnight standing on this bank telling nine people that a number without a thing behind it is a decoration, and this one is a decoration, and it is a decoration about a man with a bad shoulder.*”

“*And you are the man who taught it, and I am not going to pretend to you that I am refusing you out of meanness. I am refusing you because you would not have given it out.*”

Then a man of thirty-one took it back, and it took him about four seconds, and he said the thing that made it worse for him than the refusal was.

“*He is right and I would not have given it out. I had it in my mouth for about a minute and I was going to give it as a number and I was going to let about nine people have it and about four of them would have said it was a fine thing.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and forty days. A hundred and forty is six hundred and ninety-four less five hundred and fifty-four. And that is a count of days and the day I came in is not a day I was in, and I am not going to put the two halves of that in the same line with what I nearly did.*”

Then the man of thirty-one offered, and it was the fourth time in about three weeks and it was the same offer. A man who cannot see well refused it in a sentence he has said three times, and did not raise his voice about it.

“*Then I will take the boat up. I have said that I would be the thing that is sent for three times and I am saying it a fourth and nobody has asked me to.*”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of those sheets of deal. He has said the day he is a keeper of any of this is the day he is a thing that is sent for.

“*No. I said it on the Sunday of the hundred and eighteenth week and I said it on the Wednesday after and I have said it every day since and you have offered three times.*”

“*And I am not going to be a fourth thing for you.*”

“*A man who cannot see well that poled nine miles up that water on a Thursday in about nine hours is a man who would get it wrong at about the third hour. And you know that. And you offered because you did not want to sit on that bank.*”

Nobody on that bank improved on him and nobody said sorry, and about four of them looked at the water instead of at a man of thirty-one.

Then a man of fifty-four said one thing about a hand, and he cannot read. He said he was not going to keep counting it, and he gave the figure once and then refused to have the counting continued.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain. He has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years, and he has said that he cannot read a great many times.

“*I have been asked how many days that cut has been there three times in nine days and I have given it three times. I am not going to be asked a fourth time this week.*”

“*It was the Tuesday of the hundred and twentieth week. That is nine days, and nine is six hundred and ninety-four less six hundred and eighty-five, and I counted it because nobody else on this bank was going to.*”

“*And I am not going to be a keeper of the counting. A man of fifty-four who cannot read is the only person anybody can be sent for about a hand, and that is a gap in the forms and it is not a right, and I said that on the Saturday and I am saying it again and I am not going to be thanked for either.*”

Then a man of forty-one said the thing about the boat that nobody had said, and it was about a wheel and not about a shoulder, and he gave it flat.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts, and one of them has a wheel on the near side that has been making a noise for a fortnight. He is the ninth of nine and has been in and out of the ninth door twice a day for nineteen years.

“*And that is the whole of what a Thursday costs.*”

“*And I want it said that the wheel on my near cart is going to cost me the same Thursday in about four weeks. The two of those are the same size of thing to me, and neither of them is anybody’s shoulder.*”

“*I have got two carts and about nine hours of a man of thirty-eight in a boat. And I have got about four people who cannot get nine miles up that water and back in a day.*”

“*And I am not going to put the boat and the wheel in a line and call it a week.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the seventh, and she gave the count, and she said what she was not going to be asked about it next.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. She has said a thing with no person on either side of it is a thing that has not happened yet seven times this fortnight.

“*Seven. And it is about a place in a floor, and I am not going to improve on it, and I am not going to be thanked for the seventh.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked whether the eighth is due. I have said twice this week that I will tell a man of sixty-one the same two things every morning, and a thing with no person on either side of it is one of the two and I am not going to soften it for a man with a bad shoulder.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word back to her, and about four of them had the seven.

Then about nine people stood about on a bank with about twelve inches of water over the top step, and about nine steps of that flight were under about four feet of it. Nobody went down any of them.

A man in an ash on the low side of a landing about eight and a half miles up that water said nothing at all. A woman of twenty-nine in a reed on the other side of that landing said nothing at all either.

---

The fever was fifty-eight weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and forty days.

A man of thirty-eight said on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that his shoulder would be no good for carrying a pole up that water on a Thursday. On the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-first week it was not good, and he did not go down, and nobody on that bank told him he had been right and nobody told him he had not.

And two copies nine miles apart were not read, and they are not going to be read for seven days. They do not know, and there is nobody in this county who could tell them.
