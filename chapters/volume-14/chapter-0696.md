# Chapter 696: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-First Week, And A Saturday, And On Which A Bank Is Full And Still Holding, And A Man Of Forty-Three Says Out Loud That He Has Been Counting The Days Of A Man In An Ash For Sixty-Six Days And Has Never Once Asked Him What He Was Doing, And Walks Half A Mile Up The Water And Asks Him, And What Comes Back Is What Is Left Of A Burned Thing, Which Is Neither A Copy Nor Whole

Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-first week. His four hundred and forty-sixth morning. Two hundred and ninety-six days after the settlement. The fever fifty-nine weeks. Two hundred and fifty days since the division. One hundred and ninety-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the bank was full and holding, said what he thought would happen in the night, and said he had no figure for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years, and a full bank is about sixteen inches over the top step, and he has said he has nothing to say about a night four times.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Five days since it came off those steps, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And it was about sixteen inches at this hour yesterday, and a bank that is full stays full. I think it comes off in the night.*”

“*And I have said four times that I have no figure for a night, and I have got one this morning.*”

About four people on that bank had the four times and about nine of them had a full bank.

Then the man of forty-three said the thing about himself, and he said it before anybody had asked him, and it was about his own figure and not about a man of thirty-four.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of my landing for sixty-six days. Sixty-six is six hundred and ninety-six less six hundred and thirty, and I have given that figure every morning for sixty-six mornings, and about nine of you have had it.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-nine in a reed on the other side of that landing has been in it sixty-seven days. Sixty-seven is six hundred and ninety-six less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*And I have not added those two together in fifteen days and I am not starting on a Saturday.*”

“*And here is what I have not said in sixty-six mornings. I am the only person anybody can be sent for about that landing.*”

“*I have been giving you a figure of a man in a tree every morning since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week, and I have never once asked him what he is doing in it. A figure of a man in a tree is not a figure of anything except the count.*”

Nobody on that bank had asked him that either, and about four of them had not thought about it at all until that morning.

Then a woman of thirty-eight said the thing about the counting, and she said it flat, and she said it was not about the man in the ash and was about a man of forty-three.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is her mother.

“*A mill is a keeping and it grinds on the seventh morning because somebody turned the handle on the sixth. That is the whole of my trade and I have said it before and I am not going to say it better.*”

“*And a man of forty-three has been counting a man in a tree for sixty-six days and a woman of twenty-nine in a reed for sixty-seven. He has never once asked either of them what they are keeping.*”

“*And I am not going to stand here and tell a man of forty-three that his morning is wrong.*”

“*I am going to tell him the other half. Somebody is keeping a man in an ash. That is a figure of a tree and not of the man, and I have got no idea what it is and I am not going to guess at it on a Saturday with a full bank on.*”

Then about nine people went up that water in the morning, and it is about half a mile. A man of forty-three went with them because it is his landing. A man of thirty-eight went with his own lamp and did not use it.

The landing is about half a mile below where the block of limestone with a line cut round it goes under. The man of thirty-four was where he has been since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week, and he came out of the hollow when he heard about nine sets of boots on the stones.

He is thirty-four. He has been in that ash sixty-six days, and nothing has been asked of him in any of them, and he is not one of the nine and nobody has ever put him in a list of anything.

“*You have been saying a number about me for sixty-six mornings.*”

“*I know. I can hear the landing from about nine feet inside it and I have been hearing my own number every morning for about three weeks and I never said anything about it because a man in a tree is not a person anybody can be sent for about and I am not going to be the first one to complain about a figure.*”

Then a man of forty-three said his name out loud, and it was the first time he had said it in sixty-six days, and he said that, and then he asked the one question.

“*Your name is Ivo Kellow. I have counted you for sixty-six days and I have not said that name out loud once in any of them, and there are about nine people standing on this landing who did not know it until this morning.*”

“*And here is the question and it is one question and I am not going to dress it up. What have you got in there with you.*”

And the man of thirty-four said what it was, and he said how long he had had it, and he gave the figure with the subtraction the way everybody on that bank gives a figure, and he said where it came from.

“*It was in my hands when I got in. Not in the tree. In my hands, before the first day.*”

“*Sixty-six days. Sixty-six is six hundred and ninety-six less six hundred and thirty, and I have had it for every one of them, and I have counted them the same way your man has counted them, and I did not know anybody was counting until about three weeks ago.*”

“*And nobody put it there. I want that said first, because a man of thirty-one is standing there and I know what his face does. Nobody brought it to me. It was lying at the bottom of that hollow under about a foot of leaves and I put my hands in for something else.*”

About four people on that landing had the ash and about nine of them had the sixty-six days.

Then he brought it out, and about four of them saw it, and it is a board about so long that two men could lift it, and about four inches of one end is gone.

It is a board, and it is burnt at one end, and the burnt end is about four inches of it. The rest of it is the colour of a thing that has been wet and dried about nine times.

There is a mark cut in it about a foot from the burnt end. The mark is two hands cut side by side with a space between them about as wide as a thumb, and there is no bar under it and no numeral on it.

And a man of thirty-eight put his own nail against the cut in it and gave the depth out loud, and would not round it, and said what he had measured it against.

“*About as deep as my own nail is thick. That is the whole of the figure and I am not going to round it, and I am not going to say it is exactly my nail, because my nail is a nail and this is a cut in a board that has been in a fire.*”

“*And here is what the fire did and it is a figure of a fire and not of a year. It took about four inches off that end and it did not take anything off that mark, because a fire takes the surface of a thing and the depth of a cut is not the surface of a cut.*”

About four people on that landing had the nail and about nine of them had the four inches.

Then a man of thirty-eight said which of the two things it is, and he said it plainly, and he gave the two and said what each of them was, and nobody on that landing improved on it.

“*It is not a copy. A copy is a thing somebody made after, and nobody has made this.*” He put his thumb flat on the burnt end. “*And it is not whole. About a third of it is gone and the third of it is not a third of the length, it is a third of what is on it, and I have not got a figure for what is on it because I cannot read.*”

“*So it is a remainder. That is the word. It is what is left of a thing that was burned, and it is about four hundred years old, and about four hundred years is about a hundred and forty-six thousand days, and that is a figure of the guild mark in a lintel and not of this board.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what the depth is, and it came out of him before he had decided, and a man of sixty-six took it off him in two sentences.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county one hundred and forty-two days. He put his hand on a going in a wall on the Tuesday of the hundred and twentieth week and has a cut across his right palm for it.

“*Then that is the seventh. A cut about as deep as a nail and two hands with a space between them is a keeping, and a keeping is a function, and I have got the function of the seventh out of a burnt board in about a quarter of an hour.*”

Abel Fenner is sixty-six, digs for water, has been under about ninety steps of bank twice in forty-one years, and is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about the word *loom*.

“*A cut is a cut. You have a board and a burn in it and a mark in it, and the mark is a mark a person puts on a thing they have to come back to, and I have said that about nine of them and I have not said it about this one because I have not seen this one until a quarter of an hour ago.*”

“*And a depth is a depth. It is a figure of a cut in a board and it is not a figure of anybody’s work, and you cannot get nine hundred people out of it by measuring it against your own nail, and there are not nine hundred people.*”

“*And I am not going to be the man who says a burnt board is a job. Take it back. And take it back in your own mouth, and do not go at a board of oak with a man of sixty-six about nine steps back.*”

Then the man of thirty-one took it back, standing on a landing with a block of limestone under it nine miles further up, and he did not put anything in the place of it.

“*A board. About four inches of one end gone. A mark with a space in it. A cut about as deep as a man’s own nail. That is what is left of the seventh and I am not going to call it a keeping and I am not going to call it a term.*”

Then the man of thirty-four said who he was going to hand it to, and he said it before he handed it over, and he gave three names and one of them was a man who is nobody’s.

“*I am not going to give it to a man with a book. He has a book and he has not put one line in it for eleven years and he is right not to, and I have read about nine lines out of a book in my life.*”

“*And I am not going to give it to a man who is nobody’s. I do not know that anybody can be sent for him, and a thing that cannot be sent for is a thing that gets lost and I have had this one for sixty-six days and I have watched it not get lost.*”

“*I am going to give it to the man who counted me. He has given my number out loud every morning for sixty-six days and he has never once said what I was doing, and I have heard him not say it, and that is more than the other two of you have done.*”

And the man of forty-three took it, and put it under his own coat, and said one sentence about it.

A woman of twenty-nine in a reed on the other side of that landing said nothing at all, and a man of forty-three counted her at sixty-seven days on his way back down.

“*I am taking it because a man asked me a question and I am not going to be thanked for taking it and I am not going to be forgiven for having had it sixty-six days.*”

Nobody on that landing said sorry and nobody said the word brave, and about four of them stood about nine feet off a man of thirty-four and let him be about nine feet off.

---

The fever was fifty-nine weeks old. He had been in that county a hundred and forty-two days.

There is a man in an ash on the low side of a landing about eight and a half miles up this water, and he has been in it sixty-six days. His name is Ivo Kellow, and a man of forty-three counted him every morning for sixty-six days without once saying it out loud or asking him what he was doing.

And in his hands, before the first day and for every one of the sixty-six, was a board with about four inches of one end burnt off it. In the board there is a mark of two hands with a space between them about as wide as a thumb, and a cut about as deep as a man’s own nail, and fire has taken the surface of a thing and has not taken the depth of a cut.

It is not a copy and it is not whole. It is a remainder, and a man of thirty-one said it was a function and took it back in his own mouth in front of about nine people.
