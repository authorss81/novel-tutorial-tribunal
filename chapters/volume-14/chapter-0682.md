# Chapter 682: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Water Comes Off The Top Step In A Night And Nine Go Down, And A Man Who Cannot Read Finds A Space Cut In A Wall With Nothing In It, And Somebody Says The Word Out Loud

Fifth day of the hundred and nineteenth week. His four hundred and thirty-second morning. Two hundred and eighty-two days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks. Two hundred and thirty-six days since the division. One hundred and eighty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps by the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and then gave the reason he was not going to give the count with it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has given a reading on most mornings since the second day of the hundred and sixteenth week. And he stopped saying the pair of figures on the Thursday of this week in front of about nine people.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off the top step in the night and I watched it come off, because I was on that landing at the second hour and there is nothing else to do at the second hour.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you how many days that is. I gave the last number of days anybody has had from me on the Thursday morning and the pair of figures is not going out loud, and I am not going to hand a man of thirty-one a number this morning to put beside a number he has already got.*”

“*And what I will say is this. The pair of figures and what that water does have come apart. And I am the man who gives both of them, and I have stopped giving one of them, and you can decide between them yourselves.*”

About four people on that bank had the pair of figures and about nine of them did not, and one of the nine was the man of sixty-one.

Then a woman of forty-nine said a figure out loud, and nobody had asked her for one. And she counts loaves and has come down that road four times now.

“*One hundred and twenty-eight days. Six hundred and eighty-two less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I count days because I count loaves. If I do not count them I bake the wrong number and about nine houses find out at about the sixth hour. And that is the only sort of number I have ever had in my hand.*”

Then the man of forty-one went up the cart road at about the eighth hour with his two carts behind him and nothing on either of them, and he came back down it at about the eleventh hour, and he had asked one person one question and he said so on the bank before anybody had thought of asking him.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road. And about nine houses in that village take her bread, and about sixty loaves go through her oven in a night and about forty of them go up that track.

“*You asked me. I am not going to say I am glad about it and I am not going to say I am not glad about it. And I will not know which one until about the sixth hour tomorrow.*”

“*And I am going to say the other half now so that nobody has to say it to me later. On the Friday of the hundred and eighteenth week I told a bank that nobody in this county has the second thing and that I have wanted it for about nineteen years. The second thing is being asked. You have now used the only person in four counties who wants to be asked as a person to ask.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word back to her about it.

Then they went down at about the first hour of the afternoon, and the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his, and the man of thirty-one went fifth with his hands and nothing else in them.

The gallery is about nine feet high and there are about nine beams set about four feet apart and eight of them are still, and the ninth went along about two inches when the man of thirty-eight came down onto the last of the steps and went along about two inches again when he came off them, and about four people in that gallery had seen that before and said nothing about it.

Then they went past the last of the beams, and about nine feet past that place the roof came down and the walls came in and the floor came up. And the gravel is walked about a foot deep and there is no groove in it. Then along about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage at the same fall. Then up the step.

And in the wall there were nine openings, and the same mark cut over every one of them.

Then the man of fifty-four stopped, and he had been looking at that mark since the Wednesday of the hundred and eighteenth week. And he said the thing nobody had asked him about and it took him about nine sentences.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years.

“*Hold on. I have been looking at that mark since the Wednesday of the hundred and eighteenth week and I have never once looked at the middle of it. And I have never looked at the middle of it because I cannot read and there has never been anything in the middle of it for me.*”

“*There is something in the middle of it. Two hands cut side by side and a space between them about as wide as my thumb. And it is cut square, and it is cut to the same depth as the hands and it is not a mark anybody makes.*”

“*It is a space somebody left for a thing that was not a hand. I cannot tell you what is in it and I cannot tell you whether anything was ever in it. And I have not been able to read a word for about forty years and I am not going to start by looking at a space in a wall.*”

About nine people in that passage were looking at a space between two hands and nobody in that passage could read one word of what used to be in it.

Then the man of forty-one went to the shelf and put his own hand on the front edge of it, and the front edge was worn round and the back of it was not, and he said so.

“*The hook has been used and the shelf has been used and both of them are worn at the front and not at the back. That is a person who takes things off and puts them on the near side and stands facing the wall.*”

“*And a woman of thirty-eight said the same about the eighth one on the Sunday of the hundred and eighteenth week and about four of you heard it then. It is the same in this one and it is not new, and I am giving you it again because there is nothing new in this room and because she said it about a room and not about a door.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the thing he had been carrying since the Wednesday of the hundred and nineteenth week. And it was about nine spaces and about eight people, and he said both halves out loud so that nobody would put them in one line.

“*There is a space cut in the middle of nine marks and there is no name in any of them. That is nine absences and not nine people.*”

“*And there are about nine of us on this bank, and eight of the nine have been asked nothing this week. And the ninth of us has been asked everything this week and has been refused three times and is nobody’s.*”

“*I am not going to put those two nines in the same line. A woman of thirty-eight stopped a man of thirty-one putting two things in the same line in about seven sentences on the Thursday of the hundred and eighteenth week. And I have not forgotten it, and I have not forgotten the seven.*”

About four people in that passage had the nine absences and about nine of them had the two nines.

Then a man of fifty-four said the thing that made the cost of the finding plain, and he asked nobody for anything, and nobody asked him.

“*A space with nothing in it is not a door that is empty. I have carried gravel for nine years and I know an empty cart and I know a cart that has been loaded and not moved. And they do not look the same and nobody has ever asked me why.*”

“*That is what I have got. There is a place cut for a name and no name has been in it. I cannot read one word and I have not been able to for about forty years. And that is the whole of what I know about it, and I am going up those steps now and I am not going to say anything else about it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight put the lamp on the floor and counted them, going along. And the man of forty-four said there is no form that says a keeper of a book may go and look at a wall.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. And there are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she says two is the correct number of times to say one thing.

“*And there is a woman of twenty-eight on this bank who has said out loud that nobody in this county can be sent for about what she said to a stone. And she is right, and I am not going to be her.*”

“*There is one hand and one book in this county and the man holding it is sixty-one, and he has told this bank twice in a fortnight what he will not write, and I am not going to send him to look at a wall in the dark with a lamp he does not own.*”

Nobody in that passage argued with her and nobody in that passage improved on her.

Then the man of forty-one said the whole of what it is. And he said it in about nine sentences, and the first one was the word and nobody in that passage had used it.

“*A term is a job with a name on it. That is the whole of the word and I have got no better one and I am not going to go and look for a better one.*”

“*I have done the ninth of those jobs for nineteen years, twice a day when the mill was working and once a day when it was not. And there is no name on it and there is no name on any of the other eight either.*”

“*And the name is not missing off the stone. The space is cut and it is cut square and it is cut old. Somebody left a place for a name and then nobody put one there, or somebody put one there and took it out, and those are two different things and I have got a space and not a name, and I am the ninth of nine and I have been it for nineteen years and nobody ever told me there was a place for my name.*”

“*And that is the first of the nine. That is one term found, and it was found as an absence and not as a document, and I am not going to say I have got it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one asked one question out loud, and it was the question he has been carrying since the Wednesday of this week and he said he had checked it and that it was a different one from the one he asked that day.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and he offered three times in about two weeks to be the thing that is sent for and was refused three times out loud without a voice being raised.

“*Then what is a job with no name. Not what it is for. What a person does with it.*”

“*And I am asking that on a Saturday afternoon with a lamp in a passage and I know what day it is. A man of sixty-one gave three reasons out loud on the Monday of this week for not writing down a wall he has not been down to. And I am not going to be the reason there is a fourth question on a bank about nine days into a fortnight.*”

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes. And the boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and did not go down.

And nobody on that bank said a word about nineteen years, and a woman of forty-four went up the cart road at about the sixth hour with nothing on her cart, and the man of forty-one went up it at the same time about a quarter of a mile behind her, and neither of them walked with the other.

---

The fever was fifty-seven weeks old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-eight days.

A man who cannot read found a space cut square between two hands, about as wide as his thumb, on each of nine marks, and it is cut to the depth of the hands and it is old, and nobody in four counties can say what was in it or whether anything ever was.

And a man of forty-one said out loud that a term is a job with a name on it. And that he has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and nobody ever left a place for his name and nobody ever told him there was one.

That is one of the nine. It was found as an absence and not as a document, and it has not been got back.
