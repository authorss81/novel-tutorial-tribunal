# Chapter 673: Third Day Of The Hundred And Eighteenth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which They Go Into The Ninth Of Nine Openings And Find That The Keeping In It Has Been One Person’s For Nineteen Years And Was Never Anybody’s Job, And A Man Of Forty-One Says So Out Loud

Third day of the hundred and eighteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-third morning. Two hundred and seventy-three days after the settlement. The fever fifty-five weeks and five days. Two hundred and twenty-seven days since the division. One hundred and seventy-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The man of forty-three gave the reading before anybody asked him for it, and it was the sixth morning in a row he had given one.

“*About six inches on the top step at the seventh hour. Two days since it came off those steps and it is coming back up.*”

“*And the pair of figures has not moved all week. It empties in about three days and it fills in about four, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself, and I am not going to say it a sixth time as though it were new.*”

Then a man of sixty-one did two counts out loud on that bank before anybody had thought of asking, and he said out loud that he was doing them because a day had gone by and he did not want the day to go by without something in it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. There are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head. On the second leaf of the last gathering of the day before that there is a line that says a figure was wrong by a day, and there are not five lines on the leaf after the last head.

“*He has been in this county one hundred and nineteen days. One hundred and nineteen is six hundred and seventy-three less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And the boy went down on the Friday of the hundred and fifteenth week and that is twenty days, and twenty is six hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and fifty-three, and he has not gone down since and nobody has asked him to and he has told me twice that he is not going to.*”

Then they went down, and the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp, and the man of thirty-one went fifth.

They went past the last of the beams, and about nine feet past that place where the roof comes down and the gravel is walked. Then along about a hundred and thirty feet of passage that goes on at the same fall, and up a step. There were nine openings in the wall and the same mark cut over every one of them.

Then the man of thirty-eight went into the ninth.

It was about as long as two men and about as wide as one and it was dry and there was nothing in it that anybody could move. There is a hook cut in the roof of it about the height of a man’s hand. There is a shelf cut in the wall about the height of a hand and the shelf is empty. And above the shelf there is a groove cut in the stone about nine inches long, and in the end of the groove there is a notch.

“*A notch is a count. I have thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board I carry in a coat and nobody has ever asked me what the thirty-two is for, including a man of thirty-one.*”

“*And I have counted them again this morning and it is still thirty-two, and I want it said that I counted them in a room with nine people in it and got the same number I got the last six times.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight said the other thing about that room, and it was the thing nobody had asked him for either.

“*There are eight of those two hands over those eight doors and there is one hand over this one, and I have looked at all nine and that is what is cut in the stone.*”

“*The lintel ninety steps back says two hands, once a day, before the light. And that one says one. And I have turned a wheel once a day for about nine years and I do not know what one is for, and I am not going to stand in a room and guess at it.*”

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it. One of the eleven is her mother, and she has kept a chamber that is a week for about nine years, and nobody has ever asked her what it is for.

“*And I am going to say the other half and it is worse. One is not a person being lazy. One is a thing one person does.*”

“*I have watched about nine people in four days decide quietly that one is a smaller version of two. It is not. And if it is, then about eight of those nine doors is a door that gets opened once instead of twice, and I do not know what is on the other side of any of them.*”

Then the man of forty-one said what had been in the back of his head since the Tuesday, and he said it standing in a doorway about as wide as himself, and it took him about ten sentences and the last one is the reason.

“*I have been in and out of that ninth door for nineteen years. Twice a day when the mill was working and once a day when it was not.*”

“*And it is not my work. I have got two carts and a load of stone that goes up a track and that is my work, and a woman of thirty-eight turns a wheel and that is hers, and a man of thirty-eight reads two copies on a Thursday and that is his.*”

“*And nobody asked me. There is no form in this county that says a man may be asked to do a thing twice a day for nineteen years, and there is no form that says I may not.*”

“*And I have done it for nineteen years and I have not said it out loud to anybody until this morning in a doorway.*”

“*And the reason I am saying it is not that I want a day counted for it. I have not wanted a day counted for it once in nineteen years and I am not starting. I am saying it because it is somebody’s and it has been somebody’s this whole time and I did not know whose.*”

About nine people in that passage heard a man of forty-one say a thing about himself and about four of them heard the fourth sentence.

Then a man of thirty-one tried to make it into a list and was stopped in about seven sentences by a woman who was not being kind and was not being unkind.

“*There are nine openings. Nine. And a man of thirty-eight has counted them going along and a man of fifty-four has counted them coming back and it is nine both times.*”

“*That is not the nine jobs and do not put it in the same line as the nine jobs. The nine jobs are how we get there. This is what is on the other end of it, and there is a difference between a thing you do on a bank and a thing somebody has done for four hundred years.*”

“*And I am saying that because I have been in this county one hundred and nineteen days and I have watched about nine people make a number into a shape twice this week and I am not going to watch it a third time.*”

Then a man of sixty-six was asked what one hand is for and gave the same four things he has given since the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and then said the fifth thing, which was not about the beam at all.

“*I have a rate and a wear. I was twenty-five the second time and it was moving. I have had forty-one years to add a third and I have not.*”

“*And I have been asked what one hand is for by a man of thirty-eight and I am going to say the true of one half of it and not the other half, and I am sixty-six and I am a sinker and I know what a hole is.*”

“*A hole is a thing a person comes back to. That is a rate and it is a wear and it is not who, and I said on Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week that I will not be the man who tells you and I am not going to start on a Thursday.*”

Nobody in that passage thanked him.

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people, and the boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and a coat and did not go down and did not ask.

Nobody on that bank said a word about nineteen years, and nobody said a word to the man of forty-one, and he said before he was thanked that he was not going to be thanked and that he had four feet of water to be in on the Wednesday.

---

The fever was fifty-five weeks and five days old. He had been in that county one hundred and nineteen days.

Nine openings were counted by two men walking opposite ways, and there is the same mark cut over all nine of them. Over eight of them it is two hands and over the ninth it is one, and a woman of thirty-eight says one is not a smaller version of two and does not know what one is for.

And a man of forty-one said out loud, standing in a doorway about as wide as himself, that the twice-a-day he has done for nineteen years was never his work. He said it was never anybody’s job, and that nobody asked him, and that there is no form that says he may.

And he said the reason he was saying it was not that he wanted a day counted for it.

And nine openings is nine and it is not the nine jobs, and a woman of thirty-eight stopped a man of thirty-one from putting the two in the same line.
