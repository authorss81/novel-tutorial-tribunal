# Chapter 691: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Man Of Forty-One Goes In Through A Gate He Has Never Been Inside And Comes Out Of It Again, And Seven Of Eight Doors Have Been Asked In Nine Days And The One Name Is Thirty Years Dead

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and forty-first morning. Two hundred and ninety-one days after the settlement. The fever fifty-eight weeks and two days. Two hundred and forty-five days since the division. One hundred and ninety-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps by the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and then gave the other thing, which was about the night.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has two crossings of a block of limestone with a line cut round it nine miles up this water in his own hand, and no figure for the gap between them.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off the top step in the night and that is the third time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And I have now had nothing to say about a night three times. I am not going to be given a fourth. There is no figure for a night in this county or in any other.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to have heard me say that three times and not to expect it on Wednesday.*”

About four people on that bank had the three times and about nine of them had the water.

Then he did the two counts he does before he has had anything out of a cup, and he gave them in the order he gives them, and he did not add them.

“*A man of thirty-four in an ash on the low side of that landing for sixty-one days. Sixty-one is six hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine in a reed on the other side for sixty-two days. Sixty-two is six hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and twenty-nine. I have not added those two together in ten days and I am not starting on a Monday.*”

Nobody on that bank walked over to the ash. Nobody on that bank walked over to the reed.

Then a boy of thirteen said a figure of his own out loud, because he says one every morning and has done for about eighteen days, and this one was a count of days and not a count of times.

The boy is thirteen and is not a head in the book of heads and carts, because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen. He walks four miles up that track with a barrow, and four miles is about two hours.

“*Thirty-eight days since I went down. Thirty-eight is six hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*And I have said a figure out loud on about eighteen mornings. That is a count of times and not a clock, and nobody has told me to stop and nobody has told me to go on.*”

“*And it is a Monday and there is a man going in a gate and I am not going to be one of the nine that stand behind him.*”

Then a man of thirty-one did the thing he does, which is take two numbers that are correct and put them together and get a figure that is about nobody.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and no form that renews him and no rate on him and no hundred in that county either.

“*Seven of eight doors in nine days. That is a bit more than one a day and it has been the same for nine days and I have been watching it do it.*”

“*A man of sixty-one has been counting the days a man of thirty-four has been in an ash. He got sixty-one this morning. I have been on that landing twice.*”

“*And if you put my seven and a half with his sixty-one you get a rate. And a rate is a thing people can be sent for about.*”

Then a man of sixty-one took it apart, in public, in six sentences, and did it the way he takes everything apart, which is to say without any pleasure in it at all.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head, and the last of the five is a number he wrote on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week.

“*Seven is a figure of doors and nine is a figure of mornings. And the division of a door by a morning is not a smaller thing than either of them, it is nothing, because a door is not a morning and a morning is not a door.*”

“*And you have not been on that landing twice. I have been on it every morning for sixty-one days and you have not been on it once, and the difference between those two numbers is not a rate of anything.*”

“*Take it back. Say the two figures and do not do the sum, and I will not write either of them down, which is the same as I have said every morning this fortnight.*”

And the man of thirty-one took it back, in his own mouth, standing where he was standing, and he did not improve on himself while he did it.

“*Seven doors and nine mornings. That is all of it.*”

“*I am not going to give you the half. And I am not going to be the man on that bank who divides a door by a morning.*”

“*I have been doing that since the first week I got here. I am not stopping because I am thirty-one.*”

Nobody on that bank gave him back the half and nobody argued with him about it.

Then a woman of forty-four said the thing about gates, and she said it because a man of forty-one was standing at the head of that road with his coat on and about nine people were standing behind him.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. She has said a list is a column five times, and that a thing with no person on either side of it is a thing that has not happened yet five times.

“*A gate is not a door. That is the whole of it and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked whether I have thought about it.*”

“*A door is a job with a name on it. A man of forty-one said that on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week and he was right and it took him about nine sentences. And a gate is two posts and a bar and any person in this county can lift it, and there is no name on a bar and there has never been a name on a bar.*”

“*And a thing with no person on either side of it is a thing that has not happened yet. I have said that five times since the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for the fifth. And I am not going to be asked whether the sixth is due.*”

About four people on that bank had the bar and about nine of them had the gate.

Then the man of forty-one said what he was going to do, and said it flat. It was the same shape as what he had said on the Sunday, and he was not going to dress it up, because there was a gate in front of him.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts. One of the two has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making in the last fortnight. He has been in and out of the ninth of nine doors twice a day for nineteen years.

“*One gate. This morning. I said it on the Sunday and I am not saying it a second time because a man of thirty-one on this bank has already taught me what a second time is for.*”

“*And I am going in it alone. That is not manners and it is not a thing anybody has asked me to do. It is that eight of the nine of you who can come have been asked once already this week and a person can only be asked so many times before the asking is the thing that happened.*”

“*And anybody who wants to stand at the top of that flight and be told what I am saying in there can stand there.*”

“*There is a man of fifty-four who would like about four of you to be at the top of it and not in the lane.*”

Then he went in. The gate was a bar between two posts about as thick as his own wrist, and the lane inside it was about as wide as a cart. There were about four houses in it and a stream at the bottom of it.

He was in there about three hours. He came out through the same bar and he came out the way he went in and he was not carrying anything.

And then a man of sixty-three who mends dry stone told him the thing, and he had not asked for it, and he asked for it to be said first that he had not asked for it.

The man is sixty-three and has mended dry stone for forty years, and has done it in this village since he was twenty-three. His hands know a wall the way a woman of forty-nine’s know a rack. Nobody has ever asked him about either.

“*You are going to tell me you have never heard of a door under a bank. I am going to say it.*”

“*And then I am going to say the other thing, and you are not to write the other thing down.*”

“*I have been in this village forty years and I have not heard of a door under a bank and I have said it before today and I would say it again.*”

“*And there is a wall four days on from here, where the road comes off the moor and turns down to a water. And there is a hole in that wall about as wide as my two hands together. It has been opened and shut again.*”

“*I know that because a wall that has been opened has mortar in the middle of the stones. And mortar in the middle of stones does not go out. It is not a drain hole and it is not a sheep hole. It is about a man’s height up.*”

“*I was taken to look at it once. I was about nine. And I have not been back and I am not going to go back, and I have never once said that to a person and I am saying it to a man standing in my lane.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said what he was not going to do with that, and he is the one who measures things and he gave it to the man of forty-one flat and in front of about nine people.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and has a lamp that is his.

“*Four days on. That is a figure of a road and not of a door, and I am not going to let the two of them be in the same line.*”

“*And a man of sixty-three has told you what his hands know and not what he has seen this week. There is a difference.*”

“*I have got nine feet of worn groove in me. That is a figure of about four hundred years of somebody going along and coming back, and there is no person in this county who could tell me whose it was.*”

“*And I am not going to walk four days to look at a hole in a wall. Not this week and not on a Tuesday, and there is a bank on this reach and I am on it.*”

Then the man of forty-one gave the count, the way he gives every count, and it was not a good week and he did not dress it.

“*Seven of the eight. Seven in nine days. Nine is six hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and eighty-two and that is a figure of a gap between two mornings and it is not a figure of how hard anybody worked.*”

“*And I have got one name out of the eight. It is a man who has been dead about thirty years.*”

“*And a man of sixty-three has given me a wall four days on with a hole in it. Those are two different things and I am not going to add them together.*”

“*And the seventh one shut a door on me. The fourth of them said they had never heard of a door under a bank, and this morning the eighth said it again. That is five out of eight.*”

“*And the week is over and I am not going to do a ninth. There are nine. The ninth of them is under a bank nine miles up this water and I am the one who does it.*”

“*I have been doing it for nineteen years and nobody ever told me there was a number on it.*”

Nobody on that bank improved on the nineteen years. Nobody thanked the man of forty-one for walking four days of his own week at somebody else’s asking, and about four of them had been up that road and about nine of them had not.

Then a man of thirty-one said the thing that cost him, which was that he cannot be sent for, and he said it as a fact about a form and not about himself.

“*There is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for about a wall four days on in a moor. There is no form that says a person may not.*”

“*And both of those are the same as about a door. A woman of forty-four has asked for both of those sentences to be left where they are. I am leaving them where they are.*”

“*And nobody on this bank can be sent for about the ninth of those doors either.*”

“*Not me, because there is no form. Not the man of forty-one, because there is no form.*”

“*And not a man of thirty-four in an ash, because he is in an ash and has been for sixty-one days and nobody has asked him anything.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and thirty-seven days. A hundred and thirty-seven is six hundred and ninety-one less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am not going to make a sentence out of that. And I am not going to say what it is a figure of.*”

“*Somebody on this bank will do it for me if I leave it about four seconds.*”

---

The fever was fifty-eight weeks and two days old. He had been in that county a hundred and thirty-seven days.

Seven of eight doors were asked in nine days and one name came out of the eight, and that name is a man who has been dead about thirty years. And a man of sixty-three who mends dry stone gave a wall four days on where there is a hole about the height of a man that has been opened and shut again, and he was nine when he was taken to look at it and has not been back.

And nobody on that bank can be sent for about any of it, and about nine people stood in the wind with a bank off all ninety steps under them and did not go down.
