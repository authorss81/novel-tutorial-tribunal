# Chapter 675: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Eighteenth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Bank Is Full And Nobody Goes Down, And An Ordinary Woman Who Keeps A Bakehouse Four Miles Up That Road Says The Word Out Loud For The First Time In This County And It Costs Her The Only Thing She Cannot Check

Fifth day of the hundred and eighteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-fifth morning. Two hundred and seventy-five days after the settlement. The fever fifty-six weeks. Two hundred and twenty-nine days since the division. One hundred and seventy-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full on the Saturday morning, and the man of forty-three said the reading out loud. It was the seventh morning running and he gave the figure that goes with it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Four days since it came off those steps, and it fills in about four days. That agrees with the pair of figures I have been giving since the Tuesday, and it does not always.*”

“*And nobody is going down today and there is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for about a day nobody can be sent for, and I am not going to make one to be able to go down on a Saturday.*”

About nine people on that bank stood about in the wind with nothing to do, which is a thing that has not happened on that bank in about eleven days.

Then a man of thirty-one said the count of his own out loud with the subtraction. He said it first thing, because he had decided on the Thursday that he would say it first thing on the Saturday, and a man of sixty-one had told him that a figure said second is a figure said late.

“*One hundred and twenty-one days in this county. One hundred and twenty-one is six hundred and seventy-five less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I have decided this morning that I am not going to say a number about anything else on this bank. I have one number left in me that I have not said and it is not about the bank and it is not about a person and I am going to keep it.*”

Nobody on that bank asked him what the number was.

Then a woman of forty-four said a word out loud on that bank, and she said it in the middle of an argument about a road, and about nine people heard it and about four of them wrote nothing down.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road. About nine houses in that village take her bread. She has been in that bakehouse nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything that anybody can be sent for about.

They were arguing about her carts, and about whether a man of forty-one was going to leave them at the head of that track on a morning he went down. She said the thing she had said on the Friday, and then she said the other thing.

“*You have all been talking about nine doors and nine jobs as though it were a house somebody built and left.*”

“*And it is not a house and nobody built it and there is nothing in it to be left. It is a rack.*”

“*I have a rack in my bakehouse. Six boards across and four shelves. I put sixty trays on it at about the eleventh hour at night, and I take sixty trays off it at about the fourth hour in the morning. The trays are wet and they must not touch one another or they go to slime. There is no nail and no glue and no heat in that rack. It is six boards and a gap.*”

“*And it works while somebody is putting them on and taking them off. It does not work on its own. It has never once worked on its own in nineteen years and it would not last a morning.*”

Then she said the word, and she said it once, and she said it in a plain voice, and it was the first time it had been said in that county by anybody who had not heard it off a man who heard it off a man.

“*It is a loom.*”

“*I have said that word in a bakehouse about a rack of boards for nineteen years. I have never once said it on a bank. I am not going to improve on the word after saying it. If any of you want a better word than that, you are going to have to go and find one, and I am not going to help you.*”

Then nothing came out of that bank for about four seconds, and the beam in the gallery was not in the room, and nothing whatever happened, and about nine people stood in the wind.

Then a man of sixty-six said one thing about it, and it is the only thing he said, and he did not say the word.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water. He is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about a word he said in a gallery in the last week. He got it off a man he dug with when he was about twenty-five, who was not of that county and is not going to be found.

“*That word is older than me. I am not going to say it again and I am not going to say where I got it, and I am not going to be the man who says it a second time on a bank.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to notice that a man of thirty-eight carried a lamp down about ninety steps four times last week and could have said it in the dark to about nine people and did not, because it was not his to say and he knew it.*”

Nobody on that bank asked the man of thirty-eight about the lamp.

Then the man of fifty-four said the part that was his, and he said it before anybody asked him, and it took him about nine sentences and he cannot read.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years and has said that he cannot read seven times.

“*And she cannot read either. She said it on the Wednesday of last week in front of about nine people, and she has not stopped saying it.*”

“*So that is two people on this bank who cannot read a word of what a thing is called. One of them said it out loud this morning. There is no form in this county that says a person may be asked what they said.*”

“*And I am not going to say what happens next, because I know exactly what happens next. It is that somebody with a hand and a book writes it down. There is one hand and one book in this county, and the man holding it is sixty-one, and he has told us twice this week what he will not write.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to have this: two people who cannot read are the only two people anybody can be sent for about in this county who can be sent for about what they said this morning, and neither of them will ever know what is written.*”

About nine people on that bank heard a man who cannot read do the arithmetic of his own condition out loud, and about four of them had not thought of it.

Then the man of thirty-one said the thing about the cost, and he said it in the first sentence, and the cost was not his.

“*A woman of forty-four who keeps sixty loaves and forty of them go up a road four miles off has said a word out loud in front of about nine people on a Saturday morning, and I am nobody’s and it has not gone to me.*”

“*And it has gone to her. She is four miles up a road from the only person in this county who can be sent for about a word. She is not a keeper of anything, and she has never been asked anything by anybody in her life that I can find out about.*”

“*And this is the second time in eight days a person has handed a name to a bank in that county. Both times it has gone to a person who was not asked and not a keeper. The first one was a woman of forty, who has been asking for a day against a name for about nine weeks. I said out loud on the Saturday of last week that it went to her by accident.*”

“*And this one did not go by accident. A woman of forty-four said a word about a rack of boards in a bakehouse, because I asked a question about a road. I am not going to be praised for noticing it. I am not going to call it a good thing either, and I am not going to improve on it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked the woman of forty-four and nobody said the word again.

Then a boy of thirteen said a figure out loud because he said one every morning now and it had become one of the things he did.

“*Twenty-two days. Six hundred and seventy-five less six hundred and fifty-three. And I have gone up that road three times since the Friday and one of them was with an empty barrow at about the fourth hour because it is what I do and nobody asked me.*”

And a man of forty-three said the time out loud and it was about the eleventh hour and the water was on the top step and would be over the second step by the second hour of the afternoon, and about nine people on that bank had nothing to do until the light went.

---

The fever was fifty-six weeks old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-one days.

The bank was full on the Saturday and nobody went down. An ordinary woman who keeps a bakehouse four miles up that road said the word out loud on that bank for the first time in that county, and gave the reason in six boards and a gap. A man of sixty-six did not say it again, and a man of thirty-eight did not say it in the dark.

And nothing whatever happened. A man of fifty-four who cannot read said out loud that two people on that bank can now be sent for about what they said. Neither of them will ever know what is written down.
