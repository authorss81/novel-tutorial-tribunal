# Chapter 663: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Sixteenth Week, And A Monday, And On Which The Work Of Getting Nine People To A Bank And Back Is Done By People Who Are Not Famous For It, And A Woman Of Thirty-Eight Is Asked What She Thinks She Is Keeping

Seventh and last day of the hundred and sixteenth week. His four hundred and thirteenth morning. Two hundred and sixty-three days after the settlement. The fever fifty-four weeks and two days. Two hundred and seventeen days since the division. One hundred and sixty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off the steps by the seventh hour on the Monday, all ninety of them clear to the top of the board. And about nine people were on that bank by the eighth hour. And the man of forty-three said the working out loud before anybody had asked him for it because two days before he had been asked for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and the block of limestone with a line cut round it is in that reach. And the water went over it on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and is still over it.

“*The stone went over on the Wednesday. That bank was on its first three steps on the Friday morning and it is off all ninety of them this morning. That is three days, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself. And it is three days of a bank emptying and not a figure of a river and not a figure of a year.*”

“*And I am going to say the other half of it because nobody else is going to. There is a thing being said in that yard and in the town nine miles down this water, that the next time those steps can be walked is the next time that block of limestone comes out. That is a figure of a river.*”

“*It is also true of a bank, and it is a figure of a bank, and the bank is three days and the bank has a person in it and the stone has nobody in it at all. Both of those are true and only one of them is about anybody. And I am a man of forty-three and I have watched a stone for nineteen years and I know which one I would rather be.*”

About four people on that bank had the three days. Nobody on that bank had a figure for the stone.

Then the people who got the nine to that bank were named, and every one of them was named by the doing and not by the wanting. And about nine of them had been on that bank when the list of nine jobs was read out on the Saturday and had not said a word about their own jobs.

The first of them was a man of forty-one who keeps two carts, and one of the two carts has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making on the way up. And he said what a road is, and he said it in about eight sentences, and the last one is the reason.

“*A road is not a thing anybody owns. A road is four miles and a barrow and whoever is going that way.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Kell that says a person may not walk on a road. And the only thing that happened to that on the Monday of the hundred and fourteenth week is that now nobody in four counties can be sent for about whether they go on it or not.*”

“*Before that Monday I could not have told you in which direction that went, and I have two carts and I have been on that track twice a day for longer than the carts have been on it.*”

“*And the last one is the reason, and the reason is that a road is a figure of carts and not of a rule. And I would like somebody to write that down. And I know what I have just said about writing things down so I am not going to ask anybody to.*”

Nobody in that bank thanked him for a road.

Then a boy of thirteen was counted out loud as one of the two who go and fetch. And the man of sixty-one counted him and said the reason he could be counted, which is that the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen.

The boy is thirteen. He has a hand that has ink on it and he is not a head in the book of heads and carts and there is no form in that county that says a person under sixteen may be paid for going up a road with a barrow.

“*Four miles there and four miles back with a barrow and her box on the end of it. And the four miles is about two hours. And that is the figure the man of sixty-one gave on the Thursday of the hundred and sixteenth week and I have walked it since I was nine.*”

“*And nobody has ever asked me to and nobody is going to, and I am not going to say the second part of that out loud twice.*”

About four people in that bank had the barrow. The rest of them had the four miles.

Then a man of sixty-six said what a wet flight is, and it was the third time anybody in that county had asked him and the third time he had answered. And the third time was the only one that had a new thing in it.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water and has been down those steps twice, the second time forty-one years ago when he was twenty-five. And he has not said yes or no to going down a third time and it is the Monday after he was asked.

“*A passage is somewhere a person can be. A wet flight is somewhere a person goes through. And there is nowhere on it to stand, and that is the whole of the difference and it took me forty-one years to get it.*”

“*And the nine steps under the water are not in the room at all. I have stood in two of them, once, in the second hour of a morning forty-one years ago. And I have never been in the other seven, and what is past the narrowing is not one of the seven, and if I am wrong about that on the Tuesday then I will be the one who is wrong and not the water.*”

“*And nobody knows what the other seven are for, including the man of thirty-one, who asked me how deep it goes on the Thursday of the hundred and fifteenth week and got four things about a floor. And he has not asked me since, and the four things I give everybody are the four I gave on the Monday of that same week in a gallery, and they are not the same four.*”

Nobody in that bank said a word about the other seven steps.

Then a woman of forty-nine came down that bank with a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well. And she said a thing out loud that she has said before in that county, and she said it the same way.

“*I do not know what a copy is and I have said so twice and I am going to keep saying so. And I have come down this road twice now and both times there is a man asking me questions I cannot answer.*”

“*And I am not saying that to be difficult. I am saying it because a woman of twenty-eight asked me on the first day whether I had ever seen a thing written out twice, and I had not. And I have not since. And about four of the people standing on this bank have written things down and I would like about nine of them to know that I cannot tell the difference between one of them and a fence post.*”

About four people in that bank had the fence post. The rest of them had a woman of forty-nine who came twice anyway.

Then a man of sixty-one asked a woman of thirty-eight what she thinks she is keeping. And he said out loud that she had said she did not want to be asked, and he asked her anyway, and about nine people in that bank heard him do it.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it and one of the eleven is her mother. And she has kept a chamber that is a week for about nine years and nobody has ever asked her what it is for.

“*You said on the Sunday of the hundred and fifteenth week that you did not want to be asked what it was for. And you gave a reason and the reason was that a woman who has kept a thing for about nine years and never been asked has one thing, and I have had that in my head for two days.*”

“*And I am asking you anyway, and I am saying that I am asking you anyway. And you may say no. And a no from you and not knowing are two different things and I have not got a way of telling them apart from out here and that is why I am asking in front of about nine people instead of in a doorway.*”

Then she answered, and it took her about eleven sentences, and it was worse for everybody in that bank than the not-knowing had been.

“*I am going to answer, and I want it said that I am answering and not that I have been asked, and those are the same and they are not.*”

“*I do not think I am keeping a week. I think I am keeping the fact that the peg has a hole for it.*”

“*That is the whole of the answer and here is why it is worse than not knowing. If I am keeping the difference between a day and a week, then the difference is kept by me. It is not in a book. It is not in the stone. It is not in a hand that anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*And in about four years, if I stop, the peg goes into a plain hole on the seventh morning like it goes into a plain hole on the other six. And then a chamber that is a week is a chamber with a wheel in it. And there is no way that anybody in four counties will ever know that it was a week, and the not-knowing will be the same not-knowing it was about four hundred years ago.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to notice what I have just said, which is that the not-knowing had a wheel in it and this has me in it. And I have gone from a thing to a person in about nine years. And a person is a much worse thing to keep a thing with than a wheel is.*”

About four people in that bank had the whole of it. Nobody in that bank said a word about it. And nobody improved on it. And about nine people in that bank knew what the eighth job was and about four of them had been on the Saturday when a man of thirty-one read it out loud and said it could not be filled.

Then the man of thirty-one said what he wanted, and what he wanted was to be able to write nine more lines in a book and put one more name on the end of them. And the man of sixty-one told him the price of it in about seven sentences.

“*I have four lines in that book on the leaf after the last head, and the fifth line has to be a name. And there is a woman of thirty-eight standing on this bank who is the reason there is a fifth line, and you know that, and I have watched you know it since the fourth hour.*”

“*And I am not going to refuse you it, and I am not going to do it either, and I am going to say why. And the why is that a woman of forty-four said on the Sunday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a list is a column and that a column in this county is a thing people get put in inside about four years, and she is right and she has been right every time she has said it.*”

“*So the fifth line does not get written, and it does not get refused. And it is going to sit on that leaf as a thing a man of sixty-one did not do on a Monday morning. And in about four years one of us is going to have to say out loud what that cost.*”

Then the two people who had been in a doorway and in a reed were counted out loud again. Because a man of sixty-one has said that a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place, and both of them are in a place.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week. And that is thirty-three days, and thirty-three is six hundred and sixty-three less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in the reed on the other side of that landing since about the second hour of the day before he lay down. And that is thirty-four days, and thirty-four is six hundred and sixty-three less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*And I am not going to add them. One is on the low side and one is on the other side and they have not stood up and there is no figure of the two of them and anybody who wants one can go and count. And I have counted, and it is two, and that is a figure of two people and not of a county.*”

Nobody in that bank walked over to the ash that day. Nobody in that bank walked over to the reed either.

---

The fever was fifty-four weeks and two days old. He had been in that county one hundred and nine days.

A man of forty-three said out loud that the water in a bank is a figure of a bank and the water over a stone is a figure of a river. And that the bank empties in about three days and has a person in it, and that the stone has nobody in it at all.

And the people who got nine people to a bank and back were named for the doing: a man of forty-one with two carts who said what a road is after abolition. And a boy of thirteen who is not a head in a book and has walked four miles with a barrow since he was nine.

And a woman of thirty-eight was asked what she thinks she is keeping, having said for about nine years that she did not want to be asked. And she said that she is not keeping a week, she is keeping the fact that the peg has a hole for it, and that the not-knowing had a wheel in it and the knowing has her in it.

And a man of sixty-one did not cut a fifth line in the back of the last leaf that night, and a boy of thirteen is not a head in that book either, and about eleven people in four houses are not a line in either book, and one of the eleven is a woman of thirty-eight.
