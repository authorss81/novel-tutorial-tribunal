# Chapter 664: The First Day Of The Hundred And Seventeenth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Board About Four Feet By Two Feet Is Lifted By Two People Nobody Could Name On Friday, And The Bottom Nine Steps Are Under About Four Feet Of Water And Are Bailed Again By A Man Who Is Not Thanked For It

First day of the hundred and seventeenth week. His four hundred and fourteenth morning. Two hundred and sixty-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-four weeks and three days. Two hundred and eighteen days since the division. One hundred and sixty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The board had to be off its stick before anybody could go down, and it takes two people to lift it and two people to put it back. And there is no form in the county of Kell that says who either of those is.

That was said out loud at about the sixth hour by a man of sixty-one who had been up since the fourth hour. And about nine people were on that bank and about nine of them had been in a list of nine jobs on the Saturday that nobody wrote down.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years, and he has four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and he is not writing a fifth.

“*Two people lift it. That is the rule and it is not a rule, it is a board about four feet by two feet and water under nine steps.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says which two, and I am not going to make one. And I am going to say what I am going to do about it instead, and what I am going to do about it is nothing.*”

“*I am going to stand on this bank at the eighth hour. And if two people have not come then the nine of you are nine people standing on a bank. And a man of forty-three will have told me at about the seventh hour, because a boy of thirteen went for them at the fifth.*”

Then the boy came back at about the seventh hour with two people, and he had been four miles and back once already that morning. And the man of sixty-one said out loud that the finder was not the one who went.

The boy is thirteen. He is not a head in the book of heads and carts. Because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen, and he has walked four miles with a barrow since he was nine.

“*I went up the track for the man of forty-one at about the fifth hour and he was at the mill road and he said yes before I had finished the question.*”

“*And then I went on up the track for the man of thirty-eight, and he was at the boats. And he asked me three questions about the water on the steps and I answered all three and one of them was that it was off them. And then he said he would come and put his coat down and did not say thank you.*”

“*And nobody sent me. There is no form in this county that says a person under sixteen may be sent for, so I was not sent. And I am not going down there, and nobody asked me to and nobody told me not to.*”

About four people on that bank had the three questions. The rest of them had a boy of thirteen who had done two things and was not going to do the third.

Then the two lifted the board, and it took them about the length of a man of forty-one counting, and the steps drained where the water came off them, and the first two of the nine went down.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he went down with a lamp that was his.

“*Nine steps and about four feet and I have done it twice in about nine days and a pail takes three goes to a step and nine steps is about twenty-seven goes. And a man of thirty-eight wants about nine minutes a go, and that is the working and it comes to about four hours and a quarter.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for it again and I have said that twice and I am going to say it a third time and then I am going to stop saying it, which is a different thing from stopping doing it.*”

Then the man of forty-one said he would do the bailing, and nobody had asked him. And he gave the reason in about five sentences and the reason was that it was the same work as a wet load and he had been doing it for nineteen years.

“*I have shifted a wet cart off a road about nine times a week since I was about twenty-two and I know what four hours of standing in water does to a man and I know it takes a man about nine minutes a go and not eight, because the eighth one is where you start being slow.*”

“*I am going down because it is my work and because a man of thirty-eight has bailed it twice and a man of thirty-eight has a child of nine. And I am not going to say anything about that last part in a passage.*”

“*And if I am slow at the end I am to be told and not waited for. And there is no form in this county that says a man may be told to hurry, so somebody is going to have to do it out loud.*”

About nine people in that passage heard the bailing start. About four of them stayed on the bank with the lamp turned away from the hole.

Then the nine went down, and the man of thirty-one went down fifth with his hands and nothing else in them. And the bottom nine steps had about four feet of water in them as they have always had. And the man of forty-three said that out loud from the top of the flight and nobody contradicted him.

The passage is about as tall as a man and about as wide as two men for about nine feet, and then it narrows. And there is water in the narrowing. And the man of thirty-one said the whole of a thing about the narrowing being a place and not a room and then did not say the other half of it.

Then the man of sixty-six went past the narrowing, and he was the first person to have done that in forty-one years. And he stood in the water on the other side of it for about as long as it takes a man of thirty-eight to pole a quarter mile, and nobody asked him anything at all.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water and has been down those steps twice, the second time forty-one years ago when he was twenty-five. And on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week he was asked to go down a third time and said neither yes nor no.

“*I said in the dark on Monday of last week that I was coming on the Tuesday, and the reason was a narrowing and not a question. And I am here and that is the whole of what I came for.*”

“*It is narrower than it was. It is about as wide as a man and it is about as tall as a man and the water is up to the knee of a man of thirty-eight and the water is up to the middle of a man of sixty-six. And I have not been in it in forty-one years and I am not going to say anything about how that feels in a passage with nine people in it.*”

About four people in that passage had the forty-one years. Nobody in that passage had the rest of it.

Then the gallery opened out about nine feet past the narrowing, and it is about nine feet high and about as wide as a room with a hearth in it. And there are about nine beams in the roof of it set about four feet apart, and eight of them are still.

The ninth one went along about two inches and came back, and there was no person in the room, and no cord on it. And no draught that nine people can feel. And about nine people in that gallery watched it and four of them said the word out loud before anybody had asked them anything.

“*That is a machine and it is worked by the water under the steps and I have not found the hole.*”

“*Somebody built it, and a hand is cut in a lintel about ninety steps back. And there is a guild mark on that hand, and I have heard the guild from a man who heard it from a man.*”

About four people in that gallery had said the word machine on the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week. They had it on the Tuesday as well, and nobody had changed their mind and nobody had told them to.

Then the lamp guttered, and about four people in that gallery could not see anything. And the man of thirty-one was led out by a hand on the coat by a man of fifty-four who said he could not read and had said so before anybody asked him.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years. And he has said in about nine days that he cannot read. And he said it in the dark with a hand on somebody else’s coat.

“*I cannot read and I have said so five times in about nine days and I am saying it now because I have got hold of a coat in the dark and a coat is a thing you can be sent for about.*”

“*And I have not asked that man a single question this morning and I am not going to now. And I have said so on a bank twice already and I am saying it under about ninety steps of stone as well because it is the same sentence and it does not stop at the water.*”

Nobody in that passage said a word to him about the coat.

Then the day was done in the chamber of the day by two people, and the stone went about nine feet along a groove, and the light came onto it. And then it went back about nine feet and the chamber went dark. And it took about a minute each way, and about four people in that chamber had the minute and the rest of them had the dark.

And the wheel in the chamber that is a week was not turned, because the turn for that day had been done. And a man of thirty-eight said the reason in about four sentences and the reason was about a day and not about a wheel.

“*That turn is a day and that day has been had. It is the fourth hour of the morning and there is nobody in this room who has had one today and I am not going to give one of you the second turn of a wheel on a Tuesday because we are all standing here.*”

“*I did it in a boat and I did it about nine minutes at a time for seven Thursdays and I will tell you what the second one is. The second one is a memory of the first one and a memory does not turn a wheel.*”

“*And that is two, and the other two are that a day does not come round again and that the peg is in a plain hole until tomorrow morning, and I would like about four of you to have the whole of that before anybody says I am refusing anybody anything.*”

Nobody in that chamber said a word about it.

Then they came up, and the man of thirty-eight was first, and the man of thirty-one was fifth. And the man of forty-one came up last and had been in about four feet of water for about four hours and a quarter. And the board went back on its stick by two people because that is what it takes.

About nine people on that bank counted the nine who came up, and the man of sixty-one said the count out loud and said what it was a figure of. And it was nine and it was a figure of one Tuesday on one bank and it was not a figure of anybody’s opinion.

Nobody on that bank thanked the man of forty-one for four hours and a quarter in four feet of water. He said before he was thanked. And he said it to the boy of thirteen. And what he said was that he had been slow at the end and that somebody should have told him to hurry and that nobody had.

---

The fever was fifty-four weeks and three days old. He had been in that county one hundred and ten days.

A board about four feet by two feet was lifted by two people whom nobody could name on Friday and who were found at about the seventh hour on the Tuesday by a boy of thirteen who had already been four miles and back once that morning. And there is no form in the county of Kell that says who either of those is.

About nine people went down about ninety steps and the bottom nine of them were under about four feet of water, as they have always been. And a man of forty-one bailed them for about four hours and a quarter, and before anybody had got near him he said that he had been slow at the end and that somebody should have told him to hurry and that nobody had.

And a man of sixty-six went past a narrowing he had not been in for forty-one years and did not say a word about how that was. And nine people watched a beam go along about two inches and come back, and about four of them were still sure it was a machine.
