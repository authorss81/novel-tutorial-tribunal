# Chapter 689: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which A Bank Is Full And Nobody Goes Down, And Six Nines And One Eight Are Counted Out Loud In A Room And Are Not Added, And A Man Of Sixty-One Puts A Number In His Book And Says Out Loud What He Will Not Write Beside It

Fifth day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and thirty-ninth morning. Two hundred and eighty-nine days after the settlement. The fever fifty-eight weeks. Two hundred and forty-three days since the division. One hundred and eighty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped. And then he said the other thing, which was about tomorrow.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has given a reading every morning this week and stopped after it every morning this week, and the block of limestone with a line cut round it is nine miles up this water and there is still no figure for it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Four days since it came off those steps and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And tomorrow is the first day of a week and I am not going to give anybody a figure about tomorrow morning to make a week go quicker. And there is a man of thirty-one on that bank who would like it.*”

About nine people on that bank stood about with a full bank between them and whatever they had come for. And a man of forty-three told them not to hurry.

Then a man of sixty-one said what he was going to do that morning, before anybody had asked him. And he said the reason first, and the reason was about his own book and not about a person.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. And there are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five, and the fifth line has been empty since the day he took the book.

“*I am going to write a number in this book this morning. I wrote a line in it eighteen days ago about a figure a man of forty-three had got wrong by a day. And that was a line and not a number.*”

“*And I have not written a number in this book since I took it, in eleven years, and I am going to write one now because a man of thirty-one asked me on the last Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week to put a thing in it and I said no to three parts of it and gave him three reasons.*”

“*And I told this bank on the Friday of this week that I was going to write a number before the week was over. And it is a Saturday, and I would have preferred a Tuesday and there was not one.*”

The eighteen days went round that bank, and the book went with everything else on it.

Then he gave the count out loud with the subtraction, and then he read out what had to be counted and in what order. And about nine people on that bank did the counting one at a time.

“*He has been in this county a hundred and thirty-five days. A hundred and thirty-five is six hundred and eighty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am going to count the nines out loud one at a time and I am going to say what each one is before I say it, and I am not going to add them, and I would like somebody to tell me afterwards which one of them was which.*”

Then they counted, and it took about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet. And every one of the six was said by a different mouth.

“*Nine openings in a wall about a hundred and thirty feet past the last of about nine beams.* That is a count of doors and it is counted twice by two men walking opposite ways and neither of them has a hand that can write it down.”

“*Nine jobs cut over those nine openings. Eight of them two hands and one of them one hand. That is a count of work and it is not a count of people and I would like those two kept apart.*”

“*Nine marks, and the same cut in all nine of them, with no bar under it and no numeral on it. That is a count of marks and a man of sixty-six has said that a mark with no numeral on it is a mark a person puts on a thing they have to come back to.*”

“*Nine beams in the roof of a gallery about as wide as a room with a hearth in it. And eight of them still, and one that goes along about two inches. That is a count of beams and it is not a count of beams that somebody is holding.*”

“*Nine lines cut in a stone about the size of two hands laid flat, in nine hands that cannot write. And the ninth was cut on the Thursday of the hundred and eighteenth week. That is a count of lines and not one word of it has been read by anybody in four counties.*”

“*Nine words in the heads of about nine people on this bank. I am not going to say them. I said that on the Monday of the hundred and seventeenth week. And a woman of twenty-eight told me about that at the time, and it has not moved since.*”

Six nines went into the air on that bank and nobody added them up and nobody was given the answer, and about four of them had a seventh as well.

Then the man of thirty-one gave the seventh, and it was the only one of the seven that was not a nine. And he gave it flat and gave the subtraction with it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and he offered three times in about two weeks to be the thing that is sent for and was refused three times out loud by a man who cannot see well.

“*Eight. That is what the nine is and it has been eight for eighteen days, and eighteen is six hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and seventy-one, and nobody on that bank has gone looking for a ninth in about eighteen days, and I am one of the nine and I am nobody’s.*”

“*And the name is still the nine. I have been the ninth of that nine for eighteen days and I am nobody’s, and there is no form in this county that says I am.*”

Then the man of sixty-one added them up, out loud, and said what the sum was a figure of. And it was not a figure of anything that is nine.

“*Six nines and one eight. That is seven figures. A six nines is fifty-four and an eight is eight, and I have given you both of those and I am not going to give you the third, because there is nothing in that county to add them to and because a man of sixty-one who gives you the answer of a sum like that is a man of sixty-one who has started putting figures in books.*”

“*Six is a figure of how many times the word nine has been said in a room this morning. And one is a figure of how many times it has been said and been a different number, and that is all that either of them is.*”

“*And about four of you have just waited for me to say the total and I am not going to say it, and I am not going to be asked a second time today, and I would like it noticed that a man of thirty-one is the one who taught this bank to ask what a figure is a figure of.*”

Nobody on that bank got a total, and nobody has been given one, and about four of them understood why and the rest of them did not ask.

Then the man of forty-one said what the week of asking has come to. And he gave the count, and it was seven, and it was the only count that morning that came out of a mouth somebody had asked.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and asked a woman of forty-four one question on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week and has walked on foot about seven mornings this week.

“*Seven times somebody has opened a door to me this week. Six doors and one bakehouse, and one at the bakehouse was not about the bread.*”

“*And I have got one name out of the seven and the name is a man who has been dead about thirty years, and he is the only thing I have got, and I am not going to put a dead man’s name in a man of sixty-one’s book because a woman of forty-four said a list is a column.*”

“*And I am going to do the eighth on Monday and it is the one in a village I have never been inside a gate of. And I would like the seven and not the week.*”

The seven went round that bank, and so did the eighth one to about four of them.

Then the man of sixty-one wrote it, and he wrote it on the leaf after the last head. And there were four lines on that leaf and now there were five.

“*Nine. That is the figure of a room this morning and not a figure of a person. And it is the number of terms and the number of jobs and the number of places where a name would go and is not, and those are three different nines and I have written one.*”

“*And I am not going to write nine people. There are eight of you and there has been eight for eighteen days. And a book with a nine in it and eight names against it is a list of what is missing.*”

“*And a woman of forty-four said on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a list is a column and a column is a thing people get put in inside about four years, and I have watched that be right more times than I have got a figure for and I am not going to be shown a better reason on a Saturday.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about the number and nobody thanked him for it and nobody said it was a good number to have in a book.

Then a woman of forty-nine said the thing that made the count stop. And she said it before anybody asked her for hers, and she counts loaves and does not know what a copy is.

“*I have got a nine of my own and I am not going to say it out loud in a bank.*”

“*It is a figure of my own work and not of anything that is nine, and there are about nine of you on this bank who could put a number on it and make it into one of the seven, and I am not going to hand mine over to be the seventh.*”

“*And I would like it said that I have been asked for it and have said no, and that nobody has asked me twice, and that a person who cannot be asked is not the same as a person who has said no. I know that and I would like somebody here to know that I know it.*”

About four people on that bank had the ninth that was not said and about nine of them had six that were.

Then about nine people stood about on a bank with a full bank on it. And the light went, and the two who lift that board were not needed this morning and had not been needed since the Wednesday of this week.

---

The fever was fifty-eight weeks old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty-five days.

Six nines and one eight were counted out loud in a room on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week, one at a time, each with a different mouth, and a man of sixty-one added them to nothing, gave two of the three figures in the working and refused the third, and said that six is a figure of how many times the word nine was said in a room that morning and not a figure of anything that is nine.

And he wrote one number in his book, on the leaf after the last head. And there are five lines on that leaf now, and the last of the five is the number nine.

And he said out loud that he is not going to write nine people next to it, because there are eight of them and a book with a nine in it and eight names against it is a list of what is missing, and he would like about four people to be able to tell the difference between a book of what is and a book of what is not.
