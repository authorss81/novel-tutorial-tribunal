# Chapter 685: First Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which The Water Comes Off The Top Step For The Second Time In Four Days And Nine Go Down Again, And A Man Of Sixty-Six Says What A Filled Hole Is, And A Man Of Thirty-One Puts His Hand On A Going Where No Going Has Gone And Comes Out On The Other Side Of It With A Scar That Is About As Long As A Groove In A Wall

First day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Two hundred and eighty-five days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks and three days. Two hundred and thirty-nine days since the division. One hundred and eighty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps by the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading, and he said that he had watched it come off for the second time in four days and that he had now said twice in about a week that he has nothing to say about a night.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has the block of limestone with a line cut round it in that reach nine miles up this water, and two crossings of it in his own hand out of nineteen years and no figure for the gap.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off the top step in the night for the second time in four days and I was on that landing at the second hour again, because there is nothing else to do at the second hour.*”

“*And I am not going to say what a night is. I said on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week that I had no figure for a night, and I gave that answer twice in the nineteen years I have kept that landing, and I have nothing new. What I am going to do is give the reading every morning and stop. And if a man of thirty-one wants a figure about a night he can have the reading.*”

“*And the block of limestone is a different thing nine miles up this water and there is still no figure in four counties for when it comes out. And that has not moved in nine days either.*”

Then the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it. And the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his and said out loud that his shoulder was no good on a Thursday and that this was not a Thursday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and held his own lamp up on his arm at the height of a beam for about a quarter of an hour on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week and had to put it down about four times to get his shoulder back in.

“*It is not a Thursday and I am going down. If it is a Thursday I am not going down and I told this bank that on the Tuesday of last week and about four of you did not believe me until this morning came round.*”

About four people on that bank had believed him and about nine of them had not.

They went past the last of the beams, and the ninth one went along about two inches as the man of thirty-eight came down onto the last of the steps, and it did not go at all while about nine people stood in that gallery looking at it, and nobody said anything about that then or afterwards. Then about nine feet past the place where the gravel is walked. And along about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage at the same fall, and up the step.

And in the wall there were nine openings with two hands cut over eight of them and one hand over the ninth.

Then they went into the ninth, and there was a lamp on the hook cut in the roof of it and it was burning. And nobody in four counties lit it, and nobody in four counties put it out.

And the man of sixty-six went to the back of that room and put his hand flat on the wall behind the shelf. And then took it off again, and said the thing twice because it was the first thing he had not recognised.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water and has been down under about ninety steps of bank twice in forty-one years and is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about a word he heard in a gallery.

“*That is not a wall. That is a hole that has been filled, and a sinker knows the difference in the dark. And that is not the first time this fortnight that I have said a thing twice.*”

“*It is a course of stone put into a gap and then faced. The colour of it is wrong and the joint is wrong, and the facing is about the thickness of my two fingers. And the stone behind the facing is not the stone of that wall and I have never in forty-one years seen the two stones together anywhere.*”

“*And I am not going to say when it was filled or by whom, and I am not going to say who put the nine cuts in a stone a foot away from it, and a man of sixty-six has just refused a question nobody asked him, and he would not have refused it twice.*”

Then a woman of forty-nine said the name for it, and she said it before anybody had asked her for one. And she said where the word came from and that she had it for nineteen years, and that it was not about a road.

The woman is forty-nine and has come down that cart road four times and counts loaves because she counts days. And she does not know what a copy is and she has said so in front of about nine people.

“*I have got a word for it and I have had it for nineteen years and it has never once been about a road. In a bakehouse a rack is six boards and four shelves, and about half the time one of the shelves has nothing on it at all. And we call that one the blank.*”

“*That is all it means. It means a shelf with nothing on it that is still there for the next time. I have four shelves and about one blank at any moment of the working day. And I would not be without it, and I would not call anything else that.*”

Then the man of forty-one said what it was, and he said it in eight sentences. And it took him a while and he did not stop in the middle of it.

The man is forty-one and has been in and out of the ninth of nine doors twice a day for nineteen years and has a load of stone he pushes nine feet at a time along a groove in a gallery about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank.

“*A groove is a route. A route is a going and a coming. That nine-foot groove in the gallery is a route and it is about four hundred years old and the only reason anybody knows it is a route is that a stone has gone along it and come back every day of that time.*”

“*A going that does not come back is not a route. And nobody in this county has a word for one. There is not one in a guild either, and there is not one in a book, and the man of sixty-six has told us what a man who digs for water would say about a hole that has been filled, and that is the closest to a name any of us is going to get down here.*”

“*It is a blank route. And the name came off a woman of forty-nine and off a shelf in a bakehouse, and I am going to say that in front of about nine people because that is where it came from and not from me, and I would like it very much recorded that it came from a woman who does not know what a copy is.*”

About four people in that room had the shelf and about nine of them had the going.

Then a man of thirty-one put his hand on it, and he said out loud what he was not going to do first. And the reason, and the reason was about a man standing on a bank about eight days ago.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and he went down fifth with his hands and nothing else in them on every descent of the last three weeks.

“*I am not going to say a word to it. On the Monday of the hundred and eighteenth week a man who cannot see well stood on a bank and said a name to a stone and nothing whatever happened, and he asked for it very much recorded that a man who cannot see well worked it out standing on a bank and not in the room.*”

“*I am not going to be the second man to say a word to a wall, and I am not going to be the man who goes into a thing to see what it does about being spoken to, and there is a man of about fifty-two on that bank who would like about four of you to have the count of the two of us.*”

Then he put his hand on the facing and it was not stone.

It went about the length of two men and then it was not a room. There was no floor to speak of and there was no second wall either, and it was dark the whole way and he had nothing in his hands but his own two hands and he went through it in about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet.

And he came out in the ninth room, about nine feet from where he had put his hand in. And the lamp was where the man of thirty-eight had left it and the man of thirty-eight was where he had been.

And there was a cut across his right palm, from the base of the thumb to about the first joint, and it was cold and it was not bleeding much, and he held the hand up in front of the lamp and looked at it and said nothing at all about it for about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet.

Then the man of thirty-eight measured it, and he said what he measured it against, and he said the half of it that stops anybody rounding it.

“*Hold it flat. I am going to put my own hand on the groove above that shelf and then I am going to put my own hand on yours, and I have cut wicks for nineteen years and I have measured things against other things for nineteen years and I am not going to give you a number that came out of my eye.*”

“*It is about as long as that groove. About the same, and I am not going to round it and I am not going to say it is exactly that, because the groove has a notch in the end of it and there is no notch in yours.*”

“*And that is a figure of a length in a hand and not of anything else. It says nothing about a door. It says nothing about a passage. And it says nothing at all about what it cost, because there is no book in four counties that a man can look in and check what that cost.*”

About nine people in that room looked at the hand and about four of them had not known there was a hand until the man of thirty-eight asked to measure it.

Then the man of thirty-one said what it was and what it was not. And he said it in his own mouth and nobody else in that room said anything about it at all.

“*It is not a burn. It is not a cut I have seen anybody make. There is no word for it in this county and I am not going to go and find one.*”

“*And nobody is to say that it will keep, or that it will not keep, or that it is not much. There is no figure for any of those three and about four of you have been about to give me one.*”

About nine people in that room did not say a word to him about the hand. And nobody said sorry, and about four people in that room did not look at it again.

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes. And the boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and did not go down.

And the man of thirty-one gave the number he has got, and he gave it with the subtraction and he said the other thing about it in the same breath. And the other thing about it was what he was not going to say.

“*One hundred and thirty-one days in this county. One hundred and thirty-one is six hundred and eighty-five less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am not going to tell anybody in this bank that I got what I went in for, and I am not going to say it was worth the hand, and I did not say either of those two things even once in a passage in the dark where nobody would have heard.*”

---

The fever was fifty-seven weeks and three days old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty-one days.

There is a going behind the ninth opening in the ninth room where a hole was filled with stone of the wrong colour and faced about two fingers thick. And nobody has been past it before this morning because nobody knew it was there.

A man of thirty-one went through it in about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet and came out on the other side of it in the same room. There is a cut across his right palm from the base of the thumb to about the first joint. And a man of thirty-eight measured it against a groove in a wall and said it is about as long and would not round it.

And that is a figure of a length in a hand and not of anything else, because there is no book in four counties a man can look in to check what that cost.
