# Chapter 684: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Bank Is Full And About Nine People Stand On It And Do Nothing On Purpose, And A Man Of Sixty-One Is Asked To Put A Thing In A Book And Says Out Loud What He Will Not Write

Seventh and last day of the hundred and nineteenth week. His four hundred and thirty-fourth morning. Two hundred and eighty-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks and two days. Two hundred and thirty-eight days since the division. One hundred and eighty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped. And he stopped on purpose and said so.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and gave the pair of figures on a great many mornings running and stopped saying them on the Thursday of this week.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Two days since it came off those steps, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And that is all I am going to say. That is the fourth morning I have given the reading and stopped and I am going to keep stopping, and if anybody on this bank wants the pair of figures they have had them out loud on more mornings than any of us has counted, and they are in about nine heads and there is nowhere else they are.*”

About nine people on that bank stood on it and did nothing, and it was a Monday, and about four of them knew that was what they were doing.

Then the man of forty-one said the difference out loud, because he had said the first half of it on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighteenth week and had been quoted back to himself ever since.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts, and he has been in and out of the ninth of nine doors twice a day for nineteen years, and he said on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighteenth week that what has to be done on a bank on a Monday by people who are alive is nothing at all.

“*On the Tuesday of the hundred and eighteenth week I said that about nine people stood on a bank on the Monday and nobody walked down anything. And I said that about four of us did not know that was what we were doing.*”

“*It is a Monday and we are doing it again and this time about four of us know and the rest of us have decided to do it anyway, and that is a different thing and you can tell the two apart if you want to.*”

“*And I have got no idea what a bank is for on a Monday and I am not going to find out today, and that is not modesty. I have got two carts and a load of stone and that is my week.*”

The difference went round that bank. The rest of them were standing in the wind and had been since the sixth hour.

Then about nine people on that bank said what their Monday was. And nobody had asked them, and it took about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and nobody improved on anybody.

A man of forty-one keeps two carts. A woman of forty-four keeps a bakehouse four miles up a cart road. A woman of thirty-eight keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is her mother, and she has kept a chamber that is a week for about nine years and nobody has ever asked her what it is for.

“*Two carts and a load of stone that goes up a track, and about ten hours in ten days of a cart.*” That is the man of forty-one.

“*Sixty loaves and about forty of them up a road four miles off, and about nine houses and about four of the nine.*” That is the woman of forty-four.

“*About nine hours of a mill, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is my mother.*” That is the woman of thirty-eight.

“*A reading and a stoppage, and I have not given the pair of figures since the Thursday and I am not going to start them on a Monday.*” That is the man of forty-three.

“*About nine hundred heads and carts against about as many, and I did not put a line in it this morning, and I am going to tell this bank why in about four sentences.*” That is the man of sixty-one.

“*A drain and about nine feet of gravel, and I have held it for nine years and nobody has ever asked me what it is for.*” That is the man of fifty-four.

And about nine people on that bank said their Monday and about four of them had not thought of it. And the six of them are not the same size as each other and were never going to be.

Then a man of thirty-one asked a man of sixty-one to put a thing in the book, and he said out loud that he was asking and not telling, and a woman of forty-four stopped him once before he got to the end of it and then let him go on.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. And there are nine of those chairs and not ten.

“*Ask him once and let him answer. You have said the shape of it twice this week and both of them were to me and neither of them was to him.*”

“*And I am not going to stop you a third time, because a woman of forty-four who stops everything is a woman of forty-four who is doing it because she likes it. And I would rather be wrong about that than be right about it.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything to her about that, and she had not been thanked and she did not say anything about being thanked.

Then the man of thirty-one asked, and he asked it flat and he said what he wanted and did not dress it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and he offered three times in about two weeks to be the thing that is sent for and was refused three times out loud by a man who cannot see well.

“*Put this in the book. That there are nine marks cut over nine openings in a wall about a hundred and thirty feet past the last of about nine beams, and that there is a space cut square in the middle of every one of them about as wide as a thumb, and that nothing is in any of them.*”

“*And I am asking and not telling, and I am asking because a man of fifty-four who cannot read kept a fourth name out of a list on the Wednesday of the hundred and eighteenth week and I would like one thing on that bank in a book.*”

Then the man of sixty-one said what he would not write, and he said it before anybody had asked him what he would write. And he said why in the same breath.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. And there are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five, and one of those four lines says a figure was wrong by a day.

“*I am not going to write it, and I am going to tell you all of the reasons so that you cannot say afterwards that I refused you in a small voice.*”

“*One. I have not been down that flight in eleven years and I am not going to be sent for about a wall. And I am not going to write down what a man told me about a wall I have not seen.*”

“*Two. There is no name in it. A line with no name against it is not a thing my book does. And my book has got about nine hundred heads in it and every one of them has a head against it.*”

“*Three, and this is the one to hold on to. The day I start writing down things I have been told about in a place I have not been, my book stops being a book of what happened and starts being a book of what somebody said happened, and I have not got eleven years left to find out which of those two I have been keeping.*”

The three reasons went round that bank and so did the refusal.

Then a man of fifty-four said the thing about himself that nobody had asked him for. And he cannot read, and it was the longest thing he has said since he said the word about a rack of boards.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has said that he cannot read a great many times and has stopped a man of thirty-one five times in five days.

“*You are all talking about that book as though the writing in it settles a thing. It does not settle a thing for me, because I cannot read a word of it and neither can anybody else who is stood where I am stood.*”

“*That is the whole of what I am. Every one of you can be shown what was written and you can go and look at the wall and see whether it is true. I cannot be shown anything. If a man of sixty-one writes nine spaces in that book this afternoon then I am the only person on this bank who can be sent for about what he wrote. And I will not know what it says, and neither will anybody else who comes after me.*”

“*And I am not going to be a keeper of it. I have said that about a man who cannot see well’s finding and I am saying it about myself and it is the same sentence and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I would like the count of me stopping a man of thirty-one instead, which is five in five days and it stopped because he stopped.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him and nobody said the word brave and nobody said the word sorry.

Then the man of sixty-one gave the count out loud with the subtraction, and then he said the other thing, which was about tomorrow.

“*He has been in this county one hundred and thirty days. One hundred and thirty is six hundred and eighty-four less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And tomorrow is the first day of a week and I am not going to start anything on a Sunday night and I am not going to start anything on a Monday either, and a man of sixty-one does not start things on Mondays. That is not a form and it is not a rule, and it is a man and not a rule.*”

And the wind came up the reach and about nine people went on standing about. And the two carts did not go up that track, and the water was sixteen inches over the top step and going.

---

The fever was fifty-seven weeks and two days old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty days.

There is a book on that bank with eleven years of about nine hundred heads in it and carts against about as many, and on the Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week a man of sixty-one gave three reasons out loud for not writing down a wall he has not been down to, and he wrote none of them, and the third of the three was that his book is a book of what happened.

And there is a man of fifty-four who cannot read, and he said it himself this morning: if a book is written down here he is the only person on that bank anybody can be sent for about, and he will not know what it says, and neither will anybody who comes after him.
