# Chapter 677: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Eighteenth Week, And A Monday, And On Which They Find A Lamp Burning On A Hook In A Room Nobody Lit And A Stone With Nine Lines On It In Nine Different Hands And Nobody Has Ever Read, And The Thing Is Said Out Loud On A Bank By A Man Who Is Not A Keeper And Is Nobody’s

Seventh and last day of the hundred and eighteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-seventh morning. Two hundred and seventy-seven days after the settlement. The fever fifty-six weeks and two days. Two hundred and thirty-one days since the division. One hundred and seventy-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was at about nine inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the working and then gave the half of it that nobody wants.

“*About nine inches. Six days since it came off and it fills in about four days and it is going to be full again by the middle of the week and that is the pair of figures and I am not going to improve them.*”

“*And I have been asked six times in about nine days what happens in a bank like this between the fourth day and the full, and the answer is nothing, and nothing is the answer, and I have no figure for how long a nothing is.*”

Then the man of sixty-one did two counts on that bank before anybody had thought of asking, and he said out loud why he was doing them.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. There are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five.

“*He has been in this county one hundred and twenty-three days. One hundred and twenty-three is six hundred and seventy-seven less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing for forty-seven days. Forty-seven is six hundred and seventy-seven less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in the reed on the other side for forty-eight days. I am not going to add the two together.*”

Then the nine went down, and the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his.

They went past the last of the beams, and past the place where the gravel is walked, and along about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage at the same fall, and up the step, and there were nine openings in the wall.

They went into the ninth.

Then the man of fifty-four stopped in the doorway, and he said one word, and nobody had asked him a question.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years.

“*Wait.*”

“*That room is lighter than the passage is. I cannot see very far and I cannot see well and I know a lit room when I am stood in one, and that is the ninth one and I have been in two of those eight times in nine days and none of them were lit.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight put his lamp down on the floor of the ninth room, and there were two lights in it.

There was a lamp on the hook cut in the roof. It was burning. It was not the man of thirty-eight’s and his is on the floor.

“*I have never seen that lamp. There are two lamps on that bank and both of them are accounted for. One of them is on the floor of a room about a hundred and thirty feet past the last of the beams.*”

“*And it is about a quarter full of oil and the flame is the size mine gets at about the second hour and I know that because I trim wicks and I have trimmed them for nineteen years.*”

“*And I am not going to say who lit it. A man of thirty-one has spent a week in this county telling us that a thing with no figure behind it does not go in a book. I would like about four of you to notice that he was talking about people, and he was right about it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight found the stone, and it is on the wall of the ninth room at about the height of a man’s chest, and it is about the size of two hands laid flat, and there are lines cut in it.

“*Nine lines. One under another. I counted them twice by lamp and it is nine, and nine is a figure of two counts by one man. It is not a figure of whatever cut them.*”

“*And the wear on them is the only thing in here that is true. The first line is nearly gone. The second is not. The ninth is sharp, and it was cut last. The cut on it is about four days old and I would not stake a year on that.*”

Then the man of fifty-four said what nobody else on that descent could have said about a stone with writing on it.

“*Nine lines in nine hands. That is all I know and I know it because I cannot read and I have looked at more cut marks than anybody on that bank.*”

“*A hand that cannot write cuts hard on the first stroke of a line and light on the last stroke of the same line, because the hand is guessing where the line ends. That is what a hand like mine does and that is what every one of these nine does, and they are nine different hands because the amount they guess by is nine different amounts.*”

About nine people in that room were looking at the stone and nobody in that room could read one word of it.

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes, and the boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and did not go down and did not ask.

And the man of thirty-eight put the finding on that bank, in about nine sentences, in front of about nine people. That is the only thing anybody in that county can do with a thing.

“*There is a ninth room past the last of the beams. In it there is a hook with a lamp on it, lit, and about a quarter full. Nobody in four counties lit it.*”

“*And there are nine lines cut in a stone in the ninth room. The ninth was cut about four days ago and the first is nearly worn away. They are in nine hands and every one of the nine is a hand that cannot write.*”

“*And over eight of the nine doors there are two hands cut, and over the ninth there is one. The lintel ninety steps back says two hands, once a day, before the light. That line has been cut a long time and I have never once seen a person near it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him and nobody asked him a second question.

Then the man of thirty-eight said the thing that was under the whole of it, in about eleven sentences. It was the reason he had put it on a bank instead of carrying it up about a hundred and thirty feet and giving it to one person.

“*There are nine doors and about nine people and one of them each. Nobody can be sent for about any of them.*”

“*That is the whole of what is down there. It is not a machine and it was not made by one man. It is nine jobs. Eight of them want two hands and one of them wants one, and it has been like that for about four hundred years.*”

“*And every one of those nine jobs is kept by somebody who cannot be sent for about it. There is no form in this county that says one may be sent for about a door nine miles down a river.*”

“*And I am not saying that as a complaint. I am a man of thirty-eight with a child of nine. I have carried a lamp down there four times this week and I would like somebody else to carry it too.*”

Then the man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said the sentence that put the whole of it together, and it was fourteen sentences, and it was the sentence he had given twice before and refused to improve on, once on the Sunday of the hundred and sixteenth week and once on the Sunday of this week.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and the fifth line of one of those sheets of deal is his and not the man of forty-three’s, and he has been that person for eleven years without knowing it was a thing to be.

“*Listen to what the man of thirty-eight has actually said. He has said it slowly and about half of you are still looking at the water.*”

“*Nine lines. Nine weathers. The first one is nearly gone and the ninth one is four days old. Nine hands, and every one of them a hand that cannot write. Every one of them is a hand that has had to get it right by itself.*”

“*And eight of those nine lines were cut on a morning when somebody could not find the two hands that the lintel says are wanted, once a day, before the light.*”

“*That is the whole of what it is. It is not a person. It is not a machine. It has got no opinion, and nobody put a face on it this morning.*”

“*It is what nine people agreed to do. It is written down in a stone in the ninth room, on nine different mornings, for the mornings they are not there.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine mornings. About nine of them had the lamp on the hook.

Then he said the half of it that made it theirs, and he said it in four sentences.

“*And it is the only thing in four counties that anybody can be sent for about, because there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a line of nine that was cut by somebody who cannot write.*”

“*And I am that, in two places, on two sheets of deal, for eleven years, and I have never once been asked what my line was for.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to notice that I have been saying a thing for about four days and it has turned out to be the true of a place.*”

“*And I would like it very much recorded that a man who cannot see well worked it out standing on a bank, and not in the room.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about that and nobody said he was right.

Then the man of thirty-one said what he wanted said about the two halves, and nobody agreed with him.

“*The lamp is burning and it is about a quarter full, and nothing has failed in about four hundred years. I am not going to be the man who calls that the wrong part.*”

“*And the ninth line was cut about four days ago and none of us knows what it says. It was cut on a morning somebody could not be found. I am not going to be the man who calls that the right part either.*”

“*I have had five things in me since the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week about a beam in a gallery. I am not going to say one of them this morning, and this morning is not about a beam.*”

Then the man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said a name out loud on that bank, flat, once, and he said it to the ground and not to anybody in the bank.

“*I am saying it to the stone and not to you, and I am saying it once, and I am not going to say it again and I am not going to shout it.*”

“*The First Witness.*”

Then nothing came out of that bank for about four seconds, and nothing whatever happened, and about nine people stood in the wind, and the water was nine inches up the top step and going.

Then he said what the saying had cost him, out loud, before anybody else did.

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked what they said to a stone, and there is no form that says a person may not.*”

“*And I am the fifth line of two sheets of deal, and I am nobody, and there is nobody in this county who can be sent for about what I just did.*”

“*Nobody is to thank me for it and nobody is to forgive me for it either. I am not going to be a man who said a word to a wall in front of nine people and thought well of myself.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight who had been standing about nine feet off said the thing she has said once before, and she said it again, and she said it had been checked twice.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week. She has been in that county thirty-five days, and she cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from.

“*Nobody on this bank has said that the thing that keeps the record in four counties and a lamp on a hook in a room about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank are the same thing.*”

“*I have been standing here since the Thursday of last week listening for somebody to say it and about four of you have thought it and not one of you has said it and I have checked twice this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be the one who says it, and it is not because it is not true, and it is because I have been four hundred miles with a bag and I do not carry a thing I have not worked out.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her and nobody argued with her and nobody improved on it.

---

The fever was fifty-six weeks and two days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-three days.

There is a lamp burning on a hook in the ninth of nine rooms, about a quarter full of oil, and lit within about an hour. Nobody in four counties lit it.

And there is a stone on that wall with nine lines cut in it in nine hands that cannot write. The ninth line is about four days old and nobody in four counties has ever read one word of it.

A man of thirty-eight said out loud on a bank that there are nine jobs down there, and that eight of them want two hands and one of them wants one. He said every one of them is kept by somebody nobody can be sent for about. He said he was not saying it as a complaint.

And a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said a name out loud, flat, once, to the ground, and said it was not a person and not a machine and had no opinion, and said there is no form in that county that says a person may be asked what they said to a stone.
