# Chapter 653: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Fifteenth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Name Is Written On The Leaf After The Last Head And Read Out Loud, And On Which About Nine People Are Counted Out Loud Into Two Parties, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Says Out Loud What Going Down Will Cost Her In The Only Unit She Has

Fourth day of the hundred and fifteenth week. His four hundred and third morning. Two hundred and fifty-three days after the settlement. The fever fifty-two weeks and six days. Two hundred and seven days since the division. One hundred and fifty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

A man of forty-three wrote a name into a book on the Friday morning at about the eighth hour, in the open, in front of about nine people, and then read it out loud, because he had said on the Wednesday that he would and about four of them had been counting on it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept the landing nine miles up that water nineteen years, and he has a whittling block, and the name went in with a knife.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and kept the book of heads and carts, and he asked one question out loud before the knife went in, and about four of the people standing about did not answer it and about nine did.

“*Is a man of sixty-one permitted to write a name into his own book on a leaf that is not for names.*”

“*I am going to wait about four minutes, and if nobody answers me I am going to do it anyway and say out loud that nobody answered, and about four of you know that I do that.*”

About four minutes went past and nobody answered and nobody said no.

“*A leaf in that book is a penny a head and a penny a cart. A man of sixty-six who digs for water is not a head and he is not a cart, and he does not come through that landing nine times a year or nine times in nine years, and he is not going to be a penny to anybody.*”

“*And I have been treating a nothing as a reason for about two years and a man of sixty-one said that out loud on the Monday of the hundred and twelfth week, and about nine of us heard it, and I have not stopped doing it since.*”

Then he read it out loud on the bank, and the ground was said before the name, the way every line on that sheet of deal is said.

“*Abel Fenner. Sixty-six. A sinker. He digs for water and he keeps a thing under that bank and the man of forty-three has kept his name out of a room for about nine years and is not doing it this morning.*”

About four people on that bank had the whole of it. About nine had a name they did not have an hour ago.

Nobody on that bank thanked the man of forty-three for a name.

Then the man of sixty-one counted the party out loud, and he said what a count of a party is a figure of before he gave it, and about nine people stood about while he did it.

“*There are about nine going down and about nine staying, and that is a figure of one morning on one bank and it is not a figure of a county and it is not a figure of anybody’s opinion about what ought to happen.*”

He read the nine who were going down, and every one of them was named by the person who had said they would go.

“*The man of sixty-six, who is a sinker, and who is going first and who has been told he may be the first because he is the one who knows the steps.*”

“*The woman of thirty-eight who keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and who is going second, and who has not said she will and who said on the Thursday that she would not, and I am going to let that stand in the book with the other eight and not smooth it.*”

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off the landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is her mother. She had walked in from the mill that morning with a lamp in her hand and had not lit it.

“*The man of thirty-eight, who is the only one who may carry the board of notches, and who is going because he is the only person here who has been on a wet floor in the dark for nine hours of a morning and knows what it is like.*”

“*The man of sixty-one, who keeps the book, and who is going with his own lamp, and who said out loud that a lamp in that county belongs to a person by name and that the lamp is his and the lamp goes where he goes.*”

“*The man of forty-one with two carts, and the man of fifty-four who holds the gravel and cannot read and said so on the Thursday before he was asked anything, and the woman of forty who has asked for a day against a name for about nine weeks.*”

“*The man of thirty-one, who asked to go first, and who is going fifth.*”

About four people on that bank had the five. The rest of them had the nine.

Then the woman of twenty-eight said what going down would cost her, and she said it out loud, and she said it in about nine sentences, and about nine people on that bank had not known until that moment that she was going.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight. She is not of that county and cannot be sent for about in it, and she cannot be sent for about in the county she came from either, and she came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week with two letters in a bag with the seals unbroken.

“*On the Thursday I said I was not going down those steps and nobody has asked me to and nobody was going to make me.*”

“*I am going. And I am saying what it costs before anybody asks, because the only unit I have is a day and everybody in this bank has one, and there is a woman of seventy-one in this town who has been asked four things and has answered three.*”

“*It is a day and a half down and up and about nine hours of it in the dark, and I have not slept in two nights and I am not going to pretend to anybody on this bank that I have.*”

“*I have come four hundred miles with two letters and both of them are in that bag with the seals unbroken, and they will be unread on the day I come up that bank, and they will be unread a day later than they were, and there is nobody in four counties who can be sent for about either of those facts.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for four hundred miles.

“*And there is the other half and it is worse and it is not about the letters.*”

“*Nobody up this bank can check whether I came up. Not the man of sixty-eight, not the woman of forty-four, not the man of thirty-one. There is no form in this county that says I shall be on that bank at a day, because I am in no book in this county and I said so out loud on the Monday of last week before I said anything else.*”

“*And a thing that goes down a bank and cannot be checked coming up is a lid with a name on it, and a man of sixty-eight said that on the Friday of the eleventh week and was right, and I am going down anyway, and I would like about four of you to hear the two halves of that in the same breath.*”

About four people on that bank had both halves. About nine had a day.

Then she said what her nine sentences are for in that place, and about four of the nine people going down had not known that they were the reason.

“*I said on the Wednesday of the hundred and fourteenth week that I would write down what a body says about each person in my own hand on the day it says it, and that nobody in four counties can check it except one person at a time.*”

“*There is no body in that gallery. There is a man of sixty-six and a floor and about nine people in the dark. So I am going to write down what each of the nine said about going down, on the day they said it, and give each of them the copy with my own hand, and no other hand between.*”

“*And I am going to keep no second copy in any bag or coat or book, and if I fail there is no form that says I may be sent for about it, and I said that out loud on the Wednesday of last week and I am saying it again on a Friday.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about the nine sentences.

Then the woman of forty-four refused him a lamp, and she refused him in about four sentences, and the reason was about him and not about the steps, and about nine people on that bank had been waiting since the fourth hour for somebody to say it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept the chairs book nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and she is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about a chair.

“*No.*”

About four seconds went past. The wind came off the water.

“*There is not a lamp in the county of Kell that does not belong to a person by name. I have a lamp. The man of sixty-one has a lamp and has had one nineteen years. The boy of thirteen has one his mother gave him. The man of forty-three had one before he kept a landing and it is in a box.*”

“*And if a lamp goes down that bank with a man who is in no book in this county, then there is one lamp in this county that nobody owns, and I have spent nineteen years making sure that is not a thing about a person.*”

“*That is four sentences and the last one is the reason and the reason is about you and not about the steps, and I would like about four of you to notice that and about nine of you to notice that I have not said one word about whether the steps are safe.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word to her about it.

Then the man of thirty-one said the true of one half of it, and it was the eighteenth time in that room, and there was no day-count in it, and he held it in about four seconds.

“*A woman of forty-four has just told me the true of a thing I would have got wrong, and I held it in four seconds, and I would like it said that I offered four things in one morning and was refused three times and that all three of the refusals were right.*”

“*The first was going first. The second was a rope. The third was a lamp. I have not offered a fourth yet and I am not going to, because a man who offers four things in a morning is a man counting, and I have been counting for ninety-nine days and counting is the whole of what I do.*”

About four people on that bank had the three refusals. The rest of them had the man.

Then the man of thirty-eight and the man of forty-one lifted the board off its stick and set it against the bank, and the man of sixty-six went down, and a woman of thirty-eight went down behind him, and the man of thirty-one went down fifth with his hands and nothing else in them.

Nobody on that bank thanked the nine for going.

---

The fever was fifty-two weeks and six days old. He had been in that county ninety-nine days, and a name that had been kept out of rooms for about nine years was written on the leaf after the last head of a book of heads and carts and read out loud on a Friday morning, and about four of the nine people standing about did not answer the question of whether he might.

And a party was counted out loud into about nine who went down and about nine who stayed, and a man of thirty-one who asked to go first was told no in four sentences by a man of sixty-eight and is going fifth.

And a woman of twenty-eight said out loud that nobody on that bank can check whether she came up, and went down anyway, and said both halves of that in the same breath.

And a woman of forty-four refused him a lamp in about four sentences because there is not a lamp in the county of Kell that does not belong to a person by name, and a man of thirty-one went down about ninety steps with his hands and nothing else in them.
