# Chapter 671: First Day Of The Hundred And Eighteenth Week, And A Tuesday, And A Man Of Forty-Three Gives A Figure For The Fifth Time And Says Out Loud That He Has Got It Wrong By A Day, And A Question Is Put In The Plainest Form It Has Ever Been Put

First day of the hundred and eighteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-first morning. Two hundred and seventy-one days after the settlement. The fever fifty-five weeks and three days. Two hundred and twenty-five days since the division. One hundred and seventy-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps by the sixth hour on the Tuesday, and the man of forty-three said the working out loud before anybody had asked him for it, because he had been asked about it four times in about nine days and this was the fifth time.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and the block of limestone with a line cut round it is in that reach. And it is nine miles up this water from the plank at Hask, and he has two crossings of it in his own hand out of nineteen years and no figure for the gap.

“*It was full on the Saturday of last week. It was about a foot and four inches over the top step at the seventh hour on the Saturday, and about a foot and two inches at the seventh hour on the Monday. This morning at the sixth hour the water is not on the top step at all.*”

“*That is three days, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself, and that is the same sentence I gave on the Monday and on the Friday and I am not going to improve on it.*”

Then he gave the other half of it, and the other half was the figure he has given four times.

“*The next time those steps can be walked is about four days after that bank is full. It was full on the Saturday and four days from the Saturday is the Wednesday, and the water came off those steps on the Tuesday night, and that is a day early.*”

“*I have said that figure four times and I have been wrong by a day once. I am not going to say it a fifth time this morning as though it were a new thing I had found out.*”

“*I am going to say it a fifth time and it is wrong. I would like a man of sixty-one to say so on that bank rather than me. A figure a man of forty-three has got wrong five times is a thing that ought to be got wrong by somebody who keeps a book.*”

About four people on that bank had the day early. The rest of them had a man of forty-three handing back a figure of his own.

Then the man of thirty-one said the want he had, and there was no number in it and he said so himself before anybody asked.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he was nobody’s, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and no form that renews him and no rate on him and no hundred in that county either.

“*I want to keep asking in a room. That is the ninth of nine jobs and it is the only one of the nine I am any good at, and I have not put a number in it since the Wednesday of the hundred and sixteenth week.*”

“*And I am going to say that out loud and then I am not going to say it again this week. The reason is that a woman of forty-four told me on the Sunday of the hundred and seventeenth week that two is the correct number of times to say one thing. She said it in front of about nine people, and she was right.*”

Then he put the question, and he put it plainly, and he said before he put it that he had dressed every question he had asked in that county up and this one is not going to be dressed.

“*What has to be done on a bank on a Monday by people who are alive.*”

“*That is the whole of it. Not by the nine and not by a coalition and not by a person anybody can be sent for about. By people who are alive, on a Monday, on a bank, in the morning.*”

Then the man who works answered it, and he answered it before the wind had finished getting across that landing, and it was the shortest thing anybody said on that bank all week.

The man is forty-one and he keeps two carts and one of the two carts has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making on the way up.

“*On the Monday morning the bank was full and there were about nine of us on it and nobody walked down anything.*”

“*That is what has to be done on a bank on a Monday this week. Nine people stand on it for a morning and do nothing at all, and about four of us did not know that was what we were doing until a man of thirty-one said it out loud on a Tuesday.*”

Then he said the other half, and the other half was about stone.

“*And a load of stone went up that track this morning and not on the Monday, and that is a day late and a Monday that somebody else does not get, and I told this bank on the Friday of last week that it was one day late.*”

“*It is one day late. I want that said out loud because I have said it twice and both times to a bank that did not write it down.*”

“*And I do not know whose Monday it was. I said that on the Friday and I am saying it again on the Tuesday and I have not looked for it in between, and there is no form in this county that says a man may be sent for about a Monday.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word back to him about the Monday.

Then the boy of thirteen was counted out loud again, and he counted himself out loud, and he had a figure now and did not know what to do with it.

The boy is thirteen. He is not a head in the book of heads and carts because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen. And he has walked four miles up that track with a barrow since he was nine, and four miles is about two hours.

“*It is eighteen days since I went down, and eighteen is six hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and fifty-three, and the Friday was the Friday of the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*And I am not going down there and nobody asked me to and nobody told me not to, and I have got a figure for it now the way the man of thirty-one has got figures for things, and I do not know what a figure of eighteen days is for.*”

Then the man of forty-three counted the two people who are in a place and not in a room, and he counts them because they are on either side of his landing and he walks past both of them twice a day.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in that ash on the low side of my landing since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week. That is forty-one days, and forty-one is six hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in the reed on the other side of that landing since about the second hour of the day before he lay down. That is forty-two days, and forty-two is six hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*I am not going to add them. I have walked past both of those mouths about four times a day for nineteen years, and I have never once stood at either of them. That is not modesty. It is that there is a thing about a man in an ash and I do not have a figure for it.*”

Nobody on that bank walked over to the ash. Nobody on that bank walked over to the reed.

Then the man of thirty-one said the count of his own, and he said it out loud with the subtraction, and it is the only number he has that anybody in four counties can check.

“*One hundred and seventeen days in this county. One hundred and seventeen is six hundred and seventy-one less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place, and I am in it. A man of sixty-one stopped another one of those clocks in this county three weeks ago and said out loud why. I am not going to say what he stopped it at, and I have never once been glad about it.*”

Then the man of sixty-one said what the week was going to be, in about three sentences. He did not put it in the book.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years, and there are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five.

“*Then it is the Wednesday and not the Tuesday, because a man of forty-three gave us the Wednesday on the Saturday and I am not going to overrule a man who keeps a landing on the strength of a better night.*”

“*And the two who lift that board have to be found, and there is no form in this county that says which two, and I am going to do about it what I said I was going to do about it a week ago and what I said was nothing.*”

“*And I am going to write down that a man of forty-three has been wrong by a day about a figure he has given four times, because that one I will write down, and because there is a difference between a figure a man gets wrong and a figure about a person.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for that either.

Then the wind came up the reach and about nine people went on standing about. The two carts went up that track at about the second hour of the afternoon with a load of stone in one of them. It was the first thing to go up that track in four days.

---

The fever was fifty-five weeks and three days old. He had been in that county one hundred and seventeen days.

A man of forty-three gave a figure for the fifth time and said out loud that he had got it wrong by a day, and asked a man of sixty-one to say it on the bank instead of him, and a man of sixty-one said he would write that one down.

And the plainest question anybody has put in that county was answered on a Tuesday morning by a man of forty-one. The answer was that about nine people stood on a bank on a Monday and did nothing at all, and that about four of them had not known that was what they were doing.
