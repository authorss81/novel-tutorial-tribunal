# Chapter 674: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Eighteenth Week, And A Friday, And On Which Nobody Goes Down Because The Bank Is At About Eleven Inches, And A Baker Up That Road Is Told What It Has Cost Her And Was Never Asked

Fourth day of the hundred and eighteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-fourth morning. Two hundred and seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-five weeks and six days. Two hundred and twenty-eight days since the division. One hundred and seventy-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Nobody went down on the Friday, and the reason was given out loud before anybody asked, and it was a reason about a bank and not about nine people.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has given a reading on nine mornings out of the last nine.

“*About eleven inches on the top step at the seventh hour. Three days since it came off those steps and it fills in about four, and it will be full by the Saturday morning and I will tell you that on the Saturday and not before.*”

“*And the two who lift that board came at about the seventh hour yesterday and they will not be needed today. I am glad about that and I am not going to pretend I am not. There is no form in this county that says I may not be glad about a day off.*”

About four people on that bank had the day off. Nobody on that bank thanked the man of forty-three for a bank.

Then the man of forty-one said the thing he had worked out on the Wednesday night and had said out loud to nobody until that Friday morning.

“*A man of forty-one has two carts. One of them has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making on the way up.*”

“*And I take up that cart road four miles to a village and I have taken it up that road since I was about twenty-two, and I have taken bread up it, and I did not know until this morning that the bread on it was hers.*”

“*And I did not know on the Wednesday when I went into four feet of water from about the fifth hour to about the ninth hour and her cart was standing at the head of that track with me not on it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen did a sum out loud on that bank and nobody had asked him for it, and it was the first time he had ever said a figure that was about a person and not about a barrow.

The boy is thirteen and he is not a head in the book of heads and carts because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen. And he walks four miles up that track with a barrow and four miles is about two hours, and he has done it since he was nine.

“*One hundred and twenty days. Six hundred and seventy-four less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And the nine have gone down five times since the Tuesday of last week, and that is nine days and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And four miles twice a day is four hours, and that was the figure I had on the Friday of last week. It is not the figure this week, because there is a third four miles on two of the mornings, and that is two hours more. Six hours out of eight is three-quarters of a working day, and that is a figure of my legs and not of a man of thirteen.*”

About four people on that bank had the three-quarters. The rest of them had a boy of thirteen who had gone to a number again.

Then she was told, and she was told in front of about nine people, and she was not asked whether she wanted to hear it, and she had come up that road herself and nobody had sent for her.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road from the landing, and about nine houses in that village take her bread, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a tradesman four miles off may be sent for about a morning of his work.

“*And I am the one who should have thought of it and I have not, because I have been baking for nineteen years and the road has been there every one of them and the carts have been on it every one of them.*”

“*About sixty loaves in a night and about forty of them go up that track. An hour and a half up with the cart loaded and an hour and a quarter coming down. That is two hours and three quarters of a cart for one morning of bread.*”

“*On the mornings a man of forty-one is in four feet of water, his two carts stand at the head of that track from about the fourth hour. Mine is the one that stands there. My bread goes up on the morning after, or it does not go up at all.*”

“*About nine houses get it a day late. About four of the nine are people who cannot do a day without it. I have known those four since I was twenty-two. One of them is a man of eighty-one, and he does not eat on a Thursday unless it is there in the morning.*”

Then she said what she wanted said about it, and what she wanted said about it was not a day.

“*And I want it said that I was not asked, and that I would have said yes. I would have said yes to a man coming to my door on the Friday and saying here is what it costs, and here is what we do about it.*”

“*And I want it said that saying yes to that is not the same as being asked. Nobody in this county has the second one, and I have wanted it for about nineteen years.*”

Then the man of forty-one said what he was going to do about it, and he said it flat, and nobody on that bank said the word for what he had done.

“*Then I leave my carts at the head of that track on a morning I go down. That is about a morning of cart work on about five mornings in ten, and about two hours of a cart each. That is about ten hours in ten days, and that is a figure of my carts and not of her bread.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked on this bank for it. I am not going to be forgiven on this bank for it either, because there is nothing to forgive me for. I did not do it to her. And if a person wants me to be sorry about it, they can come and find me on a Wednesday. I will not be sorry on a bank at about the ninth hour before I have had anything to eat.*”

About nine people on that bank heard a man refuse to be forgiven and about four of them understood the refusal.

Then she said the thing about him that was true and not kind, and nobody improved on it.

“*You will do it twice and then you will stop. I am telling you that now so that nobody has to say it thirty days from today.*”

“*Not because you will not keep it. Because you have two carts, and one wheel is making a noise, and the noise is going to get worse. The first morning you cannot get up that road is the morning my bread stops going up it. You will have found that out on your own, and by then you will have a reason, and it will not be me.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about that.

Then the man of thirty-one said what he had got wrong, out loud, in about eleven sentences, and he did not put it in the shape of a want.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he was nobody’s. He had read a list of nine jobs out loud on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week, and had said that a list read aloud once was gone by the time the light went, and that there was no form anybody could be sent for about it.

“*On the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week I said that about a list of nine, and I was pleased with myself about it, and a man of sixty-one was glad with me.*”

“*A list read aloud once is a list nobody can be sent for about. That is true. It is also the reason a woman of forty-four is not standing in this bank, and the reason a woman of forty-nine has come down that road twice. It is the reason a man of forty-one was in four feet of water on the Wednesday from nobody telling him to.*”

“*It is the reason a woman of forty-four with sixty loaves is about a day late on about five mornings in ten. Nobody in this county could have told her on the Wednesday. Nobody would have been able to tell her afterwards, because there is no form and no column, and she is not a name in any book in this county.*”

“*I have been in this county one hundred and twenty days and I have found four things that cost somebody something. I found the last one out of a woman telling me what she bakes. I am not going to make that into a lesson, and I am not going to give it to anybody else to learn.*”

Nobody on that bank said the word brave and nobody said the word sorry.

Then about nine people stood about on a bank with the water eleven inches up the top step. The boy of thirteen went up that road with an empty barrow at about the fourth hour, because it is what he does. A woman of forty-four went back down it with nothing on her cart. Both of them were going the same way and neither of them walked with the other.

---

The fever was fifty-five weeks and six days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty days.

Nobody went down on the Friday, because a bank was at about eleven inches over the top step. A man of forty-one found out on a bank in the morning that his two carts and a woman’s bread were the same road. About nine houses four miles up that road are a day late on about five mornings in ten.

And a woman of forty-four said she would have said yes and that saying yes to being told is not being asked, and that nobody in that county has the second one and she has wanted it for about nineteen years.

And she said out loud, in front of about nine people, that the man of forty-one would do it twice and then stop, and that by then he would have a reason and it would not be her, and nobody on that bank said a word about that.
