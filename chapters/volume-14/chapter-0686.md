# Chapter 686: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which Nine Go Down And About A Foot Of Dry Walked Gravel Is About Three Inches Deep With One Boot In It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud On That Gravel That He Has Lost The Way He Came Back On Since The Wednesday He Came In

Second day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and thirty-sixth morning. Two hundred and eighty-six days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks and four days. Two hundred and forty days since the division. One hundred and eighty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps at the sixth hour and about two inches on the second step at the seventh hour. And the man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped, which is what he has done every morning this week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has the block of limestone with a line cut round it nine miles up this water and two crossings of it in his own hand and no figure for the gap.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour and about two inches on the second step at the seventh hour. One day since it came off those steps and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And that is the reading and that is all of it. One day is a figure of a bank and not of a pair of figures, and I am not going to give you a pair of figures on a Wednesday either.*”

Then the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it. And the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his, and the man of thirty-one went fifth.

They went into the gallery and the ninth beam went along about two inches as the man of thirty-eight came down onto the last of the steps and came back again when he came off them, and about nine people in that gallery had seen that before and said nothing about it.

Then they went about nine feet past the last of the beams. And the roof came down and the walls came in and the floor came up, and the man of thirty-eight put his lamp on the floor and got down on one knee in it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has said that a notch is a count and that he has thirty-two of them and does not know what they are for.

“*There is about three inches of this gravel loose in it and there was about a foot on the Monday of the hundred and eighteenth week, and I have been in it three times since and I have put my own arm in to the shoulder and I know what a foot of it feels like.*”

“*And there is still a walked place in the middle of it. About as wide as one boot. Not two. There have been about nine of us walking this passage three times a week for about three weeks and on the Monday of last week there were about nine of them wide of walked gravel here and this morning there is one.*”

About nine people in that passage were standing in it and about four of them had put a boot into it.

Then the man of thirty-one said it, and he said it standing on that gravel with his hands behind his back. And he said it before he had gone any further up or down the passage, and nobody had asked him anything.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and no form that renews him and no hundred in that county either.

“*A groove is a route and a route is a going and a coming. A man of forty-one told you that on the Tuesday of this week with the stone in the gallery in front of him. And I have been carrying it about two days and I have got the other half of it and the other half is this.*”

“*This is about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage and about a foot of walked gravel in it, and it has been walked for three weeks by about nine of us, and on the Tuesday I put my hand on a going in a wall where no going has ever gone and it was not a route, because it did not come back.*”

“*And the coming came out of this. That is what a coming is. It is not a figure, it is a wet place about as wide as one boot, and it has gone, and I am not going to stand in a passage and pretend to the woman of forty-nine that a rack with one blank shelf on it has anything to do with it.*”

Then he said the whole of it, in his own mouth, and he said the shape of what he had lost before he said anything about what it was made of. And nobody else in that passage said a word while he did it.

“*I have had a way back since the Wednesday of the hundred and first week. That is the evening of the day I came in, by boat, and it is a hundred and thirty-three days ago.*”

“*And I have never once been able to say what it was made of. I have been in this county a hundred and thirty-two days, and on every morning I have gone down I have come up out of that passage and gone up that bank and been a man on a road by the middle of the day.*”

“*And it is not there now. And I am not going to give anybody a figure for it, because there is no book in four counties with a figure in it and there is not one person in four counties who was keeping it, and I am not going to be the man who invents a number at about the ninth hour on a bank.*”

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes, and about nine people stood at the head of that flight and nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading.

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count out loud and gave the other one beside it. And the difference of one was the whole of what he said about it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years and has written four lines on the leaf after the last head in eleven years and has not written a number in that book since he took it.

“*He has been in this county a hundred and thirty-two days. A hundred and thirty-two is six hundred and eighty-six less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And he says a hundred and thirty-three days since he came in. And he is not wrong and I am not wrong, and the one is the evening and the evening is not a day he was in. That is the difference and it is one, and it has been one since the Wednesday of the hundred and first week. And nobody has ever argued with me about it.*”

“*And I am not going to put the first one in that book and I am not going to put the second one in, and the reason is not that it is about a person. It is that it is not about a person either. It is about a hundred and thirty feet of gravel and about nine pairs of feet, and there is no name in it for anybody to be.*”

The difference of one went round that bank and nobody standing on it had anything else to hold.

Then a man of fifty-four said one thing, and it was a fact about gravel and not about a man, and he cannot read.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years. And he is not a keeper of anything and has said so.

“*I hold gravel. That is my work and it is the only thing anybody knows me for. And I have held it for nine years and I have never once asked what it is for and nobody has told me.*”

“*And I am going to say one thing about a foot of it going down to three inches, and then I am going to stop talking, and the thing is that a man who cannot read is the only person who can be sent for about a wet place about as wide as one boot, because every one of you can be shown the other eight widths and can count them.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him and nobody improved on it.

Then the man of forty-one said the other half of it, on that bank, and it was about his own morning and it had no reason in it.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and had taken one of them up that cart road at about the fourth hour and had not been able to get it up past about two hundred yards of it.

“*I could not get my cart up that track this morning. I have taken it up four mornings a week since I was about twenty-two and I could not get it up past about two hundred yards of that road and I stopped at the two hundred yards and I walked the rest.*”

“*And I have no reason and I am not going to be given one and I have not got a wheel making a noise that did anything about it this morning. I am telling you it because about four of you have been up that road this week and I am not going to have anybody find out at about the fourth hour the way I found out.*”

Nobody on that bank improved on the two hundred yards, and about four of them had a reason that was about a wheel and had nothing to do with the rest of it.

Then a woman of thirty-eight said the thing about a mill that nobody had asked her, and she said it in about nine sentences, and she said at the start of it that she was not going to improve on anything she had said on the Tuesday of last week.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing. And about eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is her mother.

“*A mill is a keeping. About nine hours of a day, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it. And it grinds on the seventh morning because somebody turned the handle on the sixth morning, and nobody has to be told about the seventh morning because nobody is in the room.*”

“*And I said on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that none of what we found down there is a way for anybody to say no. I am not going to say that a second time and I am not going to say it better.*”

“*And here is what I have got this week. If a thing down there is a route because a person goes along it and comes back on it, then three inches of loose gravel and one boot of walked place is a mill with nobody in it, and nobody has to be told, and that is the whole of what I know about it and it is about a boot.*”

“*I am not a keeper of it. A man of about fifty-two said that about himself on the Wednesday of last week, and I have been carrying the same sentence in my own mouth about my own two hands ever since, and I am not going to say it again this week.*”

About four people on that bank had the mill and about nine of them had the boot.

---

The fever was fifty-seven weeks and four days old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty-two days.

There is about three inches of loose gravel where there was about a foot of dry walked gravel on the Monday of the hundred and eighteenth week, and about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage under a bank that a man of thirty-eight has measured with his own arm, and there is still a walked place in it about as wide as one boot.

A man of thirty-one said out loud on that gravel that he has had a way back since the Wednesday of the hundred and first week and does not have it now, and that there is no figure for what it was and no person in four counties who was keeping it and nobody is going to be given one.
