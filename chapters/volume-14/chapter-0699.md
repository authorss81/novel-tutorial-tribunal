# Chapter 699: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Second Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Name Flat To The Ground For The Second Time In This County That Anybody Has Said It To, And Something Says One Sentence Back In A Gallery With About Nine Beams In The Roof Of It, And Nothing Whatever Happens, And Then Two People Put Their Hands On A Door-Stone About Five Feet High And It Does Not Move

First day of the hundred and twenty-second week. His four hundred and forty-ninth morning. Two hundred and ninety-nine days after the settlement. The fever fifty-nine weeks and three days. Two hundred and fifty-three days since the division. One hundred and ninety-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and nobody asked him for anything else, and about nine people went down at about the ninth hour.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. He has said he has nothing to say about a night four times and has said he will say it a fifth time on Wednesday.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. One day since it came off those steps, and I count it the way I count it every morning of my life and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And it was off all ninety at the sixth hour yesterday, and that is where you get a figure from, and I am not going to be asked for a night by anybody this week.*”

About nine of them had the four inches and about four of them had the working, and it was the four who had come down.

Then they went down. The man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his. The man of thirty-one went fifth with his hands and nothing else in them, as he has gone down every descent for about three weeks.

And they went past the last of the beams, and about nine feet past the place where the gravel is walked, and along about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage at the same fall, and up the step.

There were nine openings in the wall with two hands cut over eight of them and one hand over the ninth.

And in the ninth room there was a lamp on the hook cut in the roof of it, and it was burning, and about a quarter full of oil, and nobody lit it and nobody put it out. The shelf cut in the wall about the height of a hand was empty.

And the man of thirty-four went to the back of that room and put his hand flat on the wall behind the shelf. It was still the course of stone put into a gap and faced about the thickness of two fingers, and the colour of it was still wrong.

Then a man of thirty-one said a name out loud, flat, once, to the ground and not to anybody. He said out loud before he said it how many times it had been said in that county, and what he was not going to do with it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He said a name flat on the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week. The same name was said on the Saturday of the hundred and seventeenth week by a woman of forty, and a man who cannot see well said it on the Monday of the hundred and eighteenth week.

“*This is the fourth time that name has been said in this county and the second time anybody has said it to anything.*”

“*I am saying it to the floor. I am not going to shout it and I am not going to say it twice and I am not going to explain it.*”

“*And a man who cannot see well worked this out standing on a bank nine weeks ago, and asked for it to be recorded that he worked it out standing on a bank and not in the room.*”

“*And I am not going to be the man who says a word to it and then reports what it did.*”

“*If it says something back, I am going to say what it said, and I am not going to say what I think of it.*”

“*The First Witness.*”

Then nothing came out of that gallery for about four seconds. Nothing whatever happened. The beam in the roof of that gallery was where it is, and the lamp went on burning on its hook.

About nine people were standing in a room about as long as two men and about as wide as one, and nothing moved.

About four people in that room had the four seconds and about nine of them had been waiting about nine weeks for something and got one sentence.

Then a voice in that gallery said one sentence, flat, in about as many words as it took to say it. It came out of the middle of the room, and it was not a shout, and nobody shouted anything back at it.

“*The chamber behind the wall in the ninth room is being sealed. It is being sealed because somebody under it is about to do something to it.*”

Then nothing whatever happened again, and about four people in that room said nothing at all for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and nobody said the word brave and nobody said the word sorry.

Then a man of thirty-one said the sentence back out loud in his own mouth, exactly as it had come. He said he was doing it so that about four of them would have it in the same words, and he did not add anything to it.

“*The chamber behind the wall in the ninth room is being sealed. It is being sealed because somebody under it is about to do something to it.*”

“*And I am not going to ask it a second question this morning.*”

“*I have had a question in my mouth since the fourth second and I am not going to ask it, and I would like about four of you to be able to tell in nine days that I did not ask it.*”

About four people in that room had the sentence and about nine of them had the question he did not ask.

Then a man of thirty-eight said what a reason is, and he said it in front of about nine people with a lamp on the floor of the room, and it took six sentences and nobody improved on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and the lamp on the floor of that room is his.

“*A reason is not a permission. I have been given a reason and I have not been asked for anything.*”

“*Those are two different things, and I have been on this bank for about six weeks telling nine people they are different.*”

“*And it is not wrong to say that a thing has a reason. I am not going to stand in a room and say that is not a thing a thing can do.*”

“*What I want to know is who it is a reason to, and that is a question, and I am not asking it out loud because nobody in this room can be sent for about the answer.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the other half of it, and it was about a person and not about a room. She asked for it to be said that she was not going to be thanked for it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*It is not wrong to say a thing is being sealed to stop somebody doing a thing. That is a thing with an opinion about the thing, and I am not going to say a thing cannot have an opinion.*”

“*And a thing with an opinion is a thing that can be asked a question. There is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether it is sure, and there is no form that says a person may not.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the eighth time this fortnight. I am not going to say a list is a column this morning because I have nothing new and a woman of forty-four who has said it four times is not improved by saying it a fifth.*”

Nobody in that room argued with her and about four of them had the form and about nine of them had the opinion.

Then a man of sixty-one said what has actually changed this morning, in a room, on about the ninth hour of a Tuesday, and it took six sentences, and he did not put anything around them.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and the last of the five is a number, and it is the only one of the five that was not there when he took the book, and he knows the day he put it there.

“*Three things. It has spoken. It has given a reason nobody asked it for. And it has said the word sealed, and sealed is a word about a keeping and not about a stone.*”

“*And I did not know that about the word until this morning, and I have kept books for eleven years.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for about a sealing, and there is no form that says a person may not, and both of those have been true since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week and this morning is the first time one of them has said anything at all.*”

Then they went back out of that room to go up. The man of forty-one and the man of thirty-eight put their hands on the door-stone of the ninth room, which is about five feet high and about two feet across at the top. There is water under it, and it takes two people to swing it.

And it did not move.

And a man of fifty-four who cannot read put his own hand down into the gap under it and said there was water in it, and said how much, and said that it was the same as it had been on the Monday morning of the hundred and twentieth week, which is eight days ago.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain. He cannot read, and he has said so a great many times.

“*There is about an inch of water in it and it is cold, and it is the same as it was on the Monday of the hundred and twentieth week.*”

“*I have put my hand in that gap before and I know what an inch of it feels like, and I am not going to round it.*”

“*And two men put their hands on that stone about a quarter of an hour ago and it did not move. There is an inch of water under it.*”

“*And I am the only person anybody can be sent for about that water, and nobody has sent me for about it, and I have not been asked whether I would move it.*”

About four people in that passage had the inch of water and about nine of them had two men and a stone that did not move.

Then a man of thirty-eight said the whole of what a door-stone not moving is, and it took eleven sentences, and it was the plainest thing said in that room all morning.

“*It is not a stone that has got heavier, and it is not a stone that has been fastened. It is not water that has come up under it, because there is an inch of water under it and a man of fifty-four has had his hand in there.*”

“*It is that the room is shut. I have been opening that door twice a day for about nineteen years and it has never once not opened.*”

“*And it is not a door I can shut, because I have never been able to shut it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight is not going to stand in a passage and tell about nine people that a chamber has stopped being a chamber.*”

“*A chamber is a day, and it is kept by two hands once a day before the light, and nobody has come to that room this morning that I know of.*”

“*Nothing has fallen down. I want that said. The roof is where it was and the walls are where they were and the shelf is empty and the lamp is burning and about nine feet of groove is worn in that floor. Nothing has fallen down and one door will not open.*”

Nobody in that passage said anything at all, and about four of them had nothing fallen down and about nine of them had one door.

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and did not get. It was the fifth time in about three weeks, and he said it in his own mouth, and nobody else said it after him.

“*I want to know what somebody under it is about to do to it. That is the want.*”

“*I have had it since the fourth second, and I am going to say it out loud once so that about four of you know I had it.*”

“*And I am not going to put a room in the sentence. It said the chamber behind the wall in the ninth room, and I have been saying the ninth room in my own head all morning as though it had said the ninth room. It did not.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said that on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week about a space in a wall, and I have been making a figure a man of thirty-one has made about a sentence ever since.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for about a chamber behind a wall, and there is no form that says a person may not, and I am nobody’s, and I am not going to put a thing behind that wall and I am not going to ask anybody in this room to.*”

About four people in that passage had the want. About nine of them had a door-stone between them and about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage and ninety steps.

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes. A man of thirty-eight came up last and did not say anything about the lamp on the way past it, and about nine people stood at the head of that flight in the wind.

---

The fever was fifty-nine weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and forty-five days.

A name was said flat to the ground in a room about as long as two men about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank, for the fourth time in that county and the second time anybody has said it to anything. One sentence came back, and it was that a chamber behind the wall in the ninth room is being sealed because somebody under it is about to do something to it.

And two people put their hands on a door-stone about five feet high that takes two people to swing and it did not move. A man of fifty-four who cannot read put his own hand under it and found about an inch of water, the same as it was nine days ago.

And nothing fell down. The roof is where it was and the shelf is empty and the lamp is burning and one door will not open.
