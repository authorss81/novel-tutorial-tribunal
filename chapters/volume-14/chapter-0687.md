# Chapter 687: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twentieth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which About Nine People Could Go Down And Do Not Because A Man Of Thirty-Eight Told This Bank On The Tuesday Of Last Week What His Shoulder Would Do On A Thursday, And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Finds Out Something About A Hand That Nobody Asked Her To Look For

Third day of the hundred and twentieth week. His four hundred and thirty-seventh morning. Two hundred and eighty-seven days after the settlement. The fever fifty-seven weeks and five days. Two hundred and forty-one days since the division. One hundred and eighty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The water was about six inches on the second step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped. And about nine people on that bank could have gone down and did not.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has given a reading every morning this week and stopped after it every morning this week.

“*About six inches on the second step at the seventh hour. Two days since it came off those steps and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And that is the reading. That is the whole of what I am going to say about water in this bank until somebody asks me a question I have not been asked. And no one has asked me one since the Thursday of last week.*”

About nine people on that bank had the six inches and about four of them had already decided they were not going down and about nine of them had not.

Then a man of thirty-eight said why he was not going down, and he had said it in advance.

“*It is a Thursday. I said on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that my shoulder would be no good for carrying a pole up that water on a Thursday, and I said it in a gallery with a lamp in my hand, and I put the lamp down about four times to get the shoulder back in.*”

“*And it is no good. It went on the Tuesday of this week and it has not got better and it is not going to get better by standing on a bank, and I am not going up that water today with a pole in my right hand and I told this bank a week before it happened, and about four of you believed me, and the rest of you are finding out today.*”

About nine of them had not believed him in advance and about four of them had, and he said one word about it and the word was that they did not have to.

Then about nine people stood on a bank with six inches on the second step and did not go down. And that is a Monday thing and it was not a Monday.

Then a woman of twenty-eight looked at the right hand of a man of thirty-one for about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet. And nobody had asked her to, and she said afterwards that she had not decided to.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from, and she is not one of the nine and is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*I did not decide to look at it. I was stood about nine feet off with nothing to do and he turned round to get past somebody and I was stood next to him.*”

“*It is dry. That is the whole of what I have found out and it took about as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet.*”

“*Every hand on this bank is wet. Every one of them. They have been wet every morning for about three weeks, and mine is wet now. And that man of thirty-eight has oil on two fingers of the left one, and the man of forty-one has both of his cut to the elbow. And that hand has been in that water on two mornings of this week, once in about four feet of it on the Saturday and once in about two inches on the second step for two hours on the Wednesday, and it is as dry as the back of my hand.*”

About four people on that bank had the hand and about nine of them had looked away from it.

Then she said the rest of it, and she said what she was not going to do with it. And she said that before anybody had asked her what she was going to do with it.

“*The skin round it has not gone white and it has not gone soft and the palm of it has not come up the way a hand comes up when it has been in water that long, and I have had my own hands in that water about nine times and I know what mine look like at about the ninth hour.*”

“*And I am not going to be a keeper of it. A man of about fifty-two said on the Wednesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that the day he is a keeper of this he is a thing that is sent for and that he will have said so himself, and I am saying the same sentence about my own two eyes and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked about it. If a person on this bank asks me what I think it means I am going to say that I have given you what I saw and that is all I have got, and that is not modesty, it is that I have been four hundred miles with a bag and two letters and I do not carry a thing I have not worked out.*”

Nobody on that bank asked her and nobody comforted him and nobody said the word brave.

Then the man of thirty-eight said what his shoulder was and what a Thursday was for, and nobody had asked him and nobody offered to do it for him.

“*I carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. And nobody has ever asked me what the thirty-two is for, and that is my own doing because I have never told anybody.*”

“*And on a Thursday I read two copies nine miles apart. That is what a Thursday is. It has been a Thursday for about eleven years and I have done it about eleven years. And it takes me from about the fourth hour to about the ninth.*”

“*And I have been in that water on more mornings since the Saturday of the hundred and eighteenth week than I have a figure for, with a pole in my right hand, and this morning I cannot lift that hand above my shoulder, and there is no form in this county that says a man may be sent for about a shoulder and there is no form that says he may not.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two said something very nearly that about a fifth line of a sheet of deal on the Wednesday of last week and he was right. And I am not going to improve on him and I am not going to thank him for it either.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said a figure out loud, and it was a figure of his own days and a figure of times. And he gave both and said which was which.

“*Thirty-four days since I went down. Thirty-four is six hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and fifty-three, and that is a count of days.*”

“*And I have counted nine times this week that I decided a thing, and that is a count of times and not a clock, and I am counting them because I am thirteen and it is the only sort of number I have got, and nobody has told me to stop and nobody has told me to go on.*”

The thirty-four days went round that bank, and so did a boy of thirteen counting the times he had decided things.

Then about four people on that bank asked how many days it had been there. And about nine people on that bank had a different answer, and every one of them was wrong in the same way.

“*It was the Tuesday of this week.*” That is the woman of twenty-eight.

“*It was the Tuesday of the hundred and twentieth week and that is two days. And it is not three, and I am not going to let anybody on this bank say three.*” That is a man of sixty-one.

And about nine people said two and about four people said three, and about four of them had not been on that bank on the Tuesday when Ilyan Vester put his hand on a wall at all, and have been saying what they were told.

Then she said what she had done about it, and nobody had asked her to do that either. And it was the first thing in about nine days she had put anywhere that was not a number.

“*I wrote it in my day book this morning. In my own hand. There is no other hand between.*”

“*I write two numbers a day in that book and I have written two numbers a day for about nine days and I have never once written a thing in it that was not a number, and this morning I have written a sentence, and I have broken my own rule and I am not going to tell anybody what the rule was for.*”

“*And nobody is going to thank me for writing it down. I said that on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week in front of about nine people and I am not going to say it a second time. And a woman of forty-four told this bank that two is the correct number of times to say one thing.*”

The day book went round that bank, and there was a sentence in it that nobody standing there would ever read.

Then she gave two counts out loud, and she gave them both with the subtraction. And she gave the second one because she gives it every week and not because anybody asked.

“*I have been in this county forty-five days. Forty-five is six hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and forty-two, and that is the second longest count anybody on this bank has said out loud this week and it is mine.*”

“*And he has been in this county a hundred and thirty-three days. A hundred and thirty-three is six hundred and eighty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. I have written both of those down and I have given him his copy with my own hand and there is no other hand between and I am going to go on doing that.*”

Then she said the third thing she has said in that county. And she said it once, and it was the same sentence with a day on the front of it.

“*I have checked it a fourth time this morning and it has not moved. And nobody on this bank has said out loud that a lamp on a hook in the ninth of nine rooms and the thing that keeps the record in four counties are the same thing.*”

“*And I am still not going to be the one who says it. And it is still not because it is not true, and that has now stood for about ten days and about four of you have heard me say it three times.*”

Then about nine people stood on a bank and a boy of thirteen went up that road with an empty barrow at about the fourth hour because it is what he does, and a man of forty-one walked up it with two carts and could not get past about two hundred yards and walked the rest.

The fever was fifty-seven weeks and five days old. He had been in that county one hundred and thirty-three days.

About nine people could have gone down on the Thursday and did not, because a man of thirty-eight told that bank on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that his shoulder would be no good for carrying a pole up that water on a Thursday, and he was right on the first Thursday, and the second one is seven days off and he says it will be the same and he has not asked anybody to take his word for that part.

And a woman of twenty-eight who is not one of the nine and keeps nothing looked at a hand for as long as it takes a man of forty-one to push a stone nine feet and found out that it is dry, and said out loud that she is not going to be a keeper of it and that she is not going to be asked about it.

She wrote one sentence in a day book that has had nothing but numbers in it for about nine days, in her own hand. And there is no other hand between.
