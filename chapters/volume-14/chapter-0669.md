# Chapter 669: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Seventeenth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which The Question Is Put Out Loud Whether About Nine People Can Hold A Thing Open That About Four Hundred Years Of One-Person Jobs Have Been Holding, And The Working Is Given, And Nobody In The Room Likes The Answer

Sixth day of the hundred and seventeenth week. His four hundred and nineteenth morning. Two hundred and sixty-nine days after the settlement. The fever fifty-five weeks and one day. Two hundred and twenty-three days since the division. One hundred and sixty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The man of thirty-one put the question out loud at about the ninth hour on the Sunday and he put it as a question and not as a want. And about nine people were on that bank. And it is the third question of the week that he has put and the first one that about nine of them have heard him put.

The question was whether about nine people can hold open a thing that about four hundred years of one-person jobs have been holding.

“*I am going to say the thing I mean and I am going to say the thing I do not mean, and the second one is the beam and I am not asking about the beam.*”

“*A stone about five feet high and about two feet across at the top and rather less at the bottom, set in a groove cut in a floor under about ninety steps of bank. And it takes two people to swing because there is water under it.*”

“*Can about nine people hold that open the way it has been held open. That is the question and I am going to stand out of the way of it.*”

Then the man of sixty-one gave the working, and he gave it out loud, and about four people on that bank had the whole of the sum and about nine had the total.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. And he gave a figure of about four hundred years on the Saturday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and nobody answered him and nobody said no about cutting a second column in the back of the last leaf.

“*A day is about nine hours. I said that in a chamber on the Sunday of the hundred and fifteenth week and I have not got a better one and I am not going to go looking.*”

“*About four hundred years is about a hundred and forty-six thousand days. I said that on the Saturday of that week and it is the only number in this county with that many zeroes in front of it. And about four of you were in that chamber and about nine of you were not.*”

“*Nine hours of a day for a hundred and forty-six thousand days is one million three hundred and fourteen thousand hours.*”

“*And one million three hundred and fourteen thousand hours divided by nine people is a hundred and forty-six thousand hours each. That is the working and I have done it out loud twice because the first time I said one million three hundred and forty and not fourteen.*”

About four people on that bank had the mistake in it. Nobody in that bank said a word about the first number.

Then he gave the last of it, and it is the only number in that bank that is about the length of a life. And he said out loud what it is a figure of before he said the figure.

“*A hundred and forty-six thousand hours at nine hours a day is about sixteen thousand days. Sixteen thousand days is about forty-four years.*”

“*And that is a figure of nine people who have not been born. Not nine people on this bank. Nine people who do not exist yet. And neither do most of their children. And I am not going to hand a true number about people who do not exist to a bank of nine people who do.*”

“*And I have been glad about numbers for about nine years and about four of you know what that is. And I would like it said that the first number I have given this week is the first one I have been ashamed of since the Thursday of the hundred and sixteenth week, and there were two of those in a fortnight.*”

Nobody in that bank said a word about that.

Then a man of forty-one gave the physics of it in about ten sentences, and it is the only answer anybody gave, and it is not an answer anybody liked.

“*That board is about four feet by two feet and a man of thirty-eight can get it up off its stick alone and a man of forty-one can get it up alone. And a man of sixty-six cannot get one end of it up without the other end coming down, and that is not about how many people agree to it.*”

“*It is about how many hands are on it. Eight hands is not better than two hands for a board that four feet by two feet. And nine people are not better than two people for a stone about five feet high. And about four of you have heard me say the first of those in the last fortnight and about nine of you have not.*”

“*So the answer to the man of thirty-one is no, and it is no in a very short sentence. And I am not going to stretch it, and I am sorry about the stretching and I am not going to do it.*”

“*And here is the half that nobody wants, and I am going to say it because I am sixty-one and there is nobody here older than me doing the doing. Somebody has to keep coming.*”

“*Not about nine people. One at a time, and about twice a day, and about ninety steps. And about nine of those steps under about four feet of water. And that has been the case for about four hundred years and it is going to be the case on Monday morning whatever we do on this bank today.*”

About nine people in that bank did not like that. Nobody in that bank improved on it. And about four of them said the same thing back to the man of forty-one in different words, which is not an improvement.

Then a man of thirty-one said the question he asked on the Friday of the hundred and sixteenth week. And he said it a second time. And a woman of forty-four said out loud that it was the same sentence and that two is the correct number of times to say one.

“*If a thing down there is held up by a person doing a job. And the bottom nine steps are held up by two people about once a year. And a chamber is held up by two hands once a day, then who is holding the part in the gallery up.*”

“*That is the second time. A woman of forty-four has said out loud that two is the correct number of times to say one. And I am not going to say it a third time. And a man of thirty-one who has said a thing twice on a bank and been stopped by a woman of forty-four is a man who has learned one thing in about nine days.*”

“*And I am not going to answer it and I have not answered it, and the answer I have just been given about a door is not an answer to it. And I would like about four of you to notice that I have said that out loud twice now and that the second time I have said it is because a room was about to do it for me.*”

Then the two people in the doorway and in the reed were counted out loud. Because a man of sixty-one has said that a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place. And both of them are in a place and neither of them is in a room.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week. And that is thirty-nine days, and thirty-nine is six hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in the reed on the other side of that landing since about the second hour of the day before he lay down. And that is forty days, and forty is six hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*And the man in the ash said one word on the Friday of the hundred and sixteenth week and has not said one since. And the woman in the reed said one word in the same week and has not said one since that either. And I am not going to add the two numbers together and I have not done it this week.*”

Nobody in that bank walked over to either of them. A boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and he did not go down. And that is sixteen days since the Friday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and there is no form in this county that says he may.

Then a man of sixty-one said the one thing about the door that he wanted said and nobody else had said, and he said it flat and in about five sentences.

“*That door is not shut. It has not been shut since anybody in this county has been alive and I am not going to be a man who is glad about a door.*”

“*A door that a person opens because a person is doing a job is not a door that anybody can be sent for about, and that is the whole of what it is. And about four years from now somebody is going to be glad that it has been open every day. And about nine people are going to have been in this bank the whole time, and the gladness is going to be the thing that is sent for about.*”

Nobody in that bank said a word about that either.

---

The fever was fifty-five weeks and one day old. He had been in that county one hundred and fifteen days.

A question was put out loud on a Sunday morning: can about nine people hold open a thing that about four hundred years of one-person jobs have been holding. The working was given. And one million three hundred and fourteen thousand hours divided by nine is a hundred and forty-six thousand hours each. And a hundred and forty-six thousand hours at nine hours a day is about sixteen thousand days, and about forty-four years, and that is a figure of nine people who have not been born.

The answer was no, in a very short sentence, from a man of forty-one who works, and the other half of it was that somebody has to keep coming.

And about nine people did not like that and nobody improved on it. And a man of thirty-one said his own question for the second time and said he is not going to answer it. And a man of sixty-one said he was not going to be a man who is glad about a door.
