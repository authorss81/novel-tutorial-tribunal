# Chapter 693: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-First Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which The First Of The Nine Is Put Back Together Out Of Five Things That Are All In That Bank And None Of Which Is A Name, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Keeps A Bakehouse Is Asked A Second Time And Says Out Loud What It Has Cost Her To Be Asked Twice And Will Not Say Which Of The Two Cost More

Second day of the hundred and twenty-first week. His four hundred and forty-third morning. Two hundred and ninety-three days after the settlement. The fever fifty-eight weeks and four days. Two hundred and forty-seven days since the division. One hundred and ninety-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, because that is what he does now and he is not going to be thanked for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. He gave the pair of figures out loud on about twelve mornings and then stopped, and he has said he has nothing to say about a night three times.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Two days since it came off those steps. I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And it was about four inches at this hour yesterday, and that is the working.*”

“*I am not going to call that a pair of figures. It is a bank and a morning, and I have told this bank which of the two I am giving.*”

About four people on that bank had the four inches and about nine of them had the eight.

Then the man of forty-one said what he was going to try, and it was not going down, and it was the first time in three weeks he had said that.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine. He has a worn place in a floor that is not his and he has not put his own boot in it.

“*I am not going down this morning. I have a ninth of nine and there is a person standing in the middle of that room and I am not going to walk past them twice today as though they are a thing in the way.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one wants to put one of the nine back together. He is going to do it up there on that bank with about nine of us standing round him.*”

“*And I am going to tell him where he can put it, and I am not going to help him.*”

Nobody on that bank asked him where he was going to be allowed to put it, and about four of them were glad and about nine of them were not.

Then a man of thirty-one said the whole shape of what he was going to try, and he said it before he started. A woman of thirty-eight stopped him once and let him go on.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has offered three times in about two weeks to be the thing that is sent for and has been refused three times out loud.

“*A term is a job with a name on it. A man of forty-one said that in a passage on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week and he is the only person in this bank who has got a better one than I have.*”

“*I have got no name. I have got five things in that bank and I am going to read all five of them out loud on this bank and then I am going to say the job, and the name is going to be a space and everybody here is going to be able to see the space.*”

Then the woman of thirty-eight said the one sentence that stops a man of thirty-one making a shape out of a number. She has said one like it three times this fortnight.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing. About eleven people in four houses eat out of it, and one of the eleven is her mother.

“*You have not got five things. You have got five things that people who cannot be asked anything have left in a room.*”

“*And I say that because I turn a wheel once a day before the light and I have done it for about nine years.*”

“*The lintel ninety steps back says two hands, once a day, before the light. The ninth opening has one hand over it.*” She stopped there. “*And one is not a smaller version of two. I have been made to say that to a man of thirty-one twice in a fortnight and I am not going to say it a third time to a man of thirty-one on a Wednesday.*”

Nobody on that bank argued with her and about four of them had the lintel and about nine of them had the ninth opening.

Then the man of thirty-eight read the lintel out loud again, because it is ninety steps back and no hand can write it. Then the man of forty-one gave the rest of it. The five things were said out loud one under another in the wind.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has been down under about ninety steps of bank five times in a fortnight.

“*The hand in that lintel is a hand and not a letter. It has a forearm and four fingers, and the fourth is cut off level, and the cut is old.*”

“*And there is a guild mark on it, and a man of sixty-six has said three times that a guild mark is a different mark from the mark over the openings.*”

“*And the line cut under that hand says two hands, once a day, before the light.*”

“*I have read it out loud four times in six weeks. I am not going to read it a fifth time in a bank with water in it.*”

The man of forty-one gave the other three, and he gave them in the order he would have to do them in.

“*One. A door-stone about five feet high and about two feet across at the top, set in a groove, and there is water under it, and it takes two people to swing it.*”

“*Two. A hole in a ceiling about as big as a hat, and a stone under it about as big as a man can lift alone.*”

“*And about nine feet of groove worn in the floor, from the hole to the far wall.*”

“*Three. The mark over the opening, which is two hands cut side by side with a space between them about as wide as my thumb, cut square, cut to the depth of the hands, and old.*”

“*And that is the five of them, and there is no man in any of them and no woman in any of them.*”

“*A man of sixty-six has told us there is no numeral on the mark, and a bar under it would be a guild mark, and this is not that.*”

About nine people on that bank had the five things and about four of them had the space.

Then the man of thirty-one put the job together out loud on that bank, and a man of sixty-one counted him on his own knee in the wind, and gave the count out loud before anybody had asked him for it.

“*Two people. Once a day. Before the light.*”

“*That is three of the five things, and I have said them as a count because they are a count.*”

“*Two people is a figure of hands and not a figure of anybody. A man of sixty-one wrote a nine in a book on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week and would not write nine people next to it.*”

“*About a minute is a figure of a man of forty-one’s forearm and not a figure of a day.*”

“*About four hundred years is a figure of a guild mark cut in a lintel. A man of sixty-six has said three times that a guild mark is not the mark over the openings.*”

“*The name is a space about as wide as his thumb. It is cut square. It is cut to the depth of the hands and it is old, and there is nothing in it, and that is the whole of the first of the nine.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and the last of the five is a number he wrote on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week, and he has said in front of about nine people that he will not write a sixth.

“*Twelve. He has said twelve and I counted twelve and I counted them on my knee in the wind like a man counting sheep and I am not ashamed of that in front of these people.*”

“*And a term is a job with a name on it, and the job is: two people, once a day, before the light, about a minute, and about four hundred years, and there is nobody. That is the first of the nine and I am not going to write it in this book.*”

Then he said why, and he said the same three reasons, and he said he was not going to give a fourth. About four people on that bank had been waiting nine days to see whether he would.

“*I gave three reasons on the Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I have watched the thing they are about get worse for nine days.*”

“*And three is the number of reasons that kind of refusal has.*”

“*The third one is the whole of it and I am not going to improve on it. The day I start writing down what I have been told about in a place I have not been, this book stops being a book of what happened.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying no on a Wednesday when I said no on a Monday, and I am not going to be forgiven for it.*”

“*And a book that has a first of the nine in it is a book with a first of the nine in it in about four years.*”

Then the man of thirty-one said the thing that made it a finding. It was the third time in three weeks he had got it wrong, and this time a man of forty-one took it off him before he had finished it.

“*That is the first of the nine found.*”

“*It is not found.*” The man of forty-one did not raise his voice. “*It is put back together. I said that word on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week and I said it is a term found as an absence and not as a document, and I have been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and I know the difference in my hands.*”

“*You have got a job with no name on it. That is not a man down.*”

“*And the word for what you have done is put back together, and I will not say found. I will not be the man who says it, and nobody thanks me for saying it either.*”

Nobody on that bank said he was right, and nobody on that bank said he was wrong, and about four of them had both of those in their heads.

Then a man of thirty-one went up a cart road at about the eighth hour and asked a woman one question. He said out loud why he was asking it and what he was asking, and he said it before he knocked.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road. About nine houses take her bread, about sixty loaves go through her oven in a night, and about forty of them go up that track.

“*I am going to ask you when it gets light. That is the whole of the question and I am telling you what it is about before I ask it.*”

“*It is about a door nine miles under a river. It is not about your bread and it is not about the last time. I asked you about a door on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week and I cost you a morning and you said so on the Sunday and I have not forgotten it, and I am going to do the same thing again because I have a job with a hole in it and it is the only person in this county who can fill it.*”

And she answered him at the door, in the wind, with a tray in her hands. It took about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading.

“*It gets light here about a quarter of an hour before a man of thirty-eight can read two copies nine miles apart. It gets light in that room about the same. It gets light on my bakehouse floor about the same.*”

“*And I have known that for nineteen years without ever once being asked for it.*”

“*You are the second person to ask me about a door. That is two. And I am going to say the two out loud so that they are a figure and not a feeling.*”

“*One was the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I have given you what it cost.*”

“*It was a morning of bread, about nine houses a day late, and about four of the nine who cannot do a day without it. The man of eighty-one is one of the four, and his Thursday was three days after that Sunday.*”

“*Two is this. And two is not a smaller number than one and it is not a bigger one either, and I am not going to say which of the two cost more, and if I say it I will be saying it about myself and not about you.*”

Nobody on that cart road said a word back to her about it, and nobody said the word sorry. About four people there did not look at her and let her get the tray inside.

Then she said the last of it, on the step, with the door open behind her, and she said it before he had gone.

“*I am not going to be thanked for answering. And I am not going to be told it was worth the walk, and if anybody says that to me I will stand here and let them say it and then it will have been said and I will not have said it.*”

“*And the next time one of you comes up this road about a door, you come to this door. That is all I am going to say about it and I have said it.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said one thing about a book, and it was not about the woman on the step. She said it standing in the wind, and she said she had checked it a sixth time.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She writes two numbers a day in a day book in her own hand and is not one of the nine.

“*I have checked it a sixth time this morning and it has not moved. Nobody in this county has said out loud that a lamp on a hook in the ninth of nine rooms and the thing that keeps the record in four counties are the same thing.*”

“*And I am still not going to be the one who says it. And it is still not because it is not true, and I would like it recorded that I have now said the same sentence six times and have not said it once.*”

Then they came up the cart road, and about nine people stood about on a bank with about eight inches of water over the top step. A man of sixty-one said the thing that is going to be a problem, and said it was not going to be one this week and that he would rather say so before anybody found it out.

“*A thing said out loud in front of about nine people is a figure of about nine people. That is not a complaint, it is a count, and about four of you have the twelve sentences and about nine of you have the five things.*”

“*And none of the nine is a name, and I am not going to put a space in a book.*”

“*A man of fifty-four who cannot read is going to be the only person anybody can be sent for about the whole of this, and he has said that himself, and I am not going to say it for him.*”

About four people on that bank had the space and about nine of them had the two.

---

The fever was fifty-eight weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and thirty-nine days.

The first of the nine was put back together out of five things on a bank in the wind. The five things are a lintel ninety steps back, a door-stone about five feet high, about nine feet of worn groove, a hole about as big as a hat, and a mark with a space in it about as wide as a thumb.

And a man of sixty-one counted twelve sentences and refused to write any of them. And a man of forty-one said it is not found, it is put back together. And a woman of forty-four was asked for the second time in eleven days, gave the two out loud as a figure, and would not say which of the two cost more.
