# Chapter 666: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Seventeenth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which A Woman Of Forty Who Keeps No Book Says No Out Loud To A Reasonable Request, And Nobody On That Bank Argues Her Out Of It

Third day of the hundred and seventeenth week. His four hundred and sixteenth morning. Two hundred and sixty-six days after the settlement. The fever fifty-four weeks and five days. Two hundred and twenty days since the division. One hundred and sixty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The man of sixty-one asked her at about the seventh hour on the Thursday morning and he asked her in front of about nine people and he said three things before he asked it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years, and there are four lines in that book on the leaf after the last head and a fifth line that is not there.

“*The nine went down on the Tuesday and eight of the nine can go down on the Thursday. That is the request and I am going to make it plainly and then I am going to stop talking.*”

“*You have been down once. On the Friday of the hundred and fifteenth week, with about nine people, and you have never been asked for anything since and you have never asked for anything.*”

“*And I am asking you and not telling you, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be told. And I am saying that out loud before the question so that nobody in this bank can say afterwards that I did not.*”

About nine people on that bank heard the three things. Nobody on that bank said the request was not reasonable, because it was reasonable, and that is the whole of the difficulty.

Then she said no, and she said it out loud, and it was the first time in about four days that anybody in that county had said no to a thing that was asked fairly.

Wenna Callow is forty. She is on no board and there is no board and she says what everybody is standing in front of before she says anything else, and she has asked for a day against a name for about nine weeks.

“*No, and I am going to give the reason before anybody in this bank starts making it into a different reason, because I have watched a room do that and I have been the one it was done to.*”

“*What you have is nine people who agreed to a thing on a Sunday with no seal and no list, and every one of you knows what a thing with no seal and no list is. And about four of you said it out loud on the Sunday and it was a man of sixty-one, and he was not wrong about it.*”

“*I have been asking for a day against a name for about nine weeks and there is nothing in this county that will give me one, and on the Sunday the same man of sixty-one said that a coalition of nine is a bigger Crown that binds nobody, and he said it out loud so that nobody could have it later.*”

“*And here is what a Crown that binds nobody does: it does not bind anybody, and after about four years there is one person in it that everything goes through, not because anybody decided that and because there is no column and the only place a thing can be kept is a person, and then that person is the only place the nine are.*”

“*And then anybody who wants to know what the nine agreed has to be able to reach that person, and anybody who wants to be one of the nine has to be able to reach that person, and the nine does not stop existing and it stops being nine.*”

“*That is eight sentences and I have one left, and I am not going to spend it on how I feel about it.*”

“*The ninth is this. The reason is not about me and it is about what happens in about four years to a room of nine people who liked each other on a Sunday, and I would like about four of you to hear that and about nine of you to hear the number in it.*”

About four people on that bank had all nine sentences. The rest of them had a no.

Then the man of thirty-one opened his mouth to say something and did not say it, and everybody on that bank saw him not say it. And he said out loud that he was not going to and why. And the why was about a thing he had been told on the Wednesday of the hundred and sixteenth week in about four sentences in a yard.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one and he had been in that county one hundred and twelve days, and one hundred and twelve is six hundred and sixty-six less five hundred and fifty-four, and he was nobody’s.

“*I was going to say about four sentences and I am not going to say them and I would like about four of you to notice that I have been in this county a hundred and twelve days and I still want to argue with a woman of forty who has said no.*”

“*And I am not going to, and the reason is not that she is right, because I do not know that she is and I have not got a way of finding out from where I am standing.*”

“*The reason is that a man of about fifty-two told me on the Wednesday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a thing that is sent for is a thing people are sent for about. And he said it in four sentences and he was right. And this is not the same reason and she is not the instrument of anything, and a man who cannot stop arguing with a person who is not his instrument has not learned one single thing in a hundred and twelve days.*”

Then the man of sixty-one said what would happen next, and it was about five sentences, and nobody on that bank argued with the woman of forty while he said them or after.

“*Then it is eight. That is the whole of what happens next and I am not going to go and find a ninth. And if I go and find a ninth I am doing the thing she has just described and I am doing it on purpose.*”

“*And nobody is going to say a word to her about it, and I would like about nine of you to notice that I am including myself in that. Because I am the one with the book and I am the one it would go to.*”

Then about four people on that bank said the word coward, and about nine of them said the other thing. And the man of thirty-one heard both and said the second one out loud so that it would be in somebody’s mouth besides his own.

“*Nobody on this bank is going to say the word coward, and I would like it said that I have not said it either, and that I have wanted to say it every day of about nine days and did not, and I am not going to say why.*”

“*And nobody is going to thank her for saying no, and I would like about four of you to notice that a man of thirty-one who has been counting refusals in this county for about nine days is not going to put this one in the count. And I do not know what to do with it instead and that is the first time in a hundred and twelve days I have had a refusal I did not want to hold.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her. Nobody on that bank said the word either. The word stayed where it had been before she opened her mouth.

Then a woman of thirty-eight was asked to go down for the fourth time in about nine days and said yes in about four seconds. And a man of thirty-one said out loud that he had noticed the four seconds and was not going to make anything of them.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and about eleven people in four houses eat out of it and one of the eleven is her mother. And on Monday she said out loud that she is not keeping a week, she is keeping the fact that the peg has a hole for it.

“*Yes, and I did not stop to think about it and I would like somebody to notice that I did not stop to think about it. And I have been not stopping to think about it for about nine years and it has not gone badly.*”

Then the man of thirty-one said the thing he could not fill, out loud, in front of about nine people. And it was the eighth of the nine jobs and he had read it out loud on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week.

“*The eighth of the nine is a man of thirty-one who is nobody’s, to be the one that is sent for. I read it out loud on the Saturday of the hundred and sixteenth week and I said it could not be filled and I meant it. And today there were eight instead of nine and it is the eighth thing on that list that would have filled it, and I am not going to offer it again.*”

“*A person who cannot be sent for about is not a spare pair of hands. I have known that since the Wednesday of the hundred and sixteenth week, and I have had about eight days to find out whether I could make it be anything else and I could not.*”

About four people on that bank had the whole of that. The rest of them had a woman of forty standing on a bank with a no still in her mouth.

Then the eight went down at about the ninth hour, and the man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and a quarter, and the board went on its stick by two people at about the second hour of the afternoon. And a boy of thirteen was at the head of the flight with a barrow and did not go down. And it is thirteen days since he went down on the Friday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and there is no form in this county that says he may.

---

The fever was fifty-four weeks and five days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twelve days.

A woman of forty said no out loud, in front of about nine people, to a request that was made plainly and fairly by a man of sixty-one who said three things before he made it. And her reason was that a coalition of nine with no seal and no list becomes in about four years a thing with one person in it that everything goes through, and she said it in nine sentences and the last one was not about her.

Nobody on that bank argued her out of it, and nobody on that bank thanked her. And nobody on that bank said the word. And a man of thirty-one said out loud that a person who cannot be sent for about is not a spare pair of hands and that he was not going to offer to be one again.

And eight people went down instead of nine.
