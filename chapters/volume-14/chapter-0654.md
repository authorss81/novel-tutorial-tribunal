# Chapter 654: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Fifteenth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which About Ninety Steps Go Down, And A Stone Door About Five Feet High Takes Two People To Swing, And There Is A Line Cut Under A Hand In A Lintel Saying Two Hands Once A Day Before The Light

Fifth day of the hundred and fifteenth week. His four hundred and fourth morning. Two hundred and fifty-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-three weeks. Two hundred and eight days since the division. One hundred and fifty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The last nine of the ninety steps were under about four feet of water. The man of thirty-eight went down them first with a rope round his middle and a cut-down pail. He bailed for about a quarter of an hour before anybody else put a foot in.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge. He has a child of nine. He has been on a wet floor in the dark for nine hours of a morning before, and he knows what it is.

“*Nine steps. About four feet. And a pail takes three goes to empty a step and about nine steps wants about twenty-seven goes and a man of thirty-eight wants about nine minutes a go.*”

“*That is about four hours and a quarter of bailing and none of it is a hearing and none of it is a book and I would like it said that I have not been thanked for it and am not going to be.*”

Nobody in that passage thanked him for the pail.

The bottom of the steps is a floor, and the floor is about nine feet back from the last step. There is a passage in it going along. The passage is about as tall as a man and about as wide as two men, and it goes on in the dark for as far as anybody has ever been.

Then the first door. It is a stone about five feet high and about two feet across at the top and rather less at the bottom, set in a groove cut in the floor. It takes two people to swing, because there is water under it and because it weighs what a stone that size weighs.

Nobody in that passage said anything about the weight. Two of them just stood on it and got it round.

Over the door there is a hand cut in the lintel. It is a hand and not a letter. There is a forearm, and the fingers are four and not five, and the fourth is cut off level, and the cut is old.

Under the hand there is a line cut in the same stone in the same hand. About four of the nine in that passage could not read it. About nine of them could not see it, because the lamp was behind them.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years. He said it before anybody asked, and he said it to the room and not to the man with the lamp.

“*I cannot read, and I have not said so this week, and a question about a thing cut in stone is a question about reading, and about four of us in here cannot and about nine of us can.*”

Nobody in that passage asked him to read it.

The man of sixty-one read it, and he read it with the working, and he counted the people who could see it first.

“*It says two hands, once a day, before the light.*”

“*And that is nine words and about nine of us heard them and about four of us have the whole of what that is.*”

About four people in that passage had the line. The rest of them had a hand with four fingers on it.

Then the man of sixty-six said what the four-fingered hand is, and he said it flat, and he said he was a tradesman and that a tradesman knows.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water and has been down those steps twice, the second time forty-one years ago when he was twenty-five.

“*That is not a person cutting a mark. That is a guild cutting a mark, and there were about four of them in that trade in the time of my grandfather, and three of them were in this county and one was over the water, and I have heard that from a man who heard it from a man.*”

“*I am not telling you it is true. I am telling you that is where I got it, and that the man is dead about thirty years and there is nobody left to ask.*”

“*And about four of you have that as a fact and about nine of you have it as a story, and both of those are correct and I am not going to spend the morning deciding which.*”

About four people in that passage had the guild. About nine had the tradesman.

Then the man of sixty-one gave the sum, and he refused it first and then gave it, and he said why, and that is the whole of what a man of sixty-one is for.

“*Four hundred years is about a hundred and forty-six thousand days, and once a day is once, and that is a figure of a stone cut by somebody and it is not a figure of anybody in this passage.*”

“*I am not going to write that number in a book of heads and carts. And if I do write it I am going to cut a second column in the back of the last leaf, and the first column is a number of people, and I am asking again whether a man of sixty-one may.*”

About four minutes went past in the dark and nobody answered and nobody said no, and about nine people in that passage watched a man of sixty-one not write a number down.

“*And I would like it said that the refusal to write it is mine and is not a form, and that I have been treating a nothing as a reason for about two years and I have stopped.*”

Then the door was got round, and the chamber was inside. The chamber is a day. It took about nine people about four minutes to work out what it was, and the four minutes were the fourth hour of the morning.

There is a hole in the ceiling about as big as a hat.

There is a groove worn in the floor, running about nine feet from the foot of the steps to the far wall. It is worn and polished, and it is worn the way a thing gets worn when it is done every day for a long time.

There is a stone standing under the hole. The stone is about as big as a man can lift alone, and it weighs what a stone that size weighs.

“*Push it away from the hole, and the light falls on the stone, and the chamber is lit, and that is a day.*”

“*Push it back under the hole and the chamber is dark, and dark is the end of it, and the hole is at the near end and the groove is about nine feet and the far end is about nine feet away.*”

“*The whole of this chamber is a stone, a hole, about nine feet of worn floor, and two people who turn up.*”

About four people in that chamber had it. About nine had a room with a hole in it.

Then the man of thirty-one said what he wanted, and he said it out loud, and it was the first want he had said out loud in four counties in two days.

“*I am going to ask who cut it, and I am saying that first so that about four of you know what I am going to ask before I ask it.*”

Nobody in that chamber moved.

“*And I am going to be wrong, and I know I am going to be wrong, because about four of you have already said the word guild and about nine of you have said the word hand, and both of those are about a person, and the stone in front of us is not about a person at all.*”

Then a woman of thirty-eight told him the true of it, and she said it in about four sentences, and she said it about the floor and not about the lintel.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and she is going down for the second time, and she has not said yet that she will come down again.

“*Nobody knows who cut it. I have been in this chamber in about nine years and about nine times and I have asked about four people and the answer was always the guild and nobody could name a man.*”

“*And the chamber is not about a person. The chamber is about the stone and the hole and the nine feet and the two people, and if you go at it as if there is a man behind it you will be in here about four years and you will still not know who.*”

“*That is three sentences and the fourth is that a man who came down here to find a builder has come down for nothing, and I have come down for the floor, and the floor is worn, and the wear is the only thing in here that is true.*”

About four people in that chamber had the wear. The rest of them had the guild.

Then the man of thirty-one offered to be one of the two hands, and he was refused in about four sentences by the same woman, and the reason was about him and not about the chamber.

“*No.*”

About four seconds went past. The lamp moved about an inch.

“*That stone at the near end is where a day starts. Two people push it and the light is on it and the day has begun and it has been begun about a hundred and forty-six thousand times.*”

“*And if a man who is in no book in four counties is one of the two hands today, then today is his and not the chamber’s, and a day with a man in it that nobody can be sent for about is a day nobody can check.*”

“*That is four sentences and the last one is the reason and the reason is about you, and I would like about four of you to notice that I have not said one word about whether you could do it.*”

Nobody in that chamber said a word to her about it.

Then two of the nine did it. It took about a minute. The stone went about nine feet along a groove that has been worn by that exact motion, and the light came onto it, and the chamber was lit.

“*The man of forty-one and the woman of forty. And the woman of forty has asked for a day against a name for about nine weeks and there is a day on that floor and neither of them is the same thing and both of them are today.*”

Nobody in that chamber thanked either of them for the day.

Then the man of thirty-one said the true of one half of a thing, and nobody stopped him, and he noticed that nobody stopped him, and he said so out loud, and it was the nineteenth time he had done it in any room and the first time nobody had held him.

“*I said the true of one half of a thing in a chamber under a bank and about nine people heard it and nobody held me, and about four of you know what that means and about nine of you do not.*”

“*It means the holding was never about the sentence. It was about the room. And this is not that room, and I have been counting the times as if it were about me, and it was not.*”

About four people in that chamber had the room. The rest of them had a stone at the far end of a groove.

Then the day’s work was put back, because the light was going. It took about a minute. The stone came about nine feet the other way and went under the hole, and the chamber was dark, and nobody in it said anything for about as long as it takes to pole a quarter mile.

“*And that is the whole of it. We did a day and we undid it and nothing happened, and tomorrow somebody will do it again, and the undoing is the part nobody thanks anybody for.*”

About four people in the dark had the undoing. About nine had the dark.

---

The fever was fifty-three weeks old. He had been in that county one hundred days, and a chamber about nine feet deep and as wide as two men is a day, and it is kept by two hands once a day before the light, in a hand that has four fingers on it and a fourth cut off level.

And a line cut under that hand says so in nine words, and a man of sixty-one read it out loud and gave the sum and then refused to write the sum down.

And a man of thirty-one offered to be one of the two hands and was refused in about four sentences by a woman of thirty-eight, and two other people did it, and it took about a minute, and then it was put back, and the chamber went dark, and nothing fell down.
