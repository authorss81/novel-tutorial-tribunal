# Chapter 678: First Day Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Beam About Nine Feet Long Goes Along About Two Inches And Comes Back When There Is A Person On The Steps And Does Not Move When There Is Not, And Nobody In That Gallery Says What That Means

First day of the hundred and nineteenth week. His four hundred and twenty-eight morning. Two hundred and seventy-eight days after the settlement. The fever fifty-six weeks and three days. Two hundred and thirty-two days since the division. One hundred and seventy-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps by the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and then gave the figure that goes with the reading, and it is the eleventh morning running.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has given a reading on eleven mornings out of the last eleven.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. That is three days from full on the Saturday. It empties in about three days and it fills in about four.*”

“*And the pair of figures has not moved once in eleven mornings, and I would like about four of you to have noticed that it is the same pair and not two pairs.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight did two counts on that bank before anybody had thought of asking her to, and she said out loud that she kept a day book and that a day book is a thing people lie to.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week. She has been in that county thirty-six days, and thirty-six is six hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and forty-two.

“*One hundred and twenty-four days for him. Six hundred and seventy-eight less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I have written both of those down and I have given him his copy with my own hand. There is no other hand between. I am going to go on doing it, and nobody is going to thank me for it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her and a man of thirty-one took the paper and did not read it in front of anybody.

Then the nine went down. The man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and a quarter and said nothing about it. The man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his.

The gallery is about nine feet high and about as wide as a room with a hearth in it, and there are about nine beams in the roof of it set about four feet apart, and eight of them are still. And the ninth goes along about two inches and comes back, and there was no person in the room.

About four people in that gallery said the word out loud, the way they had said it on four mornings in about eleven days. Nobody in that gallery argued with them.

Then the man of thirty-eight put his lamp on the floor and stood up under the ninth beam with his arm out and the lamp in his hand at the height of it, and he said what he was going to be doing for the next while and asked for it to be counted.

“*I am going to hold this up at the height of that beam. I am going to be here about a quarter of an hour, and I am going to have to put it down about four times to get my shoulder back in.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to count the four and not the quarter of an hour. The quarter of an hour is mine. The four are not.*”

About four people in that gallery counted the four.

Then the man of thirty-eight went up the ninety steps to fetch a coil of rope out of the passage and it took him about four minutes, and the lamp was standing on the floor of the gallery the whole time and nobody touched it.

And the ninth beam did not move for the whole of those four minutes.

And when he came back down, about nine steps at a time and not quietly, the ninth beam went along about two inches and came back.

Then the man of forty-one went up, and it took him about the same, and it did not move while he was on the steps either, and it did not move while he was in the passage.

And when he came back down into the gallery the ninth beam went along about two inches and came back.

Then nobody went up at all. About nine people stood in that gallery and looked at that beam for about as long as it takes a man of thirty-eight to pole a quarter mile, and it did not move.

About four people in that gallery had the control. About nine of them had nothing.

Then the man of thirty-one said the one sentence he was going to say about it, and he said it standing nine feet off with his hands behind his back.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he was nobody’s, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and no form that renews him.

“*It goes when a person is on the steps and it does not go when a person is in the room. That is two trials and one that did not move anybody, and I am not going to supply the other half of it.*”

“*And I am not going to say it, and it is not that I have not worked out what it is. It is that I have worked out the half of it that has two trials behind it. I have not got one person in four counties who is entitled to hear the other half from me.*”

Then he said the one sentence he has had in him since the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and he said it out loud, and it was not about the beam.

“*I have had five things in me for about two weeks and I have not said one of them, and I am going to say one now and then I am going to stop carrying the other four, and this is the one and it is not about that beam.*”

“*It is that I have spent one hundred and twenty-four days in this county counting what I know. I have never once counted what I do not know.*”

“*And that is the only figure anybody has ever wanted out of me, and I am not going to give it to anybody. I am not going to make a sentence of refusing it either.*”

Nobody in that gallery said a word about that.

Then the man of thirty-eight put his lamp down and said what it had cost his arm. He said it flat, and he did not wait to be thanked. He had said four times that he was not going to be thanked, and he had said that stopping saying it was a different thing from stopping doing it.

“*A quarter of an hour and about four times. A man of forty-one counted the four and nobody counted the quarter of an hour. My shoulder is going to be no good for carrying a pole up that water on Thursday, and that is a figure of my shoulder and not of anybody else.*”

“*And I am not going to say what I was doing up there for four minutes. I did not plan it and I have not said it out loud. About four of you were standing in this gallery the whole time and not one of you said anything to me about it either.*”

About four people in that gallery had heard a man of thirty-eight say that nobody had asked him anything about the thing he did.

Then the man of forty-one said the thing about the roof, and it was three sentences, and it was the nineteenth year of him not having been asked.

“*Nineteen years and I have never once seen anything come out of that roof. Not a stone and not a lump and not a piece of the wall at the far end.*”

“*And I had assumed like about nine people standing in here that somebody was holding it up, and I have got to thinking about that on the Wednesday night and I have not put it down, and I am not going to put it down in a passage with a lamp in it.*”

Then the man of thirty-one said the thing that had to be said on a Tuesday morning, in about ten sentences. He said who was not to be forgiven, and why.

“*Nobody in this county is to be forgiven for anything that happened last week or this week. I am saying that in a passage with nine people in it and I am including myself in it first.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well was told on the Sunday of the hundred and sixteenth week that a thing that is sent for is a thing people are sent for about. He was right. It turned out to be the true of a whole set of doors about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank.*”

“*Being right is not a thing anybody is thanked for. It is not a thing anybody is forgiven for either, and I do not want anybody in this gallery to go up that bank and say that man was right.*”

“*And I offered to be the thing that is sent for three times in about two weeks and was refused three times. I am not to be forgiven for that either.*”

“*And I am not going to be forgiven for a fourth, because I have not offered it and I am not going to.*”

Then the woman of thirty-eight said what the whole of it is not, and it was ten sentences, and it was about a thing nobody had asked her about.

Aveline Marsh is thirty-eight and keeps a mill four miles off that landing. About eleven people in four houses eat out of it and one of the eleven is her mother.

“*None of it is a way for anybody to say no. That is what I have been asked about four times since the Saturday, and I am going to answer it once.*”

“*A thing that a person wrote down on a morning they were not there does not give anybody a way to be told they are not wanted. It is the other way round.*”

“*It means that if a person stops, the line is still there in the stone, and nobody has to be told.*”

“*And I am not being hard. I am a woman who turns a wheel once a day, and if I stop then the peg goes into a plain hole on the seventh morning. About four years from now nobody in four counties will ever know that chamber was a week.*”

“*I have already had that. I am not having it again for a stone with nine lines in it.*”

And a man of sixty-one said the other half of that, on the bank, after everybody was up, and it was about a form and about four years.

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether they will still come, and I am not going to cut one.*”

“*A woman of forty-four said on the Sunday of the hundred and seventeenth week that a list is a column and a column is a thing people get put in inside about four years, and I have watched that be right twice since, and I am not going to be shown a better reason today.*”

Nobody on that bank improved on that and nobody thanked him for it.

---

The fever was fifty-six weeks and three days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-four days.

A beam about nine feet long went along about two inches and came back while there was a person on about ninety steps. It did not go at all while there was a person standing in the room. It was done twice and controlled once, and about nine people in that gallery watched it and nobody said what it meant.

A man of thirty-eight held a lamp up on his arm at the height of that beam for about a quarter of an hour. He had to put it down about four times, and he asked for the four to be counted and not the quarter of an hour. His shoulder will not carry a pole up that water on a Thursday.

And a woman of thirty-eight said out loud that none of it is a way for anybody to say no. She said a thing written down in a stone on a morning a person was not there means that if a person stops, nobody has to be told.
