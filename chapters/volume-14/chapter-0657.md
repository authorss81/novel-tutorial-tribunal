# Chapter 657: The First Day Of The Hundred And Sixteenth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which Nine People Come Up About Ninety Steps Again, And A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud What Has Been Lost And Not How Long It Will Stay Lost

First day of the hundred and sixteenth week. His four hundred and seventh morning. Two hundred and fifty-seven days after the settlement. The fever fifty-three weeks and three days. Two hundred and eleven days since the division. One hundred and fifty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

They were on the water at about the eleventh hour and the last nine steps were bailed again, and it took the man of thirty-eight about four hours and a quarter to do it the second time in four days, and nobody on that bank thanked him for either.

“*Twenty-seven goes. Three goes a step. And a man of thirty-eight is slower the second day than the first and I have told you why and I am not going to tell you again.*”

“*Nine hours of a boat and a pole on the Friday and about nine hours on the Tuesday, and nine minutes of reading on the Friday and nine minutes on the Monday, and the count of that is of Thursdays and the reading is separate from the boat and nobody has ever asked me for any of it.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said what has been lost, in about nine sentences, and he said it about a stone and not about a man, and he said it in the open on a bank with about nine people standing about.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years, and he had put four lines in that book the night before and had not told anybody he had done it.

“*A man of thirty-one has been in that county one hundred and three days, and one hundred and three is six hundred and fifty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four, and I am not doing it out of a book, and a day is a day.*”

“*And on the Wednesday of last week the water went over a block of limestone with a line cut round it, and the man counted from that block every morning for ninety-six days, and this morning the block is under about a foot and three inches of water and there is no figure in four counties for when it comes out again.*”

“*So a man has lost the thing he counted from. And I am not going to say how long for, because I do not know, and a figure of how long that is would be the second thing this week that nobody can check.*”

About four people on that bank had the block. The rest of them had the rope, wet.

“*And I put four lines in that book last night. I am going to say what book it is and why. About four of you will already know why and about nine of you will not.*”

“*It is the book of heads and carts, on the leaf after the last head, under the name of a man of sixty-six who is a sinker. It is the wrong book. There is no right one.*”

“*The reason I used the wrong one is that it is the only book in the county of Kell that anybody can be sent for about. That is the whole of the argument, and I have been arguing it with myself for nine days, and I have got to a figure of four lines on the wrong leaf, and that is where I have got to.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word about the wrong book.

Then the man of thirty-one said the count of people who cannot be sent for about, and he said it himself, and it was his own figure and not anybody else’s, and a man of sixty-one corrected the class of it without correcting the number.

“*There are four of us in four counties that nobody can be sent for about. I am one. A man of sixty-six who is a sinker is two. A woman of twenty-eight who came up a cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week is three. A woman of seventy-one who keeps a house that is not hers is four.*”

“*About four of you have the four. About nine of you have a man in an ash and a woman in a reed.*”

“*And I would like it said out loud that a man of sixty-one gave one of these on the Saturday of last week and a woman of seventy-one gave three on the same afternoon, and that mine is a different figure of a different thing and I am not going to use either of theirs for mine.*”

“*Mine is a count of four people in four counties over about nine days. It is not a figure of a county and it is not a figure of a year.*”

“*It is a figure of four people that a room of about nine found out about in about nine days. That is all it is and I would like it not to be made larger.*”

“*That is correct,*” said Nevin Trask.

“*Mine was a count of one person in this county at one moment on a Saturday morning. Yours is a count of four people in four counties over about nine days.*”

“*The first is a figure of one morning and the second is a figure of about nine days. Neither of them is a figure of a county, and I have been glad about mine for nine days, and I am not going to be glad about yours, because you have told me what it is.*”

About four people on that bank had the two figures kept apart. About nine had four.

Then a man of thirty-four was at the top of that bank in the doorway of an ash, and about nine of the nine people who had been down those steps had not known he was there, and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him to come out.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four. He has been in that ash since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week, which is a Wednesday, and he has not stood up once since, and he is on no board and in no book, and nobody can be sent for about him.

He said one thing. It was not a question about a bank or a boat or a beam.

“*Is the water over the stone.*”

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them.

“*It is,*” said Corvin Tace.

“*It went over at about the sixth hour on the Wednesday morning. About a foot and three inches above the cut, and I have been watching it since.*”

“*Nineteen years of watching a stone and the answer was four words and a man of forty-three said them.*”

“*And I have not been asked anything. I asked.*”

“*Nobody sent for me and nobody put a hand on my shoulder on the way up that bank. About four of you have been walking about all morning past a mouth that has been here for twenty-seven days, and about nine of you have not.*”

About four people on that bank looked at the ash. About nine had been looking at the rope.

Then a woman of twenty-nine said one word from the reed at the top of that landing. She has been in the reed since about the second hour of a Tuesday in the hundred and twelfth week, and it is twenty-eight days.

Nell Prince is twenty-nine and is the only person in that town who has taken a yes back in the open, and she has not stood up since she sat down, and she said one word and did not stand up to say it.

“*Both.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for twenty-eight days.

Then a woman of forty-four, who keeps a book of twenty-nine chairs and has kept it nineteen years, said the one thing about a book that a man of sixty-one had not thought of saying, and she said it in about four sentences, and about four of the people on that bank heard all of it and about nine heard the part about the chairs.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and is paid a hundred and sixty pence a year for the chairs book, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and she is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about a chair.

“*A man of sixty-one has put four lines about a gallery under a sinker’s name in a book of heads and carts. He has said out loud that it is the wrong book and that there is no right one, and he is right about both, and I am not going to let it stand as the whole of the answer.*”

“*There is a right book and it is the one I keep. It has twenty-nine chairs in it and a column for every person who has ever sat in one, and about four of the nine people who went down those steps this morning are in no column of it and never will be.*”

“*A line in a book of heads and carts says that a man of sixty-six is a head. A line in my book says that a woman of thirty-eight is a chair. Neither of those is what either of them is.*”

“*And both of those are the only two places in this county anybody can be sent for about a thing that was said in a room.*”

“*That is four sentences. I am not going to say what should be done about it, because there is nothing to be done about it, and I have said no three times to putting a hearing in that book, and I am not going to make a fourth refusal out of this.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word to her about the chairs.

Then the man of thirty-one said what he wanted. It was the first want he had said out loud in three days, and it was the wrong shape, and a man of sixty-one heard the shape of it and said so.

“*I am going to say what I want and it is about going back down, and I am going to say it before anybody offers it, and I would like about four of you to notice the shape of it.*”

About four people on that bank had the shape. About nine had the man.

“*When can I go back down.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word.

“*That is the wrong question and I know it is the wrong question about nine seconds after I have said it, and a man of sixty-one is going to tell me what the right one is, and I am not going to say it first because I have got it wrong three times in about nine days.*”

About four people on that bank had the three times. The rest of them had a man who was waiting to be corrected.

Then the board was put back on its stick by two people, because that is what it takes. The two were a man of forty-one and a man of thirty-eight, and the man of thirty-eight had bailed about four hours and a quarter twice in four days.

About nine people on that bank said the water is going to be on those nine steps in about four days. About four of them said nobody knows.

Nobody on that bank thanked the two men for the board.

---

The fever was fifty-three weeks and three days old. He had been in that county one hundred and three days. A man of sixty-one said out loud that a man has lost the thing he counted from, and said he was not going to say how long for, because he does not know.

And four lines went into a book of heads and carts on the leaf after the last head, under the name of a man of sixty-six who is a sinker, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that it is the wrong book and that there is no right one and that the reason he used it is that it is the only book in that county anybody can be sent for about.

And a man of thirty-four who has been in an ash for twenty-seven days and has not stood up asked one question, and it had an answer in four words. Nobody sent for him. Nobody put a hand on his shoulder.

And a woman of twenty-nine said one word from the reed and did not stand up to say it.
