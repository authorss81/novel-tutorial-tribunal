# Chapter 692: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-First Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which Nine Go Down And A Man Of Forty-One Looks At The Floor Of A Room He Has Been In And Out Of Twice A Day For Nineteen Years And Never Once At, And There Is A Worn Place In It That Is Not His, And Nobody In Four Counties Can Be Sent For About Which Of The Two Things It Is

First day of the hundred and twenty-first week. His four hundred and forty-second morning. Two hundred and ninety-two days after the settlement. The fever fifty-eight weeks and three days. Two hundred and forty-six days since the division. One hundred and ninety-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and stopped. Then he said the working out loud, because the man of thirty-one had tried to divide a door by a morning on the Monday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has a block of limestone with a line cut round it nine miles up this water, two crossings in his own hand, and no figure for the gap.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. One day since it came off those steps, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And it was off all ninety at the sixth hour yesterday, and that is where you get a figure from. I am not going to be asked for a night by a man who divides doors by mornings and I am not going to offer him one.*”

About four people on that bank had the working and about nine of them had the four inches.

Then they went down at about the first hour. The man of forty-one bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it. The man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his.

The gallery is about nine feet high and there are about nine beams set about four feet apart and eight of them are still. The ninth went along about two inches when the man of thirty-eight came down onto the last of the steps. It went along about two inches again when he came off them.

And about nine feet past the place where the gravel is walked, about nine people stopped. The man of forty-one was not going on, and he had been in and out of that door twice a day for nineteen years.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge. He put his lamp on the floor of the passage while the other of them went on.

“*Hold on. I have carried a lamp into that ninth room four times this week and I have not once looked at the floor.*”

“*And I have been in and out of it twice a day for nineteen years.*”

“*And I know what I am doing and I am going to say it before I do it.*”

“*A man of thirty-one on this bank has spent a fortnight telling us that a thing with no figure behind it does not go in a book. I have had a week of asking people about doors and I have got one dead man out of it.*”

Then he was in the ninth room for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading. He came out and said it, and he gave the count of it first and said what the count was a count of.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts, and a wheel on the near side has started to make a noise. He is the ninth of nine and has been in and out of the ninth door twice a day for nineteen years.

“*Nineteen years twice a day is a figure of mine and I am not going to multiply it out loud on a bank or in a room. It is a count of a pair of hands and it is not a count of anything anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*There is a worn place in the middle of that floor. About as wide as my two hands laid flat, side by side, and worn about as deep as the thickness of this finger.*” He put the flat of his own hand on it. “*And I measured it with my own finger. I am not going to round it and I am not going to say it is exactly that.*”

“*I have been the only person in that room for nineteen years and I know what a room with one person in it looks like on a floor.*”

Then he said the part that made it a finding, and he said it before anybody had asked him for it.

“*There is nothing in that room that a person has to stand in the middle of. The door is in the wall by the shelf and the groove in the floor is by the wall on the other side and the stone under the hole is at the end of the groove and I go the length of the room and back. Nobody stops in the middle of it.*”

“*And there is a place in the middle of that floor that is not mine. It is worn about as deep as my finger and it is as smooth as the rest of it.*”

“*And smooth means it is not being walked on. It is being stood on. And I have been going in and out of that room twice a day for nineteen years.*”

About four people in that room had the door-stone and about nine of them had the middle of the floor.

Then a man of thirty-one said the thing that made it a person. It came out of him before he had decided to say it, and it was wrong, and a man of thirty-eight took it back off him in two sentences.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. There is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it, no form that renews him, no rate on him and no hundred in that county either.

“*Then somebody is still doing it. Somebody is standing in the middle of that room at a time I have not been in it, and that is the finding and we can go and ask.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said it in front of about nine people. He was not cruel about it and he was not gentle about it either.

“*Take it back. You have made it a person because you want it to be a person and you have not got one thing in this county that says it is a person.*”

“*A worn place is a worn place. A floor that has been stood on for four hundred years is worn the same as a floor that has been stood on for nine days, and I have been in that room four times this week and I have not got a figure for either one and neither has anybody else in here.*”

“*And I have said the other half of that about a stone since the first week I came down here. A worn thing is the only thing in that bank nobody can lie about.*”

“*Nobody can cut wear and nobody can rub it out. That is all a wear says, and it is not who is wearing it.*”

Then the man of thirty-one took it back, standing in a passage about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank. He took it back in one sentence and did not put anything else in the place of it.

“*A worn place that is as deep as his finger. That is all of it. I said somebody is still doing it and I have not got a thing to say it with, and I am not going to say it again.*”

Then a man of sixty-six said the true of the two possibilities. He has been down under about ninety steps of bank twice in forty-one years. He is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about the word *loom*.

Abel Fenner is sixty-six and digs for water. He put his hand flat on the back wall of that room on the first day of the hundred and twentieth week and said it was not a wall. It was a hole that has been filled.

“*There are two of those and only two, and I am going to say both of them and I am not going to choose between them in front of you.*”

“*Either that place has been worn by somebody standing in it since the guild mark was cut, which is about four hundred years, and there is no figure in this county for the guild mark.*”

“*Or somebody stands in it.*”

“*And I have been in that room twice in forty-one years and I put my own thumb in that place this morning while nine of you were standing round it.*”

“*And I am not going to say how deep a floor is when it is old. That is a thing about my thumb and not about a room.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked which of the two it is and neither is anybody on this bank.*”

“*I would like about four people to be able to tell in nine days that nobody chose.*”

Nobody in that room chose, and about nine people went on standing in it, and the man of thirty-eight picked his lamp up off the floor.

Then a woman of forty-four said the sixth of the same sentence. She gave the count, and she asked for it to be said that she was not going to be thanked for the sixth.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. She is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*A thing with no person on either side of it is a thing that has not happened yet. Six times now, and it is the sixth and it is still the same sentence and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*And that one is about that place in that floor. I know it is and you all know it is. It is the first time I have said it about something I can see.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for the sixth. I am not going to be asked whether the seventh is due, and if a man of sixty-one asks me I am going to tell him the same two things I have told him every morning this fortnight.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said the one thing about it that he had been keeping for a week. He said it flat, and he said it before anybody had thought of asking him for it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head, and the last of the five is a number he wrote on the Saturday of the hundred and twentieth week.

“*I am not going to write either of the two down. I said on the Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week that I would not write down a wall I had not been to and I have not been to this floor either, and I have had eleven days to change my mind and I have not.*”

“*And the reason is the same reason and I am not going to give you a fourth reason for it. Three is the number of reasons that kind of refusal has and I gave them in ten sentences on a Monday and I have watched the thing they are about get worse ever since.*”

“*And there is a worse one and it is this. A book that says somebody stands in the middle of the ninth room is a book that has a somebody in it.*”

“*And in about four years that book is a column, and the column is him. And he cannot be sent for about anything, and neither can anybody else.*”

About four people in that room had the column and about nine of them had the floor.

Then a woman of twenty-eight said the thing she has said on and off for a fortnight, and she said it about her own two eyes. She was not in that room and said so.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week. She cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She is not one of the nine, and she keeps nothing anybody can be sent for about.

“*I have not been in that room this week and I am not going to be. I said that on a bank on the Sunday of the hundred and eighteenth week and about four of you were standing there.*”

“*And I am not going to be a keeper of a floor. I am not going to be a keeper of a wall, or a hole, or a lamp, or a stone with nine lines on it, and I have said that sentence four times since the Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week and I am not going to say a fifth about a floor.*”

“*And nobody is going to thank me for saying it on a Tuesday either.*”

“*And I would like it said that I am not going to be asked about the floor, because I have not decided not to be asked. There is nobody here who could.*”

Nobody in that passage asked her and nobody improved on it.

Then the man of forty-one said what he was going to do about the room, and it was not to stand in the middle of it. He said why in a sentence.

“*I am not going to stand in it to see if it wears. I have a woman of forty-four up this road four miles off, a mill four miles the other way, and two carts. A man of thirty-eight has a board he is the only one who may carry. I have a ninth of nine.*”

“*And I am not going to add a second job in a room where I do not know who is standing in it.*”

“*And I am not going to keep it. I am going to go in and out twice a day the way I have done for nineteen years.*”

“*Nobody thanks me for that and nobody is going to forgive me for it, because there is nothing to forgive and I have not said there is.*”

Then they came up, and the board went on its stick by two people because that is what it takes. A boy of thirteen went up that road with an empty barrow at about the fourth hour, because it is what he does.

---

The fever was fifty-eight weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and thirty-eight days.

There are two worn places in the floor of the ninth room. One of them is about as wide as a man of forty-one’s two hands, and one boot has been walked in and out of it twice a day for nineteen years. The other is about as deep as the thickness of his finger and is as smooth as the rest of the floor, and he has never once stood in it.

And a man of sixty-six put his own thumb in it and said there are two of those and only two. And a man of thirty-one said somebody is still doing it and took it back. And nobody in that room chose between a floor that has been worn for about four hundred years and a floor somebody stands on this morning.
