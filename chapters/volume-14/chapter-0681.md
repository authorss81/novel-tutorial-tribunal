# Chapter 681: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Man Of Forty-Three Gives A Reading And Says Out Loud That He Is Not Going To Give The Pair Of Figures Again, And The Ninth Line In A Stone Is Counted Back To A Thursday, And A Man Of Forty-One Says He Is Starting In The Morning

Fourth day of the hundred and nineteenth week. His four hundred and thirty-first morning. Two hundred and eighty-one days after the settlement. The fever fifty-six weeks and six days. Two hundred and thirty-five days since the division. One hundred and eighty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading. And he gave it without the other half, and he said why before anybody had thought of asking him.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and the block of limestone with a line cut round it is in that reach nine miles up this water. And he has two crossings of it in his own hand and no figure for the gap.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the sixth hour. Six days since it came off those steps, and I count from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself.*”

“*And that is a reading and not the pair. The pair of figures has been said out loud on more mornings than a man of forty-three can carry in his head, and a man of sixty-one is not writing any of it down, and I said on the Wednesday of this week that I was going to stop saying it and I am going to stop saying it.*”

About four people on that bank noticed that the pair was gone and about nine of them had it in their heads anyway, and nobody said so.

Then he did the two counts he does, and he does them before he has had anything out of a cup. And he said out loud what they were figures of before he gave them.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of my landing for fifty-one days. Fifty-one is six hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed on the other side for fifty-two days. Fifty-two is six hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and twenty-nine, and I am not going to add the two together. And I have not added them in nine days.*”

Nobody on that bank walked over to the ash. Nobody on that bank walked over to the reed.

Then a man of thirty-one said the only number he has got. And he said it with the subtraction, and he said the other thing about it in the same breath and it was about a clock that is not running.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and he is nobody’s, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and no form that renews him and no rate on him and no hundred in that county either.

“*One hundred and twenty-seven days in this county. One hundred and twenty-seven is six hundred and eighty-one less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*And I am not going to say a second number about any other place in this county, and there is a man of sixty-one standing on this bank who knows exactly what I am not saying and why, and who said it the first morning he came up that plank, and he said it out loud on the Sunday of the hundred and sixteenth week in front of about nine people and about four of you were on that bank.*”

Nobody on that bank asked him what the second number was.

Then the man of thirty-eight said the thing about the stone, and he said it standing with his own lamp in his hand at about the height of a man’s chest, and he said it on a bank because that is the only thing anybody in that county can do with a thing.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has a lamp that is his and he trims wicks and has trimmed them for nineteen years.

“*The ninth line is the same as it was on the Monday. I have been in that room twice since and it is the same sharp cut at the same depth and it has not gone soft at the end of it the way the first one has gone soft.*”

“*I am not going to say which morning it was cut on. Except that it was cut on the Thursday of the hundred and eighteenth week. And that is eight days ago, and eight days is how long it has been, and it is not a figure of how long that stone has been standing.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the subtraction out loud, because he is the only person in that county who has a hand that goes with a figure. And he said what the eight was a figure of before he said what it was not a figure of.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept the book of heads and carts eleven years. And there are four lines in it on the leaf after the last head and there are not five.

“*Six hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and seventy-three. That is eight days, and it is a figure of a gap between two mornings and not of a cut in a stone.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for about a Thursday. I have looked and I have not found one and I looked this morning and not this week.*”

Then he gave the other figure, which he has given twice in a fortnight and which he gave with its own predicate attached to it, because a man of sixty-one has learned what happens to a number that is reported without one.

“*I have eleven years of a book of heads and carts. About nine hundred heads in it and carts against about as many. Not one name in it of anybody who did anything in those eight doors.*”

“*And that is eight doors and no names. It is a figure of a book and not of the doors, and I said that on the Wednesday of this week and I am saying it again this morning and I will go on saying it until somebody gives me a figure that is a figure of the doors.*”

The nine hundred heads went round that bank and did not go into it. Neither did eight doors and one empty space in a wall about a hundred and thirty feet under them.

Then a man of forty-one said what he is going to do. And he said it before anybody had asked him, and he said the whole shape of it in the first sentence so that there would not be a second one.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts, and one of the two has a wheel on the near side making a noise it was not making on the way up, and he has been in and out of the ninth of nine doors twice a day for nineteen years.

“*I am going to ask about eight doors and not one. On foot. One at a time. It is going to take me about a week, and two of the eight are four miles off. And one of them is in a village I have never been inside a gate of in my life.*”

“*And I am starting in the morning. That is not a speech. It is a man walking up a road and knocking at a door and asking a person one question. And there is nobody standing behind me when I do it unless a person wants to stand behind me.*”

Then he said why the nine were not going down that morning. And it was the reason and not a figure, and about four people on that bank had already thought of it.

“*I am not going down today. I am going to be up that cart road at about the eighth hour tomorrow and a man who has been standing in four feet of water since the fifth hour is no use at the head of a cart road at the eighth.*”

“*And I am not going down today because there is nothing to go down for. There is a stone with nine lines in it that nobody has read and there are eight doors with no names against them. And going down again is not one of the two things that would change either of those.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said the count of something, and nobody had asked him for it, and he cannot read.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and has not walked a quarter mile he holds in nine years. And he has said that he cannot read a great many times and stopped a man of thirty-one five times in five days.

“*The count of me stopping a man of thirty-one is five in five days and I am not going to do it a sixth time, and I am not going to be thanked for not doing it, and I would like the five instead of the thanks.*”

“*And the reason I am stopping is that he has stopped. That is the whole of the reason. A man who says five names in five days and then says none of them for a week is a man who has learned one thing. And I am not going to be the man who teaches him the other one.*”

About four people on that bank heard a man who cannot read let a man of thirty-one off and about nine of them had not noticed there was anything to let him off.

Then a woman of twenty-eight said one thing, and she said it once. And she had said it on the Monday and she said it again and it was the same sentence with a day on the front of it.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week, and she cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from, and she writes two numbers a day in a day book in her own hand and gives him his copy with no other hand between.

“*I have checked it a third time this morning and it has not moved. Nobody on this bank has said that a lamp on a hook in a room under that bank and the thing that keeps the record in four counties are the same thing.*”

“*And I am still not going to be the one who says it. And it is still not because it is not true, and I would like it very much recorded that I said the same thing three times and did not say it once.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her and nobody improved on it and nobody argued with her.

Then about nine people stood about on a bank with eight inches of water over the top step. And nothing whatever happened, and the two carts went up that track at about the second hour with a load of stone in one of them.

---

The fever was fifty-six weeks and six days old. He had been in that county one hundred and twenty-seven days.

Eight doors and about nine hundred heads in eleven years of a book. And a man of sixty-one who says out loud that the second of those is a figure of a book and not of the doors.

And a man of forty-one who is going to start asking about the first of them in the morning, and there is no form in that county that says a person may be sent for about a door nine miles down a river, and there is no form that says a person may not.
