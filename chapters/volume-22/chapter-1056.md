# Chapter 1056: A Man Refuses A Kindness

The week turned in the night without anything happening to say so. The sheet was on that bank at the same sixteen inches in the morning that it was on the night before, and it held, and the light came up on a landing that looked exactly like the landing of the morning before, and the only difference anybody could have put a name to was that the man with the broom said the week of it out loud.

First day of the hundred and seventy-third week, and it is the Tuesday of that week. His eight hundred and sixth morning. Six hundred and fifty-six days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and ten weeks and three days old. Six hundred and ten days since the division. Five hundred and fifty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and five hundred and two days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and gave the turn of the week with them, from this side only, and did not say a word about what is on the other side of it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the first day of the hundred and seventy-third week that nothing comes over that turn with the week, that the broom is in his hands now exactly as it was in his hands yesterday, and that he was not going to stand up there and make a morning of a calendar.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and two days of the coming back, on the first day of the hundred and seventy-third week, and it is the Tuesday of that week. Three figures, and the week turned between the Monday of the hundred and seventy-second week and this morning.”

He drew the broom head through the sheet and the sheet shut behind it.

“Go and do your gravel. It is the Tuesday of the hundred and seventy-third week and the bank is holding, and I have got a sheet to work.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow, and asked in the same words at the same hour, and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and put his hands flat on the wall in the same place he has put them.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and thirty-fifth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and fifty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and thirty-fifth. One thousand and fifty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He kept one boot on the wall ledge and spoke to the stone above it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.”

Nothing, and the wall, and the same week with a new name on it.

“A new week has not changed the wall and I did not expect it to, and I want to say that I am not disappointed, because a man who is disappointed is asking the calendar to do his work. I asked at six. It is past six. That is the whole of the morning and I am going to go and do my gravel.”

Then the man of thirty-eight came up that cart road at about the eighth hour and put his hands on the barrow at the top of the flight and asked a small thing in a small voice, and got a small answer, and had asked it wrong and knew it while he was still asking.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, with a board of thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it inside his coat, who has been refused on this landing three times inside one week and has learned the shape of a proper asking and has not learned anything else, and said on the first day of the hundred and seventy-third week that he came up to ask whether the gravel wanted doing before the water went, and that he asked it too small on purpose and knew it while it was in his mouth.

“Does your gravel want doing before this goes off.” He said it lightly, with a shrug in it. “That is all. I will do it either way.”

Corvin Tace did not stop working.

“No.” He said it in the middle of a stroke, without looking up. “You asked me that small and you do not get the reason. Ask me properly and I will give you the whole of it.”

Then he went on with the broom for a while, and then he stopped, and came down the sheet to where Garrin Tolley was standing, and put the broom head on the stone and looked at him straight.

“There. I have a thing I could use and I am going to put it in front of you, and I am not doing it to be kind and I want that said first because you will think it is. The grit off this flight goes down that cart road on his barrow and I have been carrying it down in pails for a week and it takes me out of the sweeping twice a morning.” He stood the broom against the wall. “Take it down the road to his barrow for me. Empty pails. That is the whole of it.”

Garrin Tolley looked at the broom standing against the wall and then at the man who had put it there, and did not touch it.

“No.” He said it, and then he heard himself say it and took it seriously, which is not a thing that happens to him often. “And here is the reason, because you asked me and not the landing. If I take those pails down for you then I am a man on this landing who was given something, and there is no getting back from that. A man who is given something has stopped asking, whether he knows it or not, and I have not finished asking. I would rather be a man who carries his own board up a bank every morning for nothing than be helped once on the top step.”

He put his hands behind his back.

“You have just handed me the first thing anybody has put down on this landing for me, and I have said no to it, and I am not going to pretend I said no lightly. I wanted it. I have wanted a thing handed to me on this bank since the Friday of the hundred and seventy-first week and I did not know it until it was against the wall in front of me.”

Corvin Tace took the broom back off the wall without any shape on his face at all and went to the top of the flight with it, and worked the sheet, and did not say one word about what Garrin Tolley had done, and nobody up there or down there said one word to him about it either.

The refusal was given by the man it was put to, in his own words and by nobody else, and nobody thanked him for refusing and nobody was thanked for putting it in front of him, and nobody told Garrin Tolley he was right.

Then the boy came up that lane at about the tenth hour and set his pails in the sheet and gave his own count out loud and shut his mouth on it at once.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and counts his own days in this county, and said on the first day of the hundred and seventy-third week that his count is four hundred and three days, and that it is his and he will not make it into a count of anything standing on that landing.

“four hundred and three. That is mine and that is all.” He set the pails clear of the running sheet. “Is it a new week.” Nothing came of that and he answered it. “Nothing came of that. I have been awake all night and it has not felt like anything.”

Then Ilyan Vester came up at about the ninth hour with the sheet at his waist, stood clear of the top step, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county five hundred and two days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and seventy-one days old.

“Five hundred and two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and seventy-first days old, and the standing offer asked at the eight hundred and sixth is three hundred and fifty-six days old, which is one thousand and fifty-six less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and it has not been answered on and not been withdrawn on. It is a Tuesday and it is the first morning of a week and I did not think that was going to be the day I asked it on, and it is not.”

He looked at the broom standing against the wall at the top of the flight and then at the man working the sheet with it.

“One hundred and ninety-sixth wrong things in one hundred and ninety-sixth mornings, and this is the hundred and ninety-sixth, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is. My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that I am standing here watching a man refuse a kindness and I wanted it granted so badly that I could feel it, and I did not put my hand up, and I am not going to tell myself that not putting my hand up was a decision.”

He turned his palm over and shut it.

“And my mistake was wanting that small asking at the top of the flight answered. Just the one. So that this landing would have a yes on it somewhere, so that I could see what one looks like standing on that stone and know whether I could ever be the man who put one there. That is what I came up this bank for and I have not got it and I am not going to ask for it.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and the boy went down the lane, and Garrin Tolley went down the cart road with his hands behind his back.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went at the whole flight from the bottom upward. The fortieth step went round the broom and not underfoot, and the chair went round it in the same turn, and the iron in that seam took the broom head and took nothing else. He swept the sheet to the edge of the dry strip and stopped there, and what was on that strip at the seventh hour was on it at the eleventh. The two lengths were still on the dry strip above the standing sheet, a forearm long each and split down the middle along the grain, thick end rough where it parted, and on the second a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches in the flat with one run out of the side.

Nobody lifted either length or turned one over or held one up to the light or felt for its weight in a palm or read one out loud or carried one inside or laid one back or set one to work, then or afterwards, and nobody put a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it or put anything on it. Nothing standing there was named that morning by anybody, and what that wood is was not said in any other words, and no figure of what stood there was given out loud or otherwise.

That morning came off the wall and left, and the account on that landing was as open at eleven as it had been at six and still in use at both hours, and the name on that second length was still the wrong one, and nobody on that flight said that any of it was over.

And Garrin Tolley, going down that bank last of anybody, stopped at the foot of the flight and picked up the two empty pails that stood in the wet there, and carried them the rest of the way down himself, and stood them against the leg of his own barrow, and then put his shoulder against the barrow shafts and pushed the barrow a foot along the wall so the pails would not be standing in the run-off, and said nothing about it to anybody at all.
