# Chapter 1052: Nobody Improved On It

The water came up that bank overnight and came up the way it comes, which is that you do not hear it arrive. In the morning there was simply more of it than there had been, eight inches on the top step and the sheet spread out over the flight so that from the bottom the whole shape of the bank read as a line drawn across a brown field, and the top thirty steps stood out of it clear.

The man with the broom came up in the grey and worked the shallow top of the flight first, and while he worked it a woman came up the cart road on foot with her hands empty and no coat over her arm, and stopped at the foot of the steps and stayed there, which is her place and always has been.

Fourth day of the hundred and seventy-second week, and it is the Friday of that week. His eight hundred and second morning. Six hundred and fifty-two days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and nine weeks and six days old. Six hundred and six days since the division. Five hundred and fifty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and ninety-eight days in this county of Kell.

Eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures from the top of the flight with the sheet at his ankles, and gave them to nobody and did not look down the road while he gave them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and seventy-second week that eight inches is a depth that gives a man his ankles back to him twice a day, and that he was going to use the sheet to carry the grit down instead of his own two hands.

“Eight at the seventh hour, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back, on the fourth day of the hundred and seventy-second week, and it is the Friday of that week. Three figures.”

He put the broom head into the sheet and drew it toward himself and the grit came with it and went over the lip of the step below.

“Go and do your gravel, and do not come up here for me. It is the Friday of the hundred and seventy-second week and I have got a bank to get down.”

Then the woman who reads for a living came up that cart road at about the eighth hour, and stopped at the foot of those steps, and did not come up, and asked what she had come to ask.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, whose book is shut in her hands and opens by her hand and not by anybody else’s, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and seventy-second week that she had come up to ask for one step of work and had thought about it for two days before she asked.

“Give me one step.” She said it to the man with the broom and not to the landing. “Not the whole flight. One step, and I will do it the way you do it, and I will not go near anything that is lying on the dry strip, and you may stand there and tell me if I have it wrong. That is all I am asking for.”

Corvin Tace stood with the broom in the sheet and looked down at her from the top step, and did not answer for long enough that the water moved.

“No.” He said it evenly, with the sheet going past his boots. “And here is the reason, because you asked me and not the landing. That step is where the grit goes. Everything I lift off this flight ends on that one step and goes down the cart road on the barrow, and the direction of it is in my hands and not in the water. If you have a broom on that step then there are two places the grit can go and I cannot see both, and I would spend my morning watching a foot instead of a step, and the step is what I answer for.”

He put the broom head back into the sheet and drew grit out of it.

“It is not that your hands would be bad. I have not seen them work. It is that I do not have a way of telling you where that grit is going that would be worth your morning, and I am not going to spend a morning telling a stranger where my own hands are.”

Sena Dorr stayed at the foot of the steps and did not come up a step, and her face did the thing that a face does before a person decides what to do with the thing they have just been told.

“I wanted one step for the feel of it.” She said it evenly, and she said it out loud, which is the only part of it that was hers to do. “Not for anything anybody could check and not for a line in a place. My hands have been holding a shut book for twenty-three years and I came up a bank this morning to find out whether they would still do something that is not holding a book shut. That is what I wanted and you have refused it with a reason about a step and not about me, and I can carry that, and I am not going to hand it to anybody to hold for me.”

The refusal was on the landing with its reason beside it, and nobody shaped it afterwards, and nobody said that it had been about her work and not about her, and nobody thanked her for coming up or for asking plain.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow, and asked in the same words at the same hour, and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and put his hands flat on the wall with the water at his shins.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and thirty-first mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and fifty-two less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and thirty-first. One thousand and fifty-two less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He did not take his hands off the wall and he did not say it any faster than he said everything.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.”

That found nothing, and he told the wall so in his own words, and nobody filled the space he left.

“It found nothing and there is a woman standing at my foot of the steps who has just been refused, and I have watched that and I have not said anything to her about it, because it is not mine and I am not the man who hands reasons back out. I asked my question at six. It is past six and nothing has come of it, and the water is higher than it was on the day I asked.”

Then Ilyan Vester came up at about the ninth hour with the sheet at his shins and stood well clear of the top step, and gave his figures, and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and ninety-eight days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and sixty-seven days old.

“Four hundred and ninety-eight days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and sixty-seven days old, and the standing offer asked at the eight hundred and second is three hundred and fifty-two days old, which is one thousand and fifty-two less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and it has not been answered on and not been withdrawn on. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I have stopped putting that in, because a man who says it every morning is asking it in a slower voice.”

He looked down the flight at the woman at the foot of the steps and then at the wall.

“One hundred and ninety-second wrong things in one hundred and ninety-second mornings, and this is the hundred and ninety-second, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is. My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that I now know the shape of a refusal with a working in it, and I watched that woman walk down a road with one this morning, and I could not have carried it, and that is the cost and it has no figure in it.”

He shut the cut hand and opened it again and the water ran off it.

“And my mistake was sitting up there while she was refused and wanting it to stand, because I have been refused three times in three mornings and a man who wants company in a thing will begin to want a fourth person refused before he has understood the first three. I did not want her refused. I wanted to be the only one who is. That is not a want I am going to be proud of in front of a landing.”

Then the boy came up that lane at about the tenth hour with his pails and stood them in the shallows and gave his own count, and did not turn it toward anything.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and counts his own days in this county, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and seventy-second week that his count is three hundred and ninety-nine days, and that it is his and he will not make it into a count of anything standing on that landing.

“three hundred and ninety-nine. That is mine.” He watched the water come over the edge of the top step. “Has anybody swept the top step since the water came back.” Nothing came of that and he said so himself. “Nothing came of that. I will keep my eyes on it then and I will not ask.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and Ilyan went last and stood a moment on the second step from the bottom with the water going past his boots and went on down.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about eighty of them were under, and went at the whole flight from the bottom upward with the grit going ahead of him in the sheet. The fortieth step went round the broom and not over it, and the chair went round it in the same turn of the broom, and the iron in that seam took the broom head and did not take a finger. He worked the sheet up to the edge of the dry strip and stopped, and what was lying there at the seventh hour was lying there at the eleventh. The two lengths were still on the dry strip above the standing water, a forearm long each and split down the middle along the grain, thick end rough where it parted, and on the second a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches in the flat with one run out of the side.

No hand in this county went on either of those lengths this morning to pull one, turn one, lift one, weigh one in the palm, carry one inside, put one back or set one to work, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it, and nobody stood a hand up over it to read it by. Nothing on that strip was given a name that was not already in somebody’s mouth in an earlier morning, and what that wood is was not said out a second time, and nobody set a count against what lay there.

That morning drew down and let the light go off the wall, and the account on that landing had not moved a step between six and eleven and was still being worked at eleven, and the name on that second length was still the wrong one, and nobody up there said that any of it was past.

And Sena Dorr went down that cart road with the shut book flat against her knee the whole way, and she did not look back up at the top step once, and she went past the bottom step without putting her hand on its stone, which she had done every morning for a week.
