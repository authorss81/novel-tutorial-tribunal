# Chapter 75: The Door

The room over the pump has a table, two stools, a chair with a split leg that has been mended with a length of hoop-iron, a fire that goes out by the third bell, and about nine feet of wall of nothing between a window and a door. It is the room a refuse hauler lives in and keeps a page in, and in the first eleven days of the third month of this case it became the room that five people a day came to, and none of them had arranged to meet anybody, and all of them had come because there was nowhere else in that district to go.

That is the finding of the sixth of the month and it is nine words long and Wenna Tace said it at the end of a long day with a slate on her knee.

“They have made me the door,” she said. “And there is nothing behind it.”

---

The first of the five came at the second bell on the sixth, and he was about twenty-six, and he had a notice.

He wanted to know whether a notice could be given back.

“It came on the eighteenth,” he said. “Left at the door. And I cannot read it. I have had it eleven days and I have carried it about eleven days and I have shown it to two people and one of them said it was about water and the other one said it was about a road and I am going to be turned off somewhere and I cannot find out where.”

She read it standing, in the doorway, and gave it back.

“It is a notice of the third sitting of this bench on the twenty-eighth of last month. It is about a rate. It was put on your door because your door is a door the district can find, and there is a man in this district who is a body and knows where about forty of them are, and he swore them out of his own mouth in a room and did not put them in the bench’s book.”

“So I have to go to the sitting.”

“There is no sitting,” she said. “It was the third sitting. It was a fortnight ago. I was not at it because a holder is not a party and is not a bench and does not sit, and the page says the rate was set and read on the fifth at the sixth bell, which is what I did, and I read it to about two hundred people, and I read all nine sheets and not only the barrel lid, and nobody asked me to read all nine and I have asked for that to be entered.”

“Can I see the page?”

“No.”

He said, “Why not,” and she said the thing that she has said eleven times in a fortnight to eleven different people and has got faster at.

“Because the page is not a public thing. It is a page a person keeps, and a person is not a district, and if anybody can come and read it then the person in it stops being able to say anything of their own in it, and then they are a clerk with a barrow.” She put the notice on the table, squared to the edge. “And I cannot make a copy for you, and I want that on the record too, though nobody is writing it. There is a bench of four that can enter a copy and I am not a bench. And I have not got a seal and I am not going to get one, because the fifth term says I hold this for myself and not for a district, and the day somebody gives me a seal is the day this page is the district’s again and I am a woman with a barrow holding the district’s book.”

He was angry for about a minute, and then he was not, and then he said the thing she has decided she will remember.

“Who do I go to?”

“You go to the bench,” she said, “and you say the paper was left on a door and not given to a person, and you ask whether the leaving of it is a service, and it is a question about a room and not about a rate and they can answer it or not answer it and both are on the page. And I will read their answer to you. That is what I can do and it is not a small thing and it is not a door.”

---

The second was a man of about forty who wanted a rate changed, and he wanted it changed by the fourth of the month, and he had a carter’s chalk line on a board at a weighhouse and a rate of his own that the district did not know about.

The third was a young man of nineteen who had heard the word *exception* from a carter and wanted to know whether his wage stopped when it ended.

“It ends,” she said, on the sixth, at the third bell, with the fire gone out. “That is what the word means and I am not going to have you go on thinking otherwise. It means a thing is allowed for a time and it has an end on it, and the end is written somewhere, and here is the difficulty. The paper that says your wage ends has nothing in it about a wage stopping at all. It is a summary of a charter, and the charter has no end on it, and the charter does not pay you, and the charter’s pay line is for the gate. Your wage is from a carter and the carter’s chalk is the carter’s and this district has no line for it and no rate for it and never had, and that has got nothing to do with the word.”

“Then somebody has told me a thing.”

“Somebody has handed you a piece of paper with a big word on it and asked you to live inside it, and I cannot make him unhand it to you, and I have tried, and I have been up the road to the place it was printed in the sense that I have written to a clerk and been told that a court has no power over a form.” She refilled the lamp. “Write that down for me and take it. Not the word. What I have just said. It is yours and I have not put it anywhere.”

He did not write it down. He said he would remember it and she said, “Then you will remember it for about three weeks and then you will be frightened of it again, because that is what happens to a thing that is only in a person, and I have eleven years of that and it is why I am the one that people come to, and I have worked that out about nine days ago and I am not sure I like it.”

---

The fourth was the one that mattered and she has entered it herself, in her own hand, in the middle of the page, which is where she puts the things that matter.

He was about fifty and he had been in the district thirty years and he was owed something by a bench of another district, or by a man in one, and he wanted to appeal.

“Then I need you named in it,” he said.

She put the lamp down.

“I am not named in anything,” she said. “Not in yours, not in anybody’s. The fifth term says a holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one. That is the fourth of the four things it says about me and it is the one that is going to cost you and I have not got a way round it.”

“You are the only one who can read.”

“I am the only one who can read in this district and there is one other seven miles away and she is not yours.”

He said, “Then I have got no appeal,” and she said, “You have got an appeal. You have got a perfectly good one, and there is a bench four miles up the fen road that can hear it, and the charter says an appeal runs to any person in the district, and you have a claim, and you can swear it yourself, and it will be served and heard, and I will read you everything that comes back and you will know what has happened to it every time.”

“Then what do you want for it?”

“I want the name of a person in it,” he said, “because the last one I swore, in another district, went to a room and I could not find out what was in the room, and there was a boy who said it was lost, and it was not lost, it was in a drawer with a man's name on it who did not want to be in it, and I am fifty years old and I am not going to swear another one into a room on the word of a boy who was being kind to me.”

She wrote it down. Then she said the other thing, and it is the finding, and it took her four minutes to get to and she has never been quicker.

“Listen to what has just happened in this room,” she said. “You have come to me because a thing has gone into a room. You cannot go into the room because you cannot read what is in it. You cannot have me in your paper because the paper will not have me. So you have to find somebody else, and the only person you can find is somebody who can read, and the only person in this district who can read is the one the paper will not have, and it is the same woman, and she is standing in front of you with a lamp and has been for eleven days.

“That is the fifth term working. That is what it is for. And it has never once been what anybody thought it was for, and nobody drew it, and it cost a bench of four nine sheets to find out, and it has been running for thirteen days and five people have walked up those stairs.” She turned her slate round so he could see what she had written on it in the last quarter of an hour, which was a column of five lines with dates against them. “They have come about a notice, a rate, a word, and two hours of a chamber in Orison, and one of them wanted me to put my name in a paper and I have said no, and the reason I am a door is the same reason I am not allowed to be in a door. It is one rule and it is doing both. Whoever wrote that was right about it. Somebody wrote that *on purpose* to stop a person carrying the law of a district and the claims of the people in it at the same time, and they succeeded, and it has left me in a room with a table and no bench behind me, and I cannot appeal, and everybody in this district now knows that the person they bring things to is the person they cannot use.”

He sat on the stool with his hat in his hands for a while.

“Then what is the use of you?”

“The use of me is that I can read and that I will not charge you and that I am here every day and that I will tell you the truth of what a page says,” she said. “That is all of it. That is the whole of the use and there is no other and you should have been told that on the first day, and nobody told you, and I have not told anybody until just now, and I have been putting things right for eleven days, which is a job I did not ask for and cannot refuse and am not paid for, and I am the only person in this district who can read, and if I am ill there is a notice with a seal on it that nobody in four miles can take in.”

She entered the whole of it in the page that evening, in her own hand, in the middle, where she puts the things that matter:

> **ON THE SIXTH OF THE THIRD MONTH, FOUR PEOPLE CAME TO THIS ROOM AND NONE OF THEM HAD ASKED TO COME TO IT, AND A FIFTH CAME ON THE SEVENTH.**
> **ONE OF THEM ASKED THAT I BE NAMED IN AN APPEAL. I SAID NO. THE FIFTH TERM SAYS I MAY NOT BE NAMED IN ONE. I AM THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO CAN READ AND I AM THE ONLY PERSON HERE THAT A PERSON CAN ASK, AND IT IS ONE RULE DOING BOTH THINGS, AND IT IS WORKING, AND I AM THE RESULT OF IT.**
> **I AM NOT A BENCH AND I AM NOT A PARTY AND I CANNOT APPEAL FROM ANYTHING I AM ASKED ABOUT, AND A HOLDER CANNOT BE RELIEVED OF THE FIFTH TERM BY ANYBODY, INCLUDING ME, INCLUDING A COURT, AND THAT IS NOT A PROTECTION.**
> **I AM ENTRIES AND I AM THE ONLY THING THAT CAN BE PRODUCED, AND BEING PRODUCED IS THE PRICE OF THE PAGE.**

---

The fifth came at the fourth bell on the seventh and he was the one she had expected and was not ready for, and his want was the hundred and fifty hauls, and it had been hers for eleven months.

Hollis Dune of Nethergill had walked seven miles and asked her a question about the rate for a haul.

“A barrow’s load,” he said. “A woman of yours hauls about two hundred a year. Your district pays her a third of a haul rate and calls the rate a rate, and there is a barrow in your district and there is no cart in your district, so there is nobody who can say what a haul is where there is no cart. And a month ago a man from a bridge stood in a basalt court and said in words that I have had written down by a drover’s clerk — that the largest single want of the people she was reading to was not provided for, and that it was not provided for because she had cut it out herself, and she did not cut it out because it was wrong, she cut it out because there is no rate anywhere in the world for a thing a barrow does.

“Three benches have adopted that settlement and nine of them have answered a question in writing and seven of those nine have said they hold nothing. Our bench at Lowgill has had the terms for six days and has adopted nothing, because a bench cannot adopt a thing it has not read and half of what we were sent was not in the paper. And our road fund is nil. So I have walked seven miles to ask a question that has been in a record for three weeks.”

“And you have come to the wrong door,” she said, before he had said it, “because I cannot set a rate and I am not a bench and I am not a party and I have no standing in this or anywhere and I am the last person in this district who should be asked.”

“You are the only person in this district who knows what a haul rate is.”

“Yes,” she said. “And that is exactly why I cannot be the one who asks.”

He did not understand that and she had to do it in the flat voice, and it took the rest of the afternoon, and it is entered in the district’s book in the district’s own hand because Oakes wrote it down because it was a matter of the bench and the bench of four was sitting that afternoon and did not know it was sitting about this.

The chain is: a holder is not a party, and a party is a person who asks, and asking to have a rate set is asking. A bench can hear its own holder speak, as it heard the gate hand's own refusal in the page and minuted it. But a holder who asks for a rate is a holder asking for a rate *for herself and for about two hundred other people*, and about two hundred other people are people and a holder is not a party and cannot be one and cannot be made one by anybody in this city, and if a bench of four enters her as a claimant then the fifth term is broken in the middle and there is no holder, and the page is in a room with a barrow against the wall and no keeper and the rate on the pump is read aloud each quarter by nobody.

“I can do the asking without being a party,” she said, after a while. “The fifth term says I may not be a party. It does not say I may not speak. A bench of four can hear its own holder speak and can set a rate and can minute who said what and can be wrong. That is the way it is done and it is the way the gate hand’s was done and the gate hand is not a holder and the gate hand swore her own refusal, so there is no precedent and I have checked, there is nothing about a holder in any instrument in this city except four lines in nine sheets in this room.”

“So you will ask.”

“I will ask,” she said, “and I have been thinking about it for three hours and I am going to tell you what it costs, because you have walked seven miles and you are owed the cost, and then I am going to ask anyway.

“A rate for a haul in this district will have to be set by four people, and one of the four voices is going to be mine, because I do not hold a seat and will not be given one, and because the only person who can say what a barrow-load is worth in a district where nothing is carted is the person who does it two hundred times a year. So the rate will be set by the person who wants it. And in about a year a carter in this district will stand where you are standing and say the rate in this district is set by the refuse hauler, and he will be right, and there will be nothing on a page to answer him with.

“And then the third thing, which is worse, and which I have been carrying up and down the stairs since the fifth. I read that rate out loud at a pump at the sixth bell on the first of every quarter, to about two hundred people, for the rest of my life. Not this quarter. Every quarter there is one. The charter says the rate is to be read aloud at the sixth bell on every first day of a quarter by the holder, and I wrote that, and Harrow said it, and nobody argued, because we were all pleased to have something in the charter that a person had to do. And a rate for a haul is a rate. So I will stand on a bank and read out the number that I asked for and the district will pay me at, and every person who hears it will know exactly who set it, and I will not be able to say one word against it, because I cannot appeal, and I may not be named in an appeal, and I may not appear in support of one.

“That is what it costs. I have had it eleven years and I want it and I have never been able to ask for it, and the reason I have never been able to ask for it is the reason I am holding this page. And I am going to ask, and I would like all three of those to go on the page in that order.”

Oakes entered it in the district’s book. It is in the district’s hand. It is the fourth line of that day’s entry and there are nine of them and the ninth is that the bench will set the rate and that the holder asked and cannot appeal from the rate and that this is entered against the bench and not against the holder.

And then she wrote the fourth entry in the page that week, and it is the one that made her a body, and she wrote it in the middle, and she wrote it in her own hand, and she has not been thanked for any of the four.

> **THE SIXTH OF THE THIRD MONTH. NETHERGILL ASKED FOR THE TERMS UNDER THE SECOND HALF OF THE FOURTH TERM AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME THEY HAVE BEEN ASKED FOR BY ANYBODY.**
> **IT COST TWO DAYS OF A MAN'S WALKING WHO IS NOT PAID AND CANNOT BE SENT FOR AND DOES NOT LIVE IN THIS DISTRICT, AND A DAY OF A WOMAN'S TIME AT A BAR, AND A CROOKED SHEET OF PAPER IN A KITCHEN SEVEN MILES UP A LANE WHICH IS THE ONLY COPY OF THE WHOLE OF IT IN THIS WORLD THAT WAS MADE IN A KITCHEN.**
> **AND IT COST ME THIS: THE TERMS TRAVEL NOW, AND A THING THAT TRAVELS CAN BE READ BY SOMEBODY WHO IS NOT ME, AND I AM NO LONGER THE ONLY PERSON WHO CAN READ THEM, AND I AM NOT ASKED FOR MY VIEW BY THAT PERSON BECAUSE NOBODY HAS ASKED HER FOR ANYTHING.**
>
> **AND I WANT A RATE FOR A HAUL AND I HAVE WANTED ONE FOR ELEVEN MONTHS AND I AM NOT ASKING FOR IT YET AND I AM GOING TO, AND THE REASON I AM NOT ASKING TODAY IS THAT I AM WRITTING THIS DOWN, AND THE REASON FOR THAT IS THAT A THING I WANT IS A THING I CAN BE USED WITH, AND I WOULD RATHER HAVE IT IN THE PAGE WHERE ANYBODY CAN READ IT THAN CARRY IT ABOUT.**
>
> **A SECOND BARROW. THAT IS WHAT THE MONEY IS FOR AND I HAVE SAID IT ON A PAGE AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME I HAVE SAID IT TO ANYBODY.**
