# Chapter 57: The Page With the Hours On It

The props came out of the ninth bridge some time in the middle of the twentieth day and Ilyan was four miles away at a culvert, and he found out about it at the last bell, from a boy of fifteen who had the pump handle and a message and did not want to be at a court, and Ilyan did what he has been able to do for a month, which is to make himself useful in the wrong place.

He wrote it on Coll's slate and read it out to nobody and then read it out to the field, because the field had been sitting since the sixth bell on a burden schedule and a bridge reopening four miles away was a fact and facts are currency.

“PROPS OUT AT THE NINTH BELL. SPAN OPEN. NO LOAD. FOOT, HANDCART, WHEELBARROW, ON THE BELL. COUNT AT EVERY CHANGE, TWO HANDS UNDER. THE SEAT IS BEDDED AND IT IS CURING AND IT GOES ON SITTING FOR A FORTNIGHT AFTER THE PROPS CAME OUT, SO NOBODY IS TO PUT A LOAD ON IT BEFORE THEN AND THAT IS NOT MY SENTENCE. THE SEAT IS EMPTY SEVEN INCHES UNDERNEATH AND THE WATER COMES UP FOUR INCHES IN A DAY, AND THAT IS PUCK'S ARITHMETIC AND HE IS NOT A MEASURE. READ BACK: I. BRAY, O. SEDGE, COLL, PUCK, J. VELL, D. RUSK. THE FOURTH BRIDGE'S FOUR PROPS CAME OFF IT ON THE DAY OF THE HEARING AND GO BACK ON THE SAME CART BEFORE THE REVIEW OR THE DISTRICT PAYS FOR THEM, AND W. ANHALT HAS SAID FOUR WORDS ABOUT IT IN WRITING AND THE COURT HAS NAILLED THE FOUR WORDS TO THE FRAME, AND HE IS NOT A PARTY, AND HE SENT TWO HANDS UNASKED.”

And Coll read it back in a hand that was nine years old and had learned to write in a guild's book, and the bell at the gate of a culvert went about a foot and a half from him and made no noise, and Bryn Sallow looked at it, and nobody said anything about it, and Ilyan found that he had missed the props coming out and did not know what to do with that for about a day and a half.

---

Harrow took the burden schedule at the sixth bell, which is the same hour he had opened the field in, because he does not take a new hour for a new kind of sitting. The court had asked the gate hand which hour and the gate hand had said *anything but the sixth*, and the court had taken the sixth, and had entered that it had taken it against the recommendation of the only person in the field who was going to have to stand in it.

“The court asked for a burden schedule,” Harrow said, “and the court is going to read it, and the court is going to be honest about the fact that it has asked nine benches for their burdens since the hearing and has never once been given a person’s own hours, because in this city a person’s hours are not evidence of anything, they are just a person having been somewhere, and a person having been somewhere is not a thing anybody has ever thought to put in a document.

“I am going to be wrong about the first one and I am doing it anyway, because the alternative is a schedule made by four clerks with a rule for the gate in it, and we have had that for four days and it is on a board in the rain. Who is going to carry anything in this instrument.”

Nobody answered for a while, and then Wenna Tace stood up at the back, which she had not done in any of the rooms, and she was in the flat voice, the load voice, the one she counted in.

“The question is asked wrong,” she said. “You are asking who is going to carry it. There is a sheet on the gate with the gate hand’s name on it — not her name, her *office* — and it says the gate is worked by the gate hand and by no other person, and there is one gate hand, and so the sheet has already answered your question and it answered it before anybody was asked. That is not anybody carrying it. That is a document choosing a person by not choosing.”

“Miss Tace,” said Harrow, “is the court hearing an objection to the schedule or an argument about the sheet.”

“Both,” said Wenna Tace, “and I am the one who has got eleven months of your bridge's sentence in the pay book and I would like to say one thing in this room and then I will sit down, because I have been holding it since the fourteenth and I said I would say it to the court and not to a man on a step.

“If you pay for that gate, you pay for it with water.

“There is no other money in this district. The district's road fund has a name and the water has a name. The gate is on the water. If the court writes a line that pays the gate hand and somebody has to find the money, then the money comes off the water, and the water goes on every bucket in this district, and the buckets are on Weir Lane, and Weir Lane is not in the traveller book either. So the poor pay for the gate out of their own buckets, by the bucket, and it will be called a rate, and it will be entered, and it will be correct, and it will be the first time in nineteen years that the people in this district have paid a tax to anybody at all, and it will have been invented by a court in Orison on the strength of a bridge.”

That went about as far into the room as a sentence can go, and Harrow wrote it down, and Roke wrote it down, and Odile read it back twice because the field's record could not hold a sentence that had only been said.

“Enter it as stated by the party,” Harrow said, “and entered with the witness’s own name and not the district’s, and entered that the witness is not a party and has asked to be heard and has been heard, and entered that the witness has a want of her own in this and that her want is money and it is eleven months of it.”

“It is,” said Wenna Tace. “And I am not being brave about that. I want it.”

---

The schedule took three hours and four of its lines are the finding.

Bryn Sallow wrote her own line, in her own hand, and she wrote it on the step with the tide coming and she wrote it slowly, which nobody who has watched her do anything for thirty years would have believed until they watched it.

> **ONE. THE GATE. FROM THE FIRST LIGHT ON THE RISE TO THE DARK ON THE FALL, EVERY DAY, BY THE GATE HAND. NOT MY HOURS. I HAVE NEVER COUNTED THEM AND I AM NOT GOING TO START NOW FOR A PAGE.**

“Strike that,” said Harrow.

“No,” said Bryn Sallow. “Read it again and hear it. I have never counted them. That is the sentence. If I give you a number then I have made it a wage and a wage wants a rate and a rate wants a body and there is no body, and we go round again for four days. If I leave it as *the first light to the dark* then it is a fact about a machine and a machine does not need a rate, and you can all see it and none of you can pay it, and that is the true shape of the thing, and I would rather the page said the true shape.”

Nobody in that field had a better sentence, and three of them were paid to have them.

> **TWO. THE CULVERT FOOT-GATE, EIGHTEEN INCHES, HINGED AT THE BOTTOM, OPENING OUTWARD TOWARD THE FLAT. FIXED BY THE GATE HAND, ONCE, AT THE GATE'S OWN COST, IN THE ELEVENTH YEAR, WHEN THE BANK WENT SOFT AND A MAN COULD NOT GET HIS BARROW THROUGH.**
> **THREE. THE HAND-BELL, BOUGHT AT A MARKET, ATTACHED TO NOTHING.**
> **FOUR. NO RATE IS ENTERED AGAINST ANY LINE OF THIS SCHEDULE AND THE COURT HAS NO POWER TO ENTER ONE HERE, BECAUSE THE RATE IS SET IN A BOOK FOUR MILES UP THE ROAD AND THIS COURT HAS NOT AMENDED THAT BOOK.**

The third line made a noise in the room, and the noise was about nine people laughing and one of them being Bryn Sallow.

Then Tarin said the thing nobody else in the field was going to say, and he said it to the field and not to Harrow, because that is the difference between a route-arbiter and a trialwright.

“Marshal,” he said, to Dain, who had been standing at the rope for two hours in a borrowed capacity with no standing on anything, “you have been doing this for nineteen years and I have been listening to you do it for a month, and I would like to ask you the question a neutral boundary is for. Four hundred and eleven loads are on that flat. Who said yes to them.”

Dain did not pretend not to understand.

“Nobody said yes to them,” he said. “That is the entire answer and it is the only one available. Four hundred and eleven loads went into a low water over four years because six or seven people a week carried them, and every one of those nine had somebody downstream who wanted them gone, and the somebody downstream was a bench, and the bench wanted them gone and did not want to be the one carrying them, and so the carrying fell to the lowest hand in the chain, and there is no name in the chain, because a name would have to be at the top, and the top is a bench and a bench is a body and a body pays its own.

“Mr Vester,” he said, and turned round, and Ilyan had not expected it and was glad of it. “A burden that is assigned to nobody is not assigned to nothing. It is assigned to the person with the least standing in the room, every time, without exception, in nineteen years, and the reason it is assigned to her is not malice. It is that she is the only one in the chain who can be made to be there without anybody having to write anything down. I have watched this for nineteen years. I have never seen it done better than here and I have never seen it cost a person more.”

“Enter the marshal’s account,” Harrow said. “And enter that it is an account of a practice and not of these parties, and that the court has no finding on it and is not making one.”

> **THE RATE AND THE HOURS OF THE GATE, ENTERED AS THE GATE HAND STATED THEM, IN HER HAND, ON THE TWENTIETH DAY, IN A FIELD OPENED ON THE NINTH-BRIDGE MATTER. THE SCHEDULE IS PART OF THE FIELD'S RECORD AND IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT OF THE GATE AND IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT OF ANY SPAN.**
> **THE HOURS ARE NOT A NUMBER. NO RATE IS ENTERED. THE COURT HAS NO POWER HERE AND SAYS SO ON THE FACE OF IT.**

---

The System put the burden schedule into Ilyan's sight at the moment Roke entered it, and it was the second time since the hearing that a thing he could read had arrived in a form that was not paper, and he had still not got used to it and had stopped expecting to.

**BURDEN SCHEDULE, TAKEN IN THE FIELD AT THE LOW-WATER CULVERT, THE TWENTIETH DAY. LINES: FOUR. LINES WITH A RATE AGAINST THEM: NONE.**
**THE HOURS ARE ENTERED IN THE GATE HAND'S HAND AND ARE NOT A NUMBER.**
**WHAT THE SCHEDULE SHOWS: THE INSTRUMENT NAMES AN OFFICE. THERE IS ONE PERSON IN THE OFFICE. THE PERSON IS NOT PAID.**
**WHAT THE SCHEDULE DOES NOT SHOW, AND THE COURT WILL NOT FIND: WHO IS OWED THE FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN LOADS, AND WHO OWES THEM.**

Nobody in that field was going to argue with a number, and there was nothing on the page to argue with, and that is the whole of the finding: a four-line schedule, entered by a woman's own hand, with nothing against it.

Then the court did something that cost a day and a half and was not in anybody's plan, which is that it began to take statements, and it took them from the people who are in the schedule, and it took them at length, and it produced a second page.

Wenna Tace, four hundred and eleven loads, eleven months of arrears, ninety days of a third of a haul rate, and a barrow that is four loads to a cart, and which is not a haul and is entered as neither and is therefore invisible.

Cael Rundle, a ratepayer, thirty-one years, a bad chest in the wet, a daughter of fourteen who is not in the traveller book and walks to the pump, and *I am not asking for the water, I am asking for the paper gone*, and a motion withdrawn as to the withdrawal of the precedent and unwithdrawn as to everything else.

Nell Ambrey, convenor, a sanitation complaint entered eleven weeks ago that is still a complaint, that has cost her the low water and has not yet cost anybody anything at all, and a clerk who printed an instrument at the fourth bell and has not slept properly since.

And Corva Silt, who said that she had printed a form out of a public instrument because a notice that said nothing about who opened a gate was a notice that said anybody might, and that she had read the fourth paragraph four times and the fifth once at night on a lamp, and that the fifth paragraph said *a ward rate paid to a person at the moment of the work in chalk on a day-book line*, and that this district has no ward and has a woman.

“Enter that last sentence as the clerk’s own,” Harrow said, “and enter that the court read the fifth paragraph of a district’s own instrument out of a public instrument copied from a bridge charter, and that the clerk who copied it says she did not know there was a woman in it, and that the court is of the same opinion and had not known either, and that the difference is that the court is a court and the clerk is a clerk and neither of us is a bridgekeeper and the sentence came from a keeper.”

He put the tray down.

“The court is going to do one thing before it rises and it is going to be done in the worst possible way, which is on the step, in the open, with sixty people on the bank, and I am going to state it and I am going to state why I am doing it badly.

“This court cannot pay the gate. This court cannot amend the road book. This court cannot enter a rate. What this court can do is refuse, today, on the record, to treat the absence of a rate as the absence of a wage, and to enter the hours as hours, in her hand, on a page in a field, and to send that page to nine benches and one poor bench and one district's clerk, and to enter that the page is the first in this city that carries a woman's own hours and that the hours are not a number and the court does not know what to do about that and is not going to pretend otherwise.

“Mr Sedge is not here and the guild is not a party to this field and I will not send the guild's hands to a gate in a poor district on a matter I have no mandate for. Mr Quill's license is a license of a public record and not of a district bench, and she may carry this page to any district that asks and to no district that has not asked. Mr Vester may read the numbers and may not read the words and his reading of the numbers was the first thing that was right in this field, and it was wrong by about nine feet of flat, and I am entering that too.”

He said the last part looking at Ilyan, and it was not unkind, and it was the fourth time since the hearing that a court had entered a thing about Ilyan that he would have preferred not entered, and he had stopped being able to tell the difference between that and being a participant in his own case.

“Your left leg,” Harrow said. “Is it still there.”

“It is not mine,” Ilyan said.

“Then the court enters that too,” said Harrow, “in the words of the party, and does not ask for an explanation, because an explanation would be a thing this court could hear and use and I am not going to hear it. Mr Roke, write: *the attached record-holder states that his left leg is not his. Entered without explanation at the direction of the bench.*”

And then he did the thing that made the page different, which was to ask the gate hand whether she wanted it kept in a book or in a column, and Bryn Sallow said, before anybody had explained the question:

“Column. A book is a thing you can lose and a column is a thing you have to look at, and I have had that sentence from a woman on a lower road four miles up this road for about a week without knowing who said it.”

Sera Quill wrote it down as the gate hand's words, and did not say that she had said it, and did not look at Ilyan, and Ilyan, who had been in this city a month and had spent most of it in rooms where people wrote things down so that they could be used later, understood that the only person in the field who had not wanted the page for anything was the person whose hours were on it, and that this was the same woman who had gone back to work on a bank at the sixth bell and said *I have waited two days to say it and I can wait*, and that the whole of the difference between the ninth bridge and this culvert was going to be whether anybody in a court could get that far.

---

They came off the bank at the ninth bell, and the ninth bell on that road is when the turn is over and the water is at its highest, and Bryn Sallow had the gate, and the gate had gone down at the fifth and three quarters and would not come up until the third bell in the morning, and the four hundred loads would be on the flat in the dark with a woman in front of them who could not be everywhere.

Ilyan stopped at the top of the bank and put his weight on his right leg and it was not a decision he made.

“Wenna Tace,” he said. “You never asked for the eleven months. You asked for the sentence, and the sentence got you the eleven months, and about forty people in this room think you are a clever woman and you are, and I want to say the other thing, because nobody else in the field is going to say it and I have been in this city five weeks and I have been wrong about the near end of things once today in front of sixty people.

“You did not get the eleven months. You got a line in a pay book with a number on it, and the number is the district's own rate for a haul, and the district's rate for a haul is a number that a person may be paid at, and the reason it works is that somebody in a court read one line of a charter out loud and a woman with a barrow was in the room and had a want. That is not a rule. That is a coincidence with a name on it, and the name is yours, and if you had not been there it would have gone past, and the sentence on the page would still be on the page, saying a thing about a person who is not a trespasser, and about nine hundred people on this low water who are not trespassers and are not paid.

“I do not know how to make a coincidence into a rule. I have been in this city five weeks and I have won one thing and it was a charter and it is now a gate, and I have not slept properly since the fourteenth.”

“You have slept,” said Sera.

“Not properly,” Ilyan said, “and not on account of the flat, and I am not going to be interesting about it.”

Wenna Tace put her hand on the barrow's handle and did not get it down the bank because there were nine of them waiting to help a boy who had not known where to put his feet.

“If you want to do something useful tomorrow,” she said, “there is a boy of nine on this bank who watched a load of district sand go back up the hill on the fourteenth and who has asked me twice this week whether he is a trespasser, and his mother says a carter told him so, and I have not answered him, because I did not think it was my business to tell a man what a carter said, and it is not, and it is also mine.

“Read him the sentence. The one about the trespasser. He is nine and he will not understand a word of it, and he will understand it anyway, because that is what you were doing when you stood at the second gallery and told the truth at the interval, and a boy of nine can tell the difference between a man doing that and a man doing the other thing, and I have watched you do it once and so has he, because he was there, on the bank, with the sand.”
