# Chapter 78: Twenty-One Names

The matter on the docket board was called *Kearn, upper street, party wall* and it had been on the board since the eighth of last month and had never had a place, and Abel Kearn had stopped coming to look at it on the third week and had started coming again on the fourth, which is what a person does when a thing they have lost is written in chalk where they can see it.

He was thirty-five and a builder and there was lime in the seam of his apron, and he had said in a basalt court a month ago that he lived in the ward and was in neither of that room's two words for it, and it was in the record, and a record is a thing a clerk can use.

The wall belonged half to his mother's house. Not in a figurative sense, and not the way a wall belongs to a house when two people live on either side of it and neither of them is named on it: the wall was a party wall, and a party wall is a surveyor's line, and the line was in a book made a hundred and forty-one years ago by a man who is dead, in a district called Fallowditch, eleven miles away, and Fallowditch in those days ran four miles further left than it runs now and the boundary was moved and the wall stayed where it was and the survey stayed in Fallowditch.

A timber merchant three doors up from Abel Kearn had begun, in the middle of the month, stacking green baulks against that wall in the rain, on his own side, and the wall had a crack in it that had been a hairline for nine years and was by the following morning the width of a thumbnail, and the crack is a nail head and the wall is forty-one years old and a baulk of elm against a wall at that height puts about nine hundredweight on it.

Nobody could stop it. That is the part that took a month and it is worth being exact about, because the exactness is the whole of this case.

The court could order the baulks moved. The court had said on the face of a field that it has no power over any gate in this city and had said it twice, and it has no power over a yard either, and a wall in a lane is a wall in a yard. The district could have had the wall surveyed again and could have had the line declared where it plainly was, which is a foot and a half inside Abel Kearn's mother's house, and has not done it, because a district cannot move a boundary and a district cannot swear a survey that is not in its own book, and Fallowditch's book said in the district's own hand, on the eighteenth of last month, in public, that it holds no page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected may swear to in its own room.

It was true. Abel Kearn believed it was true. It was the most reliable thing anybody had said to him in a month, and it had been said about his mother's wall by a bench that was not trying to take it.

So he did what anybody in this city does with a thing nobody can hear, which is he put his name on a board.

---

The board was twenty-two names and eleven places on the fourteenth of the third month and it had been twenty-two names and eleven places on the sixteenth of the last month, and in between, four places had gone to a water and a name and a boundary and an inheritance, and three names had gone back on the board for the second time, and one of those three was a man of nineteen who has been on and off this board three times and has asked for nothing and has a case about his name.

The System put the standing of the board in front of Ilyan while Roke was still copying the list onto a second sheet, and it was a record of a list and not a rule about the world.

**DOCKET OF THE FIRST VERDICT, THE FOURTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH, ON PUBLIC NOTICE.**
**TWENTY-TWO NAMES ENTERED. ELEVEN PLACES IN A MORNING.**
**NINE MATTERS HAVE ENTERED SINCE THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH. OF THOSE NINE, FOUR HAVE BEEN PLACED, TWO HAVE LEFT THE BOARD, AND THREE ARE ON IT AGAIN.**
**OF THE TWENTY-TWO, FOUR ARE FOR A PERSON WHO HAS ASKED FOR NOTHING.**
**A NAME ON THIS BOARD MAY BE CALLED OUT. A NAME ENTERED ON THIS BOARD IS A NAME IN THIS CITY'S RECORD, AND THE RECORD IS A THING A CLERK CAN COME BACK WITH, AND THIS COURT HAS NO POWER TO TAKE A NAME OFF THE BOARD AGAINST THE PERSON'S WILL AND NO POWER TO PUT IT ON EXCEPT AT THEIR ASKING.**

The last line was not Rell's and Nahl had not written it, and it had come out of the translation of a thing the board's own preamble said in a sentence nobody reads, which is that a docket is a list of persons and not a list of matters, and that has been true since the block was cut and has cost this city about four people who cannot name.

Abel Kearn was not in the room and had not been asked to be. He had a right to be, because his matter was on the board, and the court had sent him notice, and the notice had gone to the upper street to a house and been given to a woman of sixty-four who is his mother and who cannot read it, and who had put it face down on the table under a bowl, because she has been getting things she cannot read on that table for a month and the trick with the bowl is hers and not the court's.

He came at the second bell. He had a piece of chalk in his hand, which he put down on the step when he came up it, and then picked up again, and then put down again, and the whole of the room saw that and nobody said anything about it, because the room has been in this case for two months and has learned that the only two things worth noticing are a number and a man's hand.

“Mr Kearn,” said Ansa Rell, from the well of the court. “You are before a court that has eleven places and your name is on the board and there is nothing for you today, and I am going to say that out loud before you say it, and then I am going to ask whether you want to say anything, and if you say nothing then you have said nothing and it is in the record as nothing.”

“Magistrate,” said Abel Kearn, “I have come four times and I have stood on this step and looked at a board since the eighth of last month and I have worked out how it works and I am going to tell you how it works and then I am going to do a thing and I would like you to let me do it before I do it.”

“All right.”

---

“There are twenty-two names,” he said. “I have counted them every time. And there are eleven places, and eleven of the twenty-two will be heard this month, and every month it is twenty-two or twenty-four or nineteen, and eleven, and the ones that get heard are the ones that are ready, and being ready in this city means one thing, and it does not mean being right. It means there is a document somewhere with a date on it that a person can swear to.

“My wall is a hundred and forty-one years old and it is in a book in Fallowditch and Fallowditch has said in writing, in its own hand, that it holds nothing anybody can swear to. And I have believed that, and I still believe it, and I have had a bad thing at home since the eighth of last month and I have a crack in my mother's wall that is the width of a thumbnail and a timber merchant who is stacking elm against it in the rain and is not a criminal and has said so and is right.

“And so I put my name on this board, on the eighth of last month, because a name on a board is a name somebody has to call out. That is what I wanted. I did not want a place. I wanted to be a name that somebody had to read.”

He stopped, and looked at the board for a while, and the wind came off the water at the angle it comes at.

“And then I worked out what a name on this board is, and it took me about a fortnight and I have not been to a room in a month where anybody was in my company, and nobody here is going to like it. A name on this board is not a thing that gets you heard. A name on this board is a name in this city’s record. And in eleven days a district wrote a letter saying *we hold nothing a person may swear to*, and the whole of that letter is true, and the whole of the reason it is true is that a person does not have anywhere to put a piece of paper about herself, and the reason she does not have anywhere to put it is that the rooms that exist for it are benches and a bench is a body and a body is not a person.

“And I have got a name on this board, and it is a name in the record, and it is a name that a clerk can come back with, and I do not have a holder and I do not have a page and I cannot swear anything, and I have been a name on a piece of chalk since the eighth of last month and I have gained nothing out of it except the ability to be produced.

“I am not prepared to be produced. That is the whole of it. I am thirty-five and I have put up nine walls in that district and I have never been hungry and I have got a mother in a house with a wall going and I am not prepared to be a name on a record with nothing under it. I have been a number in enough columns. I was a number in the count on the low water and the count was right about the number and wrong about me, and it is on a page, and I would like to be struck off it in my own hand and I cannot be, because the count was taken and a count cannot be un-counted, and that is the same machinery and it is the same man asking.”

The block was wet on the west face and dry on the east and the whole of the open air was listening, which is the one thing this court has that no room in this city has.

“Magistrate, I am going to withdraw my name off this board. Not my matter. My name. The matter will go on the next board and the next board is a month and the wall is going in a fortnight, and I know exactly what I am doing and I have worked it out and I have told you the working out so that it is in the record in my own words before I do it.”

Harrow said, “A name on a docket is not entered by a court and is not withdrawn by a court. It is entered by a person and it is withdrawn by a person, and there is no motion and no notice, and the court cannot hold you to it and cannot stop you and I am going to say that the court takes no view.”

“Do you want to be heard first,” said Rell. “That is a different thing and it is available and I am offering it and nobody is going to think less of you for taking it.”

“No,” said Abel Kearn.

“Why not.”

“Because I have said the thing I came to say and it is nine sentences long and I have said them, and if I am heard then I am a person who was heard and I am a person who was heard about a wall, and I am back to being a name. I would like to be a man who took his name off a board in the open air with a piece of chalk and was not heard, and I would like there to be one thing in this case that is a man doing a thing and not arguing about it.”

Nahl gave him the chalk.

He did not cross it out. He rubbed it out with his thumb, which took most of a minute because the board is old and the chalk on it has been gone over about four hundred times, and he rubbed until the board was the colour of the board, and then he put the chalk down on the step, and Nahl wrote under the list in her own hand:

> **NAME WITHDRAWN BY THE PERSON. THE MATTER REMAINS. THE COURT HAS NO POWER OVER EITHER AND TAKES NO VIEW.**
> **THE NAME WAS ENTERED ON THE EIGHTH OF THE SECOND MONTH AND WITHDRAWN ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE THIRD. IT CANNOT BE PUT BACK ON THIS BOARD.**

Twenty-one names. And eleven places, because a place is not made by a name being on a board; a place is made by a matter being ready, and the number of places has been eleven every month in nineteen years and it would have been eleven whatever was written in chalk.

And the clerk read the list out afterwards because a clerk does that, and the reading took a quarter of an hour, and at the end of it Nahl said the thing that Nahl says, and it is the reason the whole case is on a board and not in a book:

“*Kearn, upper street, party wall* may now be heard this month. It does not want to be. It is a boundary matter with a document in a district that has publicly said it holds nothing, and it is not ready, and it will lose its place next month and the month after. But it is on the list and there is a place, and a name matter may be moved up, and I am moving it up because it has been on this board since the eighth of last month and it is the only thing on it that a man withdrew his own name to put in a better position.

“Nobody move it,” said Abel Kearn, from the step, where he was still standing. “I have taken my name off it. Leave it where it is. It can wait for a mechanism.”

---

Nobody had a mechanism. That is the honest end of the fourteenth of the month and it took three-quarters of an hour in the open air to establish it, and it is in the record in nine hands, and there was no eleventh thing to say about it because there is no eleventh thing to say about it.

A matter is heard when a document can be sworn. A document is sworn where it is kept, or in a room that holds it, or before a body that has received it. There is no clause anywhere in this city that allows a person to carry a sworn page four miles to a room, because a person is not a page and a page is not a document and a document is not a room, and the machinery has no word for a room that is a woman's kitchen.

There were two people in this city who could have heard it, and there was one who had ever tried, and the trial is in the record in a room over a pump four miles from a bridge.

“Two shillings and threepence,” said Hollis Dune, who had walked to the court because he had been at the bar that morning and had come on, and who stood in the fourth of the eleven places and had no matter of his own and had not been asked for one. “Nine sheets at three a sheet, and a sheet of string and a bit of sealing, and the ninth is read out twice at a pump to two hundred people, and I would do it in a kitchen and I would not be paid and I would be doing it because I copied it once in a room I was not asked to be in.”

“And the woman who keeps it has a barrow,” said Rell, “and a name, and no right to appear in a support of a thing or to be named in it, and about a pound and eleven a quarter for keeping the nine sheets, and she has said in her own page that she wants a rate for a haul and a second barrow and that she is not asking for either yet.

“If I were a man with a wall, and I am not, and I am telling you what I would do, and I would go to a room over a pump and I would ask a refuse hauler whether she would swear a document about a wall in Fallowditch into her own page, and she would do it, because she is the door and that is what the door is for, and she would not be able to be a party in it, and she would not be paid for the half hour, and she would get a wall sworn for a man she has never met, and in four years she would be the first name anybody asks for whenever there is a document in this city that has nowhere to be.”

The court entered that. It is the shortest of the day's four entries and it is the one that has been entered twice since, in a different district, in a different hand, about a different document, and nobody in Orison has read the second one, and the second one is about a place with nine yards in it and no bench and no name.

Nobody thanked Abel Kearn. He had not asked. He asked, at the step, going down, one question, and he asked it to the magistrate and not to the court, and he asked it in a builder's voice and not in anybody else's.

“Magistrate. You counted five places on the low water and you said a place is the category this court came to get rid of. I have read that minute. I want to ask you for the thing I have been trying to ask for since the twenty-ninth and I could not get a sentence for it in front of you then, and I have got one now, and it is only six words, and I have said it to no one.

“Can I be counted as a place.”

Rell looked at him for a moment and then at the board with twenty-one names on it, and then at the block, which has been standing in the open air for four hundred years so that anybody may stand in front of it and be answered.

“No,” she said. “And I am going to tell you the reason in the plainest words I have, because you have earned a plain answer and because a man who has been told no nine times this month deserves to be told it well.

“A place is not a person and a person is not a place, and a court that has just spent two months taking places out of its arithmetic cannot put one back in for a builder with lime in his apron, and if I did it, then in about a year there would be eleven places on that board with names in them and no documents, and every one of them would be a person who had been told that the way to be heard is to be a location, and I would have made it up out of the ground I stand on.

“You are not a place. You are a man who took his name off a board in the open air because you did not want to be produced, and that is the whole of what you have done today, and it is the first thing in this case in nine weeks that was a man doing a thing instead of a room producing a document, and I have put it in the record in my own words, and it is not a ruling, and it is not a precedent, and nobody in this city is bound by it, including you.”

Kearn looked at the board for a while, with twenty-one names on it and eleven places and the wet on the west face of the block.

“Then I will wait for a mechanism,” he said, “and I have a wall going in a fortnight, and I have found out one thing about waiting for a mechanism, and it is that it costs exactly what it costs to be produced, and I can pay either, and this is the first time in nine weeks I have been able to choose.”

And the timber merchant on the upper street took the elm off the wall that a hundred and forty-one years of surveyors had agreed about, and he paid for the crack out of his own pocket and would not be repaid and did not want to be, and he was not a criminal and he had said so and he was right, and that is the whole of what happened to a party wall in the twelfth week of a case nobody has finished.
