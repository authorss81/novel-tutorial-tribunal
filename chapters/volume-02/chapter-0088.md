# Chapter 88: The Second Reading of a Quarter's Rate

The tide at the low water is a seven-day tide and the sixth bell is the turn of it, and at the turn the flat is walkable and the gate is half up and the water is coming, and about two hundred and forty people went up a mud bank in the cold at the sixth bell on the nineteenth of the fourth month because a man who sells salt had put his name under a rate.

Nobody called it a hearing. It is a pump and about two hundred people and a woman with a barrow reading out of a book.

She read the lid first, and it took a while, because the lid is read before anything else. The lid is a barrel lid nailed to a gate-side board and the rate is on it in the district's own small hand in chalk, and the chalk has been gone over about four hundred times since the seventeenth of the second month and the words have got the shine back on them.

> **HALF A FARTHING A BUCKET. THE FIRST OF EVERY QUARTER. THE SIXTH BELL. THIS IS THE FIRST TAX IN NINETEEN YEARS IN THIS DISTRICT AND IT IS THE RIGHT RATE AND IT BUYS THE THINGS THAT LET YOU APPEAL.**

Then she put the book on the lid and read the day's entries, and the first of them was the one that made the bank go quiet, and it was the fourteenth of the month, and it was the whole of it, including the price of the coarse.

Then she read the rule of the fifteenth, which was made by a bench of four for itself, in its own hand, on a day it appointed, in its own room.

> **WHERE ANY PERSON WHO HAS SET A RATE IN THIS DISTRICT WITHDRAWS HIS CONSENT TO IT IN WRITING AFTER THE RATE HAS BEEN READ ALOUD, THE RATE SHALL BE READ ALOUD AGAIN INSIDE THE QUARTER, AT THE SIXTH BELL, AT THE STANDPIPE, BY THE HOLDER, TO WHATEVER PEOPLE COME, AND THE WITHDRAWAL SHALL BE READ WITH IT.**
>
> **THE COST OF A READING IS ABOUT TWO HUNDRED PEOPLE GOING UP A BANK IN THE COLD, AND IT IS PAID BY THE PEOPLE WHO GO UP IT, AND THE BENCH HAS NO MONEY AND THIS BENCH KNOWS IT.**
>
> **THIS IS NOT A RIGHT. A RIGHT IS TOWARDS A PERSON. THIS IS TOWARDS A BARREL LID.**

Nobody laughed. That is worth saying, because a person standing in the cold who has walked up a mud bank to hear a barrel lid's contents and has been told by the district itself that the barrel lid is not a right would be within her rights to laugh, and about two hundred people did not laugh, and the reason they did not is that the third line is true and they had all worked it out on their own way up the bank.

Then she read the numbers, and she read them the way she reads everything, which is the same way every quarter, and there are four entries in that book and a figure in it and both of those things have changed in the last ten days and neither of them was changed by the bench.

> **THE THIRTY YEARS: A HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS AT TWO THIRDS.**
>
> **I AM GOING TO READ THAT TWICE AND I AM GOING TO TELL YOU WHERE IT CAME FROM AND IT DID NOT COME FROM THE BENCH AND IT DID NOT COME FROM ME AND IT CAME FROM THE GATE.**

And she did read it twice, and there was noise on the bank the second time, and she waited it out the way she waits out everything, and then she said the rest of it.

> **IT IS NOT A FIGURE OF MONEY. IT IS WHAT THE GATE HAND SAYS IT IS AND SHE SAYS IT IN THE PLAINEST WORDS IN THIS CITY, AND WHAT SHE SAYS IS A HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS AT TWO THIRDS, OVER THIRTY YEARS, AT THE HANDS OF ONE WOMAN, AND THAT THE NUMBER THAT MADE HER A CLAIM OF IT IS ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS AFTER HER OWN CORRECTION, AND THAT NEITHER OF THE TWO CAN BE PAID, AND THAT SHE HAS REFUSED IT AND WAS NOT COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE OVERRULED, AND THAT THE FIGURE STANDS IN THE BOOK BECAUSE A CLERK CANNOT CARRY A SHAPE AND WROTE A NUMBER, AND BECAUSE THE HOURS ON THE SLATE WERE WRONG AND THE SLATE HAS A LINE DOWN THE MIDDLE OF IT THAT STAYS, AND A PERSON IS NOT A MEASURING INSTRUMENT.**
>
> **THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS HAVE NO FIGURE. THEY WILL KEEP HAVING NO FIGURE UNTIL THIS BENCH AGREES WHAT A LOAD IS WORTH, AND A BENCH OF FOUR MAY DO THAT AND MAY NOT, AND IT IS NOT ANYBODY ELSE'S BUSINESS.**
>
> **THE RATE FOR A HAUL IS UNCHANGED. IT IS SET AT THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE. ONE OF THE FOUR WHO SET IT HAS WITHDRAWN FROM IT. THE RATE HAS NOT GONE UP AND HAS NOT GONE DOWN AND IT IS THE SAME HALF A FARTHING IT WAS ON THE FIRST OF MARCH, AND I AM THE ONE WHO ASKED FOR IT, AND NOBODY MAY APPEAL IT, AND THAT WAS ANSWERED OUT LOUD IN THIS ROOM ON THE FOURTH OF THE MONTH AND THE ANSWER IS IN THE BOOK AND I AM THE PERSON IT IS ABOUT.**

And then about two hundred people who had come up a mud bank in the cold for nine minutes of a man's name and a barrel lid went up the same bank to get to the pump, and there was a queue at the standpipe that had not been there at the sixth bell since the third of the month, and Cael Rundle was in it with his coat buttoned to the throat and his daughter of fourteen in front of him, and neither of them had known the other one was coming.

---

Two hundred feet away, at the gate, Bryn Sallow stopped working the gate for four seconds, which she does not do.

She heard it. There is no wind at the turn and the flat is open and the bank is not high. She heard a hundred and fifty hauls at two thirds read out twice by a woman with a barrow, and she heard the rest of it, and she stood with one foot on the sill-stone and one on the bank with two hundred feet of chain in her hands and she did not move, and then she went on working it, and the chain came up and the water came under and the flat began to go.

Nobody at the pump said a word about the gate hand and that was correct, because a bench of four reading a rate has nothing to say about a person at a gate, and because a hundred and fifty hauls at two thirds is thirty years of a duty with nobody in it, and there is nobody at the pump to be angry and only one person at the gate to be angry at, and she is forty-four years old and she stands there.

---

The rate has not gone up. That is the finding of the nineteenth of the fourth month and it is not a disappointment; it was never going to be anything else, and the bench entered on the seventeenth that it could not set the rate again with three of its four, or with four, this year, because it has no money, and that the reason it cannot set the rate again is the same reason it set it.

The rate has not gone up and it has not gone down and it is the same eighth of a penny a bucket that it has been since the first of March, and a man has put his name under it, and about two hundred and forty people have been to the pump in the cold to hear that, and nothing whatsoever about the water has changed.

And there is exactly one thing that has changed on the ground in three weeks, and it is not a rate and it is not a figure and it is not a man's name, and it happened on the second of this month and nobody noticed it for a fortnight, and it is a woman of sixty-one who has drawn at that standpipe for nineteen years with a yoke and two buckets and who has stopped.

---

Her name is Ottoline Fenn. She is sixty-one and she takes in washing at the upper end of the street and there are four other women who wash with her and she is the one who goes first because she is the one who gets up first, and she has drawn at the low-water standpipe every morning of nineteen years except about nine, and she does it at about the fourth bell with a yoke and two buckets, and then again at the sixth, and again at the eighth, and once at the tenth when there was a frost.

That is eight buckets a day. Half a farthing a bucket is an eighth of a penny. Eight buckets is a penny. A penny a day is seven pence a week, which is one pound ten shillings and fourpence a year, and a rate of half a farthing a bucket in a district of about two hundred and forty people is exactly that for her and about fourpence a year for most of the rest of them, and nobody in this district has ever asked a single person how many buckets their household takes, and the district cannot afford to ask and there is no form for it.

She worked it out in about four minutes on the second of the fourth month, sitting on the step of her own house, and she did it twice, and she did not tell anybody, and she went down to the standpipe on the third of the month and paid the penny, and she went on the fourth, and on the fifth, and then on the sixth of the fourth month she did not go.

She went four miles up the fen road to the ninth bridge.

There is no rate on that pump. It is in a different district and the rate on it is the ninth bridge's district's business, and the ninth bridge's district has never had a water rate in its life that anybody has heard of, and a person walking up from the low water can fill a bucket at that pump at the fourth bell, before the count, and be back on the flat before the seventh with about half of it in the yoke and the other half gone into a bridge four miles away, and there is nobody at either end of that walk who has any power over any part of it.

It takes her most of a morning. She is sixty-one. She is a washer and she is the best washer in the upper street and there are four women who wash with her, and the money in this district has not changed shape at all in three weeks, and she is one pound ten shillings and fourpence a year poorer and she is the reason, and there is no name for that.

The System put the reading in front of Ilyan while Wenna Tace was getting the barrow out of the shed for the run, and it was a record of a pump and not a rule about the world.

**THE SECOND READING OF A QUARTER'S RATE, THE NINETEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, AT THE SIXTH BELL, AT THE STANDPIPE AT THE LOW WATER, IN THE OPEN AIR, TO ABOUT TWO HUNDRED PEOPLE, READ ALOUD BY THE HOLDER OF THE FIFTH TERM OF THE CHARTER OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY, WHO IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO CAN READ.**
**THE RATE HAS NOT GONE UP. AN EIGHTH OF A PENNY A BUCKET, AS SINCE THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH. THE ENTRY OF THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF LAST MONTH STANDS AND IS NOT AMENDED AND IS NOT WITHDRAWN.**
**READ WITH IT, UNDER A RULE MADE BY THE BENCH OF FOUR FOR ITSELF ON THE SEVENTEENTH: THE NAME OF ONE OF THE FOUR WHO SET THAT RATE, WHO HAS WITHDRAWN HIS CONSENT TO IT IN WRITING, ENTERED ON THE SIXTEENTH IN HIS OWN HAND, WITH A DATE, AND STANDING TWICE IN THE BOOK ON PURPOSE.**
**THE FIGURE OF THIRTY YEARS IS READ AS ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS AFTER THE CORRECTION GIVEN BY THE PERSON IT IS OWED TO, AND AS A HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS AT TWO THIRDS BY THE SAME PERSON'S OWN SAYING, AND NEITHER CAN BE PAID, AND THE PERSON OWED REFUSED IT AND WAS NOT COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE OVERRULED.**
**THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS HAVE NO FIGURE AND WILL KEEP HAVING NO FIGURE UNTIL THE DISTRICT AGREES A RATE FOR A LOAD.**
**THE COST OF A READING IS ABOUT TWO HUNDRED PEOPLE GOING UP A BANK IN THE COLD, AND IT IS PAID BY THE PEOPLE WHO GO UP IT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED HERE: WHETHER ANY RATE IN THIS DISTRICT IS FAIR. WHETHER EITHER FIGURE IS RIGHT. WHETHER THE RATE MAY BE APPEALED BY ANY PERSON. IT MAY NOT, AND THAT WAS ANSWERED IN PUBLIC ON THE FOURTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE ANSWER IS IN THE DISTRICT'S BOOK.**

---

Nobody found out until the twentieth, and it was found out like this. Merrick Vaunt, who is nineteen and cannot read the entry and gets most of it, reads the book twice a week in a room over a pump because the woman who keeps it told him to on the fifth of the month and paid him for the paper out of a pound and eleven a quarter. On the second reading he noticed that Wenna Tace was not reading anything at all. She was counting. She had been counting the buckets that went into the standpipe since the first of the month because she is the only person in the district who can read and she had begun, without deciding to, to find out who came.

She had started counting them on the first of the month, when she took on the second reader, and she had got to about the ninth of the month before she noticed that she was counting, and the reason she was counting was that she was the only person who could read and she had begun, without deciding to, to find out who came.

She said it at the second bell on the twentieth, to Corva Silt, in about four sentences, and Corva Silt wrote it down and then put her pen down and did not write any more for a while.

“She told one person and one person only,” said Corva Silt. “A man at a booth at the top of Weir Lane, who is one of the four that set the rate, because he asked her at his counter on the eighteenth and she told him, and he could not do one single thing with it. She has not told this bench. She has not told the man she buys her salt off, which is the same man, and she has not told the four women she washes with. As far as this district knows, she is still coming down to that pump every morning, and she has not been down to it since the sixth of the month.”

“Has she been to the ninth bridge’s pump?”

“She has been to the ninth bridge's pump at the fourth bell every morning for eleven days and it takes her most of the morning and she is sixty-one and she is the best washer in the upper street and there are four women who wash with her and there is no rate on that pump in a different district and there is nobody in that district who has anything to do with a woman at a pump four miles away.”

Corva Silt then did the thing that made her the archivist of her own mistake for the second time in a month, and she did it in the ordinary way, and she read it back to herself twice in a loud voice in her own office, and it went in the book.

> **ENTERED IN THE BOOK OF THE BENCH OF FOUR, THE TWENTIETH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, AND NOT SERVED ON ANYBODY AND NOT ENTERED BY ANY COURT.**
>
> **A RATE OF AN EIGHTH OF A PENNY A BUCKET SET ON THIS DISTRICT ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH HAS PRODUCED, BETWEEN THE SECOND AND THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE FOURTH, ONE CUSTOMER WHO HAS STOPPED USING THE STANDPIPE. SHE IS A WASHER, SIXTY-ONE, AND SHE TAKES EIGHT BUCKETS A DAY, AND EIGHT BUCKETS IS A PENNY, AND A PENNY A DAY IS ONE POUND TEN SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE A YEAR, AND IN A DISTRICT OF ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY PEOPLE THIS BENCH'S RATE FALLS ON HER AT EIGHT TIMES WHAT IT FALLS ON MOST OF THE REST OF THEM.**
>
> **SHE NOW FILLS AT A PUMP FOUR MILES AWAY IN ANOTHER DISTRICT WHERE THERE IS NO RATE, AT THE FOURTH BELL BEFORE A COUNT, AND WALKS BACK. NOBODY IN EITHER DISTRICT HAS ANY POWER OVER ANY PART OF THAT WALK. THE LOST PENNY IS NOT ENTERED IN THIS BENCH'S BOOK AS A LOSS BECAUSE NOBODY COLLECTS BUCKETS, AND A TAX THAT LOSES A CUSTOMER LOSES NOTHING THAT ANYBODY CAN COUNT.**
>
> **THE RATE IS THE RIGHT RATE. IT WAS CALLED THE RIGHT RATE BY A RATEPAYER OF THIS LANE BEFORE ANYBODY SPOKE. IT HAS BEEN UNCHANGED SINCE THE FIRST OF THE MONTH BEFORE LAST. A BENCH OF FOUR ENTERS THAT A RATE WHICH FALLS AT EIGHT TIMES THE AVERAGE ON THE POOREST HOUSEHOLD IN A DISTRICT IS A RATE THAT FALLS ON THE POOREST HOUSEHOLD, AND THAT THE BENCH KNEW THE RATE WAS THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE AND KNEW THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE IS A RATE FOR A LOAD, AND THAT A BENCH THAT WILL NOT SAY WHAT A LOAD IS WORTH CANNOT KNOW WHO A RATE FALLS ON, AND HAS ENTERED THAT IT DID NOT KNOW AND DID NOT ASK.**

---

At the ninth bridge, on the evening of the twentieth, a builder of thirty-five with lime in the seam of his apron watched a woman of sixty-one come past the north end of the deck at about the seventh bell with a yoke and two buckets, half full, going down the fen road on foot, and he did not know her and she did not know him and there is no reason in the world why he should have looked up from the rail.

“Ilya,” said Abel Kearn, from the step of the workroom, with a piece of chalk in his hand, “she has been coming past here eleven days running at the seventh bell, going four miles, and she is sixty-one, and there is no rate on that pump. I have watched her every one of them. And I have got a wall at my mother's that is a hundred and forty-one years old and a survey in a district that says in its own hand that it holds nothing, and I have been sitting on this step for a month waiting for a mechanism, and one has just walked past me with a yoke and two buckets and does not know I exist.”

“Do not go and tell her,” said Ilyan.

“I am not going to go and tell her,” said Kearn. “I have thought about it for about a minute and I am not, and I want you to notice that a mechanism you take off a person who has not offered it to you is the machinery that put my name on a board, and I have already done that once this year and I did not like it.”

He put the chalk in his pocket and went down the ramp, and the span took a load at the ninth bell and the pale permanent set has not moved and the count at that change was taken by one man and is a count and not a record, and there is nobody in this city that four hundred people can be sent for except a man of thirty-one who was standing at the counter and had said nothing at all.

He put the chalk in his pocket and went down the ramp, and the span took a load at the ninth bell and the pale permanent set has not moved and the count at that change was taken by one man and is a count and not a record, and there is nobody in this city that four hundred people can be sent for except a man of thirty-one who was standing at the counter and had said nothing at all.
