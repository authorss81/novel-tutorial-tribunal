# Chapter 58: A Loss Taken on the Record

On the twenty-first morning Ilyan could not feel his left foot from the ankle down, and he knew before he put a shoe on that this was the second day and not the first, and the second day is where a thing stops being an accident and becomes a rate.

He had tried four things. Cold water on it at the fifth bell, which did nothing. Standing on it, which was worse. Counting the loads on the flat from the top of the bank, which he had done on the fourteenth, the seventeenth, the nineteenth and the twentieth and had got four hundred and six, four hundred and nine, four hundred and nine and four hundred and eleven, and which was the only part of his working day that had got easier.

And the smell. That was the other thing and it was the other half of the problem and he had stopped trying to explain it to himself about a day after the token had gone cold on the milestone.

The low water was in his nose in a lane with nothing in it. On the fourteenth, in a room with a stove in it, the smell of the flat had arrived on the flat, and the water underneath, and the water was a night smell, wet and cold and with a sweetness in it, and he had never in his life been on that flat at night and had no business knowing what it was like, and on the twenty-first morning the two layers were so close together that he could not get at either one of them, and the lane where he was standing had hay in it and a dog in it and a woman doing something with a bucket, and what he could smell was four hundred loads at the second change of the tide.

Sera had watched him do it for three mornings and had not once said a word about it, and on the twenty-first she said one thing.

“It is going to be four days or it is going to be a month,” she said. “Those are the two shapes it comes in. In four days it fades when you are away from a bank and comes back in about ten seconds when you are not. In a month you will not notice it and that will be worse, because you will have stopped being able to tell that it is not yours.

“I am not a physician and I am not going to pretend. I am going to say that you are going to walk into a room in an hour and be asked to read a schedule you cannot read, and that you will be tempted to look at the thing the way you looked at it on the nineteenth, and I want you to decide now, tonight, what you are going to do when it happens, because deciding now costs you nothing and deciding in a room costs you the room.”

“I am going to lose the leg,” Ilyan said.

“Yes,” said Sera. “That is one of the two answers and it is the one I would choose, and I would like you to hear me say that I have no idea whether it is true.”

---

The field sat at the fourth bell on the twenty-first day with the tide on the turn, and the question was one sentence long and Harrow put it in the record in the flat voice.

“Whether the ninth-bridge settlement, entered as a precedent witness available to other districts, has any force in a district that has printed it, quoted it, or built an instrument out of it without adopting it as a rule of that district.”

It is the same distinction the court drew in the first week of the case, between a precedent witness and a ruling, and the court has never regretted it, and it has never before cost anybody anything.

Kett argued the small answer and argued it well: the settlement is about a span. It has a deck in it and a keeper in it and a review date at the fourth morning bell of the third month, and a review date is a fact about one span and cannot be a fact about four districts at once. Nothing in the ten terms says any district adopts them, and H-9 is therefore not the settlement and cannot be, and the sentence about a trespasser never reached this gate and that is why we are all standing in the rain.

Harrow held the argument and held it correctly and then said, to the field:

“Mr Kett has argued the true thing. I want to say the other true thing before anybody relies on the first one.

“Three days ago, at the First Verdict in Orison, a delegate from the low fen stood in a basalt court and said that his district had adopted the ninth-bridge settlement entire as its own maintenance term, and that its keeper had called the deck clear twice, and that both times the load was off the deck in nine minutes, and that both times the keeper had been paid out of road funds to the person on a line in chalk. And a delegate from the cut banks said the same thing and said his clerk had cried about the water on the seventh day.

“Those two men adopted the settlement by printing it out of a public book and writing it on their own paper. That is the whole of their adoption. It took an afternoon. It is not a formality and I am not going to call it one, because it is the only way a district with no terms of its own has ever got terms, and it worked, and both men have said in open court that it worked.

“If I rule that the settlement does not reach a district that has not adopted it, and it does not, and that is the answer, then those two men adopted a copy of nothing. Their keepers have no power. Their pay lines are for hours that a term does not require. And neither of them can produce their own maintenance terms, and they could not produce them at the fen crossing, and they cannot produce them now, and one of them has one bridge and a peat cart that cannot turn and thirty-two years in the post.

“Mr Kett has argued my ruling. I am telling the room what my ruling costs before I rule.”

And then he said, “The attached record-holder has a request on this and it is his to make and nobody may make it for him, and I will read it because he entered it in a writing on the twentieth and asked that it be read in full.”

> **THE ATTACHED RECORD-HOLDER REQUESTS A NAMED DECLARATION THAT THE NINTH-BRIDGE SETTLEMENT BINDS NO DISTRICT THAT HAS NOT ADOPTED IT AS A RULE OF THAT DISTRICT, AND ASKS THAT THE DECLARATION BE ENTERED IN THE NINTH-BRIDGE RECORD AND SENT TO EVERY BENCH THAT HAS PRINTED OR QUOTED THE SETTLEMENT SINCE THE RULING.**

It is a clean request and it is a good request and it was going to be granted in about nine minutes, and every person in that field knew it, and the reason everybody knew it was that it was right.

Ilyan stood up off the step, which took a hand on the rope, which Tarin did not stop and did not help with.

“Yes,” he said. “I withdraw it.”

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds.

“I withdraw it in full and I withdraw it on the record and I am not asking anybody to hold it for me and take it out later, because the thing it would do to two men is not a thing that gets taken out later. I am going to say why in about four sentences and then I am going to sit down, because I have to sit down, and I would like that entered before I start, and Mr Quill is going to enter it and she is going to enter it exactly as I say it and not as a clerk would.”

“Entered,” said Roke.

“I made this citable in a corridor at two in the morning a month ago, and I did not send it to anybody, and I have said in four rooms for a month that a remedy copied past the place it was made becomes a different instrument, and that is a true sentence and I have said it every time I have been asked for anything. And this is the consequence of that sentence, and the consequence is that the sentence does not travel, and the day a man decides that his own instrument is the exception to his own argument because the exception is him, he is the thing he has been describing, and I would have been able to do that with a speech and I have not got a speech and I am not going to look for one at six in the evening in a smell.

“The two men who printed it did not print it to be bound by it. They printed it because they had nothing and a cart that could not turn. And they did not ask me. And I cannot get to either of them in a day, and a decision on a public instrument with notice to every named party is not a decision you make in nine minutes and pay for in a fortnight at four miles' distance, and I am the only person in this field who would be affected by that and it is the one thing I am actually able to be the authority on.”

“You are not an authority on it,” said Harrow.

“No,” Ilyan said. “I am a man with a name on a paper, and the reason I know what that costs is that I put the name there, and if I withdraw this on the record then for once the paper and the name and the thing are all pointed in the same direction, and I would like that to be true once.”

Then a cart came down the fen road at the fifth bell with a folded sheet in it in a keeper's hand, and the boy on the box was a peat man and had been given the sheet at the weighhouse four hours earlier, and he handed it in and did not get down, and it was two lines.

> **LOW FEN. FIFTH BELL, THE TWENTIETH DAY. IF IT BINDS NOWHERE I HAVE A COPY OF NOTHING AND A CART THAT CANNOT TURN. YOU DO NOT OWE ME THE POWER. I WANT YOU TO KNOW THAT I KNOW, AND THAT I HAVE HAD TWO DAYS WITH IT.**
> **A. NYLE, KEEPER.**

Harrow read it out twice, because the field's record was a record and a keeper's letter about his own bridge is a witness, and a witness gets read twice.

“Entered,” he said. “Entered with the keeper's own declaration that the district's term is a copy of a public instrument, which is true, and with his own statement that he does not want the power and wants the term, which is also true, and I am going to say that the second one is the reason this court has not got a better instrument in eleven days and it is going to be the reason it does not get one for another eleven, and I am not going to be talked out of it by anybody including myself.”

Kett said, from the step, in a voice with nothing in it at all, “He is a keeper of one bridge. He has written a letter to a stranger in a smell to tell him not to do him a kindness.”

“Yes,” said Ilyan said. “And that letter is the reason the record reads the way it is going to read, and if I had not withdrawn it before it came I would have had to be asked to withdraw it after, in front of a man I had hurt, and that is the version of this where I am told to be reasonable by somebody who has been kind.”

---

What he got was smaller, and he said so out loud before anybody else could.

“Two things and neither is what I asked for. The first is a declaration that a citation is not an adoption, entered in the ninth-bridge record and sent to every bench that has printed or quoted the settlement since the ruling, and it will say on its face that a district which has printed or quoted a public instrument is not bound by it and is not in breach of anything, and that H-9 is an instrument of the Halloway bench and of nobody else. That is worth having and it is worth having a great deal to a district, and it is not worth having to a woman at a gate.”

“And the second thing is four hours.”

Corva Silt had it in front of her, and she had fought for it for two days, and she read it in the room, and she read it and then said, “Before I read it, I want to say that this is not in the ninth bridge's charter, it is not in my instrument, and the district has not agreed to it, and the district's clerk is not in the room and I have not had his answer, and I am reading it anyway because the court asked me to and because I have been a clerk for six years and I have read a great many things I did not have the answer to.”

> ENTERED IN THE NINTH-BRIDGE RECORD, ON THE TWENTY-FIRST DAY, IN A FIELD OPENED ON THE NINTH-BRIDGE MATTER.
> A CITATION IS NOT AN ADOPTION. A DISTRICT THAT HAS PRINTED, QUOTED, OR COPIED THE NINTH-BRIDGE SETTLEMENT SINCE THE RULING IS NOT BOUND BY IT, IS NOT IN BREACH OF IT, AND MAY NOT BE ENTRUSTED WITH IT BY ANY BENCH OR COURT.
> THE ATTACHED RECORD-HOLDER'S REQUEST FOR A DECLARATION THAT THE SETTLEMENT BINDS NO DISTRICT THAT HAS NOT ADOPTED IT IS WITHDRAWN BY HIM, ON THE RECORD, ON THE TWENTY-FIRST DAY, AFTER A LETTER FROM A. NYLE, KEEPER OF THE LOW FEN, WAS READ IN THE FIELD.

> AND, WITHOUT ANY TERM OF THE NINTH-BRIDGE CHARTER AND AS A MATTER OF THE FIELD'S RECORD ONLY: FOUR HANDS AT THE LOW-WATER GATE, FOUR HOURS A DAY, FROM THE FIFTH BELL, BY NAME, ON A DAY-BOOK LINE, IN CHALK, AT THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE, OUT OF THE DISTRICT'S ROAD FUNDS, FOR EIGHT DAYS FROM THE TWENTY-FIRST, AND THE BALANCE OF THE TIDE ENTERED AS UNPAID.
> **THE DISTRICT'S CLERK HAS NOT AGREED. THE DISTRICT MAY REFUSE IT ON THE THIRD DAY OF NEXT WEEK AND IF IT DOES NOT REFUSE IT THE WAGE IS PAID AND NOBODY ASKS WHY.**

“The difference between four hours and the tide,” said Bryn Sallow, “is four hours and a half in the morning and about six in the evening, and I have known that since I was fourteen and nobody has ever asked me how I know it.”

“You have just told the court,” said Harrow, “and it is in the record, and the court has no power to make you keep it and you may withdraw it.”

“I am not withdrawing it,” said Bryn Sallow. “I want it in a column and I want my name on it and I do not want the word *gate hand* on it, because *gate hand* is a post and I am not a post, and I have been a post for thirty years and my name is Bryn and it is Bryn Sallow and it has been on a gate in chalk twice a year since I was twenty-eight because somebody had to write who was on the gate and I wrote it and nobody ever took it down.”

Harrow said, “Entered: the gate hand's hours at four hours a day for eight days from the twenty-first, and the gate hand's request that the name on the line be her name and not the office.”

Nobody in the field noticed the man on the step go out. There is a great deal that nobody notices in a field, and it is nearly always a person walking off, and nearly always the same person.

---

He was a quarter of a mile up the bank, sitting on the milestone with his back against it, when the shaking started, and it was not fear and it was not the leg, and it was in both hands, so that he had to put them on his knees and hold them.

The token was in his coat and it was hot again, and he understood at that point that he did not have a choice about this at all, because the thing in his pocket had decided the terms of the arrangement some weeks earlier in a city four thousand miles away, and the only thing still open was whether he went and got the rest of it on his own or waited for it to come.

He took it out.

The low water went. The lane came back — the hay and the dog and the woman with the bucket — and the two layers of it went with it, the day and the night, the flat and the water, all of it, and his nose was a nose again, and his left leg was his own leg, cold from the ankle to the knee in the ordinary way of a man who has been sitting on a stone in the cold.

It lasted fifty minutes.

He counted them, because it was the only thing he could do, and at the fifty-first minute it came back, and it came back wrong, and the wrong was this: it came back with the water *over* the flat, and underneath both layers there was a layer that was not a smell at all. It was the sound of a chain on a gate moving before anybody touched it, at the wrong end of the afternoon, from about four hundred yards away, in his right hand.

He got up and walked down the bank again and it took him eleven minutes, which it had taken him four days ago, and the leg was his own and the smell was layered and his hand was full of a sound.

Sera was on the step with the page in her coat. Ansa Rell was on the bank with her clerk, four days early for a sitting she had adjourned to the eleventh, in a district with one bench and a magistrate and eleven benches' worth of what was coming, because a field was open and a magistrate who is a magistrate can walk into an open field.

“You withdrew it,” Ansa Rell said.

“Yes.”

“Why.”

Ilyan looked at the flat, and the nine people on it with barrows, and the gate, and the girl of fourteen who was not in the traveller book and walked to the pump every morning and had been told by a carter that she was a trespasser.

“Because I got a letter four days ago from a man I have never met telling me he does not want what I asked for, and he is four days away and I am not, and if I had won it I would have taken something off his bridge in a room he was not in, on the strength of a sentence I wrote in a corridor about being citable.” He put his hands in his coat, because the right one was doing something he did not want a magistrate to see. “And because the declaration I wanted would be true and true is not the same as good, and I found that out on the nineteenth with a leg.”

Ansa Rell was quiet for a moment.

“I am going to say the thing nobody in that field said, because I have been a magistrate for eleven years and it is my trade and it is worth something. If you had won it — and you would have won it, and it would have gone in clean in about nine minutes — then in four districts the answer to a man with a bell, for the rest of your life, would be *that is the ninth bridge, and the ninth bridge is the ninth bridge*. And you would be quoted. Not argued with. Quoted, by clerks, in rooms you will never be in, for a sentence you wrote at two in the morning without a reason.

“You have just made yourself a smaller man in nine benches and a larger one in a room with a keeper in it, and there is no court in this city that would have told you to do it, and I am telling you that you did the right thing and that I am not going to be thanked for it, because what you did was not administration. It was a person declining to be convenient, and there is no mechanism anywhere in this city for that and there never has been.”

She looked at the gate, where Bryn Sallow was doing four hours' work in a tide and coming back at it, and four names were going to go on a board in chalk at the gate from the twenty-first, on four hours a day, which was Bryn Sallow's own demand and not the court's, and which was the only instrument in this entire case that a person had asked for.

“The adjourned sitting is on the twenty-ninth,” Ansa Rell said. “I am going to put the ward question to Mr Vale in writing and I am going to put your withdrawn request in the same paper as a thing that has been asked for and refused, because a request that is withdrawn on the record is worth more to a court than a request that was never made, and a clerk in nine benches is going to read the two of them together and understand something neither of them says alone.

“And then we are going to sit in a room about a ward, and there is no ward, and the ward is where the people who have to be paid live, and I want you in the room, and I want you in it as a man who has already lost something, because the choice that is going to be put in front of that room is between a clean rule that shuts the gate and gets paid for, and a charter that four hundred people can read and that nobody can afford, and there is no third thing, and I have looked.”

“There is a third thing,” Ilyan said, and then stopped, because he had said it and had nothing after it, and Ansa Rell waited, and he had to find it in the open, in front of a magistrate and a clerk.

“There is a third thing and I have written it once and it took a bridge and a guild and a child and a landlord and a clerk's slip on the back of a piece of paper,” he said. “And it is a charter with ten terms and a review and an appeal and an exit, and four of the ten terms are about a bridge and only about a bridge, and it is the best thing I have ever been near and it is in four districts and it has a gate on it. So the third thing exists. It is just that nobody has ever written one for a ward, and I am the only person alive who has tried, and I did it with a keeper, and I could not do it without a keeper, and that is the answer to your question and it is a bad one and it is the true one.”
