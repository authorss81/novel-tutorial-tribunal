# Chapter 99: What the Volume Is For, On the Ground

Nothing happened for three weeks, and the three weeks are part of it.

A bench of four in a district four miles from a bridge had a rate for a load in its book and no money, and a number of loads in a barrow in a hauler's head, and two debts in one unit and neither of them in any coin. A man in a building with no name on the door had a day book with nine hundred and forty forms in a box on the fourth shelf and a quarter of a sentence in a record and four days of sweeping entered against his name in the same column as the work. A clerk of thirty-eight had a pitch back at the bottom of a slipway and nine sentences in a bench of five's book. A recordwright of a public record had ten words with a crease in them, entered under her licence, in a cold room, and had stopped asking a desk questions.

All of that was true on the twenty-eighth of the fifth month and all of it was still true on the twenty-eighth of the sixth, and on the nineteenth day of the sixth a man of thirty-one wrote down the only sentence he had about it, and the sentence is in his own book and in no record but his, and it is that the whole of the last four months of this case arrived, and none of it was secure, and the difference between the two facts is a room over a pump where a page is kept by a person who cannot appeal from one word of it.

And then, on the fourteenth of the sixth month, somebody who had not been in the case at all came down a lane in a district seven miles from Halloway and eleven miles from a bridge, with a slate under her arm, and she wanted to know whether she had a claim.

---

Salla Kern is forty-four. She has lived in the district of Nethergill for nineteen years and has carted nothing and driven nothing and has kept, since she was thirty-three, a slate off the wall of a weighhouse office where a man who has paid her every pay day for eleven years writes down what he wants written down and then takes the slate home on his shoulder.

That is the whole of it. There is no book. The man is not a villain; he is sixty-one and pays her in coin on a pay day and cannot read and did not know for about four years that there was a line on the charter of another district that said a work record was portable, and when she told him, in a lane, on the eleventh of the month, he said, is that so, and went on loading peat.

She had the charter. Everybody in Nethergill has some of the charter, because a bench of four in that district swore a copy out of it by candlelight in a kitchen on the first of the third month and read it out twice at a pump by a woman of fifty-one who is not paid. She has nine sheets and they are sworn and they are in a folder in a house in Nethergill and she cannot read a clause.

She read the fourth term, which is the one that carries it. She cannot read a clause. On the second of the sixth a man who reads notices for money and is paid by the line read it out loud to her in a public house on the low side, and afterwards he told her in about thirty words what it said, and what it said, in the words of the ninth-bridge instrument, is that the work record is portable.

“And that is me,” said Salla Kern. “That is my eleven years and it is on a slate and it is going with me. Where is the man to complain to.”

That is what she said, and it is a fair sentence, and it took a day and a half of a cart and eleven miles and a night in a village to bring it here, and it is the first time in four months that anybody has put one of the ten terms of the ninth-bridge instrument to a use that a person believed in and that turned out to be a different thing.

---

The room over a pump on the fifteenth of the sixth month at the second bell had about nineteen people in it, which is a great many for that room, and the water was out and the flat was walkable and the gate was half up.

Corva Silt read the fourth term out, word for word, from the holder's copy, at a bench's own rate and not a court's, and the fourth term of that charter is a copy of the eighth term of the ninth-bridge instrument and it is marked as a copy, and the whole of the promise is this, and it is a real one, and a court made it four months ago.

> **THE FOURTH TERM, WHICH IS MARKED IN THE SHEET AS A COPY OF THE EIGHTH TERM OF THE INSTRUMENT OF THE NINTH BRIDGE. ACCESS, HOUSING AND WORK; THAT A PERSON NOT ENTERED IN A LAWFUL-TRAVELER REGISTER IS NOT A TRESPASSER BY THAT FACT ALONE; THAT A KEEPER ADMITS ANY PERSON ON FOOT, OR WITH A HANDCART, OR WITH A WHEELBARROW, WHO OFFERS NO LOAD AND DOES NOT ENTER THE LOAD PATH; THAT THE WORK RECORD IS PORTABLE; THAT THE TENANCY IS PROTECTED UNTIL AN APPEAL OR A REVIEW SAYS OTHERWISE AND IS NOT ENDED BY A REFUSAL; THAT THE LANDLORD IS BOUND; AND THAT THE PROTECTION IN THAT TERM IS NOT CONDITIONAL ON A PERSON SIGNING ANYTHING.**

“Eleven years of pay days,” said Salla Kern, from the step, with the slate under her arm. “Eleven years of that man taking my slate home and bringing it back with a figure on it and giving me the coin, and it is portable. It goes with me. That is what it says. That is the plainest thing anybody has ever read to me.”

“It says that,” said Wenna Tace. “It does not say there is one.”

“There is one. It is on a wall.”

“It is on a wall in a weighhouse office and he takes it home at the end of the day and brings it back on the pay day and if he dies it goes in a box with his things, and I have had a look at it because the boat boy read it to me and he cannot read it, and there is one line in it for every pay day since he started, and the lines are figures, and the figures are loads and the loads have no names on them, and there is no name on that slate and there is no name on the wall and there is no name in a room, because a man wrote a figure down because a figure is what he wanted written down.”

“Then he is the one who keeps it.”

“He is the one who keeps it, and he keeps it in his own house, and he is sixty-one, and he is a person, and I have never seen him, and you have. And if you went back to Nethergill and told him that the work record of a person is portable, he would say, is that so, and he would go on loading peat, and in about four years he would stop, and then the slate would be a slate in a house with nobody in it.”

---

And here is what it is, and it is the whole of the case showing up again in a different shape, and nobody in that room said it out loud except a man with about two hundred guild words who was not a party to it and could not be.

The case began with a child under a bridge, in a household that was not in a book, and the child was safe inside four chapters and the household was not. The household's problem was not that it was refused. It was that a rule existed, worked, was printed, was copied, and had a person in it, and the person was in it by name in two districts and there was no page on which the person could be *found*.

Salla Kern's problem is not that her eleven years are not portable. It is that there is nothing to carry, and the reason there is nothing to carry is that a man wrote figures on a slate for eleven years because figures are what a slate is for, and the man has no obligation in this city to write her name anywhere, and there is no form for it, and there is no line on which he could be paid to write it, and asking him to write it is a favour and a favour is a thing this district does not do by the ninth bridge's own tenth term, which is on a gate board four miles away in a district that has never heard of her.

“Can I be named,” she said.

The System put the room in front of Ilyan Vester on the fifteenth, on the fen road, and it was a record of a room and not a rule about the world.

**IN THE ROOM OVER A PUMP, THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH, AT THE SECOND BELL, NINETEEN PEOPLE.**
**A PERSON OF THE DISTRICT OF NETHERGILL, WHICH IS SEVEN MILES FROM THIS DISTRICT AND ELEVEN FROM A BRIDGE, HAS ASKED WHETHER SHE HAS A CLAIM UNDER A TERM OF A CHARTER WHICH BOTH DISTRICTS HAVE RECEIVED, ON AN ELEVEN-YEAR RECORD OF HER OWN WORK WHICH EXISTS ON A SLATE.**
**WHAT THE ROOM ESTABLISHED: THAT THE CLAIM IS ABOUT A RECORD, AND THAT THE RECORD EXISTS, AND THAT THE RECORD IS ON A SLATE IN A HOUSE, AND THAT THE LINES ON IT ARE FIGURES AND HAVE NO NAMES ON THEM, AND THAT THE PERSON WHO WRITES THE FIGURES HAS WRITTEN THEM ELEVEN YEARS IN HIS OWN HOUSE WITH NO OBLIGATION TO ANYBODY.**
**WHAT THE ROOM DID NOT ESTABLISH: WHETHER A SLATE IN A HOUSE IS A WORK RECORD. WHETHER A WORK RECORD MAY BE BROUGHT INTO A ROOM FOUR MILES AWAY BY A PERSON WHO IS NOT A PARTY IN IT. WHETHER THE SHORT COPY IN THAT DISTRICT AND THE COPY THE CARRIER WALKS WITH ARE THE SAME COPY. THEY ARE NOT. THE SHORT ONE CAME DOWN A LANE ON A CART WITH NO SEAL AND NO CLERK. THE CARRIED ONE IS COMPLETE.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A PROMISE CAN BE USED BY A PERSON WHO IS NOT INSIDE IT. WHETHER THE DISTANCE A COPY TRAVELLED IS PART OF WHAT IT SAYS. WHETHER A FIGURE WITH NO NAME ON IT IS A RECORD ABOUT A PERSON.**

---

The notice went into the chamber on the sixteenth of the sixth with a full copy of the terms, and the full copy went up the fen road on a cart at the district's cost, three shillings and ninepence, and a day and a half, and the day and a half was a day of a clerk of the district of Halloway who is not paid for reading anything and a day of a boy of nineteen who is not paid at all, and both of those are in the same book as the four days of a man's sweeping in a building with no name on the door, and nobody has drawn a line between the two prices and I have looked.

And the short copy is the copy that was carried. That is the thing nobody had put together until the sixteenth of the sixth, and it took four people in a room in Orison about eleven minutes.

A copy of a charter of this district was sworn out by candlelight in a kitchen seven miles from here on the first of the third month and read out twice at a pump by a woman of fifty-one who is not paid, and it binds nobody and cannot be came back for, and it is a true copy of the sworn sheets.

And a copy of it was carried on a cart down a lane on the twenty-seventh of the month before that, with no seal and no clerk, and it is short of the second half of the fourth term, and the second half of the fourth term is the clause with a man's name in it.

Which means that a district with a complete copy is a district that knows a man exists and can produce him, and a district with a short copy is a district that has a charter and a page and an appeal and a rate and a fifth term, and no name in it, and does not know that there is a person in this city who can be sent for.

Salla Kern's district has the short copy. She has it in a folder in a house. It is a sworn copy and it is true and it is short.

“Which one did she read,” said Maren Kett, who had come from the chamber and who is a counsel of nineteen years and the advocate of the limitation and has not been in this case since the twenty-fifth of the fourth month.

“The short one,” said Sera Quill.

“Then the promise she has read is a whole promise, and her copy of it is short of nine lines, and the nine lines are the half of it that says who has to come when it is used, and she does not have them, and she came here anyway, on her own money, on a cart, with a slate, to find out what the rest of it is for. And the finding of this afternoon is not about the slate. It is that a copy of a charter travels about this county without anybody stopping it, and that a copy with a name cut off it is still sworn, still true, and still binding on everybody in a district of about four hundred people, and that the part that was left off is the part with a consequence in it.”

“Every time,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Every time,” she said. “Every copy of every term in this case has stopped exactly where a person begins. The short copy stopped at the carrier. The other short copy, in a district at the other end of this county, stopped at the person whose name the bench did not know. A district that copied a settlement out of a book because a sealed letter told it to did not get the sealed letter. Eleven of them.”

---

The chamber asked for the person named in the second half of the fourth term, and he came, and this is the third time, and the first two are in a book in a room four miles down a fen road with a date on them.

Sel Harrow read the production out in the flat voice of a clerk producing a witness and it is eleven words and there is no order in it, because a charter's carrier's clause does not require anybody to produce anybody; it says the terms are carried to any district that asks, and a district asked, and a district eleven miles off asked through a woman with a folder and a slate, and the terms were carried, and the person who carried them is a man and is in the city.

> **THE CLAIMANT IS A PERSON OF THE DISTRICT OF NETHERGILL AND IS NOT A PARTY IN THIS MATTER AND CANNOT BE. THE TERMS OF THE CHARTER OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY HAVE BEEN CARRIED TO THE DISTRICT OF NETHERGILL TWICE, ONCE IN THE SECOND HALF OF THE FOURTH TERM BY THE PERSON NAMED IN IT AND ONCE AT THE REQUEST OF THE DISTRICT ITSELF, AND THE COPY THE DISTRICT HOLDS IS SHORT OF THE SECOND HALF OF THAT TERM.**
>
> **THE CLAIM IS ABOUT A WORK RECORD. A WORK RECORD EXISTS. IT IS ON A SLATE. THE PERSON WHO KEEPS IT IS A PERSON AND HAS BEEN PAID ON A PAY DAY EVERY WEEK FOR ELEVEN YEARS AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED TO WRITE A NAME ON ANYTHING AND IS NOT OBLIGED TO BY ANY INSTRUMENT IN THIS CITY.**
>
> **THE CLAIMANT HAS BEEN PRODUCED ONCE. THE CARRIER HAS BEEN PRODUCED TWICE, IN A DISTRICT HE DOES NOT LIVE IN, AND HAS ENTERED IN THE SECOND OF THEM THAT A WITNESS CAN BE PRODUCED TWICE AND THREE TIMES, AND HE HAS NOW BEEN PRODUCED THREE TIMES, AND THE COURT HAS NO ORDER TO COMPEL HIM AND IS NOT COMPELLING HIM AND IS ENTERING THAT HE CAME.**

He came on the seventeenth and the eighteenth of the sixth, and on the second of those days he was in the room over a pump with about nineteen people in it, and he did the only thing he is able to do in this city, which is to say out loud what a thing costs and who pays it, and he said it in about four minutes, and the last of it was a question, and the question was to Salla Kern, and it was: what is the man called who writes the figures on your slate.

“Ordnay Vesk,” she said. “He is sixty-one and he has paid me every pay day since I was thirty-three and he cannot read and he said, is that so,.”

“There is a form in this city,” said Ilyan Vester, “with a word at the top of it, and the word says a rule is temporary, and it has taken a coal yard at the bottom of a slipway off a woman of thirty-eight this month, and there is no power in this city that can take it off her, and I am not going to pretend to her that I have one. What I have got is this, and it is yours if you want it, and it is not a remedy and it is not a right and nobody may appeal it and you may not be named in a support of it.

“You have a sworn copy of a charter in a folder in a house. The sworn copy is short of the second half of the fourth term, and the second half of the fourth term is nine lines about a man in a city, and it is the only part of the nine sheets that is not about anybody in your district. You have read a promise that is nine-tenths of a promise, and you came down a lane to ask about the nine tenths, and the nine tenths are real, and they were written four months ago for a household at a bridge that was not in a book, and they are the only thing anybody in this city has made in four months that a woman with a barrow can read out to two hundred people at a pump and be right about.”

The room was quiet for a moment.

“The other half of that term is a man who turns up,” he said. “That is all. And I know what that is worth, because I have been the man who turns up twice and I have written down seven things into this city's record that I cannot un-enter and there is no line in this city on which a man is paid for an eighth, and the seventh of the seven cost me a counting I stopped on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month, and my leg has not cleared since.”

---

And then a woman of forty-four asked a man of thirty-one a question, and he refused it in one sentence, and nobody pressed him and nobody asked why, and the refusal is the only line about it in a clerk's own book in a city four miles from a bridge, on the eighteenth of the sixth month.

“Is your eleven years the same as the household at the bridge,” said Salla Kern. “The one the charter was made for. I have read the ninth sheet and it says a name and a gate and a child, and I have been in this room four hours and I have heard about that house in every one of them, and I would like to know if what happened to them is what is happening to me, because if it is then I know what to do and if it is not then I do not know what to do and I am forty-four and — ”

“No,” said Ilyan Vester.

“No?”

“I will not answer for a household that has not been asked,” he said, “and there is a household in this case with a standing right to stop everything it is part of, itself, without asking anybody, and I am not going to put it in a support of a question of yours, and you are not going to be able to make me, and there is no procedure in this city that would let you, and that is not a thing I have thought of this afternoon. It has been true since the second month and I have not said it out loud before and I am not going to say why today.”

The room waited. Nobody asked. The water came up the flat and about four people went out to look at the gate, which is a thing about two hundred feet of oak on a frame and is not a hearing and is not a procedure.

“All right,” said Salla Kern. “I did not think you would. I asked because I wanted to see whether you would stop.”

“I stopped,” said Ilyan Vester. “I have not been able to do anything else since the second of the second month.”

---

The finding of the nineteenth of the sixth month, entered in the bench's own book of the district of Halloway in the district's own hand and read back twice, and it is four lines long, and it is the only finding in four months that anybody in this case can act on with a piece of chalk.

> **A COPY OF A CHARTER IN THIS CASE HAS TRAVELLED SEVEN MILES WITHOUT A SEAL AND WITHOUT A CLERK AND IS SHORT OF THE SECOND HALF OF THE FOURTH TERM, AND IT IS SWORN, AND IT IS TRUE, AND IT BINDS EVERYBODY IN A DISTRICT OF ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED PEOPLE, AND THE PART THAT WAS LEFT OFF IS THE PART WITH A CONSEQUENCE IN IT. THIS IS NOT A DEFECT IN THE SHORT COPY. THE SHORT COPY IS FAITHFUL TO WHAT IT WAS GIVEN. THE GIVING IS THE DEFECT, AND THE GIVING WAS DONE BY A DROVER ON A CART ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THE MONTH BEFORE THE FIRST, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED, AND NOBODY WILL EVER BE ABLE TO SAY WHO GAVE IT.**
>
> **AND A PERSON WITH A FOLDER AND A SLATE AND ELEVEN YEARS CAME DOWN THAT LANE ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THIS MONTH ON HER OWN MONEY TO FIND OUT WHETHER SHE HAD A CLAIM, AND SHE HAS ONE, AND IT IS NOT ABOUT THE SLATE. IT IS ABOUT A NAME NOT BEING ON IT, AND THERE IS NO LINE IN THIS CITY, IN ANY DISTRICT, IN ANY CHAMBER, ON WHICH A PERSON IS PAID TO PUT A NAME ON A FIGURE.**

Nobody improved it, and the bench of four in that district did four things on the twenty-fourth of the sixth, and what is set out here is not those four things. It is a counsel's answer. Somebody in a city asked Maren Kett on the twenty-third what had actually changed in four months in a poor district, and he wrote it down in his own hand and read it out in a room, and he is a counsel of nineteen years and he is the advocate of the limitation, and he has been the advocate of the limitation since the seventeenth of the second month and has never once pretended it is the other side of the argument.

“One. A page a bench can come back with, kept by a person who cannot appeal from it, and read aloud twice a week by a boy of nineteen who is not paid and was not asked.

“Two. A man's name under a rate, twice on purpose, read out four times a year, which nobody can do anything about, which cost him his lane and which he did anyway.

“Three. A definition in a bench's own book of what its own rate is a rate for, and a rate for a load, and the two debts of two women put in one unit and neither of them in any coin.

“Four. A rule that a reading happens inside a quarter when a rate-setter withdraws, and a rule that any person of the district may ask for the book to be read aloud to them and is not to be asked why.

“Five. A bench of three eleven miles off a fen road with a room of its own and four entries in it and a boundary that is not going to be done this spring.

“Six. A public answer that cost four pounds fifteen for twenty-two names in four months, entered against a road fund four miles away, which is a tenth of what is left in that year.

“Seven. Ten words off a leaf four hundred years old with a crease in them, entered under a licence in a building with no name on the door, in a record a poor district cannot be sent for and cannot be sent anything.

“Eight. A form with a word at the top of it found in a box and in nine districts, and a pitch at the bottom of a slipway given back, and a clerk of twenty-six years who entered the whole of it against herself and a clerk of thirty-one who has asked five times to be relieved of a print authority and has been refused five times and has stopped asking.

“Nine. A woman of forty-four with a slate and eleven years, who came down a lane on her own money, and who was not refused and was not helped.

“Ten. A gate hand of forty-four who has three figures for thirty years and cannot pay any of them and was not thanked and did not ask, and a hauler of twenty-nine who has one hundred and fifty hauls with a number against them at last and no money, and both of them read out four times a year by a woman with a barrow who is not allowed to appeal from one word of it.

“Not one of the ten is a ruling. Not one of the ten would survive a district being squeezed, and four of the ten cost somebody an hour a day or a day a week or four days of a man's sweeping, and one of the ten cost a woman a pitch at the bottom of a slipway and one of them cost a recordwright a shop, two rooms and a business, and one of them cost a man of thirty-one the only thing his body ever gave back, and the counsel of nineteen years read all ten out in a room on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and did not put a figure on any of them and did not say that they were good.”
