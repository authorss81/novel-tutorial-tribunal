# Chapter 77: What It Costs to Have Said No in Public

The thing that made Dessin Nahl go and ask was not a decision anybody took. It was that in the second week of the month she had become a person who was stopped talking to in a corridor.

Not anything anybody said. She is a clerk of the Orison chamber and she has written nine of the twenty-two names on the docket board and taken two of them off it, and she has stood in a lane in Coldwater reading a cart's docket aloud to a woman with a coal yard, and she is competent at two things and has never in her life needed anybody to be glad of her.

What she had begun to notice is that when a person in a corridor said *where can I swear this*, four people in nine had begun to say *not here*, faster than the question, and with a particular expression, which is the expression a person makes when they have read something of their own in a public record.

So she went and found out whether that was a thing about four people or about a city, and it took her eleven miles on foot and two days out of her own pocket and it is entered in the chamber's book without being asked, in the flat hand of a woman who has never once entered a false reason.

> **THE CHAMBER HAS PAID FOR FOUR DAYS. I HAVE PAID FOR TWO, OUT OF MY OWN, AND I WOULD LIKE IT ENTERED THAT NOBODY ASKED ME TO GO, AND THAT THE TWO DAYS ARE NOT THE CHAMBER'S MONEY AND ARE NOT THE CLAIMANT'S EITHER, BUT A CLERK'S, AND THAT I AM ENTERING IT BECAUSE IN FOUR YEARS SOMEBODY WILL READ THE ANSWERS IN A BOOK AND WILL NOT KNOW WHAT THEY COST, AND A THING WITH NO COST IN IT LOOKS LIKE EVIDENCE.**

---

Fallowditch is a district of a bench of three on the low side, eleven miles from the fen road, and its clerk is Perditta Nye and has been three years in the post and is twenty-nine.

What Nahl asked her was not whether the bench would hold a page. She was careful about that, afterwards, and it was the one piece of craft in six days that she was proud of. What she asked was one question, on one matter, with a date on it, and it was this: *will this bench hear this one thing, in this bench's own room, on a day this bench appoints, and answer yes or no in writing, and if the answer is no, say why in the bench's own hand.*

Perditta Nye had the answer in under an hour. It is four lines long and it is the answer that made the walk worth eleven miles.

> **THIS BENCH ANSWERED THE ELEVENTH-DAY QUESTION ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF LAST MONTH IN WRITING AND IN PUBLIC AND SAID THAT IT HOLDS NO PAGE, BOOK, COLUMN, REGISTER, SCHEDULE, OR SWORN INSTRUMENT WHICH A PERSON AFFECTED MAY SWEAR TO IN THIS BENCH'S OWN ROOM.**
>
> **IF THIS BENCH NOW HEARD THAT THING, THAT ANSWER WOULD BECOME FALSE IN THIS BENCH'S OWN HAND, IN THIS BENCH'S OWN BOOK, ON A DAY THAT CAN BE FOUND.**
>
> **WE HAVE ASKED OURSELVES WHETHER A POOR PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WOULD RATHER HAVE A HEARING, OR RATHER HAVE A BENCH THAT TOLD HER THE TRUTH TWICE. WE HAVE DECIDED SHE WOULD RATHER HAVE THE HEARING.**
>
> **THE REASON IS A MATTER OF FACT AND NOT OF PRINCIPLE, AND THE FACT IS A NAME: MRS ADELA CRAY OF THIS DISTRICT ASKED THIS BENCH FOR A PLACE TO PUT AN AGREEMENT SHE HOLDS, AND THIS BENCH HAD NOWHERE, AND SHE WAS TOLD SO IN THE ROOM, AND SHE WENT HOME.**

She read that last line four times and then said the thing Nahl wrote down in the third of a column on the back of the same sheet.

“You have put her in it,” she said. “You have put a woman in a four-line answer because she is the reason, and you did not have to, and about four benches in a hundred and forty years have ever put a name in an answer, and every one of them did it to be thanked and one of them did it to be frightened.”

“I put her in it because a bench that will not say who it did that for is a bench that has decided the answer was free.”

“It was not free,” said Perditta Nye. “It cost that woman a fortnight’s notice and it will cost her the room unless somebody moves, and the bench knows that and the bench is three people and two of them are out on a boundary this week. Write it down. Then go to Orison and tell the magistrate that the emptiest answer this city has ever given is now the first one anybody has paid for, and that the price was not five pounds and was somebody's address.”

---

The other six of the seven are reported and not staged, because they were answered in writing and Nahl had them all on a sheet by the sixth, and what they said, in Nahl's summary, was: five of them said no for the reason Fallowditch had given, one of them said no for the reason that it is three people and no money and one of them is out on a boundary, and one of them did not answer the question Nahl asked at all and answered a different one, which was that it holds nothing and would rather be a bench that can be honest than a bench that can be relieved.

That last one is the sentence of the third month of this case and it is in a letter and it is in no record but the file, and it was read out once at the First Verdict on the seventh of the month by a clerk in a flat voice and was not improved on by anybody, including the magistrate, who said it was the most useful thing a bench had said to her in eleven years and that it was not useful to anybody at all.

And then, in the two weeks after the answers came in, three of the seven were asked by a person in their own district to produce a page, and had to say, from their own letter, that there was nothing in their room to produce it from.

That is the cost of having said it in public, and it is not a rhetorical cost. It is this: a bench that has told the truth about itself in its own hand has priced a room in its own district at one answer, in eleven days, and the price is being paid by whoever needs the room, and the bench did not know it was setting a price when it set it.

---

And in Fallowditch, on the ninth of the month, a landlord put a letter on a table.

He was not a bad man and he had a bad week, and the letter was Fallowditch's own answer of the eighteenth, in the bench's own hand, and he put it face up in front of a woman of about forty who had a fortnight's notice and an agreement she had held four years and could not produce, because her district held nothing she could produce, and the bench had said so in writing, and the bench was right, and the letter said so in the clearest language anybody in this case has produced.

“I am not doing anything wrong,” he said. “I am using what the bench said about itself. If you had taken it to the bench on the twentieth it would have been the same, because there is nowhere in this district for a piece of paper about you to be. And the bench has told you so in its own hand and I have not had to say a word about it.”

She sat with it for a while.

“You have read it out to me,” she said, “which you did not have to do.”

“No.”

“Nobody else is ever going to know that you read it out.”

“No,” he said, and then, “I have a room with three in it coming free and I have four to put in it and I am not going to pretend to you that this is about the law.”

And then she asked him a question that the bench of three could not have asked her, and that is how the bench of three found out it had a hearing, and it is in the file in a clerk's hand with a date on it: *will you hear this one thing, in this bench's own room, on a day this bench appoints.*

The bench appointed the eleventh, and it was held in the bench's own room over a shop that had been a chandler's, and two of the three turned up and the third was out on a boundary, and the landlord was not there and was not a party and had not been asked, and there was no other room in that district that could have heard it, which by then was not a fact anybody needed to say out loud.

The thing about the agreement was that the agreement was real. Everybody in the room agreed that it was real. It was four years old, it was in her name, it had been in her name since the day she signed it, and it had never once been produced anywhere, because the only room it could have been produced in was a room that had publicly said it held nothing.

Perditta Nye read it out in the bench's own room, in the flat voice of a clerk reading a thing out, and then put it in a folder, and then entered in the bench's book that the bench held it, and the date, and the name of the person it was about, and that the bench could do nothing whatever with it.

“Is that all this is,” said the woman.

“It is a page,” said Perditta Nye. “You can put it in front of anybody. You can put it in front of a man who has taken four rooms and you can say *a bench of three holds this and will not have you*, and there is a date on it and a name on it and two of us signed it, and it is worth more this morning than it was on the ninth. And a fortnight's notice is between you and a man, and no bench in this city hears that, and not this one, and if it did it would be hearing it in a room that is not a bench and would be a man in a wig. So no. That is not all this is. That is what this is, and it is a thing, and you may shake it, and the man you want to shake is not standing here.”

Nobody shook anything. The landlord was not there and had not been asked and did not know, and the woman took the folder away with her because the bench could not keep it and did not have anywhere, which is the whole of what changed for her in a fortnight, and it was not nothing, and it was not a room.

---

And on the fourth day, instead of a bench, Nahl went up a stair four miles from the First Verdict and put a paper on a desk with a stamp on it, and it is the first time in four years that anybody has asked that office for anything at all.

She did not go up the stair to make a point. She went up it because a question she had been asked twice had no answer on this side of the city, and the answer was a form of words that somebody had already written.

The question was not *may I read something*. A person asked that on the eighteenth of the month before, in writing, with a seal on it, and got back a *yes* in nine words, and it is the only *yes* in eleven answers and it has been on a public record ever since.

The question was: **will this authority receive and keep a page sworn by a person in a district, for that person, and produce it on request.**

The answer came back on the sixth, in the same hand as the other one, which is a hand that belongs to an office and not to a person, and it is eleven lines, and the first line is the answer and the other ten are the reason.

> **NO. AND THE REASON IS NOT A REFUSAL OF THE PERSON, AND IT IS NOT A DEFICIENCY.**
>
> **THIS AUTHORITY HOLDS WHAT IS PRINTED. A SWORN PAGE IS NOT PRINTED. A SWORN PAGE IS A RECORD OF A PERSON, AND AN AUTHORITY THAT KEEPS RECORDS OF PERSONS IS A BENCH, AND THIS IS NOT A BENCH, AND IT DECIDES NOTHING.**
>
> **THIS AUTHORITY CAN SHOW THAT A RULE WAS PRINTED. IT CANNOT SHOW THAT ANY PERSON MAY SWEAR TO IT, AND IT CANNOT HOLD A PERSON'S CLAIM, AND IF IT HELD ONE IT WOULD BE A DOOR, AND AN OFFICE WITH NO PERSON IN IT IS NOT A DOOR, IT IS A SHELF.**
>
> **THE AUTHORITY'S CONDITION IS THAT WHAT IS PRINTED IN THIS CITY IS NOT LOST. THE AUTHORITY HAS NO CONDITION ABOUT PEOPLE, HAS NEVER HAD ONE, AND CANNOT BE GIVEN ONE BY POST.**
>
> **THE ONLY THING THIS AUTHORITY CAN DO WITH A RULE IS PROVE THAT IT WAS PRINTED. THERE IS NOWHERE IN THIS CITY THAT A PERSON CAN BE KEPT AGAINST A PRINTED RULE, AND THIS AUTHORITY IS NOT IT, AND HAS NOW SAID SO TWICE IN FIFTEEN DAYS, IN THE SAME HAND, IN ANSWERS TO TWO QUESTIONS ASKED BY TWO DIFFERENT PEOPLE.**

Nahl read that last line four times in the stair and then went down it and sat on the step outside and did not go and tell anybody for about ten minutes.

---

And that is the discovery of the third month, and it is a worse answer than anybody in this case wanted, and it took a clerk on a road and a stamp on a desk and nothing else at all.

Seven districts have said in public, in their own hands, on their own letter paper, that they hold nothing a poor person can swear to. One body in this city has said yes, and it holds every instrument printed here for four years, and it has now said twice, in the same hand, that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person.

A city that has established it is empty has established, in the same fortnight, that the one thing it is not empty of is a shelf.

And the leverage is real, and Nahl worked it out on the seventh day, at a lamp, in a room she was paying for, and wrote it in her own book and not in the chamber's, because it was her finding and it was not good.

“Anybody in this city who needs a page sworn can now name the one desk that said yes,” she wrote. “Not to read from it. To point at it. They can go into a bench in a district that holds nothing and say *ask the authority that answered yes*, and a bench cannot refuse that without being asked why it differs from the one body in this city that has on the record that it holds everything printed here for four years.

“And it holds paper. It does not hold people. So the leverage is real, it is on a desk with a stamp, and it cannot be used, and in about a year somebody is going to try to use it anyway, and there is going to be a form about it with a word at the top of it.”

And underneath that, because she was a clerk and she copied things that were going to be asked for again, she copied the eleven lines out in her own hand and dated them, and she could not tell you then why she had done it and can now.

---

The docket board had twenty-two names on it and eleven places on it, and had had twenty-two names and eleven places on it for nineteen years, and the finding at the fourth bell on the seventh was not a remedy and was not a procedure. It was four sentences from a magistrate in the well of a court in the open air.

“This is a yes with a reason in it from a body that holds paper, and seven nos with reasons in them from bodies that hold nothing, and one bench has changed its answer in writing because of a woman called Adela Cray, and I entered the duty to answer and I have entered nothing since and I am not going to.

“The duty to answer has cost a woman a room and got a bench a hearing, and I did not see that coming, and that is what a duty to answer is.

“Twenty-two names, eleven places. That is this court and it is every court in this city and it has been for nineteen years, and I have been eleven years in this office and I have never once been able to make a place, and I have now been shown in writing in nine hands what the scarcity has made: it has made a public answer into a price, and it has made one desk into a monopoly, and it has not done either of those things on purpose.

“A city that has said out loud that it is empty is a city in which a person with a document and nowhere to put it is a person with a problem that no bench in this city can hear. That is not a remedy. I have wanted a remedy since the eleventh day of the month before and I have got nine letters, a change of mind in one of them, and a hearing in a district of three that could do nothing and said so, and I am going to carry all of it and I am not going to pretend that carrying it is a court.”

Three names went back on that board for the second time in three weeks, which has happened to two names in this case so far and has now happened to three, and one of the three is a man of nineteen who has been on and off this board three times and has asked for nothing. And the eleventh place that morning went to a water and a boundary, and a name matter went back on it for the fourth time, and the third bell found a bench of four four miles down a fen road with a rate to set, a page to swear, and a figure of a thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings in its book that nobody had been able to check and nobody had been able to refuse.
