# Chapter 89: What It Costs To Make a Document

The board at the First Verdict had twenty-one names on it at the fourth morning bell on the twentieth of the fourth month, and it had had twenty-one since the fourteenth of last month, and it had had twenty-two for every day of the nineteen years before that, and a name had come off it in the middle of March, in the open air, by the person it belonged to, and the matter had stayed on the list without it, and the man had said he would wait for a mechanism and gone home to a wall.

It was back to twenty-two at about the fifth bell, and the clerk who wrote the second twenty-second name in was Dessin Nahl, and she wrote it in her own hand and it is on the board, and the number is twenty-two, and it has been twenty-two for nineteen years, and the one thing that has happened to it in nineteen years is that it went to twenty-one for forty-two days and came back.

Maren Kett is of the Orison chamber and has been nineteen years in it. He was the named advocate of the first appeal in this case, because a claimant with limited standing may only be heard with one, and his first nine words were that he is not the man's friend, and he argued against his own client for three hours and said on the record that he knew the outcome on the day he took the brief and took it anyway, and then he went away and was not in this case again for two months, and he has been the advocate of the limitation ever since, and both men he has spent that time being right about are right.

He came up to the block at about the sixth bell with a paper in his hand and about sixty people standing in the open air in front of the basalt, and the wind was off the water at the angle it comes at, and the whole of the court is the thing this city has that no room in it has.

“I am going to put a matter on that board,” he said, “and I am going to ask a question that four of you have thought of asking and forty of you have not, and I am going to enter what it costs to ask it, and I am going to read the cost out before I ask, because a question with a price on it is a different animal from a question without one and I have spent nineteen years watching this city give answers to the cheap ones and go quiet on the expensive ones.”

“Mr Kett,” said Ansa Rell. “The court will take a matter from a counsel of this court. State it.”

“I am asking what it costs, in money, to make a matter ready,” said Maren Kett.

There was a moment of noise in the open air, and then it stopped, and it stopped because he had said *in money* and every person standing there understood that he had said it on purpose.

“For nineteen years,” he said, “this city has had eleven places in a morning. That is the whole of the scarcity. It is not the block and it is not the weather and it is not a judge's mood. It is eleven, and it has been eleven every month since before I came, and nobody has ever costed it, and every person in this city has spent four months this year learning that a place is not made by a name being on a board and is made by a matter being ready, and that a matter is ready when a document can be sworn, and that a document can be sworn in a room that holds it or in a room that will hold it.

“So: a place is a document. A document is a room. A room is a bench. And this morning I am asking what the document costs, because everybody in this case has been looking at the board and nobody has looked at the paper.”

---

Rell did not refuse it and did not improve it, which is the thing she has done in every other month of this case and has never once done well.

“The court will answer what it can,” she said. “Not the limit — the limit is that this court has no power over any bench in this city, has no power over any rate, and has no power over any page anybody keeps in a room over a pump, and takes no view on all three, and has said so on the face of a field about a gate. What I will do is put the chamber's own figure in the open air, and I have had to go and get it, and getting it took nine minutes, and nine minutes is the first time in eleven years that anybody has asked this court what a sitting costs.

“Nahl. What does a sitting cost.”

“Two days of a clerk's time and about four pounds of cart hire, in this case, across four months, for the serving, and it is more in a matter with more parties, and it is the same shape in every matter and it has been the same shape in every matter since before I came into the room.” Nahl read it off a slip and did not pretend she had it in her head, because she did not have it in her head and there was no reason for her to pretend. “And two of those days were mine, out of my own, in the third week of March, and I entered that in the chamber's book in the same place as the answer, because a thing with no cost in it looks like evidence.”

“All of it,” said Kett. “For twenty-two names in four months.”

“The whole of it, my two days included, is four pounds fifteen,” said Dessin Nahl, “and the twenty-two of them have cost this city less than the price of the four new timbers that were bought for a bridge four miles away on the twenty-seventh of March, which is ninety-six pounds.”

---

That got the noise back, and it got it back in the middle, and it was the only noise in this case that has ever been about a good thing.

Ninety-six pounds of timber bought a bridge four miles away a deck instead of four square holes in it, and four pounds and fifteen of clerks and cart hire bought a woman a fortnight's notice, a bench of three a room, a district of sixty-one people a name for what was done to them, and a carrier's clause into a district eleven miles off that nobody had asked for it.

“Ninety-six against four fifteen,” said Maren Kett, when it went down. “I have been nineteen years in a chamber that has told this city for nineteen years that it cannot make a place, and it has been right, and the reason it cannot make a place is that there are eleven, and the reason there are eleven is not money. Not one pound of the ninety-six is in this. So I am going to ask the other half of my question, and the half that nobody has asked, and I would like the room to notice that I am the one asking it and not the court and not the man's book.”

“Ask it,” said Rell.

“What does it cost to make the document.”

---

There is a great deal of silence available in the open air at the First Verdict and Rell let about two minutes of it go by, and then she said it, and it is the true sentence of the fourth month of this case.

“Eleven benches were asked and nine answered, in their own hands, on their own letter paper, on dates that can be found, and seven of them said they hold no page, book, column, register, schedule or sworn instrument which a person affected may swear to in their own room. One of the seven has changed its answer in writing since, on the third of March, because a woman called Adela Cray asked it for a place to put an agreement she held and went home when it had none, and it can hold a page now, eleven miles from the fen road, and it did it for one agreement and cannot do one thing with it. One body in this city answered yes, and it holds every instrument printed here for four years, and it has now said twice in the same hand that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person, because an office with no person in it is a shelf.

“None of that is new and this court is not going to read it out again, because it has been read out.

“Here is the part nobody has asked for. Every one of those answers was about a room. Not one of them was about the making of the paper, and the making of the paper is where the cost is, and it has been there the whole four months, in front of all of us, and not one person in this city has ever costed it, because it is not done in a room and it is not done by a body and it is not entered in a book with a name on the top of it.”

Nahl had it written out while the magistrate was speaking and Roke read it aloud from the step, and it is in the record, and it is a sheet of paper and not a judgment.

> **AT THE FIRST VERDICT, THE TWENTIETH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, AT THE FOURTH MORNING BELL, IN THE OPEN AIR.**
> **A MATTER HAS BEEN ENTERED ON THE DOCKET BOARD BY A COUNSEL OF THIS COURT. THE BOARD NOW HAS TWENTY-TWO NAMES AGAIN, AFTER HAVING HAD TWENTY-ONE FOR FORTY-TWO DAYS, AND IT HAS HAD TWENTY-TWO FOR NINETEEN YEARS.**
> **THE QUESTION ENTERED: WHAT DOES IT COST, IN MONEY, TO MAKE A MATTER READY, AND WHO PAYS IT.**
> **A PLACE ON THIS BOARD COSTS ABOUT TWO DAYS OF A CLERK'S TIME AND ABOUT FOUR POUNDS OF CART HIRE, AND FOUR POUNDS FIFTEEN FOR ALL TWENTY-TWO OF THEM IN FOUR MONTHS. THE WHOLE OF THE TWENTY-TWO NAMES ON THIS BOARD, IN FOUR MONTHS, HAS COST THIS CITY LESS THAN THE PRICE OF FOUR NEW TIMBERS BOUGHT FOR A BRIDGE FOUR MILES AWAY, WHICH IS NINETY-SIX POUNDS.**
> **WHAT NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR UNTIL THIS MORNING: EVERY ONE OF THOSE NAMES IS A NAME A PERSON MAY BE CALLED OUT BY. A MATTER IS READY WHEN A DOCUMENT CAN BE SWORN. A DOCUMENT IS MADE BY A PERSON WHO CAN READ. THIS CITY HAS NO LINE ON WHICH A PERSON IS PAID FOR READING.**
> **THE COST OF MAKING THE MATTER READY IS PAID BY THE PERSON WHO READS, OUT OF HER OWN TIME, AND IN THIS CASE IT HAS BEEN PAID BY A REFUSE HAULER, A GATE HAND, A COWMAN'S WIFE, A LANDLORD'S MOUTH, A CLERK, A BOY OF NINETEEN WHO IS NOT PAID AND WAS NOT ASKED, AND A WASHER OF SIXTY-ONE WHO HAS STOPPED USING A STANDPIPE.**
> **THIS COURT HAS NO POWER OVER ANY RATE IN THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY AND TAKES NO VIEW. THE COST OF THIS SITTING IS ENTERED AGAINST THE ROAD FUND OF THE DISTRICT OF THE NINTH BRIDGE, WHICH IS NOT THE DISTRICT ASKED, AND NO REPLY IS ASKED FOR.**

---

Then Rell said the thing that made her difficult, and it is the reason she is out-voted and undefeated and has not been redeemed and is not going to be.

“I am going to say the other half and nobody is going to like it and I would like it in the record that I said it first and that I have been against it for two months.

“If this chamber begins paying people for reading, it will not be able to stop. Not because the money is short — four pounds fifteen against ninety-six is not a shortage and I have done the sum twice since you started. It is because a body that pays a person to prepare the paper in a matter has bought the paper, and the paper is the thing that gets produced in front of a bench, and in about two years there will be a bench of four in a district somewhere that is being very careful about what its holder puts in her page, for reasons that are entirely proper and entirely about her time and her back and nothing whatever to do with anybody's freedom.

“I have been the magistrate in this city for eleven years and I have watched a district pay a favour and learn to rule by habit, and I have watched a district that pays a wage learn to rule by something worse, which is arithmetic, because a wage has to come out of somewhere and a rate that has to come out of a bucket gets read aloud at a pump four times a year by a woman with a barrow who is not allowed to appeal from one word of it. That is not a future. That is the sixteenth of this month. That happened three days ago and there is a book about it and a man’s name in the book twice.

“So the honest answer to a question nobody has thought of asking is this. This city can afford eleven places and has always been able to afford eleven places. What it cannot afford is the work that makes a matter ready, and it has not afforded it for nineteen years, and it is not going to afford it now, and the reason it is not going to is that the work is done by people who are not paid, who are not named, who cannot be produced, and whose names are not in a column anywhere, and there is a bench of four in this city that has publicly said in its own hand that it would rather be a bench that can be honest than a bench that can be relieved.

“Enter all of that. I voted for the clean instrument in March and I was out-voted four to one and I said the whole of the case out loud and I was not defeated and I am not redeemed, and this is the same speech in a different month and I am not going to pretend it is smaller.”

---

Maren Kett did not answer her, which is the second time in this case that the right thing to do was not to answer Ansa Rell and he has done it before.

He turned round to the open air, where about sixty people were standing, and he read the last two lines of his own question out again, and then he said the thing that made it irreversible.

“I am a counsel of this court and I am not a party to anything and I am not the man's advocate and I have not been in his case since the seventeenth of the month before last. A counsel who enters a question in this yard is not a witness to it and cannot be examined on it and cannot be served with it. That is a privilege and I have had it for nineteen years and this morning it ran out, because a question about what a document costs is a question about what that document says, and the only people who can answer it are the people who wrote the words, and one of them is a bench of four four miles down a fen road and one of them is a woman with a barrow, and a question of mine does not travel to either of them without a party, and there is no party in this.

“Which means that in about four years, when somebody in a bench in a city wants to know what a rate is for, the way there to the answer is a name on a board, and a name on a board is a name in this city’s record, and a record is a thing a clerk can come back with, and the clerk who comes back with it is a man of thirty-one with no standing in that district at all.

“I have put my name to a question about other people’s money in front of sixty people and a block that has been standing here four hundred years so that anybody may stand in front of it and be answered, and a question asked out here cannot be called back, and I would like it entered that I did it with my eyes open and that nobody asked me to. I have been nineteen years in a chamber that is very good at questions and has never once costed one. I have found out what it costs and it is not money.”

“Entered,” said Roke, from the tray.

And a counsel of nineteen years walked up the steps of a basalt block that has been standing in the open air for four hundred years, and wrote his own name on a board in chalk, in a hand that half the room could tell from anybody else’s, and went down them again, and the clerk of the chamber read the list out afterwards because a clerk does that, and the number at the end of it was twenty-two, as it has been for nineteen years.

---

Nobody thanked him either. He had not asked for that and it was not in the record and Roke said so when somebody in the crowd tried.

At the seventh bell a clerk of the district of the ninth bridge arrived with a letter, which was a piece of bad news that Vale had sent down by a boy on a cart and had entered before he sent it, and which was the price of the morning.

> **TO THE BENCH OF FOUR OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY, AND TO THE HOLDER OF THE FIFTH TERM, AND TO WHOM IT CONCERNS, WHICH IN THIS DISTRICT IS NOBODY. THE SECOND OF THE FOURTH MONTH, AND THE TWENTIETH.**
>
> **A SITTING WAS HELD THIS MORNING IN THE OPEN AIR AT THE FIRST VERDICT, AT A COUNSEL OF THIS COURT'S EXPENSE AND NOT AT ANYBODY ELSE'S, AND THE WHOLE OF IT, FOUR MONTHS AND TWENTY-TWO NAMES, COST FOUR POUNDS FIFTEEN, OF WHICH TWO DAYS WERE A CLERK'S OWN AND OUT OF HER OWN PURSE, AND THE CHAMBER HAS ENTERED IT AGAINST THE ROAD FUND OF THIS DISTRICT. I AM THE CLERK OF THAT FUND AND I HAVE ENTERED IT BECAUSE THE FUND IS MINE AND THE MONEY IS MINE AND NOBODY ELSE WILL ENTER IT, AND BECAUSE THE QUESTION ASKED THIS MORNING WAS WHETHER THE PRICE OF A DOCUMENT FALLS ON THE PERSON WHO MAKES IT, AND THE PRICE OF THAT DOCUMENT FALLS ON YOUR PUMP, AND YOUR PUMP IS FOUR MILES FROM THAT BLOCK AND FOUR MILES FROM THIS BRIDGE.**
>
> **FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND FOUR SHILLINGS IS WHAT IS LEFT OF OUR YEAR AND FOUR POUNDS FIFTEEN IS ABOUT A TENTH OF IT. I AM NOT COMPLAINING. I AM TELLING YOU THAT A BENCH FOUR MILES AWAY IS BEING ASKED, ON OUR MONEY, WHETHER A RATE IN YOUR DISTRICT IS FAIR, AND YOU CANNOT APPEAL THAT EITHER, AND I HAVE LOOKED FOR A WAY TO CHARGE IT TO YOU AND THERE IS NOT ONE, BECAUSE A HOLDER MAY NOT APPEAL AND A BENCH OF FOUR MAY BE ASKED AND HAS ANSWERED, AND THAT IS THE END OF IT.**
>
> **NO REPLY IS ASKED FOR. I AM A CLERK AND THIS IS MY FIGURE AND IT IS NOT THE COURT'S.**

Oakes read it out in a room over a pump at about the eighth bell on the twentieth of the fourth month and did not read it twice, and then he said the only thing he had to say about it, and it was the thing he had already said in this case about a different sum on the twelfth.

“Four pounds fifteen of our district's water has paid for four months in a city in which a man asked whether our water is fair,” he said, “and there is no rate that can go down for it, because the rate is what we have got, and the rate is the right rate, and a ratepayer of this lane said so before anybody spoke and has said so four times and has been right four times.”

Nobody read the last thing of that morning down to him, because it was on the flat and the water was coming and about two hundred people were going up a mud bank with buckets, and it is the same bank and the same pump and the same woman with the barrow, and the rate is an eighth of a penny, and it is not going up.
