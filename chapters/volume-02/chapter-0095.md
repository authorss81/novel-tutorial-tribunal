# Chapter 95: A Claim That Is Not Filed

The collision happened on the thirteenth of the fifth month at about the fourth bell, in the cold room, and it happened because a man swept a building for nine years and had been given a question and had spent it.

She had not told him anything. That is the part worth having on the record first, and there is no record, so it goes here: on the fifth, on the sixth, on the ninth and on the twelfth, Corrin Vask had been in a room with a recordwright of a public record while she spent four days and four shillings and a man's hand on a leaf four hundred years old, and he had been told that the leaf is the shadow of a removed page, and that the removed page is not on the shelf and has not been since before she was born, and that the mark beside the empty slot is in a newer hand than the book.

He had not asked whose page. He had asked about the fourth shelf and about a word at the top of nine hundred forms and about the price of standing about, and those were his questions, and they were the ones he had been waiting nine years for, and he spent them.

And on the thirteenth she was in the room alone, writing the fourth-day entry out again in fair, because a thing copied by hand from a thing copied by hand loses whatever was not in the pen, and she had learned that from a print shop five days earlier, and she was saying it out loud to an empty room, and Vask came in with the day book under his arm to write the price of a day that he was going to have to be paid in a day he was not going to get, and he stopped in the doorway.

“You have been in here four days,” he said, “about a page in a cold room.”

“Yes.”

“And you have not said whose page it is.”

She put the pen down.

“Mine,” she said.

---

And then he said the thing that cost him, and he said it in one sentence, standing in a doorway with a day book under his arm, and he had thought about it for four days and he had not been asked anything at all.

“Then why have you not got it out.”

---

It is six years. The claim is her family's and not hers, and it is a withdrawal, and it is unfiled, and it has never been in a case and is not in this case, and it needs a page that may not exist.

It has a page in it and the page is gone. Not the page in the cold room four miles from where she is standing. The other one. Her own copy, in her own hand, in a case, in her own rooms, taken out of her own house — a duplicate of a page of terms, made by her, for herself, six years ago, and it was in the case on a night in February, and it was not in the case on a morning, and nobody cut a thread, and nobody wrote a mark, and nobody has ever been charged with anything.

And the thing she has wanted for six years, and has never said out loud to anybody, and said out loud in her own book on the sixth of the second month and once on a step in this case, is that it is the same thing. That somebody sat in a room and took a page out of a book and that is what happened to her too, and that if it is the same thing then it is a removal, and a removal has a reason and a reason has a person, and the person can be found.

It is not the same thing. She has known it for six years. It was in a book in a building with nineteen shelves in it and the thread was cut and the mark beside the empty slot was in a newer hand. Hers was in a case in her own rooms and there is no thread and there is no slot and there is no mark, and the only thing the two of them have in common is the subject, and she wants them to be the same event every single day of her life, and they are not, and there is not one thing in this city that will make them the same event, and she wrote that down on the second of the second month and she has written it down again on the fourteenth of this month, in her own hand, in her own book, with both dates on it.

> **THE THING I WANT. I WANT THE TWO OF THEM TO BE ONE THING. I HAVE WANTED IT FOR SIX YEARS AND IT IS THE ONLY THING I HAVE WANTED IN SIX YEARS THAT COSTS NOTHING TO HOLD.**
>
> **AND IT IS TWO THINGS, AND THEY ARE TWO DATES, AND ONE OF THEM IS FOUR HUNDRED YEARS OLD AND THE OTHER ONE IS SIX, AND THE FIRST WAS CUT OUT OF A BOOK BY SOMEBODY WHO LEFT A MARK IN A NEWER HAND, AND THE SECOND WAS CARRIED OUT OF A HOUSE BY SOMEBODY WHO LEFT NOTHING AT ALL, AND NOBODY HAS EVER PROVED THAT IT WAS A PERSON AND I HAVE STOPPED HOPING THAT IT WAS A PERSON AND I HAVE ALSO STOPPED HOPING THAT IT WAS NOT, WHICH IS NOT PROGRESS, IT IS ONLY A THING I CAN CARRY.**

---

And the fourth term is what is between her and the only thing she could do about it, and it is not a technicality, and by the fourteenth of the fifth month she had stopped being able to pretend that it was.

A matter is a claim, a subject, a burden, a witness, a remedy and an exit. She wrote the six on a sheet of cheap paper in a stone room off the second gallery on the second of the month and five of the six were blank and one was filled, and the one that was filled was a different matter, and that was correct.

A family claim of six years, unfiled, needs a page. A page is the thing a matter is sworn in. Therefore the family claim, if it were filed, would be a matter. And the fourth term of her licence says she takes no matter of her own while she holds this one, and the term is correct, and she agreed to it in a room in front of three hundred people, and she told them what it cost out loud, and nobody has asked her since.

And it was not a technicality on the second of the fifth month, when it was an abstract question about a hauler's question. It became a technicality — it became a wall with a door in it, the phrase she used in front of a trialwright — the moment she put a fragment of a page in a record on the twelfth, because a record is not a matter, and she entered it as a record, and she was right, and the record is hers and hers alone and it is in a book in a cold room four miles from a city, and the claim is not a record.

She can hand a district ten words with a crease in them. She cannot hand her own family a page. And the difference between those two things is not a loophole and it is not a technicality and it is not a thing anybody in this city finds flattering: a record is what a person knows and a claim is what a person wants. And a recordwright of a public record may enter the first and may not have the second, and she has spent six years being proud of the sentence that says so.

Which means that the fragment came, and the claim did not, and they are the same two halves of a woman, and she is the woman, and there is nobody else.

---

She went and told Sel Harrow on the fourteenth of the fifth month, at the third bell, in the stone room off the second gallery with the stove that does not draw, and she told him in about nine sentences and she did not ask him for anything, which was the first thing about it that she had thought about for two days.

Harrow heard it out. He did not interrupt. He put his tray on the table with the sanded edge up, which is a thing he does when he is going to be careful.

“You have told me that you cannot file a claim of your own,” he said, “and you have told me that the fourth term is the reason, and you have not asked me for anything, and I am going to answer all three, and the third one is the one that will cost me.”

“I did not ask.”

“You did not ask, and you came, and coming is the asking, and if I do not say the rest of it now then in about four years there will be a book in this city with a line in it that says a trialwright was told, and the line will not say what he said, and that is how a record becomes a thing a clerk can be frightened of.” He turned the tray over. “I cannot help you. There is no procedure in this court for a licence-holder's matter and there is no procedure in this city for a person's own claim, and I have looked, in about nine minutes, which is longer than it usually takes me, and there is nothing.”

“I know there is nothing.”

“Then you came for the other thing.”

“I came because you put it there,” said Sera Quill.

“Yes,” said Harrow. “I did. I drafted it and you agreed to it and I have said in three rooms since the second of the second month that I used it against you four times, and there is a fifth use and I have been saving it, and this is it, and I have not been sleeping particularly well about it and I am not going to pretend otherwise in front of you.

“The fourth term says a recordwright takes no matter of her own. Your family claim is a matter of your own. Therefore you cannot file it. That is what I wrote, and it is exactly what I meant, and it is correct, and there is no reading of it that gets you what you want, and I have looked for one for two days, which I have not done for anything in eleven years except a line somebody else entered.”

“Then why did you look for two days.”

“Because I did not want the sentence to be that short,” said Harrow, “and because I am a man who drafts things, and because a term I have drafted that has cost a person the one thing they have carried for six years is a term I will look at for two days. And I have looked, and there is nothing, and here is what is left, and it is not a procedure, and I am naming it as my own so that no clerk can be blamed for it in about four years.”

He turned the tray back over.

> **I HAVE BEEN TOLD, BY THE PERSON THE FOURTH TERM BINDS, THAT IT STOPS HER FROM FILING A CLAIM OF HER OWN FAMILY'S WHICH IS SIX YEARS OLD AND UNFILED AND WHICH NEEDS A PAGE THAT MAY NOT EXIST. I CANNOT RELIEVE HER. I HAVE NO POWER OVER THE TERMS OF A LICENCE AND I HAVE SAID SO TWICE THIS MONTH ON THE FACE OF MY OWN HAND. THERE IS NO PROCEDURE IN THIS COURT AND NO PROCEDURE IN THIS CITY AND I HAVE LOOKED FOR TWO DAYS.**
>
> **AND I AM THE PERSON WHO DRAFTED THE TERM. I AM ENTERING THAT. I AM NOT ENTERING ANYTHING ELSE, AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE ENTERED AS A MAN WHO REFUSED A WOMAN SOMETHING, BECAUSE I DID NOT REFUSE HER ANYTHING, I DRAFTED IT, AND A THING A PERSON DRAFTED THEY DO NOT GET RELIEVED OF BY DECIDING TO BE BOUND BY IT.**

“That is the fifth use,” he said. “I have now put it in a tray of my own and it is on a piece of paper in a room and I cannot take it back, and I would like you to notice that I have given you a thing that is worth nothing and that is the only kind of thing I have.”

She thanked him. He said that was not permitted and she said it was not a favour and they were both being exact about the same thing.

---

She wrote two letters on the fifteenth, one in the morning and one in the evening, and sent neither until the sixteenth, and there are two people in this city who have been keeping pieces of paper for her for six years and neither of them is a clerk and neither of them has ever been asked to keep anything.

She did not give a reason. That was the cost, and it was deliberate, and she wrote in her own book that it was deliberate and that the deliberateness was the cruelty, and that the cruelty was a thing she had chosen on the fourteenth with a pen in her hand in about four minutes, and that a reason would have been a matter, and that she had spent six years writing down what things are for and she was not going to begin on two people she is fond of.

> **A REASON WOULD HAVE BEEN A MATTER. I HAVE SPENT FOUR MONTHS TELLING A CITY THAT A THING ENTERED WITH A REASON IN IT IS A THING A CLERK CAN COME BACK WITH, AND I HAVE BEEN RIGHT EVERY TIME, AND I AM SENDING TWO LETTERS IN THE MORNING WITH NO REASON IN THEM TO TWO PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT CLERKS AND WHO HAVE KEPT PAPER FOR ME FOR SIX YEARS WITHOUT BEING ASKED, AND THEY WILL BOTH READ IT AS A WOMAN WHO HAS BEEN TOLD NO BY SOMEBODY IMPORTANT, WHICH IS NOT TRUE AND IS NEAR ENOUGH TRUE TO BE NASTIER THAN THE TRUTH, WHICH IS THE SECOND THING THAT HAS HAPPENED TO THOSE TWO PEOPLE IN SIX YEARS THAT I HAVE CAUSED.**

And then, on the sixteenth, she did not open the book.

The book is in her own rooms and it is a family thing and it is a case and it has been on a shelf in her own rooms since she was twenty-three, and she has not opened it in six years, and she went and stood in front of it at about the sixth bell on the sixteenth of the fifth month and put her hand flat on the boards, the way a man puts his hand flat on a table when he is about to say something, and she did not open it, and the hand was there for about forty seconds.

A person refusing herself is not the same thing as a person being refused, and she has known that since the second of the second month, and she could not tell you on the sixteenth of the fifth which of the two had happened in this room, and that is the honest account and there is no better one, and she is not going to write it down as one or the other, because whichever of the two she writes down, a clerk in twenty years will read it as an excuse, and she is not going to be an excuse in her own book.

---

And then, because a person who has refused herself once has about a quarter of an hour of clarity left in her and is not going to waste it, she worked out what she has actually done, and it took nine minutes, and it is on the back of the same leaf of her own book with the date on it.

She has entered a record. It is in her own book. Her own book is in her own rooms in a city four miles from a bridge, and a copy of the part of it that matters is in a day book in a building with no name on the door four miles from the court, and neither of those two rooms is the room over a pump in a district of about two hundred and forty people, and that room has a page in it and a boy of nineteen who can get most of a sentence and not the whole and who is not paid.

There is no instrument in this city that carries a record from one room to another room. There is one thing in this case that carries anything at all, and it is a clause, and it names a man, and it carries nine sheets of a charter to a district that asks, at his own cost and without pay, and if he is not within four miles the terms are not carried and the appeal is stayed. He is not within four miles of a building with no name on the door. He is four miles from a bridge.

So the fragment is in a building that the district cannot reach, in a book that nobody in four miles has ever seen, entered by a woman who may not be in a matter and is not a party to anything and has no office in that district and cannot be asked to attend, and the one person who asked is the only person who cannot be named in a support of it.

A record of a page is a thing a person can be sent for. She wrote that on the twelfth. What she had not written on the twelfth is the other side of it, and here it is, and it is the whole of what she has done in four months in two sentences: a record of a page is also a thing that cannot be sent anywhere.

> **SO I HAVE THE ONLY COPY OF TEN WORDS THAT NOBODY IN THIS CITY HAS, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO COULD ASK FOR IT TO BE READ ALOUD AT A PUMP IS A WOMAN WHO CANNOT APPEAL FROM ONE WORD OF THE PAGE SHE HOLDS, AND I CANNOT SEND IT TO HER, AND SHE CANNOT ASK ME FOR IT, AND IF I PUT IT IN A LETTER IT IS A MATTER, AND IF IT IS A MATTER IT IS MINE, AND IT IS NOT MINE.**

On the sixteenth, in a lane at the low end of a street of counting-houses, a man of thirty-one said a thing out loud that he had said out loud twice before, and this is the third time, and it is in a lane and not in a room and he was not asked and he did not go anywhere to say it.

“There is a docket in this city that a seal went on before I came into the world, and eleven things went into a week through a routing code with no name on it, and there is an authority in this city that has answered three times now, in the same hand, that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person,” he said, “and I am not going to chase it, and I said that in a room two months ago and I am saying it in a lane because a lane is where a thing gets said twice.

“And the reason I am not going to is not that it frightens me. It is that the price of going on after that desk is that records stop coming, to everybody, and there is a woman of twenty-nine four miles down this road with nine sheets and a page and a boy of nineteen who is not paid, and a man of nineteen miles off at the cut banks who has lost four hours a day, and a woman of sixty-one four miles further on who is one pound ten shillings and fourpence a year poorer for a rate, and none of those three would be owed anything by me if I went up a stair with a tray. The records that come back down that stair are the ones that put a bench's own number in a clerk's own book. I have watched it happen three times. I am not doing it for an eighth thing in a record, because there is no line on which a person is paid for having an eighth thing either, and the eighth one would cost four days of a man's sweeping and four shillings of a woman's, and a hand he cannot open on command and I have seen what that looks like.”

Nobody argued with him, because there was nobody there, and he did not tell anybody that he had said it, except a man with a bad hand who was putting a day book away and who heard all of it and who has not repeated it and who was not asked to.
