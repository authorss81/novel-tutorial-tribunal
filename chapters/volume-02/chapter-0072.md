# Chapter 72: What the Word Does to a Man With a Season

The form was on the bench of four's table at Nethergill on the first of the month, face up, under a candlestick, and it had been there since the twenty-eighth of the month before, which was the day it came in the post with a district's own circular about drainage and a bill for a horse trough.

Nobody in that room had ordered it. Nobody in that room knew who had sent it. It was sealed, and it was numbered, and it was a single sheet printed in Orison in a district's typeface by a shop that fills a space in a docket, and at the top of it in a bigger letter than any other word on the page was EXCEPTION, and underneath the word, in the same size as everything else, it was a summary of the charter of the district of Halloway with an appeal in it.

There is no court in this city that can stop a form. A form is a habit with a seal on it. That sentence was said out loud on a step in the middle of the last month by a man of nineteen years in the chamber who wanted one legible rule, and it was correct, and nobody did anything about it, and here was the thing it predicted, in a cold room off a lane eleven miles from a bridge, in the hands of four people who could not pay for a form and had not been sent one.

Jonas Pyle, who was sixty-three and convenor of a bench of four and had farmed eleven acres that his son did not want, put his thumb on it and said:

“It came with the horse trough bill and I have read it twice and I do not know what to tell you, and I will tell you what I think it says, which is that we have a rule now and we can send a man up the road to complain.”

“You cannot,” said Hollis Dune, who was thirty-seven and the district’s only person who wrote a hand that anybody paid for, having been a drover’s clerk for nineteen years and having lost the job when the drovers went to the canal. “That is Halloway. That is four miles in the other direction and it is a charter of a different district and it does not touch this lane.”

“Then why is it on my table?”

“Because it came in the post.” He turned it round so the seal was at the top, which was how he had learned to do things. “And because I have got a shelf of these things in the back room that arrived in the post, and I have never thrown one away in six years, and I will tell you why, and it is not tidiness. It is that every one of them has been sent to somebody in this district and I have never been able to work out by whom, and if a paper arrives addressed to nobody and is put on a table, then after about a year you start being the place it arrives at.”

Nobody had read it out. There is not one person in that room who can read a legal sentence. There is a woman in this district who can read a notice and a psalm and a tally, four miles up the lane, and she was not in the room, and the room is four, and the room is a bench.

Marguerite Skeath, who was fifty-one and kept the chapel book and had brought the water in, said the only useful thing anybody said that morning, which was that a man had come to her at the bar two days before and asked her whether the paper in his hand was the rule he was living inside, and that she had said she did not know, and that he had said *I will find somebody who does*, and had gone off down the lane at a run.

---

The man was Hal Dreeve, thirty-six, and he hired out barrows, which meant he owned two and hired himself with them and made his living off the difference between what a man would pay to have a thing moved two hundred yards and what it cost to move it.

He had a mother in two rooms at the top of a lane four miles off and he had put his name down in the middle of the month before for a season at the weighhouse on the main road, carting for a coal factor, at a figure he had done out twice and that would put a roof on those two rooms. A season is four months. He had been in the weighhouse yard that morning, on the strength of the season, getting the weight of a load of slack with a man who was going to be his foreman, and the foreman had a form on his bench, and the foreman had said he was sorry.

“Not you, mate. Not this district’s trade.”

“It is carting. Carting is carting. What is it about the form?”

The foreman had turned it round, and the word at the top of it had been about two inches high, and Hal Dreeve could read, because he had had to learn to read rates to hire barrows and could not have done it otherwise.

“It says you are on an exception,” the foreman said. “That is a temporary thing. That is what that word is, mate, my father was on one at the yard and I know what they are. It comes with a limit and an end on it.” He had put his thumb under the word. “And the factor will not hire a man for four months on a district’s say-so if the district’s say-so is a temporary say-so, and he will not be the man who explains it to you in four years when you have put four months of wages in a roof. I am sorry. Take the form and go and get them to change it.”

“Change what?”

“The word,” said the foreman. “If it said *charter* it would be a charter and a charter is a charter. That is what I would say to him. A charter has no end on it.”

That is the whole of what happened, and none of it was anybody’s fault, and the season was gone.

And the fifth sentence happened on the second of the month, in a weighhouse yard at the fourth bell, and Ilyan was inside it because he went back to ask one question and stayed eleven minutes and did not get it for nine of them.

The foreman was Parnell Slee, forty-eight, nineteen years at the weighhouse, with a way of standing with one foot on a scale-house step that made it look like a decision.

“You have been up that lane,” he said. “I heard about it from Troth the day before last. The man is not stupid and I have nothing against him, and I want to tell you the other thing, because you are going to go back and tell him the first thing and the first thing is half of it.”

“Go on.”

“Two seasons is what a man does at a weighhouse. Not one. A season is four months and a weighhouse runs on a man who knows what a load of slack weighs in the rain, and a man who has been here one season knows it in August. The factor is not a villain and he will not put a man on for four months out of a season he cannot use, and that is true whether your district has a charter or a by-law or nothing at all. That is the second thing, and I would have said it to him with or without your paper in my hand.”

“Then what did the paper do.”

“The paper gave me a sentence,” said Parnell Slee. “That is what it did, and I will not be ashamed of it in front of a man who has been in a court. I had a man on my step at eight in the morning wanting four months, and I had a reason, and the reason was *I do not know you*, and he would have gone away and he would have been right. I did not say that. I had a form on my bench — one of nine that have come in the district post inside a fortnight, all with a word at the top of them I have no use for — and I picked it up and I said, *you are on an exception, mate, and an exception ends.* He said *I do not know what an exception is.* I said *it is a temporary thing.* He said *temporary till when.* I said *till it is over.* And that was ninety seconds, and it was my foreman’s job, and it was the most useful thing I have said to anybody this month.

“That is all it takes. You do not have to lie to a man about his own wage. You only have to hand him a reason with a big word in it. And there is now a shelf in nine districts where a person can be handed a reason with a big word on it, and I took one off the shelf, and I would like somebody to send the shelf back.”

He got his own question answered on the tenth minute, and it was not the answer he came for.

“Why did the shop print it?”

“Because it fills a space in a docket,” said Parnell Slee. “And because I sent the post back once and it came again. And because I did not write and say *we do not want this.* I have forty men on this yard and I did not write a letter about a form. Somebody in a chamber has to decide that nine forms a fortnight is a thing worth a letter, and I am a weighhouse foreman and I have a scale to keep in order.”

That is the discovery of the whole of this business and it is nine words long and it is not a discovery about a court. The word is not a label on a rule. The word is a claim that the rule is temporary.

A form that says a rule is temporary can be used against the person who relies on the rule, and the person who relies on the rule cannot see the difference, because the difference is the whole of the rule, and a rule is two hundred words and a difference is four letters at the top of somebody else’s printing.

And the charter of the district of Halloway has no end on it. It was made by a bench of four on the seventeenth of the month at the ninth bell and it can be appealed against and the rate on the pump is a half a farthing a bucket and it is read aloud at the sixth bell on the first of every quarter, and there is nothing in it that expires, and there is nothing in it that anybody in this city can point to and say *that is when it stops.* It has no end on it, because the thing it was made out of is a bridge, and a bridge does not end.

The other one did. The clean instrument that lost expires at the third sitting of next quarter, and that is a proper thing for a clean instrument to have, and the court’s own reason for making one was speed, and speed needs a date. So there was a word in this city for a temporary permission, correctly, in a document, with a limit and an expiry and a burden on it, and it is not a wrong word and it is not a new word and it is not anybody’s invention.

And somebody’s print shop filled a space in a docket and put it on top of a permanent instrument in a poor district, and a man lost four months of wages and a roof.

---

He got it out of Hal Dreeve by going down the lane at the second bell and asking to see it, and Hal Dreeve handed it over without suspicion because men hand over things with big words on them to anybody who looks as though he might know what the words mean.

And he read it in about nine seconds and he found the seam in about four more, and this is the honest account of what he is for.

The form was assembled out of six things and the six things were each correct. It took the fifth term’s rate off the barrel lid and the fourth term’s pay line off the ninth bridge’s charter and the appeal off the whole of it and put EXCEPTION above all of it, and where the charter said *may not be refused*, the form said *except*, and the form did not say *except* about a gate or a rate or a person. It said *except* about the district.

And there was not one mistake in it. Every line of it was a true line. It was the assembly that was the lie, and assembly is the only thing he can see.

“The word at the top,” he said, “is not in your charter.”

“There is no charter of ours,” said Hal Dreeve. “Yours is Halloway’s. I have got a form they sent me and I asked the woman at the bar whether it was the rule I was living inside.”

“You are not living inside it. It does not touch this lane, it does not touch this district, and it was made by a bench four miles in the other direction for a district that has one gate and one pump.” He put his finger on the word. “And this is not in it. Not in the charter, not in the instrument that lost, not in the ninth bridge’s, not in anything in this case. It is a Bench word. It means a temporary permission to act against a standing rule, with a limit on it and an end on it and a burden on it. That is what it means and that is the whole of what it means and you have had it for two days.”

“Then I have lost a season over a word.”

“No,” he said. “You have lost a season over a word that somebody printed, and I want to be careful here because the difference is the difference and you are owed the difference. The word is not your fault. The word is not anybody’s fault, and there is nobody in this city I could take it to who could make them stop. A court has no power over a form. I have said that sentence out loud twice in a month in front of a magistrate and both times it was true and neither time it did anything.”

“Then what was I supposed to do.”

And here is where he found the thing he could not do, and it was not the language this time, and it was worse than the language.

He could follow all of it. Every line of the form, and the charter, and the clean instrument it was summarising, and the six things it had been assembled out of, and the four miles. He had been in a city of instruments for nine weeks and a city of instruments was the worst place in the world for a man with two hundred guild words in him, and today it was not. Today he read a form that had ruined a man’s season in about thirteen seconds and he could have said what was wrong with every line of it in words that a weighhouse foreman would have understood.

And he had no way to do one single thing about any of it.

The charter was Halloway’s and he was not a party to it. The form was un-ordered and sealed and numbered and no bench in this city can refuse to produce it and no court can forbid it. The weighhouse on the main road was not a party to anything and had never heard of the ninth bridge and did not pay the ninth bridge’s road fund a penny. The man’s season was a contract between a man and a coal factor on a road eleven miles from a bridge, and the whole of the law of this case was in the last nine weeks and none of it reached that contract.

And the one thing he could have done — stood in that weighhouse at the third bell and said, out loud, that the word is not in the charter, and the charter has no end on it, and this form is not the rule you are living inside — the one thing he could have done was a thing that would have made a clerk in a weighhouse decide a question about a word on a form, and a clerk who decides a question becomes the mechanism, and the mechanism does not know it is the mechanism, and the last mechanism in this case is thirty-one years old and has a stone round her neck and has asked to be relieved of the print authority five times.

So he could not speak, and the reason he could not speak was a rule, and the rule was *correct*, and being correct is the thing that has stopped him three times in nine weeks.

He stood in a lane with a form in his hand and worked out, in about nine seconds, that the only thing available to him was a definition.

“Go back,” he said. “Not to the weighhouse. Go to the bar, and find Mrs Vole, and ask her for the copy of the terms. Ask her to bring it, because you will not get it out of that room and she will not let you carry it.”

“What for?”

“For the word,” he said. “You are the only person on this road who can read a rate and you have just lost four months of wages over a four-letter word, and I can tell you what the word means and I have just told you, and it means you are not on one. But you will not believe a stranger on a lane, and neither would the foreman, and you are right not to, because in nine weeks four men in this city have said the exact opposite to you in four different rooms and at least two of them were on my side.

“Go and get the nine sheets. Read the top of the fifth term. And when the foreman asks you again, do not tell him what the word means. Tell him where to go and read it, and let the woman at the bar read it out loud to him, because she reads it out loud to two hundred people at a pump every quarter and it is the only thing she is paid for and he will have to stand there and listen to the whole of it.”

That is what he had. Not a remedy. Not a name. Not a place to take it to. A direction and a woman and nine sheets, and the four miles of lane between them, and the man would have to walk it twice.

Hal Dreeve looked at the form for a while, and then he said the thing that has been said to him twice in a month by men in rooms, and he said it standing in a lane with a barrow's shaft in his hand:

“Nobody comes out here.”

---

He walked the four miles up the lane that afternoon and read the nine sheets aloud in a cold room to about nine people, and read them twice, and the second reading was by Hester Vole and the second reading was better, and the second reading had four words in it he had got wrong and one of them was *holder*, and he was not in that district's business and there was nobody he could hand the reading to.

And the word on the form went into the record the next day, from a magistrate's clerk in Orison who had put it in because a woman of twenty-nine read it out at a pump on the twenty-second of the last month and said the word had come from a hearing, and the clerk wrote down what she had said and nothing else, and the record says:

> **A FORM HAS BEEN RECEIVED IN NINE DISTRICTS. IT IS SEALED, NUMBERED, UN-ORDERED, AND SUMMARISES AN INSTRUMENT OF ANOTHER DISTRICT. THE WORD AT THE HEAD OF IT IS NOT IN THAT INSTRUMENT OR IN ANY OTHER. THE COURT HAS NO POWER OVER A FORM. THE COURT HAS TAKEN NO VIEW ON WHAT A FORM IS FOR AND SAYS SO ON THE FACE OF THIS ENTRY.**

The court took no view and there was nothing else for it to take a view on, and in a weighhouse yard eleven miles from a bridge a coal factor has written in a book that he will not employ out of the district of Nethergill for a season.

The season was four months. It was gone on the twenty-eighth of the month before, in about ninety seconds, in a yard, over a four-letter word, by a man who was sorry.
