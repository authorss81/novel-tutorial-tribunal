# Chapter 98: The Price of a Form

The box came off the fourth shelf counting back from the window on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month, at the fourth bell, in about four minutes, with a man who cannot open his right hand on command holding the lid while a recordwright of a public record lifted nine hundred folded sheets out of it in stacks of fifty and laid them on the counting-house floor in a lane where the rain gets in.

The forms are all one print. They are all the same sheet, in the same shop's typeface, with the same heading at the top in a letter bigger than any other letter on the page, and the heading is one word, and it is a word that is in no instrument in this case.

> **EXCEPTION**

That is the whole of the top of it. Underneath the word there is a district's name, then a number, then a line for a person, then six or eight short clauses in a clerk's small hand, and the clauses are not the same in all of them. The word is the same in all of them. It is set the same size in all of them. It is in the same place in all of them, which is at the top, above the district's name, and there is nothing above it.

“They came on a cart in the winter before last,” said Corrin Vask, from the doorway, because he was not going to be asked twice about the box and he had not been asked about it since the twelfth. “There were nine hundred and about forty of them and the docket on the lid said a bench of four and it did not say which one, and I have dusted them about two hundred times and I have read the top of every one of them about four hundred times, and I have never known what the word means, and there is a word for the man who does not know what a word means and it is not the same word.”

“That is a *form*,” said Sera Quill. “Not an instrument. A form is a thing made to be filled in, and a form is not binding on anybody, and a court has no power over a form, and a bench has no power over a form either.”

“Then what is nine hundred and forty of them in a box for.”

“An ordinary district prints a form and fills it in and sends it back, and the copy goes in a book and nobody ever calls for it, and the box is where the box goes.” She put the stacks down. “This one was never called for. That is all. It is in a building because buildings are where things are, and not because anybody in this city has wanted a form.”

“Who printed them.”

“A shop. Not this month.” She squared the stacks with her boot. “Mr Vask, I am going to ask you one more question and it is the last one I am going to ask you this year and I am telling you that in advance so that you can decide what to do with it, and I would like you to know before you answer that I already know the answer, which is that a shop in a city fills a space in a docket, and a foreman does not write a letter about nine forms in a fortnight, and nobody in this city is anybody's fault.”

---

The first district to print the terms of another district's charter on a form with that word at the top of it is the district of Coldwater, and the clerk of Coldwater is a woman of fifty-one who has held the post for twenty-six years and is called Hulda Vane, and she found it herself, on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month, in about nine minutes, standing at a table in her own office with a sheet in her hand.

She had ordered it. That is the whole of it and she said so in the first four words of everything she said on that day, before anybody had asked her anything, which is a thing she has done twice before in this case and has never once pretended was somebody else's doing.

“I ordered four hundred,” she said. “I have a bench of five and three spans and eight notices a quarter that I serve myself because there is no clause in this county that says how to serve a district, and a notice takes me a clerk's hour and about thirty-one of those hours in a quarter are me working out who lives where, and a form is a shilling and fourpence and I did the sum on the back of a docket and the sum was right. And a form is cheaper. I am the clerk of this bench and I have said for twenty-six years that the work of making a document ready is the only thing in this city nobody pays for, and I have paid for it out of my own time for twenty-six years, and in the fourth month of this year a counsel of a court stood in the open air in a city four miles off and said the same thing in a louder voice than I could manage, and I went home and thought about it for two days and then I ordered four hundred forms.”

“What was on them,” said Dessin Nahl, who had come eleven miles on a cart and had entered the price of it.

“The terms of the charter of the district of Halloway,” said Hulda Vane. “Nine sheets, sworn, sealed, five terms, with a person in the fifth of them. We received it in the first week of March. We adopted nine terms of the ninth-bridge settlement on the twelfth and we minuted that term six was not noticed and not adopted and that the settlement is not binding on this district and never was, and we adopted it. We did not adopt the Halloway charter, and nobody asked us to, and I entered that on the fifteenth of March in our own hand and nobody in this case has ever read it, including the man whose name is in the second half of its fourth term.”

“Then why is their charter on your form.”

“Because it is the only instrument in this case that a poor person was *given*,” said Vane, “and my bench received it, and I thought a district that received a thing ought to be able to show a person in a lane what it received, and a sheet of paper with the terms on it is what a person can be handed at a turnbull, and I ordered four hundred of them and I did not read the top of the print before it came.”

---

She had. That is the thing that is not in anybody's finding and that Hulda Vane entered herself in the sixth line of the entry she made on the twenty-fourth.

“I had read the top of the print,” she said. “I read it on the eleventh when it came back from the shop and I thought about it for the better part of a minute, and my minute was this. The word is not in the charter. I have read all nine sheets; the word is not in any of them, in any term, and it is not in the clean instrument that lost either, because I have read that one too, it is in a drawer in a weighhouse office four days up the road and I have read it. So there is a word at the top of my form that is in no instrument. And my minute and a half was: it is a Bench word, and it means a temporary permission with a limit and an expiry and a burden on it, and the Halloway charter has no limit and no expiry, and if I put that word at the top of a form carrying those terms then the form is saying that the terms of a charter with no end are temporary, and anybody who wants to get round a clause in those terms can now get round it by pointing at the top of my own paper.

“And I ordered the balance anyway,” she said. “Three hundred and twenty. I have four hundred and sixty-three in a room and the shop has a hundred and forty in a drawer against an order that was filled out of a docket and I never sent a second order and I do not know why the shop printed one hundred and forty of a thing nobody ordered, and I have stopped looking into that, because I have looked into it for two days and the answer is a space in a docket and a foreman who is not a liar and is not anybody's villain and is a man with a form to fill in a week.”

Nobody in the room said anything for about a minute, and the rain came down the gutter on the other side of the wall.

“Enter the whole of it,” said Nahl. “Including the balance of three hundred and twenty and the one hundred and forty that nobody ordered.”

---

And here is the price of it, and it is not nine hundred and forty pieces of paper in a box, and the first print is not anybody's fault, and both of those things are true at once and about four people who are all doing something defensible.

Perdy Sill is thirty-eight and has a coal yard at the bottom of the slipway and she has wanted a cart for about two years and she has said so out loud in a room in a city and got nothing for it, and she is the second body of a form in this case, and she is the reason a form is not a mechanism. It is a person. She is four times poorer than most of the people on her own slipway and has no word she can take hold of and shake, and Coldwater's own Form C-4 has one good clause in it, and the good clause is clause two, and clause two says no person not entered in a carter's book shall pass the turnbull with a load, and the word traveller is not used and is not to be read into it.

Clause two is the only thing in that district that she can put in front of anybody, and it is the only reason the lane at the bottom of the slipway is not shut.

And on the twenty-fifth of the fifth month a man with a sledge produced a sheet of paper in Hulda Vane's own office, in front of five people, and held it up with the word at the top of it in a bigger letter than any other letter on the page, and he did not have to raise his voice and he did not have to lie.

“That is your clause in there,” he said, “under the terms of a charter that is an *exception*. It is a temporary permission. It has an expiry on it. I have been bringing stone down that lane eleven years and there are four hundred and sixty-three of your own bench's paper in this building with that word at the top of every one of them saying the whole of that is temporary, and when it is temporary it ends, and I am not going to be a man who is tied to the bottom of the slipway when it ends.”

Nobody in the room could tell him he was wrong, and two people in the room could tell him he was half wrong, and the half he was wrong about was the half that mattered, and it is this: an exception is a permission against a rule, with a limit and an expiry and a burden on it, and the charter of the district of Halloway has no limit and no expiry and is not temporary, and a word at the top of a form does not make a rule temporary, and a form is not an instrument, and a bench has no power over a form and a court has no power over a form.

And all of that was said, in a room, by five people and a clerk from a chamber eleven miles away, on the twenty-fifth of the fifth month, in about twenty minutes, and it is true, and it changed nothing at all, because what the man with the sledge had taken away was not the clause. It was the belief that the clause was on a piece of paper.

That is the whole mechanism and it is the thing nobody in this case has ever had to say out loud before, and it is the finding of the twenty-fifth, and Nahl entered it in the chamber's book on the twenty-sixth and Harrow read it at a counter off the second gallery on the twenty-seventh and neither of them improved it.

> **A FORM IS A HABIT WITH A SEAL ON IT. THE WORD AT THE TOP OF ONE IS A CLAIM THAT A RULE IS TEMPORARY, AND A CLAIM LIKE THAT IS TRUE OF NOTHING AND IS ENOUGH TO LET A PERSON WHO IS NOT A LAWYER TAKE A CLAUSE AWAY FROM THE ONLY PERSON WHO WAS USING IT. IT DOES NOT TAKE THE CLAUSE. IT TAKES THE PAPER. AND THE PAPER IS THE ONLY THING A POOR PERSON IN THIS CITY CAN PUT IN FRONT OF A MAN WHO HAS A CART.**
>
> **NO COURT IN THIS CITY HAS POWER OVER A FORM. NO BENCH IN THIS CITY HAS POWER OVER A FORM. A BENCH CAN SAY, IN PUBLIC, WITH A DATE, THAT A FORM IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT, AND EVERY ONE OF THE NINE BENCHES THAT HAVE NOW SAID IT HAS SAID IT IN A ROOM THAT HAS NO READER AND NO PUMP AND NO RULE AND ABOUT NOBODY, AND A FORM THAT IS COPIED AND SENT ROUND IS USED A LONG WAY FROM THE ROOM WHERE IT WAS ANSWERED.**

Perdy Sill was in that room and she said about nine sentences and they are in the book of the bench of five of Coldwater in the bench's own hand and she has asked that they be read out at the ordinary times and nobody has done that because Coldwater has no ordinary time at which anything is read out to anybody.

The nine sentences are this, in her words, and she is right about six of them.

“One: a form is not a charter and I did not think it was. I am not a fool and I have been in a coal yard at the bottom of a slipway for two years and I can read a clause and I can read a number.

“Two: the word at the top is not in the charter and I have now read all nine sheets because they are in my own bench's room and I asked, and I am not going to be a fool about that either.

“Three: what the man with the sledge has taken is not clause two. Clause two is in Coldwater's own instrument, published at the fourth bell on the sixth of the second month, numbered, sealed, and nobody can take it out of that. He knows that. He is not a fool either. We are two people who both know exactly what a piece of paper is.

“Four: and that is the thing, and it is the thing I have not got round to in two years, and I would like somebody to tell me what to do with it. A piece of paper is not a thing. It is a thing somebody can be sent for. Clause two is a thing I can take to the turnbull and hold up and a carter has to answer, because a carter has answered a piece of paper before, because that is what carters do. And now there is another piece of paper and it is *mine*, it is in my own bench's room, it was printed by my own clerk, and it is better than his, because it has got a seal and a number and five terms and a word at the top, and there is nothing in the world that stops me from holding it up.

“Five: so I went to my bench and I asked them whether the terms of that charter are temporary, and they said no, and they said a form is not an instrument, and they said the word is not in the charter. And they are right, and I have written it down, and I would like it noticed that the man with the sledge was in the room when they said it.

“Six: he did not care. He wrote down what they said and he put his pen away and he said he was not stopping and he was not going to be a man who is tied to a lane that is going to shut. And then he asked me, in front of five people, for the pitch at the bottom of the slipway, back, in writing, in a month.

“Seven: I have a coal yard at the bottom of that slipway and four people eat off it, and one of them is me and I am thirty-eight, and the pitch is the yard, and I am going to give it back. I am not going to be able to stay in a lane that a form says is going to shut, because who do I have to say shut to, and I have nobody, and the whole of this case is that about nine people have been saying that for four months and there is no room in this city to say it in that is not a room with somebody else's book in it.

“Eight: and the clerk who printed it, who has been a clerk for twenty-six years and who did it in a good cause and who has entered every word of it including the parts that are against her, is not going to lose her post over it, because a form is not an instrument and she has said so, and she is right, and I am not going to be the woman who makes a clerk of fifty-one lose her post for a thing she has already written down in her own hand.

“Nine: and I want it in the book that I would still be in that lane if the word was not on the form. That is the last thing I have to say and I have thought about it for a day.”

---

Corva Silt read the whole thing on the twenty-seventh in a room over a pump, four miles down a fen road, and it took about nine minutes, and when she had finished she put her pen down and said the only thing she had ever wanted to say about a form and had not said in March, because in March it would have been an argument and now it is a fact.

“In March I put one of these on the gate board under the rate,” she said, “and wrote on the back of it, in the district's own hand, that the word is not in the charter and to read the charter. I have it still. It is nailed up there with four nails and no glass and about two hundred people a day go past it getting water, and in March I thought that was the whole of what a person can do with a form, and it was, and it is still the whole of what a person can do with a form, and it is not enough, and it was never going to be enough, and I knew it was not enough on the day I wrote it, because a note on the back of a form is a thing a person says in a lane and a form is a thing a person holds up in front of a carter, and the carter has a cart and the person with the note has a yoke.

“The form they came up the fen road with on the twenty-second of the second month is a form summarising a charter with the word at the top of it, and it is in nine districts, and I have known since the fourth of the fourth month that the word was coming, and I said so out loud in a room over a pump on the twenty-second of the second month and I said nobody can stop the printing, and I was right, and I have been right about almost everything since the fourth of the fourth month, and I would like it entered that a woman of thirty-eight has just given up a coal yard because a clerk of fifty-one in another district was right about a sum on the back of a docket.

“I have asked five times in six weeks to be relieved of this district's print authority and been refused five times and I said I would not ask a sixth, and I am not asking, and I want the sixth refusal on the record anyway, from whoever is standing in the room: the print authority of this district printed H-9 and put a copy of the ninth-bridge settlement on a gate board with four nails and no glass, and it holds a form in its own drawer with a word at the top of it, and I cannot be relieved of it, and I would like to be relieved of it, and I have stopped asking, and a woman of thirty-eight has paid for the authority in a coal yard.”

Nobody asked her a sixth time and nobody refused her a sixth time, and that is the finding of the twenty-seventh of the fifth month, in one clause, and it is that a request that is never made and never refused is a thing that goes on being true for ever, and the only way anybody finds out is the way the washer of sixty-one found out, which is by walking four miles.

And the System put the price in front of Ilyan Vester on the twenty-eighth in a room in a city four miles from a bridge, and it is a record of a room and not a rule about the world, and he was not in the room and it is the only copy of it he has.

**BETWEEN THE TWENTY-FOURTH AND THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH, IN THREE ROOMS AND A LANE:**
**A CLERK OF TWENTY-SIX YEARS IN ONE DISTRICT ORDERED FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE FORMS CARRYING THE TERMS OF ANOTHER DISTRICT'S CHARTER, IN A SHOP'S TYPEFACE, UN-ORDERED BY THE SHOP IN THE LAST HUNDRED AND FORTY OF THEM, SEALED AND NUMBERED, WITH ONE WORD AT THE TOP OF EACH IN A LETTER BIGGER THAN ANY OTHER WORD ON THE PAGE. THAT WORD IS IN NO INSTRUMENT IN THIS CASE. THE CLERK READ IT, THOUGHT ABOUT IT FOR ABOUT A MINUTE AND A HALF, AND PRINTED IT ANYWAY, TWICE MORE, AND ENTERED ALL OF IT IN HER OWN HAND.**
**WHAT SHE BOUGHT: A SHILLING AND FOURPENCE A FORM AGAINST THIRTY-ONE CLERK'S HOURS A QUARTER OF SERVING NOTICES BY HAND, WHICH IS A CORRECT SUM AND A RIGHT CAUSE.**
**WHAT IT COST: A PITCH AT THE BOTTOM OF A SLIPWAY, FOUR PEOPLE, AND A THIRTY-EIGHT-YEAR-OLD WOMAN'S BELIEF THAT A PIECE OF PAPER CAN BE HELD UP.**
**THE WORD ON THE FORM IS TRUE OF NOTHING. A TEMPORARY PERMISSION HAS A LIMIT, AN EXPIRY AND A BURDEN, AND THE CHARTER IN QUESTION HAS NO LIMIT AND NO EXPIRY. A FORM IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT. NO COURT AND NO BENCH HAS POWER OVER A FORM.**
**AND A FORM IS A HABIT WITH A SEAL ON IT, AND THE HABIT IS OLDER THAN THIS CASE BY ABOUT TWO WINTERS AND IS IN NINE HUNDRED AND FORTY UNCALLED-FOR FORMS IN A BOX IN A BUILDING FOUR MILES FROM THIS COURT, AND NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED WHERE THE HABIT CAME FROM, AND THE FIRST DISTRICT TO PRINT ANOTHER DISTRICT'S CHARTER TERMS ON ONE THAT ANYBODY IN THIS CASE HAS FOUND IS THE DISTRICT OF COLDWATER, AND IT IS NOT THE FIRST PRINT OF THE WORD, BECAUSE A SHOP PRINTED A HUNDRED AND FORTY OF THEM OUT OF A DOCKET AND NOBODY EVER CAME FOR THEM.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER THE WORD WILL BE ON A FORM THAT CARRIES AN INSTRUMENT. WHETHER ANY BENCH WILL SAY A FORM IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT WHERE THE PERSON WHO RELIES ON IT CAN HEAR IT. WHETHER A CLERK MAY REFUSE A PRINT ORDER. WHETHER A MAN WITH A SLEDGE AND A MAN WITH A CART ARE, IN THIS CASE, THE SAME MAN.**
