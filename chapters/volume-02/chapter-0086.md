# Chapter 86: Three Rates and a Hole in the Middle of One of Them

The room over a pump was the only room in four miles with anybody awake in it at the first bell on the fifteenth of the fourth month, and Oakes had been in it about an hour, and the slate on the barrel lid had three sums on it and a fourth that had been scratched out four times and not wiped, so that the figures underneath were still legible to anybody who knew how a man's hand dies in the middle of a number.

Oakes had been in it since before the first bell. He is thirty-four, he has held a seal of this district for two years, and he set the district's rate for a haul on the twenty-eighth of last month in a room with three other people in it, and he wrote it out, and he has been unable to make the thing add up for eleven days.

He had done it this way, because it is the way a clerk does a thing that has gone wrong: you get the shape on the board first and the people later.

> **ONE. WHAT THE DISTRICT OWES, IF IT COULD BE PAID.**
> **THE THIRTY YEARS: A HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED HOURS AT THREEPENCE THE HOUR IS ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS.**
> **B. SALLOW'S OWN CORRECTION, GIVEN AT THE GATE, UNASKED: THE WORKING TIDE OF THE TURN IS ONE DAY IN SEVEN AND THE WEEK IS ABOUT SIXTY-THREE HOURS AND NOT SEVENTY-THREE AND A HALF. THAT TAKES OUT SIXTEEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED HOURS AND TWO HUNDRED AND TWO POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS, AND LEAVES NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED HOURS.**
> **SO: NINETY-SEVEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED HOURS AT THREEPENCE THE HOUR IS ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS.**
> **THE CORRECTION FOR THE RATE CANNOT BE FOUND IN ANY DISTRICT IN THIS CITY AND IS NOT ENTERED AS A NUMBER.**
> **THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS OF THE LAST ELEVEN MONTHS: NO FIGURE, BECAUSE SEE THE END OF ONE.**
>
> **TWO. WHAT THE DISTRICT HAS.**
> **THE ROAD FUND OF THIS DISTRICT IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY POUNDS A YEAR AND IS FED BY CARTAGE ON A SHUT ROAD. THE CARTAGE HAS BEEN NIL SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF THE MONTH BEFORE LAST. THE FUND IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY POUNDS ON PAPER.**
> **THE FUND ON PAPER HAS BEEN PAID OUT TO NIL. IT IS NIL.**
> **THE CHARTER IS PAID OUT OF A DIFFERENT DISTRICT'S FUND. IT IS NOT OURS. IT IS NOT ENTERED IN OUR BOOK AS OURS AND IT IS NOT ENTERED IN THEIRS AS THEIRS.**
>
> **THREE. WHAT THE DISTRICT IS PAYING WITH.**
> **HALF A FARTHING A BUCKET AT THE STANDPIPE. A FARTHING IS A QUARTER OF A PENNY. THIS IS AN EIGHTH OF A PENNY. IT IS ELEVEN POUNDS FOUR IN THE QUARTER AND FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND SIXTEEN SHILLINGS IN THE YEAR.**
> **OF THE ELEVEN POUNDS FOUR, THE LARGEST PART IS THE SERVING OF THE NOTICES, BECAUSE THERE IS NO CLAUSE IN ANY INSTRUMENT IN THIS CITY FOR SERVING A DISTRICT, AND SO A CLERK IS PAID BY THE HOUR TO WORK OUT WHO A DISTRICT IS.**
> **ONE PERSON IS PAID ABOUT A POUND AND ELEVEN A QUARTER TO KEEP NINE SHEETS AND A PAGE. SHE ASKED FOR THE RATE. SHE READS IT OUT. SHE CANNOT APPEAL FROM IT.**

The fourth sum, the one he scratched out four times, was the one that would not close, and the reason it would not close is the whole of what this room has been for three weeks, and it took him eleven days and a woman of twenty-nine and a man of thirty-one to find.

Corva Silt came in at about the second bell with her coat off and looked at the board and said, “That is my hand on the third one and I have never seen the second.”

“It is your hand,” said Oakes. “I copied it out of the book at about the second bell yesterday. I have not touched the book.”

“Then sit down, both of you, and do not tell me the answer, because you have both got the same face and I have seen that face on this bench four times this month.”

---

The bench came in at the second. Cael Rundle with his coat buttoned to the throat because of his chest in the wet. Nell Ambrey, who sat down and did not take her coat off. Garvin Slade, thirty-eight, who has sold salt out of a booth at the top of Weir Lane since he was thirteen, which is twenty-five years, and who has never once in his life been in a room where anything was settled, and who came because he is one of the four and had been told there was a sum.

The fourth rate is the one that would not close, and it is the one Oakes had scratched out four times, and he put his hand over it for about a minute and then took it away.

“A haul is a barrow’s load,” he said. “A barrow’s load is four loads to a cart. The district’s own rate is a rate for a load. That is the unit, and it is not arguable, and it came off a page of a charter somebody wrote in this district in a room in Orison on the seventeenth of the second month, and it is true.”

“And the bench set the haul at the district’s own rate,” said Cael Rundle, “which is a third of a haul. And the same entry says a haul here is paid at three times the district’s own rate, which is the same number said twice over. And a hauler in this district is paid a third of a haul, and every hauler in this county is paid a haul.”

“Yes,” said Oakes. “And I have known for eleven days that those do not make one another, and I have not been able to say it out loud until about four minutes ago.”

“Why not,” said Nell Ambrey.

Oakes put the chalk down on the barrel lid, which was a thing he did not do.

“Because the district’s own rate is a rate for a load, and there are no loads in this district,” he said. “There has not been a cart on the fen road since the fourteenth of the month before last, and there has not been a handcart’s worth of work done in it in my lifetime that anybody has paid for. A load is what a man takes off the bank on a cart. We have got barrows. A barrow in a district that has carts is a quarter of a cart. A barrow in this district is four loads to a cart that does not come, and the four in that sentence is a fact about a cart and not a price. So a haul in this district cannot be priced at all, not at a third and not at two thirds short, and I cannot name the shortfall because there is nothing here to name it against, and the hundred and fifty hauls will go on having no figure against them until this district agrees what a load is worth or says out loud in a book that it never will.”

Nobody said anything for a moment.

“So the entry is wrong,” said Cael Rundle.

“The entry is not wrong and I will not have it said that it is. The entry says what the bench set and the district is two thirds short and both of those are true, and the two of them do not make one another, and that is not a mistake, that is a hole, and a hole is worse than a mistake and lasts longer.” Oakes said it without heat. “And it is my fault and I am not going to let anybody else have it. I set the rate. I had four months to say what a haul is worth in this district and I did not, and if I had asked one question in that room on the twenty-eighth of last month this would not be on a slate at this hour, and I did not ask it because I did not want to be the man who asks a bench of four what a barrow is.”

“The cost of not asking it,” said Garvin Slade, “is that the woman with the barrow does not get paid and she is owed it for eleven months and it is in your book and nobody can do anything about it. I can do that much arithmetic in my head without being told the units.”

“Yes,” said Oakes. “And the cost of asking it is that a bench of four would have had to say, out loud, in a room, that a haul in this district is a barrow and a barrow is not a haul, and then the hundred and fifty hauls would have gone on the book as a hundred and fifty barrows at two thirds, and she would have read that out at a pump on the first of the month. She read out a *haul*.”

---

The System put the sum in front of Ilyan while Garvin Slade was still standing up, and it was a record of a room and not a rule about the world, and it was the only copy of it he would ever have, because he cannot read a slate.

**IN THE ROOM OVER A PUMP, THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, AT THE SECOND BELL. A SLATE WITH THREE SUMS ON IT, WRITTEN BY THE MAINTENANCE CLERK OF A BENCH OF FOUR.**
**ONE: WHAT THE DISTRICT OWES. THE THIRTY YEARS, AS ENTERED, IS ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS. AFTER THE CORRECTION GIVEN BY THE PERSON IT IS OWED TO, ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN POUNDS. THE CORRECTION FOR THE RATE CANNOT BE FOUND IN ANY DISTRICT IN THIS CITY AND IS NOT ENTERED AS A NUMBER. THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS OF THE LAST ELEVEN MONTHS HAVE NO FIGURE AT ALL, BECAUSE A HAUL IS A BARROW'S LOAD, A BARROW'S LOAD IS FOUR LOADS TO A CART, THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE IS A RATE FOR A LOAD, AND THERE ARE NO LOADS IN THIS DISTRICT.**
**TWO: WHAT THE DISTRICT HAS. TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY POUNDS A YEAR ON PAPER. NIL IN FACT SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF THE MONTH BEFORE LAST. THE CHARTER IS PAID OUT OF ANOTHER DISTRICT'S FUND AND IS IN NEITHER DISTRICT'S BOOK AS A DEBT OF EITHER.**
**THREE: WHAT THE DISTRICT IS PAYING WITH. AN EIGHTH OF A PENNY A BUCKET. FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND SIXTEEN SHILLINGS A YEAR, OF WHICH THE LARGEST SINGLE PART IS THE SERVING OF NOTICES, BECAUSE NO INSTRUMENT IN THIS CITY SAYS HOW TO SERVE A DISTRICT. ONE PERSON IS PAID ABOUT A POUND AND ELEVEN A QUARTER TO KEEP NINE SHEETS AND A PAGE, AND SHE ASKED FOR THE RATE, AND SHE READS IT OUT, AND SHE CANNOT APPEAL FROM IT.**
**THE THREE SUMS ARE THE SAME MONEY. THE ONLY ONE OF THE THREE THAT ANY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT CAN SEE IS A PUMP.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED HERE: WHETHER THE RATE FOR A HAUL WAS SET TOO LOW. WHETHER THE UNIT WAS WRONG OR THE UNIT WAS NEVER CHOSEN. WHETHER A DISTRICT THAT SETS A RATE FOR A THING IS OBLIGED TO SAY WHAT THE THING IS.**

---

Then the gate hand came, and she had not been asked and she had not sent word.

Bryn Sallow came up the bank at about the third bell with her coat up over the gate hand's apron and stood in the doorway of the room over the pump, which she has never done in her life, and which is not a complaint and was not treated as one.

“The boat boy has been talking,” she said. “The one that cannot read. He came past the gate this morning and told me there was a sum going on that slate about my thirty years and a fourth one that would not come out, and I have not come to be argued with and I have not come to be asked anything, and I am here because I have not been inside this room in thirty years and I would like to know what a thousand four hundred pounds is.”

“It is one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds,” said Oakes, “after your own correction.”

“I know what it is after my correction. I gave the correction. What I want to know is what the hundred and fifty are, and Corva Silt has been unable to tell me for eleven days, and I have watched her unable to tell me for about nine of them because she is a good clerk and she has been getting up early to look at it.”

“They have no figure,” said Oakes, “and I have given the reason and the reason is me.”

Then he said the other thing, and he said it to the room, and he said it to a bench of four and to a man with a leg and to a woman of twenty-nine who was in the doorway, and what he said is the finding of the fifteenth of the fourth month and it is the only thing in that room that was not arithmetic.

“I am going to say the part I have not said, and I am going to say it because a bench that says nothing has made a man in a city write a number about thirty years of a woman's work into a book she had refused, and I would like it in this room before the end of the day.

“The figure is in the book. The figure is a claim. A claim wants somebody to answer it. For thirty years I had been the ward and a ward is a duty and a duty cannot be answered, and there were four words in that book in a guild's hand about a hundred and ninety years ago that say exactly that, and I found them, and I did not give them to a court, and the court has found them since. So she was safe. She was the safest person in four miles, because there was nothing to answer.

“And then a man with two hundred guild words and no licence multiplied six days of a slate up by thirty years, and he said out loud that it was wrong before he wrote it, and he wrote it anyway, because I asked him for a number on the eighteenth of the second month in this room and I did it in order to put a shape on a debt and put the shape on a rate and put the rate on a barrel lid. That is the whole of it. I asked him. I am the reason she is a claim. I did not take thirty years from her. I took the shape off her that meant nobody had to answer her, and I did that in a room with a bench of four in it, and the figure is a thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds and it is wrong, and both of those are true.”

Nobody in the room said anything at all for long enough that a cart went past on the fen road.

“You are right,” said Bryn Sallow. “That is what makes it unbearable and I am going to say it once so that it is in the room and then I am never going to speak to either of you about it again. You are right. And I have not been this angry in eleven years and I am not angry about a number, I am angry that a duty with nobody in it was the only thing that had ever protected me and it has been taken off me in three weeks by a bench of four and a man from a bridge who did not know what he was doing, and neither of you has said one word to me about whether the thirty years are paid, and they are not, and they cannot be, and there is no money, and you are the man who put a claim on me and you are the man who took the ward off, and I have to be angry at you, Oakes, and not at a word, and that is the first time in my life I have had to be angry at a person about it, and I am not going to pretend that is a good thing.”

“It is not,” said Oakes. “It is a worse one. A duty with nobody in it is a debt that never gets asked about. I know that. I have relied on it twice this month and I would like it entered that I said so in a room and not afterwards.”

---

What changed that afternoon was not the sum. It was a definition.

Corva Silt took a clean sheet off the clip and wrote one line and read it out twice, because she has been the second head about this bench's arithmetic since the fourth of the month and she is not doing it again in front of a witness.

> **ENTERED IN THE BOOK OF THE BENCH OF FOUR, THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH. THIS DISTRICT HAS NEVER SAID IN WRITING WHAT ITS OWN RATE IS A RATE FOR, AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH AT THE THIRD BELL IT SAYS.**
>
> **THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE IS A RATE FOR A LOAD. A LOAD IS WHAT ONE MAN TAKES OFF THE BANK IN ONE GO ON A CART. THERE ARE NO CARTS ON THE FEN ROAD AND THE CARTAGE HAS BEEN NIL SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF THE MONTH BEFORE LAST AND THEREFORE A LOAD IS A FIGURE IN A BOOK AND NOT A THING, AND A HAUL IN THIS DISTRICT IS A BARROW, AND A BARROW IS FOUR LOADS TO A CART THAT DOES NOT COME, AND THE FOUR IN THAT SENTENCE IS A FACT ABOUT A CART AND NOT A PRICE.**
>
> **THE RATE FOR A HAUL SET ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF LAST MONTH IS NOT AMENDED BY THIS ENTRY. IT IS NOT WITHDRAWN BY IT. IT STANDS BECAUSE IT IS SWORN AND A SWORN THING IS NOT UNDONE BY A LINE IN A BOOK, AND BECAUSE THE PERSON WHO ASKED FOR IT AND THE PERSON WHO SET IT WERE BOTH IN THE ROOM AND THE THING WAS READ OUT AT A PUMP ON THE FIRST OF THE MONTH TO ABOUT TWO HUNDRED PEOPLE.**
>
> **THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS HAVE NO FIGURE AND WILL KEEP HAVING NO FIGURE UNTIL THIS DISTRICT AGREES A RATE FOR A LOAD, AND A DISTRICT MAY DO THAT AND MAY NOT, AND IT IS NOT A COURT'S BUSINESS AND IT IS NOT AN APPEAL. THE CLERK SAYS IT IS ABOUT FOURPENCE A YEAR TO WRITE DOWN AND THAT IT HAS TAKEN ELEVEN DAYS TO FIND OUT WHETHER IT IS FOURPENCE OR NOTHING.**

Nobody improved it. Cael Rundle read it and said that a rate for a load was the cheapest thing anybody had put in the book all year and that it was worth about fourpence a year to write down and that it had taken eleven days to find out whether it was fourpence or nothing.

Then he said the thing that had been sitting in his chest since the first of the month, and he said it to the slate.

“Eleven years of a cart's worth of rubbish has gone out of this district at the hands of one woman, and a cart's worth is four barrows, and this district has paid her for three of the four and never for the fourth, and the fourth is a load, and there is nowhere in this district that a load is worth anything at all, and that is not an accident. That is a hole in a book with a bench's name on it. And I have wanted to say it out loud since the twenty-eighth of March and I could not, because I did not know what a haul was.” He looked up. “That is the first thing I have said in this case that is a number and not a feeling, and it is wrong by however much a barrow is worth in a district with no cart, and I would like it minuted that I know that.”

“Two hundred and forty people,” said Nell Ambrey. “It is about two hundred and forty and it is the number in a letter we have had from another district's clerk and not in anything we have counted ourselves, and a woman read that charter out on a bank in the mud on the third of last month to about a hundred and ninety people, and that is the first time the whole of this case has had a number for how many people are inside the thing it has done, and she was standing on the bank when she read it, and the number in the letter is the clerk's estimate from a bridge and the number on the bank was hers.”

Nobody wanted the conversation to go there, and it went there anyway, and it stayed there, and a district that has a rate, a page, an appeal and a bill, and no count of its own, and the number it is using is a clerk's estimate from a bridge.

---

At the ninth bell Ilyan Vester walked the four miles back up the fen road with the leg his own for the first hour and then not his, and the gate hand was at the gate side working an oak frame with two hundred feet of chain on it with the tide coming, and she had a piece of chalk in her hand because she had been helping the boy with the slate, and she did not put it away.

“Did you know,” said Bryn Sallow, “when you put that number in my book, that the units were wrong.”

“Yes.”

“You said it was wrong before you wrote it. That is in the book in your hand and it is in his hand and it is in mine and it is the only reason I have not had this conversation with a magistrate.”

“I could not correct it,” he said. “That is the honest answer and it is a bad one. The rate in that figure is a rate that exists because a day's figure for six and a half hours has been divided by the four hours of a ten-and-a-half-hour tide that this district does not pay anybody for. There is no rate in this city from thirty years ago. I could have written *a sum nobody can check* instead of a number, and I wrote a number because Oakes asked me for a number and because a number is the only thing that can be carried in a book from a room to a bench, and I have thought about it for about nine days and I still think I would have written it and I would write it again, and that is the part I would like entered if anybody ever enters anything about it.”

“Then I will tell you what I have worked out, and you can put it wherever you go,” said Bryn Sallow, and worked the chain, and did not look at him. “I have stood at that gate thirty years and I have watched that woman with the barrow go past it about two hundred times a year, and a working tide at that gate is ten and a half hours and six and a half of them are what this district pays me for, and there is no arithmetic in this county that turns ten and a half hours into a barrow. So I have done it the only way a person at a gate can do it, which is to say that thirty years of what I have done is about six thousand of what she does, at two thirds. The number that made me a claim of it is a thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds. Neither of them can be paid, and both of them are in a book in the room you have just left, and the woman they are about is me, and the woman who is going to read them out at a pump four times a year is her.”

She put the chalk in her pocket and took her foot off the bank onto the sill-stone.

“I would like to be six thousand hauls at two thirds,” she said. “I have never once in my life been six thousand of anything. And it will be read out on a bank by a woman with a barrow, and I will be standing at the gate when she reads it, because that is where I stand.”
