# Chapter 56: Two Shadows and a Door That Opens the Wrong Way

The bank at the fourth bell on the nineteenth day smelled worse than it had on the fourteenth, and it smelled worse for a reason that could be measured, and Ilyan measured it, because it was the only thing in his two hundred words that covered this.

Wenna Tace had hauled forty-one loads since the sheet went up and the carts had taken eleven, and the four hundred loads on the flat were four hundred loads on the flat, and the tide was going out and would not take them anywhere, and the queue at the fifth bell was thirty-eight and at the sixth was forty-six and at the ninth was eleven, and the difference between the two numbers was the pump's second shift and would have been the same in any month of the year.

He was standing at the sill-stone with Coll's slate in his hand and the court's clerks behind him on the lane, when he noticed the two shadows.

His own was on the oak leaf of the gate and it went the ordinary way, long and to the left, because the light is low at the fifth bell on the low water and there is nothing above the bank on the west side except a barrow and a pole.

The other one was on the flat.

It was not his and it was not anybody's, because there was nobody between the bank and the flat; the bank ends in a step of loose stone and then it is the flat, and the low water is four hundred yards off, and the middle of the flat at the fifth bell on the nineteenth day was four hundred loads of what Wenna Tace hauled and about nine people with barrows and a boy of fifteen who had come to help and did not know where to put his feet.

The second shadow lay across about sixty feet of that flat, and it was the shape of a person walking on the level with their arms down, and it was not moving the way a shadow moves, and the thing that Ilyan understood in the next four seconds — with no ceremony at all, in the middle of a smell, in daylight, in a lane — was that it was the shape of the second person in the sentence, and that the second person is the one the rule is not about.

Because a sentence about a person has two people in it: the person the rule is for, and the person the rule leaves out, and a rule cannot say where the other one is, so the other one is not nowhere. The other one is four hundred loads on a flat.

He put the slate down against his knee.

“That is two shadows,” said Bryn Sallow, at the gate, without turning round, and she had been the gate for thirty years and had spent four days watching a man look at things and had decided to be helpful about it. “I have not got time to give it a name. Say what it is and I will stop looking at you.”

“Two,” Ilyan said.

“That is a load-carrier’s number for the second shadow at the eighth bell on a span when the sun is behind you and there is a person under the deck,” said Bryn Sallow. “You do not need a court for that. You need to sleep.”

---

The court came down the fen road at the fifth bell and set up at the second change of the tide, which is the only time in a day when the flat is walkable and the gate is half up and the water is coming, and Sel Harrow chose it in about nine seconds and did not explain himself and was right.

“Court sits at the sill-stone,” he said, “because a field which tests records behind a bench tests nothing, and I have said that once already in this case and been right, and I am not going to be asked for the reasoning twice.

“The court has no power over the gate. I want that said first and I want it said where the gate can hear it. This court has amended one charter and paid one wage and it does not own a culvert and it is not going to pretend that a field on a bank makes the gate a court matter. What this field does is put two documents next to each other in the same air and let them show what they do when they disagree. Everything else in it is a man looking hard at paper.”

Tarin put the boundary up with a rope and four stakes and did it without an order, as he had done on a span for a month, and about two hundred and forty people stayed outside it, because there is a limit to how many people will stand in a smell, and the people inside it were Harrow and Roke and Odile, Tarin at the rope with his roll over his shoulder, Dessin Nahl on the step, Corva Silt, Nell Ambrey, Cael Rundle, Wenna Tace, Bryn Sallow, and Ilyan.

The nine benches did not come. Dessin Nahl came, with the draft, and sat down on the step, and that was the whole of the city’s presence and it was enough.

The System put the field into his sight while Tarin was still driving the fourth stake, and it was a record and not a rule about the world, and it was the first thing since the hearing that it had shown him that he had not already been told out loud in a room.

> **HEARING FIELD OPENED AT THE LOW-WATER CULVERT, HALLOWAY, AT THE FIFTH BELL, ON THE NINTH-BRIDGE MATTER.**
> **COUPLED: THE NINTH-BRIDGE RECORD, AND INSTRUMENT H-9 (CULVERT), HALLOWAY, NUMBERED, PUBLISHED AT THE FOURTH BELL ON THE FOURTEENTH.**
> **THE DISPUTED WORDS: "A PERSON WHO IS NOT ENTERED IN THE LAWFUL-TRAVELER REGISTER IS NOT A TRESPASSER BY THAT FACT ALONE."**
> **WHAT IS ASKED: WHETHER AN INSTRUMENT BUILT OUT OF A PUBLIC INSTRUMENT THAT A DISTRICT DID NOT ADOPT IS WITHIN THE NINTH-BRIDGE SETTLEMENT.**
> **WHAT IS NOT ASKED: WHETHER THE GATE SHOULD BE OPEN OR SHUT. THE COURT HAS NO POWER OVER THE GATE AND SAYS SO.**

The coupling went the way it had gone on a span in the first week of the case, and it went in about four seconds, and then the two documents started disagreeing with each other in the only way documents can, which is by meaning different things while saying the same words.

Harrow read H-9 aloud. Odile read the access condition. And Ilyan stood four feet inside the rope with his slate and could not follow a word of either, and had known before the fifth bell that he would not, and had come anyway, because on the last day of a week he had watched a clerk couple a petition to a bridge record and a span had answered it, and this was the same instrument and the same procedure and there was a gate under it.

What he could see was the shape.

It came up out of the flat in about the time it takes water to find a low place, and it was not a picture and it was not a voice and it was not a system message and it was the least mystical thing that has ever happened to him. It was *assembly*. He could see the instrument standing there in pieces with the joints showing, the way you can see how a gate is put together if you have spent thirty-one days watching gates and joints and props and seats and a bell on a frame, and the pieces were these:

The traveller book, four inches thick, a hundred years dead, standing on its shelf at the gate side. That was the foot of it.

The phrase *lawful traveller*, which is the ninth bridge's own word out of an access condition two hundred years old, sitting in the middle of it like a pin through two boards.

The district's road book, a hundred and sixty years old, upstairs, saying this is a penal road and not a haul road. That was a rib.

Clause three, which was the one good clause, bolted through the whole thing and running down into the gate hand.

And the load, which was the whole of the case, and which was not in the document at all. The document said the road was a penal road and not a haul road. The loads were not in the document at all. They were on the flat, in the sun, in the fourth week of a tide, and the sentence that would have moved them was the sentence that pointed at a foot-gate in the leaf of the gate.

There was a foot-gate. He had walked past it four times and not seen it: an iron gate in the bottom third of the left leaf, eighteen inches wide, with a latch and a hinge at the bottom, hinged so that it swung.

It swung the wrong way.

It swung out, toward the low water, toward the flat, toward the people who were not on a list. A person on the lane side could not use it without stepping off the lane and out onto the side the rule had put them on. The rule had not barred the footway. The rule had built a door and hung it facing the wrong person, and then had it described, in a read-back filed beside the instrument in words a keeper can say out loud on an approach, as a provision for a person on foot who offers no load. The read-back does not govern. Nobody had sworn it and nobody had asked it not to be sworn, and the door was in it anyway.

And the bell.

The chain on the gate hand's handbell was trembling in a wind that was not touching it, four seconds before Bryn Sallow put her hand on it, and Ilyan felt it in the bones of his right hand, and it was the same note as a rope over a wheel on a post four miles up the road, and there was no way on this earth that a handbell at a gate was connected to a warning bell on a span, and both of them were in the same two documents.

---

“It is assembled out of five things and every one of them is a document,” Ilyan said, and stopped, and started again, because the first version had been a speech and he could hear it being one.

“No,” he said. “I am going to say it in the guild's way because I cannot say it in the law's way and this is not my court.

“Five things. The book, the word, the road book, the third clause, and the load. And the load is the only one that is not a piece of paper, and the load is four hundred piles of it on the flat, and nobody has to know that to read the sheet. The sheet is complete without it. The sheet would be exactly the same sheet if the flat were empty.

“Everything in that instrument is a document, and every document in it is real, and one of them is a hundred and sixty years old and says this is a penal road and is right, and one of them is two hundred years old and says the word *lawful* in a meaning, and one of them is a clause I would defend in a room, and the whole of it is well made.”

He put his hand flat on the rope.

“It is the best-made instrument I have read in this city and I cannot read a word of it. It is made the way a good bridge is made, out of everything that was to hand, each piece doing its own job, and it is standing up, and it is doing exactly what it was built to do, and there are four hundred loads on a flat because of it. Somebody made it well. That is the whole of my finding and I have not made a speech about it and I am not going to.”

Harrow said, “Is the court to understand that a well-made instrument can be unjust.”

“The court is to understand that those are two different questions and this city keeps answering the first one,” Ilyan said. “I have watched four districts argue for a month about whether a settlement is well made. It is very well made. I sat on a span for a month while the most careful people in four districts fought over a clause, and the clause is a good clause, and I could not read it, and I have said so on the record, and nobody has lost anything by it and a district four miles down the road has lost four loads of sand and a lane of forty houses and a woman her afternoon.”

Harrow said, “Then where does the burden sit.”

Before he said anything, he did the thing he had spent four days deciding he was allowed to do, which was to look at a second thing on purpose, in daylight, and to see whether it held up.

Hanne Kolm's entry of the fourteenth, and the ninth bridge's term four, are two documents about money and they were coupled in the same field, and there is nothing in this city that a man who spent a month on a bridge is better at than looking at two pieces of paper that both say the word *wage*.

It held up. For about four seconds he could see the shape of it, and the shape was legible and it was true and it was useful: Kolm's entry had a person in it, a name and a duration and a *first light to the dark* that was not a number, and the ninth bridge's term four had a person in it, a name and a moment and a rate, and the two of them sat in the same air and agreed with each other completely. Two documents, two people, no body anywhere in either.

And the cost of four seconds of that was not nothing. His left hip went cold from the front, the way a hand goes cold when it has been in a bucket, and there was a moment about six seconds after the looking stopped when he was fairly sure he could not have picked up a pencil, and he had not tried to pick up a pencil.

He put the slate down on the step, at his feet, with the numbers on it, and wrote nothing, and after a while he was able to stand up again, and he did not tell anybody what he had just done and he was not going to, because the court was in a field and a field is not the place to tell a room that a man has started being able to see things, and the truth was that he had known for four days that something had started and had not known whether it was a thing he could do or a thing that was happening to him.

And then here is where Ilyan was wrong, and it took eleven seconds, and it is the only thing in the whole of that morning that he would take back if the court would let him.

The seam ran from the phrase down through the third clause and into the gate. It ran to a person. The joints showed a person, and the person was the one at the end of the seam, and there was only one person at the end of that seam, and she was forty-four and had been standing at that gate for thirty years and had said in a room on the twelfth day that nobody had ever told her which of two things was true.

“It is on her,” Ilyan said. “The whole of it. It comes out of the fifth paragraph and it goes through the third clause and it comes out at the gate, and the person it lands on is the gate hand, and if the court wants to know what this instrument costs it costs her, and it costs her every tide, and the cost is not a number, it is a posture.”

The field disagreed with him the way a field does, which is by putting two records beside one another and letting them show what they do.

Roke read back the district's day book, the fourteenth, in the hand of a clerk of six years:

> **THE GATE HAND STATES THAT NO PERSON HAS TOLD HER WHICH OF TWO THINGS IS TRUE, FOR THIRTY YEARS, AND ASKS THAT IT BE WRITTEN DOWN.**

And then the second thing, which was in the ninth bridge's own amended charter, term four, and which Roke read twice because Odile made him:

> **THE WARD RATE IS PAID AT THE MOMENT OF THE WORK TO THE PERSON PERFORMING IT, BY NAME, ON A DAY-BOOK LINE, IN CHALK, AND NOT TO A BODY AND NOT TO A PLACE AND NOT TO AN ADDRESS.**

A burden that can be paid to a person is a burden somebody pays. Somebody had been paying it — for thirty years, out of the gate, at a rate nobody had written down, in the shape of a woman who never sat down. And the word that let her be paid was *ward*, and the ward was not a body and had no name, and the whole of the wage had been a favour with a head on it, and the seam did not end at Bryn Sallow.

It ended past her. It went out through the shape she was standing in and down through the shape on the flat and away under the flat into a place with no name at all, and the person standing in the middle of it was nobody and had no face and was four hundred loads and two hundred and forty people and a rate on a page that said a fifth of what it should be.

“It is not on her,” Ilyan said.

Nobody spoke.

“It is not on her,” he said again, and his voice had gone strange and thin, and he put his hand out and found the rope with it and held it, and the rope was a rope.

“It is on the person with no name. She is the nearest end of it and she is the only end anybody can see, and I have spent a month learning to look at the near end of things and I have just done it in front of a court. She is not carrying it. She is *standing in it*. And the reason I cannot find the far end is that there is no far end, because the far end is a ward and the ward has no name, and the seam goes down through a thing that is not a person, and I do not know what a rule is resting on when it is resting on nothing with a name.”

“Mr Vester,” Tarin said, from the boundary, in the flat voice he used for a thing a field does and a thing a man does, “take your hand off the rope.”

“I am all right.”

“You are not standing the way a person stands,” said Tarin. “You have been on that leg with your weight on the other one since the fifth bell and you have not put it down once, and I have watched a hundred men stand in a field and I know what it looks like when a man is standing in a field and what it looks like when a man is standing in something else. Take your hand off the rope and step back four feet and sit on the step, and if you argue with me I will put it in the record that you argued with the neutral boundary, which is my record and not the court's.”

Ilyan sat on the step.

His left leg was not there.

That is the whole of it and it is not a figure of speech and he could not have hidden it: from the hip down, the left leg had gone the way a leg goes when a person has been standing for nine hours, except that he had been in this field for twenty minutes and had not been standing still for one of them. It was not numb in the way a numb leg is numb. It was *occupied*. It had somebody else's weight in it, somebody else's forty-four-year-old knee in it, and the weight was the weight of a woman who has not sat down since the fifth bell, and it was not going to move because nothing was going to move it, and the leg was doing its best and the leg was not his.

Wenna Tace crossed the flat in about ninety seconds, which is a long time, and came into the rope, and looked at him, and said, “You have got somebody's legs.”

“I have got somebody's legs,” Ilyan said.

“Mine hurt like that on the fourteenth and I thought it was the barrow.” She looked at Bryn Sallow, who was at the gate, about sixty feet off, standing with one foot on the sill-stone and one on the bank in the position she had been in for thirty years and had not moved out of. “That is hers. You have got her hour and you did not ask her for it, and she has not had to stop for it either, and I want to say something about that and I want to say it to the court and not to you.”

“Say it to the court,” Ilyan said.

“I will,” said Wenna Tace. “Later. When the court is not in the middle of something. I have waited two days to say it and I can wait.”

---

The last hour of the field was spent on the question of what the foot-gate was, and it took an hour because nobody in the field could read an iron hinge, and the answer came from the gate hand, who had to be asked, twice, and who answered it the way she answered everything, in the past tense, as a machine.

“It has always opened that way,” said Bryn Sallow. “Thirty years. I have never once thought about it. It is on the hinge at the bottom so that the water cannot lift it, and it opens out, because if it opened in it would swing against the mud on the bank, and the mud is soft in the fourth week of a tide and a door against soft mud is a door that will not be shut again.

“And the person who uses it is on the bank, on this side, and the person it opens away to is on the flat. And I have watched two hundred people cross that gate in thirty years and every one of them has come off the flat and gone up the bank, and not one of them has ever wanted to go the other way, and I never thought about why, because nobody has ever wanted to go the other way.”

“Until now,” said Ilyan.

“Until a carter with four loads of district sand stands in it and wants to go up the hill and cannot, because to go up the hill he has to come through it and the hinge is at the bottom and the mud is soft, and a man with a loaded barrow cannot turn a foot-gate on a soft bank.” She said it without any weight at all. “It was not a rule. It was a hinge.”

Nobody in the field had anything to say to that for about twenty seconds.

“Enter it,” Harrow said, to Roke, “as the gate hand's account of a machine, in her own words, without improvement, and enter that the court asked her twice, and enter that nobody in this field has accounted for it before now and the court has been in this place for forty minutes.”

And then the court rose, and the field stayed open, because Harrow had one more thing to take and it was not a ruling and it was going to take the whole of the next day, and he said so on the step with his tray under his arm.

“There is a burden in this instrument and it is a burden on a person and I am told by four benches that I must name it and by a county clerk that she cannot pay it, and the court is going to take a burden schedule tomorrow at the sixth bell, and everybody who is going to carry anything in this instrument is going to write their own hours on it, in their own hand, and it will be read out, and I will not have a schedule from a clerk.”

He looked at Ilyan on the step.

“And the attached record-holder will sit down for the whole of it,” Harrow said, “and he will not read anything, and he will not be asked anything, and Mr Quill has told me in a corridor that he is going to try to read it and lose the thread in front of sixty people, and I am telling him now so that it is not a thing that happens to him. He will sit down.”

“I would rather not sit down,” Ilyan said.

“I am not asking what you would rather,” said Harrow, and went up the bank.

---

Sera got him to the top of the hill before the light went and made him sit on the milestone, and he had a token in his hand and he had not put it there.

The blackglass was warm.

It is a spent thing. It is the seal of a passage that has already happened and will not happen again, and it has been in a pocket for five weeks and it has never once been warm, and it was warm now to about the temperature of a hand that has been on a chain.

He put his thumb on it and the low water went out of his nose.

That is what happened. It did not go far away. It stopped. For about forty seconds he could not smell the flat, or the bank, or the culvert, or the two hundred loads, or his own boots, and the world was clean and quiet and about nine notes out of tune, and he understood in the middle of it — not in words, in the body — that the two things were the same kind of thing. That the thing in his nose was not a memory of the low water. It was a *rendering* of the low water, made for a person who had never stood on a bank, delivered into the part of him that had no reason to have been in a room, and that the passage in his pocket had been the same operation, and that it had cost the ninth bridge its emergency reserve and a woman on the lower road her flood account and had carried him here without asking him, and that whatever put the low water in his leg on the bank had put it there the same way it had put him on a bridge.

Then the smell came back, and it came back wrong.

It came back in two layers, the flat on top of the water, which is the order it is in if you stand at a gate, and the water on top of the flat, which is the order it is in if you are lying on the flat at night with a barrow handle under your arm. He had never been on the flat at night. He had been on the flat for twenty minutes in the fifth week of a tide.

He put the token away and pressed both hands flat on the milestone until the stone came up through his fingers, and his left leg was still somebody else's, and it was going to be somebody else's that night, and there was no line in any book in this city on which that could be entered.

“Nobody can have this,” he said.

“You cannot put that in a record,” Sera said, sitting down on the milestone on the other side of the road, “and I would have stopped you, and you were not going to say it as a finding. You were going to say it as a cost. Say the cost.”

“It is in a leg,” Ilyan said. “It is in a leg that is not mine, and it came out of a piece of paper on a gate, and it is going to be there in the morning, and there is nobody I can give it back to because I gave it to a woman who did not hand it over and has not asked for it back, and if I stand on that bank again tomorrow at the fifth bell I do not know what happens, and I do not know that in a way I can put in a sentence, and I have never in my life not been able to put a thing in a sentence.”

“Write the numbers,” Sera said. “You have always written the numbers when the sentence would not come. How many piles.”

Ilyan picked up the slate.

“Four hundred and six,” he said, “on the fourteenth, from the flat, at the second change, counted by me and not by anybody else and I am telling you it is mine. Four hundred and nine yesterday. I have been counting the same pile four times since the fourteenth because it is a long flat and I do not have a second pair of eyes and a person is not a measuring instrument, and the number may be wrong by forty and it is the only number in this case that is mine and I have not given it to anybody, and I am giving it to you now because you write things down and I am frightened of keeping it.”

Sera wrote it down, and gave it back, and did not say anything about the hand, and they walked up the fen road in the dark, and about halfway up it Ilyan said that the smell of his own coat had gone and that he could still smell the flat, and she said “yes,” and neither of them said anything else about it, because there was nothing to say about it yet and because the third of the four things her license says she does is to carry the terms of the ninth-bridge matter to any district that asks, and Halloway had not asked, and she was going to be in a room at the sixth bell in the morning reading out nine sentences in a keeper's words, and there was a burden schedule to be taken, and the schedule was going to be the first page in the history of this city with a woman's own hours on it, and there was no way on earth that anybody was going to pay for the three hours that taking it would take.
