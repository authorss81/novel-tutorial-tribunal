# Chapter 94: One Quarter of a Sentence

The three printings were on the table in the cold room on the ninth of the fifth month at the first bell, and Corrin Vask had carried them down himself from the case on the run, one at a time, under his left arm, because his right hand does not open on command, and that was the first thing she saw, and she did not comment on it, and he noticed that she did not comment on it, and neither of them said anything about that either.

The lamp was on the table. The volume was on the table with the boards lying open at the middle hole of the three, and the facing leaf after the empty slot was where it had been, and the crease in it was where the crease had been, and the crease is the whole of what made the four days necessary.

It is about an inch long and it runs across the leaf from about three inches above the second shadow to about two inches below it, and it is not a fold in the sense that the paper has been folded and stayed folded. The paper was folded and opened and it has been opened so long that it lies flat again, and the fold is left as a line of slightly different grain and a place where the surface takes the light at a different angle, and along that inch the ink of the shadow underneath it is gone, because ink that has been pressed hard into paper comes off in one piece when the paper is worked and the rest of it stays.

So there is a gap. It is an inch long and it is in the middle of the second line and it is not a shadow and it is not a line and it is not anything, and it is going to stay like that for ever, and the eleven words on the printed form do not go into it.

She had the form on the table. She had had it for a day. She had read it eleven times, which is four more than anybody should read anything, and the reason she had read it eleven times is that it is a true line and it is the truest line on the sheet and it is eleven words long and it is sitting in her head saying *duty of the ward* in a print shop's voice about forty times an hour since the eighth.

And the craft of it, the whole of the craft, is this. A person who has eleven correct words in front of her and four inches of faint grey on a leaf is not reading the leaf. She is checking the leaf. Those are two different acts and they produce two different records, and one of them can be sworn to in a room in twenty years and the other one cannot, and the difference between them is whether the hand that did the reading knew what it was looking for before the lamp was lit.

She had done it once already on the twentieth of the second month, in this room, standing up, with one finger, and what she had written down that day was *there is a shadow and not a line, and the shadow is the rest of it, and I am not going to guess at a shadow in a public archive and put it in a record.* And the eleven words on the form are the answer to two of the characters in the crease, and she cannot un-know it, and the only honest way to deal with having been handed the answer is to do the work first and the comparison second, and to write down which of the two she did first with a date on it, so that a clerk in twenty years can see the order of it.

So on the ninth, at the first bell, she put the second printing face down on the table under the weight of the volume, and she said to Vask, who was standing at the door because there is one chair in the cold room:

“Tell me what you would do if you were paid ninepence a day and somebody had left you a form that said most of the thing you are looking for, and you were going to be paid nothing to do the rest.”

“I would want to know what the form said before I started,” said Vask, “and I would want it on the table, and I would want to be able to look at it.”

“Yes,” she said. “And that is exactly the wrong thing to want and you cannot help it, and I have just done the same thing, and I have put the form face down and under a book, and I am going to read the leaf for three days without it, and then on the fourth day I am going to turn it over, and I am going to write down both things and the order they were done in, and you are going to be in the room on the fourth day and you are not going to say anything about it.”

“Why not.”

“Because on the fourth day, if I read you a line and you say *that is right*, then the line has a witness, and I have spent eleven weeks in a case arguing that a witness is a person and not a copy, and a man who cannot read is a person and a form is a form, and if I use your face I have used a copy with a face on it.”

“All right,” said Vask, and went out, and shut the door, which he had not shut before, and shut it because she had asked him to and had told him why, which is a thing that has happened to him about four times in nine years.

---

Three days. The lamp, the leaf, the flat of a hand.

The cold in that room is not a temperature, it is a damp that comes up through the stone floor and settles on the boards of the volume, and by the fourth hour her right arm has stopped being an arm that can hold anything and has become a thing that is warm, and on the second day she changed hands and the leaf has to be held from the left on the second day because the shadow of the second line runs the other way, and by the third day the hand holding it flat is his and hers is the one with the lamp and her lamp arm goes at about the seventh hour on every one of the three days and by the fourth it goes at the fourth.

You cannot hurry it and there is nothing to hold it against. The shadow is not under the surface; it is in the fibres of the leaf and it is about the colour of a thumbprint on a window in August and it is four inches long and it is a line, and a line that is four hundred years old and has been behind a leaf the whole time.

On the first day she got, in order, at eleven in the morning with the lamp almost on the paper: an *o*, and then a gap of about a fifth of an inch which is not enough for a word, and then *the entry*, and then *is a*, and then the crease.

On the second day, the *the entry* got better and *is* got a little better and she was still at the crease by the seventh hour.

On the third day, after the lamp had been moved four inches to the left and the leaf had been lying at that angle for an hour, the crease gave her the end of one character and the start of another, and neither of them was enough to name a word, and she wrote them both down as what they were, which is shapes, and she entered a note that says that a shape is not a character and that she has entered the shapes because she saw them and because a person in twenty years with better eyes or better light may want to know that there was something there at all, and that she is entering the shapes as shapes.

On the fourth day, at about the second hour in the afternoon, she read past the crease.

That is what a crease does. It takes an inch and it gives you the rest.

*of the ward, and*

and after that the shadow ends at about four inches and there is nothing at all to the end of the line, and the end of the line is at about eleven inches, and seven inches of it has been gone for four hundred years or would have been if the leaf had ever been a page and it never was, because the page is the page next door to the one that is missing and the page next door to the one that is missing is a leaf with the second line of the missing page's shadow on it and nothing else.

She wrote it out, and then she read it out loud, twice, to a man who cannot read.

> **OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND**

Ten words, in that order, with a gap of an inch at the beginning of the line and nothing at the end of it, on the shadow of the second line of a page that is not on its shelf and has not been on its shelf since before a man who is now thirty-one came into this world.

Then she turned the form over.

---

The form said: *and the keeping of it is a duty of the ward.*

She put the two side by side on the table, and they are eleven words against ten, and the difference is in the middle, and the difference is where the form's copy-holder had to put a name, because the line on the leaf begins with a subject the form could not read to the end and a form has to name its subject.

The leaf does not say *the keeping of it*. The leaf says *of the entry is a duty of the ward, and* and the subject is in the crease or before the crease or in the seven inches that are gone, and the person who made the form put in four words of their own to stand in front of a line they could not read to the end, and those four words are *the keeping of it*, and they are almost certainly right, and there is no way in this city to know that, and she did not use them.

The reading of the fourth day, entered under her licence, dated and signed, in the terms of a recordwright of a public record and not of anybody's advocate:

> **ENTERED UNDER LICENCE, THE TWELFTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH, IN A COLD ROOM IN A BUILDING WITH NO NAME ON THE DOOR, FOUR MILES FROM THE COURT, BY S. QUILL, RECORDWRIGHT OF A PUBLIC RECORD, UPON THE FACING LEAF BESIDE THE EMPTY SLOT AND NOT UPON THE REMOVED PAGE, WHICH IS NOT ON THE SHELF AND HAS NOT BEEN ON IT SINCE BEFORE I WAS BORN.**
>
> **THE FACEING LEAF ALREADY CARRIES THE SHADOW OF THE FIRST LINE OF THE REMOVED PAGE, WHICH I ENTERED ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SECOND MONTH, WHICH BEGINS WITH A LOWER-CASE A, AND I DO NOT RE-ENTER IT HERE AND IT STANDS IN THE RECORD WHERE IT STANDS.**
>
> **FOUR LINES BELOW IT, FAINTER, THERE IS THE SHADOW OF THE SECOND LINE OF THE SAME REMOVED PAGE. I HAVE BEEN AT IT FOUR DAYS WITH A LIGHT HELD LOW, AND THE LEAF HAS BEEN HELD FLAT BY A HAND THAT IS SIX YEARS GONE AT THE WRIST, AND THE WORK AND THE PRICE OF IT ARE IN A DAY BOOK AT THE BACK OF THIS BUILDING IN THE SAME COLUMN AS THE SWEEPING OF ITS FLOORS.**
>
> **ABOUT FOUR INCHES OF THE LINE CARRY A SHADOW. ABOUT ONE INCH IN THE MIDDLE OF IT DOES NOT, AND THE INCH IS A CREASE IN THE LEAF, AND THE LEAF HAS BEEN FOLDED AND OPENED AND LAID FLAT AGAIN, AND I CANNOT TELL YOU WHEN IT WAS FOLDED AND I AM NOT GOING TO.**
>
> **WHAT I CAN READ IS THIS. I HAVE MARKED THE GAPS AND I HAVE NOT SUPPLIED THEM:**
>
> **[  ] OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND [  ]**
>
> **I ENTER THIS AS ONE QUARTER OF A SENTENCE. I AM NOT ENTERING IT AS A CLAUSE. IT IS NOT THE CLAUSE. IT DOES NOT RESTORE ANY RIGHT OF ANYBODY TO ANSWER NO, AND THE FIRST LINE DID NOT RESTORE IT, AND THIS DOES NOT RESTORE IT, AND IT WAS NOT RESTORED BY ANYTHING IN FOUR MONTHS AND IT IS NOT RESTORED TONIGHT.**
>
> **WHAT THE CREASE TAKES, I DID NOT SUPPLY. I HAD A PRINTED FORM IN MY HAND WHICH SAID *AND THE KEEPING OF IT IS A DUTY OF THE WARD*, AND IT GIVES ME TWO OF THE LOST CHARACTERS AND PROBABLY THREE, AND I DID NOT USE IT, AND HERE IS WHY, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF MY CRAFT AND THE WHOLE OF MY LICENCE IN ONE LINE: A FORM IS A COPY MADE BY A PERSON WHO HAD TO LEAVE A VOLUME AND PUT DOWN WHAT THEY COULD CARRY, AND A COPY IS NOT A WITNESS, AND A WITNESS IS A PERSON, AND A PERSON MAY BE EXAMINED, AND A FORM CANNOT BE EXAMINED BY ANYBODY EVER.**
>
> **I READ THE LEAF FOR THREE DAYS WITH THE FORM FACE DOWN UNDER THE WEIGHT OF THE VOLUME AND I TURNED IT OVER ON THE FOURTH, AND THE ORDER IS ENTERED ABOVE THE READING AND NOT BELOW IT, BECAUSE A CLERK IN TWENTY YEARS WHO FINDS THE COMPARISON FIRST WILL THINK THE FORM CAME FIRST, AND IT DID NOT.**

Nobody improved it. She read it out in a cold room to a man who cannot read, who asked her to read it and who then said, “That is about a duty,” which is the only thing he said about it, and he was right, and he said it in the voice of a man who has swept nine years and has heard the word on a bench and in a guild book and in a hundred and sixty years of tolls and has never once heard it in a room where somebody was owed something.

---

And then she asked him his question, because she had told him on the sixth of the month that she would, and she had said it at the counter in four minutes and had not known then whether she meant it.

She did it in the ordinary way, at the end of a thing, with the lamp still on the table, and she told him first that he could say no, and he said no before she had finished the sentence, and then said that he had said no to a thing he had been waiting nine years to be asked, and that he would now say yes to the other side of it, which is that he would rather be asked and say nothing than not be asked at all.

“Say no to what,” she said.

“To a question about the fourth shelf,” said Vask. “I have been ready since the sixth. You have been in this building four days and you have asked me about the third shelf counting back from the window and you have not asked me about the fourth one, and I have said nothing about it for four days, and four days is ninepence times four and I would like it noticed that I have said nothing.”

“All right,” said Sera Quill. “What is on the fourth shelf counting back from the window.”

He did not have to go and look. That was the thing she was not ready for.

“A box,” he said. “It came in on a cart in the winter before last with a bench's seal on the lid and a docket on the lid, and nobody ever came for it, and it is still on the fourth shelf, and it has been dusted every week for about two years by a man who is not a clerk and does not read and does not open boxes.”

“What is in it.”

“Forms,” said Vask. “About nine hundred of them, near enough. I have looked at the top of them while I have dusted and they are all the same print and the same shop's typeface, and the one on the top has a word at the top of it in a bigger letter than any other word on the page, and I have read that word about four hundred times because I dust it every week and I cannot help reading what is in front of me, and it is a word I have never had anywhere, and I do not know what it means.”

He stopped there, and he stopped on purpose, and she watched him stop.

“You have not told me what the word is.”

“No.”

“Why not.”

“Because you have not asked me that one,” said Vask, “and because I have wanted one question asked of me for nine years and I have had about four, and I am not going to spend a fifth on a word when I cannot tell you what it means, and a man who sweeps a building is not a man who opens boxes and is not a man who guesses at words. If you want to know the word you will open the box, and when you do I will stand here and tell you the price of the standing here, which is nothing, because I am here anyway.”

She wrote it in the day book at the counter that evening, on the line for the twelfth, and she wrote the price beside it in the same column, and the price was a shilling for the opening of a volume and nothing at all for the standing about, and she wrote that down too, because a man had told her that those are two different prices and it had taken him nine years to be able to say so.


---

Here is what it does and it is not a recovery of anything.

A ward is a duty, and that is four hundred years older than this case and it is not new, and it is in a standing of wards a hundred and ninety years old and in the front leaf of a traveller's book, and it does not say where anybody lives. It says a thing a district owes and has not got round to.

Read as a place, the second line of a removed page says that the ward is a building, and the entry is kept in it, and somebody in it is the keeper, and the keeper is a person, and any person in a district who needs an entry sworn has to find that person and go and stand in front of that person. Read as a duty, the same ten words say that the keeping of the entry is a thing the ward owes, and a thing a ward owes is not done by a ward, and is done by whoever is doing the ward's work at the time the work is done.

There is a district four miles from this building of about two hundred and forty people. It has a bench of four, a clerk, a keeper's four words on a frame, a rate of half a farthing a bucket, a book of a hundred and sixty years that calls a culvert road a penal road, a page in a room over a pump, and no ward. It has never had a ward. It has a book in a room and a woman in the room and a boy of nineteen who reads the book out twice a week for nothing because a page with one pair of eyes on it is a page that cannot be checked.

The keeping of that entry is a duty. The district owes it. Nobody in the district has written down that the district owes it, in any book, at any time, and the district has not been asked, and it cannot afford to be asked, and there is no form for it. And a duty with nobody in it is a duty nobody does, and the work goes to whoever is standing nearest, and the person standing nearest in four miles is a woman of twenty-nine with a barrow who hauls about two hundred loads a year at a third of a haul rate and about a pound and eleven a quarter for keeping nine sheets and a page.

So: that is what a page is for. Four hundred years after the page was written, a quarter of a sentence came off the leaf next door to it and it says that the keeping of the entry is a duty of the ward, and the ward is not a place, and the person doing it is not named in it, and there is no word in the nine for the woman, because a duty that named the person would be a wage, and a wage in this city is a number on a line in chalk, and the number on the line in chalk in that district is the gate hand's, and the gate hand is two hundred feet away at a gate and has never been told what the page is for either.

Nobody in this case is going to tell her. She reads a charter in order and a charter ends at the fifth term.

---

And the comparison, which had to be made, and which is the fourth thing she has put on a page in two months and the first one she is frightened of.

On the twenty-fourth of the second month, in a room over a pump, she put two sentences side by side in a record and said they were not the same sentence, and wrote that if they were the same then somebody with more evidence than she had would find out, and if they were not then she had written down that they are not, and she left it to a reader in twenty years to decide.

They are still not the same sentence. The first line of a removed page says a refusal is entered, and the entry is against the burden and not against the person, and the entry is open to the ward. The second line says the keeping of the entry is a duty of the ward, and. A page in a room over a pump in a district of about two hundred and forty people is neither of them.

And she wrote that down again on the twelfth, and she wrote what she thought about it, and what she thought about it is this and it is in her own book and not in a record, because it is a thing a person thinks and not a thing a court can use.

> **THE COMPARISON OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE SECOND MONTH STANDS UNAMENDED. I AM NOT BETTERING IT. I HAVE ONE QUARTER OF A SENTENCE MORE THAN I HAD IN FEBRUARY AND ONE QUARTER OF A SENTENCE DOES NOT MAKE A BETTER COMPARISON, IT MAKES A LONGER ONE, AND THE EARLIER ONE WAS MADE BY A PERSON WITH LESS EVIDENCE IN NINE MINUTES AT A QUARTER, AND IT IS SHORTER, AND A READER IN TWENTY YEARS MAY PREFER IT, AND SHE WOULD BE RIGHT TO.**
>
> **WHAT I HAVE GOT THIS MONTH IS NOT A BETTER COMPARISON. IT IS A REASON, AND THE REASON IS THIS, AND IT IS BORING, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING I HAVE FOUND IN FOUR MONTHS: THE FIRST TWO LINES OF THAT PAGE ARE ABOUT A DUTY, AND THE PAGE OVER A PUMP IS ABOUT A PERSON, AND A DUTY AND A PERSON ARE NOT THE SAME THING AND THE ONLY ONE OF THE THREE THAT ANYBODY IN THIS CITY CAN ACT ON IS THE ONE WITH THE PERSON IN IT, AND IT IS FOUR MONTHS OLD, AND IT IS HALF A FARTHING A BUCKET, AND IT IS READ ALOUD BY SOMEBODY WHO CANNOT APPEAL FROM ONE WORD OF IT.**
>
> **SO THE QUARTER OF A SENTENCE HAS NOT ANSWERED HER QUESTION AND IT HAS NOT BEEN SUPPOSED TO. IT HAS MADE HER QUESTION WORSE. BECAUSE IT SAYS THAT THE KEEPING OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND THERE IS NO WARD IN THAT DISTRICT, AND THE DISTRICT HAS NEVER WRITTEN DOWN A DUTY, AND A DUTY WITH NOBODY IN IT IS A DUTY NOBODY DOES, AND SHE HAS BEEN DOING IT FOR FOUR MONTHS, AND SHE WILL GO ON DOING IT, AND THE OLDEST THING I HAVE EVER READ DOES NOT KNOW HER NAME.**

---

And the cost, which was named on the fifth and entered on the twelfth, and which is not a document and does not cost a shilling.

Four days. Four shillings, paid. Four days of sweeping, not done, not done by anybody else, entered in a day book in a building with no name on the door in the same column as four days of a lamp held low. A right hand that took nine seconds longer than it did in the spring to open on the sixth of this month took rather longer than that by the twelfth. A floor in a counting-house has dust on it, and dust gets into a ledger, and a ledger is a figure about a year, and a man four miles down this road has held a seal for two years and cannot make one add up. He is not a party to this and he is not to be told.

And the other cost, which is the one that cannot be paid back in paper, and which she entered in her own book on the twelfth with no date on it because it is not finished:

She has put her name and a date under a record of a page. She has done it under a licence with four terms and the fourth of them is the one about matters, and this is not a matter, and she wrote that down first, on purpose, on the ninth, before she read anything: this is a record, a record is not a matter, and I am entering it as a record.

And the truth is that a record of a page is a thing a person can be sent for. She said that sentence out loud in a room over a pump in the third month of this year about a different person, and about a different page, and she said it as the reason a woman with a shutter could not have one, and she was right, and she has now done the same thing to herself, four miles from a city, in a building with no name on the door, about a duty of a ward that has not existed in this case for four hundred years.

She has made herself a door. In a room nobody in four miles can find, in a building whose door is not locked, in a district that has not asked for anything, holding a fragment of a line that begins with a lower-case a and has a crease in it.

There is exactly one way this can go wrong and she wrote it down on the twelfth night at about the eleventh bell with her right arm not closing properly:

> **IF SOMEBODY IN FOUR MILES FINDS OUT THAT A RECORD OF A PAGE EXISTS IN A BUILDING WITH NO NAME ON THE DOOR, THEY WILL SEND SOMEBODY FOR IT, AND THE SOMEBODY WILL BE A MAN, AND THE MAN WILL BE THE SAME MAN WHO CARRIES NINE SHEETS FOR A DISTRICT THAT CANNOT PAY FOR THEM, AND HE IS NOT WITHIN FOUR MILES OF A BUILDING WITH NO NAME ON THE DOOR, AND HE IS NOT A PERSON IN THIS MATTER, AND IF HE CAME HE WOULD COME BECAUSE A CLAUSE WITH HIS NAME IN IT SAYS HE COMES, AND THAT IS A THIRD SENTENCE AND I WILL NOT LET A LEDGER READ IT AS THE OTHER TWO.**
>
> **AND THE PART I AM NOT WRITING DOWN TONIGHT BECAUSE IT IS THE PART THAT IS TRUE: I DID IT ANYWAY. I KNEW IT ON THE NINTH. I HAVE BEEN A DOOR ONCE IN THIS CASE AND I TOLD A MAN HE COULD NOT BE ONE AND I WAS RIGHT, AND I HAVE DONE IT AGAIN, FOUR MONTHS LATER, FOR NINE WORDS WITH A CREASE IN THEM, AND THE ONLY REASON I CAN GIVE IS THAT NOBODY WAS GOING TO ASK, AND THAT IS THE SAME REASON A DESK GAVE ME, AND I AM NOT GOING TO WRITE IT DOWN AS A REASON IN THE PLACE WHERE A REASON GOES.**
