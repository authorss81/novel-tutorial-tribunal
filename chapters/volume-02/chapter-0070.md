# Chapter 70: The Fifth Thing He Entered

The eleven-day answers came in on the eighteenth, the nineteenth, and the twenty-first, and they are the most useful thing this court has produced in a month, and nobody has said so, and the reason nobody has said so is that they are all the same and the same is *no*.

Nine of the eleven benches answered in public on the day. Seven answered in writing that they hold no page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected by a received record may swear to, in the bench's own room, at the bench's own counter, without leave. Two answered that they hold one and named it, and both of the two were a hundred and sixty years old and neither of them had been sworn by anybody living, and one of those two is the district whose road book is a hundred and sixty years old and calls a culvert road a penal road, and that bench answered the question by naming a book that nobody can swear to and then said so on the second line, which was more honest than the seven.

And two did not answer on the day.

One answered on the twenty-first, and its answer is a page in a record in Orison with a number in the corner and a signature under it, and the signature is not a person's name: it is *standing authority of preservation*, and the answer says that the office holds every instrument printed in this city for the last four years in a series that runs by plate, that any person may read any of it at a counter on a day, that no person has ever asked, and that the answer is *yes* and it has been *yes* for four years.

Nobody in that court had ever asked. That is the whole of the finding and it is nine words long: nobody in that court had ever asked.

And one did not answer at all.

Ansa Rell entered it in eleven days, in the well of the court, in the open air, in eleven words: *that a bench which has received an instrument and has not answered the question is not in breach of anything, and the court has no power to make it answer, and the court says so, and the court's own duty ends here.*

---

The charter of the district of Halloway is five days old on the twenty-second of the month, and it is a real instrument, and it is working, and nothing about it is secure.

It has a pay line and a name in chalk on a board at a gate and a rate of half a farthing on every bucket at a standpipe and about two hundred people a day going past both of them to get water. It has a page, kept in a room over a pump by a woman of twenty-nine who hauls about two hundred loads a year and is paid by her district at a third of a haul rate, and the page has two entries in it and the second one is a refusal of the office of gate hand sworn by a woman of forty-four who cannot read it.

It has one appeal heard and partly allowed and one refused on a rule that is in the charter, and a third sitting of the bench is fixed for the twenty-eighth, and nobody knows what is on that agenda and the convenor of the bench has told the court in writing that she does not know either.

It has a carrier, whose name is in the fourth term in a chamber hand, and who has walked four miles twice in six days with somebody else's hour in his leg, and who is a party to nothing in the district and a person in its record, and whose carrying has been done twice for nothing and will be done again.

And it has the thing that was predicted for it, out loud, in a hearing, a month ago, by a recordwright at the ruling, two months ago, who said that in the room it would be called an amendment and in a few days it would be called something else, and that nothing in the ten terms would stop a form being printed with that word at the top of it.

The form came on the twenty-second of the month.

It was not ordered by anybody. It was printed in Orison, in a district's typeface, in a print shop that fills a space in a docket, and it has a seal on it and a number, and it is a summary of the charter of the district of Halloway with an appeal in it, and at the top of it, in a bigger letter than any other word on the page, is EXCEPTION, and the word means a temporary permission and has a limit and an expiry and a burden, and none of the three is in the charter it is summarising.

Nobody can make anybody stop printing it. The court has no power over a form. A form is a habit with a seal on it, which is what Maren Kett said on a step in the sixteenth of the month and was right about, and the word is in every clerk's drawer in nine districts within eleven days, and it means nothing, and it is the name of the thing this city has been doing to itself for a month, and it is going to be in the district's own papers in a year.

Corva Silt put one on the gate board under the rate and under the sheet with the seal on it, because she has put everything anybody has ever given her on that board for a month, and she wrote a line on the back of it in the district's own hand that says *this word is not in the charter. It is at the top of the form. Read the charter.*

Wenna Tace read that form aloud at the pump on the twenty-second at the sixth bell to about two hundred people, and she said the word at the top of it out loud, and it is the word, and in this city it means a temporary permission to cross or act against a rule that is standing, with a limit on it and an expiry on it and a burden on it, and not one of those three things is in the charter she read out on a bank in the mud, and then she read the line on the back of it, and a woman of about sixty said *so what, that is what it is called*, and the reader of the district said:

“No. That is what somebody in Orison has started calling it. Those are different things and in about a year every form in this city will have that word on it and everybody will think the word came from somewhere. It came from a hearing. It is a word a man in a room said and a woman in a room wrote down, and it is on the top of that page because somebody's print shop filled a space.”

---

Bram Orren came down the fen road on the twenty-second with his line book and a thing he had done on the eighteenth and wanted to be told about after he had done it, which is that he had written to Wen Lask at Carrisand.

He is seventeen and he runs a line and a line goes where a line goes, and there is no line to Carrisand and there is a road, and a boy who runs a line for a living will walk eleven miles in a day and have told four people about it on the way, and he had not told four people, and he had sat down about a mile short of the gate and worked out that he was frightened, and had gone on, and had come back, and had written to her first.

He said so in about eleven sentences on a barrow on the bank with his boots not touching the ground.

“I did not tell Mr Vester and I did not tell the court and I did not tell Mr Sarn in the cut banks, and I am telling you now because I did it and I am not going to do that, and I have sent the terms of your charter to a woman of seventy-one at a gate on a good road, because the fourth term says the terms are carried to any district that asks, and there is no district there, and a district is not going to ask, and so I asked her. Not her. A boy of seventeen.

“And it cost me the only thing I had, which is the thing I told you about in a lane a month ago, and it cost it in an afternoon instead of a year, and I want it minuted that it cost it in an afternoon.

“If a person in a city has written to a woman at a gate about a rule, then in about a year that gate is a gate that a man from a city has written to about a rule, and that is the only reason anybody in that family would ever have heard of us, and it is exactly the reason you told me not to do it, and I have done it, and I would like somebody to be annoyed with me about it and I would like it to be a magistrate and not a friend.”

Ansa Rell was on the bank, and she was not annoyed, and said so, and then said the other thing, which is that she was going to be careful.

“Enter that,” she said, “and enter that this court has no power over a road in a region with no district, and takes no view on it, and has said so on the face of a field about a gate and will say so again, and that a boy of seventeen has asked a question on a road that is not this court's, and that nothing this court does in the next five weeks is going to be about that road.

“And I am going to be careful about one thing and I want it in the record. Eleven benches have answered a question in public in eleven days and seven of them said they hold nothing. That is the emptiest public statement this city has made in nineteen years, and it was made because a boy of seventeen put a line book in nobody's file, and I do not want anybody in this room to think that what I have done at a weighhouse desk has cost nothing. I have not cost anything. I have taken an answer that was owed. That is the whole of my month.”

---

Bram's letter had gone up the road on the eighteenth and came back on the twenty-ninth, and it is a letter of one side, and it is in a hand of a person of seventy-one who has been collecting a toll for eight years and whose father did it before her, and it is in the line book, and he read it out on the bank.

> **TO THE BOY WHO ASKED. I HAVE YOUR PAPER. I HAVE READ IT TWICE AND I CANNOT READ HALF THE WORDS AND I HAVE HAD A MAN FROM THE WEIGHHOUSE READ THE OTHER HALF TO ME AND HE DID NOT WANT TO AND HE DID IT ANYWAY.**
>
> **WE HAVE HAD THE TOLL EIGHT YEARS AND MY FATHER HAD IT BEFORE ME. THE ROAD IS GOOD. THE TOLL HAS NEVER BEEN SHORT. THE LOADS GO THROUGH. I HAVE FOUR HANDS AND THEY ARE PAID ON THE DAY AND I WRITE THEIR NAMES IN THE BOOK MYSELF AND NONE OF THEM HAVE EVER ASKED FOR ANYTHING.**
>
> **I AM NOT GOING TO TAKE YOUR PAPER INTO THE HOUSE. MY GRANDFATHER COPIED YOUR NINTH BRIDGE ON THE FOURTH OF A MONTH EIGHT YEARS AGO BECAUSE A MAN CAME DOWN THE ROAD AND SAID THIS IS WHAT THE CITIES ARE DOING AND THE TOLLS ARE IN IT. HE COULD NOT READ IT EITHER. HE WROTE THE NAMES IN AFTERWARDS BECAUSE THE FORM HAS A LINE FOR IT. HE WROTE HIS OWN NAME AND THEN MY FATHER'S BESIDE IT AND HE DID IT SIX YEARS, AND WHEN HE DIED MY FATHER TOOK THE TOLL AND WROTE HIS OWN NAME IN AGAIN UNDER HIS, AND THE LAST NAME IN THAT LINE IS MY FATHER'S. IT HAS BEEN IN THAT LINE EIGHT YEARS. I HAVE WRITTEN OVER IT TWICE AND I DO NOT KNOW WHY I KEEP DOING IT.**
>
> **ONE QUESTION AND NO MORE. YOU HAVE WRITTEN DOWN A RULE FOR A PLACE WITH PEOPLE IN IT AND YOU HAVE ASKED ME WHETHER I HAVE ANYTHING A PERSON MAY SWEAR TO, AND YOU HAVE NOT ASKED ME ANYTHING THAT IS ABOUT ME. SO HERE IS THE QUESTION AND I AM NOT BEING CLEVER, I AM SEVENTY-ONE AND I AM TIRED.**
>
> **DOES YOUR PAPER SAY ANYTHING ABOUT A ROAD WITH NOBODY IN IT.**

Nobody in that bank could answer it, and it is in the record, and it was not answered, and it was not going to be answered in the next five weeks, and nobody chased it.

---

And on the twenty-second of the month a line came out of the bottom of a page, and it came out under the fifth term, and it was the sixth time.

Sera Quill found it at the second bell, in a room over a pump four miles down the fen road, in the copy that is kept in the page, because she had gone down the road to check the copy against the fair one on the twentieth and had taken the copy with her, and it was not in the fair one.

It was in a hand that nobody in the room recognised and it was in the same cadence as every other time it has ever come, and the word at the end of it had changed again.

> **CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD AGAINST THE HOLDER.**

Harrow was four miles up the road at the ninth bridge at the time and came down on the following morning with his tray under his arm and read it standing at a pump in a smell with nine people looking over his shoulder, and he read it twice, and then he said the thing he has said every time, in the same words, and this was the sixth time and it was the same words:

“I will not rule on a line I did not enter.”

He did not ask who wrote it. He did not ask when it was put there. He did not ask whether it was there when the page was sworn or whether it came afterwards, and the reason he did not ask the third question is that the answer would have been the whole of it and the court has no power over the question, and he said that too, and entered it, and then he did a thing that has happened four times now and that nobody has ever reported as a procedure.

He entered the line into the record as an unentered line, in the words *continuity preserves the record against the holder*, with a date, and did not rule on it, and did not find it, and did not use it, and did not let it be used, and said that he would not rule on it, and that on the seventh time he would not rule on it either, and that he had been a trialwright for eleven years and had come to the conclusion that a line he did not enter is a line he may not rule on and that this is not modesty and it is not neutrality and it is the only procedure he has.

And nobody named anything. That is entered, and it is the finding of the sixth appearance, and the sixth appearance is in a document that a woman with a barrow keeps in a room over a pump and is not allowed to appeal from, and the word in it is the word for the person, and the person does not know.

He paid for it in the body, and it was the only cost of the whole morning, and it lasted about ninety seconds and it was not hidden.

He was on the floor of a room over a pump with his hands on a board floor and a leg that was not his from the hip and the low water in his nose in four layers, the day over the night, the flat over the water, the chain under those, and a door under that, and he stayed down for about a minute and a half and then got himself up against a wall and nobody helped him because nobody in that room was asked and Wenna Tace came in from the lane with a barrow at the fourth bell and found him sitting on a floor with a copy of a page in his hand and looked at him and said, “You have got somebody's leg,” which is what she said to him on a bank five weeks ago, and then she said, “It is not mine. I have got a barrow's worth in my arms and it is not the same and I know the difference now.”

“It is not yours,” he said. “It is worse. It is a page.”

“A page,” she said, and put the barrow down, and did not ask, and got him a chair.

---

The fifth thing was entered on the twenty-fourth of the month in a stone room off the second gallery with a stove that does not draw, and Sera Quill entered it herself because it is her record and nobody else's, and it is in the case's record and it cannot be taken back, and there are five of them.

Two copies of a private sequence from a game called *The Sovereign's Wake*.

The precedent he asked for in a corridor at two in the morning a month into the case, which four districts are now printing and which a woman of thirty-one filled a form in with because it was lawful and free.

The first appeal in the history of this city, which worked perfectly.

The carrier's clause, which put his name into nine sheets of paper in a district four miles from a bridge with a duty on it and a consequence, and which no court can strike and no person asked for and no person was consulted about, and which she wrote on purpose and told him about before she wrote it.

And he named the sizes himself, in his own hand, into a record he does not control, because he said in the sixteenth that in four years a clerk is going to read five entries and assume they are the same size, and they are not.

> **THE CLAIMANT STATES HE HAS ENTERED FIVE THINGS INTO THIS CITY'S RECORD WHICH HE CANNOT WITHDRAW, AND STATES THE SIZE OF EACH SO THAT A CLERK DOES NOT ASSUME THEY ARE THE SAME.**
> **TWO ARE HIS OWN PRIVATE SEQUENCE AND HE CANNOT RECITE THEM AND HAS NOT TRIED SINCE THE FIRST WEEK.**
> **THE THIRD IS THE PRECEDENT AND IT IS ON A GATE BOARD FOUR MILES AWAY AND WILL BE ON A FORM IN NINE DISTRICTS INSIDE A YEAR.**
> **THE FOURTH IS THE FIRST APPEAL AND IT WORKED PERFECTLY AND IT REACHED NOBODY WHO NEEDED IT.**
> **THE FIFTH IS THE CARRIER'S CLAUSE AND IT IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE FIVE THAT SOMEBODY CAN CALL OUT AND ASK TO SEE HIM, AND IT COST A DISTRICT A DAY AND A HALF OF A CART AND COST HIM EVERY AFTERNOON HE WILL EVER SPEND ON THAT ROAD, AND HE ACCEPTED IT IN NINE WORDS AND WOULD ACCEPT IT AGAIN AND THAT IS THE PART THAT SHOULD FRIGHTEN A CLERK IN FOUR YEARS.**

She read that back and did not tidy it and did not put her own name against it, and then she said, in a stone room, to a man who was sitting down because he could not stand up:

“There are two things in this city that belong to me and are not in that list, and I am going to say them out loud once and then never again, because my licence has four terms and the fourth one says I take no matter of my own while I hold this one, and it has been held since the ruling, which is eight weeks and four days, and I have counted, because counting is what I do.

“A quarter of a sentence on the facing leaf of a page that is not on its shelf. It says that a refusal is entered, and the entry is against the burden and not against the person, and the entry is open to the ward. And I have read it eleven times since the ruling and I have read it eleven different ways, and a fortnight ago in a lane I said that *open to the ward* does not say the ward's clerk holds it, and I was right, and I was also wrong about why, and here is the wrong, and it is the finding of the twenty-ninth and it is not mine: a ward is a duty and not a place. I have been reading a place into a sentence for a fortnight because a place was what I had. A duty is what it is, and I have had a month with that.

“There is now a district where a page is open to a person. It is a room over a pump and the person is a hauler and she cannot appeal. That is not the sentence. I am not going to say it is the sentence. I am going to write it in my own record on the same page as the sentence and let the two of them sit there and let a reader in twenty years decide, and if a quarter of a sentence and a page over a pump are the same thing then somebody with more evidence than I have will find out, and if they are not then I have written down that they are not.

“And a family claim of my own, six years old, unfiled, which needs a page that may not exist, which I have not looked for in a month because I am holding a charter in a district with no ward in it. That is the fourth term and it costs me a shop and two rooms and a business and I said it out loud in a court at the ruling and nobody has asked me since, and I am not going to ask anybody, and I would like the record to say that a licensed recordwright of a public record has said so four times and has not been asked once.”

---

The last thing on the twenty-fourth was the review.

It is the fourth morning bell of the third month after the ruling, at the span, on public notice, callable early by any party on a named reading outside the band a keeper has kept, and not postponable, and it is in five weeks.

Ilyan must be produced at it with his report and the two copies of his own sequence, to say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and may not send somebody with his name on it, and the ruling that made his standing is confined to the ninth-bridge matter, and in five weeks a bench that has never heard of the fourth bridge is going to look at an account of a hundred and four pounds, and a magistrate is going to have to answer for four benches in nineteen years and eleven instruments that were valid and none of them that was affordable, and a charter with a person in its fifth term that is five days old and has two entries in it and no standing outside one poor district, and an appeal that ran perfectly and produced a duty to answer and a public statement by nine benches that this city holds nothing.

None of that is secured. All of it is in use. The difference between those two things is the whole of the next four weeks and nobody in this case can decide it and one of them is going to try.

The span took a load on the thirty-fourth day of the unloaded period and has taken one every change since, and on the twenty-fourth of the month the count came back at mark on a light load, and on a heavy one it walked out two and came back all the way, and the pale permanent set sat a finger's width from the iron ring where it has sat for six years, and the bell was rung for the change by a keeper who has looked at the pin on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope, and four miles down the fen road a rate of half a farthing was read out at a pump by a woman who cannot appeal, and on a road off the fen road a slip of paper is nailed to a frame on a bridge that has four square holes in it and a keeper's four words and a line under them in a clerk's hand that says *fourth bridge, four words, asked and answered*, and a cart of four old props went back to a yard at a cost of four pounds and there are no new ones this year.

The leg was not his when he came off the deck at the fourth bell, and it was his own for about an hour on a bank the next morning while he wrote a gate hand's tide in a load-carrier's figures with a line down the middle of a slate, and it was not his own at the seventh bell, and there is no line anywhere in this city on which that can be entered, and he has told four people and one of them wrote it down, and a fifth worked it out and said it first, and the sixth is a woman at a gate who has not been told and would not thank him.

And in a line book in a boy's hand, on a page about a road with nobody in it, there was a question, dated the twenty-ninth, from a woman of seventy-one at a gate on a good road where the toll has never been short and the loads go through and the names go in the book and get written over.

*Does your paper say anything about a road with nobody in it.*

Nobody in this city could answer it. Four benches have been asked in nineteen years and eleven have said they hold nothing, and this court has entered a duty to answer and cannot compel one, and the honest answer from every door in Orison is that the paper says something about a person, and a road with nobody in it is not a person, and there is no form for it, and nobody is going to be able to refuse one, because there is nobody in it to say no.

Which is either the end of the argument or the beginning of a different one, and nobody in this city is going to be able to tell which for some time, and the man who will have to try is thirty-one and is standing on a mud bank four miles down a fen road with a slate under his arm on which six days of a gate hand’s tide are written in a load-carrier’s figures that may be wrong by four minutes in any hour of them. Behind him there is a page kept by a woman with a barrow who cannot appeal from it, and a rate of half a farthing on a bucket, and four hundred and eleven loads on a flat that nobody has counted since the twentieth of last month. Ahead of him there is a review in five weeks. And in the pocket of a boy of seventeen, who is seventeen and does not know and said so, there is a question he has carried eleven miles of road from a place where the road works, and it is the only question anybody in this case has been asked this month that none of them can answer.
