# Chapter 67: What the Appeal Was For

The serving took four bells and two carts and it worked, and that is the first thing to say about the first appeal in this case, because the whole of this city has been built by people who will tell you that a thing cannot be done and then hand you a reason, and the reason this time was that it had never been tried.

One cart went to the ninth bridge and the workroom and the lower ward and the four houses, and the other went to four districts and eleven benches and one poor bench and a keeper’s house on a road entirely of its own, and the fourth bell of the serving was the second bell of the sitting, and the appeal came up to the First Verdict at the fourth with all of its papers dry, and nobody in this city has any idea how unusual that is, and the reason nobody has any idea is that nobody has ever done it before. The list on notice is fourteen names and one seal, and the Ro household and Neris Ro are at the top of it because a clerk writes the party with the most to lose at the top.

There was one thing in the serving that took eleven minutes and is in the record because Corva Silt insisted on it being in the record, and she was a clerk of thirty-one standing at a lane four miles down the fen road with a folded notice in her hand.

“The fourteenth name is a household of one at the lower road,” she said, “and the woman in it did not ask for this paper, and there is no rule that says I have to give it to her, and I stood in that lane for eleven minutes deciding whether a notice can be left with a person who has not asked to be left one.

“There is nothing in the charter that lets me leave it. There is nothing in the charter that stops me leaving it. I left it on the sill, inside, and I knocked, and I did not wait, and I wrote the time on the back of it in my own hand, because the time is the part that matters and the knock is not.

“If she sends it back I will enter that she sent it back. If she keeps it I will enter that she kept it. Neither of those is anybody's business but the two of us, and I have put it in a record in Orison because in about four years somebody is going to tell her that she could not refuse a notice and I would like the page to be able to say that a clerk of Halloway stood in her lane for eleven minutes and did not know either.”

The seal was the other thing, and it is a paragraph in the record with nothing in it, and it is the shortest thing in this case that nobody has ever looked at.

The one seal on the list of parties on notice is not a person and not a house. It is the stamp of a standing authority of preservation, and a standing authority of preservation does not have a room that anybody can be found in, and it has no clerk, and it has an incoming index that stopped on the day the routing changed four years ago, and the notice went to the desk, because the desk is the office, and a clerk of the Orison chamber walked up the stair of that building on the first day of the serving and put a folded paper on the desk under the stamp, and wrote the date and the time on a slip and took the slip, and came back down the stair.

“No answer came back,” said Dessin Nahl, reading her own entry. “Nothing came back in four bells or after. There is no clerk to refuse service and there is no clerk to accept it, so service was made on the desk and the desk received it, and a desk is a desk, and I entered the date because a date is a fact and I have got it.”

Ilyan wrote the date on Coll's slate and did not say anything about it, and did not go up the stair, and did not ask for the routing, and had said out loud three weeks before in a field on a bank that he was not going to chase the office with a stamp and no clerk in it, and gave the price of going on after it, which is that records stop coming to everybody.

Nobody in that court knew about the ninth bridge's nineteenth entry. Roke did, because he had written it at the direction of the bench and had been told nothing further. Harrow did, because a trialwright keeps the record of everything anybody says in a field. And that was the whole of the company.

“Enter the date and enter that no answer was returned,” said Harrow, “and enter that the court takes no view on it and has taken none since the ninth day of the last month, and enter that I am the court's witness in this appeal and I am not going to be asked about it.”

Nobody asked him about it.

---

The court sat in the open air at the fourth bell on the sixteenth of the month and the wind was off the water at the same angle it had been at the second sitting and the block was wet on the west face and dry on the east, and the docket board had twenty-two names on it and eleven places, and four of the eleven went to a water and a name and a boundary and a thing about an inheritance, and one of the eleven went to this.

Ansa Rell sat in the well of the court and said four things before anybody sat down.

“First: this court has no power over any gate in this city and has said so on the face of a field once and is saying it now for the second time, and nothing said in this sitting is a finding about whether any gate should be open or shut.

“Second: the appeal does not suspend access, housing, or work. That is in the charter, it is this court’s own seventh-morning-bell ground, and it is not a favour to anybody in this room and it will not become one.

“Third: a claimant with limited standing may be heard with a named advocate and I do not have one, and I am not going to appoint one from the floor, because a court that picks a man's lawyer is a court that owns the man's argument, and Mr Quill is licensed and is expressly not his advocate and I intend her to be used on the record and not on him.

“Fourth: there is no time limit on this court’s patience and there is a limit on its arithmetic. Sit down.”

Which is how it came out that the first appeal in the history of this city was argued by Maren Kett, of the Orison chamber, nineteen years in the business, who had been in this case since the first week and who had argued for one legible rule in front of nine benches and had been right, and who had warned the room about the phrase *lawful traveller* on the eleventh day and been told it was the charter's own word and been believed by nobody, and who had then gone to a poor district under protest with a written instruction in his coat that was not finished when a sheet was already nailed to a gate.

Kett stood up and said his first nine words and they were the reason he took it.

“I am not his friend. I want that read out before anything else, because in four years there is going to be a clerk who reads that I argued his appeal and thinks I believed it.”

“Do you believe it,” said Harrow.

“I believe that the question has never been answered and I believe it should be, and I have believed it since the hearing and I have been alone in it since the hearing,” said Kett, “and a man who is owed an apology is the worst advocate in this city and the reason there is one available at all.”

And that was true, and the room knew it, and Ilyan, who had spent five weeks being the name on a document in rooms he did not control, sat four feet inside the rail with nine sheets of a charter in a strap under his arm and understood that the reason he had an advocate at all was that a man who had been right in public and ignored had nothing left to lose.

---

The condition disputed is one sentence long and it is not about a gate.

Whether a review held at one span may be received in a bench that has not adopted the instrument.

Four things were said about it and three of them were about words and one of them was about money, and the one about money is the only one anybody remembers.

Dessin Nahl, who is clerk of this chamber, said that a bench receives an instrument by adopting it, and that to receive a review without adopting the instrument is to have the thing without the cost of it, and that nine benches in this city would do it inside a year because nine benches in this city are short of hands and short of money and a review that arrives for nothing is a review nobody has to answer, and that the whole of what this case is about is that a document can be very good and be a gate.

Harrow said the opposite and he said it as the court's witness, which is what he is in any appeal, and which is the reason he could not rule on it, and he said it in the flat voice he used for a machine.

“A bench may hold a paper it does not owe anything to. Every chamber in this city holds the codification and about two hundred forms it has never read and one of them is a boundary schedule from a district that stopped existing, and nothing has gone wrong, because a paper nobody acts on is scenery. The mischief is not receiving. The mischief is acting on a paper you have not adopted and not saying so, and that is not this court's power and never has been, and a form cannot be cured by a better form because a form is a habit with a seal on it.”

Kett put it in one sentence and it is the sentence the case turned on.

“Then say on the record,” he said, “that a bench which receives this instrument for its records is not bound by it, is not in breach of it, and may not enforce any part of it against any person unless it has adopted that part by its own act and its own minute; and that a bench which has received it and has not adopted a part of it is *not* the worse off and is *better* off, because it now has a paper with a date and a seal on it, and a clerk who has read a thing once is a clerk who can be asked about it.”

Harrow said, “That is not a condition. That is a remedy.”

“It is the condition,” said Kett. “The condition is whether a review held at one span may be *received*. I am not asking to widen it. I am asking whether a bench that has received it must say what it is doing, and the answer to that is the face of the ninth bridge's own tenth term, which is that a district which pays a favour is a district which has learned to rule by habit, and which is the reason a district that printed, quoted, or copied a settlement in the first fortnight could not tell afterwards which parts of it were its own — and this court entered *a citation is not an adoption* on the twenty-first day, on a keeper's letter, and it is precedent now, and it is precedent *against my client's wider request and in favour of this one*, and I would like that entered as well, because I am about to lose the rest of it.”

---

Here is what a court does when it has been told the truth twice in a morning.

It entered, first, and on the face of the record, and before any argument about consequences, the whole of the thing that everybody in the room already knew and had been saying in corridors for six weeks: that the ninth-bridge settlement binds no span but its own, and that the word *on this span* in term six was written by this court and is correct, and that this court will not widen it, and that it is the reason four hundred people at a gate four miles from here are outside every door in the instrument, and that the claimant knew it when he filed the appeal and filed it anyway.

“Enter his answer to that,” said Harrow.

“Enter it in his own words,” said Kett, and Ilyan said it, and it is in the record:

> **I KNEW THE WORD WAS THIS SPAN WHEN I FILED IT. I FILED IT BECAUSE IT IS TRUE AND BECAUSE A CONDITION THAT NOBODY HAS ANSWERED IS A CONDITION THAT CAN BE READ ANY WAY BY ANYBODY WHO WANTS A GATE, AND I COULD NOT GET IT INTO A DOOR FOUR MILES AWAY, SO I PUT IT IN A DOOR I COULD GET INTO, AND I SAID OUT LOUD AT THE TIME THAT THE THING I COULD NOT DO WAS THE THING I WANTED, AND I WAS RIGHT, AND FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN LOADS ARE STILL ON A FLAT.**

Nobody improved that and nobody read it back in a kinder version, and Roke entered it in about nine words and the nine words are the true ones.

Then the court entered the receipt: a review held at one span may be received by any bench in this city as a matter of record, on notice, and is not binding there, and a bench which has received it may not enforce any part of it against any person unless it has adopted that part by its own act and its own minute, and the word *unless* is doing all the work in that sentence and is the only part of it that is new.

And then the court entered the thing that nobody had asked for, that Kett had not argued for, and that Harrow made up in about forty seconds in front of a magistrate and a bench of five.

“A bench which has received this instrument for its records,” said Harrow, reading what he had written, “shall answer, within eleven days, in writing, and the answer shall be read in public and entered in the place where the answer was given, one question. The question is: does this bench hold any page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected by a received record may swear to, in this bench's own room, at this bench's own counter, without leave?

“This court has no power to compel the answer. I want that on the face of it in the same line. The court has no power to make a bench hold anything. What the court has done is make a bench say out loud, in public, in eleven days, whether it holds anything at all. That is not a rule. It is a duty to answer. It is the smallest power anybody in this city has ever had and I have been doing this for eleven years and I have never once given one out that I was frightened to give.

“Eleven benches have already written to this court on another question and eleven of them have said that they hold nothing that can be sworn four miles from the road it was sworn on. Eleven written answers are on the file. And I am now going to make the same question a duty, and it will be asked, and every answer will be public, and a district that holds nothing will say so in its own hand on a day anybody can attend.”

He put the tray down on the step.

“That is the finding and it is the limit and it is about the limit, and it is not an expansion of anything, and I am aware that it is useless in the way that most of this case is useless, and I am aware that a duty to answer is not a door. I entered it because in a month I have watched this court produce four things that did not work and one thing that did, and the one thing that did was a right on a page that nobody had ever used, and it worked perfectly, and it worked for a man who already had a party, and it cost the woman it was wanted for exactly nothing because she was not eligible to use it.

“Read the first part of the disposition into the record, Mr Roke, and then read the second, and then we will get to the two texts, and I want the two texts before the light goes because I would like to have said all of this in daylight and in front of the people it is about.”

---

The System put the disposition in front of Ilyan while Roke was still writing it, and it was a record of a finding and not a rule about the world.

**CHALLENGE DISPOSED OF, THE SIXTEENTH OF THE MONTH, ON TERM SIX, BEFORE THE REVIEW DATE.**
**THE CONDITION DISPUTED IS ANSWERED YES, IN PART, AND THE PART IS RECEIPT ONLY.**
**ENTERED ON THE FACE OF THE FINDING: THE NINTH-BRIDGE SETTLEMENT BINDS NO SPAN BUT ITS OWN. THE WORD *ON THIS SPAN* IS THE COURT'S OWN DRAFTING AND IS CORRECT AND IS NOT WIDENED.**
**A BENCH THAT HAS RECEIVED THE INSTRUMENT MAY NOT ENFORCE ANY PART OF IT AGAINST ANY PERSON UNLESS IT HAS ADOPTED THAT PART BY ITS OWN ACT AND ITS OWN MINUTE.**
**A BENCH THAT HAS RECEIVED THE INSTRUMENT SHALL ANSWER, IN WRITING, IN PUBLIC, WITHIN ELEVEN DAYS, WHETHER IT HOLDS ANY PAGE A PERSON AFFECTED MAY SWEAR TO IN ITS OWN ROOM.**
**THE COURT HAS NO POWER TO COMPEL THE ANSWER AND SAYS SO.**
**WHAT IS NOT FOUND: THAT THE SETTLEMENT TRAVELS. THAT ANY GATE IS OPEN OR SHUT. THAT ANY PERSON IS PAID. THAT ANY HOUSEHOLD LOSES AN ACCESS, A ROOM, OR A WORK RECORD.**

He put the nine sheets of the charter down on the step at the fourth bell, because his arms had gone, and the leg went about nine seconds after his hands left the strap, and he was on the step for a moment before he found the low place with his back against the block, and nobody in that court made anything of it, because there was no field on a step in Orison and a route-arbiter had said no in writing.

“Mr Vester,” said Harrow, without stopping, which was a kindness, “the record will show the claimant put down his papers and sat down, and the record will not show why, and that is the fourth time this court has declined to show why.”

Kett took over again, and he took over badly, on purpose, and he said so afterwards in a corridor and did not deny it.

“I am going to say the part my client does not want,” he said. “The appeal worked. Everything he asked for in the way he asked for it, the machinery did exactly what it was built to do, in four bells and two carts, and there is not one part of it that is in trouble. And in forty-one days he will have to stand at a review with his report and two copies of his own sequence and say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and he will be able to say that the first appeal in the history of this city was properly served, and it will be true, and it will not have been for her.

“The woman four miles down that road was not eligible on the day she asked and is not eligible today, and she is not eligible because of a word this court wrote, in a week when the whole case was about whether a man could stop a cart, and the word is correct, and I have argued for six days that it should not be widened, and I would like it said in a corridor by somebody that I knew that on the day I took the brief and took it anyway, because the alternative was that nothing is answered at all and a form is a habit with a seal on it.”

Then Ansa Rell stood up in the well of the court and did the thing that she has done three times in a month and has never once done for a reason anybody could have predicted, which is to say the true thing at full pitch in daylight.

“Eleven written answers are already on that file,” she said. “Eleven benches have said, in their own hands, on their own letter paper, that they hold nothing that can be sworn four miles from the road it was sworn on. That has been true since the twenty-fourth of the last month and nobody in this city knew it, because it was eleven private letters answering a question a magistrate asked off the record.

“It is public now. It is a duty and not a rule, and it is the first time in nineteen years that this city has established as a matter of public record that it holds nothing a poor person can swear to in its own room at its own counter.

“That is not a remedy. I have wanted a remedy since the eleventh day and I have got a questionnaire. But a court is not the only thing that has to be true, and a city that has said out loud, in eleven hands, that it is empty, is a city in which an empty page is an insult, and I would like whoever writes the next one of these to understand that the ground has moved under all of this and it moved because of a man who asked a question nobody had asked.”

And Bel Ordway said, from the back, in the voice of a man with a cart on a weighbridge:

“Then I hope you have got two chairs, because I have been in this city nineteen years and I have never once seen a room where the thing everybody agreed on was that there was nothing in it, and I would like to be in the room for what comes next, and I would like it noted that the chair is against the wall and not in the sun, because I have to be at the weighbridge at the seventh bell.”

---

The two texts went on the step at the sixth bell on the sixteenth of the month and the choice was set for the fourth bell of the seventeenth, and it was set for the seventeenth because a court that can set a date can be trusted with one and a court that cannot be trusted with a date is a court that will be given a week by a clerk to think.

The clean instrument is eight clauses and it is signed at the bottom by a man who is not a lawyer, and it closes a gate, and it is payable, and it pays four hands a day, and it does not unhouse anybody, and it puts a quarter of a penny on every bucket drawn at a standpipe in a poor district, and it is the first tax those people have paid anybody in nineteen years, and it was invented by a court in Orison on the strength of a bridge.

The charter is nine sheets and four of its five terms are a bridge's and the fifth is a person and there is no person, and it was read out on a bank in the mud to about a hundred and ninety people by the only person on that street who can read, and it has a clause about a wheelbarrow in it that nobody can explain, and it costs about forty-four pounds a year out of a fund that owes ninety-six pounds to a bridge four miles away, and it gives the people of one poor district a right to be appealed against and gives them nothing else at all.

And at the last bell the block stood in the open air with nothing on it and the wind coming off the water, and a name matter went back onto a board with twenty-two names on it and eleven places and would be heard when the next board came up, which was a thing two people had lost on the sixteenth of the month and would have lost on the twenty-ninth and on the thirty-first, and one of them was a name and the other was a water, and neither of them had anybody in this case.

Four miles down the fen road, on a bridge that had been carrying loads for fourteen days, the count came back at mark on a light load, and the pale permanent set did not move, and the bell was rung for the change by a keeper who has looked at the pin on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope, and off the fen road, on the fourth bridge, four square holes sat open in a deck in the weather with the props on a cart’s boards in a yard, and a keeper of forty-nine had said in a lane that the account was open and it was on his side of the road.
