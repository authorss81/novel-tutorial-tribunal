# Chapter 80: What the Review Bought

The review was the fourth morning bell of the twenty-seventh of the third month, at the span, on public notice, and it was not postponable, and it sat in the open air at the ninth bridge in the wind off the water with the counter's outside face and a cart-board laid across two trestles because there is nothing to sit at.

It was not called early. That is worth one sentence, because for a month every party in this case has had a named reading in the back of their head, and Tarin Keel put the question in writing on the nineteenth and got the answer in writing on the twentieth, and the whole of it is this: *a callable early requires a named reading and I have read it at every change since the third of the second month and it has been inside the band every time and I am a field man and there is no field.*

The span had taken its first load on the third of the second month, fifty-four days before, and it had taken one at every change since, and the count had been taken at every change with two hands under the deck and a moving cart, and it had come back inside the band on a light load at mark and a half and inside the band on a heavy one at two and three, and the pale permanent set had not moved a hair off the iron ring where it has sat for six years.

And the fourth bridge, four miles up the fen road, had been propped for thirty-nine days by four old timbers that had already stood under the ninth bridge's west seat for a fortnight in the month before last, and had stood in the open for twenty-eight days on this district's account, and there are no new ones under it and no money to buy any, and a deck propped on a timber that has already propped a bridge is a deck that is waiting for an argument about what a timber is.

---

The whole of the argument took an hour and ten minutes and it was not an argument about safety. It was a sum, and the sum was in the ninth bridge's district road fund, and Meridith Vale had it in front of him in his own clerk's hand because it is his fund and nobody else's.

> **THE ROAD FUND OF THE DISTRICT OF THE NINTH BRIDGE IS FOUR HUNDRED AND TEN POUNDS A YEAR. OUT OF IT, IN THE YEAR OF THE RULING:**
> **TWO HANDS UNDER A DECK AT EVERY CHANGE, AT THE WARD RATE: TWO HUNDRED AND TEN POUNDS.**
> **THE TELLING OF COLDWATER, FOUR POUNDS AND ELEVEN, PAID ON THE TWELFTH OF THE SECOND MONTH AND ENTERED IN A CLERK'S BOOK WITH A COLUMN FOR THE OTHER THING: FIFTEEN POUNDS, ROUNDED UP IN THE FUND.**
> **THE CHARTER OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY, FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND SIXTEEN A YEAR, OF WHICH THE PAPER IS TWO POUNDS SEVEN AND THREEPENCE PAID ONCE AND THE REST IS A QUARTER: FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND SIXTEEN.**
> **THE FOUR PROPS OF THE FOURTH BRIDGE: NINETY-SIX POUNDS.**
> **REMAINDER AFTER ALL FOUR: FORTY-FOUR POUNDS AND FOUR SHILLINGS. SHORT OF THE PROPS BY FIFTY-ONE POUNDS AND SIXTEEN SHILLINGS.**

Nobody argued about the figures because there is nobody in this city who can argue about figures and be wrong.

Rell entered that the court has no power over the ninth bridge's district fund and takes no view on it, and the fund is the ninth bridge's own, and its own district may do as it likes, and the ninth bridge's own district is a bench and a clerk and about four hundred people and it sent two of them, and they had nine pounds between them for the fen road.

Oren Sedge said the guild's part in fewer sentences than that and did not want to be thanked for it and could not have been, because the guild has been out of money for two months and lent four benches to a court it has no standing in.

“The guild will take the ninth bridge's hands at whatever the district pays, because the district's money is the money and I have spent two months telling my crews that a ward rate is a wage and I am not going to be the man who says it is not when it is.” He put his hand on the rail of the approach. “And I will tell you what the guild cannot do. The guild cannot take half a hand. A hand is a man under a deck at every change from the fifth bell and he is there for the count and not for the work, and if there is one of him instead of two then the count is a count taken by one man, and a count taken by one man is a count and not a record, and I know that because the crew list went to one name two months ago and everybody in this case has said so and it is true.”

“Then the count is a count and not a record,” said Rell, “and I am going to say those words out loud myself so that they are in my mouth and not only in a keeper’s, because in four years somebody is going to want to know who decided it.”

“Yes,” said Oren Sedge. “And I have been a keeper for a long time and I would rather have a count than a hole, and I am not going to pretend that is a principle. It is a choice between a bridge that has moved and a bridge that has four square holes in it, and one of those two is a question that gets a man killed and the other one is a question that gets a bench embarrassed in four years.”

It was the honest sentence and it decided the morning, and the decision took about four minutes after that and was made by a clerk of nineteen years in the post who had a column in a book for the other thing.

“We buy the props,” said Meridith Vale. “Four of them, new, at ninety-six, and the old four come out of the fourth bridge's deck and go into a yard where they are not carrying anything. And I want the other half of it said out loud at the counter this morning, because the other half is worse than the ninety-six pounds and it is not mine to fix.

“The old four went back under that deck on the eighteenth of the month before last, twenty-eight days after they came off it, and they have been under it for thirty-nine days and they did not fail. I am not saying they are sound. I am saying that those four timbers have already stood under the west seat of another bridge for a fortnight in the middle of a case, and the west seat of that bridge has not moved, and there is no way in this city to find out whether that is because of the timbers or in spite of them. And a bench of four is not going to be the body that says a timber is finished, and the ninth bridge's own charter says a keeper may not stop a load, and the same is true sideways of a keeper of any span in this city.

“So we buy four new ones, and the old four come out, and the deck of the fourth bridge is open in the weather for as long as it takes a man to get four new timbers cut and carted up a fen road, and nobody in this room has any power to shorten that, and the man who keeps that bridge is going to be the man sitting on it while it is open, and I have not asked him, and the court has not asked him, and I am going to enter that I did not ask him and that I would not have known what to do with the answer.”

“One hand under the deck every change at the ward rate, which is a hundred and five pounds,” said Meridith Vale, “and the count is taken by one man, and the line down the middle of it stays, and the entry says so. And the fifth-bell reading at every change stays, and the bell's wheel and pin are span maintenance and are inspected on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope, and that is the second term of this charter and it is a copy of a bridge's and it is the only term in the instrument that is a machine and it does not stop working because the money came out of the other end of the fund.”

“And if the band is ever read outside what the keeper has kept,” said Harrow, “this review is callable early by any party, and the court has entered that in every instrument it has made since the ruling and I am putting it in this one in these words so that nobody has to find it again: *and if the band is ever read outside the band the keeper has kept, by a name and with a date, the review is callable on the day it is read and is not waitable.*”

The disposition came out of the room at the seventh bell and the System put it in front of Ilyan while Roke was still writing, and it was a record of a finding and not a rule about the world.

**THE PUBLIC MAINTENANCE REVIEW HELD AT THE NINTH BRIDGE, THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH, AT THE FOURTH MORNING BELL. NOT CALLED EARLY.**
**IT IS A MAINTENANCE REVIEW AND IT DECIDES A SUM AND NOT A QUESTION OF SAFETY.**
**THE FOURTH BRIDGE'S FOUR PROPS ARE BOUGHT, NINETY-SIX POUNDS, OUT OF THE NINTH BRIDGE'S DISTRICT ROAD FUND. THE FOUR OLD PROPS COME OUT OF THAT DECK AND GO INTO A YARD, AND THE DECK IS OPEN IN THE WEATHER UNTIL THE NEW ONES ARE IN, AND NOBODY IN THIS COURT HAS POWER TO SHORTEN THAT.**
**THE UNDER-DECK WATCH AT THE NINTH BRIDGE IS REDUCED FROM TWO HANDS TO ONE HAND FOR THIS YEAR. THE MAN WHO LOSES THE SECOND HAND IS T. BLORE, TWENTY-TWO, ELEVEN MONTHS UNDER A DECK.**
**THE COUNT GOES ON AT EVERY CHANGE AND IS ENTERED AS WHAT IT IS.**
**THE PALE PERMANENT SET HAS NOT MOVED. THE SPAN HAS TAKEN A LOAD AT EVERY CHANGE FOR FIFTY-FOUR DAYS. EVERY FIGURE TAKEN WAS TAKEN WITH A MOVING CART. NO FIGURE OF THIRTY-FOUR DAYS AND NO FIGURE OF FORTY-FOUR DAYS IS ENTERED ANYWHERE IN THIS DISPOSITION.**
**IF THE BAND IS EVER READ OUTSIDE THE BAND THE KEEPER HAS KEPT, BY A NAME AND WITH A DATE, THE REVIEW IS CALLABLE ON THE DAY IT IS READ.**
**THE COURT DID NOT ASK THE KEEPER OF THE FOURTH BRIDGE WHAT HE THINKS ABOUT THE DECK BEING OPEN AGAIN AND HAS ENTERED THAT IT DID NOT ASK AND TAKES NO VIEW.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED HERE: WHETHER THE CHARTER OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY BINDS ANY SPAN BUT ITS OWN. WHETHER ANY RATE IN THAT DISTRICT IS FAIR. WHETHER THE FIGURE OF A THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS IS CORRECT. WHETHER THE ELEVEN-DAY ANSWERS WERE RIGHT.**

---

Nobody had asked the man whose hand it was, and that was put on the floor at the seventh bell by the guild and not by the district.

“Blore,” said Oren Sedge. “Twenty-two, eleven months at the west end under a deck, an apprentice, two seasons of a span is what an apprenticeship of that trade wants and one is not two, and at the third bell this morning he had a half day and a full day and a half day, which is how a guild spells *we are taking your second hand off you and we are sorry about it*. He is nineteen miles off working a deck at the cut banks under a man of sixty and he does not know yet, and he is not going to find it out in a letter, because a letter from a trade comes at the change and he will read it standing up with a barrow in his hands and take it for a summons about something else, and I shall tell him at the second bell the first morning I can get down there and stand in front of him, with my own mouth, and I have wanted to say out loud that I have been the man who tells him and not the man who took it, and that I did not take it and that I would have taken it just the same if I had not been in the room.

“And the second thing, and it is the part of this that will not sit down. A man who loses four hours a day at a ward rate is a man with a smaller wage, and a smaller wage is not a wrong and not a harm anybody can name in a chamber, and there is no line in this city that pays a man for eleven months. The guild is out of money and the district has forty-four pounds and four shillings left in the year after everything above, and neither of those bodies is a person, and neither of them has said anything to him yet. I am a trade and I have six crews that have not voted for anything this year, and if I put this to them this month they will vote to keep the two hands and cut the props, and they will be wrong, and I have no way to tell them they are wrong except to say that the ninth bridge has not moved for fifty-four days, and that is an argument about a number and not about a man.”

“Enter it,” said Rell, “and enter that the court cannot pay him either, and that the court has no fund and the guild has none and the district has forty-four pounds and four shillings, and that a person who loses four hours a day in the year a man with a leg is standing on this bridge is the ninth line of a disposition and the whole of what this court did about it.”

---

The second thing the review did was harder and took from the seventh bell to the ninth.

He was produced at it, in person, and he brought his report and the two copies of his own sequence, and he read both of them out loud in the open air to about ninety people, which is a thing nobody in the record had asked him to do and which the ruling had required and which is not the same thing.

The two sequences took about four minutes each and are the only part of that morning that nobody could follow and nobody left. They are a private copy of a game nobody in Orison has ever played, describing a thing that four hundred people at a low-water gate have lived inside for two months, and when he had finished the first one a woman on the bank said *I have never heard of that game* and he said *no*, and she said *then how do you know it is the same*, and he said *I do not, and I have said so in three rooms, and it is in the record that I cannot say what your words mean when they are put next to each other*, and the woman said *that is a strange thing to say out loud at a review* and he said *it is a strange thing to be*.

Then he read out the six things, because the ruling said he must be produced with his report and the two copies of his own sequence to say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and he had entered six on the fourteenth of the month and the review was on the twenty-seventh and there was no reason for there to be a seventh by then and there was not.

And then he did the part that the ruling actually required and that no one in this case had yet understood was the whole of it, which was to name who paid.

“Bryn Sallow has six and a half hours a day at a third of a haul rate, in chalk, at the moment of the work, from the eighteenth of the month before last, and the thirty years before that is entered as owed by a district that never kept hours, and I have now entered a figure on it that nobody asked for and that she refused, and the figure is wrong by two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings and by an amount none of us can name, and she is owed it and will be asked about it every quarter by a woman with a barrow.

“Wenna Tace keeps a page and nine sheets for about a pound and eleven a quarter, and is paid about a third of a haul rate for two hundred loads a year, and she cannot appeal, cannot appear in support of one, and cannot be named in one, and she is the door for about five people a day in a district that has nowhere else to send them, and she has asked a bench for a rate and has written in her own page that she wants a second barrow and that she is not asking for it yet.

“Bream took a shilling a week off forty roofs for nineteen years and swore forty names out loud in a room in twelve minutes and got one of them wrong, and nobody has thanked him and he did not ask, and it is in the record that he did not ask.

“Wist Anhalt lost four props off his deck for twenty-eight days and asked for nothing, and what he asked for was a page in a book instead of four words on a nail, and nobody in this city has any use for that, and it went up the fen road in a cart.

“Corva Silt has asked to be relieved of the print authority five times in six weeks and has been refused five times, and has served her own district's appeal against a woman who works at a gate, and stood eleven minutes in a lane deciding about a notice.

“Nell Ambrey entered a sanitation complaint eleven weeks before any of this began and it is not heard this year because a bench's time went to an appeal.

“Cael Rundle pays half a farthing a bucket on his own lane, and said before anybody else that it was the right rate.

“Abel Kearn took his own name off a docket board in the open air rather than be produced, and went home to a wall with a crack in it, and a timber merchant who is not a criminal took his elm off the wall and paid for the crack and would not be repaid.

“Hester Vole of a district eleven miles from here read nine sheets out loud twice and kept a book nine years and was not asked and was not paid, and her cow was milked twice in a day.

“Tam Blore is twenty-two and had eleven months under a deck and lost the second hand this morning, and I want his name said before anything else because he is the only person in this case whose loss is this morning's, and it is not anybody's fault, and he is not in the record at all except in the line I have just read out, and I would like the court to enter his name in the disposition.”

It did. It is in the disposition in his own name, which is the only name in it, and the clerk entered it and did not know what to do with it, and Roke said “the second hand” and Vale said “his name” and Roke entered **T. BLORE, SECOND HAND, UNDER-DECK WATCH REDUCED, THIS YEAR**, and that is the only place in the whole of this case's record where a man who lost something in it is named, and it took a defendant with limited standing to put him there.

---

He did not say the sixth thing's size again. He had entered it on the fourteenth and the record had it and he said so and read the reference and that was all, and the reason he did not read it again is that he had read all six on the fourteenth in a stone room to a recordwright and had learned in thirteen days that a thing read twice is a thing being made bigger, and he had four months of the thing left to live with and did not need to be told about it in the open air.

And then, at the eighth bell, a bench that was not on the docket asked for a word, and the word was Wen Lask's, and it was on a table in Orison in a boy's line book and a copy of it in the case's record, and it asked one question and has been in the record for twenty-eight days and nobody has answered it.

*A MAN FROM A CITY WROTE DOWN A RULE FOR A PLACE WITH PEOPLE IN IT AND ASKED ME WHETHER I HAVE ANYTHING A PERSON MAY SWEAR TO, AND HE DID NOT ASK ME ANYTHING THAT IS ABOUT ME. DOES YOUR PAPER SAY ANYTHING ABOUT A ROAD WITH NOBODY IN IT.*

It was read out by a clerk and it was answered by nobody for about nine seconds, and then Ansa Rell answered it, and the answer is in the record and it is the truest thing said at that review and it does not help.

“The terms of that charter say something about a person,” she said. “They say a person may not be a party, a person may not be refused an office, a person may not be entered in a traveller book and be a trespasser by that fact, a person may not be refused at a gate, and a person may appeal. Every one of those is about a person and not one of them is about a road, and there is no instrument in this case that says a word about a road with nobody in it, and this court has no power over a road in a region with no district, and takes no view, and has said so on the face of a field about a gate and will say so again.

“So the answer to her question is no, and the answer is about our paper and is correct. The answer to her question that she is asking, which is whether a road with nobody in it is safe, is not one this court can give and has never pretended it could, and the road is a good road, the toll has never been short, the loads go through, four hands are paid on the day and their names are written in the book by a woman of seventy-one herself, and none of them has ever asked for anything, and the last name in the witness line is her father's and she has written over it twice and she does not know why she keeps doing it.

“Enter it. Enter that the question was asked on the twenty-ninth of the second month and read at a public review on the twenty-seventh of the third and answered, and that the answer is that our paper says what our paper says, and that it is not the answer, and that this court has not gone to that road and is not going to and has said why. And enter that the boy of seventeen who put the question there told a magistrate the price of it before he did it, in about eleven sentences, and that the price was that in about a year that gate is a gate a man from a city has written to about a rule, and that he said he wanted somebody to be annoyed with him about it and wanted it to be a magistrate and not a friend, and that the court was not annoyed, and that the court has no power to be annoyed at a road it cannot reach, and that the not-being-annoyed is the closest this case can come to an admission and is not one.”

Nobody improved it. The road was not touched. It is a good road and it will still be a good road and the last name in that line is still her father's, and a question has now been asked in public twice and answered once and the answer has been entered as not being the answer.

---

The last thing the review did was the smallest thing it did, and Sel Harrow did it, and it is his fourth procedure, and he named it as his own and said he was not frightened of this one.

The carrier's clause has no successor. It says the appeal is stayed *until another carrier is sworn in that district*, and there is no instrument in this city that says how a carrier is sworn in, and Harrow has said twice in this case that a line nobody entered is a line he may not rule on, and the absence of a procedure is a line nobody entered. On the twenty-seventh of the third month, in the open air, at a review, a trialwright of eleven years invented a way of swearing in a carrier and said out loud that he had invented it, and that it was the smallest power he had ever given out, and that a duty to answer had frightened him and this had not frightened him and that both of those sentences were true and that he was more frightened of the second.

> **WHERE AN INSTRUMENT REFERS TO ANOTHER CARRIER BEING SWORN IN A DISTRICT, AND THERE IS NO PERSON SWORN IN THAT DISTRICT, THEN ANY BENCH IN THAT DISTRICT MAY SWEAR IN A CARRIER ON THE APPLICATION OF A PERSON OF THAT DISTRICT, IN THE BENCH'S OWN ROOM, IN PUBLIC, ON A DAY APPOINTED BY THE BENCH, AND THE CARRIER'S NAME SHALL BE ENTERED IN THE BENCH'S OWN BOOK WITH A DATE, AND THE SWEARING IN OF A CARRIER DOES NOT AFFECT THE TERMS OF THE INSTRUMENT IN ANY OTHER DISTRICT AND IS NOT AN ADOPTION OF IT.**
> **AND THIS COURT HAS NO POWER OVER ANY SUCH BENCH, AND ENTRES IT AS A PROCEDURE IT HAS INVENTED IN PUBLIC, AND SAYS IT IS NOT PRECEDENT.**

It is a procedure. It is not a remedy, and Harrow said so four times, and it is the first time in this case that anybody has written down how a person becomes the door for four hundred people, and the reason it is not a remedy is in the tenth term of the ninth bridge's charter and in the shape of a sum in a clerk's hand, which is: a right that costs a district money, and has no standing outside one bench, and depends on a person of thirty-one being within four miles, is a favour with a seal on it.

And the carrier's clause was used once in the third month of this case and it cost a district a day and a half of a cart in the month before, and it cost a man two days of walking he was not paid for in this one, and it cost a woman at a bar a day she was not paid for, and it put a hand's width of a stranger into a district four miles from a bridge, and the consequence of the clause is visible every day at the ninth bridge and is that there is one man in this city that four hundred people can be sent for, and he is not a bench, and he cannot be replaced without a procedure that did not exist until the eighth bell this morning, and the procedure that does exist cannot be used by anybody, because the only district with a carrier is the only district that does not need one.

Nobody said that last part. Nahl wrote it in her own book on the twenty-eighth and it is in no record but hers and it is the nearest thing to a finding the next thirty chapters have.

---

And the thing nobody was told about happened at the ninth bell, and it was the seventh time the line has ever come, and the fifth time a trialwright has refused it, and it was not in the open air.

The charter of the district of Halloway went into the record of that review on its nine sheets, sworn and sealed, because a review of a span keeps the instrument it is reviewing and a clerk does not summarise nine sheets into a minute. Sabin Roke checked the copy against the fair one at the third bell, at the counter, in the ordinary way, and found a line at the foot of the last sheet that was in the copy and not in the fair one.

> **CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD AGAINST THE HOLDER.**

Harrow was at the span and read it standing at the counter with the wind off the water and about forty people on the flat who had come up to hear the end of it, and he read it twice, and then he said the sentence he has said every time, in the same words, and the words did not vary, and it was the fifth time he had said them:

“I will not rule on a line I did not enter.”

Nobody asked him who wrote it, or when, or whether it was in the copy at the counter at Halloway, and he did not offer. He entered it as an unentered line, with a date, and did not find it, and did not use it, and did not let it be used, and said he would refuse on the sixth time and on the seventh, and that on his eleventh year as a trialwright he had come to the conclusion that a line he did not enter is a line he may not rule on, and that this is not modesty and is not neutrality and is the only procedure he has.

Nobody named anything. That is entered, and it is the finding of the seventh appearance, and the seventh is in the record of a public review at a bridge and in a copy in a room over a pump, and the word in it is the word for the person, and the person is four miles down a fen road sweeping up after two hundred people who have gone up a mud bank, and she has not read it, and nobody in this case will tell her.

---

And on the first of the fourth month, at the sixth bell, at the low-water standpipe, about two hundred people came for water and the quarter's rate was read out loud by the holder, and it was the quarter's reading and the first one in which she read a rate that had been set since the last one, and she read the whole of the page and not only the barrel lid.

She read the half a farthing a bucket. She read the six and a half hours a day in chalk with a name against them and the thirty years as owed and not paid, and she read the entry of the fourteenth of the third month, which is a figure of a thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings with three hands against it and a line saying the figure is wrong and that the person it is owed to refused it, and she read the line that says the figure is wrong by two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings, and stopped for about two seconds, and then she read a fifth thing, which is new, and which had been set by a bench of four on the twenty-eighth of the third month at the third bell and had been asked for in a room over a pump by a person who cannot appeal from it.

> **A HAUL IS A BARROW'S LOAD, AND A BARROW'S LOAD IS FOUR LOADS TO A CART, AND THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE IS A THIRD OF ITS OWN HAUL RATE, AND SO A HAUL IN THIS DISTRICT IS PAID AT THREE TIMES THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE, TO THE PERSON AT THE MOMENT OF THE WORK, BY NAME, IN CHALK. THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HAULS OF THE LAST ELEVEN MONTHS WHICH ARE IN NO BOOK OF THIS DISTRICT ARE ENTERED AS OWED BY THIS DISTRICT AND ARE NOT PAID, THE DISTRICT HAVING NO MONEY.**
> **THE RATE WAS SET BY A BENCH OF FOUR. IT WAS ASKED FOR BY THE HOLDER OF THE FIFTH TERM, WHO CANNOT APPEAL FROM IT, AND IT WILL BE READ ALOUD AT THE SIXTH BELL ON EVERY FIRST DAY OF A QUARTER BY THE SAME PERSON, AND NOBODY MAY APPEAL IT, AND IT IS THE LARGEST SINGLE WANT OF THE PEOPLE IT IS READ TO.**

It is two thirds short of what a haul is worth. The bench set it at the district's own rate, which is a third of a haul rate, and a haul is three times the district's own rate, and the bench knew that, and set it at the rate anyway, because the fund has no money and the fund is the money and the minimum is a fact and not a mercy. So a hauler in Halloway is paid a third of a haul, and every other hauler in this city is paid a haul, and the charter she read out on a bank in the mud has no clause in it about a barrow, and that absence is hers, and she has now put the absence and the rate in the same entry on a page she keeps for about a pound and eleven a quarter.

And about two hundred people went up the mud bank, and the reading finished, and a boy of about nineteen at the back asked her what a haul was, and Wenna Tace said a haul was a barrow's load, and he said he had thought a haul was a cart's, and she said in this district a cart's is four hauls, and he said *then your barrow is a quarter of a cart and the man on the fourth is a quarter of a man*, and there was about nine seconds of noise on a mud bank, and she said *that is right, and it is the largest thing this charter does not say, and I cut it out myself in a room in Orison, and it is going in the next one and it is not going in this one because this one is sworn.*

Then she got the barrow out and did two runs before the water came, and the leg on the bank was a leg that was his own for two hours and then was not, and he sat on the barrel lid at the gate side with a scar at the base of his left thumb that will be there in four years, and the low water in his nose in four layers, and a door in the picture at the same height and the same distance it has always been at.

The barrow came up past him at the ninth bell and he said, “There are four new timbers going up the fen road to a bridge this week, and a man of twenty-two who has lost four hours a day because of them, and he is nineteen miles off and does not know yet, and I am going to be standing at this gate when Oren Sedge goes down and tells him, and I have no standing in that conversation and I am not going to be in it, and that is going to be the correct use of me for about a year.”

“You never have standing anywhere,” said Wenna Tace, from the step, without turning round. “That is not a complaint. I want to tell you a thing and then I am going to do my last run.

“I am twenty-nine. I haul about two hundred loads a year. I keep nine sheets and a page in a room I rent, and I am paid about a pound and eleven a quarter for the sheets, and a haul in this district is three times the district's own rate and I set that this month and I read it out every quarter and I cannot appeal from it, and if I am ill there is a notice with a seal on it that nobody in four miles can take in, and there are two entries in my page that I put in the middle because I did not want them easy to find, and there is a line under the fifth term that I have told one man about and no clerk, and that I have not read, and that I would rather not read, because I do not know what it wants and I would have to carry it about the district.

“And there is no procedure in this city for what happens to a page when the holder cannot do it. A bench of four can be asked and there is nobody who has ever asked, and the answer will be that the fifth term wants a person in the district who can read, and there is one, and she is doing it, and everybody in this district knows she is doing it, and the whole of the district's law now depends on whether a hauler's back holds out until somebody who has never done it tries.”

She got the barrow round the corner of the pump house.

“In about a year I will not be able to lift one end of this and everybody who comes up this bank will know it before I do, because I will be the one hiding it. And a court in Orison is going to sit down one day in about four years and ask how an appeal survives in a district that is being squeezed, and the honest answer is going to be *as long as a woman of twenty-nine can still read and haul at the same time*, and that is not an answer to a question about a rule. That is an answer about a woman. And nobody has ever put that in an instrument, because it is not a rule, it is a person, and nobody has ever written one down.”

Then she went down the bank, and the water came up, and about two hundred people were on the flat in the last of the light.

And in a chamber in Orison, on a table in a clerk's own hand with a date on it, there is a question that nobody in this city has read: **the only district in this city with a carrier is the only district that does not need one.** And a bench of four four miles down a fen road has two debts in its book and has paid neither, and a page is in a room over a pump in the keeping of a woman of twenty-nine who cannot appeal from it, and the fourth month of the case is one day old.
