# Chapter 69: What It Cost by Morning

The rate went on the board at the gate side on the morning of the eighteenth and it was a half a farthing a bucket and it was the first time in nineteen years that anybody in that district had been asked to pay anybody anything, and it is on a barrel lid nailed to a board under a sheet with a seal on it, in the district's own hand, and about two hundred people a day go past it going for water, and none of them knew it was coming until the morning it was there.

> **A RATE OF HALF A FARTHING ON EVERY BUCKET DRAWN AT THIS STANDPIPE, SET BY THE BENCH OF FOUR ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE MONTH, AND IT IS NOT A TAX ON THE DISTRICT AND IT IS NOT A TAX ON THE GATE. IT PAYS FOR THE PAPER, THE SERVING, AND THE KEEPING OF THE CHARTER, AND FOR NOTHING ELSE, AND IT IS TO BE READ ALOUD AT THE SIXTH BELL ON EVERY FIRST DAY OF A QUARTER, AT THE PUMP, BY THE HOLDER.**
>
> **THE REASON THE AMOUNT IS WHAT IT IS: THE ROAD FUND OF THIS DISTRICT IS PAID OUT OF THE CARTAGE ON THE CULVERT ROAD, AND THE GULVERT ROAD IS SHUT TO LOADS, AND THE CARTAGE HAS BEEN NIL SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF THE LAST MONTH. THE SUM ENTRIES THE DIFFERENCE IS ELEVEN POUNDS AND FOUR IN THE QUARTER. THE BENCH HAS SET THE RATE AT THE AMOUNT THAT PRODUCES ELEVEN POUNDS AND FOUR AND NOT A UNIT MORE, BECAUSE THE BENCH HAS NO MONEY AND THE MINIMUM IS A FACT AND NOT A MERCY.**

Cael Rundle read it at the fifth bell standing at the top of the bank, and he read it the way a man reads a thing he is going to argue with, and the argument lasted about eleven seconds, and then he put his finger under the third line and said:

“That is the right rate. I want it minuted that I said so before anybody said anything, because I have been in this case since the fifteenth of the last month and I have voted for two rates in two days and I have voted for the one I live in as well.

“Half a farthing. My mother died in the year they last moved that, and half a farthing is half of what the other instrument wanted, and it is a rate on the water for ever either way, and the reason it is half of what the other instrument wanted is that the gate is shut, and the reason the gate is shut is the reason I stood on a bank five weeks ago and said *take the paper down* and was refused.

“So we are paying for the gate with the pump, at a rate set by four people of whom two sleep on Weir Lane, and the rate is smaller than the good instrument’s rate because the good instrument would have opened the gate. I have got that. I have had a day and a half to get it and I have got it and I am not going to stand on a bank about it.

“My daughter is fourteen. She is not in the traveller book and she is going to walk to that pump every morning for the rest of her life with half a farthing on it, and I asked for a line in a district book on which an hour of work could be paid to a person by name, and I was given a date, and here is the date, and the line is full of another woman’s name, and the bucket is mine.

“I would like both of those said in the same minute and by the same man, and I would like it read at the sixth bell every quarter when the holder reads it, and I would like somebody in four years to be able to find this minute and find out that a man who voted for a rate on his own lane said *this is the right rate* before he said anything else.”

It is in the minute. It is in the district's day book in the district's own hand. It is the second thing that has been entered in this case that nobody in this case has any way of using.

---

The props went back on the same cart on the eighteenth at the seventh bell, and they went back because a bench of four with eleven pounds four a quarter in it could not buy four new ones and would not put the cartage of a shut road into a bridge's deck.

That is a decision that was made in about four minutes on the previous evening by four people in a room over a pump, and Nell Ambrey, the convenor of the bench, wrote it out in the district's own hand and it is the fourth line of that day's book, and the reason it is short is that it was written by somebody who is sixty and tired:

> **THE FOUR PROPS OF THE FOURTH BRIDGE GO BACK ON THE SAME CART ON THE EIGHTEENTH, AT THE DISTRICT'S COST, FOUR POUNDS. THE DISTRICT WILL NOT BUY FOUR NEW ONES THIS YEAR. THE ACCOUNT OF THE FOURTH BRIDGE IS NOT DISCHARGED AND IS CARRIED, AND THE DEFICIENCY IS ELEVEN POUNDS FOUR IN THE QUARTER, AND THE BENCH DOES NOT HAVE ELEVEN POUNDS FOUR.**

And the man they went to was asked, in the end, the thing nobody had asked him in a month.

Wist Anhalt is forty-nine and has kept the fourth bridge for twenty-nine years and his four props came off that span on the day of a hearing in Orison and went four miles down a fen road on a cart and held up the west seat of another bridge for thirty-four days, and he wrote four words on a slip of paper, and a court nailed the four words to a frame, and he has never met the man who wrote them and does not want to.

He had said on the fourteenth, in a lane, that he was not going to have them back on that cart unless the district paid for it, and that if the district did not pay then the props would stay where they were and he would write it in his own book on the seventeenth with the date and the weather, and that he would not send it to anybody and would not nail it to a frame and would not say four words about it in a room in Orison.

The props went back on the eighteenth, not the seventeenth, at the seventh bell, and the cart cost four pounds and the district paid it.

Nobody sent for him.

Corva Silt sent for him, on the seventeenth, in a cart, with two clauses in writing, and the clauses were written by the recordwright of the ninth-bridge's district in a chamber hand and they were short:

> **THAT THE FOURTH BRIDGE'S ACCOUNT IS CARRIED AND NOT DISCHARGED, AND THE DEFICIENCY OF ELEVEN POUNDS FOUR IN THE QUARTER IS ENTERED AGAINST THIS DISTRICT AND IS NOT ENTERED AGAINST THE KEEPER OF ANY SPAN.**
>
> **THAT THE KEEPER OF THE FOURTH BRIDGE IS NOT A PARTY TO THIS CHARTER AND HAS NOT ASKED TO BE HEARD, AND THAT THE COURT HAS TAKEN NO VIEW OF WHAT HE WANTS, AND THAT IF HE WANTS SOMETHING SAID IT IS TO BE ASKED FOR AND NOT ASSUMED.**

“Who wrote the second one,” said Anhalt, on the front step of the keeper's hut, with the slips in his hand and the cart behind him.

“Miss Quill of the Orison chamber. I asked her to write it this morning and she wrote it in about a minute and she did not ask me what it was for.”

“Then tell her the second one is the first sensible thing anybody has said about me since I was twenty, and tell her I have had a month with the first one and the first one is on a frame four miles away where a carter can read it on a wet day, and I do not like it there, and I have never taken it down, and I have walked past it about forty times.

“You have come down this road to ask me a question,” he said, “and I have been waiting since the day of the hearing to find out whether anybody was going to. So I am going to answer it properly and then I am going to go and watch four props go onto a cart, and I would like you to write it down while I am saying it, because I am fifty in about two months and I have been a keeper for twenty-nine years and I have a very good memory for what a bridge did and a very bad one for what a room said.

“I do not want a page. I have read the ninth bridge's charter. I am not in it. I am the fourth bridge and there is nothing in ten terms about a man with one bridge and a gate at the top of a slope and thirty years.

“But I want the account carried, and I want the eleven pounds four entered against the district and not against me, and I want the four words taken off that frame and put in a book on my desk, and I want the frame to say *fourth bridge, four words, asked and answered* with a date on it and nothing else on it, because a carter should not have to read a man's objection about somebody else's bridge.

“And I want to say the last one and I want it minuted, because it is the only thing I have got.

“You took my four props off my deck in the morning without a word to me and used them to hold up a bridge in another district for thirty-four days, and that was lawful, and I sent two hands to help pack the seat and nobody asked me to and I would do it again. And in those thirty-four days I had a span with four square holes in it and the weather coming, and I did not once put a hand on a square hole and I did not once sleep on that deck, and nobody in this city knows that but you.

“I have been the man who was not on the bridge for thirty-four days and I would like that on a page in your book and not on a nail.”

Nobody in that lane had anything to say to that for about a minute.

“I will write it,” said Corva Silt, in the district's own hand, on the back of a slip, in a wet lane, on a bridge four miles from a court, “and it will go up the fen road in the morning and it will be entered and it will not be put on a nail, and I am going to say one thing back and I am going to say it as a clerk who is thirty-one and has printed an instrument in a fortnight that shut a lane in a district of mine.

“I have got it wrong twice. I printed a paper with a word in it that shut a gate, and I struck out one of my own clauses four days later and had to insist the bench minute it so it would not be mine alone. And you are the fourth person in a month who has come at me with a thing I have done and told me to take it off a nail and put it in a book, and every one of the four of you was right, and I would like the court to enter that a clerk of this district is asking to be relieved of the print authority four times in six weeks and has been refused four times and is not going to ask a fifth time.”

---

The first appeal under the charter was sworn on the eighteenth of the month at the sixth bell, on a mud bank, in front of about a hundred and forty people, and the person who swore it had not read it and could not have read it, and the person who wrote it was in a room over a pump a mile away with a barrow against the wall, and the person who carried it had walked four miles to do it with a leg that was not his and a page in a strap and a document that said if the carrier refused the appeal would be stayed.

Bryn Sallow appealed the office of gate hand.

Not the rate, which is three days old. Not the gate, which she works and does not have the standing to complain of. The office: the first line of the schedule she wrote herself on the twentieth, in her own hand, on a step, with the tide coming, in the words she wrote slowly.

She wanted to refuse the post and she wanted the refusal entered against the burden and not against her, and she wanted to be paid for what she had already done, and she had worked out the whole shape of it in about nine minutes on a bank a month ago and had told a field about it and had been told that the exit was four miles up the road and does not travel.

The charter travels. The charter's fourth term says the burden refused is entered against the burden and not against the person, and the charter's fifth term says a page is kept by a person in the district, and a person may swear an entry in it, and the appeal runs to any person in the district.

The serving took a day and a half.

Larch could not serve it because the parties were four people and a district and a bench of four, and the district is not a person, and a district has to be described in a notice, and the describing took nine hours in a clerk's office in Orison, and there is no clause in any instrument in this city that says how to serve a district, and the court entered that nobody knew and went and found out by asking, and the answer was that you serve the person who keeps the district's book, and in Halloway that is the clerk.

Corva Silt served her own district's appeal on the second afternoon and minuted it herself in the district's own hand, and she did it without a word of comment, and at the end she entered one line of her own:

> **THIS APPEAL WAS RECEIVED BY THE CLERK OF THIS BENCH, WHO PRINTED INSTRUMENT H-9 ON THE SECOND DAY OF THE LAST MONTH AND IS THE PERSON NAMED IN CLAUSE TWO OF IT AS STRUCK OUT. SHE HAS ASKED TO BE RELIEVED OF THE PRINT AUTHORITY. SHE IS REFUSED AGAIN. SHE IS NOT RELIEVED.**

---

The hearing was on the twenty-first at the sixth bell, on the bank, in the open, with a rope and four stakes, because a field which tests records behind a bench tests nothing, and it lasted two hours and it refused her.

Harrow's reason was one sentence and it is the correct sentence and it is in the charter.

“A person may not refuse an office and keep it. That is not this charter's rule. It is the rule the charter was written out of, and it is a good rule, and if you may refuse an office and keep it then no office in this city is an office, it is a preference, and then the gate is worked by whoever is nearest, and on the nineteenth of last month the nearest person to a gate in this district was a carter with four loads of district sand and he could not read clause three.”

“Then pay me for the part I have already done,” said Bryn Sallow.

And here is where the charter worked, and it worked because four of its five terms are a bridge's and the bridge's terms had a pay line in them and a pay line is a machine that does not care what you are appealing about.

“Court will take the account,” said Harrow.

The account is four hours a day from the fifth bell against a working tide of ten and a half hours by the gate hand's own count, and it has been entered unpaid in five forms across six weeks by three different people in three different hands, and the charter's fourth term says the rate is a number and not a recipient and is paid at the moment of the work to the person doing it, by name, in chalk.

The district's maintenance clerk set the rate. Oakes is thirty-four and has held a seal for two years and has never once had to answer for a wage, and he refused one in writing in one sentence in the last month on the ground that a district which pays a favour learns to rule by habit, and that sentence was correct, and it killed the eight days.

He set a rate that morning, in the district's own hand, at the district's own rate, which is a third of a haul rate, and which is correct, and which is the least anybody could be paid and still be paid.

> **THE DISTRICT'S OWN RATE, WHICH IS A THIRD OF ITS OWN HAUL RATE, ON THE BALANCE OF THE TIDE FROM THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE MONTH FORWARD, TO B. SALLOW, BY NAME, IN CHALK, AT THE MOMENT OF THE WORK. A WORKING TIDE AT THAT GATE IS TEN AND A HALF HOURS BY THE GATE HAND'S OWN COUNT, AND FOUR HOURS OF IT IS PAID, AND SIX AND A HALF HOURS OF IT IS ENTERED AS UNPAID AND CARRIED.**
>
> **THE THIRTY YEARS BEFORE THE EIGHTEENTH OF THIS MONTH IS ENTERED AS OWED BY THIS DISTRICT AND IS NOT PAID, AND THE BENCH HAS NO RATE FOR IT AND NO HOURS FOR IT AND THE DISTRICT DID NOT KEEP ANY, AND THE COURT ENTERS THAT A DEBT WHICH HAS NEVER BEEN COUNTED IS NOT A SMALL DEBT AND THIS ONE IS NOT SMALL.**

She read that twice and then she said the sentence of her life, to a bench, in a field, on a bank, with a hundred and forty people behind a rope.

“Ten and a half hours a tide,” said Bryn Sallow. “Every day. Thirty years. I have never counted them and I am not going to count them now, and I am not going to have them counted, and I know what that number is, and it is over a hundred thousand, and there is no rate in the world for it and there never will be one, because the rate is the district's rate and the district's rate is a number, and a number can be divided, and I have had it divided from me for thirty years.

“You have entered it as owed. That is the whole of what a page can do with it and I have wanted that for a month and I am not going to say thank you, and I am not going to pretend I am not glad, and both of those are true and I have practised them at a queue for eleven years.”

Then she said the cost, which is the part that made it real and the part she had worked out on a bank a month before anybody asked her to.

“The four who set that rate,” she said, “two of them sleep on Weir Lane, and I have bought salt off one of them and lent a bucket to the other, and from this morning a woman I have bought salt off for thirty years owes me six and a half hours a day, and she is going to be asked for it every quarter by a woman with a barrow at a pump, and I am going to be paid in full, every quarter, out of a fund that is short, and I would like it minuted that I said that in advance and that I said it before anybody asked me and that it did not change my mind.”

“Enter it in her words,” said Harrow, “and enter that the court asked her whether the rate being set by two of her neighbours changed her refusal, and that she had entered her refusal before the rate was set, and that the court has entered the rate after the refusal, and that the order of those two things is deliberate and is hers.”

---

The hundred and forty people came off the bank at the ninth bell and the water came up and about two hundred went up the mud in the dark, and on the barrel lid at the gate side there were now two things, the rate in the district's own hand and the six and a half hours a day in chalk with a name against them, and about a mile up the fen road in a room over a pump a woman with a barrow against the wall had written out, in her own hand, the first entry in a page she was not allowed to appeal from, and she had written it in the middle of the page rather than at the top, because a person who keeps a page does not put the most important thing where it is easiest to find.

On the eighteenth of the month, in a room over a pump, four people and a clerk made Bream swear a serving list, and he did it standing, and it took him eleven minutes, and it is the first thing he has ever sworn in his life, and it is a list of forty names, and he read them out himself, and he got one wrong, and the clerk corrected him, and he said the name again, and the name is a man of nineteen who pays a shilling a week and has paid it every week since he was fourteen.

He is in the record as a person who swore something. It is entered that he wrote down forty names, which is a different thing from a number, and the difference between the two is a fifth of a page and four hours of a stranger's day, and no court in this city has ever paid anybody for either, and the clerk entered the four hours as unpaid because that is what the district does.

And the clean instrument went back to a drawer in a weighhouse office with a salt cart's worth of work still on it, and Bel Ordway did not burn it and did not throw it away, and he asked a clerk to put a note on the back of it in his own hand: *made, not adopted. It is the best of the two and it is in a drawer, and a rule in a drawer is still a rule anybody can print.* And the clerk asked whether he wanted that on the front and he said no, because the front is for a rule and the back is for a man, and a rule that has a man's handwriting on the back is not a rule any more.

That is what it cost, by the morning: half a farthing on every bucket on Weir Lane, for ever, at a pump, read aloud by the person who cannot appeal; a debt of over a hundred thousand hours entered against a district that never kept hours; four props of a bridge four miles away not replaced this year, and an account carried, and a man of fifty in about two months finally asked what he wanted and given an answer nobody had a use for; a man who took a shilling a week off forty roofs for nineteen years reading forty names aloud in a room and getting one of them wrong; a page kept by a woman with a barrow who cannot appeal; and four pounds for a cart, and a clerk of thirty-one refusing to be relieved for the fifth time and not asking a sixth, and everything visible inside about a mile of a mud bank, and every one of it in a hand, and every one of it on a page, and not one of it secured.
