# Chapter 92: A Day in a Building With No Name On The Door

It rained on the fifth of the fifth month from about the fourth bell in the morning until the ninth at night, which was the longest stretch of rain anybody in that lane could remember in that month, and the lane at the low end of the street of counting-houses had been a place where carts went and not a place where anybody walked, and there was one person in it at the first bell in the morning, on his knees, with a broom.

Sera Quill got there at the first bell having left Orison before it, and she had walked four miles in the wet with her coat folded over her arm because there is nowhere in this city to put a coat, and she had spent the walk working out the order she was going to ask the four questions in, and she had worked out that the order did not matter, and had worked out that this was the first time in four months she had not been able to arrange a room before she got into it.

The man on the floor put the broom against the wall and stood up and looked at her, and he was about forty and had a grey in the beard on one side of it only, and he did not ask her what she wanted, which she had been told to expect and had not entirely believed until it happened.

“Volumes,” he said, “or the printed shelf.”

“I did not know there was a choice.”

“There is a choice,” he said, “and nobody takes the first one. I am Corrin Vask. I have swept this building for nine years and I am paid ninepence a day and I open the cold room for anybody who asks and nobody has ever asked for the volumes.” He said it in the voice of a man reporting a figure, and then he said the other part, and he said it looking at the rain going down the gutter rather than at her, and the other part was this: “I have wanted somebody to ask me that for nine years and I have never once said so to anybody and I have thought about it every single day of the nine years, and I have just said it, so you will know that I said it on purpose and not because of the weather.”

“All right,” said Sera Quill. “I am asking you about the volumes. Where are they?”

“Third shelf along the cold wall, and it is not the third shelf along the cold wall, it is the shelf you get by counting back from the window, and there is a difference and I will show you which.” He was already going. “It is a busy season for the shelf and a dull one for me and both of those things are wrong.”

---

The building was not shut and that turned out to be the whole of the resistance and about a fifth of what she had expected.

There was a door and the door was not locked, and behind it there was a room with a counter and a desk and a stamp on the desk and no clerk at it, and on the desk there was an incoming index whose last entry was four years old, and on the wall behind the desk there was nothing at all, no board and no list and no sign, because a standing authority of preservation does not need a sign. Any person may walk in. Any person may read any of what it holds on a day. It had been that way for four years and nobody had ever come, and she had now come and there was nobody in the building at all, and that was the second half of the resistance, because there is no person to ask and no person to refuse and no person to be refused by, and she had spent four months in a city where every refusal had a person attached to it and she had got used to being refused by faces.

The cold room was at the back and it was cold in the way that a thing is cold when it has been cold since the last time anybody made a fire in it, which was in the spring in somebody's memory and not hers. There were nineteen shelves in it and she counted them twice.

“The first thing,” said Vask, “is that I have to write it in the book, and the book is the day book, and it is the same book I write the sweeping in, and that is deliberate, and whoever started it meant it.”

“Why is that deliberate.”

“Because if it was a different book then the volumes would be a thing that happens upstairs on a day of its own, and if it is the same book then the volumes are a thing that happens on a day, with the sweeping on the same line, so that a person in twenty years can see what a page cost and what it cost next to.”

He wrote it in with a pencil, in a small hand that was not a clerk's hand, and it took him a moment to find the line because the line was the fourth of about eleven and he had to count.

> **THE FIFTH OF THIS MONTH, AT THE FIRST BELL. THE COLD ROOM OPENED. VOLUMES TAKEN DOWN BY A PERSON OF THIS CITY WITH A LICENCE AND NO NAME IN THIS BOOK BUT HER OWN, WHICH IS WRITTEN UNDER. A SHILLING FOR THE OPENING. THE SWEEPING OF THIS DAY IS NOT DONE AND NOBODY ELSE DOES IT AND THAT IS ENTERED.**

“Wait,” said Sera Quill. “Say that last part again.”

“The sweeping of this day is not done.”

“Whose sweeping.”

“Mine.” He looked at the line and then at her. “I am here until the sixth bell and then I go, and if I am not on the floors in the morning then the floors are not done, and there is one man for the floors and he is me, and the same man opens the cold room, and that is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement for nine years, and nobody has ever written a line about the volumes on that page before, and if a person reads that page in twenty years they will see that a page cost a shilling and a day, and those are two different prices and both of them are mine.”

“That is two prices and one of them is not money.”

“The one that is not money is the one nobody looks at,” said Vask.

---

Then he told her what the shilling was for, which was the key, and he told her it in the flat voice of a man explaining the price of something he has explained perhaps twice in nine years.

“It is not a fee the authority sets. The authority is a piece of paper and it does not set fees. It is what the man who opens the room has always been paid, out of the drawer, by whoever is stood in front of him, and the amount went up twice in nine years because twice the price of oil went up, and there is no minute and there is no bench and there is no order. There is a shilling in a drawer and a man who takes it or does not.”

“And if you do not take it.”

“Then the volumes stay on the shelf,” said Vask, “and I do not say that as a threat, I say it because you would find out in about a minute and I would rather you did not waste a day.”

She put a shilling on the counter and he took it and put it in the drawer and came back and wrote it in.

> **A SHILLING, TAKEN. THE NAME OF THE PERSON WHO PAID IT: S. QUILL, RECORDWRIGHT, AND IT IS WRITTEN BESIDE THE WORK AND NOT IN A COLUMN OF ITS OWN BECAUSE THERE IS ONE COLUMN AND IT IS FOR THE DAY.**

“Your hand is not very good,” she said, reading it upside down from where she stood.

“It is good enough for this book,” he said. “The book has been written by four men in nine years and two of them could not write at all. I can write. That is the whole of my qualification and I have never once been asked to state it.”

---

And then the reason she had come, and it took the rest of the morning, and it began with a shelf.

The third shelf counting back from the window, as he had said and not as she would have said it, and the volumes on it are not in any order that anybody in the building has ever explained to her, and the one she wanted was the fourth from the left, and she knew it was the fourth from the left because of a thing she had been told in a stone room off the second gallery by a man who had written his own name at the top of a page at the last bell of a night in February, which is the only way in this city that anybody learns where anything is: somebody opens a room and says so.

Vask got it down and put it on the table under the one shutter of light the cold room has, and it is a bound volume about the size of a family chest and it is about four hundred years old, and the boards are soft, and the thread in the spine is linen and it is cut in two places.

“The slot,” she said. “Which one is it.”

“There are three in this volume and every one of them is a hole with nothing in it and I have never known why and I have had nine years to find out and I have not found out.”

She had found out. The middle one, the second of three, is the slot, and the thread that goes through the fold above it has been cut, not frayed, and the mark beside it in the margin is a small number and a short stroke and a letter, and the letter and the stroke are in a hand that is not the hand the volume is written in, and the number is in the hand the volume is written in.

“That is the inventory mark,” she said, “and that is the original hand, and the letter beside it is in a newer hand than the book.”

“Then somebody has been in this book,” said Vask.

“Somebody has been in this book with a pen and has not been in it with a knife, and those are two different acts, and one of them is the ordinary work of a building that keeps books and the other one is a morning of a week in about four hundred years with a blade in it, and I cannot tell you which that mark is, and I have looked at it four times and it could be either, and I am not going to guess at it in a cold room and put it in a record.”

He took that better than she expected. “I have wanted somebody to say that to me for nine years,” he said, “and you have said it in eleven seconds and you have not even looked at me while you said it.”

---

What she had come for was on the leaf after the empty one.

A facing leaf is not part of a page. It is the page next door, and nobody has ever had a reason to look at the page next door to a page that is missing, which is the sentence she wrote in a record on the twentieth of the second month in this building in front of a clerk who wrote his own name and the hour at the top of the page before he let either of them in.

The first line of a removed page carries over onto the leaf behind it when the binder sets it. The pen goes through. The shadow is on the one after.

She had it. She had read it standing up with one finger on the twentieth of the second month and entered it and signed it and dated it, and it is in the record in a city four miles from this building, and it is a quarter of a thing and it begins with a lower-case *a*: *a refusal is entered, and the entry is against the burden and not against the person, and the entry is open to the ward.*

And four lines below it, on the same leaf, in the same shallow grey, there was something else.

She saw it at about the fourth bell. She had the lamp in her left hand and Vask had the leaf flat with the heel of his right hand, and she had the lamp low and raking the way you hold a lamp to a thing when you have been told there is nothing there, and there was something there, and it was not a word and it was not a line and it was about four inches long and it was the colour of the leaf.

“That is nothing,” said Vask, from about two feet away, after he had looked for longer than she had.

“It is four inches of something that is not nothing and it is four lines below a shadow I read eleven weeks ago, and if the first line came over then the second line came over, and it came over fainter because it is four lines further from the cut.”

“Why fainter.”

“Because the leaf it came off was set at an angle and the leaf behind it was not, and the further down you go the less the binder pressed. Or because there are more leaves between.” She put the lamp down. “Or because I have been wrong eleven weeks ago and what I read was the end of a line and not the whole of one, and the thing under it is the beginning of the next one, and either of those is the same problem and neither of them is a page.”

---

Here is the whole of the day, and it is a day like any other, and it is in the day book on the fourth line of the fifth.

The lamp is the building's lamp and it is the only one and it is at the counter and it is moved to the cold room and moved back, and a person standing in a cold room holding a lamp low for three hours has a right arm that is not going to be useful to her on the sixth day, and she worked that out at the second bell and did not write it down because it is hers and not the building's.

The leaf has to be flat. That is the whole of it. It is a leaf about the size of two hands and it is four hundred years old and the boards behind it are soft, and if the leaf lifts off the leaf below it by even the thickness of a nail the light goes through the gap and there is nothing there at all. Holding it flat with one hand for three hours turns a hand into a thing that cannot be trusted on the fifth day.

So the hand that held it flat was his. Corrin Vask held a four-hundred-year-old leaf flat with the heel of his right hand for three hours, and his right hand has gone at the wrist, and it has been gone for about six years, and he had not mentioned it and would not have mentioned it, and she found out because at the end of three hours he could not uncurl the fingers and had to uncurl them one at a time with his left.

“What is that,” she said.

“That is a hand,” he said.

“That is a hand that cannot open.”

“It opens,” he said. “It takes about nine seconds longer than it did in the spring. I have had six years of it and I have not asked anybody about it, and I would have gone on for another six, and you have found it in an afternoon by needing me to do something with it, and I am not going to be one of the people in this city who says a thing like that out loud twice.”

“You have now said it twice.”

“I have now said it to the one person,” he said, “who is going to write down what a page costs, and I would like it written down that I said it and that I said it on the fifth of this month, and not that somebody found it.”

---

And the price of the day, entered in the same book as the sweeping, in the way a clerk enters a price, which is with the name of the person who paid it and the hour and the reason.

> **THE FIFTH, THE FOURTH BELL TO THE SEVENTH. THREE HOURS WITH A LAMP LOW ON A LEAF OF THE VOLUME THAT IS THE QUIET CHAMBER'S. THE WORK WAS DONE BY TWO PERSONS AND THE LEAF WAS HELD BY THE MAN WHO SWEEPS THIS BUILDING, WHOSE RIGHT HAND IS SIX YEARS GONE AT THE WRIST AND WHO HELD IT FOR THREE HOURS AND COULD NOT OPEN IT AFTERWARDS WITHOUT DOING IT ONE FINGER AT A TIME.**
>
> **WHAT CAME OF IT IN THREE HOURS: THE SHADOW OF A SECOND LINE, FOUR LINES BELOW THE FIRST, ABOUT FOUR INCHES LONG, AND NOT ONE CHARACTER OF IT READ.**
>
> **THE PRICE OF THE DAY IS A SHILLING, WHICH IS PAID, AND A DAY, WHICH IS NOT PAID AND CANNOT BE. THE SWEEPING OF THIS BUILDING WAS NOT DONE TODAY. NOBODY DID IT. THERE IS ONE MAN FOR THE FLOORS AND HE IS THE MAN WITH THE LAMP.**
>
> **AND I AM WRITING THE PRICE HERE, IN THE SAME COLUMN AS THE WORK, BECAUSE I HAVE FOUND OUT IN NINE YEARS THAT A THING WITH NO PRICE IN IT LOOKS LIKE A SERVICE, AND THIS IS NOT A SERVICE, AND A RECORD THAT A READER CANNOT LEAVE UNTIL A PERSON HAS PAID FOR IT IS A DOOR, AND THIS BUILDING IS NOT A DOOR AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE ONE.**

She read that over twice and made him read the fourth line twice, and he did, and his hand was still open on the table, and she was not going to be the person who told him to close it.

“Who else is in this book with me,” she said.

“Four men in nine years and two women and one of the women was here for a fortnight,” said Vask. “And a clerk from a chamber with a case number on a note, at the last bell of a night in the month before last, and he wrote his name and the hour at the top of the page before he let anybody in. And you. That is all of it and there is not a great deal of it for nine years.”

“Nobody has ever asked me anything,” he said, not as a complaint. “That is what I wanted to say at the start and I have said it badly and late. Not the volumes. Any of it. I have been nine years in a room that holds four hundred years of what people agreed to and I have swept it, and I know the order of the shelves and I know which one has a mouse in it, and nobody has ever stood where you are standing and said, *Vask, what is on that shelf*, and I have wanted it for nine years, and I have never once asked anybody for it, and I did not know until about two minutes ago that I had never once asked anybody for it.”

Nobody said anything for a moment, and the rain came down the gutter on the other side of the wall.

“Then I am going to ask you,” said Sera Quill, “on the sixth, and you are going to have to think about it, because I am not going to ask you in an afternoon while you are holding a leaf. The question is going to cost you nothing and it is going to be in a book, because that is the only kind of question in this city that anybody can do anything with, and I am not going to put your name in a book for a thing you have not agreed to.”

“I have agreed to it,” said Vask. “I have agreed to it nine times and nobody asked me.”

“You have not agreed to it at all and I am going to find that out on the sixth and it is going to take longer than three hours.”

---

Ilyan Vester was at the low end of that lane for the whole day, and he was not in the building and could not have been.

He had a right to be in the building in the sense that anybody has a right to be in a building, and no other right at all, and the reason he could not go in was not the door and not the price. It was that he is a person in no district and the volumes are not open to a person in no district, and a recordwright's licence does not carry a man who is not a party, and there is a word for that in this city and the word is not a technicality, it is the actual reason, and the actual reason did not change because it was raining.

So he stood in a doorway about ninety yards down the lane with the rain going past him, and he had nothing to do, and there is a thing about a body of his that is only true when he is carrying something of his own, and he has not been carrying anything of his own since the twenty-sixth of the fourth month, and by the third bell the leg had gone from the hip in about four seconds and had not come back, and the low water was in his nose in two layers, the day over the night and the flat over the water, and under both of them the thing that has never been a smell, which is a picture of a room about two hundred feet off with a table the size of a table a hand could go on, a lamp with the wick down, and a window with weather on the wrong side of it, and four people at the table, one of them about forty-four with her back to him and her right foot up on something.

The door in the picture was at the same height and the same distance it has been at since the third of the second month. It has not moved once in ninety days and he has not gone looking for it, and he was not going to go looking for it in a lane in the rain four miles from a bridge with a day of somebody else's in his hands.

At about the fifth bell he sat down on a step because standing in a doorway for two hours in the wet is a thing a body ends, and at the sixth bell he found that he could not get up quickly, and he got up in about eleven seconds, and nobody saw him do it and nobody was asked to.

He had four hours in the day and it is a limit and not a rule, and it does not matter what he is doing; what matters is whether he is carrying something of his own, and on the fifth of the fifth month he was not, and it ran the whole afternoon and into the evening and it was worse at eight in the evening than it had been at two in the afternoon, and there is no line anywhere in this city on which a man can be paid for standing in a doorway in the rain for a person who is inside.

At about the seventh bell Corrin Vask came out of the building and stood at the end of the lane and looked for the man in the doorway, and found him, and did not come over, and waited about four minutes, and then went back inside, because he had ninepence a day and the sweeping had not been done and it was getting late.

And the reason he waited is that he had noticed, at some point in the afternoon, that the man in the doorway was in the doorway for a person who was inside, and that is the same arrangement as the day book with the price on the same line as the work, and he had not understood it in the morning and he understood about two thirds of it in the evening, and the third of it he is going to be asked about on the sixth.

---

What came of the fifth of the fifth month is this, and it is the whole of it, and there is no more.

There is a second shadow. It is on the leaf after the empty slot, four lines below the shadow of the first line, about four inches long, and in three hours with a lamp held low and a leaf held flat it did not yield one character.

It is not going to yield a character in an afternoon. It is going to take days, and a lamp, and a hand, and an opening, and an opening is a shilling, and a shilling is a day of a man who sweeps floors, and the man who sweeps the floors cannot be in two places.

And there is, in the same building, behind a counter with a stamp on it, a series of printed instruments running by plate, and any person may read any of it on a day, and no person has ever asked.

And the four questions she has written out are about paper and not about a person, and three of them are the ordinary questions anybody would ask a desk that keeps print, and the fourth one is a question about what a thing is for, and she is going to put it anyway, and she has already decided that if the answer is no without a reason then she is going to say so in public with a date on it.

The rain stopped at about the ninth bell and she walked the four miles back and the leg was hers for the first mile and then it was not, and she has written in her own book, in her own hand, with a date on it, four lines that are in no record but hers.

> **THE ANSWER TO WHAT A PAGE IS FOR IS NOT IN THE PAGE. IT IS FOUR HUNDRED YEARS OLD, IT IS ON A LEAF, AND I HAVE HAD IT IN MY HAND FOR THREE HOURS AND I HAVE GOT FOUR INCHES OF IT AND NOT ONE CHARACTER, AND IT COST A MAN A DAY OF HIS SWEEPING AND A HAND HE CANNOT OPEN, AND I HAVE ENTERED THE PRICE IN HIS OWN BOOK BESIDE THE WORK BECAUSE A THING WITH NO PRICE IN IT LOOKS LIKE A SERVICE.**
>
> **A DESK IN THIS CITY CAN PROVE THAT A RULE WAS PRINTED AND CANNOT HOLD A PERSON AND HAS SAID SO TWICE IN THE SAME HAND. I AM GOING TO ASK IT A THIRD TIME AND IT IS NOT ABOUT A PERSON.**
>
> **AND I AM NOT ASKING IT BECAUSE I THINK IT CAN ANSWER. I AM ASKING IT BECAUSE A SHELF IS THE LAST THING IN THIS CITY THAT HAS NOT ALREADY TOLD ME NO, AND A PERSON WHO STOPS ASKING THE PLACES THAT HAVE TOLD HER NO IS A PERSON WHO HAS DECIDED THAT NO IS AN ANSWER, AND IT IS NOT, IT IS A WALL, AND I HAVE HAD four hundred years of a wall in my hand this afternoon and I have not read one word of it and I am still not going to call it an answer.**

The last line has a fault in it, which is that the word four is set a size larger than the rest of the line, because she was walking and it is dark, and she has not gone back over it, and there is a line under it in her own hand that says: a fault in the last line, in the size of the four, and I have left it, and I am writing this down because I do not want to be the sort of recordwright who goes back over her own writing when nobody is looking.
