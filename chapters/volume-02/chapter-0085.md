# Chapter 85: The Second Ask, Past the Fourth Mile

The third member came back from the boundary on the evening of the tenth of the fourth month, wet through, at about the eighth bell, and Perditta Nye had been sitting in the room over the shop that had been a chandler's for two hours with a folded sheet on the table in front of her, and she had not sealed it, and he came in and looked at it and at her and said, "That is the one I was on the hedge about."

"Yes," said Nye. "It is the only one I have written since the eighteenth of March. It is a sheet of our paper with four names and a mark on it and a wedge and chain that I have not pressed yet, and it asks another district four miles off this one to send us a charter, and it is a day and a half of a cart and we have nine pounds in the year and four and six of the day is mine because the other four and three is the boundary money and the boundary has to be done.

"Read it," he said, and read it, standing, with the rain off him on the flags, and then he said, "Send it."

"You have not read the line about the cart."

"I have read the line about the cart," he said. "It is our money and the cart is our day and if we do not send it then in four years there is a room over a shop with a rule in it and nobody in this district who knows what a charter is, and I am fifty-eight and I have been on this bench twenty-two years and I have never once in twenty-two years sent a thing to a place I could not walk to, and I have no idea what to do about it and I would like to send it anyway, and you may enter that I said *and I would like to* out loud."

"Then I shall enter it," said Nye, and pressed the wedge and chain into the wax at about the ninth bell on the tenth of the fourth month, and it went down the lane on the eleventh at about the first bell in the hands of a drover who was going that way anyway, and it was four names and a mark and a seal and one sheet.

> **TO THE HOLDER OF THE TERMS OF THE CHARTER OF THE DISTRICT OF HALLOWAY, WHICH THE BENCH OF THREE OF FALLOWDITCH HAS HEARD NAMES A PERSON AND DOES NOT KNOW HIS NAME.**
>
> **THE BENCH OF THREE OF THIS DISTRICT ASKS FOR THE TERMS. THE BENCH HAS A ROOM AND A RULE IN IT AND FOUR ENTRIES AND NOTHING IN THE ROOM THAT WAS WRITTEN BY A PERSON WHO DID NOT WRITE IT IN THIS ROOM, AND A ROOM WITH NOTHING IN IT CLOSES.**
>
> **THE BENCH HAS NINE POUNDS IN THE YEAR AND THE CART WAS FOUR AND SIX AND THE REST WAS WALKED. THE BENCH PAYS THE FOUR AND SIX OUT OF THE THREE OF US AND ENTERS IT, BECAUSE A DAY IS PAID OUT OF THE THREE OF US AND THAT IS IN OUR RULE IN OUR OWN HAND AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO BE A BENCH THAT SAYS IT IN ONE PLACE AND NOT THE OTHER.**
>
> **THE BENCH ADOPTS NOTHING. THE BENCH IS NOT A DISTRICT THAT HAS A CARTER AND THE BENCH HAS READ NOBODY'S PAGES.**

Corva Silt read it in the room over the pump at the second bell on the eleventh and put it down on the table and looked at the seal for a while.

“Eleven miles from the fen road,” she said. “That is not four.”

“No,” said Ilyan.

“It is a bench of three with a rule it made itself four days ago and four entries in it, one of which is a page nobody may swear to from this district's own road fund, and it is asking for the terms of a charter it has read once and cannot come back for.” She turned the sheet over. “I know exactly what that is. That is Nethergill again, and it is not Nethergill, and if you walk up there this time with nine sheets in your hand you are going to be a man who went twice.”

---

The licence says three things that matter here, and Sera Quill said all three of them in about nine sentences, standing in a stone room off the second gallery with a tray in her hand, and she was not being difficult and she was not refusing.

“Third term. I carry the terms of the matter to the review and to any other district that asks. Fallowditch asked, in writing, with a seal, and the ask is valid on its face, and it is the second time the third term has been used and both times it was used by a district that could not pay for what it asked for.

“What I may not do is send you. I am expressly not your advocate and I am not going to put a man in a cart in the character of a recordwright's carrier, because a recordwright's carrier is a thing I am and not a thing I hand to the man the fourth term names. So the ask is mine to carry and not yours, and if I carry it you are not obliged to come, and if you come you come because a clause with your name in it says you come, and those are two different sentences and I am not going to let anybody in a ledger in a year’s time read the second as the first.”

“Which one is it.”

“Your name is in the second half of the fourth term,” said Sera Quill. “The terms are carried to any district that asks, at your own cost and without pay, and if he refuses, or is unable, or is not within four miles, the terms are not carried and the appeal is stayed until another carrier is sworn in that district. That sentence is mine. I wrote the consequence deliberately and I entered that I chose it and I gave the reason: a clause that cannot be broken is a post, and you had been a post for a month and it was making you a gate.

“Fallowditch is not within four miles of this court. So nothing happens. If you do not go, the ask is not met, and the appeal in the district of Halloway is not stayed by that, because the appeal in Halloway is not stayed by an ask from Fallowditch. It is simply an ask that was not met, and the reason it was not met is in a ledger with a date on it, and in four years somebody will read it and see that a district eleven miles off asked for something and a man of thirty-one was not near enough, and that is a true sentence and it is the whole of what the fourth term of a licence costs me and you both.”

He walked out to Fallowditch two days later, on the thirteenth, and it took him most of the day, and the leg stopped about two hundred yards short of the shop that had been a chandler's. It had been his own for the first six miles of them. Past the fourth mile it was a favour, and a favour cannot be entered. He has never been paid for any of it and is not going to be.

---

Fallowditch's room is over a shop that had been a chandler's, eleven miles off the fen road, and there are two places in the district and the far one of them is four miles from the shop, and about two hundred and forty people between them, and there is a rule in the bench's book in its own hand with a date on it and three of the four who wrote it are in the room and the fourth is out on a boundary and did not sign it and has said nothing.

Perditta Nye read the nine sheets out.

She read them well. She read them the way a clerk of a bench reads a document that has come into her room, which is word for word, from the top of the first sheet to the bottom of the ninth, with the dates and the seals and the marks spoken where they are spoken and not at the end. It took about fifty minutes and nobody interrupted her once, and when she came to the end of the fifth term she came to the end of the fifth term and then she stopped.

“There is a line under it,” she said.

“There is not,” said Ilyan.

“There is. It is not in the sheet.”

He looked. It was not in the sheet. It was in the copy, at the foot of the ninth, under the fifth term, in the same hand as a good deal of the small writing on the copy and not in the same ink.

> **CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD AGAINST THE HOLDER.**

He had read that on the twenty-seventh of last month, at the ninth bell, at a counter at a span, in about forty people's hearing, standing in the open air. The line itself was in a copy and not in the air, and a trialwright of eleven years had read it twice and refused it in the same four words and said he would refuse it again on the sixth time and on the seventh.

He did not say one word about it.

“Nye,” he said. “I am going to tell you three things and then I am going to ask you to do something, and the third thing is the difficult one and I want it done properly.”

“You are a man with limited standing in a matter this court is four miles from and this room is not in,” said Nye. “You cannot tell me to do anything. Say them and I will decide.”

“That line is not in the sheet. It is in this copy. I do not know who put it there and I am not going to tell you a theory and I am not going to let anybody use me as the man who knows. It has been refused five times by a trialwright of this court, in the same words, and he will refuse it a sixth time when he gets it, and it will not be decided then and it will not be decided now.

“I cannot rule on it. I have no power over it. I have no standing in this room at all and if I had one I would not use it on a line I did not enter, because that is the only procedure that man has and it is a good one and I would not take it off him by doing it for him.

“What I want is that you do what a clerk does. You have read it out in a room in public. Enter that you read it out. Enter the words. Enter that it is not in the sheet. Enter that you do not know whose hand it is. And then write it to the clerk of the chamber of the First Verdict, in your own hand, with your name and your district and the date, and put nothing in it that asks for anything, because nothing can be done about it and I would not want anybody to think they could wait for an answer.”

---

The line took twenty days to come anywhere, because that is what a thing with nobody waiting for it does.

Nye wrote it the same afternoon, in her own hand, on a sheet of her own bench's paper, and sealed it, and the seal is a wedge-and-chain and there is one of them in the room. She put it in a handbag and it went down to the fen road on the twelfth with a man going to a market, and it sat on a tray in a chamber in Orison for twenty days with nothing on it asking anything, and on the second of the fifth month a clerk who was copying a list of a board picked it up because it was face up, and it was stamped that day, and it went into a file with a number on the spine, and the number is the only thing that happened to it.

---

And the thing that came out of the nine sheets was not the line.

It came at about the eleventh bell, when Nye had finished reading, and she asked the question that three years in the post and nine days of her own rule had put in her mouth, and she asked it standing up, in front of her own bench, with a sheet of paper in her hand.

“You said, when you were in the cart, that a bench that keeps records of persons is a bench. That is out of a letter and it is not yours and I have got it off a shelf in a room over a pump where a woman of twenty-nine has a page and four entries on it, and the letter says an office with no person in it is a shelf and cannot hold a person. So I have been a clerk for three years and on the ninth of this month I read a rule into my own book in my own room that says any person of this district may bring a document here, and it shall be read out, and an entry made, and the entry shall have a date and the name of the person it is about.

“That makes me a person in this district who keeps entries about persons,” said Perditta Nye. “And there is one other person in this district who can read, and it is me, and the coal man cannot read, and the third member is fifty-eight and reads the weather and not a book, and the man who came about the folded sheet cannot read, and Adela Cray can read about four words together. I am a holder of a room nobody made me a holder of.”

Nobody in that room had an answer, and she did not want one, and she went on for about a minute.

“My bench’s ordinary work has stopped,” she said. “The fourth term of my own rule says the day is paid out of the three of us out of our own purses at no rate, and I wrote that in a hurry on the ninth to keep a ledger honest, and it has cost this bench eleven days of a boundary in three weeks. The boundary is a hedge and a water and a hundred and forty-one years of somebody else’s argument and it is the only thing this bench does that anybody outside it wants, and it is not going to be done this spring. I have entered that. I entered it on the eleventh and I entered that the reason is a room over a shop that was a chandler’s and not a hedge.”

“Then you have made yourself a door,” said Ilyan, “with no room behind it.”

“I have made myself a door,” said Nye, “in a district of about two hundred and forty people eleven miles from the fen road, and there is no appeal in my room, and no rate in my room, and no charter in my room, and if a man in this district with a document in his hand wants to go somewhere with it there is nothing to go to, and I do not know whether a door in a room with nothing in it is better or worse than a room that has publicly said it holds nothing, and I have been three years a clerk and I would like somebody to tell me, and the honest answer is that nobody in this city knows, and the dishonest answer is the one I would like.”

---

He said it to her at the door of the shop that had been a chandler's, with the nine sheets rolled under his arm and the afternoon going, and he said the one thing he had come eleven miles past the fourth mile to say and he said it badly because he has two hundred guild words and none of them are these.

“You did not invent it,” he said. “That is what I have got. I have been in this city four months and I have watched every bad thing in it arrive by copying, and this did not arrive by copying. There is no other instance. A bench of three that had publicly said in its own hand that it holds nothing pulled that sentence up and made it false, and then built a room, and the room is eleven miles off a fen road and four miles off its own far end, and it holds four entries and it cannot do one single thing with any of them, and the third member has been out on a hedge since the rule was written.

“And I want to tell you what that costs and then I am going to walk back down your road. It costs a day and it costs a hedge, and the day is Fallowditch’s and the hedge is Fallowditch’s, and nobody in a chamber in a city is going to enter either of them. And in four years somebody in a book is going to write *Fallowditch* against *durability* in a column, and it is going to be because of a woman of forty with a fortnight’s notice and a coal man who wants to be unnamed, and neither of them will know what it did for them, and I would like you to hear that from me now, at a door, once, and put it wherever you like.”

“What will you tell them,” said Nye, “when they ask you at a review whether the appeal in this case survives?”

“That I do not know,” said Ilyan Vester. “I have been in this city four months and I have learned one thing about a rule and I am going to say it at a door because there is nowhere better to say it. There is one man in this city that four hundred people can be sent for, and he is not a bench and he cannot make anybody answer, and about four hundred people have never once been told what happens to that when he cannot walk. It is not in a charter. It is not in a bench's book. It is not in the nine sheets I carried up a lane on the thirty-first of the second month, and I have read every line of those that I can read, which is not as many as I would like, and it is not in them. I do not know what happens. That is not a complaint. I am telling you because you have just made yourself a person in a district who keeps entries, and if a page has no successor then neither has a bench of three, and I would like somebody in this room to have heard it from a man who is the thing itself.”

Nye looked at him for a moment.

“That is the third time,” she said, “that a person in this room has been told something they could not have known, and twice it has been you, and I would like it minuted that a man walked eleven miles past the point at which he was obliged to and then said out loud, at a door, the thing he actually came across the county to say. Enter that, if you want it, and it will be a fourth entry and it is the only one of the four that is a man talking.”

“No,” he said. “Do not enter me. I am not in this room.”

---

He came down out of Fallowditch and then the eleven miles to the fen road with the leg his own for six of them and then it went, and he stopped twice on the second of the eleven and the second time he sat down on a bank at about the eighth bell and found that he could not get up quickly, and that there was a low water in his nose in two layers with a third under both of them, and a door in the picture at the same height and the same distance it has been at since the third of the second month, and a woman of about forty-four at a table with her back to him and her right foot up on something.

And on the third of the fourth month, a keeper of the ninth bridge was nineteen miles off at the cut banks, at the second bell, standing on a stone with a man of twenty-two, saying a thing in his own mouth that was not going in a letter. And nobody could have said in a chamber in Orison, on the fourteenth of the fourth month, that a man of thirty-one had spent a day and a half walking to a bench of three so that a bench of three could hear a charter read out that it had not adopted, and that in the same week another man had walked nineteen miles to tell a man of twenty-two the truth in his own mouth. Neither of those things was in anybody's record. One of them was going to be, and the one that was going to be was the smaller one.

And in a clerk's own book in that chamber, on the twenty-eighth of March, with a date on it, there is a sentence very like the thing he said at the door of the shop, and it is in no record but hers, and nobody in this case has read it, including the man who said the thing.
