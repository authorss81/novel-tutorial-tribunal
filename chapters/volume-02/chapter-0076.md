# Chapter 76: What Thirty Years Is Worth, Wrongly

The bench of four had to set a rate, and to set a rate for a debt it had to know what the debt was, and to know what the debt was it had to have a figure, and there was no figure in the district, because nobody had ever counted anything.

That is the whole of the ninth of the month and it took a clerk of thirty-one about four minutes to say it and a bench of four about a quarter of an hour to hear it.

“The thirty years is entered as owed by this district and is not paid,” said Corva Silt, with the entry open in front of her on the table in the district’s own hand, “and it is entered as a shape and not a figure, because the gate hand refused to have it counted and the court entered the refusal and entered that a debt which has never been counted is not a small debt. And I cannot carry a shape. A sum enters a shape and adds it up and gets a number, and then the number goes on a rate, and a rate goes on a barrel lid, and about two hundred people read it. So I want a number and I cannot get one out of the woman and I am not going to try again because she has said no in front of a bench and in front of a field and in her own hand on a page.”

“Can it be carried without a number,” said Nell Ambrey, who was sixty and whose sanitation complaint was not heard this year.

“It can be carried without a number,” said Corva Silt. “It has been carried for two days without a number. It will be carried at the next sitting without a number and at the one after that, and the shape will be in the book, and nobody will ever be able to say what it is worth, and that is a way of being owed money for ever.”

Oakes said, “Then somebody has to count her.”

Nobody in the room said the name, and it was in the room anyway, because a bench of four that has entered a debt of thirty years against itself and cannot enter a figure has exactly one person in this city who has counted any part of it, and he was sitting on a stool by the fire with his left hand bandaged in a strip off a shirt and a slate under his arm.

“I have got six days of it,” he said. “Six days of a working tide counted by the gate hand calling her own hours and me writing them, from the sixth of last month to the eleventh, on a slate with a line down the middle of it, in my figures, which are a load-carrier's and are wrong by about four minutes in any hour, and the court has entered that a person is not a measuring instrument.”

“Then multiply it up,” said Oakes.

“No,” he said. “I have not got a licence and I have not got a rate and I have got a hundred and thirteen thousand four hundred hours if I do it, and I would like to say the three things that are wrong with that before I do it, because if I do it wrong in a bench's book it cannot be taken out.”

---

The first wrong thing was the tide, and Bryn Sallow gave it to him and he did not have to ask.

He had it on the slate in six days of a working tide of ten and a half hours, and the ten and a half was the tide of the turn, at the sixth bell, when the water is coming and the flat is walkable and the gate is half up, and it is the longest working tide of the seven, and it is one day in seven.

“A seven-day tide,” said Bryn Sallow, from the step, without being asked and in the voice she uses for a machine. “You have got the turn. You have had the turn six days running and the turn is a day in seven and the other six are not the turn, and the sixth runs at about eleven and a quarter in the middle of the spring and the fourth at about seven and a quarter at the neaps, and the week comes to about sixty-three hours and it is not sixty-three every week but it is close and it has been close since I was a girl.”

He wrote it down. He had not known that. He had six days of a tide of ten and a half hours and he had had the idea that a tide is a thing that happens all the time.

“Say it again slower and I will write the week.”

“No,” she said. “You will write what you have written and I will say it as many times as you want and it will be my words on your figures, and in four years somebody will be able to say the woman supplied the tide and the man supplied the hours and neither of them can be checked.”

He used it. Sixty-three hours a week against seventy-three and a half, over thirty years, took sixteen thousand two hundred hours out of the figure, and the figure came down by two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings, and he said that number out loud in a room over a pump in front of a bench of four and did not know what it was until Hollis Dune, who had walked seven miles and was in the room, said it.

“Two years of a lane,” said Hollis Dune. “Forty roofs at a shilling a week is a hundred and four pounds a year, and that is two years of it, off the top of a figure that was wrong by that much and got put in a book in a quarter of an hour. Weir Lane is four miles the other side of this bridge and it has nothing to do with any of it, and I want it entered that I used this district’s own figures and did not use Weir Lane’s, because a hundred and four pounds a year is a figure that exists in more places than this one.”

“Yes,” he said.

The second wrong thing was the rate, and he could not fix it, and he said so.

“The district's own rate is a third of a haul rate, and the rate for the balance of the tide was set on the eighteenth of the last month for six and a half hours a day, and that comes out at a day's figure of nineteen and a halfpence. And I have priced ten and a half hours a day for thirty years at threepence the hour, and threepence the hour is not a rate this district has. It is nineteen and a halfpence divided by the six and a half hours of a tide the district does not pay for, and I have used a rate that only exists because of the unpaid half. I can show you that it is a wrong shape. I cannot tell you how wrong it is, because there is no rate in this city from thirty years ago. There is no rate in this city from last year. The third of a haul rate in this district has not moved since the year a woman on this street had her mother buried, and she hauls two hundred of them a year and she is the only person who can tell you that, and she told a bench a fortnight ago and it went in the book.

“So the figure prices thirty years of work at one month's rate, and the error is the whole of thirty years of everything else, and it is not a number I can put a sign on. It is either a great deal more or it is nothing and I cannot tell you which and neither can anybody in this room, and it is going in the book anyway, and I would like the book to say that the man who wrote it said that first.”

The third wrong thing was the days. Thirty years of three hundred and sixty was a number he made up, and there is no record of the days on which the gate hand stood at a gate in thirty years, and there is a hundred and sixty-year-old book in this district that calls the culvert road a penal road and has never once had a column in it for a day anybody worked.

---

Corva Silt entered it at the fourth bell on the tenth in the district’s own hand, and she entered it in three parts because there were three hands in the room, and the third hand was his, and the third hand is the whole of the difficulty.

> **THE THIRTY YEARS ENTERED AS OWED BY THIS DISTRICT IS ENTERED NOW, FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ITS EXISTENCE, AS A FIGURE, AND THE FIGURE IS NOT AGREED BY THE PERSON IT IS OWED TO AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR BY HER AND WAS NOT WITHDRAWN BY HER.**
>
> **THE FIGURE IS A HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED HOURS, AT THREEPENCE THE HOUR, WHICH IS ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS. IT IS SIX YEARS OF THE ROAD FUND OF THIS DISTRICT AND THIRTY-TWO YEARS OF THE COST OF ITS CHARTER.**
>
> **THE FIGURE IS WRONG. THE HOURS ARE SIXTEEN THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED TOO MANY BECAUSE THE WORKING TIDE OF THE TURN IS ONE DAY IN SEVEN AND THE WEEK IS ABOUT SIXTY-THREE HOURS AND NOT SEVENTY-THREE AND A HALF. THAT CORRECTION WAS GIVEN BY B. SALLOW, AT THE GATE, UNASKED, AND IS ENTERED AS HERS. THE RATE IS WRONG AND THE CORRECTION FOR THE RATE CANNOT BE FOUND IN ANY DISTRICT IN THIS CITY AND IS NOT ENTERED AS A NUMBER.**
>
> **THE FIGURE WAS NOT WRITTEN BY A CLERK. IT WAS WRITTEN IN THE FIGURES OF A LOAD-CARRIER BY A PERSON WHO IS NOT A CLERK AND IS NOT A PARTY AND IS A WITNESS IN THIS MATTER ONLY BY BEING ASKED A QUESTION BY A BENCH OF FOUR, AND WHO HAS SAID, BEFORE IT WAS WRITTEN, THAT THE FIGURE IS WRONG IN A RATE HE CANNOT CORRECT.**
>
> **THE PERSON THE FIGURE IS OWED TO HAS REFUSED IT. SHE HAS NOT BEEN COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE OVERRULED, AND THE BENCH HAS NO POWER OVER THE FIGURE AND THE COURT HAS NO POWER OVER THE DEBT OF THIS DISTRICT, AND THE FIGURE STANDS IN THE BOOK AND BINDS NOBODY.**

It is entered. It is the fifth entry of that day's book and it is the only one in this case that a person has entered against the correctness of a number on the same page as the number.

---

He asked her afterwards, on the bank, at the sixth bell, with the water coming, and he asked it the only way he knew how, which was badly.

“They have entered a figure of a thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings against your district and it is in a book a bench can come back with, and you refused it, and your refusal is in the book too, and both of those things are true at once and I do not know what to do about either of them.”

“I know what to do about them,” said Bryn Sallow. “I am going to do nothing, and you are going to watch me do nothing, and here it is.”

She worked the gate. The water came up the flat and forty or fifty people went up the bank in a line and a man with a barrow went down and came up, and she stood with one foot on the sill-stone and one on the bank and worked an oak gate on a frame with about two hundred feet of a chain on it, and he stood at the gate side where a man stands who is not a gate hand and does not want to be one.

“A number is a claim,” she said. “A claim wants somebody to answer it. I have gone thirty years without being a claim to anybody, not because I am owed nothing, because I have been the ward, and a ward is a duty and a duty cannot be answered. And you and a bench of four and a magistrate have just made me a claim. A thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings, with my name nowhere on it and the hours on a slate with a line down the middle of it that only exists when a man from a bridge is standing on this bank.”

“I know.”

“You do not know the half of it. The rate in that book is a rate a bench of four set on the eighteenth of the last month for six and a half hours a day, and out of the four who set it two of them live on Weir Lane and one of them is the man I have bought salt off since I was nineteen, and I have just been made a claim of a thousand four hundred pounds by four people, and three of them are going to have to live in this district afterwards, and two of them are going to be asked about it every quarter for the rest of their lives by a woman with a barrow at a pump.”

She stopped working the gate for about four seconds, which she does not do.

“And the part that is going to be in a clerk's book in four years and that I cannot do one thing about is not the thousand four hundred pounds,” she said. “It is that the figure is in it and the refusal is in it, on the same page, in two hands. And in about two years somebody from a bench in Orison is going to read that page and it is going to be evidence that this district admitted a debt of thirty years and refused to pay it. And there is going to be a book somewhere with my hours in a load-carrier's figures in it, and the hours are wrong, and the tide I gave you is right, and neither of those facts is going to be in the same room.

“I do not want it. I have said no for thirty years and I am saying it again now with a number attached to it, and you may enter that I said it with a number attached to it, because I have entered that I said it without one, and somebody in four years is going to need both.”

“Both are in.”

“Then that is the whole of it,” said Bryn Sallow, “and I have to ask you for one thing, and it is not a favour and it is a job.”

“Ask.”

“Come and count again,” she said. “From the fifth bell. And keep the line down the middle of the slate. And do not tell me the number, because I will do it myself at the quarter, from the figures, and if your figures and mine are not the same then one of us is wrong and I would rather it was you than the bench, because the bench has a seal and you have a bad leg and a head off a shirt.”

He said he would, and he did, on the eleventh, the twelfth and the thirteenth, and the afterimage was his own for about three hours and a half of the first day and less of the second, and on the thirteenth it stopped at two hours and would not clear at all, and she wrote the figures herself on the back of the district's day book in her own hand on the fourteenth and compared them, and they were four minutes apart in a four-hour day, and she put the two sets of figures side by side on a page and wrote under them, in her own hand:

> **TWO FIGURES, BOTH HONEST, BOTH WRONG A LITTLE. THE LINE DOWN THE MIDDLE OF THE SLATE STAYS. A PERSON IS NOT A MEASURING INSTRUMENT. I HAVE SAID THAT ON A FIELD AND IT GOES ON A PAGE AND IT IS STILL TRUE.**

---

And on the fourteenth of the month, in a stone room off the second gallery, he entered the sixth thing he could not un-enter, and he entered it himself, in his own hand, and he entered the size of it, because a clerk in four years will assume they are all the same size and they are not.

> **THE SIXTH THING THIS CLAIMANT HAS ENTERED INTO THIS CITY'S RECORD WHICH HE CANNOT WITHDRAW, NAMED WITH ITS SIZE SO THAT A CLERK DOES NOT ASSUME THEY ARE THE SAME.**
>
> **THE FIRST FOUR ARE AS STATED BEFORE AND HE HAS NOT GONE BACK TO THEM.**
>
> **THE FIFTH IS THE CARRIER'S CLAUSE, WHICH HE ACCEPTED IN NINE WORDS AND WHICH NAMES HIM IN A DISTRICT THAT DID NOT ASK FOR A CARRIER.**
>
> **THE SIXTH IS A FIGURE OF THIRTY YEARS' WORK ENTERED INTO A DISTRICT'S BOOK WITHOUT THE CONSENT OF THE PERSON IT IS OWED TO, WHO REFUSED IT AND WHO WAS NOT COMPELLED AND CANNOT BE COMPELLED AND WAS NOT OVERRULED, AND IT IS WRONG BY AT LEAST TWO HUNDRED AND TWO POUNDS AND TEN SHILLINGS AND BY AN AMOUNT HE CANNOT NAME, AND IT IS A FIGURE HE WROTE IN HIS OWN FIGURES, AND NOBODY HAS EVER PAID HIM FOR IT AND NOBODY WILL, AND THE ONLY THING HE CAN OFFER AGAINST THE WRONGNESS IS THAT HE SAID IT WAS WRONG BEFORE HE DID IT.**
>
> **HE IS NOW A WITNESS IN ONE MORE MATTER IN A DISTRICT HE DOES NOT LIVE IN, AND A WITNESS CAN BE PRODUCED, AND HE WAS ASKED THE QUESTION BY A BENCH OF FOUR AND HE ANSWERED IT, AND HE DID NOT NEED TO ANSWER IT, AND HE WOULD ANSWER IT AGAIN.**
>
> **NOBODY HAS THANKED HIM. HE DID NOT ASK. HE ASKS THAT IT BE ENTERED THAT HE DID NOT ASK.**

Sera Quill read it back and did not tidy it and did not put her own name against it, and then she said the thing she says about four terms and a stove that does not draw.

“There are now six, and you have entered six of them yourself in my record in twelve weeks, and I have entered that you did so voluntarily, and in four years a clerk is going to read a record in which a man has put six things against his own name in a fortnight and has never once been paid, and is going to decide that he is a careful man, and he is not, and I know that, and you know that I know, and that is going to be the entry in my book that I am least able to defend in front of my own trade in six years.”

“Yes,” he said.

“I am not going to soften it,” she said, “because I signed a licence with four terms and the first of them says no withdrawal without an entry naming the reason, and this is not a withdrawal. But I am going to say the reason out loud, once, and then I am going to go and be very good at a schedule I have to hand in nine days. A man who enters six things against his own name is not a careful man. He is a man who has nobody to put them in except a recordwright who is not his advocate and has not been anything else, and who is frightened that if he waits for a better room to say them in he will not say them at all, and who is right to be frightened and wrong about the reason.”

He did not answer that, and she let it stand, and the reason it stood is in the fourth term of a licence and neither of them ever talks about the fourth term.
