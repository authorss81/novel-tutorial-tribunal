# Chapter 476: The First Of The Majority To Find Out What It Is Worth, A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Keeps A Sluice And Does Not Want To Be A Person With A Claim Is Named In A Minute She Did Not Agree To, For The Second Time, And Says It In A Square, And The Board Of Nine Expels Nineteen Houses Out Of The Water Of The Hallow Correctly, Under Clause Five, On A Cause Set Out In Writing By A Man Of Sixty-One, In The Words Of A Book That Has Been Off That Beam Twice

Second day of the ninetieth week. His two hundred and twenty-sixth morning. Seventy-six days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and four days. Thirty days since the division. Twenty-four days in this town.

The water board of Halloway sits in a room over a saddler’s shop at the north end of the square. There were about thirty people in the room and on the stair on the Wednesday morning of the thirtieth day after the division, and the stair is the width of one man, and about nine of those thirty were from the Fen.

There were two things on the table and neither of them had been put there by anybody in that row.

The first was a request in writing, made on the Saturday by thirty-one ratepayers of this town, and it had a head on it, and Wenna Crale had written the head herself because nobody else in that town would write a head on it.

The second was a cause, set out in writing, in a hand that was not a copyist’s hand, with a day on it and the words *water standing above the sill of a house for eleven days* on the front of it, and clause five, cause two, written at the foot of it in the same hand.

---

The cause was Ordwin Cape’s, and he had written it out on the back of a bill on the Sunday evening at about the seventh hour, and nobody had asked him for it.

“*Where you got it, and how you got it, and say it in a room and not in a square, because it is going to be in a minute this morning and I have watched four documents in three weeks arrive in a book without anybody knowing where they came from.*”

“*I was there. I am sixty-one and I have been in that row forty-one years and I cut reed on that Fen for thirty years before I kept the sluice, and in the ninth year before the division the water came over the Fen and stood against the houses.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that smaller than it is.*”

“*It stood above the sill. I have the date in my head and I can give you the day and I can give you the number of days. It was eleven.

My mother was in the fourth house and the water came up the stairs to the level of the third and stood there, and we were out of that house for a fortnight.*”

About thirty people in a room and on a stair did not make a sound.

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*I have said it. Eleven days, above the sill, in the ninth year before the division, and my mother’s house and two others, and I have not said which three, and if anybody in this room asks me which three I will say it, and I will say it in this room, and I have already said that I will.*”

“*Go on, and say the rest of it.*”

“*I am going to write the day on this paper. I have had it in my head since the Sunday and I am sixty-one and I shall not have it in a fortnight. And I have not been asked to write it and I am writing it, and I would like the minute to say that I was not asked and that I wrote it, because if it is ever found out that I was asked then this paper is worth nothing to anybody and it is the only thing I have got.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for it.

---

And then Ilyan Vester said a sentence, and it was the sentence of a boy of twenty-two in a market square in the rain on the twelfth day after the division, and he said it because the boy is on nobody’s book and cannot be asked about it afterwards, and he said it in a room with about thirty people in it because the row’s case in that room rested on the word household being a person, and he said it better than the row would have said it, and he had not been asked to say it.

“*Say what you have come to say,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*A household is a house. That is all clause four means and that is all the word in it means.*”

“*Do not make that a speech.*”

“*Coll Sarn said it in that square in the rain before anybody in this room had thought of it, and he is twenty-two and he has four beds in this town and none of them is his, and he cannot be asked about a word he used at a wool stall.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Do not make that a speech,*”

“*And the fenrow asked on the nineteenth day to be charged for the water at the same rate as the households of this town, and the third line of what they asked says they asked to be charged, knowing that the water is common to all households of this town. That is their own footing. They put it in themselves. And a request with a footing in it is a request, and without the footing it is a note.*”

“*And the other half of that, in this room.*”

“*The other half is that the fenrow have been on the roll as households for ten days and nobody has taken a penny of it off them.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*There is no form in this county that lets anybody take twopence a quarter off a household that has never been charged twopence a quarter, and I do not know what a household is and I have never been asked.*”

---

And then the board put a requirement into a minute, and the word had never been in a minute of this town before.

“*The whole of it, and do not make it a speech, and read it as it is written and not as you would write it, because the word in the third line is the word this sitting has been about for thirty minutes.*”

Wenna Crale read it out, standing at the end of the table, in the voice she uses for the minutes.

“*Three. That the board requires Sefa Lund, of the fourth house of nineteen, keeping the sluice of this board, to produce an account of the keeping of it, of the water drawn and of the persons who drew it, from the eleventh day after the division to this day.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Go on, and do not make that smaller than it is.*”

“*That is all of it. And the word *required* is on that line and it is the first time that word has been in a minute of this town in four hundred years, and I have been a copyist for eleven years and I have read every minute this board has entered in that time and I have never seen it.*”

“*Say what is the answer to it,*” said Perdy Sallow, “*and say it in your own mouth, and say it in about four sentences, because this room has thirty people in it and they have all come up four hundred yards to hear whether a board of nine can make a woman of thirty-eight do a thing.*”

“*There is no account. There has never been an account. A common is not a service and a service that is not paid does not keep accounts, and I have been turning a bar four feet long at the sixth hour and at the twelfth hour for nine years and the reason there is no account is that there is no money in it for anybody.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person shall keep one, and I have asked for one in four rooms in three weeks and I have been told about the roll and the water list and the out-of-roll book and the autumn and I have never once been told there is a form.*”

---

And then the ninth clause was said out loud in that room, out of a woman’s head. It was the third time anybody had said it in ninety years and it was not said out of the book.

Ilyan Vester had asked for the book to come down at about the eighth hour and had been refused in four sentences by a copyist who gave her reason twice and would not give it a third time. He had not asked a second time, and about nine people in that room had watched him not ask a second time.

“*Say it out loud, and say where you are saying it from.*”

“*Out of my head. And I want the room to have that first, because the book is nine feet up over a fire at the end of this square and it came down on Monday morning at the eleventh hour and went back up in about four minutes.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*It has now been read out of the book in a room twice in ninety years. The last time was two days ago, and this morning it is not coming down, and I am not going to ask for it, because a man of thirty-four asked for it on Monday morning and was refused twice.*”

“*Do not make that a speech.*”

“*No household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will. It is a hundred and thirty-eight years old and it is in a fifth hand on the back of that board and there are two names at the foot of it and one of them is four letters and a stroke.*”

Nobody in that room said anything at all.

“*And the thing underneath it.*”

“*The thing that is underneath it is that the person that line three requires is a household of this town. She is on the roll. She was put on it by a steward’s form on the twentieth day after the division and the form is in a press and any person of the town may read it. So clause nine binds her, and this board may not require her to answer, and line three of this minute is not good.*”

“*Do not make that a speech.*”

“*And I am striking line three out of the minute in front of thirty people, and I am going to say why.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*I have wanted that line since the sixteenth day after the division and I cannot have it, and the thing this room is going to do on the strength of the other two lines is going to do the work of the line I wanted.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for any part of it.

---

And then the board found the cause, and it was right, and it was in the words of the Book.

It was three lines. It took about nine minutes and it was not amended.

Nine men of a water board in a town of four thousand people found that nineteen houses at the Fen are put out of the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, under clause five, cause four, upon a cause set out in writing, and that cause two.

Nobody in Halloway was cruel about it. Four of the nine said out loud in that room that they wished it were not so, and meant it, and their names are in the minute.

A man of fifty-four with a lot on the Four Hundred said that the fenrow had not asked to be put out of the water. The board was not putting them out for asking. It was putting them out for a flood that happened nine years ago, and he had been in that water himself in that year and had come out of it with nothing.

And Ilyan Vester stopped him there, out loud, in a room with about thirty people in it, and it was the right thing to do and it was the worst thing he has done in this town, and it was the third time in six days that a sentence of his had been the ground of something.

“*Say that last part again and say it as a day-count,*” he said. “*You have said a year, and this town keeps no year, and I have been in it twenty-four days and I have learned that the only day-count anybody in this room can be checked on is the division.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*So say it as the ninth year before the division and not as nine years ago, and then a steward can put it in a form, and a form has to be checkable or it is a thing nobody can be asked about.*”

And the man of fifty-four said the number again, and it was the ninth year before the division, and Wenna Crale entered it, and it went into the minute of the board of Halloway with a day on it.

---

And the steward did the other half of it in about four minutes, in an office over a shop at the south end of the square, at about the fourth hour of the same afternoon, and nobody had asked him to.

*Third correction. The nineteen tenements at the Fen, entered in this year’s roll as land of the common and entered as households on the twentieth day after the division, are land of the common and not households of this town, from this day.*

*The water board has this day put them out of the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, under clause five, cause four, upon a cause set out in writing. The ground of the correction of the twentieth day was a request of five lines, of which the third was that they be charged knowing that the water is common to all households of this town. Corrected the thirtieth day after the division. A.T., steward. Nothing else has been altered.*

The roll of Halloway stands corrected. It stands at one thousand and fifty-two rated households.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and four days old. The water fund of that town was thirty-five pounds and one shilling and fourpence a year instead of thirty-five pounds and fourteen shillings, and the quarter in the board’s hand was eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence.

The north heel of a board’s frame is nine pounds, and it is fifty-six pence a quarter short instead of eighteen.

Nineteen houses had been paying twelve shillings and eightpence a year into that fund for ten days and had paid none of it, and they were the only people in Halloway who had ever said they would.

---

And about thirty people were in a market square at about the sixth hour, out of habit, and Ivo Cray of lot nine did the sum out loud in it, and he was the first of the majority in this business to find out what it is worth.

“*Say the number out loud, and say what it is a number of.*”

“*Eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence is a quarter of what this town has for its water, out of every household in it, and nine pounds is a heel, and it is fifty-six pence short. And a woman of thirty-eight has turned a bar four feet long at the sixth hour and the twelfth hour for nine years and has been paid none of it, and has no account, and cannot be required to make one.*”

“*Do not make that a speech.*”

“*The frame is a season away, and the season is about four weeks off, and if the bar is not turned on Thursday morning then it is not a season. It is a year. And then it is two years, and the county said two years, and the man of the county was wrong.*”

Nobody in that square made a sound, and about nine of them had not known until that afternoon that there was a season.

And then Sefa Lund said the thing that has been said in a square in this town twice before, and it was a different sentence both times, and this time there was a minute with her name in it that she had not put there.

“*Say it out loud, and do not stop in the middle of a sentence, and say the days, because a day is the only part of it anybody can be checked on.*”

“*On the fourteenth day after the division I stood in this square and said that I did not agree to not have a household. That is the first.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*This is the thirtieth day after the division and there is a minute of the water board of this town with my name in it, and the word *required* is on the line, and I did not put it there and I did not agree to it and I am not going to agree to it now.*”

“*And the other half of that, and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*And the other half is that I said in a lane on the eleventh day after the division that I did not want to be a person with a claim. A chair of this board put a whole form into four sentences in front of about nine people and I refused it, and that is the only thing I have ever wanted out of nine years of turning that bar.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And nobody has given it to me. There is not one form in the county of Ostrey that gives a person a claim, and there is not one form in the county of Ostrey that takes one off.

The only thing that has happened is that a woman of thirty-eight is a person with no claim in a book in a town of four thousand people. I did not ask for that. I have got it.*”

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and four days old. About thirty people were in a square in a town of four thousand people.

Nineteen houses had been put out of the water of the Hallow in the words of a covenant four hundred years old, correctly, on a cause set out in writing by a man of sixty-one who was in the water himself.

A bar four feet long was not turned at the sixth hour of the Thursday morning for the first time in nine years.
