# Chapter 497: The Second Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Wednesday On Which An Exit Is Written With A Handle On The Inside

Second day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and forty-seventh morning. Ninety-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and four days. Fifty-one days since the division. Forty-five days in this town.

About nine people met in the Stone House with the door open. The hearth gave little heat. The board about four feet by two and a half hung nine feet up on the north beam over a fire, and had not been off that beam since the audit nine years back, save for two takings down. A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-one days.

Ilyan Vester wanted to be the instrument. He had carried the town for six weeks, and some part of him believed that if he held the door then the door would hold.

Wenna Crale laid out the copy of the four pages from four hundred miles off where a woman of twenty-eight sits, addressed to a water board and signed with no name. She laid out the instrument of the fortieth day beside it. She is thirty-four and the only person in the room who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square.

“*Name the only exit this town has ever had,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*The ninth clause of the covenant,*” said Wenna. “*A hundred and thirty-eight years old. Read out in a room twice in ninety years and given to nobody twice. It protects a household. The row are not households. About nine people in this room will use it again if it is offered.*”

Ilyan stood. He said the sentence he had said first in a market square six weeks before, on the Sunday of the twentieth day after the division, in about nine seconds. He said it as if saying it would settle the exit.

“*A right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right,*” said Ilyan Vester.

Wenna looked at him direct. A man at the table set his cup down too hard and water slopped over the rim. A woman rose and went to stand by the open door where the cold came in.

“*A sentence a room can say is not a thing a room can hear,*” said Wenna Crale. “*That is the sentence a woman of twenty-eight wrote in four pages four hundred miles off. He said it first in a market square. This town has been building an instrument out of a sentence for six weeks.*”

“*Then let the room hear it now,*” said Ilyan.

“*No,*” said Wenna, in front of nine people. “*A copyist of thirty-four is the only person in that room who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square, and I say he may not. Nobody in that room can be asked about it and he cannot be asked about it.*”

About four people in that room found out that day what he had said in the square on the Sunday of the twentieth day. About four others looked down at their hands. The rest kept their eyes on the beam.

Perdy took up the pen. He wrote the exit so that it was a thing with a handle on the inside. A person could open it without asking anybody character. No board need meet. No chair need sign. No nineteen doors need be knocked.

“*Read it back and put the handle in it,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*Any household or person of this town, being entered or not entered, may leave the water covenant on giving a day notice at the Stone House, without forfeit of dwelling, and no question shall be put to character, rate or household,*” said Perdy. “*The handle is on the inside.*”

Ilyan sat down slowly. His mistake had been to say the sentence again, wanting to be the hinge. The cost was plain. The copyist had told him in front of nine people that the sentence belonged to the room now, and not to him, and he could not take it back for his own use.

He walked out into the square after and stood looking at the Stone House door. The finding stayed with him all evening. An exit nobody can open is a favour. An exit with a handle on the inside is a right. The ninth clause had protected a household, and the row were not households, and the new line had to do what the old one could not.

The fever was thirty weeks and four days old. An exit had been written with a handle on the inside. A right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right.
