# Chapter 497: The Second Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Wednesday On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Sentence He Said First In A Market Square, And Is Told In Front Of Nine People That A Sentence A Room Can Say Is Not A Thing A Room Can Hear, And A Copyist Strikes It Out Of The Sheet And Writes An Exit With A Handle On The Inside

Second day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and forty-seventh morning. Ninety-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and four days. Fifty-one days since the division. Forty-five days in this town.

About nine people met in the Stone House with the door open. The hearth gave out about as much heat as a wet coat gives off and nobody had offered to light it. The board hung nine feet up on the north beam over a dead fire, four feet by two and a half, a vellum on the face and a lead tag at the foot gone the colour of a wet stone.

It had not been off that beam since the audit nine years back, save for about two hours on the tenth day after the division and about four minutes on the twenty-eighth.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-one days. The wheel at the mill had been slow twenty days completed.

Ilyan Vester had come in with the sentence ready. He had carried it into nine rooms in six weeks and he had been the instrument three times in the six days before this, and some part of him had gone on believing that if he held the door then the door would hold.

Wenna Crale laid out the four pages on the long table. They had come into this room on the forty-third day out of a building of four hundred people on a road four hundred miles off, folded in four with nothing on the outside of it, and they are addressed to a water board and not to a man, and they were signed with no name at all.

She laid the instrument of the fortieth day beside them, with the day on it and her hand at the foot of it, so that the two things were lying side by side on about six feet of oak.

“*Name the only exit this town has ever had,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*The ninth clause of the covenant,*” said Wenna Crale. “*On the back of the board you are both looking at. A hundred and thirty-eight years old, in a fifth hand and a different ink. No household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will.*”

She did not raise her voice.

“*It has been read out in a room twice in ninety years and it was given to nobody twice. It binds households and the row are not households.*”

“*Say the other of those two things,*” said a man at the back.

“*It has been said out of my head in a third room, on the thirtieth day. That is not the same as being read out of the Book and I have been careful about it for nine days.*”

She put two fingers on the paper.

“*And it has been used once. On the forty-first day, by nine men against nine men. It stopped the man who would have compelled.*”

A man at the back asked her to say the last of that again.

“*It stopped the man who would have compelled. It did not stop the thing that had already happened, and it will not reach the row, and about nine people in this room would use it again this afternoon if it were offered and they would be right to.*”

---

It stood about a foot above the wet boards of a floor, in a room with the door open, and it was the only one of its kind in this county.

**CLAIM: A right that a person has to ask a body for is a favour, and a favour is a thing that can be withdrawn by the person who is not getting it, and the difference between a favour and a right is the whole of what a handle on the inside of a door is.**

**EVIDENCE: A row of nineteen houses kept a sluice for nine years and was put out of the water of this town on the thirtieth day after the division, under clause five of a covenant four hundred years old, correctly. The person it bound hardest was the woman who keeps the sluice. She is a household of this town, and she refused in a lane on the eleventh day without having anything to refuse with.**

**The only exit this town has ever had is a hundred and thirty-eight years old. It is on the back of a board nine feet up over a fire, it protects a household, and it was given to nobody in ninety years. It was used once, on the forty-first day, and the only person it protected was a man of forty-seven who had been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years.**

**BURDEN: Nothing in this county can enforce an exit. There is no office within eleven miles that would receive one, no Bench of any kind, and no register. An exit that a person must ask for is a favour, and a favour can be asked for twice and granted once. The whole of what a charter can do here is to make the asking cheap and to name who must answer, and the naming is the only part anybody in this room can check on a day out of a day.**

**CHOICE: Write the exit as a line a person may open on giving notice, with no question of character and no body to apply to, and answer for it in this room this morning in front of nine people whose names are not in this town; or write it as a clause under a covenant, and be the ninth clause, which will be read out in a room in ninety years and given to nobody.**

He chose the line, out loud, in a room, in about eleven seconds. It was the sixth time in six weeks that he had said a true thing in a room, and the first time he had said one in a room that had a decision in it.

Nobody thanked him for it, and there is no minute in any book in this county in which it appears that he wanted a decision.

---

Wenna told the room which page. The fourth, and not the first and not the third, and read it as she wrote it and not as a copyist of this town would say it.

She looked at the room and not at the paper, which is what a copyist does when she is about to read out somebody else’s words and is not going to pretend they are hers.

“*A right that a person has to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour, and not a right. The test is not the number of doors. The test is what the person has to say at the nineteenth one.*”

A woman near the press put her hand flat on the case of it and left it there. A man turned his cup a quarter turn on the oak and did not drink from it. The copyist waited about nine seconds before she said the next thing, and she looked at the four pages the whole time.

Ilyan stood up. He had been in the chair at the end for about four minutes and he had the sentence in his mouth before anybody had finished reading, and he said it, and it is worth recording that he said it well.

“*A right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right,*” Ilyan Vester said.

A man at the table set his cup down too hard and the water went over the rim and across the oak and nobody wiped it for a while.

“*I said that in a market square on the Sunday of the twentieth day after the division,*” he said. “*In about nine seconds. To about two hundred people. And I was right, and I have been saying it in rooms ever since because it is the best sentence I have and because it does not cost anybody anything.*”

Wenna Crale looked straight at him.

“*A sentence a room can say is not a thing a room can hear,*” she said. “*That is the sentence a woman of twenty-eight wrote in four pages four hundred miles off, on the fiftieth page of nothing at all, and you said it first in a square, and this town has been building an instrument out of it for six weeks. A thing read aloud in a square is a thing a person has been said to have said, and I told you that in the same square, and you have not put it down since.*”

He asked to be allowed to let the room hear it now.

“*No.*” She said it in front of nine people and it took about two seconds.

“*A copyist of thirty-four is the only person in that room who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square, and I say he may not. Not you, not them, not a county eleven miles off.*”

She put the pen down on the sheet.

“*That is why it is not in the sheet, and it is going to stay not in the sheet. If you put it in I will strike it out in front of the room, and you may enter the objection with your name on it, and your name on a thing in a press is a thing a person can be asked about, and I am not doing that to you to be unkind.*”

“*Strike it out,*” Ilyan said.

She struck it out. It is in the sheet. It is a line through the middle of it, and the objection is at the foot with his name against it.

She did exactly what she said she would do, in the order she said she would do it, and a copyist of this town keeps the record, and the record is the record.

About four people in that room found out, on that morning, what he had said in the square on the Sunday of the twentieth day after the division, and about four others looked down at their own hands, and the rest kept their eyes on the board nine feet up over the fire.

---

Perdy Sallow took up the pen. He is forty-seven and the chair of this board and he has been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years, and the ninth clause of this town is a protection against him and he said so out loud in a room on the forty-first day and did not say it again.

He wrote it so that a person could open it alone.

Ordwin Cape told him to read it back and to put the handle in it.

“*Any household or person of this town, being entered or not entered, may leave the water covenant on giving a day’s notice at the Stone House, without forfeit of dwelling, and no question shall be put to character, rate or household.*”

He read it a second time. Then he said the part that was his and not the copyist’s, and he said it looking at the board nine feet up over the fire rather than at the paper.

“*The handle is on the inside. There is no form. There is no board to apply to, no fee, and no officer, and a person gives notice and is out, and any person in this room may be asked what they think about that sentence on any day they like for the next year.*”

About nine people in that room understood what had happened and about nine did not, and the two groups were not the same two groups as on the Thursday, and nobody in that room can be asked which was which.

A man of the sixth ward, who had voted on the thirtieth day, said that a handle on the inside was a good thing and then sat down, and did not say the other half, and nobody asked him for the other half, and it is recorded here that he did not say it and that nobody asked.

---

Ilyan sat down slowly, and put both hands flat on the table, and looked at the line through the middle of his own sentence where a copyist had struck it out in front of nine people.

The mistake was that he had said it again, wanting to be the hinge. He had come into that room with six weeks of a sentence and a want to hold the door, and a door does not need a man to hold it, and the woman who keeps it is the woman who gets a hand on it.

The cost was plain, and it was the same cost as on the fortieth day with one thing turned round. On the fortieth day he had been refused a line. This time he had been refused the use of his own best sentence.

The refusal was correct, in front of nine people, and his name is on an objection in a sheet in a press against a west wall in a town of four thousand people, and it is the only thing of his that is in any room in this town that anybody can be asked about.

He walked out into the square after and stood looking at the door of the Stone House with his hands behind his back. He was still there when the copyist came out about ten minutes later to get the air and found him standing in the way of it.

Neither of them said anything about it. She went back in and left the door open, which she has done every day for eleven years and which was, on that Wednesday, the only reply anybody in Halloway had to give him.

The fever was thirty weeks and four days old. An exit had been written with a handle on the inside, and a sentence had been struck out of a sheet with a line through the middle of it and an objection entered at the foot with a man’s name against it.

A right that a person has to knock nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right, and the sheet in the press is the fifth thing in eleven days that anybody in this town can read without asking a person for it.
