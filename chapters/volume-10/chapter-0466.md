# Chapter 466: A Roll Of One Thousand And Forty-One Households Is Corrected In One Morning In An Office Over A Shop By A Steward Of Forty Years With A Form He Had Never Opened, And Nineteen Tenements Go On It As Households, And The Water Fund Goes From Thirty-Four Pounds And Fourteen Shillings To Thirty-Five Pounds And Six Shillings And Eight Pence, And Nobody In That Square Knows Yet What A Book Done In A Morning Does To A Constitution

Sixth day of the eighty-eighth week. His two hundred and sixteenth morning. Sixty-six days after the settlement. The fever twenty-six weeks and one day. Twenty days since the division. Fourteen days in this town.

The rate roll of Halloway is made once a year, in the autumn, and it is made out of a day, and the day is a day in the week the common was divided and sold in, and this year’s was made in that week and has not moved since and is two weeks old.

On the Sunday morning the steward of Halloway took out a form.

It is kept in an office over a shop at the south end of the square and it is a sheet of printed paper with a head on it, and the head says that a roll may be corrected in respect of a household entered as land, and that the correction is the steward’s and not the board’s, and that the reason and the day and the hand shall be entered.

Amos Trill had had that form in a drawer for forty years and had told a stranger about it in a market square on the eighth day of this month and had not opened it.

He opened it on the Sunday at about the seventh hour, and he wrote on it in about four minutes, and the whole of what he wrote is here.

*F. Fen. Nineteen tenements, entered in this year’s roll as land of the common and not as households, are households of this town from this day. Corrected the twentieth day after the division, on a request entered in the minute of the water board of the nineteenth day and signed by W. Crale. A.T., steward. Nothing else has been altered.*

And then he ruled a line under it and put the day on that line as well, because it is a form and he is a man of forty years.

---

The copy hangs in the Stone House against the west wall and any person of the town may read it, and by about the tenth hour on the Sunday morning about forty people in Halloway had read those four lines and worked out what they had done to their own rate.

“*Say the number out loud, and say it in a market and not in a room,*” said a man at the wool stall. He said it to a stranger standing in the wet because there was nobody else standing in the wet.

“*One thousand and sixty,*” said Ilyan. “*One thousand and forty-one, and nineteen. And the water rate is twopence a quarter, so that is eight pence a household for the year, and it was eight pence on one thousand and forty-one and it is eight pence on one thousand and sixty, and the fund was thirty-four pounds and fourteen shillings and it is thirty-five pounds and six shillings and eight pence.*”

“*Say what that is in shillings.*”

“*Twelve shillings and eightpence,*” said Ilyan, “*and the whole of it is nineteen houses, and four of them are the four that are not a rate on a water list because a house that is not a household could not be charged.*”

Nobody in that square asked him the next question, which was the one that mattered, and he did not have the next question, and the four lines in the form were already nine feet away from him being wrong about anything.

---

The next question was asked at about the eleventh hour in a room with a press in it, and it was asked by Wenna Crale, and it was not asked of him.

“*Say what you have done to the House of this town,*” she said, “*and say it as a question, because you have been carrying a face on since the seventh hour and I have watched a man of thirty-one do it in nine rooms in this town.*”

“*Nineteen households in nine wards is about two a ward,*” said Ilyan. “*So the House has about two more seats than it had.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Which is forty-three, and about two thirds of one more, and the fortieth and forty-first stools are the ninth ward’s second and third, so a fifth ward seat is about nine months off.*”

“*No,*” said Wenna Crale, and she said it the way she says a thing she has checked.

“*Say why and say it kindly.*”

“*Because the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it, and there is no form in this county that says a ward has a number of seats at all, and the House has been forty-one for as long as there is a sheet of names in this office and that sheet says five for the ninth ward and has said five for longer than anybody can account for.*”

She took a sheet out of the press. It is a list of forty-one names in the order of the wards, and it is copied every year, and the ninth ward has five of them.

“*Say the thing under that and say it in your own mouth, because I have not got the words for it and you have.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*The other half is that a head of household is elected by the households of his ward, and that the households of a ward are the households the roll puts in it. The roll was made once, on a day, out of a day. The Fen was in the ninth ward as land. Land is not a household.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*And so the fifth head of the ninth ward has been elected for four hundred years by a ward that has had nineteen houses standing in it the whole time and has never once counted them.*”

---

And then she said the sentence, and it took her about four minutes to find and about nine seconds to say, and it is in the minute of nothing at all and it is the sentence the whole of the rest of this volume is standing on.

“*A corrected roll is a corrected constituency. Not a corrected count. A constituency. And a constituency is the whole of who is in the room where the minute is written. And a body of forty-one men elected for a year by the households of nine wards is not a number, it is a set of doors, and somebody can go round and open a different one every autumn for about four hundred years and there is not one form in this county that would show anybody they had done it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say what should be done about it this morning.*”

“*Nothing should be done about it this morning,*” said Wenna Crale, “*because this morning is the twenty-first day after a common was sold, and about nine people have read those four lines, and the fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn, and there is nothing anybody can do in a morning, and I have never in eleven years seen a thing come out of a morning.*”

---

And at about the twelfth hour, in the square, in the rain, Amos Trill came out of his office and stood under the awning of the mill, and a man of thirty-one asked him, at the top of his voice, in front of about a hundred people, whether the House of Halloway was going to sit on it.

“*Say it again and say it quieter,*” said Trill.

“*I am asking whether the House is going to sit on a corrected roll.*”

“*No,*” said the steward, “*and I will tell you why, and I will tell you the part of it that is mine.*”

He put his hand flat on the wall of the mill, which is not a thing he does.

“*The House sits when I call it, and I am not going to call it, and the reason I am not going to call it is that a steward who calls the House on a question of the roll is a steward who comes in with a thing, and I have been the steward of this town for forty years and nobody in this town has ever once had to wonder what I wanted. That is not a virtue anybody put on a form. It is the only power I have ever had.*”

“*Then say the other half of that and say it in this square.*”

“*And I did it this morning.*”

About a hundred people in a market square did not make a sound.

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*I opened a form I have had in a drawer for forty years and I corrected a roll in about four minutes and I wrote nine words and a day and my own hand at the foot of it, and there is no board in this county that could have done that, and no board in this county would have known that it could be done, and nine men wrote a request into a minute yesterday evening and a steward of forty years found out on Sunday morning that his own office had a door in it he had never looked at.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And I have not slept on it. I am not going to pretend to you in this square that I have.*”

---

And Ilyan said the true sentence, out loud, on a Sunday, at the top of his voice, in front of about a hundred people, and it is the only true sentence he has said in this town in fourteen days that nobody can do anything about with.

“*Say it out loud and say it in this square and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Mab Harrow, from under the awning, “*because you have had a face on since seven o’clock and I would like a whole sentence out of you for once in your life.*”

“*A corrected roll is a corrected constituency,*” said Ilyan. “*That is not a number changing. That is a set of doors changing.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of it.*”

“*Nobody in this square knows yet what they have done. The fourth head of the ninth ward is elected in the autumn, and the fifth, and they are elected by the households of that ward, and there are nineteen more of the households of that ward today than there were yesterday.*”

He had it in a square at about a hundred people and he had said it in nine seconds and it was the right sentence and it was the worst thing he has done in this town, because in thirteen days he has learned that a sentence said in a square belongs to whoever is standing in the square, and there were about a hundred of them and he was the one standing in the rain.

Amos Trill answered it, and he was right, and it was not an answer to the thing that had been asked.

“*Say what you have just said back to me,*” he said, “*and I am going to tell you the four true things I know, and not one of them will be an answer, and I would like it noticed that a man of sixty-one has been asked a question in a public square four hundred times in forty years and has never once been asked that one.*”

“*Say them.*”

“*One: there is a form in an office over a shop and I have opened it. Two: it is the steward’s form and not the board’s, and the board has no part in it and did not know it existed.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*Three: the roll stands corrected and there is nothing in this county to un-correct it before the autumn. Four: I have altered nothing else and I shall go on altering nothing else, and that is the whole of what I have for you.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say what it is not.*”

“*It is not an answer to your question. It is not an answer to the question that will be asked in about four months by somebody who has just worked out that a book can be a door. I have four true things and none of them is that.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I have been the steward of this town for forty years and I have just found out that the count of this town is a list of who is in a room. I have no form for that. There is no board in this county that has anything to say about it.*”

The fever was twenty-six weeks and one day old, and the water fund of Halloway was thirty-five pounds and six shillings and eightpence instead of thirty-four pounds and fourteen shillings, and there were about a hundred people in a wet square, and one of them had said the true sentence out loud, and the other ninety-nine had heard it and were carrying it home to a kitchen, and about four of them would repeat it correctly by the evening and about forty would repeat it wrongly.

On the sheet of forty-one names in the order of the wards, against the ninth ward, there were five houses and a room, and the room had been a room since before the press was made, and nobody in that stone room had ever counted it.
