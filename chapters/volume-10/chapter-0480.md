# Chapter 480: The Third Of Ten Things This Band Did Not Fix Is A Sheet Of Paper In A Press And A Roll To Be Made In The Autumn And A Frame On A List Nine Years Old, And A Man Of Thirty-One Who Is On Nobody’s Book Is Asked By A Copyist To Stay Out Of The Room So That He Can Be Read Out In It, And He Agrees, And He Is Not Standing In The Room, And Nobody In This Town Says Whether Four Letters And A Stroke Can Be Read

Sixth day of the ninetieth week. His two hundred and thirtieth morning. Eighty days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and one day. Thirty-four days since the division. Twenty-eight days in this town.

A sheet of paper is in a press against a west wall in a stone room in this town. It has eleven lines on it and a day on it, and any person of the town may read it, and about two hundred people heard all eleven read out in a market square on the Saturday.

The roll for the autumn is to be made in about a fortnight, on a day out of a day, before the weather turns, by a man in an office over a shop who will do it correctly and will not know.

The frame at the head of the sluice is the third thing on a list of works of the water board of this town, and the other two things on that list are the cleaning of the tail of the race and a second winding bar, and all three of them have been on that list every year for nine years.

And the last of the things this town has had in front of it in five weeks, and can do nothing about at all, is a man.

---

The Stone House has one hearth in the corner. The stools were put away in the warm part of the year, because the House sits in this building in the winter and it was not the winter.

There were about nine people in it on the Sunday morning of the thirty-fourth day after the division. The Book was nailed nine feet up over the fire at the end of the wall and about four feet in from it, and it was not read out again.

“*Say what you are doing and why you are doing it in this room with nine people in it,*” said Ilyan Vester, from the doorway, “*because eleven lines have been read out in a square in this town and nobody has read them out in a room where a minute is written, and I would like to know which of those two is the right place for a list.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*Both are wrong, and that is why I am doing it twice. I copied the list out again this morning in my own hand and the copy is on the table by the door and anybody may read that too.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*And I have asked you not to come into this room, and you have said yes, and I would like the reason said in the room and not said afterwards in a square, and the reason is that the ninth of these eleven things is a man.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything for about nine seconds.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the wall.

“*A man in the room is a man the room can be asked about. He can say no. He can be interrupted, and asked what he meant, and asked whether he wrote it, and asked whether he is sorry.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I want to read the ninth of these eleven things out in a room that he is not standing in, because that is the only shape in which this town has ever been able to hear anything.*”

“*It is the shape it has been in for four weeks. It has cost him four weeks and it has cost me eleven years, and I would rather it cost one of us properly than both of us badly.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything at all.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Sefa Lund, from the wall, where she had been standing since the eighth hour.

“*You are not on the roll. You are not in a book. You are not in a minute and you are not going to be in one, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be in a book of this town.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*The copyist of this House is the only person in this county who can read the old hand, and she is paid two pounds a year, and she is going to read nine lines about this town out of a book in about eleven minutes, and the ninth of them is a man who is thirty-one and who is not in the room.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester, from the doorway.

“*If he is in the room then the room is a room about him, and if he is out of it then the room is a room about this town. This town has not had one of those in about four hundred years.*”

He said yes, in about nine seconds, and he did not go further than the doorway, and about nine people in a stone room watched a man of thirty-one stand in a doorway in a town of four thousand people on a Sunday morning and agree not to come in.

---

So she read the eleven lines out, standing at the long table, in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took about eleven minutes, and it was the same eleven lines that had been read in the square on the Saturday with a door open behind them.

At the ninth line she stopped, which nobody had expected, including Wenna Crale.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Amos Trill, from the end of the table, “*because you have stopped on a line about a man and there are about nine people in this room and a man in the doorway.*”

“*I am going to read it as a document and not as a line on a list, and I want it in the room first that I am doing that of my own motion and that nobody has asked me to, and that he has agreed to it and not to being read about.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Ilyan Vester. Thirty-one. Of a county four hundred miles off this town, and not upon a road of the Shelf, and not of this town, and not of the fenrow, and not of the water board, and not of the House.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*Nineteen days in Halloway on the fourth day after the division, when he read a notice out in a market square in this town at the ninth hour that nobody had asked him to read. He gave his name, his age, his county, his lack of standing and his day-count, in that order, in the first minute, to a man he had never met, because he had watched it done on the Saturday and worked out what it was for.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Ilyan Vester, from the doorway, “*because there is a ninth line and you have not finished the ninth line.*”

“*And that he has been in nine rooms in this town in twenty-eight days and is on nothing, and that there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be put in a book of this town, and that he is a man who cannot be sent for.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that.*”

“*And that the whole of what that has been worth to him and the whole of what it has cost this town are the same sentence, and that I do not know which is the larger, and I have not asked him.*”

She read the tenth and the eleventh and then she stopped, and about nine people in that stone room let her stop, because they had heard all eleven in a square on the Saturday and because the door was open.

---

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and one day old, and the ten things were still ten things and one of them was now eleven, and none of them had been done.

A board about four feet by two and a half was nine feet up over a fire. It had been read out in a room twice in ninety years, on the tenth day after the division and on the twenty-eighth, and it had been given to nobody on either of those days, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody.

It says that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will.

The right of refusal was taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years ago and has not been put back. The clause is two years older than the taking of it, and nobody in Halloway can verify the date but a man in a doorway.

The out-of-roll book was in a drawer in a house in this town. It has never been written in in this town and it is not going to be written in this year. The nineteen houses at the Fen went off the roll on Wednesday afternoon in about four minutes in a form with a day on it, and nobody was asked to do that either.

The bar at the head of the sluice is four feet long and takes two people and about an hour. It was not turned at the sixth hour on the Thursday morning, or at the twelfth hour on the Thursday, or at either hour on the Friday. The wheel at the mill had been slow for three days, and the man who has had the mill four years has a note in his own book against the name of the mill, and the note says one word.

The north heel of that frame is three-eighths of an inch down in nine years, and a season instead of two years. The fact is on the wrong side of a leaf in a press any person of this town may read, and it is also in a book twenty-two miles off at Kerby.

The heel is nine pounds. The quarter in the board’s hand is eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence. There is no form in this county that lets a board borrow against the next quarter, and no form that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made.

And at the foot of the ninth clause there is a witness line with two names in it. One of them is a name of this town. The other is four letters and a stroke in a hand that is not of this town and is not the old hand of the Book.

It has not been read by anybody in this county. It is on a road, in a second sheet folded in four with nothing on the outside of it, four hundred miles off, in a building of four hundred people, in a press on a shelf with about nine hundred refusals entered in it.

Nobody in this town has opened it and nobody in this town is going to. It is four hundred miles from a market square in which about two hundred people stood on a Saturday because a door had been opened and shut in a morning.

---

And nobody in that square asked whether anybody can be sent for.

That is the next question, and it was already open on the morning of the twenty-eighth day after the division, when a man of forty-four of the county put his thumb on the frame of a door on his way out of a room over a saddler’s shop and said that the page was twenty-two miles off and that any person in this county might walk and ask for it.

He said that he had not got it and neither had the board and neither had that man.

There is no board in the county of Ostrey with anything to say about it. The only office that has ever heard of it is twenty-two miles off, on a road, and it can make a return and put it in a book, and it cannot order a board to do a thing, and it cannot make anybody answer, and it cannot rate anybody, and it cannot put a name in a book of this town.

The nine men who voted nine to nothing against nineteen houses on a Saturday were right. The thirty-one ratepayers who put a cause in writing on a table on a Wednesday were right, and the cause was a flood nine years ago, and a man of sixty-one had been in it. The man of thirty-four who paid two pounds for lot nine was owed a hearing and there was no form in this county that could give him one.

The steward of forty years corrected a roll three times in a form out of his own drawer, slept on none of it, and asked nobody for anything. The miller of thirty-nine kept a book of nine hundred people for four years for his own reasons and gave a better reason the second time. The copyist of thirty-four was paid two pounds a year and put a list in a press a day early and said so out loud in a market square, and read a man out of a room in which he was not standing.

Nobody thanked any of them, including the man in the doorway.

A bar four feet long was not turned for the third day in a town of four thousand people. About two hundred of them stood in a square on a Saturday and worked out what a book done in a morning is, and the only sentence anybody carried out of that square was in a piece of tin on a nail in a shed at the bottom of it, driven in by somebody who is not alive.

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and one day old. A man of thirty-one was standing in the doorway of a stone room in a town of four thousand people on the Sunday morning of the thirty-fourth day after the division, and about nine people were inside the room and he was outside it, and his name was in a book in the room and his name was on nothing, and the door of that building is open.
