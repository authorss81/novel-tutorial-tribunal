# Chapter 488: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Ninety-First Week, And A Monday On Which A Board Of Nine Men Is Asked To Mend A North Heel That Is A Season Off And The Season Is About Four Weeks Off, And The Money Is Fifty-Six Pence A Quarter Short, And A Halfpenny On Each Of Eighty Lots Is Forty Pence And Was The Whole Of The Difference On The Twenty-Sixth Day And Is Not The Whole Of It Now, And A Levy Is Asked For, And By Whom, And In What Document, And It Is Entered With A Day On It And A Hand At The Foot Of It

Seventh day of the ninety-first week, and the last day of it. His two hundred and thirty-eighth morning. Eighty-eight days after the settlement. The fever twenty-nine weeks and two days. Forty-two days since the division. Thirty-six days in this town.

The water board of Halloway sits in a room over a saddler’s shop at the north end of the market square. It sat there on the Monday morning of the forty-second day after the division, and it was the second sitting that a chair of forty-seven had called in his life, and there were about thirty people in that room and on that stair.

The frame at the head of the sluice is about sixty years old and its north side has dropped three-eighths of an inch in nine years. A man of the county of forty-four sounded it to a depth of nine inches at the eleventh hour of a Friday in the rain, in front of about twenty people, with a straight edge and a nail, and said in his judgment that the north heel is a season and not two years. He would not un-say a number in front of twenty people to say so.

The fact is on the wrong side of a leaf in a press any person of this town may read, and it is also in a book twenty-two miles off at Kerby.

The season is about six days off. A man of thirty-four with a lot on the Four Hundred said in a market square on the thirtieth day after the division that if the bar is not turned then it is not a season, it is a year. About nine people in that square had not known until that afternoon that there was a season.

Nobody has been able to settle it since, because the only thing that would settle it is a bar turned at the sixth hour and at the twelfth hour on every day for four weeks, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody require that.

---

“*Say what the board has been asked to do and say it as a sum and not as a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester, from the second chair, where he had been asked to sit by a copyist of thirty-four and where he had no vote and no standing and about nine people in that room had thought about that.

“*That the board mend the north heel at nine pounds, the figure being that of the county, and that the cost be levied upon the eighty lots in the same manner as the fourteen pounds of the scouring, and that the work be done before the water rises, and that the persons making it be the nineteen tenements.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the four lines and say which of them the board can do and which of them it cannot,*” said Ivo Cray, from the stair.

“*The board can do the third of them or it cannot, and nobody in this room can tell it, and the county figure is a season and the season is about six days off.*”

“*Go on and say the money, and say it with the units on it, and then say what is short of it.*”

“*The water rate is twopence a quarter on one thousand and fifty-two households of this town, and the roll of this town stands corrected at one thousand and fifty-two, and that is two thousand one hundred and four pence a quarter, and that is eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence a quarter. The heel is nine pounds. The difference is fifty-six pence a quarter.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*A halfpenny on each of the eighty lots is forty pence a quarter. On the twenty-sixth day after the division, in a room over a taproom with about thirty people in it and on a stair, a woman of thirty-eight said that a halfpenny on each of the eighty lots was the whole of the difference.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*At that time the difference was forty pence a quarter, and the woman was right, and about nine people in that room wrote it on the back of a hand.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The difference is fifty-six pence a quarter now, because nineteen households that were on this roll for ten days in the fourth week after the division are off it.

Nineteen households at eight pence a household a year is one hundred and fifty-two pence a year, which is thirty-eight pence a quarter, and thirty-eight pence and eighteen pence is fifty-six pence. And a halfpenny on each of the eighty lots is forty pence. And there are sixteen pence of it that no lot in this county can be made to find.*”

---

And the board did the arithmetic again in about four minutes, and about nine people in that room did it with it, and it came out the same, and the finding of the morning was not a finding and was not a resolution and was a question of document.

“*Say the question of the document and say it in the ordinary voice,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the fourth stool.

“*The fourteen pounds of the scouring was charged on the eighty lots at three and a halfpence a lot, and it was charged in the roll of this year and not in the next. The roll of this year was made in the week the common was sold. So a levy on the eighty lots is a line on a roll, and this year’s roll has been made, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The roll for the autumn is made in five days. On a day out of a day, in an office over a shop, by a man of sixty-one who has been the steward of this town for forty years. He will do it correctly. He will enter what this board has put in front of him with a day on it, and he will not know what else is on it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said Amos Trill, from the end of the table, where he had come and sat down without being asked and about nine people in that room had not expected him to come.

“*I will enter it. I will enter it in the same form I have used three times in five weeks, and I will enter the day and my hand at the foot of it. I am not going to ask this board for the money to do it, because there is no money to do it with, and the form has never cost anything.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And I will tell this board what it is doing, because about nine of them have been told by other people in five weeks and I am the one who has to write it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*A levy of three and a halfpence on each of the eighty lots is two hundred and eighty pence a year. That is one pound and three shillings and fourpence. The quarter in this board’s hand is eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence, and the two of them together are nine pounds and eighteen shillings and eightpence. The heel is nine pounds, and the whole of it is eighteen shillings and eightpence more than the heel, and I have done that sum four times since the Saturday and it is right.*”

---

And then the vote was taken, and it took about nine minutes, and it was not a close one, and about four men said afterwards in that room that they had gone in expecting it to be close.

“*Say the number and say it out loud and say it in a room and not in a square,*” said Ivo Cray, from the stair.

“*Eight to one. The eight are the ones who said on the Wednesday of the thirtieth day after the division that they wished it were not so.

“*Go on and say the other half.*”

“*The one is a man of about fifty-three with a small holding on the west side, who said in that room on the Wednesday of the thirty-seventh day that he had been right five times in five weeks and had never once been able to make anything happen. He voted against it because a levy is a thing that is asked for and not a thing that is found.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And he is right, and the levy is asked for and not found, and the minute says so.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I would like the minute to say that the levy is a levy and was not entered as a rate and was not charged in the roll of this year. A rate is a thing a man cannot be asked about. A levy is a thing anybody can be asked about, about nine men at that table know the difference, and four hundred years of this town does not have a word for it.*”

And so it was entered, and the third line of the minute of the water board of Halloway of the forty-second day after the division says this.

*That a levy of three and a halfpence upon each of the eighty lots upon the Four Hundred be and is hereby made, to be charged in the roll of this town for the autumn of this year and not in any other, in the same manner as the fourteen pounds of the scouring.

*The whole of which is two hundred and eighty pence, that is one pound and three shillings and fourpence.*

*And that the money be collected with the water rate, and that the water rate is collected four times a year, and that the first collection is thirteen weeks off.*

*And that this board has asked for the money and has not found it, and that the season of the year at which the north heel of the frame of the sluice will fail is about six days off, and that no person in this county can be required to turn the bar at the head of that frame.*

---

And then he was asked for his view, for the fourth time in this town, and he gave the true one, and about nine men in that room had heard it four times before that morning and heard it a fifth time on the forty-second day after the division.

“*Say what you are being asked and say the reason you are being asked in this room and not in a lane,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*Because the copyist of this House asked me to sit in this room and I have no vote and about nine people have noticed that, and I would like the true one said in a room and entered nowhere.*”

“*Go on and say it.*”

“*The frame is the board’s. The board has eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence a quarter in its hand, and with the levy it will have nine pounds and eighteen shillings and eightpence.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The money arrives with the water rate and the first collection is thirteen weeks off, and the season is about six days off. The only instrument in this county that could mend that frame is the office of the county surveyor at Kerby, twenty-two miles off, on a road. That office can make a return and put it in a book. It cannot order a board to do a thing, and it cannot make anybody answer, and it cannot rate anybody, and it cannot put a name in a book of this town.*”

About nine men in that room did not make a sound.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*” said Ivo Cray, from the stair, “*because I have heard that sentence in this town five times now and I would like somebody to say the number out loud.*”

“*It is the fifth time. It was said in a room over a shop on the twenty-eighth day after the division, and in a stone room on the twenty-ninth, and in a room over a taproom on the sixteenth of this week, and in a market square on the twenty-eighth, and it is said in about forty mouths in this town.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*It is true. Being true has turned out to be the same as being able to do nothing, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a sentence is a thing a room can be asked about.*”

---

The fever was twenty-nine weeks and two days old. A levy of three and a halfpence on each of the eighty lots upon the Four Hundred was asked for, by eight votes to one, on the forty-second day after the division, by a board of nine men of a market town of four thousand people. It is two hundred and eighty pence a year and it is one pound and three shillings and fourpence, and it is entered in a minute in a room over a saddler’s shop with a day on it and a hand at the foot of it.

It is charged in the roll for the autumn, and the roll for the autumn is made in five days in an office over a shop by a man of sixty-one who will do it correctly and will not know what else is on it. A bar four feet long is on its twelfth day in the mud, and the season is about six days off.
