# Chapter 479: A Saturday On Which There Is No Market Because The Market Is On A Tuesday, About Two Hundred People In A Stone Market Square In A Town Of Four Thousand People Because A Door Has Been Opened And Shut In A Morning And Everybody In The Town Has Worked Out What A Book Done In A Morning Is, A Sheet Of Ten Things Read Out In A Square For The First Time And Acquiring An Eleventh Thing, And Nobody Says Which

Fifth day of the ninetieth week. His two hundred and twenty-ninth morning. Seventy-nine days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and seven days. Thirty-three days since the division. Twenty-seven days in this town.

The market in Halloway is on a Tuesday. It has been on a Tuesday for longer than anybody in that square can remember, and it was on a Saturday two weeks ago for the fifth time in nine years because a wool factor had his carts on the ground by four o’clock on the Friday and would not have them turned about.

There was no market on the Saturday of the thirty-third day after the division. There were about two hundred people in a cobbled square the size of a good field, and they had not come for anything.

About nine of them had come because they had heard that a sheet of ten things was going to be read out in the square at the ninth hour. The other hundred and ninety-one had come because of the water.

The wheel at the mill had been slow for three days.

---

The ten things were read out by the copyist, standing on the flat of the sill of the Stone House with the door open behind her, in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took about eleven minutes, and about two hundred people in a market square heard every word of it.

The chair of the water board had asked her to do it on the Friday evening, in a room over a saddler’s shop, in about eleven words, and he had not read them out himself, and about four people in that room had said that they were surprised and about nine had said that they were not.

The first thing was the ninth clause and the second was the north heel, and the other eight had never been read out in a room in this town in any year, and the eighth of them was the copyist.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Ivo Cray, from the edge of the square, “*because a woman of thirty-four has just told two hundred people that she is the eighth thing that has happened in this town in three weeks and I would like to know what the other two are.*”

“*The ninth and the tenth,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*Go on and say the ninth and the tenth and do not make them smaller than they are.*”

“*The ninth is that a man of thirty-one read a notice out in a market square in this town on the fourth day after the division and nobody asked him to.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*About two hundred people in this square have worked out in three weeks that a stranger who says a thing out loud in a square is a man the square can use.*”

“*And the tenth.*”

“*The tenth is that a house of four thousand people has found out in three weeks what a book done in a morning is.*”

About two hundred people in a stone market square did not make a sound.

---

And then the finding came, and it came the way a thing comes in a town of four thousand people, which is that about nine people work it out and about a hundred and ninety-one get there after them over two days.

“*Say the finding in this square,*” said Ordwin Cape, standing on the flat of the sill with his bar-hook over his shoulder, “*because I am sixty-one and I have been in this row forty-one years, and I would like somebody to say it out loud before the evening.*”

“*A corrected roll is a corrected constituency,*” said a woman of about thirty with a child on her hip, at about the ninth hour and forty minutes. “*Not a corrected count. A constituency. And a constituency is a set of doors.*”

Nobody in that square had to be told that.

About two hundred people in that square had heard a version of it in a rainstorm on the Sunday morning nine days ago. About four of them had heard it correctly the first time, about forty had it wrong, and about a hundred and forty had it in a shape nobody had said it in.

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*And a book can be a door. And a door does not have to be opened by anybody’s character, it can be opened by anybody who finds the handle, and the handles in this county of Ostrey are four and I can name three of them and a woman of thirty-four in that building can name the fourth.*”

“*Say the three and do not make it a speech,*” said Coll Sarn, from the front.

“*A steward’s form in a drawer in an office over a shop. A minute of the water board in a room over a saddler’s shop. And a sheet of ten things in a press against a west wall, which is the handle people in this square are standing at this morning.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this square,*” said Sefa Lund, from the front, where she had been since the eighth hour.

“*And there is a fourth handle and it is shut to a hand’s width on a counter at the bottom of this square, and a man of thirty-nine is standing behind it, and I am not going to name it because I was asked a question in that shop yesterday and I answered it and I gave the answer.*”

About two hundred people in a market square in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

---

And then somebody asked the question that two hundred people in a town of four thousand people have been carrying since Wednesday afternoon, and it was asked by a boy of about fifteen who had been in that square on the Sunday morning nine days ago with his sister holding wool.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester, because somebody always said it.

“*Who is going to stop it,*” said the boy.

Nobody in that square answered him for about a minute, and then about nine people answered him at once and out of nine different mouths, and the answers were in order, and he has them and they are in a book.

“*Say the answer and say the whole of it,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*The water board of Halloway cannot stop it, because the roll is not the board’s. The board has a second book of its own and the second book is in a drawer in a house and has never been written in. The House of Halloway cannot stop it, because the steward has not called it, and because the House is nine wards and forty-one heads and five in the ninth ward, and the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it, and there is no form in this county that says a ward has a number of seats at all.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*And the county of Ostrey cannot stop it. The only office in this county that has ever heard of any of it is the office of the county surveyor at Kerby, twenty-two miles off, on a road, and that office makes a return and puts it in a book.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*It cannot order the board to do a thing, and it cannot make anybody answer, and it cannot rate anybody, and it cannot put a name in a book of this town, and it has no power over the frame, over the roll, over the sluice or over any person in this square.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in your own mouth,*” said Perdy Sallow, who had come down the lane on his own and had not been asked to and had stood at the edge of the flat of the sill.

“*And the Book is nine feet up over a fire in that building. It came down on Monday morning and went back up in about four minutes, and it has been read out in a room twice in ninety years, and both of those times it has been given to nobody.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody.*”

Nobody in that square said anything for about nine seconds.

“*Say what that is,*” said the boy of about fifteen, “*because I am fifteen and I have been standing in this square since the eighth hour and I would like somebody to say it in one sentence.*”

“*There is nobody in this county who can stop it,*” said Wenna Crale, “*and the only things in this county that anybody can be asked about are four, and one of the four is a nail.*”

---

Nobody in that square said the rest of that out loud, and about two hundred people in a town of four thousand people worked out what a nail was in about eleven seconds, and about nine of them got there before anybody else did.

There is a nail in a shed at the bottom of that square, in a board, driven in by somebody who is not alive. It is not in a press and it is not in a book and it is not in a minute.

In a piece of tin on the nail it says that the roll is a list of doors.

A carter of about thirty said it in a square nine days ago, in the rain, and he had it right, and he had got it off that nail, and he had said that he did not know who had driven it in.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a nail is a document,*” said the carter. “*And I have found out in nine days that there is no form in this county that says a shed is a room.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I am the only person in this square who has ever said that sentence out loud. I said it nine days ago, about four of you heard me, and I have not slept since.*”

---

And a man of thirty-one who had said a true sentence in that square on the Sunday morning nine days ago stood in it for four hours on the Saturday and said almost nothing.

About nine people in that square noticed, and one of them said it out loud at about the fourth hour, which was a thing that had not happened in this town before.

He wanted the sentence to be his. It was the first want of his that had a shape, and he had had it since the Sunday morning nine days ago, and he had spent three weeks teaching a town of four thousand people to carry sentences, and about nine people in that square had noticed on the Saturday that the man in the coat had not said one word for four hours, and one of them had said it out loud.

Ilyan Vester had been asked for his view three times in twenty-seven days: in a square with about two hundred people in it on the fourteenth day after the division, in a room over a shop with about nine people in it on the twenty-eighth, and on the Friday in this shop by a woman of thirty-four. He had said nothing in the first and everything in the second and a number in the third.

He had wanted, since about the eleventh hour on the Saturday morning, to be asked a fourth time. Nobody asked Ilyan Vester, and that was the correct thing to do, and he has written down nowhere that it was the correct thing to do.

It was not his.

It was a nail in a shed. A woman of thirty with a child on her hip had said it in the right words at about the ninth hour and forty minutes, and about two hundred people had heard it, and it had not come from a man in a coat, and it did not die with him.

There is no minute in any book in this county that will let anybody ask him what he thinks of it.

At about the fourth hour he said one fact, and about two hundred people heard it and about nine of them understood it. He said that the roll stands at one thousand and fifty-two rated households, and that the true figure is one thousand and seventy-four.

He said that twenty-two households of this town are not on it, and that eleven rows in it are not in the room where it is read, and that there is no form in the county of Ostrey that will let anybody put the twenty-two back on before the autumn.

It was the only correct thing said in that square in four hours. About two hundred people in a town of four thousand people did not carry any of it, because it is a number, and about four of them wrote it on the back of a hand, and a number on the back of a hand dies with the hand.

---

And the sheet of ten things acquired an eleventh thing, and nobody said which.

Wenna Crale had copied the sheet out on the Friday morning of the twenty-fifth day after the division and it had ten lines on it in a chair’s hand, and on the Saturday morning of the thirty-third day there were eleven.

The eleventh was at the bottom, under the tenth, in the copyist’s hand and in a different ink, and it was one line, and she read it out with the other ten and she did not say whose it was and she did not say which of the ten it was.

*That a door has been opened and shut in a morning in this town four times in four days, and that each of the four was done with a form and a day and a hand.*

*And that not one of the four forms was read out in a room before it was used, and that this is the eleventh thing.*

About two hundred people in a market square in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound, and about nine of them asked which of the ten it was, and about nine people in that square knew and said nothing.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and seven days old. A sheet with eleven lines on it was in a press against a west wall in a stone room, and any person of the town may read it. About two hundred people had heard all eleven read out in a market square in about eleven minutes.

At the bottom of that square there is a shed with a nail in a board in it and a piece of tin on the nail, and no form in the county of Ostrey says that a nail is a document.
