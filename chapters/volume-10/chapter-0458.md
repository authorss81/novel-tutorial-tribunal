# Chapter 458: The Water Came Up In Buckets From A Tap Four Hundred Yards Above The Sluice For Nine Days And Four Hundred People Carried It Twice A Day, And A Miller Stopped Giving Credit To A Woman Who Has Bought On Credit For Eleven Years Because He Looked Her Up In A Roll And She Was Not In It, And A Man With A Figure In His Coat Asks A Woman With A Press To Copy It And Is Refused

Fifth day of the eighty-seventh week. His two hundred and eighth morning. Fifty-eight days after the settlement. The fever twenty-five weeks and no days. Twelve days since the division.

The tap at the top of the lane is four hundred yards above the sluice and it is a standpipe, and it has been in that lane longer than anybody in that row can remember, and nobody in the row had used it twice in nine years.

The second day of the nine is the day the buckets start. Two tin pails with handles, one a hand’s width narrower than the other so that a man can carry one in each hand, and they are heavy when they are full, and four hundred yards is a long way with two full pails and a bad leg.

“*Say how many times a day,*” said Sefa Lund, on the Saturday morning, in the lane, with about thirty people in it.

“*Twice. At the sixth hour and at the twelfth, and the sluice is wound at the sixth and the twelfth whatever the gates are doing, and the gates are shut for nine days.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*So it is nine days of winding a shut gate, which is a thing nobody in this country has to do and which is the last thing on the frame before the heel goes. And it is nine days of about two hundred people walking four hundred yards with two pails, and there are about twenty-three of us in this row and about two hundred people in this town who come up here, and we run out of pails on the Wednesday.*”

---

He carried two pails up the lane four times on the Saturday and twice again that evening, and he could not do more than that, and by the time he had put the pails down for the last time there was a blister on his right palm the size of a farthing.

Nobody thanked him for it. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person may be thanked.

The man who came out of the fourth house in the evening to ask him whether he was going to keep coming was sixty-one. He had kept the sluice before the woman who kept it now, for twenty-two years, and had cut reed for thirty years before that, and had a county pension of eleven shillings a year.

“*Say your name,*” said Ilyan.

“*Ordwin Cape. And I have eleven shillings a year and nineteen houses and nine days, and I worked it out in the lane this morning. Eleven shillings is a hundred and thirty-two pence. Nineteen houses at a penny a day for nine days is a hundred and seventy-one pence. So I am short by thirty-nine pence, and there is a woman in this row going to be short by the end of it, and I do not know which.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Sefa Lund, from a doorway, where she had been listening for some time.

“*The other half is that I have had eleven shillings a year for nine years and I have never once been asked to hand any of it to nineteen houses, and I would do it, and I have told nobody until this morning.*”

---

What actually moved that week was not anything that happened at the sluice.

It happened in a shop at the end of the market, on the Saturday afternoon, and it took about four minutes, and it was a woman of thirty-four who washes at the sluice and has rented the second room of the fourth house for eleven years.

The mill keeps a book. Everybody in a market town of four thousand buys flour at one mill. The mill sells meal and oil and nails and a great deal of credit, and the credit is against a name, a house and a rent. The miller knows about nine hundred households by their door and by what they had last winter.

Mab Harrow had bought on credit at that counter for eleven years.

“*Say what has happened in your own mouth,*” said Ilyan, at about the fourth hour on the Saturday, in the shop, and there were about nine people in it and three of them did not know why the miller had stopped talking.

“*I looked you up,*” said Ott Vernay, who was thirty-nine and had had the mill four years, “*and you are not on the roll of this town, and there is no name of yours on anything at that counter, and I cannot carry a debt for a household I have never seen.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*Eleven years. I have had the mill four. I did not know that and I have looked you up this afternoon and I can see that somebody has been carrying you for seven years and it was not me.*”

“*And what do you want,*” said Mab Harrow.

“*Nothing. I want to tell you that I have stopped it and that I have said why out loud in a shop with nine people in it, and that I would like it to be said somewhere that I have not shut a shop on a woman of thirty-four in front of a stranger.*”

---

Ilyan offered to settle it, and he offered it in the wrong order, and he has offered things in the wrong order in six rooms in nine weeks.

He offered it before anybody had asked him to, and out of his own pocket, and the money was not his and had never been his, and he said where it had come from. The sentence about the four hundred and sixty-three forms and the word *temporary* went out into a shop in Halloway about two hundred seconds after he had said it.

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Mab Harrow.

“*The other half is that it is the only thing I have got, and I have got it because nobody in a hundred and thirty-eight years has needed a stranger to pay a mill bill.*”

“*Then take it out of the shop,*” said Ott Vernay, and he was not unkind and he did not raise his voice, “*and take yourself out of it, and I will tell you why, and it is the last thing I am going to say about it in this shop.*”

He put both hands flat on his counter, which is a thing millers do.

“*I have nine hundred names on this book. I know nine hundred people by their door, by their name, and by what they had last winter. That is not kindness. It is the only way I do not lose money in a bad year. And I do not know you. You have been in this town six days. The woman at my counter has been in it eleven years and I have been in it four, and I have stopped her because I cannot find her, and I would have stopped anybody.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it larger than it is,*” said Ilyan.

“*And if a stranger comes in here and pays a bill, then very soon there are four more, and before the year is out there are forty. And the four and the forty all find out that the way to get credit in this shop is to be a stranger with money. Then the nine hundred people I do know go to the other shop, and the other shop shuts in a year, and this town has one mill and nine hundred people who are owed by a stranger.*”

He took his hands off the counter.

“*I have said no. I am a man with a counter, and I have said no in front of nine people, and I have said why. If somebody wants it said again in a room with a clerk in it, they can come and get me, and it will be the same.*”

Nobody in that shop said one word for a moment, and then Mab Harrow said a thing, and she said it to Ilyan and not to the miller.

“*Say it again that you are not going to pay it.*”

“*I am not going to pay it.*”

“*Then say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*The other half is that it is the second time this week I have been the man with the thing nobody else has. The first time I put a figure in a market. The second time I put my hand on a mill counter. Both times I did it without stopping, and both times the person it was for was standing in the room.*”

---

Then, late that evening, in the room over the nails, with the tea cold, he asked Wenna Crale to copy a number into her book.

He had carried it for twenty days. It is a return on a form of an office of a country that is not this one, with his name at the head of it, four lines in the margin, and a figure in the fourth, dated the morning he wrote it. It is the only figure in ninety thousand people that the office did not have.

He put it on the table face down, which he had not planned to do.

“*Say what you are asking me and say it before you turn it over,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*I am asking you to copy a figure into the book of this town, with the day on it, and to keep it the way this town keeps things.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*And I have carried it for twenty days because about thirty people in that city wanted it and I did not send it. In the last four days I have worked out that the whole reason is that a number in a book is a thing a person can be asked about. I have been a person people can be asked about since the eighty-third week, and I cannot think of one way that having a number in a town of four thousand people is not the same thing with a longer road to it.*”

“*Then turn it over,*” she said.

He turned it over.

“*No,*” said Wenna Crale. “*I am not going to copy that into my book, and you should be glad of it, and you did not come here to be refused so I am going to tell you the whole of the reason in one go and then I am going to make the tea.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*That is a return on the form of an office, and I have never seen the form, and I cannot check the figure, and there is no other copy in this country because the office forty-one miles off has not got it. If it goes in my book it is the only number in it that nobody in this town can check and nobody in this town can be asked about, and that sounds like exactly what you have wanted for nine weeks.*”

She put her hand flat on the book.

“*And it is not, and you came six days ago to be a man who is on nothing, and if the one number in my book is yours then the book is not the town’s. And the book is the only thing in four hundred years of this place that anybody has ever been able to take and say a true thing in.*”

“*And the other half of that is that I have wanted somebody to ask me about it for twenty days,*” said Ilyan.

“*I know,*” said Wenna Crale. “*That is why I am refusing it and not the steward. Send it. Send it from a town, with a place on it and a day on it, and let it go up a road to a clerk who can put it in a book that has a head on it. And do not put it in mine, because in this town a thing in a book is a thing a person can be asked about, and you have spent nine weeks learning that, and you came here to unlearn it, and it is the one thing in this place that is worth having.*”

He put the return back in the inside pocket of his coat.

The fever was twenty-five weeks and no days old. About two hundred people were carrying water four hundred yards twice a day.

A woman of thirty-four had been refused credit in a shop by a man of thirty-nine who did not know her. A copyist had refused a number for the best reason anybody had heard in nine days. And a man of thirty-one was sitting over a cup of tea that had gone cold in it, with a figure back in the inside pocket of his coat where it had been for twenty days.
