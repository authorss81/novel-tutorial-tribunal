# Chapter 498: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Thursday On Which Five Towns Put Their Names To Six Lines, And The Sixth Line Is The Exit

Third day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and forty-eighth morning. Ninety-eight days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and five days. Fifty-two days since the division. Forty-six days in this town.

The Stone House held about nine people of Halloway and four more from outside it. The four had come on foot and by cart from towns that are not upon a road of the Shelf and have no register and no counter and no appointed person. Their coats were dusty. One carried bread and cheese tied in a cloth. A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-two days.

Ilyan Vester sat with them and wanted to be a party to what was made. He had worked six weeks in this town and he believed his name under the lines would strengthen them.

Wenna Crale spread a sheet on the long table. The charter stood beside it in the press, with six headings and a seventh that was an exit. The question of the morning was who carries what.

“*Name the holders before the burdens,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*A charter that says a burden without saying who carries it is the same as a form with a head on it and nothing under it.*”

Wenna read the four instruments out, with the holders named. A second book of about nine hundred names held by a man of thirty-nine at a mill counter. A copyist paid two pounds a year. A minute of a water board kept in a room over a saddler shop. A woman of thirty-eight who keeps a sluice and refuses things in lanes. A woman who kept an account of a row for nine years and gave it away on the fortieth day stood named in the second part.

A miller with a hundred and seventy households in a book under his counter shifted on his stool. Ott Vernay is thirty-nine. He put one hand flat on the table.

“*My book carries them and I carry the book,*” said Ott Vernay. “*That is the burden I will name. Nothing beyond it.*”

One of the out-town men, grey bearded, pointed at the line about the copyist.

“*And who carries the reading of old hands when she is gone?*” said the grey bearded man. “*Put a learner to her for a year. We will send one.*”

Wenna shook her head. A girl refilled cups from a jug and set the jug down hard enough to slop.

“*No learner is carried in this compact,*” said Wenna Crale. “*The obligation falls on people who can be named and not on an invisible future. Name yourselves or leave the line out.*”

The compact was made at noon. It was six lines long and the sixth line was the exit. The thing all five towns had was a hearth, a beam, a board and a river, or the local equivalent of one of those four. Each town named its holders. Each town took the same exit with the handle on the inside.

Ilyan spoke then. His mistake was honest and it was wrong, and about nine people in that room agreed that it was wrong.

“*Put my name to it as witness and party,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*I have been here six weeks. I have seen every paper.*”

Perdy answered, and three others gave a short word after him, and the refusal took about four sentences.

“*A man who cannot be sent for cannot be obliged by it and cannot oblige anybody by it,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*You are not of any of these towns. You are on nobody book. No form lets you be bound here.*”

“*It is the same refusal as the one on the thirty-eighth day,*” said Wenna. “*It was correct then. It is correct now.*”

Ilyan bowed his head. He was not the same both times, and that was the cost and also the proof. On the thirty-eighth day he had said yes in about four seconds to being named an instrument. Now he had offered to be bound, and had been refused, and he understood the refusal. He went out and stood in the square while the five put their hands to the six lines.

The fever was thirty weeks and five days old. A compact of five towns stood with six lines, and the sixth line was an exit anybody could open. Ilyan Vester was on none of it.
