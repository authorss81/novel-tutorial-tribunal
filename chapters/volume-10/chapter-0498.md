# Chapter 498: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Thursday On Which Four People Walk Four Miles And Eleven And Thirty-Two To A Room In A Stone House And Five Towns Name Who Carries What, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Put His Name To The Six Lines And Is Refused In Four Sentences

Third day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and forty-eighth morning. Ninety-eight days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and five days. Fifty-two days since the division. Forty-six days in this town.

The Stone House held about nine people of this town and four more from outside it, and there were not enough stools, so two of the four sat on the frame of the broken chair that had been carried up on the Tuesday and had not been carried down.

The four had come on foot and by cart, out of towns that are not upon a road of the Shelf, and every one of them had no register and no counter and no appointed person and no Bench within a day’s riding, and they had walked because there is no cart road to any of them. Their coats were dusty. One of them had brought bread and cheese tied in a cloth and had put it on the sill of the open door and nobody had moved it in the first hour.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-two days. The wheel at the mill had been slow twenty-one days completed.

Ilyan Vester sat with them and wanted to be a party to what was made. He had worked six weeks in this town and he believed his name under the six lines would strengthen them, and he is a man who has been on nobody’s book for five weeks and has learned what that is like from the inside of it.

Wenna Crale spread a sheet on the long table. The charter request was in the press against the west wall with six headings and a seventh, and the question on the table on that Thursday was not what a charter should say. It was who carries what.

“*Name the holders before the burdens,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*A charter that says a burden without saying who carries it is the same as a form with a head on it and nothing under it, and we have four of those in this town already and two of them are mine.*”

She read out the four instruments of this town with the holder of each named, and the room let her get through it.

A miller of thirty-nine keeps a second book of about nine hundred names, shut to a hand’s width under his own counter since the fifteenth day after the division. A copyist of thirty-four is paid two pounds a year and is the only public officer in this town that is paid anything. A man of fifty-four holds the water list that goes by tenement, in a room over a saddler’s shop, in a bag with a strap. A woman of thirty-eight keeps the sluice at the Fen and has a key and no authority over it and is out of the water of this town as of the thirtieth day.

And in the second part of the instrument of the fortieth day there is the name of a woman who kept an account of that row for nine years and gave it away in a room over a taproom on the fortieth day with a day on it, and she cannot have it back, and she is not a household.

“*That is four,*” said Wenna. “*Name your four.*”

The four said theirs, one at a time, and it took about half an hour, and the copyist wrote down what each of them said and read it back before the next one began, which took longer and which nobody objected to.

A woman of about fifty at the first of them has a mill book of her own with about six hundred names in it and no copy of it anywhere in the world. A man of about sixty at the second has the whole of the ground of a township in a leather case and a brother who holds half of it in a chest under a bed, and neither of them can be asked about either.

A man of about forty at the third keeps a sluice of his own and has never been paid for it. He said so in the tone of a man reporting the weather, and then said that the last man to keep it before him was his father and that his father was not paid either, and that neither of them has ever had the ground they keep the water on.

---

The fourth one had the smallest list, and the list was nine words long, and it is the whole of that town’s instrument.

“*I write things down and I am the only one who can read what I write,*” said a man of about thirty from the fourth town. “*That is nine words. I do it for nothing. There is nobody above me and nobody below me.*”

“*And when you stop?*” said Wenna.

“*Then it stops. It took me two days to learn the old hand of my own township and I have not had a day in nine years.*”

“*Then the obligation falls on a person who can be named and not on a person who exists in the future,*” said Wenna. “*Name yourselves or leave the line out.*”

She put her pen down flat on the table.

“*I am not going to write a learner into this compact. I have read four pages this week about a shelf that a person four hundred miles off has not opened in six years, and she has written down eleven times that she has not, and the eleventh of those went out of a market square in this town in a hand that was not hers. I am not writing one here. I would be writing a person into a thing she cannot be called on to keep, and I have watched that happen four hundred miles off and I am not doing it in a room over a taproom.*”

A man of the fourth town put his hand flat on the table about four inches from hers and left it there while she was speaking and took it back when she had finished.

A girl came in with a jug and refilled the cups and set the jug down hard enough that it slopped on the oak, and about four people in that room wiped their cups and about five did not notice.

---

A miller of thirty-nine shifted on his stool. He is Ott Vernay. He has the mill four years and about a hundred and seventy households in a book he has never opened since the fifteenth day after the division, and the book is shut to a hand’s width on the other side of that counter, and there is no copy of it anywhere in the world.

Ott Vernay put his book down as a burden and said he would carry it. He put one hand flat on the table to do it.

That was the whole of what he would name. His book carried them and he carried the book, and he had said in a room that he was not sorry for having shut it, and he was not sorry, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry, and he was not going to unsay it because five towns were in a room.

A man of the fourth town told him that nobody had asked him to.

He said that he knew, and that he was telling them what he would carry so that they did not put it on somebody else later, and about four people in that room wrote that sentence down because it is the sentence the whole of the compact rests on and nobody had said it in that form before.

---

They made the compact at noon, and it is six lines long, and the sixth line is the exit.

A line to say that the burden falls on a named person and not on a person who does not exist yet. A line to say that any person may read it. A line to say that no town in it may be asked anything by any of the other four. A line to say that a town may leave. A line for the day, with a hand at the foot of it. And the sixth, the exit, in the words a copyist of this town wrote on the fifty-first day with a handle on the inside.

The thing that all five of them had was a hearth, a beam, a board and a river, or the local equivalent of one of those four. That is worth putting in a minute, and it is in one, because in about ninety thousand people on a road of the Shelf there is a counter for every one of those four things, and in these five towns there is a room and a man in it.

Each town named its holders. Each town took the same exit, with a handle on the inside, on a day’s notice, with no question of character, rate or household, and the sixth line is that exit in the words the copyist of this town wrote on the fifty-first day.

---

Ilyan spoke then, and it was his mistake, and it was honest, and about nine people in that room agreed that it was wrong.

“*Put my name to it as witness and party,*” Ilyan Vester said. “*I have been here six weeks. I have seen every paper in this town. I have carried one of them four hundred miles in a hand that is not mine. You have four holders and none of you can be sent for, and I can go anywhere in this county and be in a room in a day.*”

Perdy Sallow answered, and three other people gave a short word after him, and the refusal took about four sentences.

“*A man who cannot be sent for cannot be obliged by this and cannot oblige anybody by it,*” Perdy Sallow said. “*You are not of any of these five towns. You are on nobody’s book, there is no form in this county that puts you on one, and a party who cannot be compelled is a party who is not carrying anything. If we put you to it, the first time this compact was tested would be the first time somebody asked you whether you had done it, and you could not answer, and neither could we.*”

“*Then I will not be a party. Put me down as a man who was in the room.*”

“*You will be a man who was in the room. That is the whole of what you are in five lines and it is not less than it sounds, and it is not a thing anybody is doing you a favour about.*”

Wenna said the other half of it without being asked, which is what she does.

“*It is the same refusal as the one on the thirty-eighth day after the division,*” she said. “*You said yes in about four seconds to being named an instrument of this town and nine people refused it in nine minutes. It was correct then. It is correct now. You are not the same both times and that is the only difference between the two days that anybody in this room can put a day on.*”

Ilyan bowed his head.

He was not the same both times, and that was the cost and also the proof. On the thirty-eighth day he had said yes in about four seconds to a thing he had not thought about. On this Thursday he had offered to be bound by six lines, in front of four people who had walked four miles and eleven and thirty-two to be in a room, and he had been refused, and he understood the refusal, and about nine people in that room agreed that the understanding was the thing that had changed.

He went out and stood in the square while the five put their hands to the six lines. It took about nine minutes and nobody was asked to sign anything, because there is no form in this county for signing, and a mark beside a line is a mark and a mark can be looked at.

A man of the fourth town came out afterwards and stood beside him for a while without saying anything, and then went back in, and did not offer to shake his hand on the way past, and Ilyan Vester did not offer it either, and both of those are the whole of what happened between them.

A copyist is paid two pounds a year. A miller of thirty-nine is not sorry. A woman of thirty-eight keeps a sluice and is out of the water. A man of about thirty four miles off has nine words written in a hand only he can read, and no day in nine years, and a successor who does not exist yet.

The fever was thirty weeks and five days old. A compact of five towns stood with six lines on it, and the sixth line was an exit that any person among them could open.

The burden of every one of the four instruments in this town had been named by a person who was in the room, and the same was true of the four in the other four towns, and Ilyan Vester was on none of it, and he had been refused in four sentences and had not said a word while it happened.
