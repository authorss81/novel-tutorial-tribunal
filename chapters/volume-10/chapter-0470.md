# Chapter 470: The Second Of Ten Things This Band Did Not Fix Is The North Heel Of A Board’s Frame, And A Man Of Forty-Seven Refuses In A Room In The Words Of A Request That Caused It, And A Corrected Roll Stands In The Autumn, And A Man Of Thirty-One Who Is On Nobody’s Book Goes Eleven Miles Up A Road And Puts A Number On A Counter And Sends It

Third day of the eighty-ninth week. His two hundred and twentieth morning. Seventy days after the settlement. The fever twenty-six weeks and five days. Twenty-four days since the division. Eighteen days in this town.

The water board of Halloway sits in a room over a saddler’s shop at the north end of the square, and the room has a table, nine stools, one window and a fire that was not lit, and there were about twenty people in the square in the wet outside it on the Thursday morning and the door was shut.

There were four lines on the table and they were the request, and it had been made on the Wednesday afternoon after a page out of a book at Kerby had been on a table in the Stone House for one hour and forty minutes, and it had been entered in the minute of this board on the Wednesday evening by a copyist who was not asked to enter it and who entered it.

---

“*Say the request in full and do not say it in your own mouth,*” said Perdy Sallow, “*because a man of sixty-one wrote it and I am not going to be the man who says it as though it were ours.*”

Wenna Crale read it out, standing at the end of the table, in the voice she uses for the minutes.

“*One. That the board mend the north heel of the frame at the sluice, at a cost of nine pounds, the figure being that of the county in the year before last.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*Two. That the cost be levied upon the eighty lots upon the Four Hundred, in the same manner as the fourteen pounds of the scouring.*”

“*Go on and say the last two.*”

“*Three. That the work be done before the water rises, and that the board fix a day for it. Four. That the persons making this request are the nineteen tenements at the Fen, entered as households upon the roll of this town on the twentieth day after the division.*”

“*Say the whole of that again and say it as it is written and not as you would write it, because the second line of it is the line I am going to refuse on and I would like the room to have heard it as it is written.*”

She said it again, as it is written, and the room heard it.

Nobody spoke for about a minute, and then a woman of thirty-eight with a bad hip said four words from the end of the table, where she had been standing since the eighth hour and had not sat down.

“*Ask him where the fourteen pounds went.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it a speech,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*Ask the man who wrote it where the fourteen pounds of the scouring went. It was put on the eighty lots at three and a halfpence a lot and charged in the roll of this year. And the roll of this year was made in the week the common was sold, and it was corrected once, in four minutes, on the Sunday morning, and it stands corrected at one thousand and sixty, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made.*”

The room did that thing rooms do, and Ordwin Cape, who was sixty-one and who had written the request on the back of a form of query in about four minutes on the Wednesday, put both hands flat on the table of a room over a saddler’s shop.

“*Say it slower,*” he said, to himself, and then out loud, and it was for the room.

“*The second line of it is the line I copied.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*I copied it off the minute of the water sitting of the tenth day after the division, because it is the only form of a levy I have ever seen in this town, and the levy of the fourteen pounds is the only money this board has ever moved in nine years, and I copied it out of a book because I did not know any other shape.*”

“*Say the other half of the refusal, because a form is one reason and you have had four minutes to find the other one.*”

“*The water rate is twopence a quarter and it is gathered by the quarter, and this quarter’s money is what the board has in its hand, and on one thousand and sixty households that is eight pounds and sixteen shillings and eightpence. The heel is nine pounds.*”

“*Say the other half of the refusal, because that is the half that does not move when the number moves.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets a board borrow against the next quarter, and there is no form that lets a board spend a quarter’s water on a work that will stand into the year after. If a board of this county may do either of those then there are two documents in a book in Kerby, twenty-two miles off, and I have never seen either of them and neither has anybody in this room.*”

---

So the board declined, and the chair said the refusal himself, and he said it in the words of the request, and he said that he had done it in the words of the request on purpose.

“*That is the request,*” said Perdy Sallow, “*and that is the answer, and they are in the same words because I could not find any others.*”

“*Say the whole of it out loud in this room, and then say it in your own mouth, and I know which of those two is the one you have not said yet.*”

He read it again as a sentence and said it again as a decision, and there was nothing in the second that was not in the first.

Then he said the thing he had not said, which is that the part of this room that matters is not nine men and never was.

“*I am not refusing this,*” he said. “*The one thousand and forty-one households that were on that roll on Saturday morning are refusing it, and the nineteen that were not are the only people in this square who have asked for anything since the common was divided, and I am the mouth of the first lot and I have been that mouth for nine years.*”

“*Go on and finish it, because you have not finished the first sentence and you have not started the second thing.*”

“*The frame is on a list. It is the third thing on the list of this board, and the list is a list of works to be done when there is money. The other two things on it are the cleaning of the tail of the race and a second winding bar. Nine years, and all three of them on that list every year.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this square, because that is the half I have not heard in nine years from anybody.*”

“*And a man of forty-seven did not choose to be the mouth of a thousand people, and he cannot put it down, and the only thing he has ever wanted this board to be is a thing that is not him.*”

---

The list was four lines and it went into the minute at about the tenth hour, and it was copied on the Saturday morning by a woman who is paid two pounds a year and has been for eleven years, and there is no line anywhere in four hundred years of that town that says the copying of a list may be done by anybody else.

“*Say the ten things out loud,*” said Ilyan, from the end of the fourth stool’s row, where he had been standing for the whole of it and had said four words in it.

“*No,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*Say the reason and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*There are ten. There are ten things this town has had in front of it in three weeks and has put down again, and I have got them on the back of a sheet, and I am not going to read them out in a room with the door shut and about twenty people standing on the other side of it.*”

“*Then say how many of them there are and say where the sheet is.*”

“*Ten. And the sheet is in my own hand and it is going to the copyist this morning, and she will have it in the press on the Saturday with a day on it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*It is the only thing I have done as chair of this board in nine years that I would want anybody to be able to ask me about.*”

“*Say the first two of them, because you have said the second one in this room twice and I would like to know the first.*”

“*The first is a hundred and thirty-eight years old and it is on a board nine feet up over a fire in the room at the end of this square. It says that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it kinder than it is.*”

“*Nobody in this town has ever been given it. Nobody in this town is going to be given it this year. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody, and there is one on that board that says it in words, and a thing in words on a board nine feet up is not a thing anybody can be given.*”

“*And the second.*”

“*The second is the north heel. That is the one this room spent an hour and forty minutes on yesterday and four lines on this morning, and it is the second, and it is on the sheet.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*I would like it in the book that a man of sixty-one asked this board for nine pounds and this board said no in the words he asked in, and that the reason is on the second line of his own request and was not put there by anybody else.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for any part of it, and about four of the nine men of the board said nothing at all, and the two who said something said that it was the right thing and then said why, and the why was a form.

Garrick Nye was in the room until about the eleventh hour, and he had been due home on the Thursday, and he said one thing at the door to about twenty people in the wet and then went.

“*I am the county and I am going home. I have been two years on that frame and I have said two years in two years and I am not going to say it a third time in a lane. It is a page. The page is twenty-two miles off. Any person in this county may walk and ask for it, and I have not got it and neither has the board and neither has this man.*”

He put his thumb on the frame of the door as he went through it, which nobody saw and nobody wrote down.

---

On the Wednesday evening a woman of thirty-four who rents a second room in the fourth house of nineteen walked four hundred yards of mud to the second house and told an old woman of eighty-one, in about nine words, that the water of the Hallow is not hers.

The old woman asked her one question, and it was about the rent, and it was the right question, and about four minutes of it went into the answer.

---

And the out-of-roll book was not written in.

It is a line and a name and a day, and the water board may at any time enter a household that is not on the roll, and it has never been written in in this town, and the last entry anywhere in this county is a man at Ostrey with no roof on him, entered by a different board four years back. The chair of this board had the key on him at the tenth hour that morning and had it in a drawer in his own house at the ninth hour at night.

The nineteen houses at the Fen got on the roll of this town by a form in a steward’s office over a shop, corrected in four minutes on the twentieth day after the division, and not by the board’s own book, and there is no line in the minute of this sitting, or of any sitting this year, that says the book was offered and refused, because the book was never offered to anybody by anybody, including on the eleventh day after the division when the chair put the whole of it in four sentences in a lane and was refused in a lane by the woman it was for.

The twenty-fifth day after the division is tomorrow. There is nothing set down for it. A man of forty-seven put a day on a piece of paper in a room over a taproom on the sixteenth day after the division as a courtesy, and has not touched it since, and has said in a room this morning that he is not going to let a day he chose himself be the reason a thing was done late.

The roll of Halloway stands corrected. It stands at one thousand and sixty rated households, out of a figure of one thousand and forty-one that had not moved in eleven years.

The correction is four lines in a form in an office over a shop, with a day on it and a steward’s hand at the foot of it. It is in the press against the west wall and any person of the town may read it.

It is wrong by a hundred and twelve pence and nobody knows which way. The row it was done for holds twenty-two households and not nineteen. Eleven other rows in the town are entered as land in some year and hold households, and there is a twelfth row with a dead man’s name against it and no one in it.

The roll for the autumn is made on a day, out of a day, before the weather turns, by the steward of this town, who will do it correctly and will not know.

A man of thirty-one said out loud in a market square on the Sunday that a corrected roll is a corrected constituency. He was right and it did not help. The sheet of forty-one names in the order of the wards is still in the press, with five houses and a room against the ninth ward.

A fact about the north heel of a frame is on the back of a statement in four hundred and ten words with a fourth paragraph struck out of it in a woman’s own hand and signed across the strike, in a press against a west wall, on the wrong side of a leaf, and four hundred years of that building nobody had ever read the back of anything.

The four letters and a stroke under the ninth clause have not been read by anybody. They were on a road.

---

And then he went and did it himself.

He had a cracked steel pen and a bad leg and a cup of tea that had gone cold in it.

He had a return that was thirty-two days old, with his own name at the head of it, four lines in the margin and a figure in the fourth. It was the only figure in ninety thousand people that an office had not got.

Wenna Crale had refused it on the Saturday before last and had told him what to do with it in about nine sentences, and the ninth had been the whole of it, and he had thought about it every day since, and on the Thursday afternoon he took the pen and wrote three lines at the foot of the margin.

*His two hundred and twentieth morning. Written at Halloway, in the county of Ostrey, which is not upon a road of the Shelf and is eleven miles from the counter at Ostrey and has no register, no counter and no appointed person of its own.*

*Sent to the counter, and not to a person. I have not asked for the answer to come back to me, and a copyist in a stone room refused this on the twelfth day after the division and told me to send it to a book that has a head on it, and she was right, and I carried it for twelve days after that and I have not got a better reason than liking having it.*

He read it twice and he did not cross anything out, and there was nobody in the room to tell him not to.

He wanted a town where his name was not on anything. He had said the want out loud in front of about thirty people in a room at the foot of a hill a long way from here, and had said the price of it out loud in the same room, and the price was that he was not going to get it.

He had got a different thing instead, and he did not have it yet.

A town that had never been on a list of any kind had been put on one at the bottom of his own return, in a place a clerk would write, in a column. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be entered in a book. There is no form that says a town not upon a road of the Shelf may be checked either.

He left the room over the nail shop at about the sixth hour and walked eleven miles.

He had a bad leg and there was a cart on that road going the other way every day of the week and he did not get on it, and that is not a thing anybody in that town ever asked him about and never will.

He got to Ostrey at about the tenth hour with a blister on his right palm the size of a farthing, which was where it had been twelve days before, and the counter is a counter in a room with a press in it and a clerk of about thirty with a bad cough.

She did not ask who he was, because the form has his name on it, and she did not ask where it was written, because the form says.

She asked him one question, and it was the only question, and it is the question a counter asks.

“*Shall I enter a person for the answer to go to?*”

“*No,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*Enter no person. I am on nobody’s book and I have come up the road myself and I would like the answer to go to the office and be kept there, and if anybody ever asks for that figure they can ask the office and not ask me, and I have never once been able to help anybody by being asked.*”

She wrote *no answer* in a column on a sheet with a head on it, and put the sheet into a book that had a head on it, and shut the book, and did not ask him anything else.

The fever was twenty-six weeks and five days old. A figure was in a book that had a head on it and nobody in that town could check it.

A man who had spent eighteen days teaching a town of four thousand people that a thing in a book is a thing a person can be asked about had arranged, in eleven miles on a bad leg, for the whole of it to be a thing that nobody can ask him about.

A bag had come into Halloway on the Tuesday with his letter in it and nothing else, and on the Thursday there was nothing in it for him either.

On a road between here and four hundred miles off, a woman of twenty-eight had folded a sheet in a round hand and had not put her own name to it. She was right and he was not. He would not know that for about a week, and there was nothing he could do about any of it in a room over a nail shop or on a road or in a town that is not on anybody’s list.
