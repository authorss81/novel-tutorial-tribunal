# Chapter 462: A Board Of Nine Is Asked In A Room Over A Taproom A Question It Has Never Been Asked, And Its Chair Says The Answer Is Yes And That The Last Time He Was The Man Who Came In With A Thing Was The Year Before Last, And A Man At Ostrey Has Still Not Paid His Rate, And So He Writes Down The Shape Of The Question And Puts A Day On It And Does Not Answer It

Second day of the eighty-eighth week. His two hundred and twelfth morning. Sixty-two days after the settlement. The fever twenty-five weeks and four days. Sixteen days since the division. Ten days in this town.

Nine men of the water board of Halloway walked down the lane to the Fenrow on the Wednesday morning in the rain and sat down in a room over a taproom, because that room has a table in it and there is nowhere else in that row with a table in it that is not somebody’s kitchen.

There were about nine of the row there and about twenty from the town who had come up the lane in the wet and were left standing on the stair, and the stair is the width of one man, so twenty people on a stair above a taproom is a thing that was heard in the taproom for an hour.

The chair of the board was forty-seven and was called Perdy Sallow, and he had made a mistake walking down there and knew the name of it before he sat down, which is rare, and he said so out loud before anybody had asked him anything.

“*Say the whole of it,*” said Ilyan, from the wall, where he had been for nine of the last ten days.

“*I have brought nine men of a water board into a room over a taproom to be asked a question I could answer in nine seconds at a table in my own kitchen,*” said Sallow. “*And I would like it written down that I said that sentence first, before I said anything else, because in about four years the only thing anybody in this room will remember is that the water board went down to the Fenrow on a Wednesday and asked to be let in.*”

Nobody wrote it down, because there is no minute in that room, and he had said the reason for that in the same breath without noticing he had.

---

The asking was done by a man of sixty-one who had kept the sluice for twenty-two years before the woman who keeps it now and who had a county pension of eleven shillings a year and who had done a sum out loud in a lane a week ago and been thirty-nine pence short.

“*Chair,*” said Ordwin Cape. “*I have got no speech and I have not written one. I have one question and it is the question of the man at Ostrey and it is the question of a tap four hundred yards above my head. The roll is made in the autumn. Anything not on it in the autumn and not in your book is not a household of this town in the year after. You said that in a lane and you would not put it in a minute. I am asking you to put the other half in.*”

“*Say the other half and say it in your own mouth.*”

“*Put the fenrow in the book. Nineteen lines. A name and a day. That is all it is. I have been in this row forty-one years and I have watched your board keep that book shut for four years and I would like it opened while I can still walk to it.*”

---

Sallow said yes.

He said it in about four words and he said it before anybody had finished, and then he sat with his hands on a table in a room over a taproom and let the rest of the sentence sit there on its own, because a man who says yes and then says nothing is doing a thing, and nine men of a board watched him do it.

“*Say the rest of that and do not let anybody else do it for you,*” said Sefa Lund, from the second chair.

“*The answer is yes. The board may at any time enter a household that is not on the roll. I have had that book in a drawer in my own house for nine years and I have the key on me now, and I have never once opened it, and the last entry anywhere in this county was a man at Ostrey with no roof on him four years back and that was a different board and a better reason than ours.*”

“*Then open it,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*No. And I am going to say why in about nine sentences, and none of them are about the water.*”

---

“*The year before last I was the man who came in with a thing,*” said Perdy Sallow, “*and I have said that sentence in this room in my own mouth and nobody made me.*”

“*Say the thing itself and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Ilyan.

“*The thing was a rating at Ostrey. A man there owed the water rate and had been in two rooms about it and had been told to go to the board. I took it up, and I put it in the minute in my own hand with the day on it, and the minute of this board is the only thing in this town that has ever been used against anybody.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*About two years on, that man at Ostrey has paid everything else and has still not paid his rate.*”

“*Say what you want out of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*I want it understood that I have not been afraid of a book in nine years. I have been afraid of a minute, and the two come together, and the day a man uses the one he has the other one forever.*”

Sefa Lund turned the hat over in her hands, which is what she does.

“*Chair. I refused your book in a lane on the Friday before last in front of about nine people and I said that I did not want to be a person with a claim. Have you come down this lane to argue me out of it.*”

“*No,*” said Sallow. “*I have come down it because a man of sixty-one asked me a question in the plainest words anybody has used on me in nine years and because I said yes in four words and I have not been able to un-say it since I got to the top of the lane.*”

---

Then they tried to make it small, and being small was the whole of the trouble, and it took about forty minutes.

A man of thirty-eight proposed that it should be a thing of the clerk. A woman of fifty-one proposed that it should go in the autumn with the roll, where the roll is made, and that the board should only speak to it when the roll was made. A man of forty-four asked whether the board might do it in a room in the town rather than a room over a taproom, so that it would look like what it usually looks like.

And every one of those three was a way of arranging for one person to be the man who came in with a thing, and every one of the three people it would have landed on said no for the same reason and in nearly the same words.

Wenna Crale said it first, and she said it standing, and she said it to the board and not to Sallow.

“*No. I will copy the line into the press of the Stone House on the day the board enters it and not before, and I will not be asked to carry it there. The last time I was asked to carry a thing into a book in this town a stranger read it out in a market square before I had finished writing it, and I have not forgotten which of us it was.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it kindly,*” said Sallow.

“*The other half is that if the line is in the press then the clerk is the officer of this board for the out-of-roll book, and there is no form in this county that says the clerk of this House is an officer of the water board, and in four years the steward will say that the clerk did it on her own.*”

“*And if the stranger did it,*” said Sallow, and turned round on the stool he had brought down the lane.

Nine of the room looked at a man of thirty-one against the wall.

“*Say that again and do not be clever about it,*” he said.

“*You cannot be entered,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*Not in the roll, not in the book, not in the minute, not in the poor rate, not in the ward list, not in anything this town has. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be put in a book, and I have looked for one since Monday and I have not found it, and you have not either, and that is not a compliment, that is the reason you have been useful for ten days and the reason you cannot be the man.*”

“*Say the rest of that in this room, out loud, and go on to the end of it.*”

“*I am going to say the rest of it once and I am going to say it kindly, because you have been in this lane twice in a week and you have carried pails. The only thing you have that nobody else in this room has is that there is nobody in this county who can send for you. That has been worth something to you for ten days and it has been worth more to you than it has been to anybody else in this room, and I have watched you spend it twice in front of me like a man paying with money that is not his.*”

---

So Ilyan did the thing he does, and he did it in a room of about thirty people, and he did it because a woman of thirty-four had struck his fourth paragraph out nine days earlier and he had never found a better shape.

“*Chair. Write the line. Do not make it a request and do not make it a favour. Write it as the board doing a thing it may do at any time, at its own sitting, in its own minute, and let the roll catch up in the autumn and not before.*”

“*Say the rest of that and tell me what you have just done.*”

“*I have just made you a man who uses a power for the first time in nine years and nobody has said thank you to.*”

Perdy Sallow put both hands flat on the table of a room over a taproom, and it was not a thing millers do, and he took them off again.

“*That is the best argument anybody has made to me in this row in nine years,*” he said, “*and it is the exact argument I made to myself on the Friday, and I had already decided it was the one I was afraid of.*”

---

What he wrote before he got up off the stool was not the line. It was four lines long and it is the first thing that man ever wrote in a room over a taproom, and it is in the minute of the board of the second day of this week, and Wenna Crale copied it on the Saturday morning into the minute and read it out in the taproom below to about thirty people, which she had not been asked to do and said she had not been asked to.

It says that the board has been asked whether it will enter the nineteen tenements at the Fen in the out-of-roll book, and that the board has not answered, and that the board will consider the question on the twenty-fifth day after the division of the common.

And then one more line, which is the line that has been argued about since.

“*Say the rest of that and say the day again,*” said Ordwin Cape, when she had finished.

“*The twenty-fifth day after the division.*”

“*Chair. That is six days off and not nine.*”

“*It is six days off. I wrote nine into a copy of it on the Friday in my own kitchen, before anybody had asked me for anything, and I have taken three off it since this morning and I have not told anybody that I have taken three off it until this second.*”

“*Then say why,*” said Ilyan, “*because a day is either a courtesy or a wall and I have been in this town ten days and I cannot tell which one I am looking at.*”

Perdy Sallow did not answer that, and the room did not make him, and about twenty people on a stair above a taproom heard a man of forty-seven not answer a question that a stranger had asked him in the plainest words anybody had used all morning.

He said, at the end, standing up, with the rain going on the roof:

“*It is a courtesy. I wrote it to be a courtesy. And I would like it entered that a man of forty-seven who has sat in this chair nine years said out loud in a room over a taproom that he wrote a day down to be a courtesy, and that nine days is long enough for a man to think of everything he wants to say and not say it, and that on the sixth day somebody is going to come and use me.*”

The fever was twenty-five weeks and four days old, and there were about thirty people in a room and on a stair above a taproom, and a man of forty-seven had put a day on a piece of paper and had shortened it by three and had not known he was going to.

The rain had been going two days. In the lane above, about four hundred yards from that taproom, there were two tin pails in a doorway that had been standing there since the Monday, and on the Wednesday morning one of them was gone.
