# Chapter 459: The Copy Of The Book Is Four Leaves In A Press In The Stone House And Was Made Ninety Years Ago By A Woman Of Twenty-Four, And On The Back Of The Fourth Leaf There Is A Strike Through The Ninth Clause And Two Margins In The Same Hand, And The Second Margin Says She Was Not Able To Learn Whether The Striking Was Of That Clause Alone, And A Man Reads Out The Best News Of His Week And Is Correct And It Does Not Help

Sixth day of the eighty-seventh week. His two hundred and ninth morning. Fifty-nine days after the settlement. The fever twenty-five weeks and one day. Thirteen days since the division.

She had found it on the Friday and she had not said anything about it for two days, and on the Sunday morning she sent for him before the sixth hour, and she sent for him with a boy and a shilling, which is the way she sends for anybody.

“*Say how long you have had it,*” said Ilyan, in the room over the nails, before he had taken his coat off.

“*Since the Friday. I want that written down first, before I show you anything, because everything that happens in the next four minutes is going to be about a piece of paper and I would like it in the book that the person who had it for two days is the person who read the old hand, and not the man.*”

---

The press is against the west wall of the Stone House and it is not locked, and that is the first extraordinary thing about it, and she told him so.

“*Four leaves. A fair copy of the Book, made in the ninety-first year of this town keeping its covenant, by a woman of twenty-four in this office whose name is on the foot of the fourth leaf. It is Isabel Tuke. Any person of this town can read it. It has been read in this room about forty times, and not one of the forty has ever read the back of the fourth leaf, because the front of it is the schedule of the four causes at clause five and everybody wants the causes.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*The back of the fourth leaf has the strike and the two margins. I read both of them on the Friday. I did not sleep on the Friday. I have not said one word about it to anybody until about four minutes ago, because I did not believe it.*”

“*Say why you did not believe it.*”

“*Because my grandmother taught me that a strike is a strike. That is the whole of what is in a hundred years of my family. And I have taught two apprentices that a strike is a strike, and one of them is my grandmother’s granddaughter and she is standing in this room.*”

---

The first margin is in the hand of Isabel Tuke, twenty-four, ninety years ago, and it says this.

“*Struck by order upon a paper brought to me to be fair copied. The paper is not of this town and I have seen the hand of one man only on it.*”

The second margin is on the same leaf, in the same hand, and smaller, and it says this.

“*I have not been able to learn whether the striking was of this clause alone.*”

“*Say that again and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Ilyan, and his voice went, and he did not mind that it went.

“*Say it again, and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*I have not been able to learn whether the striking was of this clause alone.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*The other half is that the striking is at the ninth clause and not at the Book. Clause nine is struck. Clause four is not struck. Clause four is on the second leaf, and it is in the copy in the ordinary hand with the ordinary ink, and it says the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward is common to all households of this town, and it is in this copy as a live clause, and nobody has read the copy from that side in ninety years.*”

“*And say the rest of that, and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Ilyan, and he was already at the press with his hand on the second leaf and he had his sentence ready and it was the right one and it was the wrong one.

“*Then the Book was not repealed. This town has been treating a struck clause as a repeal of the whole Book for ninety years. The fenrow’s objection rests on clause four, and clause four has been in force the whole time, and the House could have read it out in a room of nine men any day in ninety years, and nobody has, because nobody has read the back of a leaf.*”

---

Mab Harrow came in at about the seventh hour, because she is a woman who is at the sluice from six to twelve and the sluice is wound at six and she was on her way up the lane.

She stood in the doorway, which she has done twice in a week, and she listened for about two minutes, and then she asked the third question of her week and it was the shortest of the three.

“*Whose.*”

“*Say the whole of that,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*Whose. Clause four says the water is common to all households of this town. Read that out loud and then tell me which household in that room is not a household.*”

Nobody answered her for about five seconds.

“*Out loud, and all of it,*” said Ilyan, and he said it out loud, and his own sentence came back at him in a room with about four people in it.

The water of the Hallow from the sluice downward is common to all households of this town.

“*The rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Mab Harrow. “*I have washed at that sluice for eleven years. I am thirty-four. I rent the second room of the fourth house. I am not a household. I am not on the roll, I am not in the House, and I have never been in it. Somebody in this room should read me the word in that clause again and tell me which half of it I am.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*The other half is that the word is *household*. The fenrow are not households. The thing that would make them households is the thing the House has just declined to do. So the clause that is supposed to be the answer is written in the only word that shuts the door on us.*”

And Ilyan sat down on the stool in front of the press, with a bad leg, in a stone room, in a town he had come to on Sunday.

“*Then I was wrong about that on Friday, and it was not a hard thing to be wrong about. I have been wrong about something in this town five times in seven days and right about something twice, and I would like the right ones counted as well, because they are the ones that did the damage.*”

“*Say the whole of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*On Sunday I made myself the man who reads. On Tuesday I put a figure in a market. On Wednesday I wrote a sentence for nineteen houses that they had not asked for. On Thursday I put a date in this room that nobody here can check. And on Friday I told a woman in a lane that I would carry her objection to the county and to Ostrey as one document, so that she need never stand in a room with it. It is not carried, and the reason is standing in this room. What is a hundred and thirty-eight years old is a clause that says the same thing in a word that shuts the door on us.*”

“*Say what the right ones were,*” said Mab Harrow.

“*The right one was the ninth clause on the back of the board and the date on it and the witness line with two names in it, and one of the two is not of this town and I cannot read it. And the wrong one was the sentence I said in that lane on the Friday, and it was a sentence about carrying something for somebody else, and I said it before I had thought about it, and I have not carried it, and I would like both of those in the book in that order.*”

---

Then Amos Trill came, and he was sixty-one and he had walked up from the town, and he read the second margin twice, and it took him about four minutes, and he did not say anything for the four minutes.

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*I have been the steward of this town forty years and I have been right about the common for forty years, and I was right in the week I sold the common. And I have never once had to read the back of a leaf, because the front of a leaf is where a man puts the thing he wants looked at. In four hundred years of this town that has been true, and it is going to keep being true, and I am not going to be able to stop it.*”

“*Then say the thing that is under it.*”

“*The thing under it is that a woman of twenty-four wrote two margins ninety years ago and the first one is the only sentence in this building about whoever struck the ninth clause, and it says the paper came from outside this town and she saw the hand of one man only on it. And the paper is not in this House. And the paper is not in this county. And I have no idea where it is and I have had no idea for forty years, because I did not know the sentence existed until about four minutes ago.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it larger than it is,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*The rest of it is that I am not going to be able to say who struck it, and I am not going to be able to say why. I am going to enter both margins in the minute of this House. And I am going to enter that the paper is not of this town, and that this House has had ninety years of a document that told it who was struck and did not read it.*”

“*And that is a thing about this town and not about the fenrow,*” said Ilyan.

“*It is the only thing I have got,*” said the steward of Halloway, “*and I have been waiting nine weeks to find out that I have something, and it is a sentence a woman of twenty-four wrote on the back of a leaf, and I am sixty-one years old and I have never once been shown the back of anything.*”

The fever was twenty-five weeks and one day old. The copy of the Book was four leaves in a press against the west wall that anybody in that town could read, and a clause a hundred and thirty-eight years old was alive in a fair copy made by a woman of twenty-four who had written on the back of it that she could not learn what had been done.

About four people in a stone room had understood that the word in the clause that was supposed to be the answer was the same word that kept them out of the room.
