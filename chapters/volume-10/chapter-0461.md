# Chapter 461: The Roll Of This Town Is Made In The Autumn, And A Man Of Thirty-Nine At The Mill Has Been Keeping A Second Book Since The Saturday Before Last, And In It There Are Eighteen Dashes And One Blank, And He Will Not Let Either Of Them Out Of His Shop

First day of the eighty-eighth week. His two hundred and eleventh morning. Sixty-one days after the settlement. The fever twenty-five weeks and three days. Fifteen days since the division. Nine days in this town.

The rain came in on the Tuesday about the fourth hour and it was still going at the ninth, and the market went on because the market goes on, and about two thousand people were in a cobbled square the size of a good field with their hoods up and their boots in it.

The mill is at the bottom of that square. It sells meal and oil and nails and a great deal of credit, and the credit is against a name and a house and a rent, and the man who sells it is thirty-nine and has had the mill four years.

He had refused credit to a woman on the Saturday before last in a shop with nine people in it, and had said why out loud, and the reason had gone round this town faster than the rain.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan, at the ninth hour, on the wrong side of that counter, with the water coming under the door. “*You stopped her because she is not on the roll. Say what you did about the other eighteen.*”

“*I have not stopped the other eighteen.*”

“*Say how that is, when they are the same eighteen houses.*”

Ott Vernay put his hand on a book under the counter, which he had been doing since the Saturday, and did not move it.

“*Say it and go on to the end of it,*” he said, “*because you have not asked me the whole thing. You have asked me a question about the fenrow and you are standing in my shop.*”

---

So he said the whole thing, and it took him about four minutes, and it was the most useful thing anybody said to Ilyan in nine days.

“*The rate roll and the water list are two books and they do not agree and they are not supposed to.*” He turned the first one round without giving it away. “*The roll counts households, and the roll is made once a year in the autumn, and the roll is a year old before anybody has finished arguing with it. The water list is the board’s and it is kept by the steward and it goes by tenement, because water goes to a building and not to a name.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*So when a man comes to my counter and I cannot find him on the roll, I go to the water list and I look for his house. And if his house is on the water list then he is a person and he pays and I carry him. And if his house is not on the water list then he is a person I have never seen, and I have carried him four years, and that is a different thing.*”

“*And the Fen.*”

“*The Fen is on the water list.*” He said it flatly and it was the first time in nine days he had said a sentence about the row that was not an argument. “*Nineteen tenements, rated as property, in respect of water, at the old rate of the tenement and not of the person. I do not care whose name is on that line. It is a house, it is a rate, and I can sell to the house.*”

---

Then he took out the second book, which was not the counter book, and put it on the counter between them with his own hand still on it.

It was about the size of a prayer book and it had been written four times in four years and it was not a copy of anything.

“*That is not the mill’s book,*” he said. “*That is mine.*”

“*Say what is in it.*”

“*Nine hundred names.*” He turned it round so that Ilyan could see the near page. “*Nine hundred people who buy at this counter on credit and pay me back. And against each of them a mark, and the mark says one of three things, and I have never once shown this to anybody.*”

Three marks down the left-hand column, in a hand that was not a copyist’s hand and had never been in a press.

“*A cross, if the name is on the roll and the rate has come through my counter. A dash, if the name is not on the roll but the water list has the house and the house has been paying me for four years. And a blank, if I have carried the man and I cannot find him on either book and I have been carrying him because I would rather have the debt.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*Nine hundred names and about seven hundred crosses and about a hundred and eighty dashes and about nine blanks, and the nine blanks have been the same nine for four years, and I know every one of them by their door and by what they had last winter.*”

---

And then he turned to the page, and the page had a fold in it the length of a hand, and he had been keeping the fold for a week.

The Fen was on that page. Nineteen lines, in his hand, done in one go on the Saturday afternoon with a pen that had gone blunt twice.

Eighteen dashes.

And then a blank, on the nineteenth line, and under the blank he had written one word in a different ink and smaller.

“*Say the word,*” said Ilyan.

“*Harrow,*” said Ott Vernay. “*And she is not a house. She rents the second room of the fourth house and the fourth house is a dash, and so is the room, and I could have carried her on the house. And I did not, because I did not think of it, and then on Saturday I thought of it and I had already stopped her.*”

He shut it to a hand’s width and kept his hand on it.

“*Say the whole of that, and do not make it a speech.*”

“*Eighteen dashes and one blank,*” said Ilyan, “*and the blank is not a woman who has gone away. The blank is a rate that was never made. And you have had it in a book for four years and it took you one afternoon on the Saturday to write, and it is the only paper in this town that says the Fenrow are people.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it before I do,*” said Ott Vernay, “*because you have got it wrong and you have got it wrong in the way men get it wrong.*”

---

And here is the panel, and it is the only one in this stretch of the year, and it stood about a foot above a book on a mill counter in the rain.

**CLAIM: A household that is not on the roll is not a household that has gone away. It is a household that was never counted, and the counting is done once a year by a man who counts houses, and a house is not a person and a person is not a rate.**

**EVIDENCE: The rate roll of this town was made in the week the common was divided and sold, and on it the nineteen tenements at the Fen are entered as land of the common and not as households. The water list has the same nineteen tenements as property in respect of water. Between those two books there is a gap of one woman, and it is a gap of method and not of fact, and it has been open since the week of the division.**

**BURDEN: The proof that the gap is not a fact is in a shop, in a book kept by a man with a counter, for his own reasons, out of a habit of four years, and he has nine hundred names in it and about nine of them are people the town has no other way of finding. Nobody has asked him for it. He has never offered it. He is not going to be asked twice.**

**CHOICE: Ask him for it, or leave it on the counter where a man with no standing in this town can see it.**

It did not say what a book in a shop is worth. It did not say whose it would be, and it did not say that a person can be found by it in any other way than by asking a man who has said no.

He did not choose anything out of it that day, and he has not told anybody that it stood there.

---

“*Say it and go on to the end of it,*” said Vernay, before Ilyan had got to four words of it.

“*You want it in your book, not mine.*”

“*That is the whole of it.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say why.*”

“*Because a book in this shop is a thing I can use. Nine hundred names and a mark against each, and the mark says whether the man pays, and if it goes into the press in a stone room then it is nine hundred people in a book in a town of four thousand, and a line in a book is a thing a person can be asked about, and I have watched you work that out in nine days and I have not got nine days.*”

He turned the book round again so the near page was showing, which is not what a careful man does.

“*And I will say the half of it that is for me. I keep this because nine hundred people buy on credit off me and the roll is a year old and the roll is made in the autumn, and if I had to find a man on the roll I would find him in a year, and a man who owes me four shillings does not wait a year. That is the whole reason and it is a good reason and it has nothing to do with you.*”

“*Say what it would take.*”

“*Nothing. I am not going to give it to a stranger and I am not going to give it to the clerk, and I want the two of those said out loud in my shop so that if anybody asks me later I can say I said it.*”

---

So Ilyan asked for it in the wrong mouth, at the ninth hour on a wet Tuesday, in about nine seconds, and he asked for it to be copied into the press of the Stone House inside a day, and he did it in a shop with a woman and two men buying nails in it.

Vernay did not raise his voice and he did not take long.

“*No,*” he said. “*And I am going to say why, and you are going to hear the reason, and the reason is better than yours.*”

He put two hands flat on the counter, which is the thing a miller does.

“*You want it in your book, not mine. Your book is in a stone room and anybody in this town can read it, and there is no form in this county of Ostrey that says a man may be left out of a room. And a clerk is a woman who copies things, and she is very good at it, and I have watched a copy of a thing be the thing that people are asked about instead of the thing.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it to yourself, not to me.*”

“*The other half is that you have been in this town nine days and you have already asked me for mine. In about four months there will be a clerk with your number in it and a board behind her, and then she will ask me for it, and I will not be able to say no to her the way I can say no to you, because you are nobody and she is the office.*”

Two men at the counter had stopped choosing nails.

“*Say the rest of that in this shop, out loud, in the ordinary voice, because those two men have heard it and one of them is a foreman and I would like it in the room rather than in your head.*”

He said the rest of it out loud in the ordinary voice.

“*And you have not got a copy of that refusal anywhere. You have got it in your mouth and it dies with you, and I have said the best reason anybody has said to me in nine days to a stranger who is going to go out of my shop in about four minutes.*”

---

Ilyan wanted one thing all that morning and he has not said it out loud in any room, which is a thing he has been proud of for nine weeks.

What he wanted was a paper in this town that said something about the Fen that the Fenrow themselves had not had to ask for, and a man who keeps nine hundred names had just shown him one, and it was standing on a counter with a hand on it, and the hand was the only obstacle and the hand belonged to a man with a counter and no standing whatever in anything else.

He had been in the shop for forty minutes. He asked for the one thing that would have made the hand lift, and he asked for it in nine seconds, and he asked for it into a book he had been in nine days.

The word *blank* stayed in his mouth all the way up the square and it was not the fact of the blank that stayed there. It was that a man of thirty-nine had said *I have seen the hand of one man only on it* about a piece of paper ninety years ago, in a margin, in a hand, and a man of thirty-nine had said it again this morning about a book four years old, standing up, to a woman he was stopping paying credit to, in a shop with nine people in it.

He did not write it down. There was nowhere in Halloway to write it down that would not have made it a thing a person could be asked about, and he had learned that in nine days from a woman with a press.

The fever was twenty-five weeks and three days old, and the rain was coming under the door of a shop at the bottom of a market square, and a second book with nine hundred names in it was shut to a hand’s width and belonged to a man with a counter.

In four hundred yards of churned lane above the sluice there were two tin pails standing in a doorway that nobody had come back for, because the water had come back on the Monday and the race was full, and nobody in that row had thought to say that the pails were theirs again.
