# Chapter 471: The Twenty-Fifth Day After The Division Is A Friday, And A Man Of Forty-Seven Wrote That Day Down On The Sixteenth Day As A Courtesy, And It Arrives With Nothing On The Agenda, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Asks In A Square For The Other Eight To Be Read With The Door Open, And He Says She May Have Them When He Has Found Them

Fourth day of the eighty-ninth week. His two hundred and twenty-first morning. Seventy-one days after the settlement. The fever twenty-six weeks and six days. Twenty-five days since the division. Nineteen days in this town.

The twenty-fifth day after the division was a Friday, and the water board of Halloway did not sit on it, and by about the tenth hour about nine people in a market square had worked out that there was nothing on the agenda because there had never been anything on the agenda.

Ilyan Vester had been in that square since the seventh hour with a sentence ready for it. By the tenth hour he had understood two things about the morning. There was no sitting for his sentence to go into. And his sentence was about the out-of-roll book, and the day was not about the out-of-roll book.

So he did the thing he does. He went round that square for three hours asking people what they thought the day was for, and he wrote nothing down, and he got eleven answers, and four of the eleven were a sentence of his own come back at him.

---

The sentence was this: that a corrected roll is a corrected constituency, and that it is not a number changing, it is a set of doors changing.

He had said it in that square on the Sunday morning at the hundredth hour, in about nine seconds, to about a hundred people. About forty of them had it by the evening and about four of them correctly.

Six days later he heard it four ways.

“*Say that again and say the whole of it,*” said Ilyan, to a man of about fifty at a wool stall.

“*I am saying the roll says who is in the House. A corrected roll is a corrected constituency, and a constituency is who is in the room, and the room is the House.*”

“*That is all I took out of it,*” said the man of fifty, “*and I have not got time to take anything else out of it.*”

At the turnips, a woman of about fifty with a barrow said that the fen is nineteen more seats, and when he asked her where she had got that she said a stranger had said it in a square and she had been standing near him.

A carter of about thirty said that the roll is a list of doors, and he had got it right, and he had got it off a nail on a shed at the bottom of the lane and he did not know who had driven it in.

And a boy of about fifteen, standing at the edge of the awning with nothing to do, said the whole of it, in order, in the words.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The man in the coat said the roll is a list of who is in the room,*” said the boy. “*That is all. I have got it right because I was stood next to him when he said it and my sister was holding the wool.*”

---

He could have put the other three right. A woman of thirty-four was standing eleven feet from him who had checked the half of it for him on a Sunday in a room with a press in it.

The half was that the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it. That the House is nine wards and forty-one heads, five of them in the ninth ward. And that there is no form in this county that says a ward has a number of seats at all.

He had been asked for his view in a square four hundred times in nineteen days and had said it every time. This was the one morning in three weeks when four people in a square needed it, and he said nothing, on purpose, in about four seconds, and could not have told anybody afterwards why.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be entered in a book. A sentence said in a square in this town now belonged to the square. And *a corrected roll is a corrected constituency* was the truest sentence he had said in three weeks, and four people were carrying it round a market who had never been in a room, and one of them had it wrong in a way that was going to be useful to somebody.

He knew that on the Friday morning. He has not written it down anywhere, and there is no minute in this town in which it appears.

---

The chair of the water board came down into the square at about the ninth hour on his own, which is not a thing anybody in that square had seen him do. He was without the minute and without the other eight men, and he stood under the awning of the mill where a steward of forty years had stood on a Sunday and said that he had done a thing.

“*Say what you have come down here for,*” said Ilyan, “*because there are about nine people here and I would like all of them to hear the answer.*”

“*I have come down here to say that I did not call the board,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*I wrote the twenty-fifth day on the sixteenth day in my own kitchen, on the back of a sheet, with the number nine on it.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*I said out loud in a room over a taproom that day that I wrote it to be a courtesy. And I said out loud in a room over a saddler’s shop on the twenty-fourth day that on the ninth day somebody is going to come and use me.*”

“*Where are you standing.*”

“*Under an awning in a market square at the ninth hour on the twenty-fifth day after the division, without a meeting, having had nine days, and nobody has come and used me, and I have not called it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*I cannot tell you which of the two things I am. Whether I am a man of forty-seven who kept a courtesy, or a man who built a wall on the sixteenth day and wrote the word courtesy on it so that he could sleep.*”

He took his hand off the post of the awning, which he had not known he had put it on.

“*And I would like it noticed that in nine years of this chair I have never once not known which of the two things a thing was. The reason I do not know now is that the two of them look identical from the outside, and they looked identical from the inside as well, and I wrote it anyway.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets a man call off a day he wrote himself. There is no form that lets him call one on either. There is a form for a meeting and a form for a rate and a form in a drawer in this town that a man of forty years opened in four minutes on a Sunday, and there is no form anywhere for a day.*”

---

About nine people in a market square did not make a sound, which is about what happens.

“*Why have you no book.*” said Ilyan.

Wenna Crale had come down the lane and was standing at the end of the awning with her sleeves up and a pen behind her ear and no book in her hand, which she has not done in eleven years.

“*Because I have not got anything to copy yet,*” she said. “*A man has to say a thing in a room before there is anything to copy, and he has not said a thing in a room. He has come down here on his own, and a thing said on its own in a market square is not a minute, and I will not make it one.*”

“*Say the other half of that as well,*” said Perdy Sallow, “*because you have been the clerk of this House for eleven years and you have just told a market square that you are a door.*”

“*I have told a market square that a thing said in a room is a thing anybody can be asked about and a thing said on its own in a market square is not. That is not a door. That is me saying I will not be used to open a door on somebody else’s word.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*The difference took me eleven years and it is the whole of what I am worth to this town.*”

---

Then he said why the day was not about the out-of-roll book, and he said it standing in the open air where nine people could hear it.

“*The question is whether this board will enter the nineteen tenements at the Fen in the out-of-roll book, in whose name, on what day, and at whose request. I wrote it on the sixteenth day and set the twenty-fifth day against it. I have not answered it and I am not going to answer it today.*”

“*Say the reason and say it in nine sentences and do not stop in the middle of one.*”

“*An answer is a minute. A minute is a use. The book has been in a drawer in my own house for nine years and I have had the key on me and I have never opened it.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*The last entry anywhere in this county was a man at Ostrey with no roof on him four years back, entered by a different board for a better reason than ours. The day this board uses that book the board owns it, and there is no form in this county that takes it off the board again.*”

“*What does that make the day.*”

“*So the twenty-fifth day after the division is a day on which a board of nine sat in a room over a saddler’s shop and did nothing, and I have not called the sitting, and I am the chair, and there is no form that makes me.*”

He put his hand flat on the post of the awning and took it off again.

“*I have been right about the arithmetic for nine years, and right about the book, and right about the day. Being right about all three has cost this board a day it chose itself. I would like somebody to tell me which of the three was the mistake.*”

---

A woman of thirty-four who rents a second room in a house of nineteen and washes at a sluice had been in that square since the eighth hour and had not said anything. She said it now, and it was to Ilyan and not to the chair.

“*You are the only person in this county that nobody can be sent for,*” said Mab Harrow. “*I have been thinking about that since the Monday I knocked on nineteen doors and you held a bill and said nothing for an hour and eleven minutes.*”

“*Say the rest of it and say which you have decided.*”

“*I said then that I had not decided whether it was a gift or a hole. I have decided the half of it that is a gift and I have not decided the other half.*”

She turned round and looked at the man of forty-seven under the awning, and then back.

“*The half that is a gift is that you can walk into any room in this town and nobody has been sent for you, and that is worth more to you in nineteen days than it has been worth to anybody else in this square.*”

“*And the other half.*”

“*The other half is that when you have gone out of the room there is nobody in it who was sent for, and that is not the same room, and that is the half I have not decided, and I have had it in my mouth since the Saturday.*”

Nobody in that square made anything of it, which was the correct thing to do and which she had not expected.

---

It stood about a foot above a wet cobble in a market square, and it was the only one of its kind in this county.

**CLAIM: A day that a man wrote down for himself is a wall, and a courtesy is a wall, and the two are the same shape from the outside, and the man who built it cannot tell which he built, because the wall is the part of the courtesy that gets used.**

**EVIDENCE: A chair of a water board wrote a day nine days forward on the sixteenth day after the division and said out loud that he wrote it to be a courtesy. He said out loud on the twenty-fourth day that on the ninth day somebody was going to come and use him. The twenty-fifth day after the division is a Friday. On the Friday the board did not sit, the chair did not call it, there was no agenda, and the out-of-roll book was in a drawer in a house in this town and was not written in.**

**BURDEN: The only thing in this town that says which of the two it was is the reason he gave in a room over a taproom on the sixteenth day, that he had not been able to sleep on the third line of a minute of the House. That reason is in his mouth. It is not in a minute, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man shall be asked to put a reason of his own into a book, and the chair of this board has said in a room that he is the mouth of a thousand people and would rather be anything else.**

**CHOICE: Ask him for the reason in a book, so that the day is either a courtesy or a wall on the face of it, or let the twenty-fifth day stand as a thing that happened to a man in a square.**

He chose nothing out of it. He had a reason in his mouth and had been carrying it for nine days, and he has not told anybody since that it stood there, and no minute in this town in which it appears.

---

The copy came out of a press that afternoon and not on the Saturday as the chair had said it would, and there was a reason for that, and the reason was the copyist.

The sheet of ten things this town has had in front of it in three weeks and put down again had been in the chair’s own hand since the Thursday morning. She copied it out in about an hour and a half on the Friday, and put it into the press against the west wall of the Stone House with the twenty-fifth day after the division on it. The door of that building is open and any person of the town may read anything in that press.

“*Why did you put it in a day early.*” said Ilyan, at about the fourth hour, with about eleven people round the awning.

“*Because he told me on Thursday morning that it would be in the press on the Saturday with a day on it. A thing with a day on it is a thing that can be asked about on that day and not the day before it. He did not think of that and I did not ask him.*”

“*Have you ever done it before.*”

“*Never. I have never put anything into that press that the body which owns the thing had not settled. I said no to a man in a room over a taproom about that in my own mouth, and I said no to a man of the county about the north heel of a frame at the eleventh hour on the seventeenth day.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*Both of those were no and this is a different thing. If anybody in this square wants to say it is the same thing, they may say it out loud and I will write it down.*”

Nobody in that square said it.

Then she asked him for the eight.

“*Chair. I am going to ask you for a thing in a market square and I am going to say first why I am asking you for it here and not in a room.*”

Perdy Sallow looked at her for a while.

“*Say the reason and say it in your own mouth.*”

“*Because a room has a door, and about nine times in twenty-five days a thing has been said in a room with the door shut and about twenty people on the other side of it, and every one of those nine times is the reason this town is where it is.*”

“*Go on and say what you want.*”

“*I want the other eight read out. Here, with the door open and about eleven people in it. The square is open to anybody who wants to stand in it and there is nothing behind this awning that is not already behind a mill.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*One is the ninth clause and two is the north heel and I have read one and two in this square, in a room and in a taproom. I have read eight out of ten and I do not know what they are.*”

“*And the nine people who have read it.*”

“*Read them out, so that the eleven of us who have not been in the Stone House since the seventh hour have heard them from a person and not from a leaf.*”

He said the thing he said on the Thursday, and he said it in about eleven words, and they were not the same eleven words, and about four of the eleven people in that square were looking at the press.

“*You may have them when I have found them,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*When will you have found them.*”

“*When I have found them. I have had them on the back of that sheet since Thursday morning and I have read them in my own kitchen.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I am not going to say them in a market square on a day I chose myself, and there is no form in this county that makes a man say a list of ten things in the open air because a copyist asked him to in front of nine people.*”

---

And then Wenna Crale read them out herself, standing at the end of the awning of the mill in a market square at about the fourth hour on a Friday, in the voice she uses for the minutes.

It took about eleven minutes. About nine people heard it. About thirty heard it from a doorway and out in the square.

The first was the ninth clause and the second was the north heel, and she said them exactly as the chair had said them on the Thursday, word for word. About four people in that square heard the words *a hundred and thirty-eight years old* and worked out that they had nothing to do with a water bill.

The eighth was the copyist.

“*Say the eighth again and say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*That a copyist of this town, in twenty-five days, entered into books in this town eleven things that no body of this town had asked her to enter, and read out in a room over a taproom a thing that had been written by a man in a chair, and that every one of the eleven is correct, and that in four hundred years of that building nobody had ever read the back of anything.*”

“*Give me the eleven with the days, because a number of what is a thing a person can be asked about is not the same as a number of nothing.*”

So she said the days, standing at the end of an awning, out loud, in a market square, on a Friday.

“*The sixteenth, four lines out of a chair’s own hand, which I read out in the taproom below to about thirty people and was not asked to read out.*”

“*Give them all of them, one at a time.*”

“*The nineteenth, five lines agreed by nineteen houses at the sixth hour of the evening, entered in the minute of a water board.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*The twentieth, a line saying that the correcting of the roll was the steward’s form and not the board’s, and that the board did not know it existed. The twenty-third, a request of four lines made by a man of sixty-one. The twenty-third, a certified copy of a page out of a book at Kerby, twenty-two miles off.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the list.*”

“*And the twenty-fourth, a refusal, in the words of the request that caused it.*”

“*Who made them,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the awning.

“*I made them. Every one. A man of sixty-one made a request and a woman of thirty-eight made a sum and a steward made a correction and a man of the county made a measurement, and a chair of this board made a day.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I made eleven entries out of those six and read four of the six out in rooms, and that is the whole of the eighth thing. I have never in eleven years entered a thing in this town that no body asked me to enter, and I have done it eleven times in twenty-five days.*”

“*Say the reason and say it in your own mouth.*”

“*The reason is that a person not of this town cannot be entered in a book, and I am the only person in this town that can be asked about any of this, and a thing nobody can be asked about is a thing that does not happen.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And about nine of you would not be standing in this square if I had waited for somebody to ask me to write it down.*”

About thirty people in a market square did not make a sound.

“*Say whether that is the same thing as what he said in a room over a taproom on the sixteenth day,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*It is the same thing and it is the other half of it. I have known the other half for twenty-five days and I did not have it on a sheet, and he has had his eight on a sheet since Thursday morning and he will not read them out.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*Between the two of us this town has a list of ten things and not one line of it in a room.*”

The fever was twenty-six weeks and six days old. A day a man of forty-seven had chosen for himself came and went with nothing on the agenda and no minute and no sitting, and a copyist of this town was paid two pounds a year and read ten things out in a market square at the fourth hour on a Friday. The first of the ten was a hundred and thirty-eight years old and it was nine feet up over a fire in a stone room at the end of that square, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody in this town be given it.
