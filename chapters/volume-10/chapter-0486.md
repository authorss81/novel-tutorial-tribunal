# Chapter 486: A Saturday On Which An Arbitration Happens In A Room Over A Taproom At The Top Of Four Hundred Yards Of Mud, With No Procedure And No Standing And No Rules And No Chair With Power, And About Nine People Turn Up And Bring Eleven More, And It Produces An Instrument And Not A Decision, And About Four People In That Room Understand What Has Happened And The Rest Of Them Are Applauding A Woman Who Has Given Them Six Weeks Out Of Her Own Head

Sixth day of the ninety-first week. His two hundred and thirty-sixth morning. Eighty-six days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and seven days. Forty days since the division. Thirty-four days in this town.

The bar at the head of the sluice is four feet long, and it was not turned at the sixth hour of that Saturday morning for the tenth day running. At about the seventh hour the water came over the low sill of the sluice and stood at the step of the fourth house of nineteen.

About thirty people from the row and from the town were standing round it in the rain, and about nine of them had been there since the sixth hour and had not put a hand to it.

Nobody called the arbitration. There was no notice and there was no agenda and there was nobody whose business it was. About nine people came up four hundred yards of mud to a room over a taproom at about the tenth hour, because the sheet with a head on it and two lines under the head is in a press that anybody in this town may read. They brought eleven more between them. By the time the water had stopped about four hours later there were about thirty people in that room and on that stair.

---

The two lines were read out at about the tenth hour and forty minutes, standing, in the voice a copyist uses for the minutes, and it took about forty seconds.

*That this matter, upon which no body in the county of Ostrey has power, be heard by an arbitration, in a room, in this town, with the door open.*

*And that any person who comes to it be asked nothing touching his character, his rate, or his household.*

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said a man of about thirty-four who had a lot on the Four Hundred and had paid two pounds for it in the week of the division. “*Say who heard it and say how many of them are on the roll of this town, and then say what a room with no body in it is.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*It is a room with about thirty people in it and about nine men of a water board absent, and there is no procedure, and there is no standing, and there are no rules, and there is no chair with power in it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*Every one of those five things was asked for in this room in the first nine minutes by about four different people, and about nine people agreed with the refusal each time.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And a minute belongs to a body, and there is no body, and so there is going to be no minute of this morning, and if anybody in this room wants a minute they can go and find a body and I will copy it for them for two pounds a year.*”

About thirty people in that room and on that stair did not make a sound, and the taproom underneath heard all of it, as it hears everything that happens in that room.

---

And the question was put at about the eleventh hour, and it was put four times, and the four times were four different questions and they are the same question.

“*Say the question and say it in the ordinary voice, and do not do it in a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*What is to be done about the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, and about the north heel of the frame, and about the bar four feet long, and by whom, and in what document.*”

That is the whole of the question, and it had been asked in this town in nine rooms in six weeks and it had been answered correctly four times, and about nine people in that room said the four answers at the same time, in about four different orders.

“*Say the four answers and say them one at a time and do not make them into one thing.*”

“*The board cannot make anybody do it and has said so in a minute with a day on it.*”

“*The House has not been called and the steward will not call it, and he has said out loud that he will not and given a reason, and the reason is good.*”

“*The county office at Kerby is twenty-two miles off, on a road, and it can make a return and put it in a book. It cannot order a board to do a thing, or make anybody answer, or rate anybody, or put a name in a book of this town. There is no other office in this county that has ever heard of any of it.*”

“*And the fourth is that a bar four feet long is not a thing anybody in the county of Ostrey can be made to turn, and the person who used to turn it is out of the water and is not a household, and a household is the only thing anybody here has ever been able to require anything of.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say what is left,*” said Sefa Lund, from the wall, where she had been standing since the tenth hour with her hat in her hands.

“*That is what is left. And about two hundred people in this town have known that since the sixteenth day after the division and about nine of them could say it in a room and the other hundred and ninety-one could not.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And a thing that has been asked for by everybody is not nothing, and that is not a right and it is not a privilege, and it is not a clause, and it is a room. And this is the room, and there are about thirty people in it, and it has no power over anybody in it, and every one of the four of us who can be sent for in this room is in a different building.*”

---

And then the thing happened, and it happened in about eleven minutes, and it happened because about nine people in that room were not able to leave without doing something and the only thing any of them could think of to do was to write.

“*Say what you have written and say why, and say it in this room and not in the taproom,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*I have written a sheet of paper with a head on it,*” said Wenna Crale, “*and I have put the day on it and my hand at the foot of it. It is not a minute and it does not belong to anybody, and it is the first thing I have copied in eleven years that no body of this town has asked me to copy.*”

“*Say the head and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

*In the county of Ostrey. In the town of Halloway. Upon the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, and upon the frame of the said sluice, and upon the bar four feet long at the head of it. An instrument of this town, made on the fortieth day after the division of the common, with no body behind it.*

*First. The instruments of this town are four. A second book of about nine hundred names, held by a man of thirty-nine at a mill counter at the bottom of this square. A copyist, paid two pounds a year, who is the only person in this county able to read the old hand. A minute of a water board, kept in a room over a saddler’s shop. And a woman of thirty-eight who keeps a sluice and refuses things in lanes.*

*Second. The persons of the Fenrow have been told what was done to that row, and by whom, and on what day, and it is set out underneath this line. The woman who has kept it has given it, and her name is at the foot of it, and she is not a household of this town and is out of the water as of the thirtieth day after the division.*

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*” said Sefa Lund, “*because about thirty people in this room have been waiting five weeks for me to say what I am going to say and I would like to do it in a room and not in a lane.*”

And she said it, and it took about nine minutes, and it was the account she has been keeping in her head since the Monday of the twenty-first day after the division.

It is made of four men and four accounts and three weeks, and of eleven other rows in this town that are entered as land and are not land. She said it flat, in the order it happened, with the days on it, and nobody in that room interrupted her once, and about nine people in that room had been waiting five weeks for it.

And about nine people in that room put their hands together when she had finished, and about eleven more of them were talking, and a taproom underneath was hearing the whole of it.

---

And about four people in that room understood what had happened, and they were not the ones who were applauding.

One of the four was the copyist, and she knew because a thing a person can be asked about is a thing with a day on it and a hand at the foot of it. She had entered eleven things in twenty-five days that no body asked her to enter, and had never once written down a thing she was told to be wrong about, and that was the same act, and it took the same eleven minutes.

One of the four was a man of thirty-four with a bill of sale in his coat, and he knew because the sheet could be read out on any day by anybody, and about nine people in that room could be asked about it tomorrow. About nine men of a water board could be asked about it and could answer, because the second part of it is about what they were told and by whom, and they are not in it.

One of the four was a man of thirty-nine with a counter, and he knew because his name and his book are in the first part of it, and a thing a person can be asked about is a thing that cannot be shut afterwards. He had shut his book since the fifteenth day after the division, and had said the reason in a shop, and had been told the reason was a sentence said in a square.

And the fourth was a woman of thirty-four who rents a second room in a house of nineteen, and she knew because a person is a party to a thing the moment their name goes at the foot of a sheet with a day on it. About nine people in that room were not parties to anything on the fortieth day after the division, and were parties to something by the fourth hour of the afternoon.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ivo Cray, “*because four of us have stopped clapping and about twenty-six have not and I would like the room to know which half it is in.*”

“*Say it in about four sentences and do not make it a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*An instrument is a thing a person can be asked about. That is all it is, and it is the whole of it. About six weeks ago a man of thirty-one said that in a square, and about nine people had it right. About four of the four in this room understood what he meant by it, and about nine people in this town do not, and the whole of the difference between those two groups is whether they have been in a room.*”

---

And then he asked for a clause, and it was the wrong thing to do and he did it in front of about thirty people, and it is the last thing he has wanted out of six weeks in this town.

“*Say what you are asking for and say it in the ordinary voice,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*I am asking for one line under that head. Not a right. A line. That says a person may be asked no in this town, and that the answer no is an answer, and that nobody may go on asking after it has been given. One line, and I will read it out myself, and it will be the third thing anybody has written in this town that is not a correction of a roll.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*Can anybody be given that line.*”

“*Yes. Anybody can say it. I have said it in a square once.*”

“*I am not asking whether anybody can say it. I am asking whether anybody can be given it. That is the whole difference and it is the only difference, and it is the whole of what a woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off has spent six years and a half writing about in four pages. You have four of those lines in a coat, and you have said the rest of them in nine rooms of this town, and this is the fifth room.*”

Nobody in that room and on that stair said anything at all.

“*Say the answer and say it in this room,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the wall, where he had been standing since the eleventh hour and had sat down twice and had not called on anybody.

And Ilyan Vester gave the answer, and it is the answer he has been giving in this town since the tenth day after the division, and it took him about eleven seconds, and about thirty people in that room and on that stair heard the fifth room have it.

“*No. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives a person a line like that, and there is no body in this county that can be given one, and there is no office that can hold one.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The ninth clause of the covenant of this town is a hundred and thirty-eight years old, and it is in a fifth hand on the back of a board nine feet up over a fire. It says that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will. It has been read out in a room twice in ninety years, and it has been given to nobody both times, and it is not going to be given to anybody this year.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And the right of refusal was taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years ago and has not been put back, and that clause is two years older than the taking of it, and nobody in the county of Ostrey can verify that date except a man who is on nobody’s book.*”

---

It stood about a foot above the wet boards of a floor in a room over a taproom, and it was the only one of its kind in this county.

**CLAIM: An arbitration with no power produces an instrument and not a decision, because a thing that has been asked for by everybody is not nothing, and nothing is the same shape as a refusal and is not one.**

**EVIDENCE: About nine people went four hundred yards up a lane to a room with a table in it on the Saturday of the fortieth day after the division and brought eleven more. There was no procedure and no standing and no rules and no chair with power, and the water board of this town had answered the same two lines correctly in a minute on the Wednesday and could do nothing with the answer.

**BURDEN: The sheet is a thing a person can be asked about and it binds nobody. It cannot turn a bar, or mend a heel, or put a household on a roll, or give a person a line. It is the first instrument this town has made, and about four people in that room understood that it was one, and about twenty-six of them were applauding a woman who had given six weeks of a row out of her own head. The difference between those two facts is the whole of what a town is.**
**A copyist has written a sheet with a head on it, a day on it, and her hand at the foot of it, with two parts under the head: the four instruments of this town and who holds each, and an account of what the row at the Fen was told and by whom and on what day, given by a woman who is not a household and is out of the water.**

**CHOICE: Put a line in it that says a person may be asked no, and be told that nobody can be given a line, and write the answer into the sheet under the head; or leave the sheet as four instruments and a list of who holds them, and go on.**

He chose the answer, out loud, in a room, in about eleven seconds, and it was the fifth time he had said it in this town and the first time he had said it in a room that was not a room of a body. He wanted a line and he could not be given one and about thirty people in that room heard a man of thirty-one say so without making anything of it, and there is no minute in any book in this county in which it appears that he wanted it.

---

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and seven days old. A sheet of paper with a head on it and two parts under the head, a day on it and a hand at the foot of it, was in a press against a west wall in a stone room in a town of four thousand people, and any person of the town may read it.

It is the first instrument anybody in Halloway has made, and it decides nothing. About twenty-two people in the row below the sluice were parties to it by the fourth hour of the afternoon, and one of them gave away the only thing she had been keeping since the twenty-first day after the division, in a room, with a day on it, and cannot have it back, and nobody thanked her for it, including the man who asked for the only line.
