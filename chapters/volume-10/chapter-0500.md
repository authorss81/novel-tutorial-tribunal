# Chapter 500: The Fifth Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Saturday Which Is The Hundredth Day After The Settlement, On Which A Charter With Six Headings And A Seventh Is Read Out In A Stone Room With The Door Open, And At The Eleventh Hour A Page Comes Back From Four Hundred Miles Off With Two Names In A Witness Line, And One Of The Two Is A Hand A Man Of Thirty-One Has Seen Before Because He Put It There Himself

Fifth day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and fiftieth morning. One hundred days after the settlement. The fever thirty-one weeks and no days. Fifty-four days since the division. Forty-eight days in this town.

The Stone House stood with the door open from the ninth hour. About nine people of the town sat within. About forty-one stools stood ready against the wall for a House that was not called. About two hundred people moved in the square outside in the way they move in the square outside a stone room in a market town on a Saturday, which is slowly, and toward the bread.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-four days. The wheel at the mill had been slow twenty-three days completed.

Wenna Crale stood with the charter request in her hand. It was in the press, it had been read out on the fiftieth day, and it was being read out again because the last man of the ninth ward had said in a room on the forty-ninth day that a thing ought to be heard more than once before it becomes the thing people think it is.

---

She read the six with the day on it, standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and read the fourth with the objection under it, because the objection is part of the heading now and she reads the two together.

Then Ordwin Cape said the seventh and read it plain.

“*Any household or person of this town, being entered or not entered, may leave the water covenant on giving a day’s notice at the Stone House, without forfeit of dwelling, and no question shall be put to character, rate or household.*”

A man in the square shouted a question through the door about whether a person who is entered has to go and say so at the Stone House in person.

A woman on a stool said that was what *giving a day’s notice at the Stone House* meant, and about four people in the square said so at the same time, and the man said that was what he meant and went away.

A child ran across the light and back. A second child stopped in the doorway and would not come in and had to be fetched, and the fetching took about two minutes and everyone waited.

The reading ended and the room went back to its work, which is what a room does.

A man of the ninth ward asked Perdy Sallow, in front of about nine people, whether the seventh heading meant anything this morning or whether it was a thing for later.

Perdy said it meant whatever anybody made it mean on the day they used it. A heading that waited for somebody else to make it mean something was a clause and not an exit.

He had written it in a form he could be asked about every day for a year and would rather be asked about it twenty times than once. About four people in that room said that was the first sensible thing anybody had said about it, and about five had said it a week ago.

The compact holds and the town is outside central control, and that is a fact about a market town of four thousand people on a river and not a victory. A levy of three and a halfpence on each of the eighty lots is charged in the roll that was made on Saturday at one thousand and seventy-four houses. The first collection is thirteen weeks off. The season it was for went on Sunday with a sound and not a finding, and a bar at the tail of the race is being cut out of timber this town can cut.

Nobody in that room was grateful and nobody was ever going to be. A man of thirty-nine had said out loud in a room on the thirty-eighth day after the division that he was not sorry and was not going to be made to be.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry, nothing happened to him on this Saturday, and nothing was going to.

---

The second thing came at the eleventh hour.

A carrier came into the square off the road that runs four hundred miles to a building where a woman of twenty-eight sits. He came through the open door and stood in it.

It was the same carrier who had taken a folded sheet out of this square at dusk on the Friday, four days ago, for a shilling and fourpence out of the two pounds a year that a copyist of this town is paid.

He was holding a page folded in four with nothing on the outside of it.

Wenna Crale took it off him at the door and read the outside of it, which said nothing, and then carried it to the table, and about nine people in that room got up out of their chairs without being asked, the way people do.

She opened it where all could see.

It was a page from a register in the building four hundred miles off. It carried the printed head of that register and the ruling of the columns and one entry, and the entry was a refusal, and the refusal was one a household in that county gave six years ago and that is dead except one person.

At the foot of the entry there was a witness line, and in the witness line there were two names.

One of the two was a name of this town. Four letters and a stroke in a hand that is not of this town, and which nobody in that room can read, and which is in about forty mouths in Halloway as a thing that might be on a road four hundred miles off in a range of about nine hundred refusals on a shelf eleven inches wide.

The other of the two was not four letters and a stroke.

---

Ilyan Vester looked at the witness line and the blood went out of his face and he did not move, and he had about nine seconds.

The other name was a hand. He had seen that hand twice in six weeks and once of the times was on the fortieth day. It was the small upright hand of a recordwright, and he had written about nine hundred words in it on the forty-third day after the division as a fair copy that anybody would take for a letter, and he had put nothing on the outside of the folded sheet, and that sheet had gone out of this town in it.

He had put it there himself. On a form about a town of ninety thousand people, a hundred and forty miles from a city he cannot get to, there is a line that says *witness* and his own hand had been on it, and it had been on it before he came to this town, and nobody in this county knows a thing about it.

He told nobody what it is. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says he shall, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says he may be asked about it either.

He was in a room with the door open, about nine people in it and about two hundred in the square, and about two hundred of those had heard him read out six years of a stranger in the open air on the thirty-ninth day after the division.

Perdy Sallow bent over the page. Ordwin Cape leaned in beside him. Sefa Lund kept her eyes on Ilyan for a long moment and then looked away at the board nine feet up over the fire.

---

And then he made the mistake, and it was his own, and it was the sixth one in a week and it was the last thing he does in this town.

“*Name the second of the two names,*” Ilyan Vester said, “*and read it again, more slowly, one letter at a time, so that anybody in this room who has ever seen that hand can say whether they have seen it before.*”

A man of about thirty-four who had come in from the second ward that morning, and who holds the ninth lot upon the Four Hundred, and who has a bill of sale in his coat, looked at him for a moment and then said it in the ordinary voice of a room.

“*Why do you want a line read more slowly than it was read,*” Ivo Cray said.

And that was the whole of it. The room was not against him. The room was waiting, the way a room waits.

A man of thirty-four had asked a question with the door open and about two hundred people in the square behind him, and the answer was going to have to be a true one or a lie. Both of those cost him the same. One of them cost everybody else more.

“*Because the hand matters,*” Ilyan Vester said.

That was true. It was not the whole of it, and about four people in that room heard the place where the answer stopped.

Nobody in that room can be asked why. A man not of this town is not on anybody’s book. He has said a true sentence and left a gap in it the exact width of the thing he cannot say.

Wenna Crale is the only person in Halloway who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square. She said no on the fifty-first day and has not changed her mind in two days. She looked at him across the table for about three seconds, and then read the second name out again at the same speed as the first.

---

Wenna named what it was and named only that, which is what a copyist does, and which she has done every day for eleven years.

“*It is a page from a register. It carries one entry, and the entry is a refusal, and the refusal was given in another county six years ago by a household that is dead except one person.*”

She turned the page a quarter round on the table so that the witness line was square to the room, and nobody in that room could read it.

“*At the foot of the entry there is a witness line and the line holds two names. One of them is a name of this town and one of them is not. There is no way of telling from this town which of the two witnessed the refusal, or whether the hand that wrote it in this county is the hand whose name is on the line, or whether that is a different thing entirely.*”

She read out the first name. Nobody in that room could read the second.

The Book hung nine feet up over the fire on the north beam and it had not been off that beam and it was not read out again. It is not the Book’s witness line. It is not read out of the Book.

It came four hundred miles in the ordinary hand of a register and it came on a Saturday to about nine people in a stone room with the door open, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says what a person is to do with a page that arrives like that one.

Nobody in that room was asked to do anything. There was nothing to do. A page is a thing a person can be asked about, and this one could be asked about.

The four people in that room who could be asked about it were not asked, because nobody in Halloway has an officer, and the chair of a water board has no power to make anybody answer and would not use it if he had.

The page was put in the press against the west wall, beside the charter, and a copy of it was put beside the door, and the press is unlocked, and the door is open, and any person of the town may read both.

A woman of about thirty came in at about half past the eleventh hour, having heard from a man at the mill that a page had come back, and asked to see it, and was not asked for anything.

She stood at the press for about six minutes with a man on either side of her reading over her shoulder. She could not read the second name either. She did not ask anybody to read it to her, and went out.

That is the whole of what the hundredth day after the settlement did in a stone room in a market town of four thousand people. About nine people were in it, about two hundred were in the square, and one page came back four hundred miles.

The room emptied slowly. The square thinned and went down the street toward the mill. The gate at the head of the sluice stood open on two hinges with the water going through it, and it is the last thing that can be looked at from that room, and there is no form anywhere in the county of Ostrey that says who may shut it and who may open it.

---

He walked out last with the line behind his eyes, and he was wrong about himself in one direction and right about himself in another, and the two of those are the whole of what a man of thirty-one has learned in forty-eight days in a market town of four thousand people.

The cost was the one he has been paying since the twenty-fifth day after the division and it was the same shape on this Saturday as it had been in every other room: he had asked for a thing that would have put him in the middle of the only business anybody in that town had, and a man of thirty-four had asked him why in front of about two hundred people, and he had answered with a true sentence with a gap in it the exact width of the thing he could not say, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be asked the second half.

He was wrong that he could be the hinge. He was right that a page can come back. And a town of four thousand people that has learned in six weeks that a book is a door had not opened one, and would not open one this year.

The right of refusal was unrestored. The out-of-roll book was in a drawer in a house and was not going to be written in. The bar was in the mud on its twenty-fourth day, and nobody could be required to pick it up.

The fever was thirty-one weeks and no days old. One hundred days after the settlement had come on a Saturday. The witness line held two names, and one of them was a hand that a man of thirty-one had put there himself before he came to this town, and he was standing in a square in the rain telling nobody, and there was no form that said he had to and none that said he might not.
