# Chapter 457: The Gates Are Shut On A Friday Morning And There Is No Water Below The Sluice For Nine Days, And The Rot At The North Heel Of The Frame Is Put A Hand On And Called To By A Man From The County, And A Chair Of The Water Board Offers Nineteen Households A Line In A Book That Has Not Been Written In For Eleven Years, And The Woman It Is Offered To Says No In Front Of Everybody, And The Man Who Was Going To Carry It For Her Is Told By Her What A Witness Is

Fifth day of the eighty-seventh week. His two hundred and seventh morning. Fifty-seven days after the settlement. The fever twenty-four weeks and six days. Eleven days since the division.

The gates were shut at about the sixth hour on the Friday morning and it took nine people half an hour, and about two hundred of the town came up the lane to watch it, and the fenrow watched it from their own doors.

The wheel is at the top of the frame and it is nine feet across and it is turned by a bar, and the bar is four feet long, and it takes two people and about an hour. Sefa Lund has wound it twice a day, alone or with her grandmother until the year the grandmother died, and alone since, and it is nine years now, and the bar weighs more than it did in her first year and she has never once said so to anybody who wrote anything down.

“*Say what the frame looks like from the north side,*” said Garrick Nye, “*and do not stand in front of it.*”

He stood to one side.

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The heel on the north side is not a heel any more. It is a wet black end about two feet long where the post has gone soft at the bottom, and you can put your thumb in it up to the first joint, and I have put my thumb in it. If the frame is dropped nine inches in two years, this whole town is under four feet of water in the worst flood.*”

“*Say how long it has been like that.*”

“*Since I was here nine years ago, in the same place, and I told the man who was steward then, and he told the man who was steward after him.*”

Nobody in that lane had been told anything about the two stewards. The board had heard it nine years ago and had put nothing in a book about it. Garrick Nye was a man from the county, and the board’s business was the water below the sluice, and the frame was the board’s frame, and had been for sixty years.

---

There were about nine of the board on the lane and the chair among them, and Sefa Lund, whose house is the fourth of nineteen, and two men of the fenrow, and a woman of nineteen with a roll of paper under her coat in the wet again.

Perdy Sallow got her aside and said it in about four sentences, and he was not unkind about it, and he had the minute of the sitting on him in an oilskin, and he was going to use it and he did not pretend otherwise.

“*The out-of-roll book. A power this board has and has never used. There is a man at Ostrey who came in out of a winter with no roof four years back and a different board entered him, and that is the last time the book has been written in anywhere in this county. It is a line, a name, a day. I can enter the fenrow tomorrow, all nineteen, as households of this town. And the water board cannot strike a household off the roll for a debt of under two years, and I can say in a minute that it will not.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And I want something from you for it. I want the sluice kept for the nine days at the head of the shut gates, and I want somebody up here in the rain turning that bar at the sixth hour and at the twelfth, because the frame has to be wound even shut, and I want that to be put in the minute, because a thing in a minute is a thing somebody can be asked about.*”

“*That is a fair offer and I am going to say no to it in front of about nine people, and I would like you to understand why before I do.*”

---

“*Say it and say it to them and not to me.*”

“*Because on Wednesday I said in a room over a taproom that I want to be a woman who keeps a sluice who is not a household. I said it in front of a man from the county, a woman in a doorway, and a stranger with a bill. I meant it. Two days have passed and I have not found out that I meant a different thing.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And because the minute of this sitting is going to say that the ninth clause is a hundred and thirty-eight years old, and it is going to say the stranger’s sentence with the word *unverified* against it. The whole of what this town has to fight with this year is a sentence it cannot prove. If nineteen houses go on the roll in the morning, then in six weeks somebody will ask how a woman who was not a household on Thursday is a household on Friday. There is one line in that minute and it is mine, and I signed it.*”

Perdy Sallow did not answer that for about nine seconds.

“*Then I have made an offer that was no offer,*” he said. “*I came up here with a power and asked a thing for it, and I told you the price of it before you said a word, and that is not how you do it. I have been doing it wrong for four years, and the only reason it took this long is that nobody in this county had anything to sell me.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*The other half is that I am right that you are not a household, and that the reason you are not a household is that a roll was made in a week when the land changed and nobody thought of the water, and that roll is a fortnight old and it is going to be a year old in the autumn, and in the autumn there is another roll, and if nothing is done then you are not a household of this town in a way that cannot be undone in a morning.*”

“*Say that last part again and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Ilyan, and he was already walking over, and he had already got the sentence ready, and it was the right sentence, and it was a bad sentence, and it was already out.

“*I will carry it. I will take the objection of nineteen houses to the county and to the bench at Ostrey as one document, with the ninth clause on it and the rot in the frame and the roll. I will not let it be three matters in three rooms. And you will not have to be in a room with any of it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And if you do that then nobody can ask me what I said. You will be in nine rooms and I will be in none of them, and everything anybody wants to know about the ninth clause will be in your mouth, and I will not have said it, and I will not be able to correct it, and there are already two sentences in this town that I have said and would rather not have said.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*The other half is that a man who links people’s accounts does not speak for a witness. Not in a room, not in a county, not anywhere. He gets them to say it and he keeps them apart. You said that to me on a Friday in a city forty-one miles off, in a room of about thirty people, and you did not tell me, you said it. I have thought about it every day since, and I have broken it inside five days in a town where I have been five days.*”

“*Say that in the lane and not to me,*” said Ilyan, “*and say it out loud, and do not be gentle about it, because I am about thirty feet away and about nine people can hear both of us and two of them can write.*”

She said it in the lane. She said it in the ordinary voice, without any of the room in it, and about eleven people heard it, and Perdy Sallow wrote three lines in the minute of the sitting on the back of an oilskin with a pencil, and a woman of nineteen with a roll under her coat heard every word of it and did not move.

“*Then say the offer again in the minute,*” said Sefa Lund, “*as it was made, and the price, and my declining of it, and that I declined it and not that anybody refused me anything.*”

---

The out-of-roll book was not written in. The chair of the water board went back down the lane with it still shut, and about two hundred people in that lane watched him go.

Garrick Nye stayed, because a man from the county stays at a shut sluice.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*Two years. And I have been in this county a long time and I have never said a thing was a year away unless I had a way of stopping it.*”

“*Say the way of stopping it.*”

“*There is not one. Not for me. I go back on Thursday and I say the frame is going and nobody writes it down. I have said it twice in nine years and both times a man told a man.*”

He looked at the wet black end of the north heel for a while.

“*The other thing I can do is put a thing in the water board’s own minute, and I cannot do that either, because I am not of this board and the board does not take paper from the county. And the only person in this town who can get a thing into a minute is a clerk, and the clerk is the woman standing over there, and the clerk is paid two pounds a year and does not want it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And the other other thing is that the frame belongs to the board, and the board is nine men, and every one of the nine has just voted fourteen pounds on the race. The frame is the reason the fourteen pounds is worth spending, and not one of the nine has written a word about the heel.*”

---

Then Sefa Lund said the thing that turned the week over, and she said it to Perdy Sallow’s back as he was going down the lane, and he stopped and turned round, and she did not take a moment over it.

“*Chair. The roll for next year is made in the autumn.*”

“*It is.*”

“*Say the rest of it out loud in this lane, because I am not a household and I am asking you to say a thing about a household.*”

Perdy Sallow stood in about two inches of water in a lane with two hundred people in it and said it out loud.

“*The roll is made in the autumn, and anything not on this year’s roll and not in the out-of-roll book is not a household of this town in the year after. That is not a rule. I have made no rule. That is what a roll is, and I have not been able to make anybody in this town understand that since the spring.*”

“*Say it again, and then say whether you will put it in the minute.*”

“*I will not put it in the minute.*”

“*Then say why not.*”

“*Because it is not a decision of this board, and if I put it in a minute it becomes a decision of this board, and the day somebody wants to strike a household off that roll that minute is the thing they will be holding in their hand, and I have spent nine years finding out that the minute of this board is the only thing this town has ever used against anybody.*”

Nobody in that lane said anything, and the rain came off the wood above the sluice, and about two hundred people stood in it and did not make a sound.

The fever was twenty-four weeks and six days old. The gates of a sluice were shut and no water was going below them for nine days, and a man from the county had put his thumb two inches into a wet black heel and said it would be a year.

A chair of the water board had offered nineteen households a line in a book that has never been written in, and had been refused in a lane. A woman of thirty-four had told a man with a bill in his hand exactly what a witness is. And the roll for next year was to be made in the autumn out of a book that had never been written in anywhere in that county.
