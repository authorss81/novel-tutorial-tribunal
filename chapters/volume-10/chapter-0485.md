# Chapter 485: A Friday On Which About Two Hundred People Are In A Market Square In A Town Of Four Thousand People Because The Door Of The Stone House Is Open And There Is No Meeting To Go To, And A Man Who Is On Nobody’s Book Reads Out Loud In That Square A Sheet Of Paper Folded In Four With Nothing On The Outside Of It, And It Is About Six Years And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Four Hundred Miles Off Whom Nobody In That Square Has Ever Met, And A Thing Read Aloud In A Square Is Not A Thing That Can Be Asked About, And He Had Said The Opposite In Nine Rooms

Fifth day of the ninety-first week. His two hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Eighty-five days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and six days. Thirty-nine days since the division. Thirty-three days in this town.

The Stone House has a hearth in the corner and a press against the west wall and a long table under a beam nine feet up. The door of the building is open, and it is open all day every day.

Nobody has called any meeting of anything in it since the third day of this week, when a board of nine men sat in a room over a saddler’s shop and answered a sheet of paper correctly and could do nothing about any of it.

About nine people were inside that building on the Friday morning of the thirty-ninth day after the division, and about two hundred people were in the square outside it.

They had not come for anything. There was no market, because the market in Halloway is on a Tuesday and this was a Friday. About nine of the two hundred had come because the sheet with a head on it and two lines under the head is in a press any person of this town may read. The other hundred and ninety-one had come because a man of thirty-nine had said nine words in a shop on the Monday morning, and this was the day before the Saturday in the nine words.

The water was a foot above the low sill of the sluice at about the sixth hour of that morning, and about two hundred people in that square had been up the lane to look at it.

---

He read it out in the square, and that is the mistake. It is a mistake he had been told about on the fourth day after the division, by a copyist standing at a table in a press room in front of nine people, and had been told about again on the thirty-first day in the same square. He had heard it twice and he did it anyway.

He stood under the awning of the mill at about the eleventh hour of the Friday morning with the sheet in his hand. The sheet was folded in four and there was nothing on the outside of it. He took it out of the fold in front of about two hundred people, and the sound of the fold going was the loudest thing in that square for a minute.

“*Say what you are holding and say where it came from,*” said Wenna Crale, who had come out of the Stone House door and was standing at the end of the awning and had not been asked to come and had come.

“*It is a sheet of paper folded in four with nothing on the outside of it and it came out of a bag that came into this town on the Tuesday of the twenty-ninth day after the division off a carrier who has driven that road for thirty-one years.*”

“*Go on and say who wrote it and say what the hand is.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight wrote it four hundred miles off, and she is a recordwright, and she has been a recordwright for six years and a half. The hand on this one is the small upright hand, which is the hand she makes her fair copies in. I asked her in a letter not to write in it, and she used it for this and not for the four pages, and she was right about that and I was wrong about it four hundred miles off for nine days.*”

Nobody in that market square made a sound.

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said Ordwin Cape, from the edge of the awning.

“*It is four lines. It is about six years. It is about a refusal that was given six years ago by a household that is dead except one person, and a claim that the refusal be entered upon a register of refusals, and about why it has not been entered.*”

And he read the four lines out, and he read them slowly, and it took him about four minutes, and about two hundred people in a market square in a town of four thousand people heard the whole of it.

*A refusal was given six years ago by a household that is dead except one person. The claim that it be entered upon a register of refusals has not been entered for six years.*

*A refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it, and the person who witnessed it is not findable, and I have been told at a counter that it is not findable four times in six years, and I have not gone and looked for it myself.*

*And if I look, and the name is in the range, then the refusal was witnessed, and then the claim can be filed, and then my name goes on the claim as the person presenting it.*

*And I am the only person in the world who can sign it, and I have not signed it for six years, and I have told nobody, and I have told him, on a road, on the second of two sheets, in a hand he asked me not to use.*

About two hundred people in that square did not make a sound for about a minute, which is not what about two hundred people usually do.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*” said Sefa Lund, who had come up from the lane and was at the front.

“*That is the whole of it, and it was read out in this square on the Thursday of the thirty-first day after the division to about thirty people, and it cannot be un-read, and about a hundred and seventy people have now had it a second time.*”

---

And then a boy of about fifteen who had been in that square on the Sunday of the twentieth day with his sister holding wool asked the first question, and it took about four seconds to ask and about nine people tried to answer it.

“*Who witnessed it.*”

That was the question. It was the only question about the four lines that anybody in that square had, and it is the whole of the four lines.

About two hundred people in a town of four thousand people found out in about eleven seconds that they had just been told the middle of somebody’s life in another county, and had no way of getting at any more of it.

Ilyan Vester stood under the awning of the mill with about two hundred people in front of him and could not answer it. The answer is four hundred miles off in a building of four hundred people, and the woman who knows it is not there and has not been asked.

“*Say the question again and say it into the square,*” he said, and the boy said it again, and the square gave him the second it, and then about nine people in the square found out at the same moment what the question was, and about nine people turned round and looked at the place where Ilyan Vester was standing.

“*Say the answer and say it out loud in this square,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*I cannot answer it. I have no standing in this county, I am not of this town, there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets me be asked a question about another county, and the person who can answer it is four hundred miles off and has not been asked.*”

“*Then ask her,*” said a woman of about thirty with a child on her hip.

“*I cannot. There is no form in this county that lets anybody send a letter to a person about a question, and I have written to her twice in five weeks and one of the two was four days late, and there is no way of telling her anything that is true in the time it takes for a thing to matter.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Wenna Crale, from the end of the awning.

“*And about two hundred people in this square have just had six years of a woman they have never met, and not one of them can be asked about any of it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The only person in that square who could be asked about it is the man who read it out, and he is on nobody’s book. That is not a thing anybody in this county has ever been able to do about a document either.*”

---

And then Mab Harrow said the opposite back to him out loud in about two hundred people’s hearing.

She is thirty-four. She rents the second room of the fourth house of nineteen and washes at a sluice, and she is not a head of household and so has never been in the House in eleven years. She had been in six of the nine rooms in which he had said the other half of it.

“*Say what you have come down here to do and do not do it in a lane,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*I have come down here to say the sentence back to you in front of the people you have just said it to, and I am not going to be asked about it afterwards, because I am not going to be in the room.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And you will do exactly what you did in a shop on the Friday of the thirty-second day after the division, which is to stand there and not say the next thing.*”

Nobody in that square made a sound.

“*Go on and say the thing.*”

“*A thing read out in a square is a thing a person has been said to have said. That is the opposite of a thing anybody can be asked about, and you have said so yourself in nine rooms in this town. You said it on the fourth day after the division to about nine people in a stone room, and you have said it four times since.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*You are the man in this square who cannot be sent for, and you have spent that like a purse for five weeks. A boy of fifteen has just asked you a question about six years of a woman’s life, and you have had to stand in front of about two hundred people and not be able to answer it, because you read her to them out loud in the open air.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And the thing she wrote is not a document. It is a letter from a person to a person, it is in a hand, and it came out of a bag. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a thing read out in a market square by a man who is on nobody’s book becomes a document. I have been a copyist for eleven years, and I could not enter it if I tried, and I am not going to try.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And that is the half of it that is a gift. I said the other half in a shop a week ago and you have not put it down.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The half that is a gift is that a man can walk into a square of two hundred people and say six years of a stranger’s grief out loud, and be heard by all of them in one morning. The half that is not a gift is that not one of those two hundred people can be sent for about any of it, and neither can you. The door you opened was not a door. It was a window, and about two hundred people have stood in front of it and got wet.*”

---

And the square did the thing that about two hundred people in a town of four thousand people do when a thing has been said in it that nobody can be carried out of, which is that it went on doing the day.

About eleven people went up the lane to look at the water. A woman of about thirty with a child on her hip said the sentence about the roll being a list of doors and about four of the two hundred had it right, and nobody in that square knew that it was in a shed at the bottom of it and not in any of their mouths. The miller of thirty-nine stood in his own doorway and did not say anything at all, and about nine people in that square noticed that he had said nothing, which is a thing about a man that is not a sentence.

The sheet went back into the fold and the fold went back into his coat. About two hundred people in a market square in a town of four thousand people had each had six years of a woman they had never met, and had each of them gone home with it. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person may be given a thing back.

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and six days old, and about two hundred people in a market square had heard a sheet of paper folded in four with nothing on the outside of it. About nine of them understood what had been done to them, and the rest had been told and had believed it.

The water at the sluice was a foot above the low sill and a bar four feet long was on its ninth day in the mud. Tomorrow was the Saturday that a man of thirty-nine had named in nine words on the Monday morning, and about thirty of the two hundred said out loud in the square that they would be at the head of it in the morning.
