# Chapter 473: A Sunday In The Fourth House Of Nineteen With About Eleven People In The Kitchen, Four Men Asked One At A Time What They Had Been Told And By Whom, Three Versions Of The Same Three Weeks That Do Not Agree, Two People Of The Row Told Nothing Carrying All Of It, And A Woman Of Thirty-Eight Who Is Not A Person With A Claim Finding Out That She Has Been Keeping The Only Account There Is

Sixth day of the eighty-ninth week. His two hundred and twenty-third morning. Seventy-three days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and one day. Twenty-seven days since the division. Twenty-one days in this town.

The fourth house of nineteen has one kitchen, one table, four chairs and a bench, and on the Sunday morning of the twenty-seventh day after the division about eleven of the twenty-three people in that row were in it, and none of them was a stranger, and there was no minute in the room and nothing on the table with a head on it.

Nobody in Halloway had asked them to meet. It had simply happened, the way a thing happens in a row of nineteen houses when it has been three weeks.

The question that was put at about the tenth hour, by Mab Harrow, who is thirty-four and rents the second room of that house and has been eleven years in it, was this.

“*Say what we have agreed to,*” she said. “*Somebody in this room knows and I have been round nineteen doors since the Saturday before last and I have not found out.*”

---

Nobody in that kitchen knew, and about nine of the eleven had an answer, and the nine answers were not the same answer.

That was the finding, and it took the whole morning, and it was not what anybody expected.

Four of the men in that room had been told something on their own, in a room or a shop or a lane, by a person with a reason, and none of the four had been told the same thing, and none of the four knew that the other three had been told anything at all.

They were asked one at a time, in the same kitchen, in front of the same eleven people, and they answered, and nobody was contradicted on a fact.

The first was Wat Priddy, who is about thirty-four and pays a shilling a week in coin for the back room of the second house of nineteen and has done for two years.

“*Say who told you and say what they told you,*” said Sefa Lund, from the rail.

“*The man at the mill. Thirty-nine. On the Saturday after the water came back down the race, and I was in his shop with my ration on the counter and I had been in his shop for seven years and I had never once been in a book in there.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*He told me the Fen is a page of his own book and not of anybody else’s book, and that against the Fen there is eighteen dashes and one blank, and that a dash is a house that pays and not a name on the roll.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say why he told you.*”

“*Because I asked him how a person is found. I asked him how anybody finds anybody in this town. And he said: out of this book, and out of a book with a head on it, and if I am not in either of them then I am a man he has been carrying because he would rather have the debt.*”

Nobody in that kitchen said anything for a moment.

“*What did he tell you to do about it,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*Nothing. He said he was not the office and could not change it and did not want to. He said: you are a house and not a person, and that is not an injustice, that is what the word means.*”

The second was Coll Sarn, who is twenty-two, and who was born in the ninth house of nineteen and came up off the low ground at twenty and has had four beds in this town and none of them his own.

“*Say who told you and say what they told you.*”

“*The chair of the water board. In a room over a saddler’s shop, on the Saturday, at the sixth hour of the evening, and there were about forty people in the square outside and I was in it because I go and look at a thing when there is a queue.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*He said that nobody in that room asked for the question that was being put, and that he had put it in himself, and that three men at that table said the same three sentences word for word, and that the third of the three was the one that could not be argued with at all.*”

“*Say the rest of it and say what the third sentence was and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*That the fenrow have not asked. And he said: if the third one goes, the other two go with it inside a year, and then that room is not a water board, it is nine men deciding what a row of nineteen are. And he looked at me when he said it, and I do not think he knew I was there.*”

The third was Ordwin Cape, who is sixty-one and has a county pension of eleven shillings a year and cannot hand it over, and who was the one who got the five lines out of that row on the nineteenth day.

“*You,*” said Mab Harrow. “*You were told about a form.*”

“*By the steward of this town, in a market square, on the eighth day after the division, in front of about forty people, and he had not been asked any more than I had.*”

“*What he told you, and do not make it a speech.*”

“*He told me there is a form in an office over a shop that a roll may be corrected on in respect of a household entered as land. He told me it is the steward’s form and not the board’s, and that the board has no part in it and did not know it existed.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And he said: I have had it in a drawer for forty years and I told a stranger about it in this square nineteen days ago and I did not open it. And I stood in that square and I understood that he was telling me there was a door and that the door was not locked, and I have not been able to put it down since, and I did not tell the row about it because I did not want to be the man who came in with a thing.*”

The fourth was Renn Selby, who is forty-seven and is of the eleventh house of nineteen and has been in that row all his life.

“*Say who told you and say what they told you,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*The man of the county. At the eleventh hour of a Friday in the rain, at the head of the sluice, with about twenty people standing in it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*He said the north heel of that frame is a season and not two years, and that he had said two years for two years and would not un-say a number in front of twenty people to say so. And that the county put a new heel at nine pounds two years ago, and that the page saying so is in a book twenty-two miles off.*”

“*Go on, and finish it.*”

“*And then he said the half of it that I have not been able to put down. He said: no law in that county makes anybody show that page to anybody, and the reason nobody in this town has seen it is that nobody in this town has walked twenty-two miles and asked.*”

---

And then Mab Harrow, who is thirty-four, and who had been in that kitchen since the eighth hour, said the thing that made about eleven people in a room be quiet.

“*Say the rest of that and say it in your own mouth,*” said Ilyan Vester, who was not there.

She said it to the kitchen.

“*Then none of you were told the same thing and none of you were told the whole of it, and every one of you has been telling the rest of us what you were told, and I have been round nineteen doors twice and I have been believed four times and I have believed you four times, and I have been carrying four different accounts of what is being done to this row and adding them up wrong.*”

“*Say the four accounts and say them one at a time,*” said Sefa Lund.

So she said them, standing at the rail of a kitchen in the fourth house of nineteen, at about the eleventh hour on a Sunday, to about eleven people.

“*Wat Priddy has been told that this row is a page in a man’s book at the bottom of a market square and that the page is the only place any of us is written down. Coll Sarn has been told that if this row ever asks for anything the row stops being a row and becomes a thing nine men decide about. Ordwin Cape has been told there is a door in an office over a shop and that the door is not locked. Renn Selby has been told that a frame is going to cost nine pounds and that nobody in this town has asked to see the paper that says so.*”

“*All four of those are true,*” said Ordwin Cape, “*and I have had three of them in my head for a fortnight and I have never once said them at this table.*”

“*And none of the four of you is wrong,*” said Mab Harrow. “*And every one of you has been carrying a page. And I have been carrying the four of them, and Sefa has been carrying something else, and the two of us have been carrying all of it because nobody told us anything.*”

---

And the finding about the two of them came out of the floor, out of the way a bar is put down, at about the twelfth hour.

It was Coll Sarn who put it there, and he put it there because he was the only man in that kitchen who had been in no room at all, and because nobody had told him anything, and because he had spent three weeks writing numbers on the back of a bill.

“*Say what you have found and say it in this kitchen,*” said Sefa Lund, “*because you have not said one word since the twelfth hour of the Sunday and you have a bill behind you with a number on it.*”

“*Say the number out loud, and say what it is a number of,*” said a voice at the door, and Ilyan Vester was in it, and he has said that sentence in nine rooms in this town and had not said it in one of them until that moment.

“*Twenty-two. Nineteen doors and twenty-two households, and that is the whole of the row and it has been in that bill since the twenty-first day after the division.*”

“*Go on,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And eleven more rows in this town that are entered as land and are not land. And the two of those numbers were put on a bill by me and by her, and she wrote them, and I wrote the figures, and about nine people in a mill read nine names off a counter to a woman of thirty-four on the Monday.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say who is holding it.*”

“*She is. It has been in her head since the Monday, and there are about nine more things in it than there are on that bill, and I have asked her twice since the Saturday what the row has agreed to and she has said the same four sentences both times, and the four sentences are about the water and the five lines and the four lines and the frame, and they are not the same four sentences as any of yours.*”

---

So Sefa Lund was asked, in her own kitchen, in front of about eleven people, what the row had agreed to.

And she gave the same four sentences, and they were the sentences she gave on the twelfth day after the division, when she said she did not want to be a person with a claim, and nobody in that kitchen had heard them since, and they were not about the four men.

They were about the fifth hour and the twelfth hour and two people and an hour, twice a day, whether or not the gates were shut.

“*That is not a thing a row agrees to,*” said Ordwin Cape, after a while, “*and it is the only thing in this kitchen that is true every day.*”

“*It is nine years,*” said Sefa Lund. “*And it is a bar that takes two people, and it is an hour, and it is at the sixth hour and at the twelfth hour, and it is done whether the gates are shut or open, and there has never been a morning in nine years that it was not done.*”

Nobody in that kitchen said anything at all for about nine seconds.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Mab Harrow, “*because you have just said a number and a number is a thing a person can be asked about.*”

“*I have not got the number written anywhere. I have got it in my head and I have had it in my head for nine years and you cannot have it, because I am the only one who winds it, and if I put it on a bill in a press in the Stone House then the next thing anybody says about this row is a number about this row, and I said in a lane on the seventeenth day that I would not hand this row to a market in one week, and I have not.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say the other half of it,*” said Coll Sarn, “*because you have said the half you said before and you are doing the thing you told the man in the coat not to do in a lane in the rain.*”

“*I have done that. I did it on the Monday with the count and I said out loud in that lane that I was wrong and why, and about four people in that lane have it correctly and about four do not.*”

“*Say what the other half of it is, and say it in this kitchen, because I have been in nine rooms in this town in three weeks and I have been in none of them as a person of this row, and you are the only one in here who has been in all of them.*”

She put the wet hat on her head and took it off again, which is what she does.

“*The other half is that keeping it is a claim. And I said in a lane on the eleventh day that I did not want to be a person with a claim, and a form was put in four sentences in front of about nine people and I refused it, and that is the only thing I have ever wanted out of this business in nine years.*”

“*Finish it.*”

“*And I have been keeping the accounts of this row since the Monday, and I did not decide to, and nobody asked me to, and there is no form in this county that says a person shall keep an account of a row, and the accounts are in my head, and that is the only account of anything in this row that exists.*”

“*What is the claim made of, in your own mouth, because a claim that is not made of anything is not a claim.*”

“*It is made of what this row has been told, and by whom, and on what day, and what was said back. Four men, four accounts, three weeks, and eleven other rows in this town that are land and are not land. And two of us carrying it because nobody told us anything. And that is a claim and it is mine and I did not make it and I cannot give it up, and the only person in this county who could hold it is a copyist in a stone room who is paid two pounds a year and has entered eleven things in three weeks that no body asked her to enter.*”

Nobody in that kitchen thanked her for any part of it.

He was not in the row and he was not told and he did not know, and that is the whole of his part in it.

He was in a room over a nail shop four hundred yards from the end of that lane on the Sunday morning with a cup of tea that had gone cold in it. He had been in three of the four rooms where the four men were told their four things, and he had not been in the fourth, and he had never once been in a kitchen of that row in twenty-one days.

He had spent three weeks putting a sentence into every room he went into. About eleven people in a kitchen four hundred yards away had spent three weeks failing to put one sentence into any of theirs, and had done it four hundred yards from a man who could have written it out for them in about ninety seconds, and he would not have been allowed to knock.

He did not go up the lane on the Sunday. He had thought about it at about the eighth hour and had decided not to, and the reason he gave himself was the reason Mab Harrow had given a woman of thirty-four in a doorway on the fifteenth day, that a row which has been asked by a stranger becomes a thing about a stranger.

And the reason was not that. The reason was that he had worked out, standing in a room over a nail shop with cold tea in a cracked cup, that eleven people in a kitchen were doing the only thing in this town that he could not do, which was to find out what they had agreed to, and that if he had walked four hundred yards up that lane at about the tenth hour he would have taken it off them, and that a woman of thirty-eight had been taking things off him for three weeks in lanes and in kitchens and in rooms with a fire lit, and had been right every time.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and one day old. About eleven people were in a kitchen in the fourth house of nineteen in a town of four thousand people, and four men had been asked one at a time what they had been told, and two women had been carrying all of it, and one of the two had been carrying it since the Monday, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person shall keep an account of a row.
