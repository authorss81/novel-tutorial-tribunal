# Chapter 474: The Last Day Of The Eighty-Ninth Week, A Man Of Thirty-Four Asks A Steward Of Forty Years To Call The House On The Question Of Its Own Covenant, The Steward Does Not Call It And Says Out Loud In A Room Over A Shop That He Has Been Forty Years The Only Man In This Town Nobody Had To Wonder What He Wanted And That He Is No Longer That Man, A Chair Of Forty-Seven Says The Other Half Of It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Is Asked For His View For The Second Time In This Town And Says It In The Smallest Room He Has Ever Said One In

Seventh day of the eighty-ninth week, and the last day of it. His two hundred and twenty-fourth morning. Seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and two days. Twenty-eight days since the division. Twenty-two days in this town.

The office of the steward of Halloway is over a shop at the south end of the square. It has a desk, a form, a press with four leaves in it and a stool.

There were about nine people in it on the Monday morning of the twenty-eighth day after the division, which is more than have been in it at once in about nine years.

The question was put by Ivo Cray, who had come down the lane and had been up it again on the Saturday, and who put it in eleven words and then sat down.

“*I am asking you to call the House.*”

“*What is the question, and be exact about it. I have called one meeting of this House in nine years and it was a fortnight ago, and the question was on the table when I called it and I did not put it there, and I would like to know the shape of this one before I answer it.*”

“*Whether the House will take the Book down off the beam and enter the ninth clause in the minute of the House, and whether the ninth clause is a part of the covenant of this town or is not.*”

About nine people in an office over a shop did not make a sound.

“*Say the other half of it and say it in your own mouth,*” said Ilyan Vester, “*because a man of thirty-four has asked for something and there are nine people in this room who are owed a reason.*”

“*The other half is that the Book comes down twice a year, on the day of the audit and the day of the water sitting, by two people onto a long table in a hurry with the fire lit.*”

“*Go on and say when it was last off that beam.*”

“*It has not been off that beam since the audit nine years back, and it was off it about two hours on the tenth day after the division.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And I am not going to call the House, and the reason is that I have been the steward of this town for forty years and nobody in this town has ever once had to wonder what I wanted, and that is not a virtue anybody put on a form. It is the only power I have ever had.*”

---

“*What has happened to it since you said that in a square.*” said Sefa Lund, who was standing at the wall because there were nine people and eight chairs, and had come up the lane on her own.

“*I told you the same sentence in a market square on the twentieth day after the division and about a hundred people heard it, and I have had nine days to think about it and here is what has happened to it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I corrected a roll. On the Sunday morning, in about four minutes, with a form out of a drawer in this office, with a day on it and my own hand at the foot. And I came out of this door and stood under the awning of the mill and said so, in a square, and said that I had not slept on it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*And nobody has asked me to do it again, and I have not done it again, and if nobody had come to me about a roll on a Sunday morning this office would be exactly as it was on the sixth day after the division.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this room,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*The other half is that a steward who calls the House on a question of the Book is a steward who comes in with a thing. And the thing I would be coming in with is my own opinion of what a clause means, and I have got one, and it is that the clause is dead.*”

“*Do not be kind about it.*”

“*And I would rather be sixty-one and right about the division in front of forty-one men than be right about a clause in front of them, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a steward shall be able to have it both ways, and I have had both ways for forty years.*”

“*Why you cannot read it out instead.*” said Sefa Lund.

He was quiet for about nine seconds, which in that room was a long time.

“*I caused clause one to be struck,*” said Amos Trill. “*Four weeks ago, in front of a commission, with arguments I made, and I would make them again this morning. The common had not been used in ninety years and a covenant nobody uses is not a covenant and binds nobody, and that is the whole of what I said and it is correct.*”

“*Finish it.*”

“*And there is a clause on the back of that board that would have stopped all of it, and I did not know it was there.*”

“*Go on, and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*I have had eighteen days to go and look at it and be told what it says by the only person in this county who can read it, and I have not, and I am not going to.*”

“*The reason.*”

“*Looking at it puts me in a room where I have to say what I did, and I would have to say it to forty-one men and a copyist, and I am not able to do that.*”

About nine people in an office over a shop at the south end of a market square in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

---

Then the chair of the water board came, and he came because he was sent for, and he had not been in that office in nine years except once, and he stood in the doorway first and did not come further in.

“*Why are you standing in the doorway.*” said Ilyan.

“*Because if I come further in I am in it, and I have been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years and I have said so out loud in a room over a saddler’s shop and I did not enjoy it and I am not going to do it again in an office over a shop.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*Say what you have come to say,*” said Amos Trill, “*and say it in about nine sentences, and then we will find out whether this room has two reasons in it or one.*”

“*The House is not a mouth,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*I have sat in that room on forty-one stools with the door open. It is forty-one men who have all read the same two sheets, and who go out and find out what a thousand people think, and who then say it out loud in forty-one kitchens for a year, and come back in and write down what the kitchens said.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And that is the same thing you have been for forty years with a different hat on, and you have just told this room that you are no longer that man.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*So say the thing you want, out loud, in a room, with a day on it, and let forty-one men decide it. If they decide it against you then you will have been a man who wanted something, which is what you have been for nine years, and I have been a man who wanted something, and we are neither of us in a position to complain.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Amos Trill.

“*The other half is that I did say the thing I wanted. In a form, in four minutes, in this office, and it is in a press in the Stone House and any person of the town may read it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*You cannot call a thing back out of a press, and you of all men know that, because it is the whole of what you have been saying in a market square for nine days.*”

“*And the other half of that.*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*In about four years the only thing anybody in this town will remember is that a steward of forty years called the House of this town on its own covenant. And it will not matter one halfpenny what was in the minute.*”

“*Go on.*” said Amos Trill.

“*And a chair of forty-seven who has been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years and would rather be anything else.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And there is not one form in the county of Ostrey that says what a man is instead.*”

---

And then he was asked for his view, for the second time in this town, and he had said no to it once.

Ivo Cray asked him, and he said the reason out loud first, which is that the man who was the stranger in this town was the man who read the notice.

He said that he had been asked in a square on the fourteenth day after the division and had said he had no standing and would not say a word about it, and that he had been right to.

“*Say what you want of me,*” said Ilyan Vester, “*and then let me say it and do not stop me in the middle of it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that.*”

“*Because I have had it ready since the eighth hour, and there are nine people in this room, and it is the smallest room I have ever said a true thing in, and I would like it to be over with.*”

And he said it, and it was true, and it had never been said out loud in this town by anybody, and it is the reason a board of nine was able to do what it did on the Wednesday.

“*The only way to reach the House of Halloway is to be in it. The only way to be in it is to be an elected head of household in one of nine wards. The row at the Fen cannot be that, and the reason it cannot be that is not that they are not asked and not that they have refused and not that they are not nineteen houses with doors on them. It is that they are not on the roll, and so they are in no ward, and so nobody in that row can ever be in the room where the minute is written.*”

Nobody in that office over a shop said anything.

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of it,*” said Sefa Lund, “*and say what it is made of, and say the day.*”

“*It is made of a day out of a day, in the autumn, by a man in this office counting houses. And it is not a thing anybody can vote for and it is not a thing anybody can be asked about, because asking a man in this office to do it differently is asking a man in this office to come in with a thing.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this room,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the doorway.

“*The other half is that I said a sentence in this square on the Sunday morning at the hundredth hour, in about nine seconds, to about a hundred people, and it was true.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I have heard it four ways in six days and three of them are wrong, and the wrongest of them says the fen is nineteen more seats.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*The House has five heads in the ninth ward and always has and there is no form in this county that says a ward has a number of seats at all. And the fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn. And my sentence is in four mouths in this town and three of them have it wrong, and the one that is going to be used in about four days is the wrongest one, and it is mine.*”

He had about nine seconds in which to say the next thing, which was that the sentence had been carried out of a square by about forty people and repeated correctly by about four of them.

He used them on not saying it, and there is no minute in any book in this town in which it appears that he nearly said it.

---

And then Amos Trill did the one thing he had, and it was the second time in ninety years, and it was given to nobody.

The Book came down off the north beam of the Stone House at about the eleventh hour on the Monday morning of the twenty-eighth day after the division. It took about four minutes and two people did it, and the steward took the two top nails and the copyist took the two bottom ones.

The fire was lit, because it is the only light in that building.

About nine people were in the room. Four of them had come down the lane. The rest had come because there is no other room in Halloway with a hearth in it.

“*Say what you have had taken down and why,*” said Wenna Crale, standing at the end of the table with her hand on the board, “*because I am going to read the back of it and I want it in the room first that it is at the steward’s asking and not at anybody else’s.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*At my asking, in a room over a shop, with about nine people in it, on the twenty-eighth day after the division, and against the advice of a man of thirty-four who has asked me for it twice and been refused twice.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*For the reason that I have got to be able to say that I have read it out in a room, once.*”

“*Do not be kind about the other half.*” said Sefa Lund.

“*And the other half is that reading it out does nothing. I have said in this town on the twenty-fourth day that nobody in this town has ever been given it and nobody is going to be given it this year.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody, and I am not going to pretend in a stone room that I have done a different thing.*”

Then Wenna Crale turned the board over, which she has done once in this town, and read the back of it out, standing, in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took about forty seconds.

It says that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will. It is in a fifth hand and a different ink and it is dated a hundred and thirty-eight years ago, and at the foot of it there is a signed name and a witness line with two names in it, and one of them is a name of this town and the other is four letters and a stroke.

Nobody in that room asked her to read the witness line out and she read it out.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ilyan.

“*That is all of it. And it is the second time it has been read out in a room in ninety years, and there is no minute in this county that says what a person does with it, and it is going back up that beam in about four minutes and it will be there until the water sitting.*”

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and two days old. A board about four feet by two and a half went back up a beam nine feet above a fire in a stone room in a town of four thousand people.

A clause a hundred and thirty-eight years old had been read out in a room for the second time in ninety years and had been given to nobody. About nine people had heard it. One of them had asked for it and been refused twice.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man shall be given it.
