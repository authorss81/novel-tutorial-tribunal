# Chapter 487: A Sunday On Which A Chair Of Forty-Seven Who Has Never Called A Sitting Calls One, And The Ninth Clause Of A Covenant Four Hundred Years Old Is Used Once By Nine Men Of A Majority Against Nine Men Of That Same Majority, And The Word Required Is In A Minute Of This Town A Second Time And This Time It Is Good, And It Is Given To A Person Who Is Not A Household And Refused By A Person Who Is, And That Is The Shape Of The Whole Of This Business And Nobody In That Room Knows It

Sixth day of the ninety-first week. His two hundred and thirty-seventh morning. Eighty-seven days after the settlement. The fever twenty-nine weeks and one day. Forty-one days since the division. Thirty-five days in this town.

The water board of Halloway sat on the Sunday morning of the forty-first day after the division, and the chair of it called the sitting. That was the first day that man has called in this town since he was made chair of it nine years ago.

He wrote it on a sheet in his own kitchen on the Friday night and he told about nine people in a stone room on the Saturday, which is a thing a man does who has spent five weeks finding out that a day a man writes down for himself is not a day he has called.

There were about thirty people in that room and on the stair above it and about nine of the thirty were from the Fen, and the Fen were a party to it, and that was the first time in six weeks that they had been a party to anything.

They were a party to it because of a sheet of paper with a head on it and two parts under the head, the fortieth day after the division on it and a hand at the foot of it. The second part of that sheet sets out what the row was told and by whom and on what day, and a woman of thirty-eight signed it and her name is at the foot of it.

A person is a party to a thing the moment their name goes at the foot of a sheet with a day on it.

Nobody in that room had said out loud that this is what it was for, and about nine people in that room had worked it out, and one of the nine is the man who called the sitting.

---

The thing on the table at about the ninth hour was the request, and it was about eleven words long, and it had been made in a room over a taproom on the Friday night by about nine persons of the row, and it asked the board to do one thing.

“*Say the request as it was made and say it without adding to it,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*That the bar four feet long at the head of the sluice be wound at the sixth hour and at the twelfth hour on each day until the frame of the sluice is mended.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*The water came over the low sill of the sluice on the Saturday at about the seventh hour and it is standing at the step of the fourth house of nineteen this morning and it has not been wound for eleven days. The north heel of that frame is a season and not two years and the season is about four weeks off. We are asking the board to require it, and we have brought the request to the board and not to the lane, and the board has a minute and a day and we are parties to a thing with a day on it.*”

“*Go on and say the other half of that and say it in this room.*”

“*And we know before we say it that the board cannot make anybody do it, and we know why. We have not come here to be tricked and we have not come here to be refused. We have come here because the word required has been in a minute of this town twice in six weeks, and both of the times it was about us.*”

---

And the word went into the minute a second time, and it took about eleven minutes. The first four of them were the worst four minutes of the whole business.

About four of the nine men in that room said out loud, in order, and were each of them right about the face of the clause, that a person who is not a household is outside it.

“*Say the reasoning out loud and say it in the plainest words anybody has used in this room in six weeks,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the fourth stool, “*and let us find out whether it is a good reasoning or a bad one, and do not stop it while it is going.*”

“*It is good. It is as good as anything has been said in this room.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The ninth clause says that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will. It binds households. She is not a household. She is entered in this year’s roll as land of the common and has been since the Wednesday of the thirtieth day after the division, in a form with a day on it, in about four minutes.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*So the clause does not touch her, and this board may require her. This is the second time in six weeks that this board has tried to require a person, and the first time it was struck out in the room because of that clause, and this time it is not struck out.*”

About thirty people in that room and on that stair did not make a sound.

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this room,*” said Sefa Lund, standing at the back with her hat in her hands.

“*Then say what you have against it. Not what you have against me. I have heard it.*”

And the chair said the other half, and it took about seven minutes, and it is the only use anybody in Halloway has ever got out of the ninth clause of the covenant of this town, and it is used by the majority, in a room, against the majority.

“*A requirement is a thing a person can answer no to,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*That is not my opinion. That is what the word means, and the word is in the oldest book in this town and the book has been nailed up over a fire for four hundred years and it is the only thing in it that anybody has ever read out twice.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*If a requirement cannot be answered no to, it is not a requirement. It is an act. There is no form in the county of Ostrey for an act, and no form for a person to be made to do a thing in a lane. Any one of the nine of us who votes for a line of that shape is voting for a thing that has never once been done in this town, that cannot be done, and that would be done by whoever is nearest.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And the line of this minute will be signed by the chair of this board. I am a household of this town. I have a house on the west side and I have been charged twopence a quarter for water for nine years, and I am inside that clause, and every one of the nine men at this table is inside it, and about twenty-two people in the row below this room is outside it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I am the one person in this room for whom that clause is a protection, and it is a protection against me, and I have been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years, and I have never once been able to say that out loud in this room.*”

Nobody in that room and on that stair said anything at all for about a minute.

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said Sefa Lund.

“*Put the requirement in, and put the second line in under it, and let her answer it, and enter the answer whatever it is.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*And I will sign a minute of this town that says a person was asked to do a thing and said no. That is the first time in four hundred years that anybody has been able to enter that, and I do not get a right out of it, and neither does anybody else.*”

---

So the word went into a minute of this town a second time, and this time it was good, and Wenna Crale read it out standing at the end of the table in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took her about a minute.

*Three. That the board requires the person keeping the sluice to wind the bar four feet long at the head of it at the sixth hour of the morning and at the twelfth hour of the day on each day until the frame of the sluice is mended.*

*And that this requirement is made upon the request of about nine persons of the Fenrow, who are parties to an instrument of this town of the fortieth day after the division.*

*Four. And that any person to whom the third line is addressed may answer or not answer, and that the board will enter the answer in this minute whichever way it goes, and that no household of this town shall be required by this board to do a thing against its will.*

Nobody in that room and on that stair said anything at all.

“*Say the answer and say it in your own mouth and give the day,*” said Wenna Crale.

And Sefa Lund gave the answer, and it is one word, and Wenna Crale entered it in the minute of a water board of the county of Ostrey under a heading that said *answer of the person required*, with the day on it, and the hand at the foot of it.

“*No,*” said Sefa Lund. “*And I have said no, and the day of it is the forty-first day after the division. I am not a household of this town and I am out of the water. The bar is four feet long and it takes two people and about an hour, and there are about twenty-two of us in that row, and about nine of us are old enough, and I am not going to ask the nine.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech,*” said Ordwin Cape, from the rail.

“*And I have been keeping the accounts of this row since the twenty-first day after the division, and I gave them to a copyist on the Saturday in a room over a taproom, and they are in a press in a stone room that anybody may read, and I have not been asked a single question since.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*This morning the board of this town has asked me one, and I have said no to it, and the no is in a minute. That is the whole of what has happened to this row in six weeks.*”

---

And the bar was not wound at the sixth hour of that Sunday afternoon, and it was not wound at the twelfth hour either, and the water at the sluice went on doing what water does when there is nobody at it.

And there is now a minute of a water board of a market town that says a person was required to do a thing and said no, and the no is entered in it, and that minute is a thing a person can be asked about.

About four people in that room understood that on the Sunday morning, and the rest of them did not, because the rest of them had been in the lane watching a woman of thirty-eight say no. That is not the same thing, and it is the whole difference.

And he was glad. He was the only person in that room who was glad, and he was glad about a clause that had been read out in a room twice in ninety years and given to nobody twice.

It had been used once, that morning, for about seven minutes, and it had been used to stop nine men of a majority from doing a thing to nineteen houses, and every person it had protected was on the side of the nine.

He wanted a line under a head that said a person may be asked no, and he could not be given one. The thing that came instead was a clause protecting a chair of forty-seven from himself.

He has been working on the other of the two since the sixteenth day after the division, and he is not going to have it, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody.

He had about eleven seconds in which to say that he was glad, and he used them on not saying it, and there is no minute in any book in this county in which it appears that he was glad.

The fever was twenty-nine weeks and one day old. A minute of a water board of a town of four thousand people carries the word *required* in it a second time in six weeks, and the second time it is good, and the answer to it is one word and it is entered in the minute with the day on it.

About twenty-two people in a row below a sluice are out of the water of this town, and are parties to an instrument, and are not parties to a household, and a bar four feet long is on its eleventh day in the mud where two people put it down on the Wednesday of the thirtieth day after the division.
