# Chapter 481: The Fifth Day Of A Bar Four Feet Long That Has Not Been Turned Since The Sixth Hour Of The Thirty-First Day After The Division, And A Miller Of Thirty-Nine With A Counter Says Nine Words In His Own Shop Before The Eighth Hour Of The Monday, And A Man Of Thirty-One Walks In Two Hours After It Has Been Said And Spends The Morning Being Useful In The Wrong Place, Which Is What He Did On The Twenty-Fifth Day After The Division And Which Nobody In This Town Has Forgiven Him For

Seventh day of the ninetieth week, and the last day of it. His two hundred and thirty-first morning. Eighty-one days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and two days. Thirty-five days since the division. Twenty-nine days in this town.

The Fen will be under water again by Saturday.

That is the whole of what a man of thirty-nine said at the sixth hour of that Monday morning, standing behind his own counter, to about nine people in his own shop, and it is nine words long, and nobody had asked him for it.

---

The sluice is at the head of the Fen. It has a wheel nine feet across and a bar four feet long across the top of it, and the bar takes two people and about an hour. It is wound at the sixth hour of the morning and at the twelfth hour of the day, and it was wound at both on every day for nine years whether the gates were shut or open.

It has not been wound at either hour since the sixth hour of the thirty-first day after the division.

That is five days. Thursday, and then Friday, and then Saturday, and then Sunday, and then this morning, and on each of those five an hour came and went and nobody put a hand to the bar, and this is the fifth day of it.

The wheel at the mill is a different count, and the difference is worth saying out loud. A man in this town heard this count four ways in a market square on the twenty-fifth day after the division and got three of the four wrong, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be corrected about a count.

“*Say the difference between the two counts and say it in this shop,*” said a man of about thirty at the nail bin, who had a hand in a box of nails and had stopped.

“*The bar is counted in days on which an hour came and went,*” said Ott Vernay, “*and the wheel is counted in days completed, and there is a difference of one between them this morning and there will be a difference of one tomorrow morning, and it is not a mistake and it is not anybody being wrong.*”

“*Go on and say what the numbers are, because a number with no day on it is a thing nobody can be asked about.*”

“*The wheel has been slow four days. Four days completed. Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday. It went slow on the Thursday morning and I have had four days of short grist out of nine years of full grist, and I have written one word against the name of the mill in my own day book, and the word is *race*.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And it is not the book with nine hundred names in it. That one is shut to a hand’s width on the other side of this counter, and it has been shut since the fifteenth day after the division, when a man of thirty-one asked for it in my shop. I would like the room to have both facts at once and to have them in that order.*”

About nine people in a shop at the bottom of a market square in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

---

The gates have been standing open since the sixth hour of the fourteenth day after the division, when they were opened five days early, and there has been no hand on the bar since to shut them. The water of the Hallow has been going through the head of the sluice as it likes since the Thursday morning of the thirty-first day, and the race is carrying what the race carries.

The Fen is a hundred and forty acres of water meadow below that head, and it floods, and it was not divided, and it lies.

“*Say what is coming and say it with a day on it, and then say why you are the one saying it,*” said Ordwin Cape, from the rail, where he had come in out of the wet with his bar-hook over his shoulder.

“*Because I grind for this town on a Tuesday and a Friday and I have lost both of those since the Thursday before last. I grind on the water and the water has been the wrong height on the wheel for four days. I can see the Fen from my own door, and it was dry at the top on Friday and it is not dry now.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And I said it in nine words because it took me nine words and I have not got any more than that. I have been right about this counter for four years and about a hundred and seventy houses nobody has ever asked me about. This town has worked out in five weeks that a book is a door, and there is nobody in this county that can be asked to shut a gate.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this shop.*”

“*The other half is that the water is not coming because of anything anybody has done. It is coming because a bar four feet long has not been turned for five days, and that is all, and I cannot turn it, and neither can the four men who turned it with me.*”

---

And then Ilyan Vester came into that shop at about the eighth hour and the room had already said the sentence, and he was two hours late, and about nine people in it knew that he was two hours late.

He had come down the lane from his rooms over a nail shop at the sixth hour with a sentence ready for it. The sentence was that nobody in this town can be required to turn a bar four feet long. He had got as far as the bottom of the square with it and had found that about nine people in a shop had got there before him without him.

“*Say what you have come down here for,*” said Ott Vernay, “*and then say what you would like me to do with it.*”

“*I have come down here to tell this town that a bar four feet long has not been turned for five days, and that the frame at the head of it is a season gone, and that nobody can be required to turn it.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*The nineteen houses that turned it are not households of this town and are out of the water, and a household is the only thing in this county that anybody has ever been able to require anything of.*”

“*That is a good thing to have come down here with, and it is the fourth time you have said it in this town. It was said at the sixth hour by a man of thirty-nine in nine words and about nine of us heard it. You are at the eighth hour, and about nine of us have been standing here since the sixth hour doing nothing else.*”

Nobody in that shop said anything for about a minute.

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I have come down here to be useful,*” said Ilyan Vester, “*and I have been in this town twenty-nine days and I have wanted that every morning of them and there is no form anywhere that says what I am for.*”

---

So he spent the Monday morning being useful in the wrong place. That is the thing he did on the twenty-fifth day after the division, with a board of nine and a day he had not chosen. About eleven people in a kitchen four hundred yards up a lane have not forgiven him for it, and neither has anybody else in this town.

He stood in that shop for about nine minutes after he had said it and let a man of thirty-nine tell him that the sentence was four hours old. Then he went out into the square and stood under the awning of the mill with his hands behind his back. That is where a man of thirty-one stood on the Friday of the thirty-second day for about nine minutes with nothing to wait for.

The whole of what he had come down here with was in about nine mouths in a shop and none of them was his. It had got there at the sixth hour off a man who had been keeping a book of nine hundred people for four years. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a thing gets better by being said by a stranger in a coat.

He went up the lane at about the ninth hour with the fact. He got as far as the sluice, and the bar was lying in the mud where two people had put it down on the Wednesday morning of the thirtieth day, and about eleven people were standing round it in the wet. He told all eleven of them the thing he had told the shop.

They had it. All eleven of them had it, and it had been in that row since about the sixth hour of the morning from a boy of twenty-two who had run down a lane with it. The boy said so before he said anything else, and he said it without any particular heat, the way a person says a thing they have said twice.

“*Say it out loud in that lane anyway,*” said Cull Sarn, “*because it has been said four times this morning and I would like to hear which of the four of you said it first.*”

“*It does not matter which,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*It is the same fact in four mouths and none of the four mouths can do anything with it.*”

“*Then say what we are going to do about the fact, because I am twenty-two and I have been standing in the rain since the sixth hour and I would like to be told the rest of it.*”

And that was the whole of the Monday morning, and it took the rest of it.

---

Because a bar four feet long at the head of a sluice is not a thing anybody in this county can be made to turn.

The water board of Halloway has a minute and a second book and a frame. It does not have a form in the county of Ostrey that lets it require a person to do a thing with a bar, and it has never had one.

It had one in a minute on the thirtieth day after the division, and struck it out of that minute in the room in front of about thirty people, because the person it named was a household of this town. And there is a clause on the back of a board nailed nine feet up over a fire that says no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will.

The nineteen houses at the Fen are not households of this town. They are entered in this year’s roll as land of the common. They have not been on the roll as households since the Wednesday afternoon of the thirtieth day, in a form with a day on it, in about four minutes, and nobody was asked to make that form.

So the clause is on the back of that board and it binds nobody in that lane, and there is no form that binds anybody in that lane, and the bar is lying in the mud.

“*Say that last part out loud again in this lane and say it in the ordinary voice,*” said Sefa Lund.

She had come up out of the wet about ten minutes before and had not been at the bar at all, and she was standing on the bank above it with her hands in the front of her coat.

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody require a person to turn a bar four feet long, and the clause that would stop it binds households. The nineteen houses that kept this sluice are not households, and the other people in this town are not going to do it either, because it is not their bar and there is nobody who has authority to say it is.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And I am going to say the other half of it and I have never said it out loud, and I have had it in my head since the Monday.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*If the water stands above the sills of these houses by the Saturday, then the cause that this board found on the Wednesday is proved again. It was proved by nineteen people turning a bar. It will be proved by nobody turning it, and the board will be right twice.*”

About eleven people in a lane four hundred yards long in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*And the row cannot be required to do a one thing about it, and the clause on the back of that board is the only thing in this county that anybody has ever been able to say no with. I have had it in my mouth for five weeks and it has never once been any use to me at all.*”

---

And then a man of sixty-one asked the question that about two hundred people in a market square had not asked, on the Saturday before last, and that nobody in that lane had asked since.

“*Say whether anybody can be sent for,*” said Ordwin Cape.

Nobody in that lane answered him for about a minute.

“*Say the answer,*” he said, “*and say it in the ordinary voice, because I am sixty-one and I have been in this row forty-one years and I would like to know what I am part of.*”

“*A thing is sent for by a person with a form,*” said Wenna Crale, who had come up the lane behind the nine and had not made a sound. “*And there is no form in the county of Ostrey for being sent for, and there is no office in this county that could hold one. That is the whole of the answer. I have been a copyist for eleven years and I did not know it until about the ninth hour of this morning.*”

“*Go on and say what a person does with an answer like that.*”

“*Nothing. That is what a person does with it. It is a true answer about a thing that has no name, and it is the fifth true answer this town has had in five weeks. Being right is not the same as being able to do anything. I have never before heard that said about a body of men and I have been in the room of a body of men for eleven years.*”

Nobody in that lane said anything for about a minute.

He stood in the mud at the foot of four hundred yards of the worst lane in the county and listened to a woman of thirty-four give him back the sentence he had spent the morning carrying, and it was better than the one he had brought, and it had been in that lane since the sixth hour.

He had said on the Thursday of the thirtieth day, in a room with about thirty people in it, that a household is a house. It had been the ground of a board’s finding, and nineteen houses had gone out of the water in four minutes on the strength of it. This morning the same fact had come back up the lane on its own, and had put a cause back into being that everybody in that lane had thought was finished.

He had four seconds in which he could have said that the sentence was his. He used them on not saying it, and there is no minute in any book in this county in which it appears that he was standing at the foot of that lane at all.

---

And that is the whole of what was said in that lane on the Monday morning of the thirty-fifth day after the division, and about nine of it was said by people who are not of this town and about eleven of the people who were standing in it live in the nineteen houses below it.

The bar four feet long was lying in the mud where two people had put it down on the Wednesday morning of the thirtieth day, with the wet on it, and it was going to be under water by the Saturday.

A cause set out in writing by a man of sixty-one on the back of a bill, in the ninth year before the division, was going to be proved again by a thing that nineteen people had done for nine years and had stopped doing, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person shall be sent for.

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and two days old.
