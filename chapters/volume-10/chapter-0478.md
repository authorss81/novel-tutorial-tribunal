# Chapter 478: A Friday On Which The Bar At The Head Of The Sluice Is Not Turned For The Second Day, A Man Of Thirty-One Goes Into A Shop At The Bottom Of The Market Square To Ask For The Only Book In Halloway That Has Never Been In A Room, And A Man Of Thirty-Nine With A Counter Who Has Said No To Him Before Says No Again And Gives A Better Reason The Second Time, And The Reason Is A Sentence He Said Out Loud In A Square Two Days Ago, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Tells Him The Other Half Of A Thing

Fourth day of the ninetieth week. His two hundred and twenty-eighth morning. Seventy-eight days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and six days. Thirty-two days since the division. Twenty-six days in this town.

The mill is at the bottom of the market square and it has one wheel, and the wheel is fed by a race that runs a mile and a bit from the tail of the sluice.

The wheel was slow on the Friday morning. It had been slow since the Thursday. The man who has had the mill four years put it in a note in his own hand against the name of the mill on his own book, and the note says *race*.

He has not said the word out loud to anybody. There are about nine people in that shop on the Friday morning and eight of them heard it.

Ott Vernay was thirty-nine and had a second book under the counter which he had been keeping for four years, and it had about nine hundred names in it, and he had a hand on it, and he had had a hand on it every time anybody in this town had ever come at it.

“*What you have come for, and then why, and then do not make the why into a speech. You came in here four weeks ago with a reason that was a speech and I said no to it in nine seconds, and I was right, and you have not come back since, and I would like to know what has changed.*”

---

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan Vester, “*because I have never once come back to a man who said no to me, and that is not a virtue, it is a habit, and it has cost this town about nine weeks.*”

“*Go on and say what you have come for.*”

“*The book under your hand. I have come for the book under your hand.*”

Nobody in that shop said anything, and about nine people in it heard the request made and about two of them stopped what they were doing.

“*The reason, in about four sentences, and then I am going to say no, and you are going to have to stand in my shop while I say it, because that is what a refusal in a shop is.*”

“*A roll is a list of who is in a room. That was said in this square six days ago, and it is true, and about nine people in this town have it right.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*The roll of this town is now a list of who is not in the room. Nineteen houses at the Fen came off it on Wednesday afternoon in about four minutes, and about thirty people in this square have worked out that the only book in this county that says those nineteen houses are people is a book at a mill counter that has never been in a room.*”

“*And the other half.*”

“*The other half is that if it were in the press against the west wall of the Stone House then any person of the town could read that nine hundred people buy on credit off you and what each of them pays.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And a mark against each of them, saying whether the rate has come through your counter, or whether their house is on the water list, or whether you have been carrying a man you cannot find on either book.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*And then this town would have two books and a book in a press is a book that anybody can be asked about, and about two hundred people in this square have worked out in three weeks that a book is a door, and I would like the door to be a small one.*”

Ott Vernay took his hand off the book and put both hands flat on the counter, which is the thing a miller does, and he said the rest of it in one go and it took about nine seconds.

“*No,*” he said, “*and I am going to give you a better reason than the one I gave you four weeks ago, and I want it heard in my shop, and I want the two men in here who are not of this town to hear it as well.*”

---

“*The reason, and do not make it a speech,*”

“*You stood under the awning of my mill on Friday morning with about thirty people in this square and you counted the instruments of this town out loud.*”

Nobody in that shop made a sound.

“*Say the rest of it, and finish the sentence,*” said a man of about thirty at the nail bin, who had a hand in a box of nails and had stopped.

“*You said: a miller’s second book, a copyist, a minute, and a woman who refuses things in lanes. You said it in a square, out loud, in the plainest words anybody has used in this town in three weeks, and about nine of us heard it and about nine more will have heard it by the evening.*”

“*Go on to the end of that.*”

“*And I am a man with a counter and a book, and you have told a town of four thousand people that I am a public instrument, and a public instrument is a thing that gets opened. That is the reason. Not the four weeks ago reason, which was about my shop. This one is about your square.*”

“*And the other half, in your own mouth.*”

“*The other half is that you were right. About four of the four. I am one of the four and I have not been able to be one since Friday morning, and I have not been able to be one because you said it out loud, and if you had said it in a lane to me I would have had it on Tuesday.*”

About nine people in a shop at the bottom of a market square in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

“*Do not make that smaller than it is.*”

“*Say what you want and say it out loud,*” said Vernay.

“*Why a man of thirty-nine has been keeping a book of nine hundred people for four years and will not let it out of his hands.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*The whole of this town has found out in three weeks that a book is a door, and there is one door in Halloway that is not open, and it is on this counter, and about nine of us are standing in front of it.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

He put his hand back on it.

“*Because a dash means the name is not on the roll and the house is on the water list, and a blank means I have carried the man and I cannot find him on either book. That is what a mark is. It is not a list of who lives in this town. It is a list of who I can find, and a man I can find is a man I can sell to, and a man I cannot find is a debt I would rather have.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech,*”

“*And on Wednesday afternoon this town put nineteen houses out of the water in four minutes in a form, and about two hundred people in a square have worked out that a roll is a list of who is in a room. And I have got a hundred and eighty dashes in this book, and about a hundred and seventy of them are not the fenrow, and every one of them is a house that has been paying me for four years off the water list and has never once known there was a question about it.*”

Nobody in that shop said anything at all.

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech,*”

“*Put that book in a press and this town has a list of a hundred and seventy households that the roll has not got, and a town that has worked out in three weeks that a roll is a door does not then hand the door a second list. It used one. On Wednesday. In four minutes.*”

“*And the other half, in your own mouth.*”

“*The other half is that you are right, and the thing you are right about is the reason I am not going to let you have it.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*I would like that written down in my shop, and I would like the man of thirty-one at the bottom of this square to hear it, because he is the one who taught me that a book in a press is a thing that gets asked about instead of a thing.*”

Nobody in that shop thanked him for any part of it.

And Ilyan Vester stood in a shop at the bottom of a market square with a bad leg and listened to a man of thirty-nine tell him that his own sentence of Friday morning was the reason a book was shut, and he could not answer it, and he had about four seconds in which he could have said that the sentence was right and the refusal was wrong and both of those were true at the same time.

He did not say either of them. He had said four hundred times in twenty-six days that a man has to be able to be asked about, and about nine people in that shop could have asked him about any part of it, and he had spent the four seconds on not saying, and there is no minute in this county in which it appears that he nearly said it.

What he wanted, in that shop, on that Friday morning, was to be useful. He has wanted that since the first day and has not said it out loud in this town in a room, and it is the first want of his life that there is no form for anywhere.

---

And then the woman of thirty-four who rents a second room in a house of nineteen and washes at a sluice said the other half of it.

She had said the first half of it in a market square on the twenty-fifth day after the division, in the rain, with about nine people round an awning. He had not stopped her and he had not answered it.

“*What you have come down here for, and say it in the ordinary voice,*” said Ott Vernay, “*because you buy at this counter and you are on my book as a dash and you have been for seven years.*”

“*Do not make that a speech.*”

“*I have come down here because there are two men in this shop who cannot be asked about anything and about nine people in it who can. I have been in nine rooms in three weeks and I have learned one thing from that, and I am going to say it in a shop, and I am not going to be asked whether I am allowed.*”

Nobody made her stop.

“*Say the thing, in your own mouth.*”

“*On the twenty-fifth day after the division I said to you in that square that you are the only person in this county that nobody can be sent for, and that the half of that which is a gift is that you can walk into any room in this town and nobody has been sent for you.*”

“*Do not make that smaller than it is.*”

“*And the other half is that when you have gone out of the room there is nobody in it who was sent for, and that is not the same room.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*That half was true on the Friday morning and it has got truer every day since, and on Wednesday it put nineteen houses out of the water in four minutes in a form that nobody had to be asked about.*”

About nine people in that shop did not make a sound.

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*It is a hole and it is not a gift. And you have been in this town twenty-six days and I have watched you spend it like a purse, and I have never once said that to you because you had it in your mouth and I did not want to be the woman who took a thing off a man who could not be asked about anything at all.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And now you want a book. The reason you want it is that you are the only man in this town who can pick a thing up and walk it anywhere.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And the only thing in Halloway you could pick up that would have helped anybody this week is a book a man of thirty-nine has been keeping for four years for his own reasons, and he has just told you the reason in nine seconds, and the reason is you.*”

Nobody in that shop said anything for about a minute, and the man at the nail bin put a nail back in the box and took it out again and put it back.

Ilyan Vester went out of that shop at about the tenth hour and stood in the square with his hands behind his back for about nine minutes, and a carter went round him, and a woman with a barrow went round him, and about nine people in Halloway have now seen a man of thirty-one stand in a market square without doing anything, and not one of them could afterwards have said what he was waiting for, because there was nothing to wait for.

Coll Sarn came out of the shop about four minutes behind him with his barrow and stood beside him, and neither of them said anything, and the two of them were in a square of about four thousand people and there was nobody in that square who could have said afterwards what either of them had been doing there.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and six days old. A book with about nine hundred names in it and a mark against each was shut to a hand’s width and belonged to a man with a counter. It was the only book in the county of Ostrey that had never been in a room.

About nine people were in a shop at the bottom of a market square, and two of the nine were men who could not be asked about anything at all.
