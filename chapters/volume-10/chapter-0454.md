# Chapter 454: The Roll Of This Town Is Made Once A Year And Was Made In The Week The Common Was Sold, And The Nineteen Houses At The Fen Are On It As Land And Not As Households, And He Does A Division Out Loud In A Market Because He Cannot Leave A Figure Alone, And Then Has To Come Back And Take It Apart

First day of the eighty-seventh week. His two hundred and fourth morning. Fifty-four days after the settlement. The fever twenty-four weeks and three days. Eight days since the division.

The market was on a Tuesday and there were about two thousand people in a cobbled square the size of a good field, and the fenrow had come up off the low ground with reed and with baskets, which is what they have always done, and which nobody had connected to anything.

At about the eighth hour Ilyan was at the north side of the square with the copy of the roll in his hand. Any person in Halloway may read the copy. He had read it, and he was standing there holding it like a man who has found something.

“*Say the line,*” said Wenna Crale, who had walked up with him, and who had said on the way that he was not to do anything with it.

“*The Fen. Nineteen tenements. Common land. Not rated.*”

“*Say the rest of the line because there are two lines under it.*”

“*Nineteen tenements, common land, not rated, in respect of water. And then underneath, in a different hand and an older one: formerly the whole of the Fen, in respect of water, common to all households of this town, discharged.*”

---

And then he did the thing.

He has done it in nine rooms in nine weeks and he has said out loud in five of them that he cannot stop doing it, and in a market square in a town of four thousand people on a Tuesday morning he looked at a figure and could not go past it.

“*Say the count on the roll out loud,*” he said, “*and say the count of households in this town.*”

“*One thousand and forty-one,*” said Wenna Crale, “*and it is four hundred years old as a figure, and it has not moved in eleven years, and the roll was made in the week of the division.*”

“*Then nineteen more is one thousand and sixty.*”

He said it in the ordinary voice, in a market, in front of about two hundred people. He said it because he wanted there to be a figure in the world, and there had not been one since Saturday.

A figure is a thing a person can be asked about. He has believed that for nine weeks and has not once asked whether it helps.

The figure went round the market in about eleven minutes.

---

The trouble was not the arithmetic. About eighty people in that square had bought a lot on the Four Hundred. The rate is spread on the eighty lots, and the water rate falls on the households, and a man who heard one thousand and sixty on a Tuesday went and told his neighbour, and the neighbour had it wrong by thirty-one, and told a third man it was wrong by nine.

By the middle of the day the figure in the market was one thousand and ninety-one, which nobody had said. By the evening two people in Halloway had been told by a stranger that their rate was going to rise by a fifth.

“*Say what you have done,*” said Sefa Lund, in the doorway of the room over the nails, at about the seventh hour, and she had walked up from the Fen.

“*I said a true figure out loud in a market.*”

“*You said a true figure out loud in a market. It has been in this town for eleven hours and it is now one thousand and ninety-one. Two men at the mill are telling their foremen that the stranger is going to put the rate up a fifth, and one of the foremen has a brother on the water board.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*And nobody has done anything to me,*” she said. “*I want that said, because you will hear it the other way by the morning. Nobody has done anything to me and nobody has said anything to me. But you have made a thing of the water in a market, and the water is a thing I have kept for nine years without a wage, and now there is a man in a room somewhere deciding what a stranger in the market meant.*”

---

He went back into the market at about the eighth hour on the same Tuesday, in the wet, and about two hundred people were still there because it had not gone dark, and he stood on the same step.

He took it back, and he took it apart, and it took him about nine minutes, and every person on that step heard all of it, and the two mill foremen heard it and repeated it and got it wrong again by four.

“*The figure of one thousand and sixty is not right and I have said it in this square and I am going to say in this square that it is not right.*”

Somebody at the back said *go on*.

“*The roll is made once a year and it counts households, and a household is a thing that is in a dwelling on the day the roll is made, and I have added nineteen tenements to a count of households, and a tenement is not a household. There are nineteen houses at the Fen and about twenty-three people living in them, and how many households that is depends entirely on who was in which house in the week of the division, and nobody has counted it since, and I have not counted it, and I cannot.*”

“*Say what the number is then.*”

“*I do not know the number. I know it is somewhere between nineteen and twenty-three and I cannot find out which. I count people. Households are not a number of people. I have spent nine weeks treating the two as the same thing, and in a city of ninety thousand people a column of names is either right or it is a person who is made into one. Here it does not work, and the reason is that the number is the protection.*”

He put his hand flat on the step, which is what he does.

“*And here is the other half of it. A household on the roll can be charged. A house that is not a household cannot be charged, and cannot be given anything, and cannot be in the House, and cannot be in this House, because a head of household is a person the roll knows about. And I have just gone round this market adding up the only number there is, and it is wrong, and the wrongness is the whole of the difficulty and not a mistake in my sum.*”

Nobody in that square said anything at all for a while.

Then the foreman from the mill, who was about fifty, said one thing, and he said it to Ilyan and not to the market.

“*Say that last part again, and say it slower, and I want to know whether you are saying the men on the Fen are not in this town.*”

“*Say what I am saying and do not put it in a nicer mouth than mine.*”

“*Then say it.*”

“*I am saying that on the day that roll was made, nineteen houses at the Fen were land. A house is a thing to put a roof on. A land is a thing you are charged for. Nobody in this town is charged for a common, so the people living on the Fen at that moment were not anybody, in a book, at the only moment in a year that anybody looks.*”

---

The man of sixty-one was in the market at the back and had been there the whole time, and he came forward about four steps and said his name out loud, which Ilyan had not asked him to do.

“*Amos Trill. Steward of this town forty years. I put the notice on that door up on the Wednesday and my name is at the foot of it. I have never once in forty years stood in this square and been afraid of a figure. I am today. And I would like everybody to hear me say that it is a good figure and a bad one.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Wenna Crale.

“*The good half is that he is right. The roll is made once and the land changed underneath it. Nobody has done a wicked thing. That is what a roll is. I made that roll happen in the week I sold the common, because I did not think of it, and I have been thinking of it for eight days, and there is a form in this office and I have not opened it.*”

“*Say the form.*”

“*The out-of-roll book. The board may at any time enter a household that is not on the roll. It is one line, it takes a minute, it has not been used in this town in eleven years, and I have known about it for about forty.*”

“*Say why it has not been used.*”

“*Because in eleven years nobody has asked for it. That is the whole of it. It is a power nobody has ever claimed. I have been the steward of this town for forty years and I have never once put it in front of anybody. If I had put it in front of you in the week the common was sold, you would have had the Fen on the roll by the fourth day after it.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Ilyan.

“*And I am not going to put it in front of you on Wednesday either. The water board meets on the Thursday. Nine men, and the water is the water, and the out-of-roll book is the board’s and not mine. I have held that book for forty years and have never once been the man who used it. I am sixty-one years old and I have been afraid of a figure in a market.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*The other half is that if I put it in front of them on Wednesday then on Thursday I am a man who has asked them for something, and in about four years there will be forty men in this House who remember that the steward of the town comes in with a thing. And I have been the steward for forty years and I have exactly one thing I have ever used, and it is that nobody in this town has ever had to wonder what the steward wanted.*”

She stood in the mud of a market square and thought about it for a while.

“*Then do not,*” she said. “*Not because you are protecting anything. Because you are the only reason a man of sixty in this town can say no to anybody. If you use it, you stop being that, and then there is nothing in four hundred years of this place where a man does not come in with a thing.*”

“*That is the best thing anybody has said to me in nine weeks,*” said Ilyan, “*and it is about a form, and it is by a woman who is not on any roll and has not been for nine years, and she is telling the man who could put her on it not to.*”

The fever was twenty-four weeks and three days old. The roll of Halloway was made once a year, and it had been made in the week the common was sold, and nineteen houses were on it as land.

A stranger had put a wrong figure into a market on a Tuesday and had taken it apart in the same square on the same day in front of about two hundred people, and the correct figure was that he did not know it.
