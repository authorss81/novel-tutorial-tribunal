# Chapter 464: Four Days Of Rain And A Market In A Town That Keeps Its Market On A Tuesday, And A Man Of Twenty-Two Who Has Never Been Asked What He Is, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Who Tells A Stranger That The Fenrow Have Begun To Say The Word Stranger About Him And That It Is Not The Word They Used On Monday, And He Is Asked To Read A Clause To A Woman Of Eighty-One And Says No For A Reason That Is About Language And Not About Standing

Fourth day of the eighty-eighth week. His two hundred and fourteenth morning. Sixty-four days after the settlement. The fever twenty-five weeks and six days. Eighteen days since the division. Twelve days in this town.

The market in Halloway is on a Tuesday and it has been on a Tuesday for longer than anybody in that square can remember, and on the Tuesday of this week it rained so hard that about a fifth of it stood, and the carts that could not get into the square came on the Friday instead.

So there was a market in Halloway on a Friday for the fourth time in nine years, and about four hundred people were in it, and about nine of them had been in it on the Tuesday and had come back to tell somebody what it was like.

The lane above the sluice is mud to the ankle for four hundred yards.

Four days ago about four hundred people had walked that lane twice a day with two tin pails, one a hand’s width narrower than the other, and the water came back into the race on the Monday morning, and the pails went back into eight or nine doorways, and the lane is now the worst four hundred yards in the county and will be until it dries or until the frost, and a carter at the top of it will not take a load down, and has said so twice.

That is what the nine days have become. Not a hardship. An argument about mud.

---

He had got to the square by the eighth hour and was in it for four hours, and nothing happened to him at all, and that was new.

At about the ninth hour a man with a bundle of wool at his feet asked him to hold it while he went into a taproom, and gave it, and took it back, and said thank you in the way you say it to a man who is holding your bundle.

And then, at about the tenth, the same man said the other thing, and said it as though it were a continuation.

“*You are not of here.*”

“*I am not of here,*” said Ilyan.

“*You are not a household of here either, and I ask it because I have got a load on my own cart and I would sooner have a man who is a household hold a thing than a man who is not.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it a favour.*”

“*There is no rest of it. If you were a household of Halloway you would be on the roll and you would be in a ward and you would be one of the forty-one, and there is not a thing in this town you could not ask anybody about. And you cannot, so you are a man I would have to carry my wool with and swear for.*”

He said it well, and he was not being unkind, and about four people within ten feet heard the word *household* said about a stranger in a market square on a wet Friday for the third time in four days.

---

So Ilyan went round that market for four hours doing the one thing he has done in every room for twelve days, and he did it four times, and it produced nothing at all.

He asked a man of thirty-eight selling nails whether the row at the Fen had ever asked the town for anything. He asked a woman of about fifty with a barrow of turnips whether she knew a household that was not on the roll. He asked a carrier with a bad cold whether a person not of the town could be on the roll.

The man of thirty-eight said no. The woman with the barrow said there was one, and then would not say which, and then said it was a story. The carrier said he would not guess at a man he drove for.

And at about the twelfth hour, at the wool stall again, the man with the bundle said the thing that a town says when it has finished with a subject.

“*You are a man nobody has heard of asking for something,*” he said, kindly. “*And that is not a bad thing to be. It is nine days old.*”

---

Coll Sarn was twenty-two and he had a barrow of nothing in particular and a good coat for the weather, and he was standing under the awning of the mill because the awning does not leak, and he had been standing there for about an hour and had not bought anything.

“*Say your name,*” said Ilyan, “*and say how long you have been in this town.*”

“*Coll Sarn. Two years, off and on. I was born in the ninth house of nineteen and I came up off the low ground when I was twenty and I have had four beds in this town and none of them was mine.*”

“*Say what you are and go on to the end of it.*”

“*I am a man who carts.*”

“*That is not what I asked and you know it is not.*”

“*Then you have asked me a question nobody in this town has ever asked anybody,*” said Coll Sarn, “*and I have been ready for it since I was fourteen and it has never come, and I do not have an answer in me, and I am telling you that in a market in the rain and not making a thing of it.*”

“*Say what you would say if somebody asked you at the poor rate.*”

“*I would say I have no house and I have no wage and I am not on the roll, and there is a form for a man with no house and no wage, and I filled it in when I was twenty and the steward read it and gave it back and said the roll is made in the autumn.*”

“*Then it will be made in the autumn.*”

“*It is made in the autumn and I shall not be on it, because a household is a house and a man in a bed is a man in a bed, and that is not an injustice, that is just what the word means, and nobody has ever had to explain it to me because nobody has ever asked.*”

---

And at about the fourth hour a woman of thirty-four who washes at a sluice and rents the second room of the fourth house of nineteen came out of the rain and stood beside him, and she had been up the lane and down again in it.

“*Say what you have come to say,*” said Ilyan, “*and say it before we start.*”

“*The row has begun to say the word stranger about you,*” said Mab Harrow, “*and I have come up to tell you what the difference is, because I do not think anybody in this town will.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*On Monday in that square they said *the man who read the notice*. That is what forty people said and it went round with the story in it. It is a word a town gives a man it has had an event with. It puts him in a story and the story has him in it doing a thing he did.*”

“*And the new word.*”

“*The new word is that you are a stranger. And a stranger is not a word a town gives a man it has had an event with. A stranger is a word a town gives a man it was always going to have nothing to do with.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say how long it has been going.*”

“*Three days. It went round the row on the Tuesday and it went round the row faster than the rain, and the row did not do it to be unkind, and I want that said out loud. The row is nineteen houses and about twenty-three people and there is nothing in any of them to be proud of this week except that a man read a notice out in a square and did not stop half way through it, and that is the last thing any of us has got.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in your own mouth.*”

“*The other half is that on Saturday I asked a man at a counter for credit for eleven years and he stopped me because he could not find me in a book, and I have known that man four years and he was right. And this week the row has found out that it has been doing the same thing to you with its mouth for three days, and it has not cost any of us a penny, and I do not know which of the two of those is the one I mind.*”

Nobody in that square said anything for a moment, and the rain came off the awning in a line.

“*Say it anyway,*” said Ilyan, “*because I have been in this town twelve days and I would rather be told.*”

“*No,*” said Mab Harrow, “*I have said it to you and not to the square, and I am going to keep that one.*”

---

And then, at about the fifth hour, on the wet edge of the square, a woman of thirty-eight with a bad hip and a key to gates that were open asked him to do a thing, and he said no, and it was the only no he said that day.

“*Say what you want and say it in the lane where it is going to be said,*” said Sefa Lund.

“*Iven Cape. Eighty-one. My husband’s sister is her own person and she is in the second house of that row, and she has not been out of that house since the water came over the Fen nine years ago, and she is the only person left in this row who was here before the sluice was a board’s and not a common’s. And she has asked me three times this week what the water is.*”

“*Say what she asked it.*”

“*She asked whether the water of the Hallow is hers. In those words. And I have told her no twice, because she is eighty-one and it is a bad week, and I want to tell her once by somebody who has read the board.*”

“*Say the whole of that and say the rest of it.*”

“*The rest of it is that the Book comes down twice a year, and the last time it was down was eight days ago, and it is nine feet up over that fire, and there are two people in this town who can read the face of it, and one of them is in this square and one of them is in Ostrey or she is dead.*”

“*Say the reason I am saying no and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am saying no, and the reason is a language one and not a standing one, and I would like it written down in that order.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I can read the face of it. And if I go up that lane and read the fourth clause out in a kitchen to a woman of eighty-one, then I have to say the words *common to all households of this town*, and I am a stranger standing in a kitchen in the rain.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*And there is not one person in that kitchen who can be asked afterwards whether I read it right, or read it wrong, or read it to suit myself. And I would be doing the reading. The reading is the whole of what is on that board, and the whole of what is wrong with this town is that the reading is done in a hurry by two people in a stone room, and nobody reads the other side.*”

“*Say the other half of that, because you have not said it, and do not let it be smaller than it is.*”

“*The other half is that I could not be asked about it. Not by her and not by her family and not by anybody in this town, because there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may be put in a book, and I have said that sentence out loud in three rooms in twelve days and every time I have said it I have enjoyed saying it, and it is the one thing I own.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say whether both of them are true.*”

“*Both of them are true, and neither of them is the whole of it, and I have been in this town twelve days and I have not found a third thing, and I am not going to go up that lane this afternoon and pretend there is one.*”

The fever was twenty-five weeks and six days old. The rain was on its fourth day and the market square was half empty and full of noise, and a woman of eighty-one in the second house of nineteen was going to be told on Friday evening, by somebody else, in about nine words, that the water of the Hallow is not hers.

And a man of thirty-one was standing at the wet edge of a square in a town that had begun to say the word household in front of him, four days into rain, with nothing to file, and a good coat, and no place to put any of it.
