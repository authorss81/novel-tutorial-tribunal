# Chapter 484: A Thursday In A Room Over A Taproom At The Top Of Four Hundred Yards Of Mud With About Nine People In It, In Which The Instruments Of This Town Are Named Out Loud For The First Time, And A Fifth Thing Is Proposed And Refused, And A Sixth Is Named And Refused, And The Answer To Whether An Exit Is A Right Or A Privilege Stops Being A Sentence And Becomes A List Of Four Things And Who Holds Them, And A Man Of Thirty-Nine Says To A Man Of Thirty-One In Front Of Nine People The Reason He Would Not Open A Book

Fourth day of the ninety-first week. His two hundred and thirty-fourth morning. Eighty-four days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and five days. Thirty-eight days since the division. Thirty-two days in this town.

Nobody called that room. The room is over a taproom at the top of four hundred yards of mud and it has a table in it, and the taproom underneath heard everything that was said in it.

The reason the nine of them were in it on the Thursday morning of the thirty-eighth day after the division is that the sheet with the two lines under the head is in a press in a stone room at the bottom of that lane, and anybody in this town may read it. About nine people had read it, and had come up the lane to see whether anybody else had read it.

Nobody had been in that room for about two hours before about nine people came into it.

One of the nine was a miller of thirty-nine who had brought a book to the room and not out of it. One was a copyist of thirty-four who had brought a sheet of paper and a pen and nothing else. One was a chair of forty-seven who had brought nothing. And one was a woman of thirty-eight who had come up four hundred yards in the wet, because the bar was on its eighth day and the board had sat for the first time since the thirtieth and had answered nothing.

---

The question was put by Coll Sarn, who is twenty-two, and who is on nobody’s book, and who has said the true sentence three times in this town and has been right three times.

“*Say the question,*” said Ilyan Vester, “*and say it in the ordinary voice, because about nine of us are in a room and nobody has called it and that is not how this town has done anything for five weeks.*”

“*What in this town is a thing a person can be asked about.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute, and then about four of the nine said four things.

The four things are the four instruments, and they have been the four instruments since a man of thirty-one counted them out loud under an awning in a market square, on the morning after a stranger’s letter was read out in the same square.

“*Say them one at a time and say who holds each,*” said Wenna Crale, standing at the end of the table with her pen on the paper.

“*A miller’s second book at the bottom of the market square, with about nine hundred names in it and a mark against each, and the man who holds it is standing in this room with his hand on it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*A copyist in a stone room, paid two pounds a year for eleven years, and she is the only person in the county of Ostrey who can read the old hand on a board that is nailed nine feet up over a fire, and she is standing at this table.*”

“*Go on and say the third and say what a minute is.*”

“*A minute of the water board, in a room over a saddler’s shop, with a day on it, and the chair of that board is sitting at this table and has not brought it and says he will not.*”

“*Go on and say the fourth and say the last of the whole of it.*”

“*And a woman of thirty-eight who keeps a sluice and refuses things in lanes. She has refused this board in a lane in front of about nine people, and she has been right every time she has done it. She is standing by the door with her hat in her hands, and she is out of the water as of the thirtieth day after the division.*”

About nine people in a room over a taproom did not make a sound, and the taproom underneath heard all of it.

---

And then a fifth thing was proposed, and it was proposed by a man of about thirty who had a hand in a box of nails, and he proposed it because he had been in a shed at the bottom of that square and had looked at a board in it.

“*Say what you are proposing and say where you got it from.*”

“*There is a nail in a board in a shed at the bottom of this square, driven in by somebody who is not alive, and there is a piece of tin on it, and it says the roll is a list of doors.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*It has been the truest thing said in this town in six weeks and it cost a carter nothing to say, and it is in a shed and not in a room.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I am proposing that the nail is the fifth instrument of this town, and I am a man of about thirty and I sell nails, and I have thought about it for three days and I have not been asleep about it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech,*” said Ott Vernay, from the table, without lifting his hand off the book.

And the room refused it, and the refusal was correct, and it took about four minutes and about nine people in it agreed with it before anybody had finished saying it.

“*An instrument is a thing a person can be asked about,*” said Wenna Crale. “*And the first thing a town has to be able to do with a thing it is asking questions is to be told by it that it is wrong.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*A nail cannot say no. A nail cannot be asked anything at all. You can go down to that shed and look at a piece of tin and you cannot ask it whether it is right about the roll. About two hundred people in a market square have worked out in six weeks that a thing a person can be asked about is the whole of what an instrument is, and a piece of tin in a shed is the one thing in this town that nobody has ever been able to be wrong.*”

“*Go on and say why that makes it not one, and say it in a room.*”

“*It makes it not one because it cannot be asked anything, and an instrument that cannot say no is a wall and not a door, and this town has had one wall in five weeks and it is four feet by two and a half and it is nailed to a beam.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute, and the taproom underneath heard about four people stop talking.

---

And then somebody said the question that a woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off had put in four pages in a round hand addressed to a water board, and the room tried to answer it with a sentence for about two minutes and the sentence did not work.

“*Say the question in your own mouth,*” said Ivo Cray, from the stair, where he had been standing the whole time.

“*Which of the four is a right and which of them is a privilege,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*A right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right. An exit is a right when the person it is for can open it without asking anybody’s character. That is her sentence, and it is correct, and I have said it in a square in this town and about nine people have it.*”

“*Go on and answer it.*”

“*A miller’s second book is a door and the man holding it is a person who says no. A copyist is a door and she is a person who says no to a stranger. A minute is a door and a board of nine men says no on Wednesdays. A woman in a lane is a door and she has said no four times in six weeks and been right four times.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*That is a sentence and it does not answer the question, and it has taken me two minutes to say it and it is wrong.*”

“*Say what is wrong with it.*”

“*Those four are the same shape. The difference between a right and a privilege in this town is not in the first of the two lists. It is in who is holding it. About nine people in this room know all four of the holders, and not one of the four has ever been asked.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*So the answer to whether an exit is a right or a privilege in the county of Ostrey is a list of four things and a list of who holds them, and the second list is the whole of the answer.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that.*”

“*It is in four heads in this room, and a man of thirty-nine’s is the only one of the four that is written down. It is written down in a book of nine hundred names with a mark against each, and the book is shut to a hand’s width.*”

And that is where the sentence stopped being the answer.

About four people in that room noticed that it had stopped, and the rest of the nine did not notice anything at all, because it is the shape of a room to stop and go on in the middle of a thing without any of it appearing in the minutes.

---

And then a woman of thirty-four who rents a second room in a house of nineteen and washes at a sluice said the sixth thing, and she said it flat, and it was the only sentence anybody said in that room on the Thursday that anybody has written down anywhere.

“*Say what you have said and say it again,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*There is a fifth instrument. He is standing in this room, he is thirty-one, he is not of this town, he is not upon a road of anything, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that will put his name in a book of this town, and I am proposing that he is the fifth and that the list is five.*”

Nobody in that room said anything at all.

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*I have been in nine rooms in this town in five weeks and eight of the nine were rooms he had been in, and in about four of the nine he was the only person in the room who could not be asked about anything. That was the half of it that is a gift.*”

“*Say the other half.*”

“*The other half is that when he has gone out of the room there is nobody in it who was sent for. I said that in a shop and I was right, and I am not saying it again for him.*”

“*Say what you want of the room.*”

“*I want the room to write him down. He has wanted to be written down since the sixth day after the division and he has said so in nine rooms, and I am not going to be the woman who says it for him a tenth time in a lane. I am saying it in a room, in front of nine people who cannot be sent for either.*”

---

And he said yes, and he said it in about four seconds, and it was the fastest thing anybody said in that room all morning, and it turned out to be the worst thing anybody said in that room all morning.

“*Say what you have said and say the rest of it,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the table.

“*A list of four things and who holds them is an answer, and the list is in four heads, and the whole of the difference between a right and a privilege is in the second list, and I am the only person in this county who is on no list at all, and I would like to be on one. Put me in it. Put me in it in a book with a day on it and a hand at the foot of it.*”

About nine people in a room over a taproom did not make a sound.

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said Sefa Lund, from the door.

And the room refused it, and the refusal was correct, and it took about nine minutes, and about nine people in that room agreed with it before the end of it, and it is the only time in six weeks that a thing anybody in Halloway wanted was refused in a room by about nine people who were all right.

“*An instrument is a thing that can be asked about after the person who brought it has gone out of the room,*” said Wenna Crale. “*That is the whole of the test and it is not mine. I have been the copyist of this town for eleven years, and I have watched about forty-one men in a stone room fail it, because the minute they have gone out of the room there is nobody who can be asked whether the minute is a fair account of what they said.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*There is no way in this county to ask you about a list tomorrow. I have tried it. There is no way to ask you about it today. There is a copyist sitting at this table who has been asking you things for five weeks and has never once got an answer she could enter. A man who cannot be sent for is a man who cannot be asked, and a man who cannot be asked is not a door. He is the one standing in the doorway of a door, and he is not on either side of it.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this room.*”

“*And the other half of that is a form. A form with a day on it and a hand at the foot of it. And on the Wednesday morning of the thirtieth day after the division a form with a day on it and a hand at the foot of it put nineteen houses out of the water of this town in about four minutes in an office over a shop, and nobody was asked to make it, and it was correct.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*” said Ordwin Cape, from the rail.

“*And this room has spent six weeks finding out that a book is a door, and a man in a book is a door with a person in it. If this room puts him in a book, then this room has made a door with a person in it, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that can be asked whether that door is a door or a wall.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*We found out about walls nine days ago in a market square, from a man of forty-seven who could not tell which he had built.*”

---

And then Ott Vernay took his hand off the book and put both hands flat on the table, which is the thing a miller does, and he did not say it to make a point and he did not make it a speech, and about nine people in that room and about eleven people in a taproom underneath heard it.

“*Say what you have to say and say it in front of them,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*In my shop, on the morning after you read four lines of a stranger’s letter out in a square, you stood under the awning of my mill with about thirty people in that square and you counted the instruments of this town out loud. A miller’s second book. A copyist. A minute. A woman who refuses things in lanes. You said it in the plainest words anybody has used in this town in six weeks.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And I am the first of those four, and I am not going to un-say a number I have had for four years.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*About nine men in this town have that sentence now and I have it worst, because it is the reason my book is shut, and it is the reason a hundred and seventy households of this town do not know that they are in it.*”

“*Say the other half of that in your own mouth.*”

“*You did not say it to shut it. That is the other half and I am going to say it once and then I am not going to say it again. You said a true thing in a square and about nine of us heard it, and about nine more had it by the evening. A book in a shop is a thing that gets opened, and I have had it shut since the fifteenth day after the division. If you had said it in a lane to me I would have had it on the Tuesday.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute, and nobody thanked anybody for any part of it.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*And I am not sorry for it, and you are not going to be able to make me, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry, and that is the second time in three days that the answer has turned out to be that.*”

---

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and five days old. About nine people were in a room over a taproom at the top of four hundred yards of mud, and the taproom underneath heard the whole of it.

Four instruments, and a fifth thing that is not one, and a sixth that is not one, had all been named out loud in a room in a town of four thousand people for the first time, and about nine people had agreed with the two refusals before the refusals were finished.

Not one of the six was written down anywhere. One of them is in a book of nine hundred names with a mark against each, which is shut to a hand’s width. The other five are in a sheet of paper that a copyist of thirty-four brought to the table and did not write on.

And a man of thirty-one had asked that room to put him in a list, and had been refused in nine minutes, and had been right to ask.
