# Chapter 491: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Second Week, And A Thursday On Which About Nine People In A Stone Room Work Out That An Instrument And A Decision Are Two Different Things, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Says It Out Loud

Third day of the ninety-second week, and not the first and not the last of it. His two hundred and forty-first morning. Ninety-one days after the settlement. The fever twenty-nine weeks and five days. Forty-five days since the division. Thirty-nine days in this town.

The Stone House stood with the door open to the square. About nine people sat round the long table under the north beam. The press against the west wall stood unlocked. The copy of the roll lay beside the door where any person of the town might read it.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned at the sixth hour or at the twelfth hour for fifteen days. The first missed winding had been the sixth hour of the thirty-first day after the division. The wheel at the mill had been slow fourteen days completed. The roll for the autumn was made in two days, on the forty-seventh day after the division, in an office over a shop.

Ilyan Vester sat near the end of the table with his bad leg set to one side. He had come in out of a thin rain. His coat hung damp over the back of the chair. He wanted the instrument of the fortieth day to have been a mistake, so that he could go back and do it in a proper form.

Wenna Crale stood at the head of the table with the instrument spread flat under her hands. The head carried the day on it and her hand at the foot of it. The first part named the four instruments of the town. The second part carried the account of the row at the Fen, given by the woman who had kept it.

“*Tell it as it stands and leave nothing out of it,*” said Perdy Sallow from the fourth stool.

Wenna put one finger on the first part and one finger on the second. A chair scraped back near the hearth as a man made room for a boy with a wet sack.

“*It names four things and who holds each,*” said Wenna. “*It sets down what the row was told, and by whom, and on what day. It decides nothing. It was read out in a room over a taproom on the fortieth day to about thirty people.*”

Ordwin Cape leaned forward with his hands round a cup. His reed knife lay on the table beside him.

“*Put the question that has been walking round this town for nine years,*” said Ordwin.

Ilyan lifted his head. This was the want he had carried in since the fortieth day, and he said it before he had weighed the room.

“*It was made with no body behind it,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*It was made in a room with no procedure and no chair with power. I would like it to have been a mistake, so that we can go back and make it again in a form that binds.*”

Ivo Cray took the bill of sale from his coat and laid it on the table, face down. He is thirty-four and holds the ninth lot on the Four Hundred. He looked at Ilyan without heat.

“*A thing asked for by everybody is not nothing,*” said Ivo Cray. “*About thirty people asked for that hearing. About twenty of them stayed four hours. A man who needs a thing of his to be wrong has not understood what it was for.*”

A pen stopped halfway down the table. A woman near the press folded her arms and looked at the beam. Rain ticked on the sill of the open door.

Mab Harrow sat against the wall by the second window. She is thirty-four and rents the second room in the fourth house and is not a head of household. She had washed at the sluice that morning and her sleeves were damp to the elbow. She had refused nothing in six weeks.

“*An instrument and a decision are two different things,*” said Mab Harrow. “*This town has one of them. It has had the other for nine years.*”

Perdy Sallow turned on his stool. Two men near the hearth shifted and faced her.

“*Make that plain, and put the nine years in it,*” said Perdy.

Mab pressed her damp sleeves flat on her knees. She did not raise her voice.

“*For nine years the board has made decisions,*” said Mab Harrow. “*It set the scouring. It set the rate. It put nineteen houses out of the water on the thirtieth day, correctly. Those were decisions, and they bound. The sheet of the fortieth day binds nothing and names everything. That is what an instrument does. One tells people what to do. The other tells people what was done.*”

A man by the press wrote a number on the back of his hand and rubbed it out with his thumb when he saw it was wrong. A girl in the doorway stepped out into the rain and came back in shaking water from her hair.

Ilyan felt the correction land. His mistake had been to say the want out loud in a room with about nine people in it, as if wanting could turn one kind of paper into the other. Ivo had answered him without cruelty, and that made it harder to set aside.

“*The arbitration of the fortieth day produced a sheet with a day on it and decided nothing,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*The board answered the two lines correctly on the thirty-seventh day and could not appoint anybody. Both of those are true.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a body must produce a decision,*” said Wenna. “*A board may sit and answer correctly and still leave the matter where it found it. That is what happened here.*”

Ordwin set his cup down. The sound of it on wood carried further than his voice.

“*Then the four instruments are not a poor substitute for an office,*” said Ordwin Cape. “*They are what a town has instead of one, until somebody makes one.*”

“*And the somebody is the whole of the next nine days,*” said Mab.

About four people round the table agreed out loud. A man with a wet sack said aye. A woman by the press said that was right. Two more gave a short word of assent. About five did not. One stared at the beam. One kept his eyes on the bill of sale. One got up and went to stand in the doorway where the rain blew in. One refolded a list and put it away. Nobody in that room can be asked about it afterwards, because no minute was taken and no body sat.

Ilyan walked back to his rooms over a nail shop in the rain with the damp working through his coat. The cost of the morning was plain to him by the time he reached the stair. He had asked a room to call his own work wrong so that he could be useful again, and a man of thirty-four had refused him, correctly, in front of witnesses. The instrument stood. The decisions stood apart from it. He would have to work from that, and not from a second attempt.

The fever was twenty-nine weeks and five days old. He hung his damp coat over the stair rail, and left the instrument standing flat on the table in his mind, with the day on it and her hand at the foot of it.
