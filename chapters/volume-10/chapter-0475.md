# Chapter 475: A Thing Goes In A Book On A Monday In An Office Over A Shop, And On The First Day Of The Ninetieth Week About Thirty People In A Stone Room Find Out That The Roll Of This Town Is One Thousand And Seventy-One And Has Been Since The Twenty-Eighth Day After The Division And Nobody Asked Them, And Nine Of The Eleven New Houses Are In The Ninth Ward Of A House That Does Not Scale And Is Not Going To, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Finds Out That He Is On It

First day of the ninetieth week, and the first day of it. His two hundred and twenty-fifth morning. Seventy-five days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and three days. Twenty-nine days since the division. Twenty-three days in this town.

The copy of the roll of Halloway hangs in the Stone House against the west wall and any person of the town may read it, and on the Tuesday morning of the twenty-ninth day after the division there were about thirty people in that room, which is four times as many as have ever been in it on a weekday.

They had all read the same eleven lines.

The four lines of the Sunday correction were on the top of the sheet in a steward’s hand with the twentieth day after the division under them.

At the foot of the same sheet there were eleven more lines in the same hand, with the twenty-eighth day after the division under them, and they had been in the press since about the seventh hour.

Nobody had called anybody together. About thirty people had walked into a stone room because the door of it is open.

---

The eleven lines were read out by the copyist, standing at the end of the table, in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took her about a minute and a half, and about nine people in that room heard every word of it and about twenty heard it from the doorway.

*Second correction. Eleven houses in this town, entered in this year’s roll as land in some year and not as households, are households of this town from this day. Corrected the twenty-eighth day after the division, upon the account of a miller of this town and a woman of thirty-four of this town, and of the finding of nineteen doors on the fifteenth day after the division, and no request. A.T., steward. Nothing else has been altered.*

“*Do not make that a speech,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the fourth stool.

“*That is the whole of it,*” said Wenna Crale. “*And it is in my own hand and his own, and I copied it into the press at about the seventh hour, and I did not ask him whether I might, and he did not tell me to, and both of those are in this room.*”

Nobody in that stone room made anything of it.

“*Say the number out loud, and say it in a room and not in a square,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*One thousand and seventy-one,*” said Amos Trill, from the end of the table. “*One thousand and sixty on the Sunday, and one thousand and forty-one for eleven years before that, and eleven more this morning. And the water rate is twopence a quarter, and the year’s money on one thousand and seventy-one households is thirty-five pounds and fourteen shillings, and the quarter is eight pounds and eighteen shillings and sixpence.*”

“*Go on,*” said Sefa Lund, from the wall, where she had been standing since the seventh hour.

“*And the north heel of the board’s frame is nine pounds, and the quarter in the board’s hand is eight pounds and eighteen shillings and sixpence, and the heel is eighteen pence more than the quarter. On the Sunday it was forty pence more than the quarter. I have done that sum three times since Monday morning and it is right and I do not like it.*”

---

And the machinery was the steward’s, and it had been given to nineteen houses at the Fen on the twentieth day after the division, and nobody had ever thought about who else was holding it.

“*Say what the form says,*” said Ilyan, “*in the words on the form, and not in yours, because in nine days I have watched four different people describe that form in four different ways and every one of them was making it into an argument.*”

“*It says that a roll may be corrected in respect of a household entered as land,*” said Amos Trill. “*It says the correction is the steward’s and not the board’s, and that the reason and the day and the hand shall be entered. Those are the words and they have been the words for forty years and I have had the form in a drawer for forty years.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Ordwin Cape, from the rail.

“*It does not say whose household. It does not say whether anybody asked.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*On the Sunday morning I used it on the Fen because a man of sixty-one had done a sum in a lane and a girl of nineteen had carried a roll under her coat in three kinds of weather. This morning I used it on eleven houses because a man of thirty-nine read nine names off his own counter to a woman of thirty-four.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*I have been thinking about it for six days, and I have not slept on it, and I am not going to pretend that I have.*”

“*And the other half of that, in this room.*”

“*The other half is that nobody asked me to do it, and nobody will be able to say that I should not have.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*That is exactly what I said about the first one on the Sunday morning in a market square. About four of my friends in this town were in that square, and about four of them have been telling me since the Sunday that I am a good man.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a steward may be a good man, and I would like that written down somewhere.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for any part of it.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight stood up in the middle of a stone room, at about the ninth hour, and read a line off the copy on the west wall with his finger on it, and about thirty people in that room listened to a man find out that he was on the roll of his own town.

“*Say your name and say what you are,*” said Ilyan, “*because I have been in this town twenty-three days and I have not asked you anything and about two hundred people in this town have not asked you anything either.*”

“*Baldwin Trove. Thirty-eight. The fourth row, going up from the square, and my house is the one with the lean-to on it, and I have been in it nine years, and I am a carter, and I have a wife and no children.*”

“*What you have found out, and in this room and not in a square.*”

“*That I am on the roll. That is what it says. Number one thousand and fifty-nine, in a hand that is the steward’s, on a line with the twenty-eighth day after the division under it, and I have been in that house nine years and there is a rate on me now and I did not know there was going to be one.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*And I am not going to say that I do not want it. I have wanted a rate on that house since the week I moved into it, because a man with a rate is a man anybody has to be civil to.*”

About thirty people in a stone room in a town of four thousand people did not make a sound.

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And I would like it in the room that nobody came and asked me. Nobody came. If somebody had come I would have said yes, and I would have said yes quickly, and I would have liked somebody to have come.*”

“*And the other half of that, in your own mouth.*”

“*And I have been in this room since the eighth hour because I saw my own line on a wall, and I have not come down here to be asked for anything.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*If this board or this House wants to know anything about me it can come up the row and ask me in my own doorway, and I will answer it there and not in a stone room. That is the whole of what I have come for.*”

---

And the woman of thirty-eight who keeps the sluice had not said anything for forty minutes, and when she did, about thirty people in a stone room found out what a correction does to the people who were not the subject of it.

“*Say what you have found out and say it in this room,*” said Ilyan.

“*That the eleven houses in that line are not the nineteen at the Fen and they are not the row, and that nobody in this room asked for them, and that on the Sunday morning the four lines on the top of that sheet were put in at the request of nineteen houses that had gone round nineteen doors to be asked.*”

“*Go on, and finish that sentence.*”

“*And that the form is the same form. It is one sheet of printed paper in an office over a shop and it has been in that drawer for forty years, and it was used on us on the Sunday and it was used on them on the Monday, and the only difference is that we asked and they did not.*”

“*And the other half of that, in this room.*”

“*And the row has been rated for four days. Four days. Nineteen houses on a roll at eight pence a household for the year, which is twelve shillings and eightpence between the whole of us, and nobody has come and asked for a penny of it, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody take twopence a quarter off a household that has never been charged twopence a quarter before.*”

“*Do not make that a speech,*” said Wenna Crale, with her pen up, and not writing.

“*The rest of that is that the nineteen tenements are nineteen on a roll of one thousand and seventy-one, and there are twenty-two households behind those nineteen doors, and eleven other rows behind eleven other doors, and that is one thousand and seventy-four, and the number one thousand and seventy-four is in nobody’s book and cannot be got, because there is no form in this county for putting a third household into a house that is already on the roll.*”

“*Say the number out loud, and say what it is a number of.*”

“*It is a number of what the roll would be if the whole of what we found on the Monday were in it, and it is a number of nothing else, and it is twenty-two pence a year away from the figure on the wall, and that is the whole of what a correction does to the people who were not the subject of it.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything for about nine seconds.

Then the room worked out the rest of it, and it took about four minutes, and about nine people worked it out at the same moment and about twenty-one got there after them.

Nine of the eleven new houses are in the ninth ward.

“*Say the number out loud, and what it is a number of,*”

“*Nine of eleven in the ninth ward,*” said Sefa Lund. “*The House has five heads in the ninth ward. It has had five for longer than anybody in this town can account for, and there is no form in this county that says a ward has a number of seats at all, and the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*The fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn. And the roll it will be elected on is a roll of one thousand and seventy-one.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*It was one thousand and sixty a fortnight ago and one thousand and forty-one for eleven years. The fifth head of the ninth ward will be chosen by the households of the ninth ward, and there are nine more of them than there were a fortnight ago.*”

“*And the other half of that, in this room.*”

“*A corrected roll is a corrected constituency. Not a corrected count. And the constituency is the whole of who is in the room where the minute is written. The House of this town is nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, and five in the ninth ward, and the number of seats has not changed and is not going to change, and the number of doors has.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything for about nine seconds.

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And what has changed is that in about three weeks there will be a day out of a day on which a man in an office over a shop counts houses, and nine of them will be doors he did not count before.*”

“*Go on, and do not make that a speech.*”

“*And nobody in this town will be able to ask him about it afterwards, because there is no form that says a man may be asked what he counted.*”

---

And the sentence was in the room, and it was his, and it had been said on a Sunday morning in nine seconds to about a hundred people, and it had been said four ways in a square six days later and three of them had been wrong.

A woman at the back, who was about thirty and had a child on her hip, said it word for word, in the right order, with the right words, and about nine people in that room turned round and looked at the place where Ilyan Vester was standing.

“*How did you get it,*” said Ordwin Cape.

“*Off a nail on a shed at the bottom of the lane,*” said the woman. “*My husband is a carter and he asked the man who drove it in where it came from, and he said a stranger said it in a square in the rain on the Sunday.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*I have had it since the Sunday and I have been waiting for a room to say it in.*”

Nobody thanked her for it either.

He had been in nine rooms in twenty-three days and he had said a true sentence in a square at about the hundredth hour on the Sunday morning, in about nine seconds, to about a hundred people.

It had been carried out of that square by about forty of them and about four had repeated it correctly, and it was in the room now in the right words, said by a person of thirty with a child on her hip who had got it off a nail.

His want had been, since the Sunday, to be in the ninth ward in the autumn. He has not said it out loud in any room and there is no minute in this town in which it appears.

His cost was about nine seconds in which he could have said that it was his, and he used them on not saying it, and about thirty people in a stone room found out what a roll is on the same morning that he found out that a sentence in a book is a thing that belongs to the book.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and three days old. A sheet with four lines on it and eleven lines on it, in a steward’s hand, with two days on it, hung in a press against a west wall in a stone room in a town of four thousand people, and any person of the town may read it.

The ninth ward had nine doors in it that it had not got on the twentieth day after the division, and the fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn.
