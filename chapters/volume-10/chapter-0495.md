# Chapter 495: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Ninety-Second Week, And A Monday On Which The House Of This Town Is Called For The First Time In Nine Years On The Single Question Of What Forty-One Men Can Do, And The Answer Takes One Sentence And Is Not A Decision, And About Twenty-Three Of The Forty-One Have Never Heard The Sentence A Man Of Thirty-One Has Said In Nine Rooms

Seventh day of the ninety-second week, and the last day of it. The morrow is the first day of the ninety-third. His two hundred and forty-fifth morning. Ninety-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and two days. Forty-nine days since the division. Forty-three days in this town.

The Stone House filled from the ninth hour.

Forty-one men came in by wards, in the order of the wards, and took forty-one stools. The House of Halloway is nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, five of them in the ninth ward, elected for a year, and it meets in this room because the room has a hearth in it and about two hundred people stood in the square outside with the door open.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says the number of households in a ward is the number of seats in it. There is no form anywhere that says the House of this town scales, and the false version of that, which is that the fen is nineteen more seats, is in about forty mouths in this town and cannot be recalled. Nobody corrected it that day. Nobody was in a position to.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for nineteen days. The wheel at the mill had been slow eighteen days completed.

Ilyan Vester sat against the east wall where strangers sit, on a stool with no name on the list of forty-one, and he could see the whole room and nobody in it could see the paper in his coat.

He had one sentence. He had said it in nine rooms of this town in six weeks, and about nine of the people who had heard it had it right, and about nine had it wrong in a way that mattered, and about twenty-three had never heard it at all. He had run out of rooms to say it in and he wanted to say it once more, and there were forty-one men in this room and this was the last room in the town that had forty-one men in it.

---

Amos Trill stood first, because he is the steward and because he had been asked, and asking him had taken three days and a walk to his house.

“*The steward called no meeting in nine years until a man of thirty-four asked him to and he refused,*” Amos Trill said. He is sixty-one. “*He is still not going to call it on the question of the Book. The House has not been called since the fourteenth day after the division. It is called now on the question of what forty-one men can do, and that is the whole of the question and I have not added anything to it.*”

A man in the third ward wanted the question read back before anything else was said, and it was read back, and it was the question and nothing else: what forty-one men can do about a bar four feet long at the head of the sluice at the Fen.

A man in the second ward turned his stool a quarter round. Two men in the sixth ward looked at each other and one of them put his hand over his mouth and left it there. The man who had been sent for pushed his reed toward the middle of the table and then pulled it back.

The man who had asked had four things written on the back of his hand. He did not read them out, and he put the hand behind his knee, and nobody in that room asked him to, and about four of them had seen the writing from where they sat.

Perdy Sallow rose from the fourth stool of the fourth ward. He is forty-seven and chair of the water board.

“*Then I will say what forty-one men can do, and it is one sentence, and I have had nine years to be sure of it and about six weeks to be sure that I am ashamed of it, and both of those are true and I am going to say them in that order.*” He put his hand on the table. “*Forty-one men can find out what a thousand people think and can say it out loud in forty-one kitchens for a year.*”

A man of the fifth ward put his head on one side.

“*That is all?*”

“*That is all, and it is a great deal more than this town has had, because the last time forty-one men were in a room they had a book nine feet up over a fire and nobody could read it.*”

Three questions then, and they came in the order they came in. A man of the third ward asked whether the House could order the frame mended. A man of the sixth asked whether it could put nineteen houses back on the roll. A man of the seventh asked whether it could shut the gate at the head of the sluice.

Each of them went into the room and lay there. None of them was answered for about four minutes, and the four minutes were not silence; they were nine men asking other men whether they had heard right, in the low voice, along the rows.

---

The fifth head of the ninth ward stood last, because the ninth ward is taken last, and it is the order of the wards and nobody in that room thought to change it.

He had been elected on a roll three weeks old. He is a person who has been in this business for six weeks and is not a stranger, and he is a head of a household in a street of eleven houses, and he has a list in his hand with a mark against five of the doors he had been to.

“*That is right,*” the last man of the ninth ward said, “*and it is half of it.*”

Perdy Sallow sat down slowly, which he does not do.

“*Say the other half in this room and not outside it,*” Perdy said.

“*A body which can only find out what people think is a body that somebody else has to answer.*”

The room waited, because a man who says a thing like that in a room of forty-one is not finished about half the time.

“*Forty-one men can go home tonight and ask a thousand people what they think about a charter with an exit in it. They can ask it properly, because they are heads of households and there are a thousand people who will open a door to them and to nobody else. They can come back in a week and say the answers out loud where about two hundred people in the square can hear them.*”

He turned the list over and put it on the table face down, and put his thumb on it.

“*That is not a decision. That is what it is for, and I am not going to hear anybody in this room pretend otherwise to be agreeable with me.*”

A man of the second ward told him to say the other half, because he had had it in his mouth since he sat down.

“*The other half is that a House which can do that and nothing else is a thing this town has built so that it does not have to answer anybody.*” He took his thumb off the list and left it lying there. “*Nine men of a water board made three decisions in nine years and every one of them bound. On the thirtieth day they put nineteen houses out of the water, and they were right to, and the wording was four hundred years old. The thing that did it was that nobody in this county could be asked what they were doing, and a thing that cannot be asked is a thing that is never answered.*”

A man of the second ward observed that he was not saying the nine were wicked.

“*I have not said that word and I am not going to use it. I am saying the shape of the thing, and a shape is not a man. Nobody in that room over a saddler’s shop has ever wanted to put nineteen houses out of the water, and four of them said out loud on the thirtieth day that they wished it were not so, and the wishing changed nothing at all, which is the part I cannot get past.*”

He picked the list up off the table and held it against his chest.

“*I am not asking you to make anybody wicked. I am asking you to notice that a thing which cannot be compelled is a thing somebody else has to be, and that the somebody else is not going to be here forever, and neither is the water.*”

“*That is not fair on the nine,*” said a man of the sixth ward.

“*It is not, and I have not said it was. But I have said it is the shape, and a shape is not a man.*”

---

A stir went through the square outside about the middle of that. A woman shouted a question through the open door and a man on the stool nearest it answered her without getting up. Two more shouted after that. A man in the second ward stood and said that if the House was going to be asked things by the square then the House had better be a House and not a reading of a book, and a man in the fifth ward told him to sit down, and a child was lifted onto somebody’s shoulders at the back of the square to see over the door frame.

Ilyan stood up.

His mistake was ready in his mouth. It was the sentence, the fourth time in six weeks, and he had about nine seconds to decide whether to say it, and he had decided before he got to his feet, and that is the whole of what it is.

“*A refusal without a remedy can become domination,*” Ilyan Vester said. “*This town used its freedom on the thirtieth day and put nineteen houses out of the water, and it was correct to do it, and the correctness is the part that frightens me. Freedom needs a way to stop willing allies swallowing each other, and there is not one in this county.*”

About nine of the forty-one gave him their eyes. About nine frowned and looked away at the same moment, which is what nine people do when they have heard a thing four times and are tired of it. About twenty-three went on talking among themselves as though he had not spoken at all, and one of those twenty-three was on the fifth stool of the ninth ward and had written a mark on a list that morning.

A man near the hearth put more wood on the fire and sent a sheet of sparks up into the flue, and the sparks came back down and went out on the stones, and nobody swept them.

---

The House agreed before noon. It would send its forty-one men to ask in forty-one kitchens what the town thought of a charter with an exit in it, and it would meet again and say the answers out loud, and it would put the answers in a press where any person of the town may read them.

No decision was taken. No order was given. Nothing was resolved.

The forty-one went out into the square in about the order they had come in, by wards. About four of them were given the list before they got to the door. About nine of them already had it in a coat because they had written it themselves.

A man of the seventh ward said out loud that if they were going to ask a thousand people in forty-one kitchens then somebody was going to have to say what the question was, and four people in that doorway said it was the charter with the exit in it.

He said that was not a question, that was a thing to say yes or no to. About nine people in that square heard a man discover in real time that asking and telling are different, and that nobody in this town had ever asked him which of the two he had been doing for nine years. The steward entered the calling with a day on it and with his hand at the foot of it, and read it back standing.

The copy went into the press against the west wall within the hour. It is the fourth thing in that press in eleven days, and the second of those four that anybody in this town can read without asking a person for it, and the press is unlocked and the door is open and about two hundred people in the square know it.

He left with the crowd and stood in the square where the rain had cleared and the stone was beginning to give up the day.

The cost sat plain in him and there was no way of putting it down. He had said the true thing once more in front of forty-one men, and it had moved nothing, because a House that finds out is not a House that decides, and about twenty-three men had not heard him and never would from him. He would have to let the forty-one do the work he could not do, and there was no form anywhere that says a man may hand his work to a body of men and be thanked for it, and there is no form that says he may not.

A boy of about fifteen was standing at the edge of the square with a hoop over his arm, waiting for the crowd to thin. He asked the man of thirty-one, without any preamble and with the confidence of a person who has not yet learned to be careful, whether forty-one men could shut the gate.

The fever was thirty weeks and two days old. The House of Halloway had been called for the first time in nine years and had answered one question in one sentence and the answer had been that it could go home and ask. The morrow was the first day of the ninety-third week, and both sides of that boundary said so out loud, and forty-one men went out into a square of four thousand people carrying forty-one lists and no authority at all.
