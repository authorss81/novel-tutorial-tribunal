# Chapter 495: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Ninety-Second Week, And A Monday On Which The House Is Called For The First Time In Nine Years, On The Question Of What Forty-One Men Can Do

Seventh day of the ninety-second week, and the last day of it. His two hundred and forty-fifth morning. Ninety-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and two days. Forty-nine days since the division. Forty-three days in this town.

The Stone House filled from the ninth hour. Forty-one men came in by wards, in order, and took the forty-one stools. Nine wards, one elected head of household in each ward, forty-one in all, five in the ninth ward. About two hundred people stood in the square outside with the door open. The hearth burned low in the corner.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for nineteen days. The wheel at the mill had been slow eighteen days completed. The morrow would be the first day of the ninety-third week.

Ilyan Vester sat against the east wall where strangers sit. He had said one sentence in rooms of this town for six weeks, and about nine of the forty-one had it right and about nine did not and about twenty-three had never heard it. He had run out of rooms to say it in, and still he wanted to say it once more.

Amos Trill stood and spoke first, because he is steward and had been asked. He is sixty-one.

“*The steward called no meeting in nine years until a man of thirty-four asked him to and he refused,*” said Amos Trill. “*He is still not going to call it on the question of the Book. The House has not been called since the fourteenth day after the division. It is called now on the question of what forty-one men can do.*”

Perdy Sallow rose from the fourth stool of his ward. He is forty-seven and chair of the water board.

“*Find out what a thousand people think and say it out loud,*” said Perdy. “*That is what a House can do. It can go home to forty-one kitchens and ask, and come back and say the answers where all can hear.*”

A man of the third ward asked if the House could order the frame mended. A man of the sixth asked if it could put the row back on the roll. A man of the seventh asked if it could shut the gate. Each question fell into the room and lay there.

The fifth head of the ninth ward stood last. He had been elected on a roll three weeks old. He is a person who has been in this business for six weeks and is not a stranger. He carried a list in his hand with marks against five doors.

“*A body which can only find out what people think is a body that somebody else has to answer,*” said the last man of the ninth ward. “*That is the half of it this town has been circling. A House can find out and say out loud in forty-one kitchens for a year. That is not a decision, and that is what it is for.*”

A stir ran through the square outside. A woman shouted a question through the door and was answered by a man on the nearest stool. Chairs scraped. A child was lifted to see over shoulders.

Ilyan stood. His mistake was ready in his mouth, the sentence for the fourth time in six weeks, and he said it though he knew how it would land.

“*A refusal without a remedy can become domination,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*The town used its freedom on the thirtieth day and put nineteen houses out of the water, correctly. Freedom needs a way to stop willing allies swallowing others.*”

About nine of the forty-one gave him their eyes. About nine frowned and looked away. About twenty-three kept talking among themselves as if he had not spoken. A man near the hearth put more wood on the fire and sent sparks up.

The House agreed before noon. It would send its forty-one men to ask in forty-one kitchens what the town thought of a charter with an exit, and would meet again to say the answers out loud. No decision was taken. No order was given. The steward entered the calling with a day on it.

Ilyan left with the crowd and stood in the square where the rain had cleared. The cost sat plain in him. He had said the true thing once more and it had moved nothing, because a House that finds out is not a House that decides. He would have to let the forty-one do the work he could not do.

The fever was thirty weeks and two days old. The House had been called for the first time in nine years. The morrow would be the first day of the ninety-third week, and both sides of the boundary would say so.
