# Chapter 482: The First Day Of The Ninety-First Week, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Asks In A Stone Room With About Nine People In It Whether Anybody Can Be Sent For, And Is Told In Front Of The Room That A Thing Is Sent For By A Person With A Form, And A Man Of Thirty-One Tries For Twenty Minutes To Make A Form Out Of A Matter That Has Nowhere To Go, And A Copyist At Her Own Table Puts Two Lines Under A Head And They Are The First Two Lines Of Anything Anybody In Halloway Has Ever Drawn Up For Anybody Else

First day of the ninety-first week, and the first day of it. His two hundred and thirty-second morning. Eighty-two days after the settlement. The fever twenty-eight weeks and three days. Thirty-six days since the division. Thirty days in this town.

The Stone House has one hearth in the corner and a press against the west wall and a long table under the north beam, and the beam is nine feet up and about four feet in from the wall, and the board is still on it.

There were about nine people in that room on the Tuesday morning of the thirty-sixth day after the division, and the door of the building was open, and it is open all day every day, and about nine people were in it and about eleven more were in the square outside it doing nothing in particular.

---

Gault Rieve is fifty-four and has a lot on the Four Hundred and paid two pounds for it in the week of the division. He was the second of the three against the scouring on the tenth day. On the twenty-sixth day after the division he said three sentences on a stair over a taproom without knowing that they were the third line of a minute of the nineteenth day.

He stood up on the Tuesday morning and asked the question that about two hundred people in a market square had carried since the Saturday before last and that nobody had put on anything.

“*Say the question again and say it into the room and not into the square,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*Whether anybody can be sent for.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say why you are asking it in this room and not in a lane.*”

“*Because I have a lot on the Four Hundred and I have paid two pounds for it, and there is a bar at the head of the sluice that has not been turned for six days, and I want somebody to come and turn it.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I have been standing in this square for four days waiting for somebody to be sent for and nobody has come. I am told that a thing said in a lane is not a thing anybody can be asked about, so I am saying it in a room. There is a press against that wall and nine people in this room, and I would like the room to have it.*”

About nine people in that stone room did not make a sound.

---

And the answer came out of a chair of a water board in about four sentences, and it was the same answer a copyist had given in a lane four hundred yards off on the Monday, and the difference between the two answers was the whole of the morning.

“*A thing is sent for by a person with a form,*” said Perdy Sallow, from the fourth stool, which is where he sits. “*That is not new. A copyist said it in a lane yesterday at about the eleventh hour, in front of about eleven people, and she said it better than I am going to say it.*”

“*Go on and say what is different about it in this room.*”

“*In that lane it was a thing said. In this room it is a thing said in a room, and a thing said in a room is a thing that goes into a minute, and a minute is the only door in this town that anybody has ever been able to go through.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has been standing in a square for four days and cannot be sent for and cannot be sent for anybody, and that is not a thing about the water and it is not a thing about the frame. It is a thing about not having an office, and I have been the chair of this board for nine years and I have never once had an office, and I have thought about that a great deal this week.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*So put it in a minute. Ask for an arbitration.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*An arbitration is the name for a thing that is asked for when there is nobody to send for. And if nobody in this county will hold the thing, then a room in this town can hold it.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that.*”

“*I would like it said in front of nine people that I am the one who said that, and that a copyist heard it first in a lane.*”

---

And then Ilyan Vester did the thing he does, and it took him about twenty minutes, and it is worth setting out the whole of it because it is the shape of every form in this county and it took a man of thirty-one twenty minutes to arrive at the end of it.

He took a sheet out of a press against the west wall, which anybody in this town may do, and he wrote a head on it.

“*Say what you are writing and say why,*” said Wenna Crale, at the end of the long table, with her pen behind her ear and her sleeves up.

“*A form. The head of a form. A thing with an address, and a body, and a thing to be done, and a place for the answer, and a day.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it.*”

“*And then anybody may be asked about it afterwards, which is the one thing in this town that works, and it works because of a sentence a woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off wrote in four pages and that I read four lines of out loud in a square two days after the day I told you about them.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*And a form is the only instrument anybody in this county has ever had, and I have watched a town of four thousand people work that out from nothing in thirty days. I am not going to be the man who is asked for a thing in four weeks and has nothing in his coat.*”

“*Write the head then, and say it as you write it.*”

So he wrote the head, and it took about nine minutes, and it was good, and it was the best head anybody in that room had seen in five weeks.

*To the water board of Halloway. To the House of Halloway. To the office of the county surveyor at Kerby, in the county of Ostrey. To any person in this county having power in this matter. To nobody else.*

*Upon the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward. Upon the frame of the said sluice and the north heel of it. Upon the persons of the Fenrow and upon the persons of this town. Upon the bar four feet long.*

*This is a form of request. It is made on the day of the request. It is made by a person having no office in this county.*

And then there was nothing under the head, and there was nothing under the head because there was nowhere for it to go.

---

“*Go on and say the rest of that, and do not make it a speech.*” said Wenna Crale.

“*Say the four parts of a form. Say them out loud so that this room has them.*”

“*Who it is to. What is to be done. Where the answer is to go. On what day. That is a form. A form is a thing with somewhere to go, and every part of a form is either a person with an office or a day, and there is not one person with an office in this matter in the county of Ostrey.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The board of this town has a minute and a second book and neither of them has power over the roll. The House of this town has not been called and forty-one heads of household would have to be in a room to call it. The office at Kerby is twenty-two miles off, on a road, and it can make a return and put it in a book, and a return goes back to Kerby and not to a stone room with a hearth in it.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And you have written the best head of a form that anybody has written in this town in five weeks, and there is nothing under it, and there is going to be nothing under it, and you have known that since about the ninth minute of writing it and you went on for eleven more.*”

Nobody in that stone room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that in your own mouth and do not make it a speech.*”

“*What I have wanted since the sixth day after the division is to be a thing somebody can be asked about, and there is no form in this county that will do it. I have known that since the second week and I have said so in nine rooms.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of that.*”

“*This morning I have tried to make a form out of a thing that has nowhere to go, because a form is a door and I wanted to be a door.*”

“*And the door part.*”

“*And a door can only be a door if somebody on the other side of it wants to come through, and there is nobody on the other side of this one and there has not been for five weeks, and the whole of what I have been worth to this town is that nobody has wanted to come through.*”

---

And then Wenna Crale took the pen out from behind her ear and sat down at her own table at the end of the long table, which is the table she has copied at for eleven years. She turned the sheet with the head on it so that the head was at the top and the empty part of it was in front of her, and she wrote two lines under the head in her own hand.

“*Say what you have written and say it in this room,*” said Perdy Sallow.

“*I will read them out when they are dry and I will read them out in this room and not in the square, and I am not going to read them out twice.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*A thing said in a square is a thing that cannot be asked about, and a thing said in a room is a thing that can. The difference between those two is a chair and a table and nine people. Nobody in this room chose that, and the whole of this town’s last five weeks has happened in it.*”

She waited about a minute and then she read the two lines out, standing, in the voice she uses for the minutes, and it took about forty seconds.

*That this matter, upon which no body in the county of Ostrey has power, be heard by an arbitration, in a room, in this town, with the door open.*

*And that any person who comes to it be asked nothing touching his character, his rate, or his household.*

About nine people in that stone room did not make a sound, and about eleven people in the square outside it heard the last of it through the open door and got about half of it.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*” said Gault Rieve.

“*That is a request for an arbitration and it is in my own hand at my own table and there is no body behind it. It is not a form. It cannot be a form, and I have said so and I am not going to be asked to pretend otherwise in the next four weeks.*”

“*Go on and say what it is then.*”

“*It is two lines. And it is the first two lines of anything that anybody in the town of Halloway has ever drawn up for anybody else.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I have been a copyist for eleven years and I have entered eleven things in twenty-five days that no body asked me to enter, and I would like it in the room that those eleven were all for bodies and these two are not.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*It goes to the board tomorrow, because the board is a body and a body can refuse, and I would rather it were refused by nine men in a room than left on a table in a stone room for the fifth week running.*”

---

And Ilyan Vester was not on either of the two lines, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be put on a request, and about nine people in that room knew that and none of them said it.

His want all morning had been that it be a form. What came out was a sheet of paper with a head on it and nothing under the head, and a copyist wrote two lines under the head in her own hand.

The two lines were better than anything he had been in this town for five weeks and he was on none of them, and the morning had cost him the last thing he had come down here with, which was the belief that a door is a thing you build on purpose.

The fever was twenty-eight weeks and three days old. A bar four feet long was lying in the mud where two people had put it down on the Wednesday of the thirtieth day, and it was going to be under water by the Saturday.

A sheet of paper in a press against a west wall had a head on it and two lines under the head. The two lines asked for a thing that has no name and has no office, and has to be held in a room in a town of four thousand people or it will not be held at all.
