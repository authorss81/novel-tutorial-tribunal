# Chapter 472: A Market On A Saturday For The Fifth Time In Nine Years, Because For Three Weeks Nothing In Halloway Has Happened On A Tuesday, And A Man Of Thirty-Four With A Bill Of Sale In His Coat Walks Four Hundred Yards Up A Lane Into A Room Over A Taproom And Says He Has Heard The Whole Of It And Would Like To Know Whether The Water Belongs To Him, And The Majority Of That Room Discovers That It Has A Position, And Does Not Know That The Three Sentences Are Word For Word The Three Sentences Of A Minute Of The Nineteenth Day

Fifth day of the eighty-ninth week. His two hundred and twenty-second morning. Seventy-two days after the settlement. The fever twenty-seven weeks and no days. Twenty-six days since the division. Twenty days in this town.

The market in Halloway is on a Tuesday and it has been on a Tuesday for longer than anybody in that square can remember. This week it was on a Saturday, for the fifth time in nine years, because a wool factor had his carts on the ground by four o’clock on the Friday and would not have them turned about.

So about two thousand people were in a cobbled square the size of a good field with no hood between them, and it was the first day in three weeks that anybody in that town could remember the day of the week.

He had no part in it. He had been in that square on a Tuesday in the rain with a hundred people and on a Friday with two hundred, and both of those had been about something. On the Saturday it was a market, and in the middle of it a man he had never spoken to stood at the edge of the awning of the mill with a folded paper in his coat and looked at him for about a minute and a half, and then went up the lane.

---

The room is over a taproom at the top of four hundred yards of mud and it has a table in it, and there is nowhere else in that row with a table in it that is not somebody’s kitchen.

There were about nine of the row there and about twenty from the town who had come up the lane and were left standing on the stair, which is the width of one man. So about thirty people were in that room and on that stair, and the taproom underneath heard all of it for an hour.

Ivo Cray was thirty-four. He had bought the ninth lot on the Four Hundred in the week the common was divided, for two pounds, and he had a bill of sale in his coat that he had never needed to show anybody. He had a good coat and a pair of boots he had not needed in a fortnight.

“*Say your name and say what you are,*” said Ilyan, “*because I have been in this town twenty days and I have not asked you anything.*”

“*Ivo Cray. A ratepayer. Lot nine. And I have come up here to say a thing and I have been getting it ready for three weeks and I have said it in my own kitchen twice and both times I said it to a wall.*”

“*Go on.*”

He put the folded paper on the table and did not open it.

“*I have heard the whole of it. I have heard the notice read out and the gates shut and the roll corrected and the frame measured and the request refused. I have not been in a room for any of it and I have not been in a square for any of it either, because I have a cart on Thursdays.*”

“*Say what you want and say it as a question, because nobody in this room has been asked a question all day.*”

“*Whether the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward belongs to me.*”

About thirty people in a room and on a stair did not make a sound, and the stair made one.

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Sefa Lund, from the second chair.

“*The rest of that is that I paid two pounds for lot nine on the Four Hundred in the week it was sold. There is a bill in this town that says what I bought, and it says the Four Hundred acres and the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And there is no line in it anywhere saying the water did not go with the land.*”

“*Say why you have not opened the paper on the table.*”

“*Because if I open it I am a man bringing a thing into a room, and I have listened to that man say for twenty days that he is the only one who does that in this town, and I would rather ask than bring.*”

---

Nobody could answer him, and about eleven people tried, and every one of them was a person who could not be asked afterwards about it.

The steward of forty years is not in that row and has not been down that lane since the twenty-first day after the division. The board of nine sits in a room over a saddler’s shop at the north end of the square and had not been called. The Book is nine feet up over a fire at the end of that square and has not been off the beam since the audit nine years back.

And the clause that answers the question is on the back of it, in a fifth hand, a hundred and thirty-eight years old, and it does not answer this one either.

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Ilyan, and he said it standing at the end of the table with his hand flat on the wood, which is the thing he does and which did not help.

“*The half of it is that the water is common to all households of this town. Clause four. It has been there four hundred years and it is on the front of the board and it is not struck, and the word in it is *household*, and the fenrow are households upon the roll of this town as of the twentieth day after the division.*”

“*Go on to the end of the sentence.*”

“*And you are a household. You have a house and it is on the roll and you are charged twopence a quarter for water like everybody in this town. So on the face of the Book the water is as much yours as it is theirs and as much mine, and I am not of this town and it is not mine.*”

“*Go on and say the other half, because that is the half that has been in that room for three weeks and nobody has said it out loud in a room.*”

Ivo Cray opened the paper on the table at last, and it was a bill of sale with a head on it and a schedule at the foot, and he did not read the head.

“*Then read the schedule,*” said Cull Sarn, nineteen, from the doorway, “*because you have come four hundred yards up a lane with a piece of paper and everybody on that stair has been waiting since the Saturday to hear what is on it.*”

“*Lot nine, five acres, upland,*” said Ivo Cray. “*And at the foot of the schedule, in the same hand, in the same size of writing: *and the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, and the race, and the sluice, and the two closes below it.*””

Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds, and about twenty people on a stair above a taproom heard a piece of paper be the answer to a question nobody had thought to ask.

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Ordwin Cape, from the rail.

“*The rest of it is that the fenrow were not at the sale. They could not have been. They are on ground that was not sold because a man would not buy nine feet of standing water in the wet season, and the sale was legal and the sale was right, and I was one of the eighty who said so at the time and I have said so in three rooms since.*”

---

And then the three sentences came out of that room, and they came out of the stair and not out of any one man, and they took about four minutes to finish, and about twenty people on that stair said them.

*We did not lose the water. We sold it, and we were paid, and the water went with the land because the sale said the land and the water.*

*We are asked to carry nineteen houses in a roll we did not make, in a year we did not choose, and the money is thirty-four pounds and fourteen shillings a year and it has been the same for eleven years.*

*And the fenrow have not asked.*

The second of those is out of date by twelve shillings and eightpence a year and nobody in that room knew it. The third of them had not been true since the nineteenth day after the division. A woman of thirty-four who had been in that row since she was nine years old was sitting on the second chair and knew both.

“*Say the three of them again, and say them slowly,*” said Wenna Crale.

A man of about fifty-four came down the stair into the room, and he had a lot on the Four Hundred and had paid two pounds for it in the week of the division, and he was the second of the three against the scouring on the tenth day, and his name has been in two minutes since that day.

“*Three things, and I said all three of them in that room on the Saturday, and four of us had them in the same words then, and I am saying them again over a taproom because there are twenty people on that stair,*” said Gault Rieve.

“*Go on.*”

“*One: we did not lose the water. We sold it. We were paid. Two: we are asked now to carry nineteen houses in a roll we did not make. Three: the fenrow have not asked.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on to the end of the sentence,*” said Sefa Lund, standing, “*because you have just said in a room over a taproom, in front of about thirty people, the third line of a minute of the water sitting of this town, word for word, and you have not read it, and I have it in a book.*”

“*Say the day,*” said Ilyan.

“*The nineteenth day after the division,*” said Wenna Crale. “*Nine to nothing, and the three of them were named, and it is in the minute, and any person of this town may read the copy of it in a press in the Stone House.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The fenrow asked on the nineteenth day. It is five lines in the minute of this board and I entered it, and they asked to be charged for the water at the same rate as the households of this town, and they asked it knowing what the water is.*”

About thirty people were standing in a lane four hundred yards long in a town of four thousand people. The majority of them had come up that lane to hear whether the water belonged to a man.

They had found out instead that they had a position, and the position was that they had paid for it, and it was three sentences long and seven days old, and not one person in that room knew they were standing in it.

And a girl of nineteen in the doorway asked Ilyan Vester whether he had written the three sentences.

“*Say the question again,*” he said.

“*Whether you wrote them. The three. Because my father was the second of the three against the scouring and he has not been in a room in three weeks, and I heard them off a stair, and I have been in nine rooms in three weeks and I know the sound of a thing somebody wrote.*”

“*No,*” said Ilyan Vester.

It was true. A man of fifty-four had said them on the nineteenth day after the division in a room over a saddler’s shop, and four of the nine had them in the same words, and none of those four had ever spoken to him.

And it was the largest lie he had told in three weeks, and he told it in a room over a taproom in front of about thirty people. He understood about twenty-five days later that he had not told it to protect anybody. He told it because the answer *yes* would have made the three sentences his, and a position that belongs to one man can be argued with, and a position that belongs to a market cannot be argued with at all.

He had made it legible. He had said *a corrected roll is a corrected constituency* in a square in nine seconds on the Sunday. He had told the row in a taproom that the sale said the land and the water and that nobody had ever written a line saying it did not. He had stood in a room over a saddler’s shop two days ago and listened to a board refuse nine pounds in the words of the request that caused it.

Nobody had asked him for any of it. Every part of it was on a table, and he was the only man in that room that nobody could be sent for, and the position that twenty people on a stair had just found was going to be used against the only people in the room who had ever lost anything.

He did not say that. He had about nine seconds in which to say it, and he used them on not saying it, and there is no minute in any book in this town in which it appears that he nearly said it.

---

Nobody had asked for the sum, and a woman of thirty-eight with a bad hip did it anyway, standing at the end of the table, and it took about four minutes, and the room let her.

“*Say what you are doing,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am doing the sum of what the heel is and what the board has, because a man of thirty-four has come up here to find out whether he owns something, and you cannot find out whether you own something by asking nine people in a room, you find out by working out what it costs.*”

“*Go on and say it with the units on it.*”

“*The water rate is twopence a quarter on one thousand and sixty households of this town. That is two thousand one hundred and twenty pence a quarter, and that is eight pounds and sixteen shillings and eightpence a quarter.*”

“*Go on and say what the heel is against it.*”

“*The north heel of that frame is nine pounds, and the heel is forty pence more than the quarter the board has in its hand.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The rest of it is that there are eighty lots on the Four Hundred, and a halfpenny on each of them is forty pence, and that is the whole of the difference.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech.*”

“*The eighty lots are not short of forty pence a quarter, and I have been a person in this row for nine years, and nobody in this town has ever done that sum out loud.*”

“*Say the other half of that and say it in this room,*” said Ivo Cray.

“*The other half is that a halfpenny on eighty lots is a levy. The last levy anybody in this town knows of is three and a halfpence a lot for the scouring, and that was charged in the roll of this year.*”

“*Go on and say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*The roll of this year was made in the week the common was sold and has been corrected once, in four minutes, on a Sunday morning. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about a minute.

“*Say the rest of that,*” said Sefa Lund, “*because there are twenty people on that stair and they have come four hundred yards to hear whether the water belongs to him.*”

“*The water belongs to nobody in this room and it is charged to everybody in it, and the frame is the board’s frame and the frame is for the town, and I said that in a room fourteen days ago and nobody took it up.*”

She said the last of it with her hand flat on the table of a room over a taproom, the way a man does it and not the way a woman does it, and she took it off again.

The fever was twenty-seven weeks and no days old. About two thousand people had been in a market square in a town of four thousand because the day of the market had moved, and about thirty people were in a room and on a stair at the top of four hundred yards of mud.

A man of thirty-four had come up that lane with a bill of sale in his coat and had been told the price of the frame in pence, and he was carrying the piece of paper home with him. The water of the Hallow from the sluice downward was written on it in the same hand and the same size of writing as the five acres of lot nine.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that would let anybody in this town put the two halves of that on a table in a room and ask what they were.
