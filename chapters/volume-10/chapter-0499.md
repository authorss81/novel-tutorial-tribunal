# Chapter 499: The Fourth Day Of The Ninety-Third Week, And A Friday On Which A Request Goes Out Of The Town In A Hand That Is Not The Writer

Fourth day of the ninety-third week. His two hundred and forty-ninth morning. Ninety-nine days after the settlement. The fever thirty weeks and six days. Fifty-three days since the division. Forty-seven days in this town.

About nine people gathered in the room over a nail shop in the afternoon. The bed stood pushed to the wall. The table and chair and fire were as they had been on the forty-third day. A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for twenty-three days.

Ilyan Vester wanted a refusal of this town entered on a register of refusals. The one refusal this town has ever given was given in a lane on the eleventh day after the division by a woman of thirty-eight who keeps the sluice at the Fen and is a household of this town. The form in that lane was written for her. She had no right of refusal to give. There is no form for it. There is no witness line in a lane.

The register stands in the building four hundred miles off where a woman of twenty-eight sits eleven inches from a shelf eleven inches wide. The shelf holds a range of about nine hundred refusals.

A refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it, and the person who witnessed the one refusal this town has ever given is not findable.

Wenna Crale sat at her own table with paper before her. She is thirty-four. The town did the thing it had learned to do. It made a request, a head and two lines, in a copyist hand, at her own table, with no body behind it.

She wrote the head first, then the two lines, and read them out standing.

“*To the keepers of the register of refusals, and to nobody else,*” said Wenna Crale. “*A refusal given in a lane in this town on the eleventh day after the division is asked to be entered upon a register of refusals. The name of the person who refused it is Sefa Lund.*”

Ordwin Cape nodded from his stool. Sefa sat by the fire with her hands folded. She is the woman the form in that lane was written for. She gave no sign beyond meeting the eyes of the room.

“*Leave the witness line open,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*That is the whole of it.*”

Wenna left it open. The open witness line was the whole of the request and it was about nine words long. No name was put to witness. No hand was set to it in that room.

Ilyan took up the pen then. His mistake was the hand, again, as on the forty-third day. He wrote the fair copy in the small upright hand, which is a hand that is not the writer, and that he cannot be asked about afterwards. About four people in that room knew why and about nine did not, and the copyist was one of the four.

He folded it in four and put nothing on the outside of it. A carrier took it from the square at dusk. The town had asked for a thing nobody in Halloway can be sent for to do, and nobody in Halloway can be asked about after.

He sat late by the fire with the cost of it. He had written in her hand again, and made a thing of hers with no mark of origin. It was the only way the request could travel and be read. It was also a thing he could never answer for.

The fever was thirty weeks and six days old. A request had gone out of the town in the small upright hand. The witness line stood open.
