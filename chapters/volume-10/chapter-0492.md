# Chapter 492: The Fourth Day Of The Ninety-Second Week, And A Friday On Which A Board Of Nine Looks At The Third Thing On A List Nine Years Old And Finds The Only Thing In Front Of It That Is Cheap

Fourth day of the ninety-second week. His two hundred and forty-second morning. Ninety-two days after the settlement. The fever twenty-nine weeks and six days. Forty-six days since the division. Forty days in this town.

The room over a saddler shop held about nine men of the water board and about a dozen more on the stair and round the door. The rain had stopped by the ninth hour and left the square shining. The list of works of the board lay on the table in the steward hand, four lines long.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned for sixteen days. The wheel at the mill had been slow fifteen days completed. The roll for the autumn was made on the morrow, on the forty-seventh day after the division. The season was about three weeks off. The money for the frame was thirteen weeks off, to be collected with the water rate four times a year.

Ilyan Vester sat on the second chair where he had no vote. He wanted the morning to do something about the frame that did not cost nine pounds, and he wanted it to be his doing.

Perdy Sallow read the list out standing, the way a chair reads a thing nobody has read for years.

“*Three things stand on it,*” said Perdy Sallow. “*The scouring of the tail of the race. The frame at the head of the sluice. And a second winding bar.*”

About nine men in that room had never once read the other two out loud. One of them laughed short under his breath when he heard the second bar named. One picked up the list and set it down again as if it weighed more than paper.

“*Name where the second bar stands, and name it exact,*” said Ordwin Cape.

The steward pulled a second sheet from under the first. His hand shook a little with age and he set the paper flat before he spoke.

“*It is not a bar at the head,*” said Amos Trill. He is sixty-one and steward of the town forty years. “*It is a bar at the tail of the race. It was put on the list nine years back when the tail silted and the wheel lost two days of grist in one week.*”

A man on the stair wrote the word tail on a scrap and held it up for the man beside him to see. A shutter banged once in the wind and a boy ran to catch it.

Ilyan leaned forward. His mistake walked in ahead of him, the sentence about Kerby he had said three times by that hour, and he said it a fourth time anyway.

“*The frame is what the frame is,*” said Ilyan Vester. “*A second bar is a thing a town does so that it can go on not mending the first.*”

Ivo Cray stood on the stair with his bill of sale in his coat. He is thirty-four. He answered without raising his voice, and the room turned to hear the other half.

“*A town that will not mend the thing it cannot afford will mend the thing it can,*” said Ivo Cray. “*That is not nothing. A tail bar does not stop a frame going. It does stop a miller losing four days of grist while the frame is gone.*”

Perdy worked the figures on a slate where all could see. The chalk squeaked twice.

“*The tail bar is timber and ironwork a carpenter of this town can cut,*” said Perdy. “*It is labour and a day of carting. It is not nine pounds. Nine pounds is the county figure for a new heel, two years old, on the back of a form of query.*”

A man at the end of the table asked what the tail bar would save. A second man answered before the chair could, and gave the four days.

The board resolved before the tenth hour. It would clean the tail of the race and set a second winding bar at the tail, out of the ordinary fund, before the water rose. The frame at the head would go, because the money for it was thirteen weeks off and the season was about three weeks off. The resolution went into the minute with the day on it.

Nobody was thanked. A man gathered up the scraps of paper. The steward folded the list and put it back in order.

Ilyan walked down the stair with Ordwin and stood in the square where the sun had come out on wet stone. The cost to him was clear and it stung. He had tried to make the cheap thing sound like cowardice, and a man of thirty-four had shown it to be work. He had been right that the frame would go, and wrong about what that meant for the town.

The fever was twenty-nine weeks and six days old. The minute of the forty-sixth day carried the tail bar out of the ordinary fund, with the day on it, and the frame at the head stood outside it.
