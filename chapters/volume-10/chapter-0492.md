# Chapter 492: The Fourth Day Of The Ninety-Second Week, And A Friday On Which Nine Men Of A Water Board Read Out A List Of Works That Is Nine Years Old And Find The Only Thing Standing In Front Of Them That Is Cheap, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Sentence He Has Said Three Times And Is Given The Other Half Of It

Fourth day of the ninety-second week. His two hundred and forty-second morning. Ninety-two days after the settlement. The fever twenty-nine weeks and six days. Forty-six days since the division. Forty days in this town.

The rain had stopped by the ninth hour and left the square shining. The room over the saddler’s shop held about nine men of the water board at the table and about a dozen more on the stair and standing round the door, and the door was open because the room had one window and the window had a horse in front of it. There was a smell of wet leather and of somebody’s dinner under it.

On the table lay the list of works of the water board. It is four lines long and it is in the steward’s hand and it is nine years old, and the hand has not changed much in nine years except that the fingers are worse.

A bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned at the sixth hour or at the twelfth hour for sixteen days. The wheel at the mill had been slow fifteen days completed. The roll for the autumn was made on the morrow, on the forty-seventh day after the division, in an office over a shop, by a man of sixty-one working alone. The season was about two days off. The money for the frame was thirteen weeks off, to be collected with the water rate four times a year.

Ilyan Vester sat on the second chair, where he had a stool brought in for him and no vote. He had a bad night of it and his leg was out to one side under the table. He wanted the morning to do something about the frame that did not cost nine pounds, and he wanted it to be his doing, and both of those wants are in a room where nine men can see them.

Perdy Sallow stood to read the list, because a chair reads a thing out and does not sit down to do it.

“*There are three things on this list,*” he said, “*and I am going to say all three, and I am going to say them the way they are written, and I am not going to leave one out because it is a Friday and it is raining.*”

“*Say the other two,*” said a man at the end of the table.

“*The scouring of the tail of the race. The frame at the head of the sluice. A second winding bar.*”

He put the sheet down and squared it. About nine men in that room had never once heard two of those three said out loud in nine years. One of them laughed short under his breath at hearing the third one named. One of them picked the list up and put it down again as though it had got heavier.

“*Say the second bar again,*” said Ordwin Cape, “*and name where it stands, and name it exact, because if it is going to be built inside nine days then somebody has to know where to put the timber.*”

The steward pulled a second sheet out from under the first one. His hand shook a little and he set the paper flat on the table before he began.

“*It is not a bar at the head,*” Amos Trill said. He is sixty-one and has been steward of this town forty years. “*It is a bar at the tail of the race. It goes on the tail, above the wheel, on the frame that carries the race after it has come down a mile and a bit. It was put on the list nine years back when the tail silted, and the wheel lost two days of grist in one week, and I put it there, and it has sat on the list ever since with the other two.*”

A man on the stair wrote the word tail on a scrap and held it up for the man beside him to see. A shutter banged once in the wind across the square and a boy of about twelve ran out to catch it and got it wrong.

---

A man who had come to the back of the table and had not sat asked what the second bar at the tail would actually do, in words a person could check on a Tuesday morning. He sells wool and is not a miller, and he was in the room because his brother grinds on a Tuesday and a Friday.

“*It would not stop a frame going,*” Amos said. “*A bar at the tail is after the water. The frame at the head is the thing that holds the water up, and a bar at the tail of the race cannot hold up anything at the head. If the north heel goes before that bar is up then the bar at the tail will be standing in a river.*”

Somebody said *then what is it for*, and did not say it loudly, and about four people heard it.

“*It stops a miller losing four days of grist while the frame is gone,*” Amos Trill said. “*That is what it is for. It was on the list in the same year as the frame and nobody has ever read the list all the way through at one time, and I did not, and it is nine years.*”

The room did not make a noise. It is worth saying that it did not make a noise, because about a dozen men were on that stair and a sentence like that has ended a great many meetings in a great many towns.

---

Ilyan’s mistake came in about a quarter of an hour later and it came in ahead of him, the way his mistakes have come in since the twenty-fifth day after the division.

He had been saying a sentence about Kerby for six weeks. The office of the county surveyor is at Kerby, twenty-two miles off, and it keeps a book of returns on the sluices of the county. It has a return on the Hallow sluice made in the year before last and no other page about Halloway in the book at all. It cannot order the board to do a thing and it cannot make anybody answer. It makes a return and puts the return in a book.

About nine men in that room had heard that sentence three times by that hour. He said it a fourth time anyway.

“*The frame is what the frame is,*” Ilyan Vester said. “*A second bar is a thing a town does so that it can go on not mending the first. We will set a bar at the tail and we will get a quarter out of it. By the time the water drops again the heel will be a year gone instead of a season. And this county has an office twenty-two miles off that will make a return about it and nothing else, and that office has been the answer to this question since the first week I came here.*”

Ivo Cray was standing on the stair with his bill of sale in his coat. He had not said anything in that room for an hour and a half.

“*Say the other half of that,*” he said, and the room turned round on the stair to hear it, which is not a thing that happens often in a building this size. “*A town that will not mend the thing it cannot afford will mend the thing it can. That is not nothing, and it is not a way of putting off the other thing, and I will tell you the difference between those two things because a man who owns five acres is the wrong man to tell you and I am going to tell you anyway.*”

He came down two steps and stopped.

“*A frame at the head is nine pounds. The money is thirteen weeks off and the season is two days.*”

Somebody at the back said the word *season* as though it were a question, and Perdy Sallow wrote it on the slate and left it standing there.

“*If this board does nothing at all inside nine days then nothing at all happens inside nine days, and the frame goes on the Sunday, and everybody in this room will be able to say, and will say, that we looked at it.*”

He came down two steps and stopped, and turned round so that he was facing the stair and not the table.

“*A tail bar costs timber this town can cut and ironwork a carpenter here can set. It is labour and a day of carting, and it saves four days of grist a week at a mill that grinds for forty-one kitchens. Those are two different facts. The second one is true and the first one is also true, and a board that only ever hears the first one is a board that will be sitting in a room on the Monday after the frame has gone.*”

---

The working of it was done on a slate where all could see it, and Perdy Sallow wrote the figures himself and wiped the slate between each line, and the chalk squeaked, and the room watched a chair of forty-seven do arithmetic in public for the first time in nine years.

“*Timber out of the ordinary fund. Ironwork. One day of carting from the pit at the fourth ward.*” He wrote the three under one another and left a space. “*It is not nine pounds. Nine pounds is the county figure for a new heel of the north side, two years old, on the back of a form of query, and it is in a book twenty-two miles off that this board cannot make anybody enter a line in.*”

A man near the hearth asked how much of it there was, and was told that the chair of the board did not have the figure in front of him and was not going to give one out of his head in a room with the door open, and was asked to say it in terms of the fund instead.

Perdy wiped the slate and wrote the two figures on it and read them out.

Somebody wrote the two figures on the back of their hand. A second man wrote them on the same hand and got the second one wrong, and the man who had written the first one looked at it, and did not say anything, and went on holding his hand out at an angle to look at it.

A voice from the stair observed that this meant the frame was moneyed. It is not, in any sense a person in that room could act on inside a quarter: the frame is moneyed in a year, and the water does not wait for a collection, and the season is two days off.

Perdy said that he wanted both figures in the minute as two figures about two different things, and not as one.

---

The board resolved before the tenth hour. It would scour the tail of the race and set a second winding bar at the tail out of the ordinary fund, and it would be done before the water came up, and it would be done by a carpenter of this town with the carting done at the fourth ward.

The frame at the head of the sluice was not on the resolution. It was outside it. Nobody moved it out of the list and nobody struck it off the list, and the list of works is still four lines long and still in the press against a west wall, and the frame is still the third thing on it.

The resolution went into the minute with the day on it and the steward’s hand at the foot of it, in the form he has used three times in five weeks. He did not ask the board for a penny to do it. The form has never cost anything and there is no money.

Nobody was thanked. A man gathered up the scraps of paper and squared them against the edge of the table before he left. A man on the stair went out first and said something to the square that made two people laugh. The steward folded the list and put it back inside the second minute book, and the second minute book went into the leather bag with the strap, and the strap was not fastened when he picked it up and he fastened it himself.

---

Ilyan came down the stair beside Ordwin Cape and out into the square, where the sun had come out on wet stone and the whole of the front of the mill was standing in it with the water lying black at the top of the race.

The cost to him was plain and it stung. He had tried to make the cheap thing sound like cowardice because it was cheaper than the thing he wanted, and a man who owns five acres had stood on a stair and shown it to be work. He had been right that the frame would go. He had been wrong about what that meant for the town, and being wrong about it in a room of nine men is not the same as being wrong about it in a lane, because in a room it goes into a minute and in a lane it goes nowhere at all.

He had wanted the morning to be his doing, and the morning had done a thing, and the thing would be finished in about nine days by a carpenter and two men with a bar, and no part of it would be his.

A shutter banged across the square and nobody went to catch it this time.

The fever was twenty-nine weeks and six days old. The minute of the forty-sixth day carried a bar at the tail of the race out of the ordinary fund, with the day on it and a hand at the foot of it. The frame at the head of the sluice stood outside it, on a list nine years old that has not been read all the way through since.
