# Chapter 247: What The Water Did At A Frame In A Year Nobody Was Watching

The first of the four lines came the last four miles on the sixth day of the fifty-sixth week and got itself said out loud in a lane, and it is the only one of the four that anybody in this basin can do anything with, and nobody knew that on the day it was printed.

The first of the four is a return of the water in the Sallow at the frame at Hollow Sallow, taken in the eleventh year, made for the chamber's own use, and there is a figure in it.

It had been on a wall in a market town for two days. A sheet of paper in a clerk's hand, four lines, a penny, and about four hundred and ten people in this valley could not get to it.

---

They read it in a lane at the closing of the water on the sixth of that week, and about forty people stood in the road, and the man who read it was thirty-four and had four people and a house at the bottom of that lane and cannot read a word.

He said so before he started, which he has not done before.

“I cannot read this,” he said. “I have never been able to read it. Nobody has ever been able to read it. I am going to say it out loud and if I get a word wrong you are all going to know exactly which word and I am not going to pretend otherwise.”

That is the whole of why the four lines of a chamber's index came down to a lane in a valley, and there is no other reason, and no clerk came with it, and the man who read it had got the paper out of a room four miles up a hill off the wall and had it in his hand and could not follow one word of the writing, and about forty people in a road found out what a chamber of plenty had thought about their water in a year before any of them was born.

---

Here is the first of the four, as it stands on a sheet in a clerk's hand.

A return of the water in the Sallow at the frame at Hollow Sallow, taken in the eleventh year, made for the chamber's own use. The water stood at two feet and four inches above the sill of the frame on three days together. The mark by which it is known is on the inside of the north post of the frame, and it is a dark line, and it is old, and there is nobody living in this valley who put a rule to it.

That is the whole of it. It is two sentences and a figure and it took about four minutes to read out in a road with forty people in it, and at the end of the fourth minute nobody in that lane said anything at all for a while, and what they were all doing was the same arithmetic at the same time without saying it out loud.

Two feet and four inches.

The frame's sill is nine feet above the Anner's sill. Nine feet is a hundred and eight inches and two feet four inches is twenty-eight inches, and a hundred and eight and twenty-eight is a hundred and thirty-six inches, and a hundred and thirty-six inches is eleven feet and four inches.

So the highest the water has ever stood at that frame is eleven feet and four inches above the sill of the Anner's own dam, and the line in the bond is eleven feet above that same sill, and the water stood four inches above the line in the bond on three days together in the eleventh year, and this valley has spent a month with one figure of eleven feet in it and three sills under it, and nobody in that lane had ever been told what the water did.

---

A bench of plenty made a ruling nine years ago on the petition of a grocer at Coldbeck, and the ruling says two things and both of them are on a wall in a market town, and the second of them is that no order may name a height without naming the sill it is taken from.

That is the whole of what the difference between those two things is about, and it took a man of thirty-four who cannot read a paper to arrive at it in a road, and he did it out loud, and he did it in a way that nobody in that lane could have stopped.

“This is a height,” he said. “Two foot four. And it is a height above the sill of this frame, and the sill is named, and the frame is the frame. And eleven foot is a height above the sill of the Anner's own dam, and that sill is named too, and the dam is the dam. And they are both heights and both of them are true and there is not one inch of ground between this gate and the Anner's own dam, and a man with a rule could walk it in an afternoon and not one of us in this lane has ever done it.”

“And that is what the nine-year-old one says,” he said. “Do not put a number down without saying what you are measuring it from. And we have had a month of a number. Eleven foot. And nobody in this valley ever asked what it was from, and it was the Anner's own sill, and everybody knew it, and everybody said the number anyway, and the number went into a lane and into a paper and into a room, and it meant two foot above this gate.”

He put the sheet down against the wall of the house behind him because his hands had gone.

“Nobody in that room has ever stood at this frame,” he said. “Not one of them. And a woman has stood at it every day for eleven years.”

---

Ilma Treen was at the back of that lane, and she had been at the back of it since about a quarter of an hour before it started, and she is thirty-eight and she keeps the sluice and she opens the gate at the closing of the water and has done for eleven years, and about four people in that lane moved aside for her when the sheet came out and about four did not notice her at all.

She had wanted that number for six of the eleven years. She said so on the seventh day of the fifty-fourth week, standing at her own gate with a barrow handle under her arm, and she has not asked anybody for it since and she was not going to ask anybody for it today, and it came down a lane in a road on the sixth of this week because a man of thirty-four said he could not read it and did it anyway.

Here is what she did with it.

“Two foot four,” she said. “That is the top of the second step at the sluice. I put that step in myself in the fourth year because the first one was worn out and I was thirty-one and nobody was going to come. And I have stood on it in the dark twice a winter for eleven years thinking it was a step.”

She looked at the frame from the top of the lane. It is four hundred yards and it is dark and you cannot see a dark line on the north post of it from where any of them were standing.

“And I have been raising that gate eleven years,” she said, “and I have never once known how much room I had under it.”

Nobody in that lane cheered and nobody clapped and about four people said nothing at all, and the reason nobody cheered is not that the news was bad. It is that a woman was standing in a road at the closing of the water being told what her own gate had been doing in a year when nobody was watching, and there is not a thing in four hundred and ten people that is worse to be told about.

“You are all going to want me to say something about what it means,” she said. “I have wanted that number for six years so I know exactly what it feels like to be in this lane tonight. And I am not going to say a word about what it means, and here is why, and it took me about a minute to get to it. It is a return of a river. It is not a height. Somebody stood here in the eleventh year with a rule and put it to a mark on a post and wrote down what the mark said and sent it nine days to a city to be kept. That is all that happened. It is not an order and nobody had to do anything and there is nobody in it but me and a rule and a wet post.”

“A height is a thing you decide to be above,” she said. “And a return of a river is a thing that already happened. And this valley has spent a month arguing about a height, and about four of us have just found out what the water does, and the two of those are not the same kind of thing and I do not know anybody in this lane who could have told you that on the sixth of last week.”

---

A man of thirty-one took a penny exercise book with two halves in it out from under a board with a stone on it in a cart shed behind two houses, at about the seventh hour, and wrote at the head of a fresh page that it is the hundred and ninety-fifth day since the second shaking, which is the seventeenth figure of that kind he has put at the head of a page in this valley, and it is a count of figures and not a count of anything else.

The second line under it is the reason, and the reason is that the return says the eleventh year and the eighth year and the third turn, and a turn cannot be placed in this basin by anybody living, and there is no conversion between a salting and a day, and therefore the only date this valley can put on a figure written down in the eleventh year is the morning it was said out loud in a road at the closing of the water, and he did not put that morning on it either, because a man may put a day on his own act and may not put one on another person's speech.

What he wrote under that, in his own hand, and it is a short line, is the whole of what he has for two nights' work: that a chamber of plenty has had a figure about this valley's water since the eleventh year and that nobody in this valley has ever seen it, and that a woman of thirty-eight has wanted it for six of eleven years, and that the mark is on the north post of a frame and is still there.

---

And then the woman of thirty-eight went down to the frame, because it is four hundred yards and she was going that way anyway, and she took a rule with her because there is a rule in a cart shed at Hollow Sallow and it is the same one the man with the rule at the Anner has been carrying since the fifty-second week.

She put the rule to the mark on the north post of the frame at about the eighth hour with four people standing in the dark behind her and a lamp, and it is two feet and four inches, and it is what a sheet in a market town said it was, and a valley of about four hundred and ten people has a figure of its own water in its own hand on its own frame, and the water is four feet and something in the bed and falling and the gate was shut an hour ago and she had shut it herself.

She wrote the day on the post in chalk, the same day a girl of seventeen had written at the top of a fresh page that afternoon, and she wrote the number under it, and she did not write the eleventh year, because there is no conversion and she is the last person in this basin who would try to make one in chalk on a post.
