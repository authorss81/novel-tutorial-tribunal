# Chapter 227: Six Lines, And Eleven Hands

The paper was done on the seventh day of the fifty-third week and it took the length of a working day, and the day was not in a room.

There is a thing that happens in a valley like this one and it happened on the seventh day: eleven people were asked for a sentence, one at a time, in eleven different places, and every one of them was asked by somebody else and not by the man who had drafted the paper, and not one of them was asked in the Seat's one room, and the four of them who could be found in one place did not sign in front of each other.

What is on the wall of the Seat's one room this afternoon is one sheet and it is in eleven hands, and each hand is a different hand, and the clerk of the Seat has not copied it and did not copy it and said in the room that a sheet in eleven hands is a sheet that can be read by anybody who can read and that is the first time in nine years anything in that room has been able to say that about itself.

The copy that goes down the hill to Hollow Sallow is in one hand, because a copy has to be in one hand, and it is the clerk's, and it is a copy of eleven hands.

The seven lines of the draft had become six, and the line that was struck on the fifth day of that week was still struck on the seventh, and there are three hands on that line and they do not agree, and the sheet says so at the foot of it in a sentence of four and twenty words that a clerk of forty-four wrote at somebody else's request and read out twice.

The first hand on it is a man of thirty-four's and it is the strike, and under the strike, in the same hand, is the reason he gave, which is that a roof is a thing you do to a house and a house is not a thing you do.

The second hand is a woman of about thirty-one with two children and a house at the ninth door, and she wrote the sentence she said in a lane on the second day of that week and had it written down in her own hand at the request of a woman of about forty, and it is the only hand on the sheet that argues for something, and it is under the strike, and it is not struck.

The third is the clerk's, and it is the sentence about the two of them not agreeing.

The six lines are work, and days, and a wage, and a penny, and a payer, and a gate — and about four feet of the sheet is the struck line and its three hands, and the seven lines that were drafted on the second day are the six that are on the wall and one struck, and the difference between those two numbers is a man of thirty-one and about nine sentences he did not say.

---

He was in the lane at the second light of that day because the lane is where a person of thirty-four was, and not in the room, and he was not asked to be in the room and he was not asked to leave it either, because nobody in that basin has authority to ask a man from four hundred miles off to do anything, including go away.

The second refusal happened at about the fourth hour of the seventh day of that week, in a lane, in front of about eleven people, and it was about nine words long, and it was the second time this basin had said no to something he wanted and it was the first time it had said no to his own name.

“You put it there because nothing can be asked of a paper with no name on it,” said the woman of about forty, standing in the road with the sheet in her hand and not opening it. “I have watched you work that out in your own head for four days and I have watched you not say it out loud because you thought nobody here would follow it, and I have followed it, and I am not going to say it to your face in a room with a clerk in it. I am going to say it here.”

“Yes,” said Ilyan Vester.

“A paper with your name at the foot of it is the reason nobody came, in a year, when a woman at the eleventh house says a number went out of that lane and the roofs are not on nineteen houses. I have been in that room nine years and I have watched two things get taken out of a room in that town and neither of them was taken out by a thief, and one of them was a number and one of it was a name, and a number can be asked for again and a name cannot.”

“I agree with you,” he said. “Take it out.”

Nobody in that lane made a sound of any kind and the river was going by at the bottom of it.

“Then say the rest of it,” said the woman of about forty, “because a man who takes his name off a paper in a lane in eleven seconds has not been asked anything hard, and there is somebody standing in this lane who lost a house and I am not going to have this be easy.”

He took about as long as it takes to grind ink. Not because he was composing it. Because there were nine people in that lane and every one of them could contradict him and one of them was owed ten pounds sixteen and had been paid it in an envelope four days earlier and had not thanked anybody.

“I wanted it there,” he said. “A paper with no name on it cannot be asked anybody anything, and I have spent a long time learning that a rule with no person in it is carried by whoever is nearest with hands on it, and on the sixth day of this month nineteen houses were under two feet of water because four true lines named a dam and a height and a day and not a person. I put my name at the foot of that paper because I thought if anything came of it there would be somebody to come back to. That is the whole of the reason and it is a bad one and I would like it written down that a man of thirty-one said it in a lane in front of eleven people and was not asked for a reason, because I know what this basin is like about reasons.”

Nobody asked him for a reason. Not one of the nine asked him for a reason, and about thirty people alive in this valley have watched a man explain a thing he found once before into a room that had no use for it, and every one of them was in that lane, and not one of them asked him for one more sentence.

“It is off,” said the woman of about forty. “And I am right about it and I have been right about it once before, in this lane, about a sheet that is under a board in a cart shed, and neither of us is going to say anything about that today.”

The space at the foot of the paper was not filled. That is on the record. There is a place at the foot of the sheet in the Seat's one room where three words were in a man's own hand for four days and are not there now, and nothing is in the place, and nobody has put a dash there, and a man of forty-four said out loud that he was not going to put a dash there because a dash is a thing people put where a name used to be so that a later person thinks nothing was ever there.

---

Then the thing came up, in the flat white nothing that has put things in front of him in about a year and a half, on a bank above a lane with about nine people standing in it, and it did not say one word about the paper.

**THE SIX LINES NAME SIX THINGS AND THE ELEVEN HANDS ARE ON THEM, AND THE PART OF THE SHEET NOBODY WILL AGREE ON IS THE PART THAT SAYS WHICH HOUSES ARE UNDER THE LINE.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A HOUSE PUT UP AFTER A LINE IS DRAWN IS INSIDE THE LINE, OR IN FRONT OF IT, AND WHETHER ANYONE IN THIS BASIN WILL EVER BE THE ONE TO SAY.**

He read it twice and it took nothing out of him and it answered nothing, and the matter it was about had been made four hours earlier by a man of thirty-four in a room, and nobody in that lane saw anything, and his leg was what his leg has been, and nobody in that lane has ever been told a word about any of it.

---

The count of the hands was not said out loud as they went on and it has to be said, because there are about four hundred and ten people in that valley and every one of them has heard the word *eleven* said about this basin so many times in the last month that a clerk of forty-four stood up in his own room at about the sixth hour of the seventh of that week and said the following without being asked by anybody.

“There are eleven hands on that sheet,” said Hollis Nance, “and I have counted them twice and I am not going to count them a third time in a room. And I want it said, because I have watched six people in this basin go quiet today, that this is not the eleven houses and it is not the eleven marks and it is not the eleven feet and it is not the eleven seats in this room and it is not the eleven men at the pit. It is eleven people who were each asked for one sentence. If it were the same eleven it would mean something and it does not mean the same eleven, and I have been in this room nine years and I have never once been able to say that about anything.”

Nobody in that room had ever heard him say a sentence of that shape about anything and about four of them wrote it down in their own books and about six did not.

And the six lines were on the wall of the Seat's one room at the closing of the water on the seventh day of that week beside the four pages, which were still there, and which are still correct on their face, and the copy went down the hill in the hands of a girl of seventeen who was asked to carry it and said yes before she was asked twice, and the paper she was carrying had one hand on it and eleven people had put their hands to the original and one of them had said out loud that he wanted to be asked again in a year in a line he wrote himself in ochre dust, and it is going to be read out in a lane at the closing of the water and it is not going to be read out by anybody in Sallowby.
