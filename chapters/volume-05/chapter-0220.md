# Chapter 220: A Person Doing Their Work

The Sallow at Hollow Sallow was a foot and a bit in the bed on the seventh and last day of the fifty-second week, which is where it had been on the fourth day of the fifty-first, and the lane was out of it, and the mud was in all of it.

There is a difference between water and mud and everybody in that village had been on the correct side of it for about six days. The water goes down a bed that has a shape in it. The mud does not. The mud is in the doors and the mud is under the boards and the mud is three inches deep in the fourth house and it is on everything anybody owns, and it is not going anywhere, and the sun came out on the sixth day and made a crust on the top of it, and underneath the crust it is still mud.

Eight of the nineteen houses were still under water. The eleven on the upper half of the lane were out of it and were full of mud, and the eight on the lower half were under about a foot and a half, and the third house from the top, which is the highest of the eleven, was the driest of the lot, and the woman who lives in it had been up and out of it since the second hour of the fourth night with a barrow.

Nobody in the Red Basin was redeemed. The order was on the wall in the Seat's one room and it was on the wall on the doorpost of the third house from the top and it was correct on its face and there was no remedy on it and there was not going to be one this week, and there is no line in it about a roof or a floor or a bed or a door, and the mine was a mine, and about eleven hundred tons a year was going to go down a road that adds a morning and a half each way, and the road was shut, and the Anner was carrying it.

---

The letter came up the Shelf road on the seventh day with a man who was going to Coldbeck for a horse, and it was addressed to the water-seat at Sallowby in that order, which is now the third thing in this basin that has come addressed in that order, and it was in the room for about an hour before anybody opened it.

Hollis Nance opened it in front of six people, and he said the thing before he did it, which is a thing he does, and it was the third time in eleven days.

“I have no authority to open this and I am going to open it, and I have said that sentence three times in eleven days now and it has not got any shorter, and I would like it noticed that the reason it has not got shorter is that nothing in this valley has changed to make it shorter.”

The letter is two paragraphs and it is signed by a clerk of the chamber of plenty and the clerk is not named and did his job properly.

The Bench is seized of the matter, and will consider it in its own time, and no time is given, and the second paragraph says that the Bench is aware the Seat is competent upon any matter of water, ground and way in the basin and does not require the seal of the chamber or the journey.

“That is nine years old,” said Hollis Nance. “That is the first of the three, word for word, and they have printed it back to me on a sheet that took a man a fortnight to write, and they have been seized of this matter for about nine years already, and it has taken ten pounds and twelve to be told so this morning.”

“It came back in nine days,” said Ewan Troke. “It is the first thing anybody in this valley has ever had back out of that city in less than a season and it has come back properly written and it has cost what it said it would cost, and the man who wrote it is somebody's clerk and he did it well, and I want that said in a room and not only in a book.”

“Ten pounds and twelve,” said Ilyan Vester. “Two of them. A pound for entry and four for a day and nine days’ board at eightpence is one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, and two of those is two thousand five hundred and forty-four, and that is ten pounds and a hundred and forty-four pence, and a hundred and forty-four pence is twelve shillings and nothing over. Five of that was mine. Seven was the clerk of this Seat out of his own money, out of a column that does not exist, and neither of us is going to be paid for it and neither of us is going to be thanked for it and I have said all three of those things out loud because this is a room where things get said out loud.”

Nobody thanked anybody. That is not a finding and it is not a complaint, and about a hundred and seventy-one pounds of flood sat outside four walls in a market town with no document attached to it and nobody in that room thought it worth mentioning twice.

---

The day went on the page at the third light and the number was one hundred and sixty-eight, and it was the ninth time in seventeen days that a number out of that water-book had been put at the top of a page in this valley, and nine of the nine were out of the same book and the same count and none of them is a day of a grain year and none of them converts.

He said it out loud at the noon light, standing in the road at the top of the bank with about nine people on it, because he had started saying it and there was no reason to stop, and then he read back the rest of that side of the penny exercise book to the people standing on the bank, out loud, which he has not done in another county and had not planned to do here.

“The notice of four sheets, five shillings and fourpence, never paid, and it is not going to be paid by me and it is not going to be forgiven. The claim of a carter of fifty-one, with a boy, eleven miles off, forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence, not producible, no form for asking, and he is right. A dead man in the fourth line of a list of two hundred and forty whose wife is on the fourth line and knows she is on the fourth line. The minute of the first of the forty-eighth week, one sheet, the only copy, in a drawer four miles off with nobody's name on it. The box of about nine hundred and forty forms, six miles off, not found, and not going to be asked for this month. Nesh Arrad, thirty-nine, and the clock on her side of this is running, and I am not printing the figure here because the figure is a figure of the other side of this book.”

He stopped there, and a man of about forty on the bank said, “There is one more.”

“There is one more,” he said, “and I am not going to say it.”

That was the whole of it. He had read out nine things and stopped at the tenth and about nine people heard him stop and none of them asked him what the tenth was, and none of them asked him which year it was, and he is not relieved of the not being asked, and nobody in this valley has ever asked him and there is nobody here who is going to.

“The other side's year turns in about forty weeks,” he said, “and I am not printing the figure, because it is a figure of the other side of this book, and when it turns then four hundred and eighty-five pounds falls due on an account of nine thousand seven hundred, and the district of Ryefold is at nothing and has been since the sixth day of the forty-third week, and it does not stop, and it is not a Red Basin figure and it is not to be converted into one, and there is no column in this basin for it and there never will be and I have stopped hoping for one.”

---

They spent the last two hours of the seventh and last day of that week doing four ordinary things, and none of the four was a decision, and the week ended at the closing of the water.

The four men and the barrow went down to the dam at the second light and the clay went on in the same order it has gone on in for eleven years and eleven feet was to the sill and the Sallow was falling and the wet workings of the Anner were dry for the ninth day.

A joiner of forty-one took his rule and his plane and went down to the sluice and did not measure the frame, and said, to nobody, that he was not going to measure it again until there was a fund, and that the lath with five pounds eighteen on it was still in the frame and would stay in the frame for as long as the frame stood.

A woman of thirty-eight came up out of the Sallow at the closing of the water and put the barrow down first, and then opened the gate, which is what she does, and the gate did the only thing it has ever been able to do.

And in a room in Sallowby with a table down the middle of it, a clerk of the Seat of forty-four washed the pen and put it upside down on a board and turned it over, because it was the end of a week and it is not a week of the Shelf and it is not a week of anything, and then he took a chair and put it back at the table and squared the four pages on the wall, which were crooked, and did not take them down.

And about four miles below that, in a cart shed behind two houses, a man of thirty-one shut a penny exercise book with two halves in it and put it under a board with a stone on it, and the two halves do not have to be read together and are not to be read together, and on the basin side of it there are nine day-counts and a sentence about a number that was somebody else's and a line about a mill hand's handwriting on a leaf and a nine-word sentence about a height that is the dam's number and not the lane's, and on the other side of it there is a sentence that is broken off in the middle and is the only one of its kind in a book that has been kept for a year, and there is no page anywhere with the rest of it on it.

Nothing came up in the flat white nothing. It had put two things in front of him in seventeen days in this valley and it had said nothing whatever about a dam, or nineteen houses, or a rule that named a body and not a person, or a letter of two paragraphs from a city of four hundred thousand people, and it did not say it once, and there is no column in the index of any of it for an answer, and nobody in that cart shed was in a position to notice one way or the other, and the position under the Red Basin is four hundred and six feet below a sill stone and more than that by a quantity this basin has no instrument for naming, and the water at the low end of the pit has been under to the roof since before a woman of twenty-six was seventeen, and nobody went down, and nobody was going to, and the chamber at Orison is seized of the matter and will consider it in its own time.

And the gate was opened at the closing of the water, and the chair was put back at the table, and the four men went down to the dam at the second light, and it was the seventh and last day of the fifty-second week, and there was about a hundred and seventy-one pounds of flood under the lane and nineteen houses that nobody had asked and a gate that had been standing open since before the rain came, and the order was on the wall, and the mud was not down.
