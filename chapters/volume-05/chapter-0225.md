# Chapter 225: A Man Who Lost A House Is Not A House

Nobody asked Coll Ferren anything for eight days after the counting, and that is not a figure of speech, and the eight is eight: the counting was done at the first light on the fourth day of last week and this is the fifth day of this one. He was the fifth of the nineteen. He had answered a woman of about forty in his own doorway about his own house, and nobody has asked him a second question since, and then he had walked every door in the bottom half of that lane twice, at his own cost, out of a week he is not paid for, and after the second walk nobody in Hollow Sallow said one word to him about anything for four days.

He was the one the whole paper was built on and he was the one nobody went back to, and the reason is about as long as this and it is the whole reason anybody would give: everybody in that lane had a question about the nineteen and every one of the nineteen was a person, and a person with a question about them is a person who has to go and stand in somebody's doorway, and there is no form in this basin for standing in a doorway.

They asked him the wrong question on the fifth day of that week, and they asked it in a lane and not in a room, and about four people heard it.

It came from the under-manager, who had come up the hill for the third time in four days and had come for the timber, and who asked it in the ordinary way a man asks a thing he is about to pay for.

“I want a list,” said Jory Vellan. “Nineteen doors, in order, from the bottom of the lane up. Nineteen doors and a number against each one and a mark beside the number, and then the man who does the roofs works down it and nobody in this lane can stand in front of him and tell him he has done a roof he was not paid for. I have run a pit for nineteen years on a list of stops and a list of stops is the only reason anybody is still alive in it.”

Coll Ferren said no.

He said it in about four seconds and he said it in the lane and he did not explain it and about nine people were in that lane, and then he explained it, because he is not a man who enjoys a silence.

“If I am on that paper I am a thing on it,” he said. “And a thing on a paper is a thing that gets looked at, and I have not got anything that needs looking at. You put my number against that door and in four years there will be a man in this lane who says the number is wrong, and he will not be talking about the water. He will be talking about the number, because that is what a number is for. And I have been in that house since I was nine and it is gone, and a gone house is not a thing that is under anything.”

Nobody in that lane said the thing that was true, and the thing that was true is that he was the only one of the nineteen whose answer could not be read off a wall, because the other eighteen still had a door.

---

The question that was never asked him is the one a room four miles off had already answered, in a document, in nine words, on the sixth day of last week, and nobody had connected the two, and the reason nobody connected them is that nobody in that lane had been in the cart shed.

He said it himself, two days later, to a man of thirty-one who had come down to the bottom of the lane to look at a wall, and he said it without being asked and then he stopped, and the man of thirty-one did not ask him anything else about it, and did not write it down, and about four days later the whole of what he had said was on a piece of paper in eleven hands and the man of thirty-one was not one of the eleven.

“You are going to write it down,” said Coll Ferren.

“No,” said Ilyan Vester. “I am not a party to it and I have said so in four rooms this month and I am not going to unsay it in a lane. If I put my hand on that piece of paper then it is a piece of paper about a man from four hundred miles off, and you have already told me what happens to a document that gets a stranger's name at the bottom of it.”

“You have put your name at the bottom of the other one,” said Coll Ferren.

He had. He had written it himself in his own hand on the second day of that week, on a sheet in the Seat's one room, at the foot of seven lines, under three words, and nobody had asked him to and nobody had stopped him, and he had put it there because a piece of paper with no name at the foot of it is a piece of paper that cannot be asked anybody anything, and he has spent four volumes learning that a rule with no person in it is carried by the nearest body with hands, and the nearest body with hands at that table was him.

He said so, out loud, in a lane, in about four minutes, and it is the only time anybody in this basin has heard a man of thirty-one say anything about himself that was a defence, and he did not excuse it and he did not explain why he had done it and he did not take it back.

Then Coll Ferren said the sentence that changed the shape of the paper, and he said it to a lane and not to a room, and it is eighteen words and it is his and nobody in this basin has said anything like it in nine years.

“A paper with a man's name on it is read out in a year when he is gone.”

Nobody asked him what he meant and about six people worked it out in their own time and it took the rest of the day.

---

The paper changed at about the fifth hour of that week and it changed in a room with eleven seats in it and about fourteen people in, and it took about two hours, and the only thing anybody can be shown doing in it is a man of thirty-four in the middle of a table saying that the houses are coming off.

Not the roofs. The houses. The nineteen doors, the nineteen numbers, the nineteen marks, and the four words a clerk of forty-four had written on a sheet in a fair hand on the second day of that week, which were the same four words that had been on that table since the second hour of the second day without anybody in Sallowby or in that lane having said them out loud, and nobody in that room could ask the question and everybody had understood it since the fourth hour of the second day.

“They are coming off,” he said, “and the roofs are staying on, and I want it written down that those are two different operations and that I know it. A roof is a thing you do to a house. A house is not a thing you do. And the day that lane has nineteen houses on a piece of paper is the day the twentieth house is a house that is not on a piece of paper, and there is a man in this valley who will be alive then and it will not be me.”

“Why not you,” said the clerk.

“I will be forty-one,” said Coll Ferren. “And this is not a well in a valley, it is a lane with a river at the bottom of it.”

---

The four lines that replace them were put in a different hand from the rest of the sheet and they are the whole of what a man of thirty-four said, and they took about four minutes to arrive at and about two hours to be agreed to, and the clerk of the Seat copied them out in a fair hand and read them back and then read them back a second time and the second reading did not change a word, which is the third time that has happened in a room in Sallowby in about a month and the clerk noted the date.

And on the fifth of that week a man of thirty-one with a book in his coat wrote a day at the top of a page in a cart shed, one hundred and seventy-three days since the second shaking, and it is the first time the number that has been in his own book since the day he came to this valley has gone on a piece of paper that eleven other people wrote, and he did it because the paper names days and there is no other kind of day to name one with, and he wrote it in his own hand because a man of sixty-one who keeps a board of notches and has kept it for thirty years was asked to date the paper and refused in about nine words.

The man of sixty-one said that his count is a count of dryings and the paper is about water, and that a paper about water dated in a count of dryings is the two rows that do not meet, and that he has watched that go wrong twice in thirty years and once of them was his own mother's house and he is not going to be the second.

He put his hand to the paper anyway. He said afterward that the two were different acts and that if anybody in that room could not tell the difference between a hand and a date then the room was in the wrong trade, and about nine people in it were not.

At the closing of the water on the fifth of that week a man of thirty-four went down to a house at the bottom of a lane that has a door on its hinge and no roof on it yet, and carried a bed up out of a cart shed four miles up the hill, and put it in the bottom room, and did not put it in the top room, and there is no form in this basin for saying which of those two is the right decision and he made it in about four seconds.
