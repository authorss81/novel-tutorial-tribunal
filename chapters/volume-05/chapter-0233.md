# Chapter 233: The Word That Means Not Sent

The man with the bag came up the high road on the sixth day of the fifty-fourth week, the way he does, and he is not a clerk and he is not an advocate and he is a man with a bag, and he takes what is in the gap in the wall of the Seat of Sallowby and he is paid at the Seat of Orison and not by this valley, and he has never once told anybody here what he has taken and nobody here has ever once asked him.

Hollis Nance weighed it in the hand, which is a thing he does with anything that arrives at that seat, and it was heavier than the last one, and he said the number out loud and it was eleven pounds and four ounces, and about seven people were in the room by then because it is market eve and the room is where people come.

“I have no authority to open this,” he said. “I am a clerk of a Seat. This is addressed to a chamber nine days away, and the chamber does not know there is a Seat, and there is nothing on the outside of it that says I may, and I am going to open it anyway, and if that is a crime in Orison it is a crime in Orison and not in this room, and I would like every one of you to have heard me say that, and I would like nobody to repeat it back to me afterwards.”

The sentence has not got any shorter. It was said on the second day of the fifty-first week and it was said on the fourth day of that week and it has now been said three times and it is the same length all three times and he has not made it shorter by being in a hurry, because being in a hurry is what he has instead.

Nobody repeated it back to him afterwards.

There were nine printed sheets, folded in eight, at threepence the sheet, and the Seat of Sallowby is sent sixty-six at a time twice in the grain year and has paid for every one of them without once being asked to, and eight of the nine sheets on this morning were the ordinary list of what the chamber has decided, and the ninth was the chamber's own index.

That is a different thing. The list of decisions comes twice a year. The index comes once a year, at the end of the winter, printed at Orison for the use of every Seat in nine districts, and every one of them keeps a hand-copy of it, and the hand-copy of this one is about two inches thick and is on the shelf under the window and has been in the keeping of one man since before the water.

---

He put the printed index on the table and did not open it, and then he went and got his own book off the shelf, and the two of them lay side by side on a table nine feet by fourteen in a room with eleven seats in it, and the fire in the hearth has not been lit since before the first shaking.

“Right,” he said. “Let anybody in this room stop me.”

Nobody stopped him. He opened his own book at the back and turned the last page round and put his finger on a heading and did not say anything for about as long as it takes to get a barrow back in a shed.

“They printed the same four columns we have,” he said. “It is the same page. It is the same rule. The same four hundred and sixty entries in the three, going back to the first shaking in a small hard hand that gets worse as it comes forward. Same paper behind them. I have been copying that page once a year since I came and I have never once copied a word out of the fourth.”

---

> THE BACK OF THE LAST PAGE OF THE SEAT'S OWN HAND-BOOK, TWO INCHES THICK, KEPT UP TO DATE BY ONE CLERK ALONE, OPEN AT THAT PAGE AND HELD OPEN BY ONE FINGER: the heading of the fourth column is printed and not written, and it is two words. The three columns beside it have about four hundred and sixty entries in them between them. One rule runs under all four columns. Under the fourth there is nothing at all — not a line, not a figure, not a date, no ink of a different colour, and the paper under it is the same paper as the paper under the other three.
>
> THE HEADING IS NOT SENT.

“There,” said Hollis Nance. “Two words. And the second of them is the ordinary word that a clerk writes at the head of a column of things that did not go, and it has been printed at the head of that column for longer than I have been in this room, and I have had my eye on it every working day since I came, because it is the last page and you cannot tell a finished book from an unfinished one without it.”

He said it in an ordinary voice, the way he says a price, and he said it to a book, and about four of the people in that room had heard those two words before in their lives and about five had not.

Nobody asked him about it.

That is on the record and it has to be said plainly, because nine people were in that room by the time he had read the four lines and a man of thirty-one was standing at the fourth light with his hands at his sides and a bag on the table, and the question of what is under a column headed *not sent* was about eleven seconds long in the middle of the room and everybody in it heard it and not one of them put a word to it.

Not Hollis Nance, who has said out loud in his own room that he has never asked about that column and never is going to, and who said it again on the fifth day of the fifty-second week and added on the sixth of the fifty-fourth week that he had been saying it for nine days and it had not got any shorter, and that the eleven days he had mentioned had been about four days wrong and that nobody had noticed the four days and that he was not going to explain the arithmetic to a room that has better things to do with.

Not the recordwright of twenty-eight, who was at the back of it with a satchel and has not put a hand to a document in this basin and is not going to, and who said afterwards, to nobody, that a recordwright who puts a hand to a thing is a party to it.

Not the woman of about forty who washes the pen, who looked at the page and then at the clerk and then at the wall, where four pages and one sheet in eleven hands are standing, and who said one sentence, and the sentence was that there are nine people in this room and one page on this table and she is not going to be the one who decides which of those two is the document.

Not a man of thirty-one who could have opened it.

He could have opened it. It was a printed sheet lying on a table nine feet by fourteen in a room with a press in it and a key in a clerk's coat, and the man standing nearest to it had four hundred miles behind him and nothing whatever in his hands, and he did not put his hand out, and nobody in that room saw him decide anything, and he has not told one person in this valley that he was in the room.

---

What is under that column in the chamber's own book is four lines.

The chamber's own index is the original. Hollis Nance's is the hand-copy. That is the whole of the difference between the two documents on that table and it is the whole of what the fourth day of the fifty-fourth week is, and it took about four minutes and the four minutes were spent in a clerk copying a heading out of a printed page with a piece of lead.

Under the heading *not sent*, in the chamber's own book, in a print that is a season old, there are four lines and no more, and he read all four of them out loud in a room with about seven people in it, and every one of the four is about this basin.

The first is a return of the water in the Sallow at the frame at Hollow Sallow, taken in the eleventh year, made for the chamber's own use. There is a figure in it. Nobody in this valley has seen that figure, including the woman who opens the frame.

The second is a list of the papers that the Seat of Sallowby has sent to the chamber, being forty-one of them, and of the answers, being nine.

The third is a sheet of the tonnage and the prices of the trade of this basin in the eighth year, which is four years back, and it is a figure and not an opinion, and it is the only figure anybody anywhere has ever written down about how much this valley trades.

And the fourth is a printed notice of one line, and the one line is the rule under which the first of the three rulings was made, and the rule is that no matter of a Seat is to be sent out of its own district.

Nobody in that room said anything about the second one for a long time.

“Forty-one,” said Hollis Nance. “Forty-one papers out of that gap. In nine years. And nine answers, and two of those are the three rulings in one bundle, which means seven papers have been answered one at a time and thirty-two have not been answered at all. I have known since I came that I put things in that gap. I did not know that anybody counted them, and there is a man in Orison who counted them, and he wrote the number on a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is under a heading that says not sent.”

He put the lead down.

“That is the whole of it,” he said. “Nobody has hidden anything. That is what I want said in this room today, because in about a week there is going to be somebody in here who has heard that this basin was kept in the dark, and that is not what happened, and I will not have it said over my table. A Seat sends what a Seat sends. A chamber records what a chamber is sent. And there is a column for the rest, and the rest is as old as the column and about thirty-two papers thick, and it is four lines long, and four of them are about this valley, and none of it was taken off anybody by anybody. It was not sent. That is a different thing and it is a worse thing for us, because nobody did it.”

Nobody in that room said that either, and the room went on with the four lines as though a man had not just said it.

---

The sheets went back into the bag and the bag went on the shelf under the window beside the bundle that has a knife-cut end on it and has been on that shelf since the second day of the fifty-first week, and the two of them are on the same shelf and neither of them is in the press, because the press is for deeds and bonds and the things that are owed, and a printed sheet is not owed to anybody.

Hollis Nance put the shelf square with the back of his hand, and washed the pen, and turned it upside down on a board and turned it over again, and said that there would be another order inside a month and the season was beginning.

And a man of thirty-one put his hand on the table about a foot from a printed sheet and did not touch it, and then took his hand away, and asked whether the man with the bag was going on to Coldbeck, and was told that he was not, because the horse was not ready, and that he would be back in about six weeks with whatever went into the gap, and that what had gone into the gap on this morning was nothing, because the gap is about the size of a person and everything this basin has done in a month went on a wall.
