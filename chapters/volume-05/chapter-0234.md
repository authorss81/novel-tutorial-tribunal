# Chapter 234: Nobody Asks

On the seventh day of the fifty-fourth week, which is the last day of that week, it came out into about four hundred and ten people in this basin, in the ordinary way, that a record about their water exists four hundred miles off in a building with a yard in it, and that it was not sent to them, and that until that morning nobody in this valley had known it existed.

It came out the way everything in this valley comes out. The woman of thirty-eight said one of the four things first, and she said hers standing at her own gate with a barrow handle under her arm, and what she said was that the first of those four lines is a return of the water in the Sallow at the frame at Hollow Sallow, taken in the eleventh year, with a figure in it, and that she has stood at that frame eleven years and that figure has never once been in her hands.

“There is a number about my gate in a town I have never been to,” she said, “and it was taken the year the salting ran eleven weeks and I do not even know which year that was, and I have wanted to know what it said for about six of the eleven years and there has never been anybody I could ask.”

The second of the four came out of the man with the crowbar on the causeway, who is about forty and is paid by the Anner and has been up the high road and down it four times in a week. Forty-one papers out of that gap in nine years, and nine answers, and thirty-two of them never answered. He said it badly, twice, and got it right twice, and he said it to a carter and not to a room, and the carter carried it four miles.

The third came out of a shop over a chandler's, from a woman of thirty-three who copies deeds for a penny a page and has copied about six hundred and forty of them in eleven years, and what she said was that the third of the four lines is the tonnage and the prices of the trade of this valley in a year four years back, and that there is a figure in it, and that eleven hundred tons a year is what this basin says, and that it has been what this basin says for nineteen years.

“If they wrote ours down four years ago,” said Wenna Skell, “then somebody four hundred miles off knows more about what this valley trades than this valley does, and that is not a complaint, and I would like somebody in this room to notice it and write it down and not do anything about it.”

The fourth came out of the clerk of the Seat, on the same day, in the same room, and he had already read all four of them out loud on the day before to seven people and had not been asked one question about any of the four, and the fourth of the four is a printed notice of one line, and the line is a rule, and the rule is that no matter of a Seat is to be sent out of its own district.

“It is the rule the first of their three rulings was made under,” said Hollis Nance. “It is the rule that is why there are three rulings and no papers. I have had that rule on the wall of that room in my own words every working day since I came and I have never once read it as a reason for anything, and it is a reason for thirty-two papers.”

---

Here is what the basin did about it.

The basin's answer to a record that was not sent to it is to go on doing the work, and that happened inside three days without anybody deciding it, and it has to be written down as what it is, because it is neither indifference nor heroism and it is the plainest thing about this valley in a month.

The third roof came off on the sixth of that week and it came off the fourth house from the bottom of the lane, and bed and boards and the salt out of the plaster were done first because a wet roof over a wet floor is a floor that will not dry, and a man of fifty-six does not split nine pounds, and the third of those roofs took four days, and three of them were a four-mile round trip on foot for timber that a cart cannot bring, and on the last of the four he was working to a broken strap with a length of paling wire and a cold hammer.

The gate was opened at the closing of the water and it was five pounds and eighteen shillings better than it was in the fourth year, and a girl of seventeen was paid a penny a day for every day the water ran and wrote the day at the top of every page in a book a hundred years old, in a hand of her own, and nobody has asked her where the number comes from.

And the paper was read out in the lane at the closing of the water, as it is now every time, and forty people stood in a road to hear whether a thing they had been told about was going to happen.

The clay went on at the dam at the second light, forty loads of it, in the same order it has gone on in for eleven years.

And nobody asked. Not in the lane, not in the pit office, not in the room with eleven seats in it, not in the cart yard, and not in a road four miles off, and there is nothing in those four lines that anybody could not have asked about in four seconds.

---

The reason is worth having, because the reason is not a wall and it is not stupidity, and it took a clerk of forty-four four minutes to say it in his own room on the last day of that week.

“Ask what?” he said. “There is no form in this basin for asking a chamber for a thing. There is a gap in a wall the size of a person and two in five of what goes into it is carried nine days and comes back as two paragraphs, and the price of the going and the coming is a pound for entry, which is two hundred and forty pence, and four pounds for the day at the chamber, which is nine hundred and sixty pence, and nine days' board at eightpence a day, which is seventy-two pence, and two hundred and forty and nine hundred and sixty and seventy-two is one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, which is five pounds and six. That is the price of asking for one thing, and there is no price on the list for asking about thirty-two papers, and nobody in this valley has five pounds and six to put into a wall.”

Nobody argued with that. It is the best arithmetic anybody in this valley has on a piece of paper and it has been that price since before the water and it is the reason this is a valley and not a room.

“Also the bench is seized of the matter,” he said. “It said so in two paragraphs in the fifty-second week and it is going to consider it in its own time and it is not going to hear anything else about this basin this year, and a thing that is seized of is not a thing you can put a second paper about. And also nobody in this basin has ever been in a room in which that column was on the table. Not one person. The four hundred and ten of us have been in four rooms and the column is on a page in a press in a city and none of us has ever seen the page and half of us knew the heading existed yesterday.”

He put his hand flat on the table, which he does.

“And I will say the other half of it because nobody else in this valley is going to and it is the half that matters. There is a bench of four at the other end of that column. It is four hundred miles off. It is on a good name, which is a public thing and is not ours and cannot be taken off by anybody in this valley by being careful. And nine people in a valley of four hundred and ten have begun to be frightened of it, and I have watched it happen this week in more places than I can count inside a week, and being frightened is not a matter, and there is nowhere in the only book anybody keeps in this basin to enter it, and I have looked.”

---

He said that in the room with eleven seats in it and six people were in it, and one of the six was a man of thirty-one who has been in this basin for about a month and has not been asked a single question by anybody in it.

He is the only person in this basin who could put that question. He has said out loud, in four rooms since he came, that he is not a party to anything in this valley and that there is no form here for a man with nothing in his hands to have a question about a bench four hundred miles off. He is right about both of those and he has been right about them since the second day of the fifty-first week, and there is no standing in this basin that would let him stand up in a room like that one and say a word about a printed page, and he said so himself on the sixth day of that week, to seven people, in nine words, and nobody asked him for any more of it.

He carried the thing out to the top of the lane after that and stood on the bank above the road with the wind coming off the ridge, and a man of fifty-six came down the last of his ladder from the fourth roof and put his boot on.

Brann Slee looked at him the way a man looks at a ladder he has been up a thousand times, and he has been up ladders in this valley and on the eleven above it and about forty more since he was twenty, and a thing about a man is easiest to see from about the height of a ridge.

“You have not had that leg since before the water came,” he said. “And you have not once put the thing you are carrying down, and I have watched you for about a month, and there is nobody in this valley who has asked you what it is, and I am going to ask you, because you have not got anybody else to ask you and I am the only man on this roof.”

There was nobody on the roof. He was on the ground at the bottom of it, which is where a man stands when he has just come down a ladder.

“What is it you are carrying?”

“A matter for a district four hundred miles off.”

Brann Slee looked at him.

“Then you will carry it home,” he said, “and I have four roofs.”

That was the whole of the help a man of fifty-six who does roofs in this valley has ever had to give anybody, and it was given on a bank above a lane at about the fourth light on the last day of the fifty-fourth week, and a man of thirty-one did not get anything out of it, and did not expect to, and did not tell anybody in this valley that he had asked.

---

And then the argument in the lane was ended, and it was ended by the same man, and he had not been asked to end it and nobody wanted it ended and four people in that lane were still talking at the time he said it.

It was nine words and it was this.

“A roof does not care and neither do I.”

And he said the rest of it, slower, to the ground, because the rest of it is not that short and nobody in that lane pretended otherwise.

“I have been up that roof a long time. There has been a room in a city four hundred miles off this whole time, and I have never been in it, and I could not tell you one thing about what is on any table in it, and it has not made one shilling of difference to a single nail I have driven. I have got a roof to put on in a frost. I am not going to be the man who brings a bench to a lane, and if anybody in this lane wants one brought, there is a road nine days long and it is not my road and I am not going to pay for it and neither is anybody else in this lane, because we have just found out what it costs to send one paper.”

Nobody in that lane said anything to that either, and four people went back up the hill, and the wind came off the ridge, and nine people in Hollow Sallow went on being afraid of a bench they had never met.

---

The week ended at the closing of the water on the seventh day of the fifty-fourth week, and the last day of the week passed the way this valley passed it, which is that nobody noticed it, and at the second light on the morning after, four men and a barrow went down to the dam at the Anner, and the clay went on forty loads at a time in the order it has gone on in since before the water.
