# Chapter 241: What Four Hundred And Ten People Do With A Thing

Seven days ago a page came down in a bag with nine printed sheets on it and four lines under a heading that means not sent, and all four of the four are about this valley, and about thirty people in this valley now know it, and the rest of the four hundred and ten have not asked anybody about it and are not going to.

That is the first fact. The second fact is what the thirty did with it, and it took seven days to establish and it is not what anybody in a room four hundred miles off would expect.

They went on doing the work.

---

The seventh day of the fifty-fifth week was the last day of that week and about nine people at the salt-pans noticed it, which is two more than noticed it the week before, and about four hundred did not, and the four hundred are what a week in this valley is for.

At the second light a woman of thirty-eight opened the gate at Hollow Sallow, which is five pounds and eighteen shillings better than it was in the fourth year, and she opened it at the closing of the water on the sixth and she will open it again tonight, and there is no column in this basin that says she is to and there has never been one.

A girl of seventeen was paid a penny in a jar at the closing of the water on the sixth and wrote the day at the top of a fresh page in a hand of her own, and the day she wrote is a number a man of thirty-one gave her on the first day of the fifty-fourth week and has never told her where it came from.

The sixth roof came off the sixth house from the bottom of the lane on the fifth of that week and a man of fifty-six is four days on it and there are about a hundred and forty days of this weather in front of it and roofs after that one for as long as the weather lasts.

And the clay went on at the dam at the Anner, forty loads of it, at the second light, in the order it has gone on in for eleven years, and a man of fifty-two carried three of the four barrows himself and did not say why.

None of that is a decision anybody made, and it is worth saying so, because the difference between a valley that has decided to carry on and a valley that has merely gone on carrying is the whole of what a thing it cannot act on does to a place, and the honest answer on the seventh day of the fifty-fifth week is that this one did both and could not tell anybody which, and that a man of fifty-six ended an argument in a lane nine days ago in nine words and has already told the whole of it.

---

The four hundred and ten did also ask each other what to do, and they did it badly, and they did it for seven days, and here is the whole of the trying.

Nobody in this basin has been to Orison. That is the first thing they found out and they found it out in about four minutes in a lane, and the four minutes were taken by a man of about forty-two from the cart yard at Sallowby, who has been in and out of that city four times in thirty years on business of a chandler and has never once been in the building the four lines are in.

“There is nobody to ask who has been there,” he said. “That is the whole of the cheap way and it is shut. I have been saying to people at the yard for a week that the cheap way is to find somebody who has stood in that room, and there is nobody, and I have asked four of the men who trade furthest and not one of them has.”

It is the plainest finding a valley of four hundred and ten people has arrived at in about a month, and it arrived in a cart yard and not in a room, and it has nothing to do with money.

And about four people in this basin can put a question into that gap in the wall, and they are the clerk of the Seat, who has done it nine years, and a woman of thirty-three who copies deeds over a chandler's and has sent nothing in her life, and a man of fifty-two whose mine has an address, and a girl of seventeen who writes a book and could not find the door.

Not one of the four has a form.

“There is a form for putting a thing in,” said the woman of thirty-three, on the second of that week, in her own room, to about four people. “I have copied it nine years. It is one page and it wants to know what the matter is and which district it is about and what is wanted done, and there is a box on it for a figure, and the box on it is the only box on the whole page. There is no box on it for who is to answer and there is no box on it for how long, and a thing with a figure in it is a thing somebody has to pay for, and this valley has five pounds and six to pay for one of them and it was said out loud in a room nine days ago and I wrote it on the back of my hand.”

---

Being frightened is not a matter, and the clerk of the Seat of forty-four has said so twice in public, and it is worth saying a third time what the shape of it is, because about nine people in this valley have it and the shape of it is not cowardice and is not stupidity and about four of the nine will not use the word.

Here is the shape.

A valley of about four hundred and ten people is about ninety houses and a market town four miles up a hill, and everybody in it knows which of the ninety houses a person lives in. There is no way to send somebody to a bench of four four hundred miles off and have it stay a private thing, because a lane of two hundred yards has about forty people in it at the closing of the water and every one of them will know by the second morning which person went.

And the nine are not frightened of a bench. They are frightened of being the person who went, and of what this valley will be like on the day after it comes back with nothing, and of being a person who spent the valley's one chance on a thing that comes back as two paragraphs, which is what the last one did.

“Which is a different thing from being afraid,” said Brann Slee, from the last of his ladder off the sixth roof, on the sixth of that week, and nobody in that lane had asked him. “Being afraid is what I have got. I am fifty-six and I do roofs and I have a valley to come back down into, and I would like somebody else to have gone.”

---

The question itself was said out loud on the seventh of that week, at about the second hour, in a lane, by a woman of about thirty-one with a house at the ninth door and two children and a sentence of her own under the strike on the sheet on the wall, and she is the only hand on that sheet that argues for something.

She had been asked twice that week what she thought should be done and she had said both times that she wanted to be on a piece of paper, and the reason she wanted it is eleven years old and is that a house that is not on a paper cannot be argued for.

Then she said the other thing, and she said it to about eleven people at the top of a bank and not to a room.

“You are all asking each other how we would get it,” she said. “Every one of you has asked me that this week and not one of you has asked me what I would do with it if we had it. And I have been asked that question about my own house for eleven years by people who wanted something, and I never once got an answer, and I am not going to be one of the people who does that to a piece of paper.”

Nobody in that lane had put the question back the other way round before, and it stood there for a while, and about four of the eleven people on that bank found out inside a minute that they did not know the answer, and about four of them found out that they did.

“It is the most expensive thing anybody has said in this valley in a month,” said the woman of about forty who washes the pen, and she was not in that lane and said it in a room four miles up a hill to a clerk, and the clerk wrote nothing down, which is the first time in nine years he has had something in front of him that he wanted to write down and did not.

---

A clerk of forty-four put a chair back at a table in a room with eleven seats in it on the last day of the fifty-fifth week, of which eight have never had anybody in them at all, and washed the pen and turned it upside down on a board and turned it over again, and said out loud to a shelf that a page was still on it and shut and that nobody in this valley was going to be the one to open it and that he was going to keep saying that in public until somebody proved him right.

Nobody proved him right.

And a woman of about thirty-one went back down the lane to the ninth door and lifted the lid off a pot that had been on since the light and had the salt out of the plaster off the boards behind her own house, and that is what she does, and nobody on that bank went up to help her with it and she did not want them to.
