# Chapter 231: The Copy In The Lane

The second roof came off on the first day of the fifty-fourth week, and it came off the third house from the bottom of the lane, and a roof wet through takes about four days, and a man of fifty-six who has said nine pounds and will not break it up did not price the four days and was not asked to price them again. Eighteen roofs are wanted and one is half on and the boards are in and the salt is out of the plaster in two places and the plaster in the third place is coming away in a sheet about the size of a door.

The timber for that roof went up the lane on the backs of about eleven people over two days, because a cart cannot go up that lane, and it is the third time in a fortnight that timber has gone up on backs, and the only man in this basin who writes that kind of thing down wrote the first of the three in his own book at the fifth hour of the second of the days it took, and wrote the second one under it in the same hand on the same page, and his own book is not a public record and he keeps it because that is his trade, and there are nineteen days of it in a year and none of them are this one.

Nobody in that lane has ever had a paper read out in it. There is one now, on the sixth line of it, and it says that a copy of it is to be read out in the lane at the closing of the water and kept in the lane by a person of the lane, and it was read out once, on the first day of the fifty-fourth week, in a road, in front of forty people, by the man it is about, who is thirty-four and who cannot read it.

And the second time it was not read out at all.

---

The copy went up the hill on the morning of the second of that week to be copied, because the woman over the chandler's shop does the copying at a penny a page and there are eleven people in this basin who can read a deed and about nine who can read a settlement, and the girl of seventeen is one of the nine and there is no reason in the world for her to be the one who goes back up the hill with it in her hand on a morning when there was other work.

She came down it in the evening. She says the copy took about an hour because the hand in Sallowby is a clerk's and the copy in the lane is not the same paper, and that there is a line on one of them that is not on the other, and that she has not asked about that and is not going to.

And on the second of that week, at the closing of the water, about eleven people came down the lane to the place in the road where it is read, and stood there, and there was nothing to read.

Nobody sent anybody for it. That is on the record and it is not a small thing, and it took eleven people standing in a road to establish it, and four of the eleven had come to ask whether a thing they had been told about was going to happen, and one of them had walked up from the ninth house and one of the four is a woman of thirty-eight who opens a gate four miles up the hill and had walked down for nothing.

The man of thirty-four was there. He had four people and no roof on his house until the fourth day of that week, and he had come down to the road out of the same habit everybody else had, and he had nothing in his hand.

He stood in it for about as long as it takes to undo a chain.

“I have not got it,” he said. “It is up the hill being copied and I did not know it was going up until this morning and I am not going to say that is anybody's fault because I do not know whose it is.”

About nine people in that road said the words “that is all right” at the same moment and none of them said them to him.

---

Here is what the decision was, and it was made in a road in about four minutes, and it belongs to him and not to a man of thirty-one who was standing at the top of the bank and was not asked.

“You want to know whether it is to be read out every time or once,” he said, “and there is nobody in this lane who can answer that but me, and I am the one it is about, and I have been in this house since I was nine. So I am going to say what I think and nobody is going to argue with me about it because I am the one that has to stand in this road.”

Not one of them argued with him about it.

“It is not a ceremony,” said Coll Ferren. “I want somebody in this lane to write that down somewhere, because everybody has been treating that sixth line as though it was a promise about a day. It is not a promise about a day. It is the only way anybody in this lane finds out whether a thing has been done. There is no notice board in this valley. There is no book that goes round. There is one paper and one person who keeps it, and if that person does not say it out loud at the closing of the water, then the nineteen roofs in this lane are done in a room and not in a village.”

He put his hands in his sides and looked at the mud.

“I read it every time it is the closing of the water, and if I have not got it I read it out of my own head, and if I get a word wrong that is a thing that will be written down in a book one day by somebody who was not here, and I cannot help it and I am not going to be asked about it.”

The woman of thirty-eight said the thing that ends the argument, and she said it in a road at the bottom of a lane four miles below her own gate, and she has said things in roads three times in about a month.

“You have just described a thing that has no second copy,” she said, “and the person holding the copy cannot read it, and you have just agreed to stand in a road and say what a paper said, and you have said it twice now and got it right twice, and I am not going to be the one who tells a man of thirty-four he is doing it wrong, because he is the only reason about forty people knew a roof was coming.”

She went up the hill. So did everybody else, at the time it takes to get a barrow back in a shed.

---

The man of thirty-one was in that road for the whole of the four minutes and he said nothing in it, and the reason he said nothing is not in this record because he has not given it to anybody, and there is no instrument in this basin for anybody to notice it in him.

What is in this record is what he did not do. The copy was in the hands of a man of thirty-four for about half of that week, in a road, in his own lane, and it went up the hill in the hands of a girl of seventeen and came down in her hand again, and at no time in those four days was it in his hands, and at no time in the whole of the fifty-fourth week did anybody put a page of it in front of him and hold it there.

He asked for it to be read out on the fourth day of the week, at the closing of the water, and it was read out, all of it, and the man who read it had it in his hand and it was in the clerk's hand in Sallowby and there are eleven hands on the original and the copy is in one, and the two are the same piece of paper with a line on it that exists only in the lane.

He listened to all of it and could not follow one word of it from the paper, and the whole of his instrument was what a man of thirty-four said in a road, and that is the instrument he has been reduced to in this basin and it is not enough and he has not said that it is not enough to anybody.

And nobody stopped the man of thirty-four. That is on the record for the second time in a month and it is not a small thing, and about four people in that lane have now noticed the whole of it and about six have not noticed anything, and a clerk of forty-four has said out loud that he watched a document acquire a line in about four seconds and could not do one single thing about it and that the reason he could not is that the man who acquired it is a person of the lane, and that nobody in nine years has ever once asked him which of those two is the document, and that question is still open, and nobody in Hollow Sallow has asked it, and it is not going to be asked this week.

---

The second roof went back on at about the sixth hour of the fourth of that week, which is before the frost and a long way before anybody's year, and it went on from the bottom of the lane upward, which is the order on the first line of a piece of paper in a room in Sallowby and is also the order of the ground, and the man of fifty-six put the last of the timber on and came down the ladder and put his boot on and stood in the road.

He put a roof back on.
