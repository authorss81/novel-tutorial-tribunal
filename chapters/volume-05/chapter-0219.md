# Chapter 219: What Is Under, In Money

There is no word in this basin for what a man does when his house is under water, because there has never been one to have, and there is no book in this valley that anybody could open, and there is no office nine days off that does this, and the people from the Seat who were in that room on the second day of the fifty-first week had a sheet of paper between them and no heading for it.

The sheet of paper was the back of a leaf. The front of the leaf had the sluice on it, in the mill hand, and the seat's own hand under it, and there was nothing on the other side of any leaf in that book, and a hundred years of a river had been kept on one side of it and somebody was about to put a village on the other.

“A hundred and seventy-one pounds,” said the woman of about forty, in the road, on the sixth day of the week, with the sheet in her hand. “That is the roofs. That is what a man of fifty-six says, and he says it in one piece and he will not break it up, and I am not going to ask him to. That is what the roofs are. It is not the floors and it is not the beds and it is not the seed and it is not the salt in the plaster, and none of those have a price, and the only reason this sheet has a number on it at all is that a number is the one thing in this basin everybody can hold.”

---

They did the sum three times and got three different answers, and every one of the three was somebody being honest.

The first was the Anner, and it is the only money in this basin that anybody can produce out loud. About eleven hundred tons a year out of the pit, ochre and salt together, and four pounds a ton at the pit's mouth and eleven pounds a ton at Sallowby, and the difference of seven pounds a ton is seven thousand seven hundred pounds a year, and forty-five times a hundred and seventy-one pounds is seven thousand six hundred and ninety-five, so a flood of nineteen roofs is a forty-fifth of the difference between what a ton costs at the mouth and what it costs in the market, and it is five pounds short of even that. And the seven thousand seven hundred is not a profit. It is the road. It is what the road between the pit's mouth and Sallowby is worth, and the road is about a hundred and forty days a year, and the road is what the dam is for, and there are sixty men in the working years this month and four of them have been on a dam for eight days.

“Nobody has ever said that out loud,” said Ewan Troke, and then he stopped, because a chainman had said it out loud in a room in Sallowby on the sixth day of the fiftieth week and had written it down in a book a week later, and there was exactly one such book in this basin and it was in the room with them.

The second was the chamber at Orison, and the chamber does not do this, and everybody in that room knew that the chamber does not do this, and nobody had to say it twice.

The third was the Seat, and that is where it went wrong, and it went wrong on a figure that has been in this basin for eleven years and has never once been produced by anybody.

“The Seat has four hundred and eleven pounds a year,” said Garrow Lee, from the wall. “That is in a kitchen and it has been in that kitchen for eleven years and about four hundred and ten people in this village have heard it, and there is not one of them who can say where it came from, and I have asked three people to say the whole of it out loud and two of them said a different number and one of them said she did not know it and I believe her.”

“A hundred and seventy-one is not a quarter of it,” said Tarin Keel.

“No. It is a quarter of it and rather more, and that is the part nobody in this lane has ever said, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to wish I had not.” He put the rule down on the wall. “A quarter of four hundred and eleven pounds is a hundred and two pounds and fifteen shillings. Nineteen roofs at nine pounds is a hundred and seventy-one. So it is sixty-eight pounds and five shillings more than a quarter of the whole of the Seat's year, and the Seat's year is a number that came out of a kitchen, and the more is a number that came out of a man of fifty-six with a ladder on his shoulder, and there is no more than that in this valley.”

“It is a fortnight of the Anner’s road, and there is no figure for the Anner’s road in any book in this valley, and the man who would have it is asleep in a shed four miles up this river because he was on a dam until the middle of the night,” said Ilyan Vester, and then he said: “I am not going to say any more of those. There are nine of them in this basin and every one of them is true and every one of them has a different number on it and a person in this lane can hold all nine of them and none of them is any use to her.”

---

That is the moment the week turned, and nobody in that road noticed that it had, and it turned because the woman of about forty put the sheet down on the wall, and asked the wrong question on purpose.

“You have all been asking what it costs,” she said. “I have been asking what it costs. I have been down eleven houses and a barn and I have asked eleven people what their house is going to cost to put right and every one of them has told me they do not know, and one of them told me that nobody has ever priced a roof in this valley and that the man who does roofs will not break one into pieces, and both of those are true and neither of them is a number. I am going to ask the other one. I am going to ask what you need, and I am going to write down exactly what you say and not one word more, and I am not going to read it back to you.”

“Your sheet has no heading on it,” said Ilya Kever, from the wall, and he was seventy-four and had come up the lane on his own again and had a bucket of tar in his hand because it was in his hand all of that week.

“My sheet has no heading because there is no form in this basin for this, and I am not a Bench clerk, and I am not a recordwright, and I am not an advocate, and I have said all three of those about nine times in the last eleven days and I am not going to say them again. If it is a bad document then it is a bad document and it is the first one in this valley that is about us and not about land.”

Nobody signed it either. She had not expected anybody to and she had a line under every answer with a mark on it, not a tally of anything, just a stroke to know that she had been to that door, and she did not add the strokes up and nobody else did either.

---

The nineteen, in the order she walked, and she wrote them as they were said and did not tidy them, and this is the whole of the list.

“A dry floor and a door that shuts.” “The bed out of the bottom room, that is all.” “Somebody to come and look at the wall before winter.” “Salt out of the plaster, and it is in three places.” “The chair my mother sat in, and not a new one.” “To know whether the house is going to stand.” “A roof before the frost. That is all.” “The seed in the cellar, and it is my own seed.” “To be told it was nobody's doing, that is all.” “Boards, and somebody who knows how to put them.” “The child's cot above the water, that is the one.” “To know which of my two doors to shut at night.” “Water in the well again, and it has been nine days.” “My door, and it is still on the hinge.” “Somebody to write down that it was nothing.” “The ledgers, nineteen years of them, in the top.” “A cart, to get the Anner's ochre off my floor.” “To be asked before the water and not after it.” “Somebody to hold the other end of the sheet.”

Not one of them is money. That is the finding of the sixth day of the week and it took nineteen houses to establish it and about four hours of walking and it cannot be got at any other way, and it is the reason the money is not the difficulty: everybody in nineteen houses was asked what they need and not one of the nineteen answers contained a sum, and two of the nineteen were about somebody being asked at the right time, and one of them was about the other end of a piece of paper.

The fifth house on the list is the one that made the man of thirty-one put his hand flat on a wall.

“To be told it was nobody's doing, that is all.”

“Write it down exactly,” she said, and she wrote it down exactly, and she did not add to it and she did not take from it, and she did not look at the man of thirty-one when she said it and he did not look at her, and nobody in that road said a word to anybody for about as long as it takes to grind ink, and then a woman at the eleventh house said her sentence and the walking went on.

---

He did not ask the nineteen anything. That is in this record because it is the thing that did not happen, and there are four people in that lane on the sixth of the week who could have asked, and one of them could have read a list out afterwards to four hundred and ten people, and not one of them asked, because a man with a book in his coat is not a person of this valley and a list of what nineteen people need is not a thing that can be read out by a stranger and called a finding.

He said so out loud, in the road, and the woman of about forty said the only thing she said to him about it.

“You are not a party to this,” she said, “and if you turn up at that cart shed tomorrow and put your name at the bottom of that sheet then it is not a list of what nineteen people need, it is a list of what a man from four hundred miles off found out, and the four hundred miles is in it, and the leg is in it, and I have not asked about the leg and I am not going to.”

Nobody in that road had said a word to him about the leg.

---

The list went up the hill to Sallowby at the closing of the water and was not read by anybody, and it is not in the press, and it is not in the chain-book, and it is not sewn to anything and there is no other half of it nine miles away under a floor. It went into a cart shed behind the last two houses of Hollow Sallow and under a board, and it went in with a stone on it, and it is the first document anybody in this basin has ever written about themselves, and a man of thirty-one has not read it and is not going to read it, and it is the only page in this valley that is not a record of land and the only page anybody in this valley is going to want in nine months.

And at about the time it went under the board, a woman of thirty-eight came up out of the Sallow with a barrow and turned up the lane instead of going straight on, and she went up the long way round, the whole three hundred yards of it, because the short way was still under, and she has been the sluice for eleven years and she does it every evening and she did it with two feet of water in her own house on the third night of that week.
