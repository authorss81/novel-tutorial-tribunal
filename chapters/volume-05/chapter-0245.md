# Chapter 245: He Opens It

Here is the whole of what happened on the fourth day of the fifty-sixth week, in a room with eleven seats in it, of which eight have never had anybody in them at all, with about nine people in it, and not one of them had asked for any part of it.

The man of thirty-one opened a bag.

---

The bag is a rider's bag and it is not the Seat's. It came up the high road on the sixth day of the fifty-fourth week with printed sheets in it, and it went on a shelf under the window beside a bundle with a knife-cut end on it, and the shelf has held it for twelve days and a shelf under a window in a room like this one is not a place anybody keeps anything in that matters.

It is addressed to a chamber. The chamber does not know there is a Seat. There is nothing on the outside of it that says a man in this room may open it, and the man who opened it said so, and he said it the way he has said it every single time he has said it, and the sentence has not got any shorter, and it is the same length it has been every time, and there is nobody in that room who has come to be told a thing about the water and he did not tell them one.

“I have no authority to open this,” said Ilyan Vester. “It is addressed to a body that has no seat, and I am not that body's clerk, and I am not an advocate, and I am not a person of this district, and there is nothing in this basin that makes me a person of this district. That is four sentences and none of them has got shorter since the sixth of the week before last and I am not going to shorten them today.”

“Right,” he said, and put his other hand flat on the table, which is a thing two men in this basin do and neither of them knows the other does it. “Anybody in this room who wants to stop me has about as long as it takes to say so.”

Nobody stopped him. He put his hand on the shelf, took the bag down, untied the string, and opened it on a table nine feet by fourteen, and what was in it was nine printed sheets and a return folded twice at the bottom, and he could not read a word of any of it and nobody in that room knew that and one person in that room had known it for nine years and has never once said it to him.

“What is on it,” he said, “in words, please, and slowly, and if a number comes out I want it read twice.”

---

> THE CHAMBER'S OWN PRINTED INDEX — THE ORIGINAL, AND NOT THE HAND-COPY, WHICH IS TWO INCHES THICK AND HAS BEEN IN THE KEEPING OF ONE MAN IN THIS TOWN SINCE BEFORE THE WATER — OPENED FLAT ON THAT TABLE AND HELD OPEN AT THE BACK PAGE BY ONE FINGER. The heading at the head of the fourth column is printed and is two words. The three columns beside it have some four hundred and sixty entries in them between them, going back to the first shaking in a small hard hand that gets worse as it comes forward. A single rule is ruled under all four. Under the fourth, four lines and no more:
>
> ONE. A RETURN OF THE WATER IN THE SALLOW AT THE FRAME AT HOLLOW SALLOW, TAKEN IN THE ELEVENTH YEAR, MADE FOR THE CHAMBER'S OWN USE.
> TWO. A LIST OF THE PAPERS THE SEAT OF SALLOWBY HAS SENT TO THE CHAMBER, BEING FORTY-ONE, AND OF THE ANSWERS, BEING NINE.
> THREE. A SHEET OF THE TONNAGE AND THE PRICES OF THE TRADE OF THIS BASIN IN THE EIGHTH YEAR.
> FOUR. PRINTED NOTICE OF ONE LINE. NO MATTER OF A SEAT IS TO BE SENT OUT OF ITS OWN DISTRICT.

Hollis Nance read all four of them out loud in a room with about nine people in it and read the numbers twice, because a man with nothing in his hands had asked him to, and then he put his finger back on the fourth column and left it there while he read, because the fourth column is what the whole morning was about and it is the emptiest thing on that page.

The fourth of the four is a rule. It is the rule under which the first of the three rulings was made, and it is the rule the clerk of the Seat has had on the wall of that room in his own words every working day since he came, and he said so himself, in front of all of them, and this time it cost him something.

“I have read that rule every working day since I came,” he said, “and I have never once read it as a reason for thirty-two papers, and I am going to have to read it as a reason for thirty-two papers for the rest of my working life. That is what the fourth one is. It is not a secret and it is not a mistake. It is the rule. It is the rule they worked under, and about thirty of them in a building with a yard worked under it every day, and not one of them has ever been in this valley, and I want that said in this room today because in a month there is going to be somebody in here who has heard that this basin was kept in the dark, and that is not what happened.”

“Nobody has hidden anything,” said Hollis Nance, in a room, for the second time in a fortnight, and about four people in that room had heard him say it the first time and had not asked him about it, and nobody asked him about it now either. “It was not sent. I have said that once already and I am saying it again and this time it is worse for me than it was then, because then it was a thing in a book and now it is a thing in a bag that a man four hundred miles off has opened on my table. Nobody has hidden anything. A Seat sends what a Seat sends and a chamber records what a chamber is sent, and there is a column for the rest, and the rest is about thirty-two papers thick, and it is four lines long, and I put a good deal of it there myself without once asking what it was for, and I would like it noticed that I have said that and not let anybody else notice it for me.”

---

The third of the four is the only figure anybody anywhere has ever written down about what this valley trades, and it is a thousand and forty tons, and it is four years old, and a man who counts carts for a living said two days ago that it is two counts and not one, and nobody in that room argued with him.

The second of the four is forty-one papers out of a gap in a wall in nine years, and nine answers, of which two were the three rulings in one bundle, which means seven papers answered one at a time and thirty-two not answered at all. Hollis Nance had said that number in this room a fortnight ago and had counted it himself with a piece of lead, and about four people in that room had heard him say it, and it was said again in that room on the fourth of this week and nobody in that room had a reason to doubt it and nobody had asked him to say it a second time.

The first of the four is a return of the water in the Sallow at the frame at Hollow Sallow, taken in the eleventh year, with a figure in it that nobody in this valley has ever seen, including the woman who opens that frame, who has wanted it for six years and has not asked anybody in this basin.

The fourth of the four is a rule this basin has had on a wall in its own words since before the water.

---

And then the thing that made it a choice and not an accident, and it is a small thing and it is the whole of the morning.

Nobody asked him. He was in a room he had no standing in, on a morning nobody fixed for it, about a matter the bench is seized of and has answered once in two paragraphs and will not hear anything else about this basin this year. He is not a party. There is no form. There is no line with his name on it anywhere in this basin, and the one piece of paper in this valley anybody could have put his name on had had the space at the foot of it left empty and undashed by his own hand in a lane, out loud, in front of eleven people, and he had said he was right about it and had not put it back.

He could have gone up the hill. There was a door and it was not locked and about four people in that room would have said good morning to him on the road, and nobody in four hundred and ten would ever have found out that a printed sheet had been opened, or that a date had been put on anything, or that a bag had been untied, and the column would have gone on being shut and shut is a thing a person can be blamed for afterwards as easily as a thing a person can be blamed for before.

What he had instead was nothing whatever. He has no money that is his in this valley, because the five pounds and six that went into a wall yesterday was paid by a mine that said out loud in this room that it was buying a date and not a conscience. He has no party. He has no bench, no body, no office and no remedy he chose alone. He has a name that is not on the only paper in this valley and a leg that has not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and about nine people in a valley of four hundred and ten who are being crushed by a thing that costs the four people it belongs to nothing at all.

Somebody has to spend it, said the shape of it, in his own head, standing at a table with a finger on an empty column. Not a name. A name is not his to spend. A good name is a public thing and it is free and it cannot be spent by the people it is about, and they have spent a month being afraid of one.

He had about four seconds in which he could have said the sentence that would have split the thirty in a building from the four in a room, and he did not say it, and he wants that on the record in his own words: he could have, and he did not, because the sentence that separates them is the sentence that protects the institution, and because he is not the person who gets to hand a bench of four a better word for itself.

What he said instead was this, out loud, to a room, and he said it slowly.

“About thirty people in a building four hundred miles off have been right three times in nine years,” he said. “Three times. And they have not been in this valley once. Not one time. And a record can be perfectly kept and still be wrong about a place, and the reason is that nobody who kept it was ever in it. And there is a piece of paper on the wall of that room with my name off it and an empty space at the foot of it where my name used to be, and I took it off in a lane because a woman of forty was right, and I am the only person standing in here who has a record of his own in this valley, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that those two things are the same thing.”

---

Nobody thanked him.

That is on the record and it has to be said plainly, because about nine people were in that room and four of them had been afraid of a bench for a fortnight and one of them had a name on the sheet and one of them could not read it.

Nobody thanked him and nobody was relieved. A clerk of forty-four washed the pen and turned it upside down on a board and turned it over again and said that a good name is a public thing and that it did not survive the asking and that it was never going to, and that the four people it belonged to had done their jobs properly for nine years and were going to go on doing them properly, and that being right about this basin and being liked in Orison had never been on the same list and that today was the morning the valley found out which one they were on.

The four lines went back into the bag and the bag went on the shelf, and one page of them was copied out in the clerk's own hand and put up on the wall of the Seat's room beside the four pages of the hundred and fifty-fifth day, and the four pages are still on that wall and they are still correct on their face and they are four feet from a sheet that says something they do not say.

It cost a penny. A page of a deed copied at Sallowby is a penny and a sealed copy is threepence, and this is not a deed and it is not sealed and it is not owed to anybody, and a woman of thirty-three at a penny a page copied the four lines out fair for the lane and was not asked to and did it, and the Seat paid the penny out of the press. A penny is the whole of what it cost this valley to have a printed page opened in a room, and a penny will not buy anybody in this basin one single thing that anybody in this basin has been wanting for about a month.

And a man of thirty-one took a penny exercise book with two halves in it out from under a board with a stone on it in a cart shed behind two houses, and wrote one hundred and ninety-three days since the second shaking at the head of a fresh page, which is the sixteenth figure of that kind he has put at the head of a page in this valley and is a count of figures and not a count of anything else, and then he wrote one line under it, and the line is that a man may put a day on his own act and may not put one on another person's speech, and he shut the book and put the stone back and did not write the second half of anything, because the second half of that book is four hundred miles off in a county where a woman of thirty-nine has four children and a stove and no paper at all.

The work of that morning was not enough. Nine people in a room watched a clerk copy four lines onto a sheet, and a sheet went on a wall, and a bag went back on a shelf, and one person's name is now a thing four hundred miles off that a bench of four will read, and the four people it belongs to will read it as four people read a thing that is about themselves, which is carefully, and at the right time, and correctly, and this valley will get nothing out of it today and nothing out of it tomorrow.
