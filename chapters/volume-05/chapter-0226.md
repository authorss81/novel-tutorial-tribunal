# Chapter 226: What He Drafted

The sheet that was on the table in the Seat's one room on the sixth day of the fifty-third week was one sheet and it was in the Seat's own hand, because there is nobody else in that room who writes, and because a sheet in this basin is written by whoever is at the desk, and because the man with a penny exercise book in his coat had not asked to be at the desk and was not a party to the sheet and could not have been, and everybody in that room knew all three of those things and nobody said any of them out loud.

It had a line struck through the top of it, and the strike was in a different ink and was three days younger than the rest of the paper, and the line under the strike was still legible, and nobody took it off.

The clerk of the Seat read it out. He read it because it was his hand and because there was nobody else, and he said before he began that he was not a Bench clerk and not a recordwright and not an advocate, and that the price of taking a paper to Orison and bringing it back was five pounds and six, and that he had said all three of those about nine times in eleven days and this was the tenth, and then he read it.

> A REMEDY FOR THE NINETEEN HOUSES OF HOLLOW SALLOW, DRAFTED IN THE SEAT'S ONE ROOM ON THE SECOND DAY OF THE FIFTY-THIRD WEEK, THE FIRST LINE STRUCK THROUGH AT THE FIFTH HOUR OF THE FIFTH DAY OF THAT WEEK AND LEFT ON THE PAPER:
> ONE. THAT THE NINETEEN HOUSES BE NAMED AND NUMBERED UPON THIS PAPER, AND THAT THE NUMBERING BE READ ALOUD IN THE LANE AT HOLLOW SALLOW AT THE CLOSING OF THE WATER ONCE IN EVERY FOUR WEEKS.
> TWO. THAT THE NINETEEN ROOFS BE DONE FROM THE BOTTOM OF THE LANE UPWARD, AND BED AND BOARDS AND THE SALT OUT OF THE PLASTER BE DONE FIRST, AND A ROOF BEFORE THE FROST.
> THREE. THAT THE FRAME AT THE SLUICE BE MEASURED AGAIN AND A GATE BE MADE IN IT AT FIVE POUNDS EIGHTEEN, AND THE GATE BE OPEN AT THE CLOSING OF THE WATER.
> FOUR. THAT THE WAGE OF THE KEEPER BE NINE SHILLINGS A WEEK FOR THE SIXTEEN WEEKS AND THE FIRST HALF BE PAID BY THE ANNER AND NOT BY THE SEAT, AND THE THREE YEARS THAT ARE OWED BE PAID IN ONE WEEK.
> FIVE. THAT THE WATER-BOOK BE KEPT IN TWO HANDS, AND THE SECOND HAND BE PAID A PENNY A DAY FOR EVERY DAY THE WATER RUNS.
> SIX. THAT THE ANNER PAY THE WHOLE OF THE ABOVE, AND BE NAMED AT THE HEAD OF IT, AND BE ASKED AGAIN IN ONE YEAR.
> SEVEN. THAT THE SAID FOUR-PAGE ORDER OF THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH DAY REMAIN ON THE WALL OF THIS ROOM, AND THAT A COPY OF IT CONTINUE TO BE KEPT IN THE LANE BY A PERSON OF THE LANE.
> AT THE FOOT, IN ANOTHER HAND, AND THE NAME IS THE NAME OF A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE FROM FOUR HUNDRED MILES: ASKED FOR BY.

Nobody in that room said anything for about as long as it takes to grind ink.

The objections are in the order they were made and there were five of them and every one of them was made by a person the draft names, or does not name, and not one of them was made by a man from four hundred miles off.

The first was the struck line, and it was not an objection, it was a question about the strike, and it came from a man of thirty-three over a chandler's shop who had copied the sheet for nothing.

“You have left it on the paper,” said Wenna Skell. “You have struck it and left it on. That means anybody who comes in here in a year can see that a sentence was wanted and taken back, and they will spend a month working out who took it back and why, and they will not get it right, because it was not a clerk who took it back.”

“It was a man who lost a house,” said the under-manager.

“Then it is a man who lost a house, and it is going to be read by about four hundred and ten people who were not in the room.”

The second objection was to the fourth line, and it was made by the woman the fourth line is about, and she is thirty-eight and she has been the sluice eleven years, and she had already said on the fourth day of that week that she did not need more money and she said it again in about nine words and it is a different nine words.

“You have written *paid by the Anner and not by the Seat*,” she said. “The Seat did not fail to pay me. That is the whole of what is wrong with that sentence and it is a small thing and it is the only thing in it I can get at. Nobody promised me the other four and six. It was in a book in a room and it came to me through a man who came to me when the pit paid, and if you write *not by the Seat* then in a year somebody will ask the Seat why it stopped, and the Seat will say it never started, and both of them will be right, and I will be forty in that year and I will be the only person in it who knows which of the two is a lie.”

Nobody in that room had thought of it and the clerk of the Seat wrote nine words down and read them back and she said they were right and he did not alter them.

The third objection was to the third line, and it came from a joiner of forty-one with a rule in his hand who had measured that frame twice and charged for it once.

“The frame does not want measuring again,” said Garrow Lee. “It wants a gate. There is a lath on it with five pounds eighteen on it in chalk and it has been in that frame about two years and it is going to stay in that frame until somebody buys it, and if this paper says the frame is to be measured then the frame gets measured in the spring by a man with a rule and nothing happens, and I will have measured it a third time in my life and I will be forty-two.”

“It was measured four hours ago,” said Brann Slee, fifty-six, with a ladder on his shoulder, from the doorway, and nobody had seen him come in.

The fourth objection was to the sixth line, and it was not an objection, it was the under-manager asking a question about a word, and the word was asked.

“You have written that the Anner is to be asked again in one year,” said Jory Vellan. “Not that the Anner shall pay again. Asked. I want that word read out four times and I want it read out the way I have said it and not the way the girl has said it, and I would like my own hand on that line and not yours, because I am the one who has to be asked and I am the one who would have to answer.”

So the under-manager wrote the sixth line out again in his own hand, in ochre dust, on the back of a leaf, and the clerk of the Seat copied it, and the two hands on that line are the only two hands on the sheet that anybody in the room can tell apart, and Hollis Nance said out loud that this was a defect and not a finding and that he was not going to write it down.

The fifth objection was to the last line, and it took about an hour of that same afternoon, and it is the whole of what the seventh line is for, and it was made by a woman of about forty who washes the pen.

“You have kept the order on the wall,” she said, “and you have kept a copy in the lane kept by a person of the lane, and that is the fourth line of that order and it is the only line of it that has ever been obeyed, because it is the only line that says a person is to do something. That is not nothing. I have been in this room nine years and I would fight anybody in this basin who told me that line was not worth having.”

“And?”

“And the person keeping that copy in that lane is a man of thirty-four who cannot read what is on it,” said the woman of about forty, “and he is going to keep it, because nobody else will, and he is going to have it read to him every time it is read out, and every time it is read out it is going to be a different document, and you cannot put a clause in a paper about a thing that happens to a paper every time somebody reads it.”

She put her hand flat on the table, on the two sheets, and did not move it.

“So do not write *kept by a person of the lane* in your paper as though it is a quiet clause. Write that it is read out. I do not know what the line should say after that and I am not going to write it for you, and if you leave that seventh line exactly as it is I will not fight you about it, and if you put my name to it I will fight you about it in this room and in the lane and in about four months.”

---

The man of thirty-one was in that room for about three of the six hours and he said about nine sentences in it and every one of the nine was a question and none of them was an answer, and he did not defend the paper once, and about four people in that room noticed that he had a name at the foot of it and had had it there for four days and had not mentioned it.

Nobody mentioned it. The strike through the first line was mentioned four times and the name at the foot was mentioned not at all, and the reason nobody mentioned it is the reason nobody in that room took the four pages off the wall a day earlier, which is that this basin has never in nine years done anybody a kindness in a room.

And the last thing that happened in that room on the sixth day of the fifty-third week was a woman of twenty-eight who had been standing at the back of it for about two hours, and who is a recordwright of a public record and is expressly not an advocate, saying that she was not going to put a hand to the paper at all, and saying why in about nine words, and being right.

“If I put a hand to that, I am a party to it,” said Sera Quill, “and then it cannot be used against the people in it, and there are about four hundred and ten people in that valley whose only protection on a wet night is a piece of paper they have never been able to read all the way through. I will copy it and I will witness the copies and I will not sign it.”

Nobody argued with her, and the man of thirty-one wrote down the hour of it and did not put a sentence under the hour, and did not repair it, and did not put it back.

She said one other thing that day and she said it to nobody in particular and about eleven words long and it is the first time anybody in this basin has heard anything about her at all, and he may not speak for it and did not.

“They have had my family’s claim six years and it is not filed and it is not going to be filed in a valley,” said Sera Quill. “That is not a complaint. I am telling the room so that nobody can later say that I put a hand to a piece of paper for a fee.”

And at the end of the sixth day of that week the clerk of the Seat took the sheet off the table and squared it with the four pages on the wall, which were crooked, and did not take the four pages down, and the two documents stood side by side on that wall for about four hours and anybody who came into that room that afternoon could have read both of them, and about nine people did, and the two of them disagree with each other and neither of them is wrong.
