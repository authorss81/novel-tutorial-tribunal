# Chapter 236: A Good Name, Four Hundred Miles Off

The thing that happened on the second day of the fifty-fifth week in this basin was that about nine people said out loud, in four places, that they were frightened, and every one of the four said it in a different voice and none of the four would use the word.

The first was a woman of about thirty-one with two children at the ninth door, who has a sentence on the settlement under a strike and did not put it there for this.

She said it to nobody in particular, in her own doorway, with a bucket in her hand.

“They will have heard about the roofs,” she said. “There is a chamber nine days off and it has got a yard in it and about thirty men in it, and one of them wrote down how many tons we trade in a year that we have never told anybody, and I have been lying awake for two nights doing a sum and the sum is not the point. The sum is that I do not know one single thing about those men and they have got four lines about this lane and a number about our gate, and if a woman in a room four hundred miles off ever says a thing about us, then there is a thing on a page somewhere that says it first.”

Nobody argued with her about the arithmetic, and she is one of the nineteen, and there is a sentence of hers on the settlement under a strike that she did not put there for this and has not been asked about.

The second was the man of about forty with the crowbar, who is paid by the Anner and has been walking the length of a shut causeway for about a fortnight with nothing to mend, and who said it to a carter and not to a room.

“I have been a boy at that winch for nineteen years and my father was before me,” he said, “and my father's father cut a mark on a doorpost in a lane four miles off, and I have never once in my life had to think about a bench of four, and it is not that I want to think about them. It is that I have started, and that is different, and there is nobody in this basin I can say that to except a carter, and I have said it to a carter.”

The third was a carter in a yard at Sallowby, and he is the one who said the thing that put the number in it, and he said it in the ordinary way, because a carter's mile and a bench's mile have never been the same length in any country and he has the figure for it.

“Nine days is a long way when you are not going,” he said. “I have been over it and back four times and I will tell you the honest size of it. It is about four hundred miles and it is nine days and it is on other people's ground for about eight of them, and everything I know about the law in those eight is that a thing I sign in a yard here is a thing I sign under somebody else's roof out there, and that has always been true of every court in this kingdom and I have never once minded it, and I mind it now, and the difference is a column in a book.”

And the fourth was the woman of about forty who washes the pen, and she said it in the room with eleven seats in it, and she said the whole of it, and she is the one that the other three were talking about without knowing it.

“I have been in this room as long as there has been a room,” she said, “and I have watched a good name work the way a good name works, and it has never once cost anybody anything in this basin, and it has never once been about us. There are four of them and they are good and they are four hundred miles off, and what a bench of four would say about this valley if it ever said anything at all is nothing, and I want that written down, and I want it written down that I said nothing is the answer and that I am the one in this room who has been frightened for two days, and the two things are both true and they are in the same sentence.”

---

Nine people. That is the count and it is the count of people in a valley of about four hundred and ten who have said a thing out loud, and the clerk of the Seat put a number on it in his own book on the second of that week and wrote the number out with a line under it, and the line under it says *NOT ENTRY. NO COLUMN FOR A PERSON BEING AFRAID OF SOMETHING THEY HAVE NEVER MET.*

“It is not a matter,” he said. “That is the whole of it. I have looked for a place to enter nine people being frightened of a bench and there is not one in this basin, and the reason there is not one is that they are not frightened of anything they have done, and a person cannot be entered anywhere for a thing they have not done.”

The under-manager came up the hill on the second half of the second of that week and he was not asked to and he came anyway, and he heard the whole of it from the carter at the gate before he heard a word of it in a room, and he stood in the room with the eleven seats in it and listened to about seven people say a thing he had not come to be told, and then he said the whole of it in nine words and he was right.

“I'm going to say one thing and then I'm going up the hill,” said Jory Vellan. “Their good name is public and it is free.”

Nobody in that room took the second half of that in for about four seconds, and the woman of about forty took it in first and said, “Go on,” and he said he had said all of it and she said the whole of that was also the finding.

And being right is the whole of the danger, and the room could not get at the reason why, and a recordwright of twenty-eight who had been standing at the back of it with a satchel said it on the way out, to nobody, in about nine words, and said it with no voice at all: "A public thing that costs nothing cannot be spent by the people it is about."

And nobody in that room wrote it down and it is in this record because a person said it out loud in a room and about four of the nine heard it and about five did not.

---

The under-manager was on the road to the pit at about the fifth hour and he stopped at the top of the causeway, which is shut and has been shut for a fortnight and will be shut for about a hundred and forty days, and he stood on a shut causeway in the wind for about as long as it takes to turn a cup round on a board.

He had been carrying a number about since the third day of the fifty-third week and it had been arithmetic for ten days, and on the second of the fifty-fifth week it stopped being arithmetic.

The number is this. The settlement of this valley is a hundred and seventy-eight pounds, thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and the whole of what the road between the pit's mouth and the market is worth in a year is about seven thousand seven hundred pounds on this basin's own figure, and the settlement is two and three-tenths in the hundred of it. He had put that in the chain-book in his own hand. Two and three-tenths in the hundred. It is a number about money.

And what became of it on a shut causeway at the fifth hour is that the number is no longer about money. It is about a name. In four hundred miles there are about thirty clerks and four of them are a bench, and the bench has a good name, and a good name is a public thing, and the whole of what this basin has bought with a hundred and seventy-eight pounds and thirteen shillings and fourpence a year is not nineteen roofs.

It is the right to be described, by four people whom none of us has ever met, in a way we would have to live in.

A mine that has a sheet has a standing interest in a number that put water in nineteen houses. That was true for ten days and it was a fact about a mine. On the second of the fifty-fifth week, on a causeway that is shut because four true lines said it should be, it stopped being about the mine and it started being about him, and it happened at about four in the morning in a house at the top of the hill and not in the chain-book, and it took him about an hour and a half to get to the bottom of it, and what was at the bottom of it was this.

If a bench of four ever says a thing about this valley it will say it correctly, because it has been correct three times since the first shaking, and it will say it about nineteen roofs that a mine is paying for, and there is not one line in this valley that says what a good name is.

He did not write it down. He has never written down anything in nineteen years that was not a figure of the trade, and he stood on a causeway at the fifth hour with a chain-book in his coat and did not open it, and then he went down to the dam.

Nobody in that room said that he is a good man, and nobody in this basin has said it either, and he is the only person in this valley who has paid it anything, and those two facts are both true and they have nothing to do with each other.

---

At the second light on the third of that week four men and a barrow went down to the dam at the Anner, and four loads of clay went on in the same order they have gone on in for eleven years, and the height held at eleven feet above the Anner's own sill, and the man of fifty-two put the barrow down himself and did not say anything about a good name to anybody.
