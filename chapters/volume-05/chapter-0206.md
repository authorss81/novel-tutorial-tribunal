# Chapter 206: The Eleven Houses

The last day of the fiftieth week was the seventh and there was nothing in the air at Hollow Sallow that anybody would have called weather, and about three hundred yards down the bank from the sluice the ground stops being any use at all and a lane goes down into it between eleven houses that are built along the lane because the ground is the only ground there is.

You can see all eleven from the bank without being shown them. They are in a line and they are the only roofs in Hollow Sallow that are below the top of the sluice frame, and there is a mark cut into the doorpost of each of them about the height of a man’s shoulder, and the mark is the same mark and it was cut in one day in a year when the Sallow came up nine feet in a night.

It was cut by a man with a stick and a string and a bucket of tar, and he did it in one day, and he cut it on nine of the doorposts at one height and on two at another, and nobody in this basin has ever asked him why two are different, and he is at Coldbeck and he is sixty-eight and he is the man who drew the ninth-turn half of the marl-strip deed for the Anner and was paid by the Anner.

“That is Davan Treloar,” said Ewan Troke, from the top of the bank. “And I would not say it to his face on the strength of a mark on a doorpost, and neither would you.”

“I would say it to his face with a string,” said Garrow Lee.

---

Garrow Lee was forty-one and he was a joiner and he was on the bank with a rule and a piece of chalk and a plane and about eight feet of lath, and he had been at the sluice frame since the second light measuring for a gate that he cannot get anybody to buy.

“Eleven feet the frame and one pound eight the oak,” he said, before he had been asked, and then he corrected himself, which is what a man does when he has said a figure in a room before and got it taken. “Four pounds ten the frame and one pound eight the oak, and the five pounds eighteen is mine and there is not a part of the five pounds eighteen that is anybody else’s. That is the whole of a gate.”

“Who would buy it,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Nobody, and I have been up here three times about it and I am not going up a fourth.” He put the rule down on the lath. “The Seat has not got it and will not say so. The Anner will not buy a gate for a village, and I have been told that by a man in a good coat on the Anner water, and I do not know why I am telling you and I have not told anybody else, and that is the fourth time I have nearly said that this week.”

He said it the way a man says a thing he has been carrying and has just put down, and then he picked the rule up again and went back to the frame, because the frame was what he was there for.

“There is no new gate in that frame,” he said. “There is a gate and a new frame behind it and the gate is four years old and it is doing its work and the new frame is doing nothing at all, and the reason I am measuring the same frame for a second time is that the Seat has had the same conversation three times and each time it has cost somebody a day.”

“What conversation.”

“The one about the mark,” said Garrow Lee.

---

“A height is a height above something,” he said, and he said it standing on the bank with the rule in his hand and the wind off the dry bed coming up behind him, and it is the most useful sentence said out loud in this basin in the whole of the first seven days, and it was said by a joiner who did not know it was useful. “I have been given eleven feet twice this month by two men and both of them meant something else and neither of them was wrong, and I have been given thirteen feet once by a woman and she was right, and there are three different sills inside four miles and they are not the same height and nobody has ever written down what any of them is above.”

He put the rule against the top of the frame and looked along it and then took it away again.

“The Anner sill is the dam’s own sill and it is the one every number in this basin goes back to. The eleven feet in the bond is eleven feet above that, and this frame’s sill is nine feet below the eleven feet, which puts it two feet above the Anner sill. The bed down there is thirteen feet below this sill and is therefore four feet below the Anner’s, and the ground from this frame to the eleventh doorpost is the flattest piece of the Hollow and it is not flat, and the mark on the ninth doorpost is a foot higher than the mark on the fifth, and I know that because I have put my own shoulder against both of them and my shoulder has not changed since I was twenty-six.”

“Then eleven feet is not eleven feet,” said Tarin Keel.

“Eleven feet is eleven feet,” said Garrow Lee. “It is the number the man wrote on a wall somewhere four miles away, and the ground between there and here is not level, and so the mark is not a level, and there are eleven marks and there are eleven heights and nobody has the eleven numbers. And if you write *eleven feet* on anything, then whoever reads it after the rest of us are dead will take it for a level, and it will be a level, and it will be the only level anybody in this basin has ever had.”

He had not said the arithmetic out loud and Ilyan Vester did it, in his own mouth, the way he does it, and it took him no time at all, and he wrote it in the penny exercise book and then he read it back to a bank of clay with eleven roofs on it.

“If this frame’s sill is nine feet above the Anner’s sill, then eleven feet above the Anner’s sill is two feet above this frame’s sill, and two feet above the sill is about the height of the floors of the houses on the upper half of that lane, and the mark on the fifth doorpost is a foot above the mark on the ninth, so the mark is not the floor and is not a level and is not a number anybody can use.”

“It is a man with a bucket of tar,” said Ewan Troke.

“It is a man with a bucket of tar,” said Garrow Lee, “and he is sixty-eight, and he cut them in one day in a wet year with a string, and there is no way on this earth to find out what he was aiming at, and there are eleven houses, and I have just told you that the eleven of them are not a fact. They are eleven doors that a man in a hurry marked, and I have been trying for two years to get somebody to measure them and nobody will pay me for two years of measuring.”

---

Nobody had asked the eleven.

That is the whole of it and it is worth putting plainly, because the three of them had come nine days to find out whether eleven households below a line were a category, and they were not, and the reason they were not is that nobody had ever put the eleven in a list, and there is a cost to putting eleven people in a list in Hollow Sallow and the cost is a day.

“A day is a day,” said Garrow Lee. “You do not send for eleven people in a morning. You send for one and then you have to wait, and then you send for the other ten and it is two days, and then you have to find somebody to write it down, and the writing is a penny a page for the woman over the chandler’s shop. A penny is a penny. The whole of it comes to three days, and three days is not a figure, and that is the whole of why nobody does it. A figure you can put in a minute and take out of a minute and argue with. Three days is three days of eleven people not being where they otherwise were, and there is no column in this basin for a person not being where they are, and I have never once in two years been able to make that into a number, and I have been carrying it since I first went at the marks, and it is too heavy.”

“Who says it is nobody’s business to ask.”

“Two men in the same month, and one of them in a good coat, and both of them were right, and I would like a person to say a number at me so that I can put it down.”

---

And that is the sentence that the eleven houses had been waiting a long time for, and it came from a joiner of forty-one on a bank at the second light on the last day of the fiftieth week, and nobody in the party was going to be able to say afterwards that anybody had asked them to say it.

---

The house of the eleven that had been rebuilt once is the third one up the lane and it was rebuilt nine years ago, in the year of the mark, and it was rebuilt because the front wall went out, and the rebuild is a document, because the Seat paid nine pounds toward it out of a fund that has not been named in nine years and has not been asked after in as many, and about eleven other people in this village know about that nine pounds.

“It is the only house of the eleven that is higher than it was,” said Ewan Troke, standing at the bottom of the lane. “The rest of them are all exactly where they were, and the mark is exactly where it was, and the mark on this one is a foot below where the other ten are, because the man who rebuilt it put his own mark on the new doorpost, and he was not the man with the string.”

Two different men, two different heights, one year, eleven houses, and no instrument in the basin that can hold two heights and say which is right.

And one of the eleven was a woman who kept a sluice eleven years and had never been told that her house was on anything, because there is no list, and the reason there is no list is the two lines above, and she was standing at the top of the lane in the sun while all of it was said, with her arms folded, and she had come up out of the Sallow because there was a barrow of stone on the bank and the barrow was hers.

“Ilma,” said Garrow Lee.

“I have heard my own street,” she said, and she came down about six feet and stopped.

“It is the third one from the top,” he said. “Yours. It is on the mark.”

“Then it is on the mark,” said Ilma Treen, and she looked at the eleven of them from the top of the lane, and nobody in the party had thought to ask her to and she had done it on her own in about four seconds, and then she said: “Then somebody is going to have to come and tell me what a mark is for. It is not going to be the Seat. And it is not going to be the Anner.”

Nobody said who it was going to be. There were four of them standing in a lane in a village of about four hundred and ten people and not one of them was from anywhere else, and the joiner had told them the price of a gate and did not know what any of them could do with a number, and the two men from the Seat who had told him it was nobody’s business had not been in this lane, and the one person in the lane who would have made something of that difference was standing at the bottom with a lath under his arm and had said nothing for a long time.

---

The week turned over at about the closing of the water and about nine people in this basin noticed it, and every one of the nine was at the pans, and about four hundred and six people did not.

At Coldbeck a man of sixty-one drew his board through the eleventh pan and came back and stood at the head of it and looked at the notches and did not cut one, because the day is a new day and the new day is not over until the first light of the next one, and he said to nobody in particular that there was a hundred and fifty-three of them and that a hundred and fifty-three was not a figure anybody had ever wanted.

At the Seat a woman of about forty washed out a cup and put it upside down on a board and turned it over again, which she does at the end of a week, and which is not a week of the Shelf and is not a week of anything, and which is in her own hand and is in nobody’s book.

And a man of seventy-one came down the bank above Coldbeck with a barrow and stopped in the middle of it to put his boot off and get the stone out of the sole, and he did it on the last day of a week of the Shelf, four hundred miles from the Shelf, and he does not know that there is such a thing as a week of the Shelf, and there is nobody in this basin to tell him and there is not going to be.

---

The frame was measured and not built.

Garrow Lee wrote his figure on the lath in chalk and put the lath against the frame and left it there, and the four pounds ten and the one pound eight and the five pounds eighteen was on the lath in a joiner’s hand, and it was the first price for anything in this basin that had been said out loud in front of a person who was not going to buy it, and nobody in Hollow Sallow had any money at all in that year, and the district of Ryefold was at nothing and had been since the sixth day of the forty-third week and was four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year in the hole in a county that this village has never heard of and whose name is not a word anybody here has ever had to use.

A quick ruling had to be written in the morning, and there were eleven marks on eleven doorposts and no instrument in the basin that could say which of them was a level, and the number that everybody was going to use was eleven, and it was going to be used because it was in a bond that was void and in a bond that was not, and because it was the only number anybody had.

That was the state of it at the closing of the water on the last day of the fiftieth week, and there was one lamp in the village, and the lamp was in the house of the eleven that was third from the top, and it was on because the woman who lived there came up out of the Sallow at about the time the light goes and does not light it until she has put the barrow down.
