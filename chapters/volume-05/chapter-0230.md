# Chapter 230: What The Sheet Does Not Say

The oak for the gate came up the hill in the third week of the fifty-fourth week on a cart, and the cart got to the top of Hollow Sallow and no further, and about nine people carried the rest, and a man of forty-one with a rule in his hand stood at the top of the lane and watched a thing he had measured and charged for in the fourth year after the marks were cut being carried past him for the third time in his life.

He had a lath in his coat. It had been in that frame for about two years and it had five pounds eighteen on it in chalk and no name on it, and he had asked for a number three times and been given it three times, and on the third of that week he took it out of his coat, and looked at the figure on it for about as long as it takes to drink a cup of something, and handed it to a girl of seventeen who copied papers for nothing, and she copied the figure onto the back of her own hand and then threw it away in front of him, and he did not say anything about that either, because there was nothing in this basin to say.

The gate was hung in one day by two men, one of whom is the joiner and one of whom is a pitman of about thirty-five who is on ten shillings a week and was there on his own time and told nobody and was not thanked for it, and the joiner's own words about the frame are that the frame is a frame and that it is the only thing in this valley that has waited three years and come out right, and that he is not going to be asked about the frame and does not want to be.

The gate opened at the closing of the water on the third day of the fifty-fourth week and it did the only thing a gate in that frame has ever been able to do, and about thirty people in Hollow Sallow came down the lane to watch a gate open, which is not a thing anybody has ever done in this basin, and about nine of the thirty were children and about four of the nine had been under two feet of the Sallow in the week before last.

Five pounds and eighteen shillings. A frame at four pounds ten that had been measured twice and charged for once, and an oak at one pound eight, and one thousand and eighty pence and three hundred and thirty-six pence is one thousand four hundred and sixteen pence, and the whole of what anybody in this basin has spent on that structure in three years is a gate that works, and the whole of what it cost is a figure in chalk on a lath that is in a cart shed now.

---

The other thing that happened on the third of that week is that a man of thirty-one went down to the frame to look at the gate in it, at the second light, alone, on a wet clay bank, and he went down it the way a man goes down a bank he has been down before, and his leg was what his leg has been since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and nobody in that lane has ever been told a word about any of it and he has not told one person in this valley and is not going to.

He stood at the frame for about four minutes with a hand on the new oak, and then he went back up the hill, and the whole of what he did up there was sit at a table with a sheet in front of him that has eleven hands on it and read all six of the live lines and the struck one, and that is the end of what he did that day in that valley.

Because the six lines name a roof, a gate, a wage, a penny, a payer and a reading, and the seventh line that a man of thirty-four added in a road with no figure on it, and the struck line that three hands do not agree about, and not one of them names the height.

Eleven feet above the Anner's own sill is the number that put two feet of water into nineteen houses of a village of about four hundred and ten people on the third night of the fifty-second week, and it is on a wall in Sallowby in four complete lines that are correct on their face, and it is in a bond under a floor in a room that is never opened, and it is in a chain-book in a hand that is not the under-manager's, and it is in about nine books and on about nine doorposts in a lane, and it is not on the sheet.

He looked at that for about as long as it takes to grind ink. He had a pencil out and he had about four inches of space at the foot of the struck line, because a struck line has room under it, and he did not write anything in it, and he put the pencil in his coat, and that is the whole of the last act of a man of thirty-one in this valley in the fifty-fourth week.

He did not put it in because a woman of twenty-eight stood at the back of a room in Sallowby a week ago and said nine words and was right, and because he is not a party to that sheet and has said so in four rooms in about a month, and because there is no form in this basin for a man with nothing in his hands to have a paper in front of him, and because a man who has learned a thing once in a room in another county is not entitled to say it out loud a second time in a lane in a different county to a woman of thirty-four who lost a house to about two feet of water and does not need to be told what a height is by anybody four hundred miles off.

He wrote it in his own book instead, on the basin side, in four words, and the four words were a statement of what is not on the paper, and it is the only thing he has put on paper about himself in three weeks and it is not a resolution and it is not a request and nobody is going to be asked to do anything about it.

Nothing came up. It had put four things in front of him in this valley — two in one week and one on a wet bank in the dark and one in a lane with forty people in it — and it had not said one word about a dam or nineteen houses or a rule that named a body and not a person, and it had not said a word about eleven hands either, and about eleven days ago he had begun, without noticing that he was doing it, to expect it to say something, and on the third of that week it became clear to him that he had stopped expecting it, and he did not tell anybody that he had stopped expecting it, and there is no instrument in this basin for anybody to notice it in him.

---

The week ended at the closing of the water on the third day of the fifty-fourth week and a man of thirty-one shut a penny exercise book with two halves in it and put it under a board with a stone on it, and on the basin side of it there are twelve day-counts and four words about a number that is not on a piece of paper in a room four miles off this one, and the twelfth of them is one hundred and seventy-eight days since the second shaking and it is the last one for the length of a winter, because there is nothing in this basin that a man can usefully count between one week and the next and he has found that out the hard way and it has cost him four hundred miles and a leg he has not got back, which he has not said to anybody and is not going to, and he will come back up that hill in the first week of a month he cannot name, and he knows the day off the water-book because a girl of seventeen keeps it in a hand of her own and has the number and has never once asked him where it came from.

And on the Shelf side of that book nothing was opened this week, and the carrying is off nothing, and the second notice of five shillings and fourpence is unpaid and is not going to be forgiven, and the claim of forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence to a carter of fifty-one with a boy eleven miles off is not paid and is not producible and there is no form for it, and he is right, and the letter of a clerk of thirty-four is in a bundle in a room in Sallowby with a knife-cut end on it and it is not opened, and the entry is due in about nine months, and about four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year falls due at the close of a grain year in a county four hundred miles from this valley and the district of Ryefold is at nothing and it does not stop and it was not mentioned once in a valley that has just spent a hundred and ninety-five pounds and seven shillings and fourpence on nineteen houses.

And the sheet is on the wall. The four pages are on the wall beside it and they are still correct on their face and they did this, and the two documents disagree with each other and neither of them is wrong, and a man of thirty-four who cannot read either of them keeps the copy of one of them in a lane at the bottom of a hill, and a woman of thirty-eight who is owed nothing and is paid on the day opens a gate that is five pounds and eighteen shillings better than it was, and a girl of seventeen is paid a penny a day and writes the day at the top of every page, and a man of fifty-two is to be asked again in a year and asked to be asked, and a man of thirty-one has taken his own name off a piece of paper because a woman of about forty was right and he said so out loud in a lane, and nobody in this basin has forgiven anybody anything and nobody in this basin has been forgiven for anything and the mine is a mine and the road is the road and the height is not on the paper.

A gate was opened at the closing of the water, and a chair was put back at a table, and a lath went in a cart shed.
