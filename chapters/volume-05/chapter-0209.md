# Chapter 209: A Trench Taken Out In A Straight Line

The chain-book of the Anner is on a board in the under-manager’s office and it is about the size of a door and it is a hundred and thirty years old in its binding and about nine hundred pages of it are in a hand nobody in this valley can read, and the last entry in it is in the hand of Jory Vellan’s father and is about the drainage of the wet workings and is the whole of the reason the man is a person whose time has come in this chapter.

The office is a shed with a stove in it and the stove was going and there were two men in it, and one of them was the under-manager, and the other was a man of twenty-six called Alo Vearn who had been in the oldest workings since she was seventeen and who was not asked by anybody to be anywhere and had come because four people came down the ramp past her and did not stop.

“The last workings are a long way in,” said Jory Vellan. “Twenty minutes. It is a hundred and forty feet down to the second level and then four hundred feet of steps, and the steps are steps, and the water is in at the bottom.”

“Pumping into it,” said Tarin Keel.

“Pumping into it since the first shaking,” said Jory Vellan. “Everything that comes off the levels goes down the low end and the low end has a pipe in it that goes somewhere. That is the whole of the water question at the Anner and it is the only question and it has been the same question for twelve years, and nobody has asked it, and I have asked it about four times in my own head.”

Nobody asked it. It is a pipe. It goes somewhere a man in a good coat put it to go, for a reason that was sensible then.

---

The second level is where the ochre stops and the salt starts, and the oldest workings are the oldest, and the oldest of them are the ones that were not sunk.

You do not sink a mine with a pick. You sink it with a hammer and a bit and a great deal of arguing, and it comes down as a shaft about the width of two men. A pick is for a face. A pick makes a hole in a thing and the hole is about the size of the pick.

The oldest trench on the lowest level of the Anner is eleven chains and sixteen links long and it is about the height of a man and it is straight, and it is straight for eleven chains, and there is nothing in this valley in nine hundred pages of a chain-book about a straight line that was taken out of red stone by hand before there was a bench in Sallowby.

“Water in the floor,” said Alo Vearn. “Not in the trench. In the floor, off the side of it, in a line, and it has been in a line since before I came and I have seen it in every year I have been down. Six of them. I have never once been asked what I think they are and I have had twenty-six years to think about it and I have thought about it exactly as much as you would expect.”

Nobody said anything about that for about a minute and a half, which is not long, and a minute and a half is a long time at the bottom of a mine.

“They are cuts,” she said. “Not mine. Older. You can put a chain in and it will go in about four inches and come out on a shoulder, like a joint, and I have never seen a man make one and I have worked beside a dozen men who could make one in their sleep.”

---

Here is the page, and it is page eleven of the chain-book, and it is the eleventh page and not the last, and about eight hundred pages of what follows it are drainage, and the entry is in a hand that has a very good line and no hurry in it at all.

> THE OLD WORKINGS, THE FOURTH TURN OF THE TITHE BOOK, BEING THE FIRST OF THESE THAT WERE NOT SUNK BUT TAKEN OUT, THE TAKE-OUT BEING ELEVEN CHAINS AND SIXTEEN LINKS ALONG ONE STRAIGHT LINE FROM THE FACE OF THE RED STONE AT THE NORTH END TO THE LOW END OF THE PIT AT THE SOUTH, AND NO COLUMN IN THIS BOOK FOR ANYWHERE.
> THE SIX MARKS ARE CUT IN THE FLOOR ALONG THAT LINE AND ARE CUT IN A HAND NOT THE SAME AS THE HAND OF THIS PAGE, AND THEY ARE CUT ON THE SIXTH SALTING OF THE ELEVENTH PAN-BOOK YEAR, WHICH IS A DATE THIS BOOK HAS NO MEANS OF ENTERING.
> THE LOW END OF THE PIT IS FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX FEET BELOW THE SILL STONE AT THE MOUTH, AND UNDER THE LOW END THERE IS A VOID OF DEPTH NOT YET FOUND, AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX IS THE FIGURE OF THE PIT AND NOT OF THE VOID.

Jory Vellan read the middle line twice and then gave the book to Ilyan Vester, who cannot read it, and did not know that he could not read it, and Ewan Troke read it out in a flat voice against the light of a lamp, twice, and the two readings were the same except that he said *not yet found* the first time and *not yet found* the second time, and the difference between those two is nothing and is the only thing he noticed.

“It says there is a void under the low end,” said Tarin Keel, “and it does not say how deep.”

“It says four hundred and six feet, and then it says that four hundred and six is the pit and not the void, and it has been in that book about a hundred and thirty years.” Ewan Troke put a finger on the board without touching the page. “So the answer to how deep is in that book, somewhere, and it is not on this page.”

“It is not on any page of that book,” said Jory Vellan. “I have had it in my office for nineteen years and I have had a man from Orison look at every page of it in the fourth year, and that is the only reason I can tell you it is not on any page, and I paid him a pound and four for eleven days and he did not find a void in it.”

---

The Seat’s list of drains is a different book in a different room and it is accurate in a different way, and it is kept by a different hand, and it is about eleven pages, and it is the only document in this basin that anybody has ever used to argue with a map.

It has a line for every drain in the Sallow basin and a turn against each one and a depth and a man, and there are forty-one lines on it and about nine of the forty-one are older than the first shaking, and the ninth line of them is the Anner overflow, and it is dated the ninth turn of the count, and the count runs from the first shaking and is a count of years and is the only year-count in this basin, and there is a note against that line in a different hand which says the turn was taken from the Anner’s own book because the Seat did not keep one.

Which is the whole of it. The Seat’s ninth line and the chain-book’s eleventh page are both right, and they cannot both be right, and the reason is on the face of both of them.

“The overflow was cut in the ninth turn,” said Ewan Troke, reading the Seat’s book out over the same lamp, “and the six marks in that floor are cut on the sixth salting of the eleventh pan-book year, and the ninth turn is three years back and the eleventh pan-book year is before there was a turn at all, and so the drain is the later thing, and there is no way whatever on earth to say how much later, and I have not been able to put one number into the other in nine years and I have not been able to this afternoon either.”

“Try it in this basin rather than in your head,” said Ilyan Vester, “because a man in his head is a bad instrument and the badness is not in the arithmetic. It is that you have had nothing to try it with. You have had a page and a list and they have been in two rooms in two towns for a hundred and thirty years and nine years, and nobody has ever put them on the same table, and that is the whole of the nine years.”

“Where does it come out.”

“Out of a book that is a hundred and thirty years old and a list that is a hundred and forty,” said Ilyan Vester. “Out of the second level, and out of the low end, and out of a line on a floor with six cuts in it, and at an angle of nine degrees and a quarter off the Anner’s own overflow heading, which is in the Seat’s ninth line and not in the chain-book, and at a depth of four hundred and six feet, which is the pit and not the void, so the true figure is more than four hundred and six and nobody can say by how much.”

He wrote it in the penny exercise book and then read it back, because the habit of reading a figure back in front of the people who are going to have to live in it is the only instrument he has ever had and it is not enough.

“Nine degrees and a quarter off the Anner’s own heading, at more than four hundred and six feet, in the floor along the eleventh chain of the oldest trench, in a straight line from the red stone to the low end, and the six cuts are in that line and are older than the chain-book page that found them, and the drain that was aimed along them is the later thing by a span that this basin has no instrument for naming, and the man who aimed it is not in either book and is not in this basin.”

---

Nobody went down.

That is not a decision anybody took. It is that there is a pipe at the low end and water at the low end and the last hundred and forty feet of it has been under to the roof since before Alo Vearn was seventeen, and the pump at the Anner is a pump that moves the water of the Sallow into the Sallow, and the reason the water is where it is is that the pit has been putting it there since the first shaking, and nobody has asked where it goes and it goes down a pipe into a chamber, and a chamber that four hundred and six feet is the floor of.

Ilyan Vester stood at the top of the last flight of steps with a lamp and looked down about sixty feet of it and could see the water, and the water was black and flat and had a slow movement in it that came and went, and it is the only moving thing in this volume that nobody has put a word in a book about.

“I am not going down four hundred feet,” he said, and it was the plainest sentence anybody had said in the Anner in about a year.

“You are not going down forty,” said Tarin Keel.

“No,” he said. “And the reason is not the water and it is not the steps.”

Nobody asked him what the reason was, and it was a room in another county four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and a thing he is carrying that somebody else is going to have to read, and a leg that has not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and the whole of those is one fact and it is a fact about a body and not about a rule, and he has not told anybody in this valley one word of it and there is nobody here he is going to tell.

Nobody stood in front of anything either. There was a lamp at the top of the steps and a chain on a wall and a man at the winch who is not part of this, and the position was a bearing and a depth and a page, and the page was in a book in a shed, and it is now in two books in two rooms in two towns about four miles apart, and neither of the two men who put it in them can read the other’s entry, and both of them can read their own.

The System did not put anything up. It has put five things in front of him in about a year and a half and it said nothing whatever about a void under a mine in a valley nobody had heard of, and it did not say it once, and that is the whole of what it had to say about the first missing position under the Red Basin, and it is not a thing that records and not a thing that warns, and there is no column in the index of any of it for an answer, and the answer, if there is one, is four hundred and six feet below a floor that nobody can get to and more than that by an unknown quantity.

At about the second light Jory Vellan ruled a line at the foot of page eleven and wrote the position on it, in his own hand, in a book belonging to the Anner, in a basin where the Seat of Sallowby has held every paper anybody has ever made in this matter for twelve years, and he wrote it because a man who is not going down the mine asked him to put a number down before the day was out, and nobody thanked him and he did not expect to be thanked, and he put it on the board and not in the book, because the book goes to Orison on a schedule and the board does not.
