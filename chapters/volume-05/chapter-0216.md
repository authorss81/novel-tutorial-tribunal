# Chapter 216: The Night

At the second light on the third day of the fifty-second week there were four men on the Anner dam and a barrow and a string, and there have been four men on that dam in the working years of every year since the bond was lodged, and the Anner is about four hundred strong in the working years and about sixty in the off ones, and the four were the four the pit could spare, and the pit had decided that at the second light and not at the closing of the water, and nobody asked the pit how it had decided.

The first line of the order says the Anner shall hold the dam on the Anner water to eleven feet above its own sill and keep the same until the slow is down, and the fourth of the four men had a string and a rule and cut the mark on the clay at eleven feet at about two hours after the first light, and the man with the barrow could not see it from where he was standing, and the man with the rule called it out loud, and the barrow went on when he called it and stopped when he did not.

That is what was done. There is no other sentence for what was done and the four of them from the valley were on the dam at ten past the second light, and one of the four stayed until the middle of the afternoon and one of the four went back down the ridge to see whether the road was still there, and none of them was asked to do anything, because there is nothing to be asked to do. The Anner is the party bearing the order. The party bearing the order bears it.

“Twenty loads of clay at ninepence the load is a hundred and eighty pence,” said the man with the rule, at about ten in the morning, to nobody, because he does his figures at the top of a bank. “Thirty loads of rubble at threepence is ninety pence. Twenty and thirty is two hundred and seventy pence, which is a pound two and six, out of the Anner’s book for one night. A year of it is three pounds five, and I have put a third of a year on a dam in a night, and I would like somebody to write that down and nobody ever writes anything down.”

Nobody wrote it down except a man of thirty-one, in a penny exercise book, on the basin side, under the day.

---

Downstream of the dam the causeway was shut and the high road was carrying nine carts a day with a morning and a half added each way, and at about the middle of the afternoon a carter came down off the ridge and stopped at the top of the bank and said one sentence and then stood there, and the sentence was that the Sallow was coming up backwards.

Not rising. Coming up backwards. That is the difference everybody in this valley can tell and nobody outside it can, and the difference is that a river that rises has somewhere to go and a river that comes up backwards has had its somewhere taken off it, and what had been taken off it was the causeway, and the causeway had been taken off it on the first day of the week by four lines on a wall in a market town.

The carters put the carts at the top of the bank and walked down. The order says the Anner bears the cost of the high road in every week of the closure and does not say a word about nine carters standing on a bank at the fourth light with nothing to do, and there was no column for that either.

---

The sluice at Hollow Sallow is thirteen feet above the bed and the frame in it is oak and the gate is four years old, and the woman who keeps it came out of the third house from the top at the second light and did what she has done eleven times a year for eleven years, which is to come out of the Sallow at the closing of the water and put the barrow down first, and she has done it eleven times in eleven years and the order that tells her to do it is three years old and was written by a man with no name on it.

The order of the Seat says the gate is to be open from the day the pans are turned at Coldbeck. The pans were turned on the ninth and the gate has been open since the sixth day of the fiftieth week, and it is open now, and it is as open as it will go, and the frame behind it that a joiner measured and charged five pounds eighteen for is standing behind the open gate doing nothing whatever.

The gate did its job. That is not a figure of speech and it is not a defence and it is not a small thing: the gate took the water off the Hollow and put it down the frame, and the water went down the frame at the speed of a man walking and the frame took it, and the frame is two feet higher than the lane, and the gate is four years old and it is a good gate and it was in the frame the whole time.

The job ended at the frame.

“The water is going over the top of it,” said Garrow Lee.

He was standing eight feet off with a rule in his hand and he had been in that lane since the second light because nobody had asked him to be and he had come because a girl of seventeen had told him at the shop where she worked that the water was up to the ninth post, and he had a rule in his hand at the fourth light on a rising night because that is what a joiner does with his hands.

“Go over the top,” said Ilyan Vester.

“It is going over the top. It has been going over the top for about the time it takes to say this and it is not a spill, it is a gate that has done everything it was built to do and there is nothing above it, and above it is four feet of bank and then the lane, and the lane is eleven feet below that.” Garrow Lee put the rule against the frame and took it away again. “You are going to want a number and I am going to give you the number and I have given you the same one three times this week and I am right every time, which has turned out to be a problem.”

---

The number was one subtraction and it took no time at all and it had been sitting in a penny exercise book in a cart shed since the last day of the fiftieth week.

The Anner's sill is the dam's own sill and it is the one every number in this basin goes back to. The frame at Hollow Sallow is nine feet above it, and the joiner said so on the seventh day of the last week and put a rule against the frame to show it.

Eleven feet above the Anner's sill is therefore two feet above the frame's sill. Two feet above the frame's sill is about the height of the floors of the houses on the upper half of that lane.

He said it out loud, in the dark, on a bank, with a lamp he was holding in his left hand, to about eleven people, and then he said it again because the first time nobody had heard the middle of it, and then he said the sentence that is the whole of the finding.

“The eleven feet is the dam's number and not the lane's.”

And then he went down the bank to the frame to look at it, and his leg was what his leg has been since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and it did not do anything on the way down a wet clay bank in the dark, and it is not going to do anything for as long as he is carrying the thing he is carrying, and he has not told one person in this valley what any of that is and there is nobody here he is going to tell.

Nobody said anything. Four pages are on a wall and a ruling has been made about a height without a sill, and the height is right and the sill is right and the two of them are nine feet apart, and nobody in the dark on that bank at the fourth light was in a position to do anything at all about it, and the shortest of the two numbers was the one the paper had used twice.

Nobody drowned. Nineteen houses had water in them by the time the light went properly and about a hundred and forty people were in nineteen houses, and it was a slow night in a wet year in a village of about four hundred and ten, and every door in that lane was a door that opened, and the girl of seventeen read the third line out loud to about twenty people standing in two feet of the Sallow in a lane, and the third line says that no person of Hollow Sallow is to be asked to leave any house by that order, and nobody was, and it was the only one of the four lines anybody in that lane could have used and it was useless to them and it is the reason they were standing in it.

That is not a mitigation and it is not going to be written as one. A village of four hundred and ten people on a slow night in a wet year, and the nineteen that are under water are the nineteen that are under water for the next four months, and the other three hundred and seventy went to bed.

---

Here is the page, and it is a page of the Seat's water-book, and it is the water-book that has been kept in four hands for a hundred years and that the mill hand has been writing on for eleven years because the Seat cannot make anybody buy a book.

> THE THIRD OF THE FIFTY-SECOND WEEK, THE GATE OPEN SINCE THE SIXTH DAY OF THE FIFTIETH AND NOT SHUT, AND NO ORDER OF THE SEAT HAS CHANGED IT. THE WATER AT THE FRAME AT THE CLOSING IS ABOVE THE TOP OF THE OAK AND GOING OVER.
> THE SAME DAY, IN THE MILL HAND, WHICH IS THE HAND THAT HAS WRITTEN EVERY LINE OF THAT KIND ON THAT BOOK FOR ELEVEN YEARS AND WHICH NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED HER ABOUT: I HAVE PUT THE SIDE DOWN ON THE FOURTH HOUSE AND IT IS UP TO THE SILL. THE NINTH HOUSE IS UNDER TO THE LAMP. I HAVE NO MORE LAMP OIL. NOBODY CAME.
> THERE IS NO HOUR AGAINST EITHER LINE AND NO COLUMN IN THIS BOOK FOR AN HOUR.

Nobody read that page out in the lane. Ewan Troke read it at the fourth light on the bank, twice, in a flat voice, and the two readings were the same except that he said *I have no more lamp oil* the first time and *I have no more lamp oil* the second time, and the difference between those two is nothing and is the only thing he noticed.

“The side is up to the sill of the fourth house,” he said, “and the ninth is under to the lamp. That is the two of the strongest houses in the lane, and the ninth one is hers, and she wrote it at the closing and then she went and had a look and she has not written anything since.”

---

The day went on the page at the third light and the number was one hundred and sixty-four, and he wrote it in the dark on the basin side with a stub of lead and his thumb for a rest, because there was no lamp oil left in that lane by the middle of the afternoon and the four of them had given their own away to two houses on the upper half of the lane.

At the fourth light on the fourth day of the week, in Sallowby, a clerk of the Seat of forty-four came into his one room, and a bag was not in the gap in the wall, and the man with the bag was not due for six weeks, and there was nothing to do about that, and he washed the pen.

At the closing of the water on the third day of the fifty-second week a woman of thirty-eight came up out of the Sallow in a lane with about two feet of it in, and she had a barrow, and she put the barrow down in the road, and there was one lamp in that village and it was in the house of the eleven that was third from the top, and she did not light it, and she stood in the road in the dark with her hands on the handles.
