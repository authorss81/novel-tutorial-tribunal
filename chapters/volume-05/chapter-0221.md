# Chapter 221: The Order The Nineteen Gave

The first day of a week is a day like any other in Hollow Sallow and nobody in the lane treated it as one, because a day with no water in it is a day a body in a valley of four hundred and ten people does not notice, and the Sallow had been out of the lane since the seventh day of the week before and the mud was the colour of the clay under it and had begun to be firm enough in the lane by the second hour.

There is a hole under a board in a cart shed behind the last two houses, and there is a stone on the board, and under the board on the mud of the cart shed floor there is a sheet with nineteen answers on it and no heading and no addresses and no date, and it had been there since the sixth day of the week before last.

A man of thirty-one went down the lane at the second light with a lamp he did not light, and took the sheet out, and put it in the inside of his coat against his chest, and stood in the middle of a cart shed that smelled of the thing it was a shed for, and read it by the light coming through the boards, and could not read a word of it.

He has been in this valley twenty days. The counting began on the second day of the fiftieth week and the fiftieth week is days seventy to seventy-six of a year that does not exist in this basin, and the number is a day since the second shaking, and he has written it at the head of the basin side of a penny exercise book every working day since the day he came, and it is a hundred and sixty-nine this morning, and he has said it out loud four times since he came and it is the fifth time and the reason is in his own hand and it is a new reason.

He wrote the hundred and sixty-nine at the top of the page and then he wrote a second line under it, and the second line is the reason, and the reason is that a man may put a day on his own act and may not put one on another person's speech.

The nineteen answers were given on the sixth day of the week before last, and he knows the day because he was standing in the road when they were given and the light was going, and he could put a hundred and sixty-seven on them in about four seconds, and he did not do it, and the reason is in the second line of the basin side of his own book and it is that a day is the only thing in this valley that is worth having and a person gives it away once and cannot have it back, and there are nineteen people in that lane who have not been asked for a day by anybody in their lives.

Nobody signed the sheet. She had not expected anybody to and she had put a stroke under every answer and never added the strokes up, and there were nineteen strokes and they were a record of nineteen doors and not of anything else.

---

They began at the top of the lane and not at the bottom, and that was a decision and the decision was made by four people on a bank in the time it takes to get a boot off and nobody argued with it, because nobody could see past the next house.

The first thing they established is that a list of what nineteen people need is not a list of work, and that is not a criticism of the nineteen or of the woman who took it down, it is a fact about the shape of the thing, and it took the morning.

Eighteen of the nineteen answers cannot be bought. That is the finding and it arrived at about the fourth hour and everybody who arrived at it had arrived at it in a different doorway. A dry floor is not a thing you can get. The bed out of the bottom room is a thing that is presently in a cart shed four miles up this hill along with about nine other people's beds and nobody has written down whose is whose. Boards are not a thing you can get. Salt out of the plaster is not a thing you can get and it is in three places in one house and in two places in another. Somebody to know whether the house is going to stand is not a thing you can get. Somebody to come and look at a wall before winter is not a thing you can get. The chair a dead woman sat in is not a thing you can get and is not going to be replaced. The seed in a cellar is a thing that either is or is not.

One of the nineteen is a thing you can get and it is the only one, and it is a roof, and it has a figure on it, because a man of fifty-six does roofs in this lane and on the eleven above it and about forty more and has done nine of the nineteen and has done four of them more than once, and he came over the top of the bank with a ladder on his shoulder and looked at the nineteen from the bank for about as long as it takes to drink a cup of something, and said a figure and would not split it.

“Nine pounds,” said Brann Slee. “I said it on the fourth day of that week and I am saying it now and I am not going to say it again this year. A roof wet through, taken off and put back, and the timber under it.”

And then somebody in that lane — it was a woman of about sixty and she is not named in this record because nothing else happened to her that week — said the sentence that turned the morning into something else, and she said it to nobody in particular and she said it while looking at the mud.

“The roof is second,” she said.

Nobody argued with her about it either, and the argument against her took about four seconds and then collapsed, and what it was, in her own words, at the top of the lane, with her hand out flat over the ground: “You put a roof on a wet floor and you have got a wet floor under a dry roof for four months, and when the frost is out of the ground you take that roof off again and you have paid a man of fifty-six nine pounds twice and you have got the same floor.”

That is the order of the work, and it is not anybody's order, and it was not on the sheet and it is not in any book in this basin, and it is now written at the top of a page in a cart shed in four lines in a hand that is not the hand of any of the people who said it. Bed. Boards. Salt out of the plaster. Roof before the frost. And the first two of those four are not things a person can buy and the third is a thing a person can only ask for and the fourth is the only one with a figure on it.

Which means the settlement for this valley, if there is going to be one, is a settlement about one line out of four, and about the order the other three are done in, and about the person who does the one line that has a figure on it.

Nobody in that lane said that out loud for about two days.

---

The twenty-first person in Hollow Sallow that week was in a doorway at the bottom of the lane and nobody had sent for him and nobody had asked him to come and nothing about that day was fixed before he was standing in it.

He is thirty-four. He has four people and a house at the bottom of the lane which is the second from the bottom and which had a door on its hinge on the morning of the counting and nothing else standing above the line the water got to, and he has been in that lane since he was nine, and his name is Coll Ferren, and he is one of the nineteen, and he is the fifth of the nineteen in the order a woman of about forty walked them.

“That is my door,” he said, when a woman of about forty put a hand on it. “It has been on that hinge since my father took it off and hung it. Everything else in that house is under the line and the salt is in the plaster in three places and I have had them out of one place.”

“You are the fifth,” she said, and wrote something against a stroke, which he could not see from the doorway and which was a stroke and not a figure, and then she looked up and said, “You have not asked what your house is going to cost to put right.”

“No,” said Coll Ferren. “I have been in that house since I was nine and I know what it is going to cost and it is me.”

Nobody argued with him about it and about nine people were on a bank in a lane at the second hour of the first day of the fifty-third week and one of the nine was a man of thirty-one with a book in his coat who had not taken it out, and not one of the nine said the thing that was in his own head, which is that a house is not a cost, it is a house, and that he had understood in about four minutes what eleven people had each understood separately in eleven houses, and that it took him a whole morning and a wall to say.

He was the one the sheet had been walked for. That is the part of it that had not been arranged: a list of nineteen answers with no addresses on it is not a list, it is nineteen opinions, and the only way to know which answer belongs to which door is to stand at the door, and the woman who took the answers down had said at the time that she was not going to read it back to anybody, and she was right and she was also the reason there was a morning's walking left in it.

They walked it in the second half of the day, in pairs, in the ordinary way, because about four hundred and ten people had places to be.

---

It cost a day and a half of one man, and the man was Coll Ferren, and Tarin Keel said the figure out loud on the bank at about the fifth hour for everybody to hear, because he counts things for a living and cannot help it and it is the only vice he has got.

“One man, a day and a half,” he said. “He is not on the Anner's wage and he is not on the Seat's and he has four people and he walked it because the answers are his neighbours'. That is the same condition as the woman who opens a gate at Hollow Sallow and gets four shillings and sixpence a week for sixteen weeks and is paid for a hundred and twelve days of a hundred and forty, and it is the whole subject of this valley, and nobody has ever once put it in a document.”

“Then do not put it in a document,” said a woman of about forty from the top of the lane, without turning round, and she was talking about the cart shed and not about him, and about nineteen strokes and a sheet with no heading on it.

And at the closing of the water on the first day of that week a man of thirty-one shut a penny exercise book with two halves in it and put it under a board with a stone on it, and the two halves do not have to be read together and are not to be read together, and on the basin side of it there are ten day-counts now and a second line under the eleventh one that says a man may put a day on his own act and may not put one on another person's speech, and there is no page anywhere with the rest of the sentence about whose memory it is on, and he is not going to write the rest of it, and nobody in that cart shed knew he had decided that.
