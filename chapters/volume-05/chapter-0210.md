# Chapter 210: A Person Doing Their Work

The four pages went on the wall of the Seat of Sallowby on the first day of the fifty-first week and they were still on the wall on the fourth of it, and in those four days the Anner shut the causeway and turned nine carts a day onto the high road, which adds a morning and a half each way, and put forty loads of clay and a hundred and forty of rubble on the Anner dam in about four days of ordinary work by four men and a barrow, and took three pounds and five of material for it out of the Anner’s own book without a murmur from anybody, and the wet workings of the Anner were dry for the first time since the second shaking.

Nobody in the Red Basin was ruined that week and no house in Hollow Sallow was wet and the only thing anybody could have said against the four pages was that nobody had asked the eleven, and nobody had asked the eleven, and the eleven did not know there were four pages until the second of the week, when a woman came down the lane with a barrow and a copy and put the copy on the doorpost of the third house from the top, which is hers, and said the words on the second line, and then went back up the hill.

“It is about a foot higher than it is at the Anner,” she is supposed to have said, and the man who was told about it said that that was a different number and not a different lie, and nobody in that village has agreed with either of them since and about half of them have not been asked.

The Sallow at Hollow Sallow was a foot and a bit in the bed on the fourth of the week and about four feet in the Hollow, and the season had four months to run, and the four men and the barrow had eleven feet of dam to keep, and eleven feet is a number.

---

There is a cart shed behind the last two houses of Hollow Sallow that nobody uses for carts, and a man of thirty-one slept in it for nine days and would have slept in it for a tenth, and on the fourth of the week he went into Sallowby and bought a penny exercise book of the man at the chandler’s shop, and the woman over the shop came down the outside stair to sell it for him because the man does not come up, and she charged him a penny and said the price before he asked for it, and he said thank you, and she said it was a penny and not a thank you, and he said that as well.

He had three books. Two were the ones he had carried from another county and a third was the one he had been tearing pages out of for about a year, and about a year of a page a day is three hundred and fifty pages, and the seventeenth of the old ones had four words in it that said he could not say which year it was, and he had not opened that book in front of anybody since a room four miles off with four witnesses in it.

The new one he divided down the middle of the first page with a straight line and a straightedge he had made out of a lath, because there were two numbers in this basin and he had promised himself in a lane four hundred miles away that he would not put one on top of the other, and the two are not the same number, and the reason they are not the same number is that one of them counts out of a grain year that runs in weeks and has fifty-two of them, and the other counts out of a water-book that was started at the second shaking and has been kept in four hands, and there is nothing whatever on earth to convert one of those into the other, and there is a man at Coldbeck who has said so out loud on a bank and has a board of notches to prove he has not invented it.

He wrote the heads in, and under the second of them, at the top of that half, he put the day in his own hand, and the day in this basin is a day out of a water-book and not a day of a grain year, and the number that went on the page is one hundred and fifty-eight, and it is the fifth time in ten days that a number out of that water-book has been put at the top of a page in this valley, and he has not told anybody in this basin what any of the five is.

*THE SHELF. A GRAIN YEAR OF FIFTY-TWO WEEKS, AND EVERY FIGURE BELOW IS A FIGURE IN ANOTHER COUNTY’S BOOKS AND IS NOT TO BE CONVERTED.*

*THE BASIN. DAYS OUT OF THE WATER-BOOK, FROM THE SECOND SHAKING, AND THE FIGURES ON THIS SIDE ARE NOT FIGURES OF THE OTHER SIDE.*

Then he wrote the first entry on the Shelf side, and it is a list, and every item in it is a thing that is open in a county he is nine days from and that he is carrying and is not going to be relieved of in this valley by anybody, least of all by himself.

*THE NOTICE OF FOUR SHEETS. FIVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE. NEVER PAID, AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE PAID BY ME AND IT IS NOT GOING TO BE FORGIVEN.*
*THE CLAIM OF A CARTER OF FIFTY-ONE, WITH A BOY, ELEVEN MILES OFF. FORTY-FIVE POUNDS TWO SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE. HE IS NOT OF THE DISTRICT, HE CANNOT BE PRODUCED, THERE IS NO FORM FOR ASKING, AND HE IS RIGHT.*
*A DEAD MAN IN THE FOURTH LINE OF A LIST OF TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY. HIS WIFE IS ON THE FOURTH LINE AND KNOWS SHE IS ON THE FOURTH LINE AND DOES NOT KNOW HE IS NOT ALIVE. NOT MINE TO TELL AND NOT MINE TO ASK ABOUT.*
*THE MINUTE OF THE FIRST OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH WEEK. ONE SHEET. THE ONLY COPY. IN A DRAWER FOUR MILES OFF WITH NOBODY’S NAME ON IT. NOT ASKED FOR IN A YEAR.*
*THE BLANK IN THE THIRD LINE. STILL A BLANK. I PUT IT THERE AND I COULD FILL IT AND I WILL NOT, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO ASK ME TO.*
*THE FOURTH HAND. STILL A STRANGER’S HAND THAT NOBODY HAS ASKED. A CLERK OF THIRTY-FOUR WROTE TO ASK ABOUT IT AND THE LETTER IS IN A BAG IN A ROOM IN SALLOWBY AND IT HAS NOT BEEN OPENED AND HE IS THIRTY-FOUR.*
*THE BOX OF ABOUT NINE HUNDRED AND FORTY FORMS. SIX MILES OFF. A MAN WITH A KEY, TWO DAYS OUT OF SEVEN, WHO CANNOT READ WHAT IS IN HIS OWN NINETEEN SHELVES. NOT FOUND. NOT GOING TO BE ASKED FOR THIS MONTH.*
*NESH ARRAD, THIRTY-NINE. FOUR CHILDREN, A STOVE, NO PAPER. PUT AT THE BOTTOM OF A REEVE’S OWN LIST IN A DIFFERENT INK WITH NO AUTHORITY ANYWHERE IN THAT COUNTY FOR A REEVE TO DO IT, AND ENTERED SIX MILES UP A ROAD THAT GOES NORTH IN A SECOND HAND AGAINST A FORM AT A SHILLING AND FOURPENCE. THE CLOCK IS RUNNING. I AM NOT PRINTING THE FIGURE HERE BECAUSE THE FIGURE IS A FIGURE OF THE OTHER SIDE OF THIS BOOK AND IT WOULD BE THE FIRST PLACE IN THIS VALLEY THAT THE TWO NUMBERS HAD BEEN IN THE SAME SENTENCE. NOBODY IN THAT COUNTY IS WATCHING IT AND NEITHER AM I.*
*FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIVE POUNDS A YEAR, ON NINE THOUSAND SEVEN HUNDRED, AT THE CLOSE OF THAT GRAIN YEAR. THE DISTRICT IS AT NOTHING. IT DOES NOT STOP.*

And then the last thing on that side of the book, which is the one he has written out twice in the year and has never shown anybody and has never answered.

*THE ACCOUNT’S OWN PAGE FOR THE DISTRICT OF RYEFOLD, SEVENTH LINE, IN A BUILDING SIX MILES UP A ROAD THAT GOES NORTH. A BURDEN ENTERED AGAINST NO. 63 OF ONE HUNDRED, WHICH IS A NUMBER UPON A SHEET AND NOT A NAME. THE PERSON WHO HAS TAKEN IT IS NOT A PARTY AND IS NOT OF THIS DISTRICT AND COULD NOT BE ENTERED BY NAME, THERE BEING NO LINE IN THAT BOOK FOR A PERSON WHO IS NOT A PARTY. THE BURDEN IS A YEAR OF ONE MAN’S MEMORY AND IT IS PAID. NO LINE IN THAT BOOK SAYS WHICH YEAR, AND THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR ONE, AND THE PERSON WHO PAID IT IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO COULD PUT ONE THERE.*
*THE NUMBER IS ON THE OUTSIDE OF A BUNDLE IN A YARD IN A SACKING BAG WITH A BIT OF STRING ON THE MOUTH OF IT. THE PERSON WHO WOULD KNOW IS STANDING IN THAT LANE. NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM.*
*I HAVE WRITTEN IT HERE INSTEAD OF ASKING, AND I AM NOT GOING TO ASK, AND THE REASON IS NOT BRAVERY AND IT IS NOT MERCY AND IT IS THAT THE LAST LANE I STOOD IN HAD NINE PEOPLE IN IT AND NINE OF THEM WATCHED SOMETHING HAPPEN TO A MAN IN IT FOR A WEEK AND NOT ONE OF THEM SAID ONE WORD ABOUT IT, AND THAT IS HOW THE THING IN THAT BOOK GOT THERE, AND I AM NOT THE FIRST MAN IN THE WORLD TO FIND OUT THAT*

He stopped in the middle of the last line and did not finish the sentence, and there is no page in that book with the rest of it on it, and it is the only sentence in two hundred and one days of this kind of book that is broken off, and it is broken off because he had been in this basin nine days and had not been asked a single question by anybody at all and had got used to it.

Nobody in this valley knows what is in that book and nobody in this valley is going to know what is in it, and it is in a cart shed behind two houses of Hollow Sallow in a valley four hundred miles from the sea, and the copy of the fourth sheet of the four pages is on the doorpost of the third house from the top, and there is a lamp in that house that is lit at about the time the light goes because the woman who lives there comes up out of the Sallow and does not light it until she has put the barrow down.

---

The last hour of the fourth day of the fifty-first week was spent in the Seat’s one room with the fire out, because the fire has not been lit in that room since before the first shaking, and about thirty people went in and out of it over four days which is more than has gone in and out of it in nine years, and none of them were there for a reason anybody had given them.

Hollis Nance put the kettle on the ring at the back, which he has a ring for and has never used, and a woman of about forty washed the pen and put it upside down on a board and turned it over again, and a man of twenty-two came in with a list of drains and put the list on the table and said that the ninth line wanted a turn against it and had not got one, and Hollis Nance said that he would put the ninth turn against it on the seventh day of that week, and the man said that his father had done it on the seventh day of every week for nine years, and Hollis Nance said that he had, and that he had not known there was a family, and that the turn should have been on the line in the fourth year and was not, and nobody said anything about that either.

There is a man who comes through the north of this basin about twice a year, and he does not trade and does not buy and does not take anything, and he was in the room for about ten minutes on the fourth of the week because he was going up past Coldbeck and he came in out of the weather, and he is not from this basin and nobody in the room had ever seen him before.

“There is a woman,” he said. “Or there was, two years ago, and maybe not now. She came through the north once and she did not ask anybody anything. She went along the ridges with a board and she went where there was no road, and there is no road on the north side of the Anner for eleven miles, and she came back over the flats and she went south, and I am told she did the same thing in the Briar March and nobody there would have her either, and that is the whole of what I know about her and I know it from two men who argued about whether she had said good evening.”

“What is her name,” said Hollis Nance.

“Venn,” said the man. “Somebody said Vaynn. I have never seen her myself and I would not know her in a lane.”

Nobody in that room had heard of her. There is no line about her in the Seat’s list of drains, and there is no line about her in the water-book, and she is not in any of the three rulings in the abstract, and there is no column anywhere in this basin for a person who comes through it and asks for nothing, because a person who asks for nothing has not made a matter and a person who has not made a matter does not exist in the instruments, and that is not a thing anybody in that room would have said out loud and one of them almost did.

Ilyan Vester did not say it and did not ask about her and is not going to meet her this month, and has written her name once on the basin side of a penny exercise book with two halves, and put a line under it, and did not write anything else about her, and the reason he wrote it down is that a thing you have heard once and said nothing about is a thing you will have heard about by the time it matters.

---

Nobody thanked anybody that week and nobody was thanked in the whole of the four days and there was nothing on anybody’s wall except four pages in one room, and the four pages are the only document this basin has produced in about a year, and they are four lines long, and all four of them are finished.

The dam at the Anner is eleven feet above a sill four miles up a river, and the four men and the barrow put the clay on it in the ordinary way, and the wet workings are dry, and the pit is working, and about eleven hundred tons of ochre and salt will go down that road this year at four pounds a ton at the mouth and eleven at Sallowby, and the difference of seven pounds a ton is seven thousand seven hundred pounds, and the Anner is bearing the high road and would rather not and is bearing it, and the Sallow is four feet in the Hollow with four months to run, and eleven houses are dry, and nobody is ruined, and a man of sixty-one drew his board through the eleventh pan and came back and looked at a hundred and fifty-seven notches and did not cut one.

The penny exercise book was shut at about the ninth light and put under a board at the back of the cart shed, and the last thing in it is four words and a line, and it is the last line of the book and it is the same line that is at the end of the other ones, and the two halves of the book do not have to be read together and are not to be read together, and there is a woman two streets from the chandler’s shop in Sallowby who owes eleven pence for a copy of a will and has said every market day for two years that she will pay it on this market, and there is a hand-book on a shelf in the Seat of Sallowby with a fourth column in the head of it and nothing under it at all, and the three columns beside that one are full and were full a long time ago, and there is a bundle on the same shelf tied with a knife-cut end, and there is a gate at Hollow Sallow that is four years old doing the work of a gate, and a frame behind it that a joiner has measured twice and charged five pounds eighteen for and cannot sell.

It is mine to write down and it is not mine to decide.

And the four men went down to the dam at the second light, and the clay went on in the same order it has gone on in for eleven years, and a woman came up out of the Sallow and put the barrow down and did not light the lamp until she had done it.
