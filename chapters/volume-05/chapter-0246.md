# Chapter 246: A Woman With A Board

She came into the market town of Sallowby on the fifth day of the fifty-sixth week on foot, from the north, with a board under her arm, and nobody in that room had met her and about four of the nine people in it had said her name out loud in the last month without knowing where they had got it.

Hollis Nance looked at the board under her arm and then at the sheet on the wall that he had copied on the day before, and he said the only thing he had to say, and he said it in the voice he says prices in.

“You are the woman Venn,” he said. “Somebody in this room has heard your name and could not tell you where he had it, and I have wanted to ask you about it for about a week and there has been no reason on any paper for me to ask you anything.”

“There is not now either,” said Sable Venn. “I am not here about the paper on your wall. I have not seen it and I am not going to look at it unless you tell me to, and I do not read other people’s records, and that is the whole of my trade and it is why nobody has ever been able to use me.”

She put the board on the table nine feet by fourteen and unrolled it, and it is four feet by three, and it is this valley.

---

It is not good drawing. It is a board and a piece of lead and a woman who does not do anything else and has never done anything else, and the Sallow is on it as a line and the pans are on it as four squares and the Anner is on it as a square with four small marks beside it, and the lane at Hollow Sallow is eleven small lines going down a slope, and there is a gate drawn in the line at the bottom of the frame, and the gate is drawn the right size against the houses.

She drew all of it in a fortnight, two years ago, on a board, and she asked nobody anything while she did it, and she got a great deal of it right and one thing wrong, and she said which one was wrong before anybody asked her.

“The height of the dam is wrong on here and I have left it wrong on purpose,” she said. “I could not get it and I would rather have a hole on a board than a figure I made up, and I have been over this ground twice since and I have not been able to do better, and I am telling you that in a room with all of you in it because about four would have found it in a month and been pleased.”

Nobody in that room was pleased. About four of them understood what she had just done and one of the four was a man of fifty-two.

---

“Here is what I am offering and it is not a favour and there is no condition on it,” she said. “That is your valley. I have not put a single person's name on it. There is no bench on it and no chamber and no order and no date and no height that I could not measure and nothing whatever on it that anybody has to do because a piece of paper says so. Take it or leave it. Put it on a wall. It has been in four other counties and in two of them it is in a kitchen and in one of them it is nailed up in a barn and in one of them a man threw it in a river, and I was not in that county again for three years and I did not go back and get it, and that is the whole of my standing in the world and I am telling you the whole of it in the first four minutes because you are about to decide whether I am somebody who has come to take something.”

Nobody in that room thought she had. That is worth saying because about four of them had spent a fortnight being afraid of a bench and had spent a good deal of it working out what a stranger with a board was.

“Then here is the offer,” she said, “and it is not mine, it is yours, and I have said it in three other counties and in one of them it worked. There is no rule. Nobody in this valley has to do anything, ever, because a room four hundred miles off says so. The mine is a mine. The water is water. If the mine wants your water it will have to ask you for it, and if you want to say no, you say no, and there is nobody anywhere that can make anybody do anything, and there is no paper that can be produced against you, and there is no file with your valley's name at the head of it, and there is no clerk in it that has written anything down about you, and if the mine takes your water in the night you will have a bad night and you will have no remedy and there will be nobody to complain to, and that is what you are buying.”

She said the whole of that in about two minutes and she did it twice, because a carter at the back asked her to say the bad part again.

---

And here is the thing that neither half of it was, and it is the only thing the morning needed.

A woman of thirty-eight stood up at the second hour and she did it before the man of thirty-one did and before the man of fifty-two did and she had been sitting on her hands for about an hour.

“I want to be left alone,” she said. “I have wanted it since the day before the first shaking. I have eleven years of a gate and nine shillings a week for the sixteen weeks the slow runs and three years of it that was owed to me and paid in an envelope in one week, and I have never once in all that time been able to make anybody do a thing for me by asking, and I have got a husband and a gate and a barrow and I am the only one who can be at that frame, and if there is no rule in this world then I am the only one who can be at that frame, and I have been that anyway.”

She put one hand on the table, which she does when she is at a frame and not when she is in a room, and about four people in that room had not seen her do that before.

“The pit pays four shillings and sixpence of my nine and the seat owes four shillings and sixpence, and that has been the arrangement since the second shaking,” she said. “Four shillings and sixpence a week for sixteen weeks is three pounds and twelve shillings a year. Sixteen weeks of it at fifty-four pence is eight hundred and sixty-four pence, and eight hundred and sixty-four pence is three pounds and twelve shillings, and the seat owes the same again and has owed it for three years and paid it in an envelope. And every one of those pence comes out of a piece of paper that somebody can be made to keep, and if the paper goes, then four shillings and sixpence a week is four shillings and sixpence a week that nobody in the world can make anybody pay, and I will open that gate every closing of the water for as long as I am able, and there will be sixteen weeks a year that I am not paid for that I am paid for now, for the rest of my life, and I have never had a wage for the other thirty-six and I know to the penny what those are like.”

She sat down, and then she said one more thing.

“And I would rather have it than not have it,” she said. “That is the whole of what I have come here to say and I have said it in front of a woman with a board and a room full of people and I would like it written down that I said it and not that I was asked.”

---

A recordwright of twenty-eight, who has stood at the back of everything in this basin for about six weeks and who has twice declined to put a hand to a piece of paper in it and been right both times, had a sentence ready and did not say it, and what was said instead was shorter and it was this,

What she said was shorter and it was this, and it is the only sentence anybody in that room needed, and it was said by the woman of about forty who washes the pen, and she has refused this basin twice in about a fortnight and been right twice.

“A rule-free valley cannot be given anything,” she said. “That is the whole of it and it is the only thing wrong with her and she has been in the room five minutes. If nobody may make anybody do anything, then nobody may make anybody do anything for us either, and a woman who wants nothing is not a woman who gets what she wants. She is a woman who gets no answer at all. And the honest name for that is a person who is not in the instruments, and I have been the only person in this room in that position since before the water and I wash the pen.”

Sable Venn looked at her for a while and then said, “Yes,” and did not argue with it and did not soften it, and about four people in that room noticed that a woman who had come four miles to make an offer in a room had accepted the first thing anybody had said to her, and one of the four was a man of thirty-one.

---

He could have said it, and he did not, and the reason is not a rule and is not a virtue and belongs in this record.

He had the sentence. He learned it in a room in another county, about a district of six hundred people, in front of people who had to be told, and what it cost him was a year of his own memory and he can say what the cost was in the same words he said twenty minutes before it and he cannot say which year, and the last time he said that out loud it was in a room and not in a lane. A man does not get to say a thing like that out loud a second time in a second county. It would be cheaper the second time than the first and it would be worth about half, and he had been in this basin forty days and he had not been asked one question by anybody in it except one, and a woman of about forty had said the thing he was not going to say, in her own mouth, and she had never paid a penny for knowing it.

So he said nothing, and the room had the sentence anyway, and the man of thirty-one who is the only person in this basin that anybody has ever said has any business in a room like that one sat in it and said nothing and was not asked about it and is not going to be asked about it by anybody in this valley.

---

What happened to the board was nothing, and that is the finding, and it took about four minutes.

The room kept the paper. Not her. The paper. The paper is not hers and she is not on it and it was not hers before she came in and it will not be hers when she goes, and a woman who came four miles to make an offer had it kept by the people in a room in a market town and was not thanked for it and did not expect to be and said so before anybody could.

She did not win. She did not lose. She has a board in a market town that four hundred and ten people have not looked at and about four of the nine in that room will remember to this day that she stood in it, and about six of the nine will not, and the two of those numbers are a count of people in a room and are not a count of anything else, and neither number is anybody's opinion of her.

And about four people in four hundred and ten in this valley are not frightened of a bench any more and about four hundred are, and the difference is not hers and was not hers and she was not asked about it and has not heard of it and will not hear of it.

---

At the closing of the water that evening, at the ninth door in a lane at the bottom of a hill, a woman of about thirty-one with two children had a pot on a fire behind her own house, and the salt was out of the plaster off the boards and the boards were dry enough to take it and she had a hundred and forty days of this weather in front of her and roofs after her own for as long as the weather lasts.

It was not the same pot. There is no pot in this valley that anybody has argued about this month, and it is a pot, and it was on the fire because there was a fire to be on, and nobody in that lane came up to ask her about a board that was nailed up in a market town four miles away, and she would have said she had not seen it.
