# Chapter 244: Who May Speak For Four Hundred And Ten People

On the third day of the fifty-sixth week this basin spent a day on a question that has no instrument, in a room with eleven seats in it, of which eight have never had anybody in them at all, and about nine people were in it, and the question is this.

If a bench of four, four hundred miles off, in a building with a yard in it, is going to be asked about its own record by somebody from this valley, who is the somebody?

There is no form for that question in this basin either, and everybody in the room knew there was not one inside about four minutes, and the four minutes were not spent on the answer.

---

Here is what the room had, in the way a room has things.

It had a man of thirty-one who has been in this valley for about forty days and has been asked not one question by anybody in it except one, about a thing he is carrying, and who has said out loud in four rooms since the second day of the fifty-first week that he is not a party to anything in this valley and that there is no form here for a man with nothing in his hands to have a question about a bench four hundred miles off. He was right about both of those and he has not once been asked to prove either of them.

It had a man of fifty-two who has begun paying a village this month and who sent a second paper into a gap in a wall yesterday out of his own company's money and wrote his own company's name on the outside of it, and a person who is paying is a party, and everybody in that room knew it and nobody said it, and it was said at about the fifth hour by a woman of about forty who washes the pen in that room and who is not paid by the seat or by the pit.

It had a man of thirty-four who is one of the hands on the only paper in this valley anybody can be on, and who cannot read it, and who keeps the copy of it in a lane at the bottom of a hill and reads it out in a road at the closing of the water about forty times a week and has never once had anybody check him.

And it had about nine people in a valley of four hundred and ten who are frightened, and being frightened is not a matter and the clerk of the Seat of forty-four has said that out loud twice inside ten days, and it is a thing with a shape, and the shape was put into the room on the second of this week by a man of fifty-six with a ladder on his shoulder and it is this.

“Nobody in this lane is a coward and nobody in this lane is a fool,” said Brann Slee. “It is that every one of us knows which house everybody in this valley lives in. If one of us goes up that road, then on the second morning every person in four hundred and ten knows it, and there is no way back from that, and the thing that comes back in about a month is two paragraphs or nothing, and then this lane has to live with the fact that it spent itself. And I would like somebody to have gone. That is all. I would like to not be the one.”

---

The woman of about forty put her hand on the middle of the table, not on the paper, and she said the thing that turned the question over, and it took her the length of a breath, and it is the third time this basin has been told something true by somebody who is not paid to tell it.

“You have all got the answer in your pocket and you are standing on it,” she said. “You keep asking who may speak. Nobody may speak. There is no man of this valley in this basin who may speak for four hundred and ten people and there has not been one since before the first shaking, and the reason you keep asking is that speaking for people is not a thing anybody in this room can do, and you would like one person to be able to do it so that the rest of you can stop.”

She put two fingers on the sheet that is on the wall, the one with six lines and eleven hands and a struck line under them with three hands on it that do not agree.

“That is the only paper in this valley anybody is on,” she said. “Eleven hands. And it is not a will and it is not a deed and it is not an order, it is a settlement of work and days and a wage, and it can be ended tonight by eleven people saying one sentence in a road at the closing of the water. Not by a clerk and not by a bench and not by anybody four hundred miles off. Eleven people, one sentence, and there is nothing whatever anybody can do to them for saying it, and I have watched them not say it every week since and I have watched the roofs go on every week since, and that is the whole of what standing in this valley is.”

---

That is where the third refusal came, and it was about a different thing, and she was right for the third time and she did it in one sentence.

A man of fifty-two asked her, in front of about nine people, whether she would go.

“No,” said the woman of about forty, who washes the pen. “Because if I go up that road, then there is nobody in that room in this market town who knows what a document is, and there is nobody in that room who can tell the difference between a bond and a copy of a bond, and this basin has nine years of a room like that and one clerk in it and that is exactly one too few already.”

She had refused this basin twice before in about a fortnight, both times in a lane and both times about a piece of paper, and the first was about a sheet under a board in a cart shed and the second was about a man's name at the foot of a thing, and on both of those occasions she was right inside the time it takes to cross a room and nobody had asked her for a reason.

The third time she was asked about herself and she gave the reason in one sentence and she was right again before anybody had finished sitting down, and afterwards she said in the same room, to the clerk, that those three were three different things and that anybody who put them together was doing what she had done to a man of thirty-four in a lane, which was telling him he was doing it wrong.

---

And here is the part of it that is the finding, and it came from a man of thirty-four who cannot read the paper it is about, and it came out of his mouth in a room without any preparation at all.

The clerk of the Seat of forty-four had the sheet down off the wall and flat on the table with his hand on the bottom of it, and Coll Ferren stood at the end of the table and looked at the space at the foot of it.

“That is the fourth thing I have been asked about this month,” he said, “and nobody has ever asked me the fifth one, and the fifth one is the only one that matters. Is there a name on it.”

“There is no name on it,” said Hollis Nance. “And there is no dash on it either, and I have said in this room that I would not put a dash there, and I will say it again in this room, and a dash is a thing a person puts where a name used to be so that a later person thinks nothing was ever there, and something was there, and it was a man of thirty-one who took it off in a lane in about eleven seconds because a woman of forty was right and he said so out loud in front of eleven people.”

“Then a paper nobody is on cannot be asked anybody anything,” said Coll Ferren. “I have not been able to read that sheet since the day it came down the hill and I read it out in a road about forty times a week and nobody has ever checked one word of it, and I know why nobody checks it, and it is not because I read it well. It is because there is nobody on it to be asked whether I read it right. I have been the copy for a month and the copy has no person on it and neither has the original and that is the whole of what is wrong with this valley and I have not got a better sentence than that at seven in the evening.”

Nobody in that room had a better sentence than that at seven in the evening.

---

What was decided in that room on the third of the fifty-sixth week was a thing about who is asked, and not about who goes.

The decision is that the standing of this valley is the settlement, and that the settlement is eleven people, and that they can be instructed, because eleven people can un-instruct themselves in a road with one sentence and nothing done to them, and that therefore the only people in four hundred and ten who can be sent are people the eleven have sent.

And the man of thirty-one is not one of the eleven and has said so out loud in a lane in front of them and did not put himself forward and was not put forward, and he said, when it was put to him in a room with nine people in it, that he would carry a thing for anybody in this valley for as long as they would let him and that he could not be the one who instructed them, and that a man who is not on a paper is the last man who should choose who is on it.

And the man of thirty-four, who cannot read the paper, said he would go if the eleven asked him and not otherwise.

And the nine people who are frightened were not asked, and that is the first of two things nobody in that room was going to get past, and the second one was that the eleven had not been asked anything yet, and they were in eleven houses, and about nine of them were in a lane, and nobody in that room had been to any of them.
