# Chapter 240: Shut, And Going To Be Opened

The column is shut. That is what happened in ten days in a valley of about four hundred and ten people, and it is the whole of what happened, and the whole of what has happened about it in ten days is that four people in this basin now know that a page exists four hundred miles off with four lines in it that are all about this valley's water, and that nobody has asked for it, and that nobody has opened it, and that nobody has been asked about it, and that a man of thirty-one was in a room with the printed sheet on the table on the sixth of the fifty-fourth week and did not touch it, and that no person in this basin has asked him when he is going to.

The settlement is on the wall of the water-seat of Sallowby with no name at the foot of it and no height in it, and there is a seventh line on the copy in a lane that is not on the paper in the room, and the seventh line has no figure on it and is not going to get one, and a clerk of forty-four said out loud on the seventh of the fifty-third week that he would not put a dash where a name used to be, because a dash is a thing people put where a name used to be so that a later person thinks nothing was ever there, and he has said so once and it is not going to be said again and nobody has asked him to say it again.

The route that does not go through Orison is in chalk on the side of a cart at Sallowby and in about nine mouths and there is no money behind it, and a board went up against the outside of a cart shed on the fifth of that week with a sum on it that is a hundred and seventy-eight pounds, thirteen shillings and fourpence over the top of the whole of the trade, and the board is going in about a month and a man of thirty-one knows that and put it up anyway.

A cart went over the shoulder on the second of that week for a good reason and was a day and a half late, and nobody in this basin can now say whether the mine is paying for eight carts a day or nine, and the man who can say is the man who wrote both numbers down in a book a hundred and thirty years old.

The Anner is to be asked again in one year, and asked to be asked, and the asking is a year off, and the man who is going to be asked wrote the word *asked* in ochre dust in his own hand on a line of a piece of paper in a room in a market town and does not know yet that a piece of paper with a good name behind it is what the two of them are going to have to talk about.

Nobody in this valley has forgiven anybody anything, and nobody in this valley has been forgiven for anything, and about nine people in it are afraid of a bench of four they have never met.

---

Nobody turned the year. The Shelf's grain year keeps its own weeks four hundred miles off in a county where a woman of thirty-nine has four children and a stove and no paper, and a notice of four sheets for five shillings and fourpence is unpaid and is not going to be forgiven, and a claim of forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence to a carter of fifty-one with a boy eleven miles off is not producible and has no form and he is right, and a dead man in the fourth line of a list of two hundred and forty is on the fourth line and his wife is on the fourth line and knows she is on it, and ninety-one numbered sheets are in a sacking bag in a yard and about nine hundred doors are behind them and there is no one hundredth person, and a box of about nine hundred and forty forms is six miles off behind a man with a key who is there two days out of seven, and a minute of the first of the forty-eighth week is one sheet and the only copy and nobody's name is on it, and four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year on nine thousand seven hundred falls due at the close of that grain year and the district of Ryefold is at nothing and it does not stop.

None of that came out of a valley in ten days and not one item on it is a thing anybody in this basin could act on and not one item on it is a thing that has changed, and the man carrying the first of them has carried all of them for about a month and has told nobody in this valley a word about any of it, and the second half of the book in his coat was not opened once in ten days.

And the new question — whose memory is missing from the account that records his payment — is on a page of the other half of a book in a cart shed behind two houses, and this valley counts its days out of a water-book out of the second shaking and is at a hundred and eighty-eight, and nobody in Hollow Sallow has ever heard of the question, and he is not going to ask, and he has not been asked, and he is not relieved of the not being asked and nobody in this basin is going to relieve him of it.

---

What came up in that ten days, in the flat white nothing that has put things in front of him in about a year and a half, was nothing at all.

It has put five things in front of him in this basin and it has not said one word about a shut column, and it has not said one word about a road, and it has not said one word about a good name, and it has not said one word about a name that goes round a valley on about four mouths, and it did not say one word on the sixth of the last week when the man who could have opened a printed sheet on a table nine feet by fourteen put his hand on the table about a foot from it and took it away again, and about a man of thirty-one it is true that he has stopped expecting it, and it is not true that he told anybody, and there is no instrument in this basin for anybody to notice it in him.

And nobody went down. The position is where it was identified in the fifty-first week, a bearing and a depth and a page number, and the page is in a book in a shed and in a second book in a room four miles off, and the two men who put it in them cannot read each other's entry and both of them can read their own.

Four hundred and six feet is the pit and not the void, and the void is more than that by a quantity nobody in this basin has an instrument for naming, and the sill at the Anner is nine feet above the frame at Hollow Sallow and there are two numbers about four hundred and six feet and nine feet that have never once been in the same sentence in this valley. And the last hundred and forty feet of the water at the low end of the pit has been under to the roof since before a woman of twenty-six was seventeen, and the pump that puts it there has been running since the first shaking, and nobody asked where it goes and it goes down a pipe.

Nobody in this valley has said any of that out loud this week, and nobody in this valley has thought about it this week, and a man of twenty-six is at the pit and does not know that a sentence about her is in a book six miles off that somebody has never been in this basin to write.

---

And here is the last thing that happened in the fifty-fifth week of the Red Basin, and it is not a ruling and it is not a discovery and it is not an answer.

A woman of thirty-eight opened a gate at Hollow Sallow at the closing of the water on the sixth day of that week, and the gate is five pounds and eighteen shillings better than it was in the fourth year, and the height held at eleven feet above the Anner's own sill, and four men and a barrow went down to the dam at the second light and the clay went on in the same order it has gone on in for eleven years, and a man of fifty-six with a ladder on his shoulder came down off a roof at the sixth hour and put his boot on, and a girl of seventeen was paid a penny in a jar at the closing of the water and wrote the day at the top of a fresh page, and a man of thirty-four read six lines and a struck line and a seventh line with no figure on it out loud in a road at the bottom of a lane to about forty people who had come to find out what had been done that day, and it was read out and nobody stopped him and it is a different paper every time it is read out and there is nobody in this valley who can check it.

And a clerk of forty-four put a chair back at a table in a room with eleven seats in it, of which eight have never had anybody in them at all, and washed the pen and turned it upside down on a board and turned it over again.

And he said the plainest sentence anybody said that week, and he said it to the shelf, and nobody answered it and nobody was asked.

“Somebody is going to open that column,” said Hollis Nance, “and it is not going to be me.”

And a bag with nine printed sheets and a return folded twice at the bottom of it is on a shelf under the window in a room with a press in it, and the press has a bond of forty pounds in it and a key in a clerk's coat, and neither of them has been asked about anything by anybody in this basin, and the bag is not in the press because a printed sheet is not owed to anybody, and a bundle with a knife-cut end on it is on the same shelf, and it has been on that shelf since the second of the fifty-first week, and it has not been opened.

A gate was opened at the closing of the water. A chair was put back at a table. A bag went on a shelf that nobody has asked about.
