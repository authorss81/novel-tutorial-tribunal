# Chapter 250: An Exit Review, And A Position Nobody Stood In Front Of

The second day of the fifty-seventh week is the last day anything is written down about the Red Basin, and it did not end with anything, and the not-ending is the point and it is not a cliff dressed up as a resolution, because a cliff is a thing that happens to somebody and what happened on the second of this week was that about four hundred and ten people in a valley went on doing what they were doing at the ordinary hour and got on with it.

Here is what is standing, and it is standing on the second of this week and it will be standing on the day anybody in four hundred and ten brings that sheet into that room.

---

A revocable charter is on the wall of the water-seat of Sallowby, in one hand, with no name on it and no dash on it, and it says in its own first line that it can be ended, and any person of this valley may go from it at any time and shall lose nothing by going but the charter, and it is reviewed at the closing of the water one year after the day written at the head of the page in the water-book for that day, by any person of this valley who brings it.

A third document is in three places — in the press of that room, in a lane at the bottom of a hill in the hands of a man of thirty-four who cannot read it, and on a table under a chain-book at the Anner Pit — and it is one sheet, and it names two sills, and it puts eleven feet above each of them, and it writes down that the two lines are four feet apart and that nobody in this valley has agreed to the difference and nobody has refused it.

The eleven feet is in that document and it is not on the settlement, and it is not going to be. The settlement is eleven people's and the height is the one thing about it that eleven people do not agree on, and there are three hands on a struck line under it and they do not agree, and a man who is not one of the eleven put a pencil in a coat in the fifty-fourth week and did not put the figure in and he was right.

The column is open. A page of a printed index with a heading of two words at the head of a fourth column was opened on a table nine feet by fourteen in a room with eleven seats in it on the fourth of the fifty-sixth week, by a man of thirty-one, with no authority, out loud, in front of about nine people, and four of those four lines are now on a wall a hand's breadth from four pages that are still correct on their face, and one of the four lines is a rule this basin has had on a wall in its own words since before the water.

The good name of four people four hundred miles off has been spent in public and by him and it cannot be spent twice, and nobody thanked him, and nobody was relieved, and about four people out of a valley of four hundred and ten know what it was.

The mine is a mine. It is bound in advance to a figure of a thousand and forty tons written down in a city four years ago by a man who has never been in this valley, and it is to have what goes out of its gate and what comes in written down four times a year in a book the seat keeps, at four days a year of a pitman's time, which is eighty pence, which is six shillings and eightpence, and it gave up the right to say in a room in a year that the chamber got this valley wrong, and it did that on purpose, out loud, and would like it written that it gave it up and not that it was taken.

The wage is nine shillings a week for the sixteen weeks the slow runs and the three years that were owed were paid in one week in an envelope, and if the charter is ended the wage is ended with it, and a woman of thirty-eight said that in a room before she was asked and would like it written that she said it before.

---

And the position beneath this basin, which is not a thing anybody in this valley can do anything about and which is here because it is here.

It is a bearing, and a depth, and a page number. It is in a book in an office at the Anner Pit in a hand that is not the under-manager's, and it is in a second book in a room four miles off in a hand that is not the clerk's, and the two men who put it in them cannot read one another's entry and both of them can read their own. It was identified in the fifty-first week off a run of trench measurements and six marks and a gap, and it has not moved since and it is not going to move because of anything that happened in a month of roofs and water and a printed page.

The way down is a chamber, and there is water in the chamber, and there has been water in it since before the second shaking, and the pit has been pumping into it since the first one.

Four hundred and six feet is the pit and is not the void. The void is more than that by a quantity nobody in this basin has an instrument for naming, and the last hundred and forty feet of the water at the low end of the pit has been under to the roof since before a woman of twenty-six was seventeen, and her name is Alo Vearn and she is twenty-six and she is at the pit this week, and she does not know that a sentence about her is in a book six miles off that nobody has ever been in this basin to write.

Nobody went down. That is the plainest sentence anybody said that week and it was said on a bank above a lane by a man of thirty-four who has been in that lane since he was nine and who has never once claimed to be able to do anything, and nobody answered it, and it is the last thing this valley did about the position and it is the whole of it.

---

Here is the rest of it, and it is four hundred miles off, and none of it came out of this valley in a month and none of it stopped.

A man of thirty-one walked into this valley on the second day of the fiftieth week with a thing in his hands that he had picked up four hundred miles off, and he carried it out of it on the second of the fifty-seventh, and it went up the Shelf road on a cart with a roof on it, and it is a matter for a district of six hundred people and it is not a claim and a line that is not a claim is not a thing that comes off, and it was off nothing, and it is off nothing now, and the leg went four hundred miles off before he got to the last stile and it has not come back and he has not told one person in this valley a word about it.

He took the penny exercise book out from under the board with a stone on it in a cart shed behind two houses on the morning of the second of this week, at about the sixth hour, and he had to put his weight on the wall to get at it, and that is a fact about a wall and not a discovery and he did not make anything of it and has not made anything of it since.

On the basin side of that book, at the head of a fresh page, in his own hand, is one hundred and ninety-eight days since the second shaking, which is the eighteenth figure of that kind he has put at the head of a page in this valley and is a count of figures and is not a count of anything else.

There is nothing under it but the reason, and there has not been an answer under any figure in that book since a page in the fifty-second week, and the whole of what he has written at the head of a page in forty days in this valley is a number, and a woman of thirty-eight put a figure in chalk on a post with a rule, and a girl of seventeen wrote a day in her own hand, and a man of thirty-four reads a sheet out loud that he cannot read, and a man of fifty-six is four days on a roof with a great many roofs after that one, and a man of fifty-two carried clay. None of the five of them is on a piece of paper that anybody in this basin can produce, and every one of them is the reason about four hundred and ten people in a hollow are not standing in a wet street tonight, and he has not written any of that down anywhere, and the reason is that a man who was in this basin for forty days and who is not a person of it has not got the standing to say what a valley has done, and he has not been given it, and he is not asking.

The second half of that book is not the basin's half and he did not open it. It is a county that keeps its own weeks and it has a figure in it of four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year on nine thousand seven hundred, and nine thousand seven hundred at twelve pence the pound is a hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred pence, which is four hundred and eighty-five pounds, and it falls due at the close of that grain year and it does not stop, and the district of Ryefold is at nothing on it, and it is in his own hand on the other side of a board in a cart shed behind two houses and it is in nobody's mouth in this valley and it is not going to be.

A notice of five shillings and fourpence is not paid. A claim of forty-five pounds, two shillings and eightpence to a carter of fifty-one with a boy, eleven miles off, is not producible, has no form, and he is right. There are ninety-one numbered sheets in a sacking bag in a yard and about nine hundred doors and no one hundredth person. There is a box of about nine hundred and forty forms that a man with a key six miles off is in two days out of seven. There is a minute of the first of the forty-eighth week, one sheet, the only copy in the world, in a drawer in a building four miles off, with nobody's name on it. There is a dead man in the fourth line of a list of two hundred and forty and there is a woman in that district called Nesh Arrad, who is thirty-nine and came into it with four children and a stove and no paper at all, and a sheet of hers went in on the second of the forty-seventh week of a year of theirs, and a clock that is running there is still running, and nobody in that county is watching it, and he is going to hand it on in a book with two halves in it and he is not going to stop it and he is not going to be the one who asks about it.

And a name went four miles in about two years on about four mouths with no document under it, and a woman with a board stood in a room in this market town on the fifth of this week and was right about most of what she put on the board and wrong about one thing she left wrong on purpose, and about four people in that room will remember to the end of their days that she was in it, and about six will not, and none of the four can do anything whatever about her, and she is not on any paper in this valley and she did not ask to be.

---

And the last thing that happened in the Red Basin on the second day of the fifty-seventh week is this.

At the second light four men and a barrow went down to the dam at the Anner, and the clay went on forty loads at a time in the order it has gone on in for eleven years, and the height held at eleven feet above the Anner's own sill, which is a number that is also in three copies in three places, and a man of fifty-two put three of the four barrows himself.

At the closing of the water a woman of thirty-eight opened a gate at Hollow Sallow, which is five pounds and eighteen shillings better than it was in the fourth year, and the water is four feet and something in the bed and falling, and on the north post of the frame there is a dark line with a figure in chalk under it and a day in chalk over it in a hand of her own, and she looked at it going past and did not stop, because she opens that gate a hundred and twelve times a year and she has never once stopped to look at a mark.

A clerk of forty-four put a chair back at a table in a room with eleven seats in it, of which eight have never had anybody in them at all, and washed the pen and turned it upside down on a board and turned it over again, and did not say anything to the shelf this time.

A man of fifty-six with a ladder on his shoulder came down off a roof at the sixth hour and put his boot on.

A gate was opened at the closing of the water. The chair went back against the table leg. A barrow went down a bank. And at the second light four men and a barrow went down to the dam.
