# Chapter 218: The Person The Order Did Not Name

The water turned in the night of the fourth day into the fifth and was falling about a foot a day, and by the second hour of the fifth of that week there were nine people in the Seat's one room at Sallowby, and four of the nine had been in that room on the day the four pages went on the wall, and one of the nine was a joiner who had come on his own and one was a girl of seventeen who had come because she had read a thing out loud five times and could not get it out of her head.

There was one thing in that room on the fifth day of the week that nobody had said out loud yet, and the man of thirty-one got to it in about four minutes, and it was not the thing anybody expected him to get to.

“They should come off the wall,” he said.

Nobody spoke.

“I am not going to take them down. I have no authority and neither has anybody in this room, and I am not a party to that document and I never was, and there is no form in this basin for a man with nothing in his hands to have a wall in front of him. But I would like somebody in here to say out loud that they should come off, and then I would like somebody else to say out loud why they should not, and I have never in my life wanted a thing more carefully than I want to be wrong about this.”

“Then they should not come off,” said Hollis Nance, and he said it in about four seconds, which is faster than he says anything.

“Why.”

“Because they are the only document this basin has produced in about a year.” He put both hands on the table, and the keys to the only press in the room were in the inside pocket of his coat where they live, and he did not take them out and did not touch them. “That press is the only thing in this room that has ever been asked to remember anything. Not a person. Not a bench. A press. And the four pages are in it, and they are the only four pages in it, and if they come off that wall and go in a drawer, then this valley has nothing at all, and a valley with nothing at all is a valley where the next person who comes up a road with a piece of paper is the first person anybody here has ever seen. You have been here fifteen days. I have been here nine years, and I have had two bundles in that time and had not read a word of either, and I did not open the third one either until you were stood in my room with a leg.”

---

“The second matter has not come back,” he went on. “The first one went in on the third of the week before last and it has not come back and it cannot come back before nine days and nine days off that is this morning, and it has not come back this morning, and a matter that has not come back has not been refused, and that is the second worse thing about it. And you are all going to want me to say that the chamber at Orison is negligent, and I am not going to, because there are about thirty clerks in a building with a yard in that city and every one of them is somebody's clerk and every one of them did a job properly and not one of them is going to do a ninth day's work for a basin that has not sent anybody to ask. That is not villainy. That is a distance. A distance is harder to be angry at than a man and it is exactly as expensive.”

He turned the hand-book round on the table without opening it and put his hand flat on the cover of it, and everybody in that room knew what was in the back of it and nobody said anything about it, and the reason nobody said anything is that the fourth column in the head of that book is headed with a word that means not sent, and there is nothing under it at all, and about four hundred and sixty entries are in the three columns beside it.

“I am not going to ask about that column,” said Hollis Nance. “I have never asked about it. I have kept that book up to date on the last working day of every fourth week for nine years and I have never once asked what is in a column of my own index, and I would like that said out loud in this room today, because in about eleven days one of you is going to stand in here and ask me about it and I want it on the record that I named it first and did not ask.”

Nobody said anything about that either.

---

The under-manager said what he had come to say, and it took about four minutes, and every word of it was true.

“The height is right,” said Jory Vellan. “The dam is right. The party is right. The day is a day and the day is early and I would have done it eleven days early without the paper, because I have been doing it eleven days early since I was thirty-three. You have got a pit holding a dam to a figure exactly, at a cost of two pounds six of material in a night and about four men who cannot be spared, and the wet workings are dry, and there is not a single thing wrong with any of that, and I am not going to have a room in this valley say otherwise this morning.”

“Nothing is wrong with it,” said Ilyan Vester. “That is the difficulty, and I have not said it in a room until today, and I am saying it now, and I want it written down that I said it.”

He stood up, which nobody in that room had seen him do.

“There is a sentence I said once, in a room, in another county, about a category rather than about a place, and I am not going to say it again and I am not going to explain it and there is nobody in here who is going to ask me to. Here is the part of it that is about this wall.” He put his hand out, not touching the paper, about a foot from it. “A rule that names no bystander is carried by the nearest body with hands.” The four lines name a dam and a height and a day and the party that pays. They do not name the person the water arrives on. So the person the water arrived on was found by the order, the same way a man is found by an order that names a street, and the finding was in a lane in eleven hours, and the finding was made by a woman who opens a gate for four shillings and sixpence a week.”

He sat down.

Nobody asked him to explain it. That is in this record because it is the thing that did not happen, and the reason it did not happen is that there are about thirty people alive who have watched a man explain a thing he found once before into a room that had no use for it, and every one of them was in that room at the time, and not one of them has forgotten it, and nobody in the Seat's one room on the fifth day of the fifty-second week asked a man of thirty-one for one more sentence.

---

They argued about it for two hours and the argument was not about whether the four lines were right.

The rightness was never in question. The clerk of the Seat had written four complete lines in a fair hand and had said out loud on the first morning of the business that there was no hole in any of them, and there was no hole in any of them, and nine days later the water had gone over a frame nine feet below a number and into nineteen houses, and there was no hole in the order, and the order is on a wall in a market town and it is correct on its face, and the woman who washed the pen in that room said so, and she said it in the plainest sentence anybody said that day.

“If it comes off that wall, then in three months’ time there is a woman in this lane saying a number went out of here and nobody in Sallowby has ever heard of it,” she said, “and she will be right, and there will be nothing on any wall in this basin to say she is wrong, and I have been in this room nine years and I have watched two things get taken out of a room in this town and neither of them was taken out by a thief.”

“Then it stays,” said Ilyan Vester.

“It stays, and it stays on the wall, and it stays with a hundred and seventy-one pounds of flood outside it and no remedy on it at all, and I want to be in this room when somebody comes in here in six weeks and asks what the four pages were for.” She turned the board over on the desk, which she does at the end of a week, and it was not the end of a week. “Somebody will come. They always come. There has been a woman in this village every week since the first of the week and there will be one in six weeks, and the wall is the only place she can put her hand on anything.”

Nobody in that room took the four pages off the wall. That is what happened on the fifth day of the week in the Seat's one room at Sallowby, and it was not a victory and it was not a defeat and it was not a ruling, and there is no minute of it anywhere, because the Seat keeps no minutes of its own meetings, which is a thing this record has to say for the fourth time and will have to keep saying.

---

The one other thing in that room on the fifth of the week had to do with a man of thirty-one and it is the only line of that morning that is about him and not about a wall.

His name is not on the four pages. It has never been on the four pages. It is not on the bond, and it is not on the standing order of the sluice, and it is not on the marl-strip deed, and it is not in the chain-book and it is not in the hand-book with the fourth column in the head of it, and there is no line in this valley carrying his name on anything at all, and that is the one thing in this valley that has not gone wrong, and he is the only person in that room who noticed it, and he did not say it, and he wrote it in a penny exercise book in a cart shed behind two houses of Hollow Sallow that night in his own hand, and he wrote beside it that it was not a relief, and that is a true thing he wrote and the only true thing anybody wrote that day about himself.

And nobody in that room said so either. That is on the record and it is worth putting down, because nine people were in it and about six of them knew it, and the reason not one of them said a word is that saying it would have been a kindness, and this basin has never in nine years done anybody a kindness in a room, and it was not going to start on the fifth of a week.

---

At the closing of the water on the fifth day of the fifty-second week the Sallow at the Anner was three feet in the bed and falling, and the four men and the barrow were on the dam, and the clay was going on in the same order it has gone on in for eleven years, and it was eleven feet to the sill.

Four miles off, on a shelf of flat ground above Coldbeck, a man of sixty-one came off the far side of the eleventh pan with a board in his hands and set it down against a stone, and the salt had come out of the sixth salting and the pans were in and the year had turned over, and there were a hundred and sixty-five notches in the upper edge of a board that has been cut one notch a day for thirty years, and he looked at the notches and did not cut one, because the day was not over, and nobody on the north side of the flats that evening knew that anybody in the lane at Hollow Sallow had spent the whole of that day in a room arguing about a word on a wall.
