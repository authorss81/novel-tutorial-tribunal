# Chapter 948: Eight Inches And About Eighty Of Those Ninety Under, And The Fever Ninety-Five Weeks Old, And A Woman Would Not Let A Figure Become A Habit

Fifth day of the hundred and fifty-seventh week. His six hundred and ninety-eighth morning. Five hundred and forty-eight days after the settlement. The fever ninety-five weeks old. Five hundred and two days since the division. Four hundred and forty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

Eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then gave the fourth thing, which is the fever, because this is a morning when the fever is a whole number of weeks and he says it in the same words and does not put a figure of days beside it and does not put it beside the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and this morning has a whole figure of weeks and nothing to put it beside, and says so before anybody asks.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under, on the fifth day of the hundred and fifty-seventh week, and it is a Saturday.”

“And the fever is ninety-five weeks old. That is all of it and there is no figure of days with it and there is not going to be one. It comes round every seventh morning like that and I say it the same way, and it goes in beside the three off the stone and nowhere else, and that is not a rule, it is where it sits.” He set the broom against the wall.

“A man gave a figure out loud on this step yesterday. I am not going to agree with him about it and I am not going to disagree with him about it, and I have got the same thing to say about a whole number of weeks that I have got about what he said on Friday. Both of them are a figure of a thing. Neither of them is a figure of anybody. And neither of them goes on this stone, and that is the last of it.”

Then the woman with the pound came down off the turn with the gate still standing against her own wall behind her and said that she had come to take a figure off that step that had been put there on Friday, and that she was going to do it in the open, and that nobody was to stop her.

Ruth Ansell keeps a pound two hundred yards up that cart road before the turn, has a book in her own hand with a day in it, put a mark in that book on the second day of the hundred and fifty-fifth week that meant she was in her own yard, has decided to stop keeping the pound, took the gate off its hinges on the sixth day of the hundred and fifty-sixth week, and this morning argued against a figure of asking.

“That man put a figure on your step yesterday and every one of you has looked at it since, including him, and I want to say the thing that is wrong with it and then I am going to go home and shut nothing.”

“Nothing is shut.”

“That is not a joke and it is not nothing and you will find out in a week whether it was.” She stood at the low end of the step with her arms folded. “A figure of how many times a man put his hand up is a figure of how hard a man worked. He has been up this bank three weeks wanting to be somebody and he has got a number and the number says how often he reached for it.”

She looked at the man of thirty-one and he looked at the eight inches.

“That is all it is. It is a figure of a habit. And a habit is not a record. A habit is a wall with him on the front of it and he has been standing there for three weeks and now he has measured it and put the measurement on a step so that people can see how big it is.”

“It is a figure of times a question was asked.”

“It is a figure of times a question was asked and those are the same thing once you have been on this landing a fortnight, and you know they are the same thing, because that is what happened on Friday.” She looked round the step. “There was nothing on that step on Wednesday morning. Nothing on the stone at all.”

“And every single thing anybody in this county said on that landing between seven and eleven in the morning was said on a day with no figure under it, and none of it was a habit and none of it was a measurement. That man had a figure on Friday and none of us had anything on Wednesday. So tell me which of those two mornings this county actually had.”

Nobody answered her.

“You cannot, and that is my point and I am not being cruel about it.” She unfolded her arms. “And the second half, and it is the half I actually came down for. If a figure of asking is a habit then the habit can be stopped, and a habit that can be stopped is a thing a man can be praised for, and I have watched that happen twice on this landing this week already and I am not going to be the third.”

She looked along the step and nobody was on it.

“So the figure has to be nothing, and I am not going to be the one who says it is nothing either. It is going to be nothing by itself, because there is nowhere to put it. That is the only honest way for a figure to stop being a man.”

She turned the second half of it over once before she let go of it.

“I will give you the other one and then I am going up that road.”

She took her time over it.

“A boy of thirteen asked a woman once, down by the water, before the light on Monday, and neither of them has said a word about it since and there is nothing on this step that anybody could pick up and hold. That is the one thing that has happened on that bank this fortnight and it is not in anybody’s hands.”

She looked along the step before she said the rest of it.

“And if one of you had come up on Tuesday and asked him how he got on with it, I would not have been the one who stopped you, and I would have been the one who said afterwards that you were only helping.” She looked at the man of thirty-one. “There is a whole shape on this landing of one person holding a thing so that somebody else can ask about it, and what he said on Friday is the first piece of it I have seen with a number on it. I am not saying it is the same thing. I am saying it has the same handles.”

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his figure and said that he had been up the cart road before the light to look at a gate standing against a wall, and had worked out what a thing looks like after you have taken the thing that closed it away, and was not going to say it because it was not his gate.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and ninety-five days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and ninety-five is nine hundred and forty-eight less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and ninety-five days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“You went up the cart road.”

“I went as far as the pound gate and I stood there and I could tell you what it looks like now and it is nothing like what it looked like when I was there last, and that is the whole of my observation.” He scratched his heel on the step. “And I am not saying the rest of it, because I am not going to be the man who says a thing about somebody’s wall in front of everybody while the man who took it down is standing four feet away. She would have to stand there and be described. I have watched that happen to a man with a board this week. I am not doing it.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up and gave his figure and answered the woman who had come down to take his figure off the step, and he did not defend it, and he did not give it up either.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and ninety-four days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and sixty-three days old.

“Three hundred and ninety-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and sixty-three days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and forty-eight days old, which is nine hundred and forty-eight less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

He put his hands behind him and left them there.

“Eighty-eight wrong things in eighty-eight mornings, and here is the eighty-eighth, and it is the first one I have got wrong on this step in a week and I know it is wrong now.” He looked at the woman with the gate. “You are right about the measuring. I put a number in front of a step so that it could be seen how big it was.”

“I did not do that on Friday morning when I said the thing. I did it on Friday morning when I waited until there were people there and then said it out loud. And if it had been a different figure then I would have said that one as well. And I have not told you the figure, and I am not going to, because a woman told me on Friday that it is hers to say and not mine to hand round.”

“That is not what I said to you.”

“I know what you said to me. You said it is a habit and a habit is a wall.” He put his hands behind him. “And I am not going to tell you that it is not, because you have been at a gate for twenty-two years and I have been on this step for three weeks and I am not going to argue with you about walls.”

“I am not asking you to argue. I am telling you what it is so that you cannot be praised for putting it down.”

“Then I have heard it and I am not going to put it down, because I have worked out this morning that I cannot put down a thing I only started on Saturday.” He looked at the eight inches on the top step. “And here is the last of it and I am saying it once. I am not going to keep it.”

“I am not going to say it again on this step and I am not going to go and find somebody and say it to them in a yard, and if I hear it come out of my mouth in a kitchen to the woman it was not given to then I will stop talking. That is not keeping it. That is putting it down where it can go. And it is not a decision about anything else in this county and it is not a lesson and nobody is to write it down anywhere, including me.”

Nobody on that landing told him that was the right way to handle it and nobody told him it was the wrong way, and the woman with the gate went back up the cart road without saying whether it would stay off or go back on, and the man of forty-three swept the eight inches off in two strokes and did not say a fifth thing, and the fever was ninety-five weeks old and was not going to be said again.
