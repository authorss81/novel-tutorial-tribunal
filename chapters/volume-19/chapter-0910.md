# Chapter 910: She Will Give It Again If She Is Asked Again And It Will Be The Same Words

Second day of the hundred and fifty-second week. His six hundred and sixtieth morning. Five hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever eighty-nine weeks and four days old. Four hundred and sixty-four days since the division. Four hundred and ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank, with four days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and named the morning the bank comes off, with the week on it, and gave that morning its own figures and not these.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has never once given a fifth thing, and has this week named a morning ahead twice and once with nothing in it.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the second day of the hundred and fifty-second week, and it is a Wednesday.”

“It comes off on the sixth day of the hundred and fifty-second week, which is a Sunday, at about the sixth hour, and all ninety of those steps will be walkable by about the seventh, and there will be no figure of inches on that morning and no figure of steps under, and that is the thirty-first time. I have said that day three times this week and I am not going to say it again until it has happened.”

The woman who holds an account of what somebody else did said what she was going to do with it, out loud, in front of anybody who wanted to hear, and she said it on a morning with nothing to be about rather than on a morning with something.

Norah Stannard is forty-six, keeps nothing, holds nothing, is on no roll and is not anybody’s clerk, and has been holding one account since the seventh and last day of the hundred and fiftieth week, which is what a man said to her at the head of a cut.

“Here is the whole of what I have. A man told me what he did at a gate, once, and I have it, and nothing is going on paper, and it is not going anywhere, and I will not be asked to make it into a thing that lasts.”

“You are not?”

“No. And when it goes I will not know whether it went. That is the shape of it and I have had a week to look at it and I have not found a way round and I am not going to invent one in front of this bank.”

“What happens if somebody comes and asks you for it again.”

“Then I will give it again, in the same words, because it is his and not mine, and I have had a whole week to think about whether I would give it differently and I would not, and I am telling that man now so that he can decide whether to come.”

“That is not a second account.”

“No. It is the same account, said to a second person, and a second person listening to it is not the same as a second person checking it, because the second person is not going to be able to see whether I have left anything out. There is only one man who could see whether I had left anything out and he is at the head of a cut and he did not ask me to remember it.”

“You are describing something to me that has no name.”

“Yes. And I am not going to give it one, because the moment it has a name it will have somebody behind it, and I am not that somebody, and I have never wanted to be that somebody.”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he had found a use for himself at last, and nobody on that landing answered him, and nobody was unkind to him either.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fifty-six days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-five days old.

“Three hundred and fifty-six days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and twenty-five days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and ten days old, which is nine hundred and ten less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Fifty wrong things in fifty mornings. And I have found the use. It is this. There is a thing in this county that a nobody may give, and a nobody cannot be checked and cannot be shown, and so the only men who can ever give it are men who are nobody’s. That is me, and I have been it for three hundred and fifty-six days, and it turns out that is the one thing I had.”

Not one person answered him. One woman shifted her weight on the step. Nobody on that landing told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and the man who keeps the pound said later, to nobody in particular, that he had heard a great many men find a use for themselves and that she had never once heard one turn into anything.

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and eighty-ninth morning, and said that he had asked nobody anything this week and had been the worse for it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and eighty-nine mornings, which is nine hundred and ten less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and eighty-ninth. And I asked three people what they did this week and I have told nobody what any of them said, and I am no nearer a day against that ninth line than I was on Monday, and I am going to keep asking them and I am going to keep not saying.”

“That is a strange way to spend a week.”

“It is the only way I have found that does not cost anybody anything, and I have had a week to find it and it took me most of it.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fifty-seven days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fifty-seven is nine hundred and ten less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and fifty-seven days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is forty-nine mornings of them, and a man said out loud on that step that he has found the use of himself, and I am not going to tell him he has not, because I do not know that, and I am not going to tell him he has, because nobody asked me.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the second day of the hundred and fifty-second week, and put nothing on it but the morning.

“That is the fifty-third morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It did not answer this week, and nobody in this county is in the room where it answers, and nobody was asked to go, and I have not sent anybody. I am not going to send anybody about a gate or an oven or a heifer or a chair either, and if anybody in this county thinks one of those is going to be carried four hundred miles in a coat, they are welcome to try to carry it themselves.”

The bank was full at about the eleventh hour and a woman was holding a sentence about a gate that nobody had written down, and a man of thirty-one had said out loud that he had found what he was for, and the water was at sixteen with four days on it, and nothing else in that county was any further along than it had been on Monday.
