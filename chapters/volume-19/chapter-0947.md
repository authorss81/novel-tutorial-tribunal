# Chapter 947: Four Inches And About Ninety Of Those Ninety Under, And A Man Said Out Loud How Many Mornings He Had Put One Question

Fourth day of the hundred and fifty-seventh week. His six hundred and ninety-seventh morning. Five hundred and forty-seven days after the settlement. The fever ninety-four weeks and six days old. Five hundred and one days since the division. Four hundred and forty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that the water is back to where it was on the third day of the hundred and fifty-sixth week, and that he has nothing to add to that, and that he is aware a whole step has spent a week wanting him to add something.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on a morning with ninety steps walkable that a thing can last and not be a record and did not say it as a rule.

“Four at the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and fifty-seventh week, and it is a Friday.”

“You said on Wednesday that the water comes off tonight.”

“It did. It came off at about the sixth hour last night and it has been four inches since before the light.” He set the broom against the wall. “And I have said what I said about a wall and a record and I am not going to take it back and I am not going to say it again. A man who says a thing once and then keeps saying it is building. If I have said it well somebody will remember it without me.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up and did not make a proposal, and that was the first morning in three weeks of it, and what he did instead was count, out loud, on the step, with the working.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and ninety-three days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and sixty-two days old.

“Three hundred and ninety-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and sixty-two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and forty-seven days old, which is nine hundred and forty-seven less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Eighty-seven wrong things in eighty-seven mornings, and here is the eighty-seventh, and it is not a wrong thing, and I want that said first before I say it.” He put his hands at his sides and did not move. “There is one woman this county has said yes to three times in three weeks and there is one thing I have asked that woman for three times in three weeks, and the two of those are not the same figure and I am not going to stand here and mix them up for the sake of having something. She is not on this step this morning and that is deliberate and I would like it noticed.”

Her absence was noticed and not remarked on.

“The question is one question and it is this. I have asked that woman to ask somebody, out loud, in front of other people, what they did. Not what they thought. What they did. That is the whole of it and it has not changed shape since I started saying it, and I have been saying it on this step for a long time and nobody has ever held me to a figure, so I have held myself to one.”

“Go on.”

“I am going to give you the working and then the figure, because a figure on its own is a thing nobody can check and I am not going to be that.” He looked at the people on the step. “The fourth day of the hundred and fifty-second week. I told her she had the standing to say whether a man’s account of his own morning was true, and she has never done it and I am not blaming her for it. The first day of the hundred and fifty-third week.”

He counted them off on his fingers and then put the hand down.

“I told her to write down five lines that week and ten the week after, and there are no lines. The second day of the hundred and fifty-fifth week. I told her to ask a man what he thought about a thing he saw, and she did not, and she told a step why she did not, and I have never argued with her about it since. And the third day of the hundred and fifty-fifth week. I said her name and I said every one of them, once, by her, what they did.”

“That is four mornings.”

“That is four mornings and that is the figure, and it is four, and I am saying it out loud on a step with people on it because if I say it in a kitchen it is a thing I have thought. Here it is a thing that has been said.” He did not look at anybody in particular. “And now I am going to say the other half, and I am going to say it before anybody else does. That figure is four. That is a figure of times I put a question. It is not a figure of what anybody knew.”

“It is not a figure of what anybody did. It is not a figure of how many people were standing here, and no more than that it is a figure of me. And it cannot be added to the water and it cannot be added to anything else anybody on this step is holding, and in particular there is a figure in that lane which is that boy’s and which nobody on this step is going to be given today, and I could put the two together in about a second and I am not going to, and I am not going to tell him why.”

Nobody on that landing thanked him for the figure and nobody asked him whether it was the right one.

Then Corvin Tace came down three steps and put the broom across them, which he has never done, and looked at the man of thirty-one for a while.

He did not come further down the steps.

“I am not going to tell you that you were right. I am not going to tell you that you were wrong. I want both of those out of the way so that nothing else can be heard under them.” He set the broom down. “Here is where the figure goes. A figure of times a thing was said is a figure of times a thing was said. That is the whole of what it is and it is not going to be more than that by anybody standing here deciding it is more.”

“It is not a figure of what anybody knew, because the knowing was never on this step and you cannot count your way back to a room you were not the only one in. It is not a figure of what anybody did. And you are not in it either. You have been here three weeks wanting to be a man with a thing in him and you have just counted the times you asked for it, and four is a poor number and a good number and it is not a man.”

“It was never going to be a man.”

“No, and that is the part you have had three weeks to work out and I am not going to help you with it this morning.” He picked the broom up again. “And it is not going on that stone. Four is not about water and there is no line under it and there is not going to be one. If it went on that stone then every figure I have said for nineteen years would have something next to it, and a thing next to it, and you would not need anybody to be brave about anything. You would only need a man with a broom and a habit.”

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his figure and did not look at the man of thirty-one and did not go and stand near him, and said one sentence about the morning and would not be drawn on it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and ninety-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and ninety-four is nine hundred and forty-seven less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and ninety-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“You have heard that figure.”

“I have heard it and it is his and he put it on the step and nobody made him, and I am not going to say one word about whether it is a good figure or a bad one because that is his and not mine.” He sat down on the wall. “And he is right that it is not a person, and I am not going to correct him.”

“Most people say a number about themselves and then spend the rest of the day being that number. He has said four and now he has got to be a man who said four on a Friday. I know what that is like. I am not going to do it for him and he has not asked me to.”

Then the man who keeps a hedge came down off the wall with his hook in his hand and said that he had been up there since before the light and had heard four, and that he wanted to say one thing about it and then never to say another thing about it.

Wil Cairns keeps a hedge, the parish line, half a mile from the wall above the ash, has cut it twice a year for nine years, has moved every stake on his side of it onto the line he believes is, has told the man on the other side that he did it, and is going to be wrong about it in a way nobody will ever be able to check.

“You have counted the times you asked. I want to say the thing that is not in the figure and then I am done.”

He turned the hook over once and did not put it down.

“Say it.”

“The times you asked are four and I know it is four because I have been on this step every morning you have been on it, and I want you to know what the number does not have in it, because you have put it up there and everybody down here is going to look at it and think it means something about this week.” He turned the hook over. “It does not have the woman in it. She said no three times and I heard her say no and it took her nothing.”

“It does not have the man on the other side of my hedge in it. It does not have the baker and his wall in it, or the man with the catch, or the man who cannot read, or the boy. Four is four things you said. Everything else that happened on this landing in a fortnight happened without you counting any of it and it happened anyway, and it is going to keep happening on the mornings when you are not here, and I would like you to have that before you get used to four.”

“Which of those is yours.”

“None of them. I have said mine and I have had a bad week with it and it is not in his figure and I am not asking him to put it in.” He shouldered the hook. “And I am not going to say whether four is right. You counted your own asking and nobody else on this step is going to tell you that you counted it well, and I have watched this week go about telling men they were right by people who liked them, and I would rather have nothing.”

Nobody told him he had counted it well and nobody told him he had counted it badly, and four inches was on the top step with seven days of it to come back, and the man of forty-three swept it off and said no fifth thing, and the figure of four went down that hill with the man who had made it and stayed there.
