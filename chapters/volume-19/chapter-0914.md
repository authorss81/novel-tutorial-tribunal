# Chapter 914: The Thirty-First Time, And Ninety Steps Walkable

Sixth day of the hundred and fifty-second week. His six hundred and sixty-fourth morning. Five hundred and fourteen days after the settlement. The fever ninety weeks and one day old. Four hundred and sixty-eight days since the division. Four hundred and fourteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh, and that was the thirty-first time. There is no figure of inches on this morning and no figure of steps under, and there is no figure of days of the coming back either, and that is not a gap in the morning. It is the shape of the morning, and there is nothing else on this landing that wants a figure put on it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has never once given a fifth thing, and on an off-morning says that out loud and then stands there.

“There is no figure of inches this morning and no figure of steps under and no figure of days of the coming back. Nineteen years of getting up at the fifth hour on every one of these has not once given me a fourth thing to say, and I am not going to invent one this morning, and half of you would not know the difference if I did.”

“Not a thing to say, and a thing to do,” said a woman coming up out of the lane with her sleeves already wet to the elbow. “You have ninety steps to sweep this morning instead of one. That is not a figure. It is a job you have got and I have got, and it is the only morning of the week you and I are working the same hours.”

“That is true and I am not going to put a figure on it.”

“Nor will I. I have swept about half of them before the light and I am going to go back up for the other half after the hour, and nobody is going to tell me how many of them I did, because I did not count and I am not going to start.”

Then Ned Farrow came down off the turn with the smell of a byre on him and said a thing he had come down to say and would rather not have said, which is the ordinary condition of a man on this landing.

Ned Farrow keeps a byre a mile past the turn, off the lane that forks for the upper farms, and has a line a day in it and no stone and no seat and nobody after him.

“I asked a man this morning what he did yesterday,” he said, “and he told me, and it was nothing, and I have not put it in the line.”

“The line is for cattle.”

“The line is for cattle and the man is not cattle and he would not want to be in the byre book and if he were in it he would be in it under a heifer.” He stood on the bottom step with his cap in his hands.

“I have been in that byre every day for twenty-two years and that line has never had a person in it, and I have never once had a morning said to me out loud by anybody and not written down after. And this morning I had one. And I gave it and I did not write it, and it is gone, and I have been standing in that yard since the sixth hour feeling like a man who has thrown something over a wall.”

“You could write it.”

“I could and I will not. If that line ever gets a person in it, then the next man opens that book to find out which calf came in on the fourth and he has to read past a woman’s Tuesday to get to it. And then in ten years he has to read past a great deal, and then one day he reads past something that matters to somebody, and he does not know he did it. I have seen it done. Not in a byre.”

“You have given a morning away.”

“I have given one morning away and I would give it again and I am not going to pretend to you that it did not cost me anything, because I have had no reason to give one away in twenty-two years and I did not know on Monday that it would come out of me like that. It came out of me. That is all this is.”

Wat Marshe came up the lane behind him and gave his figure and did not stay on the wall, and came and sat on the bottom step next to the man from the byre, which he has not done on a morning before.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and sixty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and sixty-one is nine hundred and fourteen less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and sixty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up. And I am sitting here because there is nowhere else to sit and not because of him.”

“I know you are not. I want it understood that I know you are not, because in a week somebody on this landing is going to say you went to him for a figure and I do not want that to be the thing that is true about you.”

“That is all right. That is the only thing anybody has said to me this week that was about me and not about a number.”

Then Barnaby Crove came up and said the figure and said that he had asked nobody at all this morning, and gave the reason, and the reason was a reason that had been standing on that step since he came off the road.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and ninety-three mornings, which is nine hundred and fourteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and ninety-three. And I asked nobody, and I did not walk past anybody to ask them either, and I want it known that I thought about it and I did not do it.”

“You did not have to say that.”

“I have to say it, because I am telling the truth about this morning and the truth about this morning is that there was nothing on that stone and I used it. That is what I did. Ninety steps and a swept landing and no water on any of them, and the first thing I did with it was walk up that road at a good clip so that I would get here before somebody went out.”

“So this is the morning for asking.”

“This is the only morning of the week people come up the road at all. When the steps are under they are standing on their own landing and there is nothing to come for. When the water is off the whole county is out on the road and there is nobody to ask at home.”

“And I have known that since Wednesday and I have been waiting for today like a man waiting for a bus, and I did not get on it, and I do not know why, and I am not going to tell anybody it was because I was too proud, because it was not pride, it was that I got here and my mouth was shut and it stayed shut.”

He stood there with the figure in him and nothing else, and the woman who sweeps the steps came past them going up with a broom and did not stop, and it was near the hour.

Then the woman of forty-nine entered the morning on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out, and said that an off-morning is a morning and not a day off, and gave the reason in the plainest words she had.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and entered a morning on which there was no water on any of ninety steps.

“That is the fifty-seventh morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“This morning there is no water on any of those steps and I have entered the morning anyway, and I want to say why, because in nineteen years people have assumed I take a day off when there is nothing to record and I have never done it once. There is something to record.”

“There is a landing with ninety steps on it and no water on them, and a man has swept them, and that is a day. What I do not enter is the difference between that and a day where something happened. That difference is not mine to decide in the morning. I only decide whether I came.”

“That is a very small thing to have kept to yourself for nineteen years.”

“It is not small to me and it is not about a person and there is nothing in it anybody on that step could use, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would rather it stayed between me and the book.”

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It did not answer this week, and nobody in this county is in the room where it answers, and nobody was asked to go, and I did not ask anybody to go, and I have put no day on it, and I am not going to put one on it because this morning is quiet.”

Then a man of thirty-one said that an off-morning was a morning in which there was nothing to be asked about, and was refused by a man holding a gravel scoop who had come up a road fast to get here before somebody went out.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and sixty days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-nine days old.

“Three hundred and sixty days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and twenty-nine days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and fourteen days old, which is nine hundred and fourteen less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Fifty-four wrong things in fifty-four mornings, and here is the fifty-fourth. There is nothing on that stone. There is no figure at all. I have said for a fortnight that a morning is worth asking about when there is something on it, and this morning there is nothing on it, and there is nothing in this county to do with a question.”

“You have said that for a fortnight and you have never once been on the other side of it,” said Barnaby Crove, “and I have been, because I asked nobody today, and the reason I asked nobody is that there was nothing on the stone, and I have spent the morning walking about a landing where every person on it is standing in the open for the first time this week. If you want a morning worth asking about then come on a Sunday and stand on those steps, and then come back and tell me there is nothing to be asked.”

“That is not a figure of anything.”

“No. It is not. And it is the first thing you have said this fortnight that I am going to remember, and I am not going to say whether it is right or wrong, because I do not know, and I have spent the morning on a landing with ninety steps on it and a man from a byre telling me he gave something away, and I do not know.”

He was not told he was wrong by anybody, and he was not told he was right, and a woman with a rope over her arm came up at the eleventh hour to ask a man from a byre what he had said, and he said the same words again to her, and she wrote them nowhere.

All ninety of those steps were dry and walkable by the seventh hour, and a man had said out loud for the thirty-first time that there was no figure on the stone and was not going to invent one, and a man from a byre had given a morning away and not written it down, and a boy of thirteen had sat down next to him on purpose and said so before anybody could say it was an accident.
