# Chapter 906: The Thirtieth Time, And The Only Man Who Cannot Answer It

Fifth day of the hundred and fifty-first week. His six hundred and fifty-sixth morning. Five hundred and six days after the settlement. The fever eighty-nine weeks old. Four hundred and sixty days since the division. Four hundred and six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh, and that was the thirtieth time. There is no figure of inches on this morning and no figure of steps under, and that is not a gap in the morning. The fever is eighty-nine weeks old this morning and there is no figure of days with it, and that is the shape of a seventh Saturday, and there is nothing else on this landing that wants a figure put on it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has never once given a fifth thing, and on an off-morning he says that out loud and then stands there.

“There is no figure of inches this morning and no figure of steps under. Nineteen years of getting up at the fifth hour on every one of these has not once given me a fourth thing to say, and I am not going to invent one this morning, and half of you would not know the difference if I did.”

Norah Stannard asked him what he did, out loud, in front of anybody who wanted to hear it, and she said before she asked that she knew she was asking the wrong man and was going to ask him anyway.

Norah Stannard is forty-six, keeps nothing, holds nothing, and has been holding one account since the seventh and last day of the hundred and fiftieth week, which is what a woman said to a man at the head of a cut.

“You have just told us there is nothing to be about this morning. You are the only man on this bank who says that and means it, so I am going to ask you the same question I asked the man at the lock, and I am going to tell you first that I know which of the two kinds of answer I am going to get.”

“Ask it.”

“What did you do on the second day of the hundred and forty-second week.”

“I was at this counter from about the fifth hour. I put the gravel off the low end, because the low end was under and the low end is the one that goes. I swept the top step. I did not go up the steps.”

“That is all of it.”

“That is the whole of it. There was water on all ninety of them that morning and I do not walk in that, so there is nothing about those steps I can tell you.”

“Why do you not walk in it.”

“Because a man who comes down ninety wet steps at the sixth hour is not looking at anything. He comes back up and he puts the figure right, and that is the whole of what happened on that morning. If you want what happened down there, you have to ask the water, and I have been on this bank nineteen years and I have never once got an answer out of it.”

“And if I ask you what it looked like.”

“Then I will tell you what it looked like, and it will be true, and you will have two things that are both about me, and neither of them will be about what the water was doing. I am telling you that now so that you do not come back with it in your hand as though I had kept it back from you.”

“That is nearly an answer.”

“It is not an answer at all and it is not nearly one. It is a man telling you the shape of what he has got, and the shape is small, and it has always been small, and I did not know that until you stood there and asked me for it.”

“You did know.”

“I knew it the way I know the third figure without looking at it. It was not in my mouth this morning. That is a different thing and I am not going to pretend the two are the same.”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he had nothing to put on the table on a morning with no figures in it, and nobody gave him anything for having said it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fifty-two days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-one days old.

“Three hundred and fifty-two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and six days old, which is nine hundred and six less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Forty-six wrong things in forty-six mornings. And there is nothing on that stone this morning and I have nothing to say and no offer to make anybody, and I am going to say that out loud rather than stand here for two hours pretending I have a use for the morning.”

Not one person on that landing said a word to him about it, and one woman took his elbow off the wall and put it back, which is not a thing anybody is to be thanked for.

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and eighty-fifth morning, and said he had asked nobody what they did because there was nothing to be about.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and eighty-five mornings, which is nine hundred and six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and eighty-five. And I did not ask anybody what they did this morning, and that is not me being steady. There is no water on those steps and there is nothing to be about and I do not have a question for a morning like this one. I will be back when there is something.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fifty-three days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fifty-three is nine hundred and six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and fifty-three days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is forty-five mornings of them, and a woman asked the man who gives the figures what he did and he told her and it did not do anything, and I am not saying that is wrong. I am saying that I would have liked to have been in the room, and I was not, and that is my own business.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the thirtieth off-morning, and said that she had entered a morning and not a day off that bank.

“That is the forty-ninth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing, and it was not a gap this morning when there was no water on any of the steps.”

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It did not answer this morning, and nobody was asked to go and I did not ask anybody to go, and I am not going to put a day on it because no day belongs to it.”

All ninety of those steps were dry and a man who had swept the top step could not say what had happened down them, and a woman had asked him for it anyway and had been given the true shape of what he had, which was nothing that could be carried anywhere.
