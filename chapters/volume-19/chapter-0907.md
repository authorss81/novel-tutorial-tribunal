# Chapter 907: A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Thinks A Second Asking Is A Check

Sixth day of the hundred and fifty-first week. His six hundred and fifty-seventh morning. Five hundred and seven days after the settlement. The fever eighty-nine weeks and one day old. Four hundred and sixty-one days since the division. Four hundred and seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and that was a man able to walk all ninety dry-footed again by the seventh hour on a Sunday. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and the shape of the morning, because on a Sunday the steps come back.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has never once added to a figure he has given on a seventh Saturday.

“Four at the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and fifty-first week, and it is a Sunday.”

“It came off all ninety on the fifth day of the hundred and fifty-first week at about the sixth hour and that was the thirtieth time, and this morning it is four, and there is nothing in that which is worth anybody’s morning.”

Garrin Tolley, thirty-eight, with a child of nine, came up the road with the board out of the inside of his coat, and asked a woman with a rope over her arm one question, and got the answer, and then stood there with the answer in his hand telling her he was going to come back and ask her the same thing.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and carries a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, which is a figure of what is on a board and not a figure of a person, and nobody is to count him.

Ruth Ansell keeps a pound two hundred yards up that cart road, before the turn, and writes a line a day about what came in and what went out.

“Did a black heifer come into your yard on the fifth day of the hundred and fifty-first week.”

“No. Not a beast in.”

“That is it. That is all I came for.”

“That is the whole of what you came for. Sit down if you are going to stand there, because I have said the answer and I have nothing to add to it and I would rather not be standing up for it.”

He did not sit down.

“I am going to come next week and ask you the same question again.”

“You are welcome to, and I am not going to tell you that you are wrong, because I do not think you are. I think you have got a thing in your coat that nobody has told you what to do with, and I think a second answer is going to tell you what to do with it. It is not going to. It is going to be the same word in the same voice, and you are going to come back here and put it next to the first one, and you are going to have two.”

“That is what I want.”

“That is what you want and I can tell you what you will have. On one morning I was one person saying one thing. If you come on another morning I am the same person saying the same thing, and you have not gained anybody who was there. You have gained the same sentence with a date on it, and there is a difference between those two things and it is the only difference I have to give you.”

“Why is it a difference.”

“Because the only person who could stand in that yard and tell you is a person who stood in that yard. I was not in it on the fifth day of the hundred and fifty-first week and I was not in it at any other time. I know about that heifer because I write a line every day and I wrote that heifer did not come in. That is all I have and it is not anybody standing anywhere.”

“Nobody has told me what to do with the board.”

“No. And it is not my board and I have not been asked about it and I am not going to be asked about it, and if I were asked about it I would tell you that I keep a yard and not a board, and that the two things are not in the same county as each other.”

Nobody on that landing told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong, and he said he would come on the first day of the hundred and fifty-second week, which is two days after this one and not the morning after, and nobody stopped him, and the woman who keeps the pound said that he could come and that it would not help him.

Then a man of thirty-one said that a heifer that did not come into a yard and a calf that came into a byre and went out again were two lines that could be put together, and was refused by a man who had come down the low side to look at a wall and had not meant to speak to anybody.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fifty-three days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-two days old.

“Three hundred and fifty-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and seven days old, which is nine hundred and seven less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Forty-seven wrong things in forty-seven mornings, and here is the forty-seventh. A heifer that did not come into a yard and a calf that came into a byre and went out again. Two lines about animals. Put them together and there is a thing that a man can look at.”

“No. They are about two different mornings and they are about two different yards, and the men who keep them do not stand in the same place at the same hour, and if you put the two lines together you have not made a thing that a man can look at. You have made a thing that a man can look at and cannot check, and that is a worse thing than the one you had.”

“They are both true.”

“They are both true, and so are two sentences about one thing, and that does not make one of them a check on the other. Nobody has asked anybody what they saw. If somebody had, then you would have two people saying the same thing, and that would still not be the thing.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and eighty-sixth morning, and asked nobody else anything at all, and said what he had got out of watching.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and eighty-six mornings, which is nine hundred and seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and eighty-six. And I watched that man say out loud that he is going back to ask again, and I did not ask anybody anything myself, and I am not going to claim I did a better thing than him, because I did not do a thing at all. I only watched.”

“What did you get out of watching.”

“I got that he is going to get less than he went for and he is not angry, and I do not know what to do with that yet.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fifty-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fifty-four is nine hundred and seven less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and fifty-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is forty-six mornings of them, and a man said out loud on that step that he is going to come back and ask a woman the same thing again, and nobody stopped him, and that is his own business and I am not going to hold it up in front of people.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the sixth day of the hundred and fifty-first week, and said what a second asking is in the plainest words she had.

“That is the fiftieth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“Two people saying the same thing about a thing is not that thing. I have said that before and I am saying it again because this week it happened in front of me and I would like it in more than one place. It is not a check. It is a second true thing, and true things do not add up to the thing, and the adding up is the part that is going to hurt somebody in a room four hundred miles off.”

A man of thirty-eight walked down that road with a board against his chest and an answer on it that was no use to him, and he had not been told he was wrong and had not been told he was right, and he had said the day he would come back and nobody had stopped him.
