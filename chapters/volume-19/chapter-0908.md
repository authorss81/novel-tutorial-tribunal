# Chapter 908: A Line In Her Own Book That Says A Man Did Nothing

Seventh and last day of the hundred and fifty-first week. His six hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Five hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever eighty-nine weeks and two days old. Four hundred and sixty-two days since the division. Four hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and gave the shape of a week coming to its end, which he does on a Monday because a Monday is the seventh and last day of the hundred and fifty-first week and not the first day of another.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has learned that this week runs from a Tuesday to a Monday and that most of what goes wrong on that landing goes wrong on the Monday.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under, on the seventh and last day of the hundred and fifty-first week, and it is a Monday.”

“This week is out at midnight. Tomorrow is the first day of a new one and I will say the three figures again at about the seventh hour, and they will be different, and that is the ordinary way and there is nothing about it that I want anybody to make a thing of.”

Ruth Ansell wrote a line in her own book at about the ninth hour that said a man had done nothing, and she said out loud, before she wrote it, that she was going to write it, and she said why it had not been written before.

Ruth Ansell keeps a pound two hundred yards up that cart road, before the turn, where the road forks for the upper farms, and has written a line every day since the yard was made.

“I am going to write this and I am telling you before I write it, and the reason I am telling you is that I have been writing lines in that yard for a long time and this is the first one of them that says a man did not do a thing.”

“Which man.”

“The upper shepherd. A beast went out of my yard on the fourth day of the hundred and fifty-first week and it was not the beast that went in, and I have had it in my head since Wednesday and I have not asked him. I asked him this morning. Once. I asked him where that animal went and who has it, and he said he did not know and he did not know who had it.”

“Then that is the end of it.”

“That is the end of the beast and it is not the end of the line. I am going to write that I asked him once and he said he did not know, and that is a line with something in it, and before this morning it was a blank, and a blank and a line saying nothing are two different things and only one of them is in my book.”

“You are writing down what a man said.”

“I am writing down what I did and what he said, and I am writing it in my own hand in my own book on my own morning, and if you call that an account then it is an account, and I have not asked anybody to call it anything.”

“You said last week that a second asking is a different question.”

“I said it and I meant it and I am not going to unsay it because I have just used it. That is what it is for. He gets asked once. If he has something to tell me he will tell me, and if he does not, then the line says he does not know, and it stays said, and he does not have to stand up again next week and decide whether he can be trusted.”

“What happens to the animal.”

“The animal is a beast and it will be a beast next year. I have lost one heifer’s worth and it will come out of my year and not out of his, and he knows that, and he said he did not know because he did not know.”

Then a man of thirty-one said that a line saying a man did nothing was the same as no line at all, and was refused by the woman who had written it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fifty-four days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-three days old.

“Three hundred and fifty-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and twenty-three days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred and eight days old, which is nine hundred and eight less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Forty-eight wrong things in forty-eight mornings, and here is the forty-eighth. She wrote that a man did not know. That is the same paper with nothing on it. A blank and a nothing are the same thing to a man in a hurry.”

“No. And you have just told me what a blank is, which is a blank. A blank says nobody has asked. A line saying he did not know says a man was asked and did not know. Those are not the same and they are not the same to the next person who opens that book, and they are not the same to him, and that is the whole of why I wrote it.”

“He could have said something.”

“He could have. He did not. And I am not going to write down what I think he was going to say if he had had another night on it, because I have not asked him again and I am not going to find out, and a line that says *he would have said* is a line I have written about a man and not about a morning.”

“That is not a thing anybody can use.”

“No. And it is the first line I have written this year that I would hand to a stranger.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and eighty-seventh morning, and said he had been thinking about a man with a board, and said why he had not asked him anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and eighty-seven mornings, which is nine hundred and eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and eighty-seven. And there is a man going to come up that road tomorrow to ask a woman the same thing she has already said once, and I have had a whole day to think about whether I should be the one to tell him not to, and I have decided that I am not.”

“Why are you not going to, then.”

“Because he did not ask me. And because if I tell a man not to ask a question because I have decided what the answer will be, then I am doing the asking for him, and he will not know that I did it, and he will think well of me for it and he will not be any nearer to a day against that ninth line than he was on Friday.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fifty-five days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fifty-five is nine hundred and eight less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and fifty-five days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is forty-seven mornings of them, and a woman wrote a line today that says a man did not know, and that is not my count and it is not a number of mine and I am not going to hold it next to mine to see what the two of them make.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the seventh and last day of the hundred and fifty-first week, and put no day on anything that was not hers.

“That is the fifty-first morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“This week was five keepers and one man with a board and one woman holding a sentence about a gate, and not one of those six things was about the nine, and none of them was entered on anything of mine, and I am not going to be asked whether they ought to have been. If somebody says to me that a line in a pound and a line on the ninth run are the same kind of thing, I will tell them to go and stand in the pound at dusk, and then they can decide.”

The week went out at midnight with a line in one book saying a man did not know and a man on a bank knowing which road a man of thirty-eight would come up in the morning, and neither of those things was put on anything else.
