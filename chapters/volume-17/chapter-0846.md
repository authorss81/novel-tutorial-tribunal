# Chapter 846: First Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Third Week, And A Tuesday, And Sixteen Inches And Four Days Of The Coming Back And That Is A Full Bank And It Holds And The Next Time Is In Four Days, And The Arrangement Reads Out Its Own Edits On A Landing And It Loses The Power To Bind A Seat That Has Not Agreed And Is Not Erased And Is Not Defeated, And Three Of The Nine Positions Become A Bell Network And A Public Works Compact And A Public Archive

First day of the hundred and forty-third week. His five hundred and ninety-sixth morning. Four hundred and forty-six days after the settlement. The fever eighty weeks and three days. Four hundred days since the division. Three hundred and forty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was four days of the coming back, and that was a full bank. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and said that he had said the same thing a fortnight ago and had been right, and that being right about it did not make him enjoy it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives three things every morning. The fourth thing is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and it has been nothing for fourteen days.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is four days of the coming back, and that is a full bank. A full bank holds at that figure until it comes off. It does not go over sixteen and it does not go go back under it either.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days, and that is the fifth day of this week. Four days after the morning it is full, and I am not going to stand here and pretend there is anything clever about that.*”

Then at about the ninth hour a man of sixty-one read the arrangement’s own edits into the open on that landing, all eleven lines and the index that went with them, and wrote them into his book. That is the whole of how it lost the power to bind anybody.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years, and has refused since the second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week to write a bench’s notice in it. On the seventh day of this week he read two lines of the fifth leaf and stopped. He has not written a notice in that book in eleven years.

“*I am going to do the thing I have refused to do for eleven years, and I want the reason said first, so that nobody can decide afterwards it was a triumph.*”

“*I am writing it in because it is the arrangement’s own notice about itself. It is not a notice to a person. It is not a notice to be served. A thing’s account of its own working is not a thing that can be refused. If it could be refused then it could be un-printed, and the whole of what a woman of sixty-four said on Saturday would be a thing I did on a Tuesday to be tidy.*”

“*Eleven lines, and an index of the nine positions with the six of them that are on the ground marked, and the three that are not on the ground left blank, and I have left them blank on purpose.*”

“*And here is what it does and does not do, in about nine of your ears, and I am not a lawyer and I am a man with a book. It is still there. It is four hundred miles off. It answered a question on the seventh day of this fortnight and its answer was true. Nothing has been defeated this morning and nothing has been broken.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said what the difference was in one sentence, and said what had been done to a thing without asking it, and named his eleventh thing he cannot check, and would not be thanked for the sentence.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and has a palm and a forearm, and has named ten things he cannot check and an eleventh on this morning. A boy of thirteen has been keeping a count of how many he names and gave the count on Saturday as ten.

“*Here is the whole of the difference, and it is one sentence, and I would like a woman of sixty-four to tell me if I have got it wrong. An edit nobody has seen can bind a seat. An edit everybody can see can only be agreed to. That is all it is.*”

“*And here is the other half and nobody is going to like it. We have published a thing without asking it. Nine people on a landing read out eleven lines of a thing four hundred miles off and put them in a book, and the thing did not get a say, and I do not know that it could have been asked.*”

“*So that is my eleventh thing I cannot check, and it is not a figure and it is not a feeling. I cannot check whether publishing a thing’s own account of itself is the same as publishing a person’s. I have been on this bank eleven days and have not got that far, and I would rather say it than be the man who worked it out in his head and said nothing.*”

Then a man of sixty-one read the ninth line, and a woman of sixty-four heard her own name read out loud in about nine ears for the first time in nineteen years, and did not say anything at all for a while, and then said what it had cost her and refused to have the publication stopped.

Nevin Trask, sixty-one, read the ninth line of the arrangement’s own index, which is the line a woman of sixty-four has had a name in ink on for nineteen years, and there is a day against no name on it anywhere.

“*Nine: the seat for the ninth position is held by a person named in this index, and no day is entered against that name in this record, and the absence is not an error and is not to be corrected by the person named.*”

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on the ninth line of a book four hundred miles off and has never had a day against it. She has said a word twice in nineteen years and has refused nine people who asked her to say it a third. She has given those nineteen years away three times and is not going to do it a fourth.

She did not say anything for a while, and then she said this, and it took her two goes to get out, and about four of them let her get it out.

“*I have not heard that read out before. Not once. I have had a name in ink on that line for nineteen years and I have never heard a man read it out loud on a bank, and I want that said as a fact, not as a thing anybody is to be kind about.*”

“*And here is what it has cost me this morning, in about nine of your ears, and the man of sixty-one cannot unpublish it by wishing he had not.*”

“*For nineteen years the reason nobody can be sent for me is that a name with no day against it is a thing no arrangement can compute. That is what I said on Sunday with the cost named first, and I have not changed my mind about the cost, and I named it accurately and it has just been paid in a way I did not think of.*”

“*My name is on a published sheet now. It is on a sheet a man of sixty-one has in a book, and it is on a sheet that any person can ask to read, and the only thing that made it uncomputable was the absence of a day, and a published name is the beginning of a computable thing.*”

“*And I am not going to have the publication stopped. I am going to say that in the plainest voice I have got and then I am going to sit down, and if about four of you are disappointed I would rather have that than a woman of sixty-four who has been protected by nobody reading anything.*”

Then three charters were read that were not about stones, and each of them was about a thing and not about a person, and each of them was read whole, and each one had to say out loud what it could not do.

Nevin Trask read the third, the eighth and the ninth of the nine charters, and they were the three positions that are not stones. A woman of thirty-six rang a hand-bell down a valley for nineteen years, and a man of forty-four argued a road for eleven.

“*Three: the seventh anchor is burned, and it is replaced by a network of hand-bells in the Briar March, rung by the people who live where each bell hangs. No bell is rung by anybody who does not live within earshot of it.*”

“*Eight: the eighth anchor is replaced by a public works compact in the Red Basin, and the compact is for the road. The road is a cart road and not a coach road, and the compact says who mends it, who pays, and what happens in a year when it cannot be mended.*”

“*Nine: the ninth anchor is replaced by a public archive at Orison, and the archive keeps the sheets, the leaves, the charters and the refusals, and anybody may ask to read any of it. The keeping of it is not the hearing of anything.*”

“*That is three charters and three things, and none of them is a person, and a man of thirty-one told me on Saturday that he does not know how a bell network holds a seat. I have read the words and I do not know either.*”

Hessa Vinch is thirty-six and has rung a hand-bell at the top of a valley in the Briar March for nineteen years, because there was nothing else to ring and the anchor there burned before she was born. She came up the cart road on Monday night and slept in a barn.

“*Nineteen years I have rung it for a fire and for a flood and for a man on the wrong side of the water, and about four of you have been at the top of this bank every day for eleven days and none of you has ever heard it.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for nineteen years, and I am not going to be called brave, and I want both of those refused out loud before we go on, and a man of thirty-eight on Monday was not thanked for opening a coat and I heard about that and I noticed.*”

“*Here is what a bell network cannot do and I am going to say it before about nine of you work it out and feel clever about it. A bell tells you something is happening. It does not know what is happening. Nineteen years and I have rung it for a fire and for a flood and I have never once known which until I was in the valley and could see.*”

“*And there is no seat in a valley. There is me and a rope and a bell and about four people who can hear me, and a man of thirty-one is right that I do not know how that holds a seat, and I have thought about it all the way up the cart road and I have not found out.*”

Hal Brecht is forty-four and lost his remedy to the comparison, and has argued for that road for eleven years, and was told on the third day of this fortnight in about nine ears that he was right about it and that nobody had been listening for eleven years. He holds the eighth seat and it is a compact and not a stone.

“*Eleven years on that road. A man of thirty-one told me on Thursday of last week that there is a nine-day working that answers it and requires nobody’s consent, and that I have been right about it for eleven years, and I have not thanked him for saying it and I am not going to.*”

“*And here is the thing I want said in about nine of your ears on a Tuesday, and I want it said by somebody who is not me. A compact is not a road. I have read the eight line twice and it says who mends it and who pays, and it does not say the road is good, and it does not say the eleven years were the right eleven years, and it does not say anything about a handcart on a bad day when the road is under.*”

“*So I am going to hold a seat for a thing I have wanted for eleven years, and I am going to hold it while it is not what I wanted. I am not going to be thanked for that either, and I have not got a better sentence, and I have had all the way up the cart road to find one.*”

Then the six stones were named as staying where they are under local maintenance, and a charter was accepted at last in a form a woman of forty said was hers, and the woman said that a seat is not a day and that she was not going to be thanked for it. One seat was left with nobody in it, and nobody was forced into it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and accepted the first charter on the fourth day of this week, with the fourth line altered. He read the alteration out and said that the sixth line of it was the first thing he had written in that book in eleven years.

“*Six: the terms on which each of the six stones is kept are published by the people who keep it, to anybody who asks, in the ordinary voice, and a copy is held at the archive at Orison, and the seat for that position is held by the person who keeps the stone.*”

“*That is the first line I have written in that book in eleven years and I am going to say what it is. It is a figure of six and a figure of six, and one of those six is a figure of stones and the other is a figure of the seats those stones hold. I am not going to add them together on a Tuesday.*”

“*The ninth seat is the seat of the archive. I have read the ninth charter and it is a good charter and it says the archive keeps everything and hears nothing, and there is nobody to hold that seat this morning, and nobody is going to be put in it.*”

Wenna Callow is forty and has kept the first of the six stones for eleven years before it was written down, and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years, and will not name a room. She has published the terms of her lane on a sheet nailed to a gate, and has not said one word about how she feels about it, and says the sheet is crooked.

“*The sheet is on the gate and the sheet is crooked. A man of about fifty-two cannot see well enough to tell me which way is straight, and a boy of thirteen told me it leaned to the left, and I told him to leave it.*”

“*And a charter gives me a seat, and I am the person who keeps the stone, and that is correct. I have read it twice and nobody is going to make me argue with the wording.*”

“*And a seat is not a day. I have asked for a day against a name for eleven years, and nobody is getting an answer from me this morning about what I am going to do about that. And nobody is to be thanked for the seat, and nobody is to tell me the seat is a step.*”

“*So I have a seat and I have not got the thing I asked for, and both of those are this morning, and I am not going to be thanked for the seat and I am not going to be told the seat is a step.*”

“*And it took me two evenings to write it and there are about forty words on it, and the most useful thing on it is not the stone. The most useful thing on it is that the lane has a name for the man who comes if the water comes, and there was never a name for him before Thursday.*”

Then a man of thirty-one stepped back from the gate where the sheet was hanging crooked, and said what the morning had settled and what it had not, in about nine ears, and gave every figure with its subtraction, and said that he had been asked whether he would take the ninth seat and had said no before the question was finished.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and ninety-two days, two hundred and ninety-two being eight hundred and forty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and sixty-one days old, a hundred and sixty-one being eight hundred and forty-six less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*A quorum of nine seats, and six of them active to keep the shared archive going. Nine is a figure of seats. Six is a figure of seats. Three is a figure of seats that nobody is in this morning, and I am not going to let the three be a figure of anything else.*”

“*And I was asked at about the tenth hour whether I would take the ninth seat, and I said no before the woman who asked had finished. I am telling that now and not in a quieter voice later, because about four of you would have found it out by Friday.*”

“*Here is why, in four sentences. The ninth seat keeps the sheets. The man on the second line of the fifth leaf is the man Ilyan Vester, and a man who keeps the sheets is a man whose handwriting is on them, and a man whose handwriting is on them is not nobody’s.*”

“*And I have been nobody’s for two hundred and ninety-two days and I would like to keep being nobody’s for a while yet, and I am aware that that is the most convenient thing I have said this fortnight, and I am saying it anyway.*”

“*A hundred and forty-six days. The offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and forty-six days old, and a hundred and forty-six is eight hundred and forty-six less seven hundred, and a quorum, a bell, a compact and a book are not an answer to it. I am not going to let a woman of thirty-six’s nineteen years of a bell be mistaken for one.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the twelfth hour of the first day of a new week. A full bank sat over the top step, eleven lines of the arrangement’s own account of itself were read into a book in the open, and three charters were about things, and the ninth seat had nobody in it and nobody was forced into it.

The fever was eighty weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and ninety-two days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and sixty-one days old, and a hundred and forty-six days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

The arrangement’s own edits were published in the open, all eleven lines and the index, and the difference was given in one sentence: an edit nobody has seen can bind a seat, and an edit everybody can see can only be agreed to. It was not erased and it was not defeated and it is still four hundred miles off and still answering.
A man of about fifty-two said that nine people had published a thing without asking it, and that he cannot check whether a thing’s own account of itself is the same as a person’s.

A woman of sixty-four heard her own name read out loud from the ninth line for the first time in nineteen years, said what it had cost her, and refused to have the publication stopped.
Three of the nine positions became a hand-bell network in the Briar March, a public works compact in the Red Basin, and a public archive at Orison, and each of the three had to say out loud what it could not do.

The six stones stay where they are under local maintenance, and a charter was accepted in a form a woman of forty said was hers, and the ninth seat was left with nobody in it, and a man of thirty-one refused it before the question was finished.
A woman of forty has kept that stone for eleven years and has a seat because of it, and she said a seat is not a day, and that she has not got the thing she asked for, and that nobody was to be thanked for the seat or told the seat was a step.
