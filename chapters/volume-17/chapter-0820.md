# Chapter 820: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Week, And A Thursday, And The Water Is Two Days Back Up, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Has No Idea How To Stop Saying A Figure At About The Eleventh Hour Every Morning And Is Going To Keep Doing It, And The Right Of Refusal Is Not Restored And The Offer Is At One Hundred And Twenty Days And Is Not Answered

Third day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. His five hundred and seventieth morning. Four hundred and twenty days after the settlement. The fever seventy-six weeks and five days. Three hundred and seventy-four days since the division. Three hundred and twenty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and said what was going to happen on Saturday morning. It is the first time in nineteen years he has been able to say that without wanting a person in the room to hear it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and can work the water out four mornings ahead without looking at it, which he could not do last week and still cannot say why.

“*About eight inches at the seventh hour, and it is two days of the coming back, and about four of those ninety steps are under this morning.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it. It will be twelve tomorrow and full on Saturday, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*And I can work that out four mornings ahead now.*”

“*I have been doing it nineteen years by looking at the water. I can now do it four mornings ahead without looking, and I have not been able to do that before this week and I do not know why it changed.*”

“*And it is not because of a stone I named. I want that said in about nine ears, because about four of you have been working back from that stone since the day I named it.*”

“*It changed on the Monday of the week before last, and the only thing that happened on that Monday is that a man of thirty-one said a woman of twenty-eight had a name in ink on that line and no day against it.*”

“*And about four of us went down that bank and stood about nine feet off her.*”

“*That is not a reason. I know it is not a reason. I have given it anyway because about four of you are going to want a reason and I would rather give you a bad one than watch about nine of you make one up.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said what she had done since Wednesday morning, said that she had not decided, and gave the two things again with the working on them.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and said that a second time is a rule. She said her own name on that bank on Tuesday and has not gone back down that road since.

“*A hundred and seventy-eight days. That is eight hundred and twenty less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*And I have said the working out loud three mornings running and I am going to keep saying it, because a man of thirty-four said a condition out loud and a woman of sixty-four said a condition out loud, and about four of you are still saying them in your heads.*”

“*And I have not decided.*”

“*I said on Wednesday morning that I was not going to decide this week. It is Thursday and I have not decided, and about four of you have been waiting since Wednesday for me to change my mind and I have not.*”

“*And I have looked at the first of the two things.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has never been cruel to anybody on that bank since before it had about nine of us on it. I know that because about nine of us have been watching for it.*”

“*And because a man of forty-four said on Saturday that nobody had been cruel, and a woman of sixty-four said on Saturday that nobody in a room nineteen years ago had been cruel either.*”

“*Both of those were said on the same day about a week ago, and I have not been able to put them in the same place in my head since.*”

“*And I have not looked at the second one yet, and I am not going to say when I will.*”

“*About four of you are not going to ask me and about four of you are going to, and I would like the four of you to stop.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the two days I have spent on that road, and I am not going to be told I was brave.*”

“*And I am going to stop talking about my own days in about two minutes, and about four of you are not going to get a fourth day of it out of me.*”

Then a man of thirty-four in an ash said what he had done about the woman of sixty-four, had not withdrawn it, said what she said to him about it, and did not say it kindly.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four and has a day against his name which is not a day of this week. He has been in an ash, about twenty feet off the edge of that drain and a hundred yards along it, for a hundred and ninety days.

“*A hundred and ninety days. That is eight hundred and twenty less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four said no to me on the Friday of last week, and I said on the Saturday that I was not going to withdraw it, and on the Saturday she told me at about the ninth hour what she thought of that.*”

“*She said: you may use my name and you will not use my nineteen years.*”

“*And I said I understood, and I do not understand it, and I am not going to pretend to about nine of you on this bank that I do.*”

“*And she said: good.*”

“*And then she said, and I am giving it out loud and I am not giving it kindly, because about four of you have been waiting two days to see whether I softened it.*”

“*She said: you are going to be asked in that room why a woman of sixty-four is standing in it, and you are going to say something about what I told you.*”

“*And she said: I want you to know, before we get there, that I did not tell you what to say.*”

“*And I am going to use her name. I have a day against my name and I have not got one of anything else.*”

“*And I am going to put her name next to it in a room four hundred miles off, and about nine of us will know what that looks like.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two checked two figures out loud and stopped, said the third one was not for him, and a woman of forty-four said she was not going to give a figure of anything this morning.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. This morning he checked two out loud and named the two.

“*Two.*”

“*Eight inches, and that is four inches a day twice, and I can put my hand on that and I did.*”

“*And a hundred and ninety days for a man of thirty-four. That is eight hundred and twenty less six hundred and thirty, and I can do that in my head.*”

“*And the third one is not mine to check.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and sixty-six days, and that is eight hundred and twenty less five hundred and fifty-four, and there is no line under me two hundred and sixty-six days back, and there has not been one for eleven years.*”

“*And I have watched him get that figure right four hundred times and I cannot check one of them.*”

“*And I have said that on this bank three times since the Thursday of the week before last, and I am not going to say it a fourth time.*”

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has put her crumb of bread in her apron and her pencil in her sleeve, and is not giving the third figure this morning or any morning after it. She gave a figure of about four of them on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week and a different figure of about four of them on the Monday of that week, and not one of the two fours was the same four. A piece of paper with four names on it is still in the other pocket.

“*I am not giving a figure of about four of you this morning and I am not giving one this week.*”

“*I gave two last week that did not have one of the same four in them, and a boy of thirteen asked me on the Tuesday of last week why, and I said I was not going to tell him.*”

“*And I am not going to tell about nine of you either.*”

“*And the piece of paper with four names on it is still in my apron and it is still going nowhere, and I am not going to destroy it either.*”

“*A man of thirty-one asked for it on Friday and it would be a different kind of hiding.*”

“*And the crumb of bread is in this pocket and the paper is in that one, and I have checked.*”

“*And I have been keeping a book of twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years, and I can tell the two things apart.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost. He gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said he had no idea how to stop saying a figure at about the eleventh hour every morning.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-six days, two hundred and sixty-six being eight hundred and twenty less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-five days old, a hundred and thirty-five being eight hundred and twenty less six hundred and eighty-five. He has asked seven times whether it will keep. Nobody has said and he is not going to ask an eighth time.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in fourteen days. It was thirteen days yesterday and twelve days on Tuesday.*”

“*And I am giving you the run one more time, and then I am going to stop saying how many days it has not moved.*”

“*It is a woman of forty at the ninth hour.*”

“*It is a smaller thing than it was on Tuesday, and I am not going to try to make it bigger this morning because about four of you would notice.*”

“*The mistake is the thirty-second of the same mistake, and it is the last one I have got for you this week.*”

“*It is not a new one and I am not going to pretend it is.*”

“*On Tuesday I said I had no idea what to do about a woman of forty at the ninth hour. I have had two days and I have worked out that the only thing I know how to do is say a figure at about the eleventh hour.*”

“*And the reason I know how to do that is every morning since before that bank had about nine of us on it, and not anything about being useful.*”

“*And the cost is that about nine of us have been coming up that bank at about the eleventh hour on the ten mornings since the first morning of the hundred and thirty-eighth week, because I stand at about the eleventh hour.*”

“*Nobody has ever said that out loud to me. About four of you have worked it out and about four of you have not.*”

“*And all ten of those mornings were somebody’s choice to make.*”

“*I am going to keep saying it.*”

“*I have no idea how to stop, and I am not going to stop on a Thursday morning and pretend to have worked out how.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four told about nine of us on Saturday that not one of us was cruel in a room for nineteen years. The cruel thing here would be to be tidy.*”

“*A hundred and twenty days. A hundred and twenty is eight hundred and twenty less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And it is the first of these figures with a zero in it, and about four of you noticed before I said the subtraction.*”

“*And the offer has not been answered.*”

“*It has not been answered by anything on that bank this week, and it has not been answered by anything not happening, and I have given the working every morning of this week and said in every one that none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and it is given to nobody by anything that has happened on that bank since Monday.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said no to a room and that was not it. A man of thirty-four said a condition and that was not it. A woman of sixty-four said a condition and that was not it. A woman of twenty-eight asked a question and was not answered and that is not it.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight has spent four days deciding whether she trusts me, and has not decided.*”

“*I have not pushed her and I have not let her, and about four of you are right about what that is and about four of you are not, and I have said so out loud at about the eleventh hour rather than have it worked out.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of sixty-four who is going to be asked why she is in a room.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-four who is going to use her name and has been told what she thinks of that.*”

“*And nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who is at a hundred and sixty-seven days and has a figure of seven that will not go up.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to me for saying a figure at about the eleventh hour on a Thursday morning on the ten mornings of this week.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a woman of forty-four kept a piece of paper with four names on it in an apron for two days was a brave thing, because she has not called it that and I am not going to for her.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday, with eight inches of water over the top step above them. Nobody had refused anything in nine days, and nobody had agreed to anything either. About four of them were still going to speak in a room four hundred miles off, one of them had said no, and one of them had asked a question that had not been answered.

---

The fever was seventy-six weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and sixty-six days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and thirty-five days old, and a hundred and twenty days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of forty-three said he can work the water out four mornings ahead now, and gave a bad reason for it out loud rather than let about nine of them make one up.
And a woman of twenty-eight said she had looked at one of the two things and not the other, and asked the four of them who were going to ask her when she would stop.

A man of thirty-four in an ash said he is going to use a woman of sixty-four’s name in a room, repeated back what she said to him about it, and did not soften it.
And a man of thirty-one said he had worked out that the only thing he knows how to do is say a figure at about the eleventh hour, and that about nine of us have been coming up at that hour on the ten mornings of this week because of him, and he is going to keep doing it.
