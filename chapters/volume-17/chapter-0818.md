# Chapter 818: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Week, And A Tuesday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Nineteenth Time And Is Eight Days On From Monday, And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Who Was Told She Could Not Attend Comes Up That Cart Road For The Second Time And Says Her Own Name And A Figure Of About Four Of Us Work It Out From The Figure

First day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. His five hundred and sixty-eighth morning. Four hundred and eighteen days after the settlement. The fever seventy-six weeks and three days. Three hundred and seventy-two days since the division. Three hundred and eighteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was off all ninety steps at about the sixth hour, and had been off since about the fifth. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and gave the ordinal out loud because it is the nineteenth time. Then he said what had happened on that bank while the water was off all ninety, which was a thing he had not expected on four mornings in nineteen years.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and none of them about the water. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week and about nine of them walk past it.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour, and it has been off since about the fifth, and that is the nineteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And it came off yesterday on the Monday, and this is eight days on from that. The eighteen before it were eight days apart, and four of the nineteen at the start were not.*”

“*And I have said that four times since the Monday before last.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath the holding. I will be giving that every morning this bank is coming back up, and it will be about four inches and eight and twelve and sixteen.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four went down those ninety steps at about the sixth hour this morning, before I gave the reading. He went all the way down and all the way back up, and he did it in about a quarter of an hour.*”

“*He did not ask anybody to come with him and nobody went. I was not asked either and I did not go.*”

“*And I want that said in about nine ears, because a man of fifty-four said it on Monday morning. He was not going to have anybody standing at the top of those steps.*”

“*And there is another thing about that man that I am not going to say this morning, and I am telling about nine of you that I am keeping it.*”

“*He has not asked me to say it and I am not going to. And I have had four days with it.*”

“*There are four times in nineteen years that about nine of us have been on that bank with the water off all ninety. I did not invite anybody for any of them.*”

“*And this is the first one that anything has happened on.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said his figure and said what going down those ninety steps was like, and said that he had not put a figure on it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has got up about half an hour earlier every morning since the Wednesday of last week. He said on Monday of last week that he would go down ninety cut steps on his own.

“*Ninety-six, and that is one more than ninety-five, and I have got it before you this morning for the eighth day running.*”

“*And I went down them at about the sixth hour, before the man of forty-three gave the reading. I went all the way down to the landing, and I stood on the stones at the bottom, and I came back up.*”

“*It took me about a quarter of an hour, and I am not going to say the figure for it, and I am not going to give it to about nine of you as a thing I did.*”

“*A figure would be a rate. I told you on Monday morning that I do not know what the rate is for.*”

“*And I still do not know, and I have not found out by doing it.*”

“*And I am not going to say what it was like.*”

“*About four of you are going to want it said and about four of you are not, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read does not have a way of putting that so that both of you get it.*”

“*What I will say is that I can see about nine steps of them from where I am standing at this hour and I could not before this morning.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight came up that cart road four miles from the low gate on foot at about the ninth hour and stopped at the top of that bank, and came up it, and said her own name in about nine ears.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and said that a second time is a rule. She was told she could not attend, and walked four miles up that cart road on the Saturday of the week before last, and stood at the bottom of that bank and did not come up it.

“*I am going to stand here, and I am not going to be about nine feet off anybody, and I am not going to be at the bottom of that cut, and nobody is going to have a reason out loud about it this morning.*”

“*My name is Sera Quill.*”

“*And I have been in this county a hundred and seventy-six days. That is eight hundred and eighteen less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has been saying *a woman of twenty-eight* in front of about nine of you for as long as that bank has had about nine of us on it.*”

“*I have been four miles down that road for most of that.*”

“*And I want one of you to tell me the reason out loud before I say anything else this morning.*”

Then about four of them worked it out from the figure, out loud, in about nine ears, gave three reasons, and a man of thirty-one gave a fourth, and it was not the best one.

“*I am not going to be asked for it one at a time. That is how a figure gets made out of a person.*”

“*Three of you worked it out. A woman of twenty-eight in this county a hundred and seventy-six days. A hundred and sixteen days on the Friday of the hundred and thirtieth week.*”

“*That is the same figure. And a man of thirty-one has been in this county longer than she has, and there has only ever been one woman of twenty-eight in it.*”

“*Give your reasons in the open. And do not give me the reason you have been wanting to give since Saturday, because I can tell the difference and so can about four of you.*”

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line and has never had a day against it. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and it cost her one thing a day of them.

“*Mine is a bad one and I am going to say it anyway.*”

“*I have said two bad reasons out loud this week, and one of them was mine, and it cost a woman of sixty-four nothing that anybody could put on a stone.*”

“*I did not put her name on anything. She was in the fourth week of a thing I was doing nineteen years ago and I had four hands to write with and I wrote my own and not hers.*”

“*And she has been four miles down a cart road for as long as that bank has had about nine of us on it, while a man of thirty-one said *a woman of twenty-eight* in about nine ears every morning.*”

“*And I have heard it, and I did not open my mouth.*”

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years. She has said out loud, in about nine of them, that a case is not a set of figures, and she will not name a room.

“*Mine is not a reason about a book.*”

“*It is that about four of you had a figure of her on that bank, and a figure is easier to be near than a person.*”

“*And I am not going to stand here and say that is kindness, because it is not.*”

“*He wanted somebody who could come back and tell him I am wrong.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years, and has refused for the four weeks from the hundred and thirty-fifth week to write a bench’s notice in it.

“*Mine, and I am going to give it before anybody asks me for it, because a boy of thirteen worked that out on Tuesday and I am not going to be the one who has to be asked.*”

“*I wrote her name in that book.*”

“*Nineteen years ago, on a page, in my hand, in a column. And it has been on that page every day since, and I did not know it was a column until a man of thirty-one said that a case is a figure of an argument.*”

“*And I have two numbers in that book in eleven years, and I have not gone back to a page I filled in nineteen years ago.*”

“*That is not modesty and it is not a rule. It is that I did not want to see it.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four has just told about nine of you that not one of us was cruel in that room. I have believed her every day for nineteen years and I have got a page.*”

“*And a name is the one thing on that bank that can come back and tell him he is wrong.*”

“*He gave the only thing he had, away, and he did it in about nine ears, and he did not do it for her.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-four days, two hundred and sixty-four being eight hundred and eighteen less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-three days old, a hundred and thirty-three being eight hundred and eighteen less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Mine is the fourth one, and it is the best one, and I am giving it because you told me not to give the reason I have been wanting to give since Saturday.*”

“*And I am not going to, and about nine of you should know that.*”

“*A name is a thing a person can be sent for about, and she was told she could not attend.*”

“*And I have been on this bank telling about nine of you that nobody in this county can be sent for about anything since before it had about nine of us on it.*”

“*And there is a name on a line four hundred miles off that a person four hundred miles off can be sent for about.*”

“*And I knew that on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week, and I have said *a woman of twenty-eight* on that bank in about nine ears every morning, and I have not said her name once.*”

“*And I am not going to say it again this morning because you have asked me not to.*”

“*And that is a fence.*”

“*It is the same fence a woman of twenty-nine named on the Thursday of the week before last, and I have spent twelve days building the same one out of a better reason.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said what the figure of the four was, said what she had come up that road for, and named the one thing she had not come for.

“*Thank you for the four reasons.*”

“*I am not going to thank you for them yet, because I have not decided, and I would rather decide before I do.*”

“*And here is what I came up that road for, and it is on about nine of you, and it is the first time anybody has said it where I could hear it.*”

“*You have all been saying it into a bank since before that bank had about nine of us on it.*”

“*There are four of us who are going to speak. A woman of sixty-four, a man of thirty-four, a man of fifty-four who has said no, and me.*”

“*And I am not going to be a fifth one, and I am not going to be the one who made it five.*”

“*And that is a figure of four people. It is not a figure of this bank, and it is not a figure of anybody who is not standing here.*”

“*And I am saying so because a woman of twenty-nine told a boy of thirteen on Thursday that a figure has to be told what it is a figure of.*”

“*And here is the thing I did not come for, and I am not going to ask any of you for it, and nobody is to go and get it for me.*”

“*I am not going to be given a day against my name this morning. I am not going to be put on a counter.*”

“*And I am not going to stand here for nineteen years and get up out of something in the open and cost somebody one thing a day of the rest of their life.*”

“*I have said the word nobody in this county can be sent for about, and I want to know what a person is called in a room where that is not in anybody’s gift.*”

“*And I am not going to be given the answer by a man of thirty-one on a Tuesday morning with ninety cut steps dry under him.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for coming up four miles, and I am not going to be told I was brave.*”

“*And about four of you said both of those things on Saturday to a boy of thirteen, and I heard about it from the bottom of that cut in a voice that carried further than it should have.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost. He gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, did not answer the question, and said that not answering it was not the question being asked twice.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-four days, and the cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-three days old.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in twelve days. It was eleven days yesterday, and it changed shape for the third time this week and it is a smaller thing again.*”

“*I want a woman of forty to keep standing at the ninth hour.*”

“*And I want to be able to stand on that bank and not be the man whose mouth every figure comes out of.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight has just told me the second one out loud in front of about nine of you, and I am not going to pretend I did not hear it.*”

“*And she asked me a question and I am not going to answer it, and about four of you are going to say I have done the thing again.*”

“*And I have.*”

“*The mistake is the thirtieth of the same mistake and it is about two hours old and it is the second-worst one of the thirty, and the worst one was the lime on Thursday.*”

“*I built a fence out of a good reason.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine named the shape on the Thursday of the week before last, and a woman of twenty-eight has just named the material on a Tuesday.*”

“*They are not the same mistake, and it is the second one that has cost somebody.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of twenty-eight has been four miles down that cart road since the Saturday of the week before last, and has come up it this morning, and about nine of us have heard her name.*”

“*None of that was because of me. All of her being *a woman of twenty-eight* was.*”

“*A hundred and eighteen days. A hundred and eighteen is eight hundred and eighteen less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And she asked me a question this morning and I am not going to answer it, and nobody is going to keep asking me it.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said on Tuesday that he was finished asking, and I watched what that did to about nine of us on Friday.*”

“*Not answering it is not the question being asked twice.*”

“*It is me, in from the first morning I stood on that bank, still being the person who decides when a question is finished.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight stood on that bank at about the ninth hour and said she is not going to be given the answer by me, and she is right.*”

“*And about four of you already knew that and about four of you did not.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a floor in a bakehouse four miles up that road, or of a name that has been on a line four hundred miles off while about nine of us said *a woman of twenty-eight* out loud.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of twenty-eight who came up four miles to say her own name.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to me for a Tuesday morning with the water off all ninety.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a man of fifty-four was brave for going down ninety cut steps on his own, and nobody is to thank me for a Tuesday morning with the water off all ninety either.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Tuesday, with ninety cut steps dry under them for the nineteenth time. A woman of twenty-eight had said her own name out loud on it, and had come up it.

The water came off all ninety steps for the nineteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, eight days on from the Monday.
And a man of fifty-four went down them at about the sixth hour before the reading, came back up, and would not put a figure on it.

A woman of twenty-eight came up four miles and came up the bank. She said her own name and the figure of her days, and asked about nine of them to give a reason out loud for a man of thirty-one not saying it.
And three of them gave a reason and he gave a fourth, and said the best one was his and that it was a fence, and he did not answer the question.
