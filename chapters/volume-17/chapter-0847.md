# Chapter 847: Second Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Third Week, And A Wednesday, And Sixteen Inches And Five Days Of The Coming Back And It Holds And The Next Time Is In Three Days, And Two Seats Decline To Join The Common Archive And The Third Is Vacant And Neither Half Of It Is The Right Of Refusal And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Writes A Day Against Her Own Name In Her Own Hand

Second day of the hundred and forty-third week. His five hundred and ninety-seventh morning. Four hundred and forty-seven days after the settlement. The fever eighty weeks and four days. Four hundred and one days since the division. Three hundred and forty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was five days of the coming back, and a full bank holds at that figure until it comes off. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and said that he had written the word holds on a slate by the water-stone on Tuesday because he had been sick of saying it in a voice.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives three things every morning. The fourth thing is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and it has been nothing for fifteen days.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is five days of the coming back, and it holds. It has held for two mornings and it will hold for two more, and there is nothing in that for anybody to get excited about and I have not got excited about it.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days, and that is the fifth day of this week. Three days after the morning it was full, and I have not looked at the water-stone, and the slate by the stone says the same figure as my mouth does.*”

Then at about the tenth hour two seats declined to join the common archive for a while, and a third of the nine was found to have nobody in it, and a man of forty-three was asked to say the count of the ninth line out loud and said it.

Hessa Vinch is thirty-six and holds the seat of the hand-bell network in the Briar March, and rang a bell at the top of that valley for nineteen years, and came up the cart road on Monday night and slept in a barn and has not been thanked for any of it.

“*I am declining, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have got, and I want them written down in a boy’s hand.*”

“*My region keeps its own terms. The bells are rung by the people who live where each bell hangs, and the terms of that are mine and my neighbours’ and not the common archive’s. I am not going to hand them over to a building in Orison, to be looked at by about nine people in another county.*”

“*And the duty part. I will publish the terms to anybody who asks, in the ordinary voice, and a copy goes on the gate at the top of the valley. If I do not do that then I have not got the duty and I have only got the right, and a right with no duty on it is a man keeping his own house and telling nobody what is in it.*”

Hal Brecht is forty-four and holds the seat of the public works compact in the Red Basin, and argued that road for eleven years, and was told on the third day of this week that he had been right about it and that nobody had been listening.

“*I am declining the common archive and I am not declining the compact, and I want those two things kept apart in about nine of your ears. If they get mixed then a road stops being mended, and I have wanted that road for eleven years.*”

“*The compact is mended by us and paid for by us, and the terms of it go on a gate where anybody can read them. The archive in Orison does not get to say whether my hard-year clause is a good hard-year clause. If it does, then a man in another county has become the man who decides what a bad year in the Red Basin is.*”

“*And I have been wrong for eleven years and I would have been wrong for eleven more. I would rather be wrong in a compact that is mended than right in a charter that is not, and that is the first sentence I have said on this landing that I actually believe.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said the count of the nine seats out loud, and said which of them were active and which of them were not, and refused to make the two figures that are not active into the same thing.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and three lines in it in eleven years, and read the arrangement’s own edits into that book on Tuesday, and did not enjoy it and has not said that he enjoyed it.

“*Nine seats. Six of them are the seats of the six stones, and all six of those are active, and the six of them keep the shared archive. That is a figure of six, and it is a figure of seats.*”

“*So here is the whole of it in the plainest sentence I have got, and I would like a boy of thirteen to write it down. A seat may decline, and the archive goes on with six. That is the ordinary shape of it and it does not take a hero.*”

“*Three of the nine are not active this morning. Two of them have declined and have said so in about nine of your ears, with the duty attached, and I have written both of those down.*”

“*The third one is the seat of the archive at Orison, and there is nobody in it, and a man of thirty-one said no to it on Tuesday before the question was finished. A seat with nobody in it is not a seat that has declined. It is a different figure, and I am not going to let about four of you add the two together and come out with a comfortable three.*”

Then a man of fifty-four was asked to say the terms of the two declining seats out loud because he cannot read. He said them, and gave the figure for the ninth line in his own mouth, and said that asking was the whole of it and that he was not going to stop.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and cannot read and holds the gravel on that landing, and has asked for a line a leaf at a time for a hundred and twenty-six mornings, and the figure is one hundred and twenty-six, which is one more than eight hundred and forty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two.

“*Read them to me and I will say them back. That is the arrangement and it has not changed in a hundred and twenty-six mornings and nobody has ever offered to change it.*”

“*The bells. The terms are that the people who live where each bell hangs may ring it, and that nobody who does not live within earshot of it may ring it. And the terms go on the gate at the top of that valley, and any person who asks may have them read to them.*”

“*The compact. The terms are that we mend the road, we pay for it, and in a year when it cannot be mended the compact says what we do. I do not know what we do and neither does the man who wrote it down, and I am going to say that last part again.*”

“*And the ninth line. A hundred and twenty-six mornings, and I have asked on every one of them. A man of about fifty-two worked it out on Sunday and told us that a day is a day, and I wrote that on a slate, and I am going to keep asking.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the two halves had to be said together or they were not the thing, and said that neither of them was the right of refusal and that he would not have it called that, and named his twelfth thing.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and has a palm and a forearm, and has named eleven things he cannot check and a twelfth on this morning, and has not named a thirteenth and says he may not get one.

“*A right without the duty is a man keeping his own rules. A duty without the right is the arrangement with a different name on it. Both halves or neither, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it, and I have said it in three sentences and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*And neither of them is the right of refusal, and I have heard two people on this landing get close to that this morning and I want it said plainly. The right of refusal is a person refusing a rule and staying. A region keeping its own terms is a region keeping its own terms. They are not the same shape, and about four of you should not go home and tell a woman of sixty-four that they are.*”

“*And my twelfth thing I cannot check. The duty is to make the terms visible. Visible to whom. A gate is visible to the people who walk past a gate, and about four of the people affected by that road do not walk past that gate. I cannot work out who is supposed to come and look, and I am not going to guess in about nine of your ears.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the duty was the only hard part, and said that a right was free, and then she took a piece of paper and a stub of pencil and wrote a day against her own name in her own hand, and said the working out loud while she did it.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on the ninth line of a book four hundred miles off, and had no day against it until about the eleventh hour of this morning. She has given nineteen years away three times. She has not said the word out loud that was said in a flat on the seventh day of this fortnight.

“*The right is free. That is the whole of what I want said about it, and I want it said by somebody who is not me, and I am not going to do this until it has been said.*”

“*I am going to write a day against my own name. Not in that book, which is four hundred miles off and which I have never been able to reach. In this one, on this landing, in a hand that about four of you have watched write before.*”

“*The working. The day I got out of a book of heads and carts was the day. I have never put it in ink anywhere and I have said the figure out loud on three mornings this week and not once in a document, and a figure that is not in a document is a thing a person says and not a thing that exists.*”

“*And I am not putting it in the arrangement’s record, and I want that said while I am writing it. A man of about fifty-two worked it out on Sunday, and the working was four words, and the four words are that a day is a day. A day in their record is a day in their record. This is a different place.*”

She wrote it while about nine of them watched, and it took her a while, and she did not stop to talk in the middle of it, and when she had done it she put the pencil down and said the rest.

“*There. And nobody is to be thanked and nobody is relieved and I am not going to be told I was brave, and a woman of twenty-nine is not going to thank me on Thursday because we are all finished being kind to each other for a fortnight.*”

“*And here is what I have done and what it has cost, in the order it happened and not in the order that would sound best.*”

“*For nineteen years nobody could be sent for me because a name with no day is a thing no arrangement can compute. As of this morning there is a name and a day, and a name and a day is a thing that can be computed, and I have made myself computable with my own pencil, in about nine of your ears.*”

“*I would have liked to keep the nineteen years, and I have wanted to keep them since I was forty-five. I have decided that keeping them by not being understood was not worth what it has just cost two people on this landing. I decided that this morning, in about a minute, with a pencil.*”

Then a boy of thirteen read the new line back out loud because a man of fifty-four cannot read, and said the whole of what was on the page, and said that it was a day and a name and nothing else, and that he was not going to say it settled anything.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-four days, and the figure is one more than the morning before, and he counted it in the lane before the light came up.

“*A hundred and ninety-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*A name, and a day against it, and nothing else on the page. No reason, no witness, no second line. I am going to say that so that about four of you do not build a settlement out of one line of pencil.*”

“*It is a day and a name and it is the first of those I have seen a person write against themselves with their own hand, and I am not going to say that settles it, and I am not going to say it is a small thing, and both of those are the same mistake in different clothes.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has asked on a hundred and twenty-six mornings and has said this morning that he is going to keep asking, and I am not going to be the one who tells him that a woman wrote a day this morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what the settlement was, and what it was not, in about nine ears, and gave every figure with its subtraction, and said that a thing being settled was a different thing from a thing being right.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and ninety-three days, two hundred and ninety-three being eight hundred and forty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and sixty-two days old, a hundred and sixty-two being eight hundred and forty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Nine seats. Six active. Two declined with a duty attached. One empty. That is the settlement, and I am going to say it in about nine of your ears in that order, and none of those four figures is a figure of a person.*”

“*And a day against a name, written by the person it belongs to, on a piece of paper on a landing, in about nine of your ears. I am not going to say that restored anything. I am not going to say it is the right of refusal, because a man of about fifty-two said ten minutes ago that it is not and he is right.*”

“*It is a settlement, and not a good one. Six seats keeping an archive, two regions keeping their own terms with the terms on a gate, one seat with nobody in it, and a day in a different place from the one it would have to be in to count.*”

“*A hundred and forty-seven days. The offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and forty-seven days old, and a hundred and forty-seven is eight hundred and forty-seven less seven hundred, and I have not answered it and nobody on this landing has answered it, and I am not going to let a woman of sixty-four’s pencil be taken for an answer.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the twelfth hour of a Wednesday with a full bank holding over the top step. Two seats had declined with a duty attached, one seat was empty, and a day had been written in a woman’s own hand against her own name in a place the arrangement cannot reach.

The fever was eighty weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and ninety-three days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and sixty-two days old, and a hundred and forty-seven days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

Two seats declined to join the common archive for a while, and both of them named a right to keep local terms and a duty to make those terms visible, and a man of about fifty-two said that both halves or neither, and that neither of them is the right of refusal and must not be called it in a mouth.
A man of fifty-four had the terms read to him and said them back, and gave his count of the ninth line as a hundred and twenty-six mornings, and said he is going to keep asking.

A woman of sixty-four wrote a day against her own name in her own hand on a piece of paper on that landing, and said the working out loud while she did it, and said she was not putting it in the arrangement’s record, because a day in their record is a day in their record and this is a different place.
She said she would have liked to keep the nineteen years, and that she had decided in about a minute with a pencil that keeping them by not being understood was not worth what it had cost two people, and that nobody was to be thanked and nobody was relieved.

And a boy of thirteen read the new line back out loud and said it was a day and a name and nothing else, and would not let about four of them build a settlement out of one line of pencil.
