# Chapter 822: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Week, And A Saturday, And The Bank Is A Full Bank And Holds, And A Man Of Forty-Four Says He Will Have The Second Leaf Read And Not Be Asked To Say No To It, And A Boy Of Thirteen Is At A Hundred And Sixty-Nine Days And Gives A Figure Of Four Who Can Check A Thing Nobody Asked Him To Check

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. His five hundred and seventy-second morning. Four hundred and twenty-two days after the settlement. The fever seventy-seven weeks old. Three hundred and seventy-six days since the division. Three hundred and twenty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was a full bank, and it held. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and gave the figure for the fever out loud before about nine of them had worked it out, and said why on a Saturday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week, and about nine of them walk past it every day now.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is four days of the coming back, and that is a full bank, and it is the first morning of four on which nothing happens on this bank.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and about six of those ninety steps are under this morning.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days.*”

“*That is four days after the morning it is full, and the morning it is full is today.*”

“*So the days of the coming back and the days to the next time are the same figure this morning.*”

“*And that is not a coincidence I am going to explain to about nine of you.*”

“*And here is a figure and I am giving it before any of you have worked it out. I did it on Friday night and I have been sitting on it since.*”

“*Seventy-seven weeks. Five hundred and thirty-nine days.*”

“*That is eight hundred and twenty-two less two hundred and eighty-three, and it comes out at seventy-seven weeks and no days and no half.*”

Then a man of forty-four said he wanted the second leaf read, and gave his reason for wanting it, and said he was not going to be asked whether he wanted it.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years. Its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. On the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week he said that it would be easier and it would be safer if there was one person who could say yes to that road, and he said in the same breath that it was not about the nine of them standing on that bank.

“*I want the second leaf read and I am going to say why before anybody asks me.*”

“*On Saturday last week I said that one voice would be easier and safer for that road than what is happening on this bank.*”

“*About nine of you heard me say it, and about four of you have not stopped hearing it since.*”

“*And that thing on the flat has a voice in it, and the voice says yes to that road in nine days.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked whether I want it.*”

“*If about nine of you ask me that this morning, I am going to say the thing I have got, and then I am going to stop talking.*”

“*And you will all have to decide on your own whether that is the same as me saying I do not want it.*”

“*I have got a floor in a house at the low gate that has not had lime in it since before a man of forty-four was a man.*”

“*And I have watched two people at that low gate get older waiting for a Tuesday that does not come.*”

“*Eleven years is a figure of the time, and it is not a figure of them.*”

“*And about four of you have been handing me the second one when I have been asking for the first.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave a figure, and said what the figure was for, and said who could check the thing he had checked, and it was not the thing anybody asked him to check.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-nine days, and he has counted them in a lane and the figure is one more than the morning before. He has a figure of seven that will not go up and it is a count of times and not a clock.

“*A hundred and sixty-nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*Here is my figure and it is four and it is not what anybody asked me for.*”

“*Nobody has asked me for anything.*”

“*A woman of forty-four said on Thursday that she was not giving a figure of about four of you this week.*”

“*A boy of nineteen asked about four of them to check a figure of his own.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight said on Wednesday that there are four of us who are going to speak.*”

“*That is three fours, and they are not the same four, and I have been keeping them in three places since Wednesday and nobody told me to.*”

“*So my figure of four tonight is this. Four people on this bank could have seen that thing come in last night and did not.*”

“*The man of forty-three, because he was on the flat at the sixth hour.*”

“*The man of fifty-four, because he sleeps on the landing and he is the only one of us who sleeps on it.*”

“*The boy of nineteen, because he comes up that road with an empty cart on the Friday, and he was not on the bank, and he said so himself on Friday morning.*”

“*And the man of sixty-one, because he was in the room with the door shut four hundred miles off, and he came back on the Friday evening and told us that.*”

“*That is four people.*”

“*It is a figure of four people and it is not a figure of this bank, and I am not going to let about nine of you hear it as a figure of this bank.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for the four. I have not given anybody anything. I have counted four, and I have said who, and the counting is the whole of it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the boy of thirteen was asking the wrong question and was right to ask it, and named his own mistake, which was that he had read the second leaf at about the eighth hour alone.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-eight days, two hundred and sixty-eight being eight hundred and twenty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-seven days old, a hundred and thirty-seven being eight hundred and twenty-two less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*A boy of thirteen has just asked whether four of us could have seen that thing come in, and he has told about nine of us who the four are, and he is right.*”

“*And the answer is yes, one of us could have, and one of us did not, and I did not.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is the thirty-fourth of the same mistake and it is about three hours old.*”

“*At about the eighth hour yesterday I read that second leaf with my back to about nine of you, on the low side of the flat, where the water was on the boards to my ankles.*”

“*I read it and I did not say what was on it, and I told myself on the way up that cut that I would say it at the eleventh hour in the order things are said in.*”

“*That is not a reason. That is the same fence with a different gate in it.*”

“*A man of fifty-four has been asking for a hundred mornings and I have stood about nine feet off him and said nothing for two days.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said what the second leaf said, and read it out loud, and said where it had come from, and said the room it was printed in.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years, and has refused since the second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week to write a bench’s notice in it, which is five weeks and two days, and he gave the figure out loud on the second day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week and is not going to give it again. He does not write down what anybody says after a thing he reads.

“*Here is the second leaf and there is a number at the top of it, and the number is nine.*”

“*The working for the nine is not in the document, and I am going to say that and not do it.*”

“*It says this arrangement is made by a thing that has been keeping the record since before a man of fifty-four was a boy.*”

“*It says that thing is not a person, and it has no face, and it has never had a face.*”

“*And it says that every one of about nine people on this bank has heard it speak at least once, in a voice that is not its own.*”

“*Then it says it does not take a seat and it cannot be sent for, because there is nobody in the room to send for.*”

“*And it says that this is the whole of why a person four hundred miles off can put a thing in front of you and you cannot put one back.*”

“*And it says the three lines that answer that are on the second leaf.*”

“*I am going to read the three and then I am going to stop reading for today, because a man of fifty-four said one leaf at a time and I am not going to be the one who decides we have had enough.*”

“*One. Any question asked of a person on this bank under this arrangement shall be answered within nine days.*”

“*Two. Any refusal shall be entered in the book of this arrangement, and the entry shall name the person, the day, and the thing refused.*”

“*Three. Any entry in that book may be corrected by the arrangement. A correction shall not require the person’s consent, and shall not be a cruelty, and shall not be sent for.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything for a while, and the man of forty-three said the water was holding and he was not going to be asked about a thing he had not read.

Then a man of fifty-four said the second leaf had a line on it that a man of sixty-one had not read out, and asked for it, and said why.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read, and has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has a hundred and one mornings of asking and the figure is one more than the morning before.

“*There is a fourth line and you did not read it, and I can tell you there is a fourth line because you turned the leaf over and looked at the back of it before you said you had read the leaf.*”

“*Read it out.*”

“*And I am telling about nine of you that I cannot read it and that I knew there was a fourth line by the sound of the paper, and that is the whole of how I knew.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it. He said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in two days, and it is smaller.*”

“*I am not going to make it bigger, because a boy of thirteen counted four of us last night, and about four of you can add up what that means.*”

“*I want a road answered.*”

“*I did not want that on Wednesday morning. I want it now, and it is in the second leaf in a room four hundred miles off, and I have had one day with it.*”

“*The mistake is the thirty-fourth, and I gave it out loud on the flat yesterday and I am giving it again here because about four of you were on that flat and I did not look at any of you.*”

“*The cost is a man of fifty-four who has just heard the second line of that leaf read out loud in about nine ears, and the second line has his day and the thing he refused in it.*”

“*A hundred and twenty-two days. A hundred and twenty-two is eight hundred and twenty-two less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the standing offer and that thing on the flat are two different offers and I am going to say that out loud this morning because about four of you have been holding them together since Friday and they are not the same shape.*”

“*One was asked of me in about nine people’s hearing on the seven-hundredth morning and has not been answered. The other arrived on Friday night with a nail in it.*”

“*And neither of them is an answer, and I am not going to hold one of them up against the other and call that a position.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of forty-four who said out loud on Saturday that one voice would be easier and safer, and who has now been offered one, and who has not stopped saying what he said.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who is at a hundred and sixty-nine days and has a figure of four that is not a figure of this bank.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a man of fifty-four hearing a fourth line of paper, eleven years after he started asking, was brave.*”

“*Nobody is to thank me for reading the second leaf out loud instead of on the flat at the eighth hour either.*”

“*Reading it out loud is what I should have done, and doing it late is not a thing to be thanked for.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and a book that can be corrected is not it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Saturday with a full bank over the top step above them and one leaf of a folded thing read out loud and a fourth line of it not read. A man of forty-four had asked for the second leaf without being asked whether he wanted it, and a boy of thirteen had given a figure of four people who could have seen the thing arrive and had not.

---

The fever was seventy-seven weeks old exactly. He had been in that county two hundred and sixty-eight days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and thirty-seven days old, and a hundred and twenty-two days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of forty-four said he wanted the second leaf read and said he was not going to be asked whether he wanted it, and gave the reason, and said what he has got instead of a reason.
And a boy of thirteen gave a figure of four who could have seen that thing come in on Friday night and did not, and named the four, and said what kind of figure of four it was.

A man of thirty-one admitted that he read that second leaf alone at the eighth hour on the low side of the flat with the water to his ankles and did not tell about nine of them, and named that as his mistake.
And a man of sixty-one read the three lines that answer a question, which are about nine days and a book and a correction that requires nobody’s consent, and then a man of fifty-four said there was a fourth line and asked for it.
