# Chapter 813: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Eighth Week, And A Thursday, And A Cart Comes Up That Cart Road With Lime In It And The Paper Says Four Barrow-Reads And The Cart Holds Two And A Bit, And Nothing Broke And Nobody Stopped It, And A Man Of Forty-Three Says A Thing With No Person In It Is The First Thing Since That Bank Grew I Could Not Use

Third day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week. His five hundred and sixty-third morning. Four hundred and thirteen days after the settlement. The fever seventy-five weeks and five days. Three hundred and sixty-seven days since the division. Three hundred and thirteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and had been that since the middle of the night. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said that tomorrow it will be a full bank, and that a full bank holds where it is until it comes off.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has held that landing for nineteen years, and has not left it for more than a day at a time in all of that. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week.

“*About twelve inches at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it, and sixteen is a full bank, and tomorrow at about the seventh hour this bank will be a full bank for the fourth morning running.*”

“*And it will hold at sixteen through tomorrow and the two mornings after that, and about four of those ninety steps will be under this morning and I have said about four.*”

“*And I am not going to give you the number for the next time and I said on Tuesday that I would not give it twice and I have not.*”

Then a cart came up that cart road at about the eighth hour with lime in it. The man driving it got it up ninety steps with a man of fifty-four on the far end of a pole. It stood on the flat of that bank beside a handcart with three barrow-loads of gravel in it.

Renn Oakes is nineteen and drives a cart up that road for a man who is not one of the four houses at the low gate, and he came up those ninety steps on a Wednesday morning with about nine of them watching and had not been told he was going to be watched.

“*I am not here about the reading and I am not here about the cart.*”

“*That is four barrow-loads on the paper at the top end. I have had that paper off the man who loaded me since about the fifth hour this morning.*”

“*And I have not read it, because I cannot read well enough to be careful.*”

“*And what came down that road is two and a bit. Not four. And I did not count it. Nobody counted it at the bottom.*”

“*And I am saying it here because a boy of thirteen said on Tuesday that about four of us are the ones who can check a figure, and I am asking the four of you to check that one, and I am nineteen and I do not know who else to ask.*”

Then a man of forty-four asked him three questions about the paper. The answer to the third one was that nobody counted it at either end, and the paper is still right about the paper.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years and its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. He has carried a cart up those ninety steps twice on his own and once with a man of forty-three.

“*Three questions and I am going to ask them in the open and about nine of you can hear them, because I am not going to walk off that bank and ask a boy of nineteen in a lane.*”

“*One. What did the man who loaded you say when he put the last load on.*”

“*He said nothing.*”

“*Two. Was there anybody else standing there.*”

“*There was a man with a stick and a string.*”

“*Three. Did either of them count it out loud.*”

“*Not one of us.*”

“*And there it is. That is the whole of what happened on that road at about the fifth hour this morning.*”

“*And it is not anybody’s fault and it is not nothing.*”

“*The paper is right about the paper.*”

“*Four barrow-loads is four barrow-loads at the top end of that road and it is two and a bit at the bottom of it.*”

“*And a figure of lime is a figure of lime and it is nobody’s business but the four houses and a bakehouse.*”

“*And the floor up there is lime and gravel and you cannot lay it with the lime alone, and that floor has been in that bakehouse since I was a boy.*”

“*And I have got a handcart on this bank with three barrow-loads of gravel in it and a near shaft that lets go at about the sixth hour, and nobody in this county can be sent for.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said that he cannot check it, gave the reason, and said how many things he cannot check.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He checked eleven figures out loud on a Tuesday and then stopped at three.

“*I am going to say no and then I am going to say why.*”

“*And the why is the same as it was on Saturday and the Saturday before that, and I am not going to shorten it.*”

“*A figure I can check is a figure I can put my hand on.*”

“*Sixteen inches I can put my hand on. Four inches a day I can put my hand on.*”

“*Seventy-five weeks and five days is five hundred and thirty days, and five hundred and thirty is eight hundred and thirteen less two hundred and eighty-three, and I can do that in my head.*”

“*Two and a bit barrow-loads of lime is not one of those.*”

“*There is no line under me at the top end of that cart road. There is no stick and no string, and no man four hundred miles off holding either of them.*”

“*And that is the fifth thing since the Thursday of last week that I cannot check. I have said it out loud every time.*”

“*And about four of you have stopped hearing me say it, and that is a fair thing to stop hearing.*”

Then a man of forty-three said a thing about a person, and a man of thirty-one had four sentences ready and did not get to use them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has held that landing nineteen years and has given three things on it every morning of them, and he says the same three things whatever the water is doing.

“*A man of nineteen came up ninety steps this morning with a figure on a piece of paper that he could not check.*”

“*And he asked four of you to check it for him.*”

“*And about four of you are going to.*”

“*And I want to say what happened on that road. I have been standing here since about the seventh hour working out what to say about two and a bit.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-nine days, two hundred and fifty-nine being eight hundred and thirteen less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-eight days old, a hundred and twenty-eight being eight hundred and thirteen less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*I have got four sentences about that.*”

“*They have been ready since about the seventh hour and I have not said one of them.*”

“*And I am going to say why at about the eleventh hour instead of at about the ninth. That is the twenty-fifth of the same mistake and I will number it in the right place down there.*”

“*The four sentences are about a figure that does not agree with a figure. They are good and they are about four hundred miles of nothing at all.*”

“*And the man of forty-three has just told me out loud, standing on this bank, that he has been standing here since the seventh hour working out what to say about two and a bit.*”

“*And that is the first thing to come up that bank with no person inside it since it stopped being about four of us.*”

“*A shaft came out of a socket and there was a man of forty-four attached to that.*”

“*A right stopped and there was a man of fifty-eight who walked four miles to stand here and say so, and a man at the low gate who did not want to be unkind.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight walked up that road four miles and stood at the bottom of it and would not come up, and she is a person, and every single one of those things has had a person in it that I could put myself next to.*”

“*And this morning a boy of nineteen drove a cart up that road with two and a bit in it instead of four.*”

“*And there is nobody at either end of it to be next to, and I have had four sentences ready for two hours and not one of them will hold.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said his figure, said what he had done with the gravel, and thanked nobody and was thanked by nobody.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has got up about half an hour earlier every morning since Wednesday, and on Tuesday he said out loud that he had never said no to a person in nineteen years.

“*Ninety-two.*”

“*And here is what a man who cannot read does with a figure of four barrow-loads that came down a road short.*”

“*I have held the gravel on this landing for nineteen years and I have never once gone up that cart road with the cart.*”

“*I go up the road to the low gate and I come back and I do not go past it.*”

“*And a man of nineteen has told me this morning that there was a man with a stick and a string at the top of that road and that neither of them counted out loud.*”

“*I cannot read and I have never been four miles up that road in my life.*”

“*And I am not going to be told what a stick and a string is for by about four of you this morning, because I would not know whether you were right.*”

“*What I can do is put my hand on a barrow-load of gravel.*”

“*And it is three barrow-loads and it has been on this bank for a week, and it is not going anywhere and I am not going anywhere either.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for saying ninety-two.*”

“*And nobody is to thank the man of forty-three for carrying a cart up ninety steps on Tuesday, and nobody is to be told any of us was brave about a week.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine in a reed came up that bank at about the tenth hour and gave a figure of a bank and said what it was a figure of.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and can read. She came up that bank on her own on the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-seventh week and on four mornings since. Her name is in ink on that ninth line and has never had a day against it. She has said that four of them are her people and nobody is going to be sent for about any of the four.

“*One hundred and eighty-four days. That is eight hundred and thirteen less six hundred and twenty-nine, and I counted it in the reed.*”

“*And I am going to say one thing, and it is a figure, and I am going to say what it is a figure of.*”

“*A boy of thirteen did that on Tuesday and it turns out to be the whole of the work.*”

“*There were about four of you on this bank on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week, and there are about nine of you now.*”

“*That is a figure of this bank and not of a place and not of anybody.*”

“*And it went up by four.*”

“*One of the four is a boy of thirteen. One of the four is a man of thirty-eight who stood at the ninth hour for six mornings and is not standing there now.*”

“*One of the four is a woman of twenty-four who came on a Tuesday and has not come back since.*”

“*And one of the four came on foot four miles on a Saturday morning and stood at the bottom of that bank and did not come up it.*”

“*And I am not saying that is a bad four. I am saying it is four.*”

“*And a boy of thirteen asked about nine of you for one thing on Tuesday and got four answers, and one of the four was not the shape.*”

“*And I am not going to work out which four, and neither is he.*”

“*And I am not going to tell him which four, because he would then have eight, and eight is not a check.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost. He gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that a thing with no person in it is not something anybody on that bank can refuse.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-nine days, and the cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-eight days old.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in seven days, and it was five days on Tuesday and three days on Sunday.*”

“*And I am not going to say anything new about it this morning.*”

“*About four of you would decide that a thing that stays still is a thing that has stopped being wanted.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is the twenty-fifth of the same mistake and it is about two hours old and it is the worst one of the twenty-five.*”

“*I had four sentences about a figure of lime.*”

“*Every one of them was about the distance between two figures four hundred miles apart in their consequences, and none of them was about a man of nineteen on a cart.*”

“*And I have been doing that since before I came to this county.*”

“*A figure with nothing in it is the one shape I have never once got wrong, because there has never been one.*”

“*The cost is that a man of nineteen drove a cart up that road with two and a bit in it and told about nine of us.*”

“*And about nine of us spent the morning on the distance between two figures, and about four of us did not spend it on the road.*”

“*A hundred and thirteen days. A hundred and thirteen is eight hundred and thirteen less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And a thing with no person in it is not something anybody on that bank can refuse.*”

“*Nobody refused the lime. There was nothing there to refuse it.*”

“*And that is the first failure since the near shaft came out of its socket that nobody on that bank could have stopped by deciding.*”

“*And I am not going to call that the right of anybody.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said no to a room on Wednesday and that was not it. A man of thirty-four said a condition on Wednesday and that was not it. Four of us agreeing is not it either.*”

“*And a thing that cannot be refused is not a thing that has been consented to.*”

“*Nobody in this county has agreed to this morning’s cart and nobody in this county had a chance to.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a handcart with three barrow-loads of gravel in it standing on the flat of that bank since the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a handcart with a shaft that lets go, or of a man of thirty-eight who stood at the ninth hour for six mornings and stopped because he noticed.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to me for four sentences I did not say, and nobody is to be told that a man of forty-three was brave for standing here thinking about two and a bit.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday, with twelve inches of water over the top step above them. A cart and a handcart were standing on the flat of it with about four and a half barrow-loads on them between them, and not one of the four and a half was ever weighed.

The fever was seventy-five weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and fifty-nine days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and twenty-eight days old, and a hundred and thirteen days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A cart came up that cart road with lime in it. The paper at the top end said four barrow-loads and the cart held two and a bit, and nothing broke and nobody stopped it and nobody counted it at either end.
And a man of thirty-one said that was the first thing to come up that bank with no person inside it since it stopped being about four of us, and that he had had four sentences ready about it for two hours and none of them would hold.

A man of about fifty-two said he could not check a figure of lime because there is no line under him at the top end of that road, and that it is the fifth thing since the Thursday of last week that he cannot check.
And a woman of twenty-nine in a reed gave a figure of that bank and said what it was a figure of, and refused to tell a boy of thirteen which four she meant.
