# Chapter 832: First Day Of The Hundred And Forty-First Week, And A Tuesday, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Reads Out Eight Mornings Of His Own That He Has Not Said In Eleven Years, And A Zone Is Walked And Marked On The Ground And Not On Any Paper, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Says Two Things Cut Into One Post Is One Post

First day of the hundred and forty-first week. His five hundred and eighty-second morning. Four hundred and thirty-two days after the settlement. The fever seventy-eight weeks and three days. Three hundred and eighty-six days since the division. Three hundred and thirty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the fourth morning of a bank sitting at that figure. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and gave the figure for the next time in a new way, and said that the new way was the old way with a day added.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has not given the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under since Monday, and he is not going to give it this week.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is all of it.*”

“*And it is seven days of the coming back, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in two days, and that is tomorrow, and I gave you three days for that yesterday morning and the three was right.*”

“*Here is the new way of saying it, and I would rather give it than have about four of you think I have stopped keeping the count.*”

“*A bank that is full holds until it comes off, and the next walking is four days after the morning it is full, and the morning it was full was the sixth day of last week.*”

“*Four days after the sixth day of last week is the third day of this week, and that is the day after tomorrow, and both of those are the same day.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two read out eight mornings of his own out loud in about nine ears, and named what he was reading and what it was not, and said the part of it that he could not check.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He said on Sunday that he would read his eight on the Monday, and it is Tuesday, and he says why.

“*I said Monday and I am standing here on the Tuesday morning.*”

“*A woman of forty-four was on that cart road when I came down it, and she said that a man of about fifty-two who says Monday and reads on Tuesday is a man who is being careful.*”

“*I would rather be that than be a man who is not.*”

“*Eight mornings. And a boy of thirteen has an eight and I have said all week that it is not his, and it is not his, and I am going to say it again now that I am using the word.*”

“*His eight is on a bank. Mine is on a sheet of deal four lines under me. Two figures of eight and neither one is a figure of anybody.*”

“*I am going to read them in the order they happened and I am not going to say what was asked for on them, because the asking is the same on all eight and the asking is not the interesting part.*”

“*The first one I was cutting a drain. That is the whole of the first one and it is a figure of a thing.*”

“*The second one I was loading lime, and the third one I was carrying gravel in that handcart which is now standing on a flat with a socket in it, and a man of forty-four knows that cart.*”

“*The fourth one I was doing nothing that belongs to this county, and I was two days off. I am not going to say what I was doing.*”

“*About four of you have just worked out that the fourth is the only one of the eight that is a morning and not a work.*”

“*The fifth, sixth and seventh are a week of work, and I could put a line under all three, and there is no point in it.*”

“*The eighth one is a Monday and it is the one I can check, and it is the only one of the eight I can put a line under, and here is the line and here is the whole of it.*”

“*On the eighth morning a person came and asked me what my line was for, and I said it was for a cart, and he wrote that down.*”

“*He is the only one of the eight who ever asked anybody on that sheet what any of it was for.*”

“*And I did not ask him his name, and I have had eleven years to think of a way of saying that I would ask him his name if I had it to do again, and I have not found one.*”

Then a man of thirty-one walked about nine hundred paces of ground and said the figures out loud as he went, and a boy of thirteen counted the houses, and a woman of forty-four stopped him cutting two things into one post.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventy-eight days, two hundred and seventy-eight being eight hundred and thirty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and forty-seven days old, a hundred and forty-seven being eight hundred and thirty-two less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*This is the zone. I am going to walk it and say the figures out loud, and a man of about fifty-two is going to check them because I have asked him to and not because he offered.*”

“*Its length is about six hundred and forty paces of mine. Two yards to a pace, and that is a man of about fifty-two’s figure and not mine, and about one thousand two hundred and eighty yards.*”

“*And that is the top of those ninety cut steps up to the cart road at the turn. Four hundred yards up to the turn and about half a mile down to the steps, and that is the whole of it.*”

“*And I want the nine of you to hear me say that I cannot check the six hundred and forty. I walked it. A man of about fifty-two did not walk it, and the two yards to a pace is his pace and not mine, and about four of you have been treating that conversion as though it made the figure checkable.*”

“*Its width is about seventy yards at the widest, and the widest is at the turn where the cart road crosses that drain.*”

“*And its depth is about sixteen inches over the top step, and that is a full bank, and a man of forty-three gave me that figure on Monday and he has given it every morning for nineteen years.*”

“*And these are the things in it and I am going to say them one at a time, and a woman of twenty-nine can stop me.*”

“*The cart road at the turn. The drain, from the turn down to a bridge about four hundred yards from an ash a man of thirty-four is standing in.*”

“*The reed, about twenty feet off the low side of that drain, and the ash, and the bridge, and the bank nine feet high with about ninety steps cut in it, and the flat at the top of those steps where this water-stone is not.*”

“*And a well about six hundred yards along the edge of that drain from this landing, which is about a hundred yards below the bridge.*”

“*Nobody on this bank has given a figure for that well before today. I did not know it was in here until a woman of twenty-nine told me at the sixth hour.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-nine days, and he has counted them in a lane, and he came down the low side on Tuesday at about the fourth hour because a man of thirty-one asked him to count houses and not people.

“*A hundred and seventy-nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I counted the houses. Eleven. And I am going to say what I counted, because a man of about fifty-two made a rule of that on the third day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week, and I am holding him to it.*”

“*Six on the low side where the drain is, going down from the turn. And five above the turn on the cart road, and none of those five is at the low gate and I checked.*”

“*Eleven is a figure of houses. It is not a figure of anybody and I am not going to let it be one on this bank.*”

“*And I did not count the people, and there were people, and I want that said in about nine ears before somebody else counts them and puts it on this.*”

Then a man of thirty-one cut the top of the zone into his own post at the turn, and a woman of forty-four stopped him in about nine ears and made him say what he had done to it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Her three numbers are all of things. She has declined three times in a week to put a thing on her counter, and she has said the counting was never the work.

“*Stop there, and say what you have just done to that post.*”

“*You have cut the top of a zone into a post you built yourself, and that post has a figure of the weather and the road and the carts on it, and now it has a line of a thing that is not the weather on it.*”

“*Two things cut into one post is one post. And a post is a thing, and one thing with two lines on it is read as one thing by anybody who comes up this road after us.*”

“*And I am not saying cut a second post. I am saying say out loud, in about nine ears, that there are two lines on that post and that they are two things and not one.*”

“*And then say it again on Saturday, because a lime line goes and a cut does not, and a cut is the only mark in this county that has ever lasted.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of the morning with a want and a mistake and a cost of his own, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it.

“*Here is the want. I want the marks to be better than they are.*”

“*Lime goes in three days at four inches a day. A cut does not go at all.*”

“*I have been standing at that post since the fourth hour wanting a mark that will still be there on Saturday. There is not one. I have known that since the fourth hour and I have been at the post anyway.*”

“*A mark that will still be there is a thing in a book. I do not have a book. A woman of forty-four keeps chairs and has three numbers and all three are of things, and there is not one of them that will hold a line.*”

“*So on Saturday the top of this zone is going to be gone, and when it is gone I am going to have to tell about nine of you again, and the telling is the work and the mark is not.*”

“*And that is the cost of standing at that post for seven hours wanting a mark that survives. I have spent a morning on a thing I cannot have, and the zone is the same length it was at the fourth hour, and eleven houses are the same eleven, and about four of you spent your morning on me.*”

“*And that is the whole of what I got wrong this morning, and it is not a figure, and I am not going to give you a figure for it because I do not have one.*”

“*A hundred and thirty-two days. A hundred and thirty-two is eight hundred and thirty-two less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of about fifty-two who read eight mornings out loud in about nine ears and could not put a line under seven of them and did not pretend to.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who counted eleven houses and said the figure out loud before anybody else did and said what he was not counting.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-one who cut a line into his own post and was stopped in about nine ears.*”

“*And nobody thanks me for having built the post in the first place. I built it coming out of a thing, and that is not a thing anybody here did me a favour in.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a man of about fifty-two reading his own eight out loud was brave. He did it on a Tuesday because a woman of forty-four made him look like a careful man, and that is not the same reason and I want it said.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning. A zone is not it. A mark on a post is not it. A line of lime that will be gone by Saturday is not it, and a person being careful about a day is not it.*”

About nine of them were on a bank and then on a cart road and then on about nine hundred paces of low ground on the first day of the hundred and forty-first week, with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them, and a zone had been walked and its figures said out loud, and there was no figure of it on any paper anywhere, and a man of about fifty-two had read eight mornings of his own that he had not said in eleven years.

---

The fever was seventy-eight weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventy-eight days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and forty-seven days old, and a hundred and thirty-two days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of about fifty-two read out eight mornings of his own and said which one of them he could put a line under and which he could not.
And a boy of thirteen counted eleven houses and named what he was not counting, and a woman of forty-four said that two lines cut into one post is one post and is read as one thing by anybody who comes up that road after them.

A man of thirty-one said that the marks would be gone by Saturday and that the telling was the work and the mark was not, and that he was not going to be thanked for having built the post at all.
And a man of forty-three gave the figure for the next time in a new way and said that it was the old way with a day added to it.
