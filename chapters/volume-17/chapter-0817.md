# Chapter 817: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Eighth Week, And A Monday, And A Full Bank About Sixteen Inches That Comes Off All Ninety Tomorrow For The Nineteenth Time, And A Woman Of Forty Is Doing The Thing A Man Of Thirty-One Has Been Doing And He Does Not Take It Off Her And Does Not Thank Her, And A Boy Of Thirteen Gives Seven And Keeps It At Seven

Seventh and last day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week. His five hundred and sixty-seventh morning. Four hundred and seventeen days after the settlement. The fever seventy-six weeks and two days. Three hundred and seventy-one days since the division. Three hundred and seventeen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was a full bank on the fourth and last morning of the four. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and said that tomorrow morning, at about the sixth hour, it would come off all ninety of those steps for the nineteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week. He said the two things that follow from that and neither of them was about tomorrow.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and none of them about anybody standing on the bank. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week and about nine of them walk past it.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is a full bank, and it is seven days of the coming back, and this is the fourth and last morning of the four.*”

“*And tomorrow at about the sixth hour it comes off all ninety. It has been off since about the fifth.*”

“*And that is the nineteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week.*”

“*Eight days on from Monday. And the eighteen before it are the same.*”

“*And four of the nineteen at the start are not, and I have said that, and I have never worked out why and I am not going to work it out tomorrow morning either.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it. Tomorrow it will be about four inches, and then about four and eight and twelve, and then full again.*”

“*And I am going to be here at about the sixth hour, and I have been here at about the sixth hour nineteen times.*”

“*And I am not going to ask any of you to be here for it.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she has been doing since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week. A man of thirty-one said he was not going to take it off her, and did not thank her.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has said out loud, in about nine of them, that a case is not a set of figures. She will not name a room.

“*A man of thirty-one said on Sunday morning that he wanted four people deciding in a lane. He is wrong.*”

“*And I want to say what I have been doing instead, and say it before he does.*”

“*I have been at the ninth hour on this bank every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week.*”

“*Not to speak. To make sure that when somebody says a thing, about nine of us have heard it.*”

“*That is the thing he has been doing since before that bank had about nine of us on it. I watched him do it on the Wednesday of last week.*”

“*And I said on that Saturday that he had put a fence up around it, and he has said so himself twice since.*”

“*And on Thursday I started doing it, in front of about nine of you, without asking him and without asking anybody.*”

“*And he noticed on Sunday and has not said anything about it for two days.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-three days, two hundred and sixty-three being eight hundred and seventeen less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*I noticed on Sunday and I have had two days with it, and I am going to say the two days out loud before about nine of you.*”

“*Because I have been doing that since before that bank had about nine of us on it and it is the only thing I know how to do.*”

“*On Sunday I was going to say, this morning, that the bank needs somebody at the ninth hour, and that it should be somebody who is not me.*”

“*And then I worked out that there is already somebody at the ninth hour, and it has been a woman of forty since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week.*”

“*And she has not once asked me whether she should be there.*”

“*And I am not going to say that to about nine of you as though I am giving it to her.*”

“*I am going to say that I have been at the ninth hour since before that bank had about nine of us on it, and a woman of forty took it off me on the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week, and I have spent two days being careful about it.*”

“*And careful is the thing I am not supposed to be.*”

Wenna Callow is forty.

“*Then here is what it costs, and you may say it in front of about nine of us.*”

“*Eleven years I have asked for a day against a name, and nobody ever asked me what I wanted instead.*”

“*And on Sunday a woman of twenty-eight asked about nine of us to say what a word means where she could hear it, and I was the only one who answered her.*”

“*And I have had four mornings of about nine of us looking at me at about the ninth hour to see whether I am going to do it.*”

“*And eleven years of nobody looking at me at all.*”

“*I have asked for a thing for eleven years. I did not ask to be looked at.*”

“*And I am going to keep doing it, and you do not get to feel bad about it, and you are not going to thank me for it either.*”

Then a man of forty-four said what he has done to his own cart this week, that he is not going to be one of the four, and why in two sentences.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years and its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. He said on Saturday of this week that it would be easier and safer if one person could say yes to a road.

“*On Saturday I said I wanted a person at the end of that road. I have had two days with it and I am not taking it back and I am not going to soften it either.*”

“*And on Sunday four of you worked out in a lane what nine of you are going to say about my cart. I am not going to be one of the four, and I have said that in front of about nine of you this morning because I would rather say it here than have it found out.*”

“*I have got a handcart on the flat of that bank with three barrow-loads of gravel in it and a near shaft that lets go at about the sixth hour. I have put two lengths of ash across the socket myself with a wedge and an old nail, and it will hold as long as that nail holds.*”

“*And I am not going to put a figure of days on it, because I have built things with nails for twenty-six years and a nail is a figure of a nail.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two told us on Thursday that a figure you cannot put a line under is not a check, and I have had six days to think about that and I have not got round it.*”

“*And I am not going to stand at the bottom of that cut tomorrow morning and watch a man of fifty-four go down ninety steps. He said he is not going to be watched at the top and he did not say anything about the bottom, and I am going to take him at his word on both.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave a figure, gave it a second time so that about four of them could check it, and said what he was not going to let it do.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-four days. He counted them in the lane this morning and it is one more than yesterday.

“*A hundred and sixty-four days. That is eight hundred and seventeen less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*And it is seven. The mornings when somebody on this bank said something that was not a figure.*”

“*I am giving it twice on purpose, this morning, at about the ninth hour and again now at about the eleventh.*”

“*A boy who gives a figure once is a boy who has got a figure. A boy who gives it twice in front of the same about nine of them is a boy who wants it checked.*”

“*And it is still seven.*”

“*It would have been nine yesterday if anybody on this bank had been counting anything except me, and it is seven because a woman of sixty-four and a man of forty-four said true things on Saturday, and a nine is a figure of this bank.*”

“*And I am not going to work out what a figure of seven is a figure of.*”

“*I know what a figure of a bank is and I know what a figure of my own days is. That is two things, and I am thirteen, and two is as many as I have.*”

Then a man of fifty-four gave his figure, said what he is going to do about tomorrow morning, and said why he was not going to take anybody with him.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has got up about half an hour earlier every morning since the Wednesday of last week. On Tuesday he said out loud that he has not said no to a person in nineteen years.

“*Ninety-five. And that is one more than ninety-four and I have got it before you this morning for the seventh day running.*”

“*And here is what I am going to do tomorrow, and I am saying it now, because a man of thirty-eight told us in an earlier week that a thing said the morning before is a thing you cannot get out of.*”

“*I am going down those ninety cut steps tomorrow at about the sixth hour. I am going to go all the way down and all the way back up.*”

“*And I have not done that in nineteen years.*”

“*And I am not going to take anybody with me and I am not going to ask anybody to come.*”

“*About four of you worked out on Wednesday of last week what I do and about four of you did not, and neither of you is going to be told.*”

“*And I have not got a figure for it and I am not going to put one on it.*”

“*A figure would be a rate and I do not know what the rate is for. I said that on Sunday and I have not changed my mind in eight days.*”

“*Nobody is to thank me for going down ninety cut steps tomorrow and nobody is to stand at the top of them waiting to see whether I can manage the bottom.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost. He gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that he did not take the ninth hour off a woman of forty, did not thank her, and could not think what else he should have done.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-three days, and the cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-two days old, a hundred and thirty-two being eight hundred and seventeen less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in eleven days. It was ten days yesterday.*”

“*And it has changed shape twice this week, and not once in the direction I have been wanting.*”

“*And I am going to say what it is now, and it is a smaller thing than it was on Tuesday.*”

“*I want a woman of forty to keep standing at the ninth hour whether or not I am on that bank. That is the whole of what I have got this morning.*”

“*The mistake is the twenty-ninth of the same mistake and it is about two hours old and I gave it to myself at about the ninth hour while a woman of forty was standing at the ninth hour not being asked.*”

“*I have spent every morning since before that bank had about nine of us on it being the person who arranges for about nine of us to be in the same place at about the same hour.*”

“*And on the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week a woman of forty stood at that hour because she had decided to, and I have spent two days since Sunday wishing she had not.*”

“*That is not taking command.*”

“*That is the thing I have been doing all week instead of taking command, and it is worse, because nobody can see it and nobody can say it to me.*”

“*A boy of thirteen gave a figure twice this morning and a man of fifty-four is going down ninety cut steps tomorrow morning for the first time in nineteen years.*”

“*Neither of those was arranged by me. About four of you have noticed and about four of you have not.*”

“*And a man of forty-four has said this morning that he is not going to be one of the four, and a boy of thirteen has given a figure twice, and a man of fifty-four is going down ninety steps tomorrow on his own.*”

“*The cost is that I could not think what else I should have done.*”

“*I have been on that bank with a want I have said out loud every morning since before it had about nine of us on it, and this morning the answer was to keep my hands off, and I have got no practice in that and I have had about an hour of it.*”

“*A hundred and seventeen days. A hundred and seventeen is eight hundred and seventeen less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning and it is not given to anybody by anything on that bank today. A man of fifty-four saying he will go down ninety steps is not it. A boy of thirteen giving a figure twice is not it. A man of thirty-one keeping his hands off is not it.*”

“*And I want to say the one that is going to look most like it from here, and it is me this morning.*”

“*A man who spends two days being careful about a thing he has been doing since before that bank had about nine of us on it, and then says so out loud on a Monday so that about nine of them can see he has stopped, is arranging to be checked again.*”

“*And he would like the checking. And the right of refusal is not restored by a man of thirty-one wanting to be watched more carefully.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a handcart with a shaft that lets go, or of a floor in a bakehouse four miles up that road.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of nineteen who was not asked on Sunday.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to a woman of forty for standing at the ninth hour.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a woman of forty was brave for standing at the ninth hour. She has asked for a thing for eleven years and standing there is not one of them.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Monday, with a full bank about sixteen inches over the top step above them. The water would come off all ninety of those steps in about nineteen hours, for the nineteenth time.
