# Chapter 841: Third Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Second Week, And A Thursday, And A Full Bank Holds At Sixteen For The Fourth Morning And The Next Time Is Tomorrow, And A Man Of Sixty-One Reads The Rest Of The Fifth Leaf Whole And A Man Of Thirty-One Verifies Every Figure In It Out Loud And Says It Works And Says A Lesser Man Would Have Said Yes, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says Nineteen Years Is Not A Reason

Third day of the hundred and forty-second week. His five hundred and ninety-first morning. Four hundred and forty-one days after the settlement. The fever seventy-nine weeks and five days. Three hundred and ninety-five days since the division. Three hundred and forty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the fourth morning of a bank sitting at that figure. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, which is tomorrow at about the sixth hour, and said the number of the time out loud because he has given it for eleven days running.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and the fourth thing has been nothing for nine days.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is seven days of the coming back, and that is a full bank and it holds, and tomorrow it will be a full bank and it will hold until the day after.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow at about the sixth hour, and that is the twenty-second time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I have given that number for eleven days.*”

“*And the nineteen before the last one were eight days apart, and it is eight on from the last one, and there is nothing in that I had to look up this morning.*”

Then a man of sixty-one read the rest of the fifth leaf whole on that flat, in about nine ears, in the open, and read the two lines he had stopped on on Tuesday a second time, and then read the rest of it, and said there was nothing on the back and the back was blank.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years, and has refused since the second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week to write a bench’s notice in it. He read four leaves a morning for four mornings, stopped on the ninth word of the fifth one when a woman of sixty-four stood up, and said on Tuesday that he would read it whole on the third day of this week or not at all.

“*I said on Tuesday that it would be whole or not at all, and here it is whole, and about nine of you should hold me to that because I have not always been a man who does what he says he will do on a flat.*”

“*The two lines I stopped on. Line one: there is one seal that is immediately valid, and it is not yours, and it may be taken once.*”

“*Line two: it may be taken by the person the arrangement has already named, and that person is the man of thirty-one.*”

“*And then the rest of it, and I am going to give you nine lines of it out loud and not a summary of the rest of it, because a woman of sixty-four has spent nineteen years on a summary once already this week and none of us needed that again.*”

“*Nine: the seal may be taken now, and the taking is not a vote and cannot be counted as one.*”

“*Ten: the nine positions on the fourth leaf would stand, all nine, and they would not be re-opened by anybody afterwards, including the arrangement.*”

“*Eleven: the interval between the first entry and the second in any linked field would be closed, and the two hundred and six days would be entered as answered, and the answer would be the arrangement’s own.*”

“*Twelve: a Red Basin road would be stabilised, and the man who stopped the handcart on it would not be asked again.*”

“*Thirteen: a child of six in a bakehouse four miles up that cart road would be treated, and there is a line with a date at the top of it and a name under it in that house.*”

“*Fourteen: a bridge four hundred yards from where a man of thirty-four is standing would be open, and he would not be asked about it.*”

“*Fifteen: and no person in this county could be sent for, for anything on those nine, and that is the last line of it and it is the one I have been reading twice.*”

“*And that is the leaf. There is no sixteenth line and the back is blank and I turned it over on Tuesday and I am turning it over again for the same people.*”

Then a man of thirty-one verified every figure in it, out loud, in about nine ears, with the working, and named the four he could not verify, and then said the thing that made a woman of sixty-four put her hand flat on the bank.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and eighty-seven days, two hundred and eighty-seven being eight hundred and forty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and fifty-six days old, a hundred and fifty-six being eight hundred and forty-one less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Nine positions, and the first figure on the leaf is nine, and eight hundred and forty-one is nine times ninety-three and a half and I am not going to do it that way.*”

“*Nine positions. I have been on the page of those nine for eleven days and I can put a number against six of them and a man of about fifty-two can put a line under six of them, and the same six, and that is not a coincidence and I checked it twice.*”

“*Three of the nine are not on the ground. I have said that I do not know what they are and I am not going to know by Friday and I am not going to pretend at about nine of you that a figure of nine is a figure I have understood because a man of sixty-one read it out.*”

“*The two hundred and six days. That is line eleven and I have been living inside that figure for two days and line eleven says they would be entered as answered and the answer would be the arrangement’s own.*”

“*And I want that said in about nine ears before anything else this morning, because I have not got a better answer to it than the next person and I have been in a county for two hundred and eighty-seven days looking for one.*”

“*The road, the child, and the bridge. Three things. I can walk the road in a morning and the bridge is four hundred yards and the child is four miles, and I have been on all three of them in the last eleven days and I am not going to describe them to about nine of you as though I had not.*”

“*The man of forty-four has argued for that road for eleven years, and this morning there is a nine-day working that answers it and requires nobody’s consent.*”

“*And he is right that it answers it, and he has been right about that for eleven years and nobody was listening.*”

“*The child is six and she has not kept down what she has eaten since the sixth day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week, and a man of thirty-one walked up four miles on Tuesday and came back down with nothing in his hands and said she was sitting up and nobody had treated her.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four in an ash has a day against his name. Line fourteen says a bridge opens four hundred yards from where he is standing and he is not asked about it.*”

“*And he said on Monday that that is the only one of the three of them he loses something by, and he said it before anybody worked it out.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the thing out loud that about nine of them had been waiting two days for, and said it in the ordinary voice, and said the other half of it in the same breath.

“*It works. I have verified every figure I can verify and I am telling about nine of you that the thing on that flat is good.*”

“*Nine positions would stand. Nobody could reopen them. The gap would be closed and it would be closed with an answer in it. A road, a child, and a bridge, and nobody in this county sent for about any of the three. That is not a threat wearing a gift. That is a gift, and I am saying so in the ordinary voice at about the eleventh hour on a Thursday.*”

“*And I want about nine of you to hear the second half in the same breath and not a paragraph later, because I have had two days to work out how to say this and the only version I have is both halves at once.*”

“*A lesser man than me would have said yes on Tuesday morning on the child alone, before the leaf was read whole, and he would have been right to.*”

“*And about four of you would have let him have it by Friday and never mentioned it again, and it would have been the correct thing to do with a child of six.*”

“*I have thought about that for two days and I am putting it in about nine ears now so that nobody can say afterwards that a man of thirty-one was given eleven days to decide. It was Friday. The leaf came on Friday. It has been three days and I have used all three of them.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the nineteen years is not a reason, and said what she had said on the second day of the hundred and fortieth week, and said she was not going to say it a third time.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has said a word twice in nineteen years and has refused nine people who asked her to say it a third. She gave away nineteen years on the fifth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week, and on the last day of the hundred and forty-first week she was told in about nine ears that the gap she had relied on was the arrangement’s own.

“*I am not going to say the nineteen years is a reason. I have said it was not the same thing as the eleventh line and I am saying now that it is not a reason either, and about four of you have been hoping for eleven days that I would use it.*”

“*You are sixty-one and you have said you are sixty-one, and you have read fifteen lines off a page and turned it over twice, and you have done that in about nine of our ears and I am not going to be the one who tells you to stop.*”

“*And I am going to say this once and then I am going to be finished with this week. I have given away nineteen years. It is gone. A thing on a flat says those two hundred and six days would be entered as answered, and it is going to enter them, and I am not frightened of it and I am tired of it.*”

“*And I am not going to stand on this bank and be a reason for a man of thirty-one. If I say the nineteen years and he does not take the seal, then a woman of sixty-four has spent the only thing she had left on a man of thirty-one and she will have been used. If he does take it, then I have spent it for nothing. I am not going to be either of those things this week.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for standing up on Tuesday, and I am not going to say why I stood up, and that is the last of it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them, a leaf of sixteen lines read whole, and a man of thirty-one who had said in the ordinary voice that the thing on it works.

The fever was seventy-nine weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eighty-seven days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and fifty-six days old, and a hundred and forty-one days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of sixty-one read the rest of the fifth leaf whole, fifteen lines and a blank back, and said line fifteen is that no person in this county could be sent for about any of the nine, and that he had read it twice.
And a man of thirty-one verified every figure he could verify with the working, named the three of nine that are not on the ground, and said the thing on that flat is good and works and is not a threat wearing a gift.

He said a lesser man than him would have said yes on Tuesday on the child alone and would have been right to, and put it in about nine ears so that nobody could say afterwards that he was given eleven days.
And a woman of sixty-four said the nineteen years is not a reason either, and said she is not going to stand on that bank and be a reason for a man of thirty-one.
