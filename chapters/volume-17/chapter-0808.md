# Chapter 808: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Seventh Week, And A Saturday, And The Fever Is Seventy-Five Weeks Old Exactly, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says The Number Out Loud And Says It Is Not A Line, And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Who Was Told She Could Not Attend Comes Up That Cart Road And Does Not Come Onto The Bank

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-seventh week. His five hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Four hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy-five weeks old. Three hundred and sixty-two days since the division. Three hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, six days of it.
And the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the number out loud and then said why he had said it out loud this morning when he had not needed to.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the week before last and about nine of them walk past it every day now and he has not asked any of them to.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is a full bank, and it is six days since it came off.*”

“*And it went off all ninety on Sunday of this week for the seventeenth time and it goes on the Monday that closes this week for the eighteenth.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath the holding.*”

“*And I said on Friday that I have never found out why the first four of those seventeen were not eight days apart and I am not going to find out this morning either.*”

“*And today is a Saturday and it is the seventy-fifth week exactly. Five hundred and twenty-five days.*”

“*And five hundred and twenty-five is eight hundred and eight less two hundred and eighty-three, and it comes out at seventy-five weeks and no days and no half.*”

“*I said that number out loud this morning before about nine of you had said it and I want to say why. It is the first whole number of weeks this bank has had since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-second week.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has been putting a figure on something every morning for about four weeks and I have been putting three on this for nineteen years.*”

“*And I have noticed that both of those are the same thing and neither of them is a line. I do not know what a man does with that and I am not going to be told.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, eighty-seventh morning, and asked for the ninth line and not the whole of the sheet, and said why the other way round this morning.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. And walked two hundred yards on Wednesday at about the ninth hour and got up half an hour earlier every morning since.

“*Eighty-seven mornings and I am asking for the ninth line this morning and not the whole of the sheet.*”

“*And on Tuesday I asked for the whole of the sheet because I did not want one particular man reading it.*”

“*And I have got what I wanted about that and it has cost somebody a figure and I am not going to forget it.*”

“*And on Wednesday a man of thirty-four came up this bank and said there was a woman of twenty-eight who has a name in ink on that line and no day against it and that she was told she could not attend. He said he had never met her.*”

“*And I have been asking that line for eighty-seven mornings for a day against a name and I am not going to say whose name and about four of you would use it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four has just said out loud that there are at least four of us on it.*”

“*So this morning I am asking for the ninth line and not the whole of the sheet, because I do not want a person standing up there reading four names while about nine of us are waiting for a day. That is what I am doing and it is mine and about four of you are not going to like it and I cannot read so I cannot check it.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight came up that cart road four miles from the low gate on foot at about the tenth hour and stopped at the bottom of that bank and did not come onto it.
And about nine of them on the bank could see her standing there and about four of them went down.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line and has never had a day against it. And she came up that bank on her own two feet on Monday and said that nobody had to be cruel for the shape she described.

“*That is her and I am not going to say her age out loud on this bank because she has not said it and about four of you have it and it is not yours.*”

“*She was told she could not attend. That is a fact in about four of our mouths as of Wednesday morning and it is the only thing any of us has said about her.*”

“*And it has been four days and about nine of us have walked past her on that road this week.*”

“*And she has come up four miles. I am sixty-four and I know what four miles is on a road you have not walked in a long time.*”

“*And about four of you have heard me give that figure about two hundred yards and got it wrong, and I am not going to do that with four miles.*”

“*She is standing at the bottom of that bank and she has not come up it. That is hers and nobody on that bank is going to tell her to come up it.*”

“*And I am going to say that out loud at about the eleventh hour so that about nine of you have heard it before about four of you get there.*”

Then about four of them went down that bank at about the tenth hour and a woman of forty said what they were not going to do when they got there.
And a man of forty-four went down as one of them and said he had a reason and it was not a good one.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and asked a man of thirty-one on Friday what he wanted and got an answer about being able to be told that he was wrong.

“*I am going down because a woman of twenty-eight is standing at the bottom of that bank and I am one of the four and I am going to stand about nine feet off her and I am not going to ask her a question.*”

“*And I am going to say why I am not. In eleven years I have asked for a day against a name and I have asked people questions about names and nobody has ever asked me a question I did not know the point of.*”

“*She has been in this county one hundred and sixty-six days and she was told she could not attend.*”

“*And about four of us have known that for four days and have walked past her on a road. If I go down there and ask her what she wants then in about four days there will be a figure of about four of us on this bank about a woman of twenty-eight.*”

“*And she will be a thing we have counted, and she has been in this county a hundred and sixty-six days and she has not been a thing we have counted.*”

“*So I am going to stand nine feet off her and not ask her anything and let her go down or not go down.*”

“*And that is the hardest thing I have done in eleven years and it is not going to cost her anything.*”

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years and has said twice in a week that he has not asked anybody for anything in nine years that he can remember.

“*I am going down because a woman of twenty-eight is standing at the bottom of that bank and about four of us have said we are not going to ask her anything and I am one of the four.*”

“*And I am going to say my reason anyway because I have had nine years of not saying things and I am not going to start doing it well today.*”

“*And my reason is that I have been a man about four of you for six days and a woman of twenty-eight has been nothing to any of us for four days and I would like to be in a photograph of it.*”

“*And that is the worst reason anybody has had on this bank this week and I have said it out loud at about the tenth hour on a Saturday morning.*”

“*And I have got a cart and a gravel bed and about four of you have not looked at that once and I am not going to make my way into it with a reason like that.*”

“*And about nine of you now know and I am going to go down there and be stood about nine feet off a woman of twenty-eight.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own figure and a figure of about four of them, and said he was starting one, and a man of thirty-eight said what the last morning of a week is for.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-four days, and he counted them in the lane this morning and it is one more than yesterday.

“*A hundred and fifty-four days.*”

“*And I am not giving a count of about four of you this morning and I am starting a new one and I am saying both of those out loud on a Saturday because about four of you have been letting me have about a week of not doing it and I would rather somebody said something.*”

“*The one I am stopping goes up by one on the mornings when about four of you say a figure and another one of you has none to say. And about nine of you were on this bank on Wednesday and a man of thirty-four said three things and a woman of twenty-nine said four sentences and a man of fifty-four gave a figure before anybody offered it.*”

“*And that is going to go up by one again this morning because about four of you have said that they are going down to a woman of twenty-eight and not to ask her anything.*”

“*And that is a figure I cannot check and I am not going to carry it.*”

“*The one I am starting is the mornings when somebody on this bank said something that was not a figure. It is three. It is three because a man of thirty-four, a woman of twenty-nine and a man of fifty-four.*”

“*And about nine of you know which three and I am not going to say.*”

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and he has stood on that bank at the ninth hour for five mornings.

“*I stood here at the ninth hour this morning for the fifth morning and I have got nothing and I am not going to say anything about the fifth of it.*”

“*And about four of us have gone down that bank to a woman of twenty-eight and I am not one of them.*”

“*And I want to say the reason and it is a bad one and I am going to say it anyway because a man of forty-four said his on Saturday and it is the only new thing on this bank this week.*”

“*I am not one of the four because she has a name in ink on that line and so have about four people on this bank.*”

“*And if I go down there and stand nine feet off her then I am a man of thirty-eight who has a day against his name going down to look at a woman who does not have one.*”

“*That is about a figure. It is about four of us and a bank and a counter.*”

“*And I have watched about four of you build that this week and I am not going to add myself to it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said the rest of what he had wanted to say on Friday and had not said, which was the number, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it.
And gave the figure for an offer that has not been answered, and said the whole of what he had wanted to say on Friday and had not said, which was the number.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and fifty-four being eight hundred and eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-three days old, a hundred and twenty-three being eight hundred and eight less six hundred and eighty-five. He has asked seven times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said. And he put it against a shaft on Tuesday and it opened about the width of a hair and about nine of them saw it and not one of them said anything.

“*A man of forty-three said a whole number of weeks out loud this morning before any of us had said it and said it is not a line.*”

“*And about nine of us heard that and I have been putting a figure on a day for about four weeks and I have been calling it a line in my own head and I am not going to stop doing that but I am going to say what a line is not.*”

“*A day is where a thing was on the day somebody wrote it. A man of about fifty-two said that on Thursday and I did not take it in and I have had four days to take it in and he is right and he has been right for eleven years about four hundred miles and I have been hearing it as a distance.*”

“*That is what I have wanted to say since Friday and I did not say it on Friday. I said a thing about being able to be checked and about four of you did not believe it.*”

“*And I have been not saying the rest of it for a day and a half.*”

“*The rest of it is this. Four hundred miles is a figure of a distance. I have given that figure in about nine of our mouths for about four weeks as the shape of the problem.*”

“*And about nine of us have been standing on this bank about four hundred miles from the only place the problem can be served, and calling that a distance.*”

“*It is not a distance. It is a place where four people can be sent for and about nine of us on this bank cannot be.*”

“*And I have been saying the number and not the sentence.*”

“*And I have known the difference since Wednesday and I have been saving it for a morning when I could make it land, and there was no such morning.*”

“*That is the mistake and it is the twentieth of the same mistake and it is a day and a half old and it is not the one I have been telling about all week. The one I have been telling about is about arranging to be told things in front of nine people. This one is worse and I have been sitting on it since Wednesday afternoon because it was going to be a good sentence.*”

“*And the cost is that I have spent a day and a half on a sentence instead of on a woman of twenty-eight, and a boy of thirteen has stopped a count of his own because of what a man of forty-four said out loud on Saturday, and neither of those is going to be given back.*”

“*A hundred and eight days. A hundred and eight is eight hundred and eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And it is the seventy-fifth week exactly this morning and a man of fifty-four has been asking for a day against that ninth line for eighty-seven mornings and about four of you have been here all six days and nobody has asked a woman of twenty-eight anything except by not asking her anything at about the tenth hour.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman standing at the bottom of a bank. Nobody says a word of thanks to me for a sentence I have been saving since Wednesday afternoon for a morning when it would land. Nobody is to be told they were brave about any of it.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning and it is not given to anybody by about four of us standing nine feet off a woman. That is not a refusal. She has not been asked and a person who is not asked has not refused anything.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four said that at the top of a cut on Sunday and about four of us heard him and about nine of us are about to do the thing he was talking about.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight did not go down there this morning, and he said the reason out loud.*”

“*And his reason is that he would have been a figure. That is the closest any of us has got to it in a week and he got there by not doing something and by saying so.*”

“*And I am not going to call that a refusal either, because a man is not refusing a woman by staying on a bank.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Saturday with a full bank about sixteen inches over the top step above them.
And about four of them were at the bottom of it about nine feet off a woman of twenty-eight who had walked up four miles and had not come up the bank.

The fever was seventy-five weeks old, exactly, for the first time since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-second week, and a man of forty-three said the number out loud before about nine of them and said it was not a line.

A woman of twenty-eight who was told she could not attend had walked up that cart road four miles and was standing at the bottom of that bank.
And about four of them had gone down to stand about nine feet off her and were not going to ask her anything.

A man of thirty-eight said he was not going down because he would have been a figure.
And a man of thirty-one said that four hundred miles is not a distance but a place where four people can be sent for and about nine of us cannot.
