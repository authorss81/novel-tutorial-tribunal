# Chapter 808: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Seventh Week, And A Saturday, And Four Hundred And Eight Days After The Settlement, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Says Out Loud With The Working That The Fever Is Seventy-Five Weeks Old, And It Is The First Time In Seventy-Five Weeks That Figure Has Been Said In A Mouth On That Bank

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-seventh week. His five hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Four hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy-five weeks old. Three hundred and sixty-two days since the division. Three hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The water was about sixteen inches on that cut at about the seventh hour and it was six days of the coming back, and the man of forty-three gave the figure and said that two days is the whole of what is left of it, and gave the day.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and a fourth he only says when somebody asks, and a fifth he keeps.

“*Sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is six days since it came off, and it holds, and it has held two mornings and it will hold two or three more, and it comes off about four days after it comes to sixteen, and I have said that for nineteen years.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it, and the next time all ninety of those steps can be walked is in two days, and that is the Monday of this week, and it will be the eighteenth time, and after that I will have given that figure two hundred mornings and about four of you will not have any idea what I have been doing with it.*”

“*And the fifth thing is still kept and still is not written down, and I am not going to talk about it this morning, and about four of you will hear that as a refusal and it is not one, it is a man of forty-three who has run out of sentences.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said a figure out loud at about the seventh hour that he had no business saying at that hour, and a woman of forty-four said it again ten minutes later with the working, and said why she was going to say it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-four days, two hundred and fifty-four being eight hundred and eight less five hundred and fifty-four. Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and she has had the figure of it ready since Monday.

“*He said that. A man of thirty-one said that figure ten minutes before I was going to say it, on a Saturday, in front of about nine people, and he has been on this bank eleven days and I do not think he knows he took it.*”

“*The fever is seventy-five weeks old, and the working is this. It is eight hundred and eight days. Seventy-five weeks of seven days is five hundred and twenty-five. Eight hundred and eight less two hundred and eighty-three is five hundred and twenty-five. Five hundred and twenty-five divided by seven is seventy-five exactly, with nothing left over, and I have done that division out loud twice on this counter since Monday and both times it came to seventy-five and no remainder.*”

“*And here is the thing I want said about it and nobody has said it in seventy-five weeks. That figure has been right every morning for seventy-five weeks and not one person has ever checked it. There is a man of about fifty-two on this bank with four lines under him and he has used them every morning since Wednesday. There is a man of forty-three with four inches a day that anybody can stand at the top of these steps and measure. And this figure has been said in a mouth exactly once, this morning, by two people, and one of them took it off the other before she got to it.*”

“*Being right and being able to be checked are two different things and I am not going to be the one who says it, because a man of about fifty-two said a version of it on Wednesday and I was standing here when he said it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open, eighty-seventh morning, and a woman of twenty-nine read it, and said one thing about the seventh morning she has read it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, and on Friday of last week he asked for a day against that line and was not granted one, and his wheel of iron is still in the mud at the foot of that stair where he put it down on Saturday of last week.

“*Eighty-seven mornings, and eighty-seven is eight hundred and eight less seven hundred and twenty-one, and it is seven mornings since Saturday and the wheel has not moved and nobody has been down there except us.*”

“*And I am going to say the thing that about four of you have been not saying since Wednesday, and then I am going to stop. That wheel is mine. That cart is mine. That road is nobody's. That gravel is going to a bakehouse four miles up it on a Wednesday and it has gone on a Wednesday for eleven years and it will go on a Wednesday next week if I can lift it.*”

“*And none of those three things knows about the other two. I have worked that out and it has taken me a week and it is the only thing I have worked out. And if you ask me what the cause is I will tell you there is not one, and I am fifty-four and I have held gravel for nineteen years and I am telling you that the three of them are not joined, and I do not know what a thing is when three things that are not joined fail in the same week.*”

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about a hundred yards off that drain a hundred and seventy-nine days, a hundred and seventy-nine being eight hundred and eight less six hundred and twenty-nine, and she can read, and her name is in ink on that ninth line and there has never been a day against it.

“*I have read that line seven mornings now and this morning is the seventh, and I did not read it on Wednesday because I was not on this bank on Wednesday, and about four of you noticed, and one of you said out loud on Wednesday that I was not going to be asked to go up that road, and I was not going to be asked, and I was not asked, and I was also not here, and those are two different things.*”

“*And I am going to say one thing about that line and it took seven mornings. It has got better to read. It is the same line and it is the same words and none of the words have changed and about four of you keep saying it has got worse. It has got better to read and it has got no better, and both of those are true and about four of you have been using the first one to not have to look at the second one.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight came up that bank on her own and gave her own figure, which nobody has given for six mornings.

A woman of twenty-eight came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-six days, a hundred and sixty-six being eight hundred and eight less six hundred and forty-two, and about nine people gave that figure for her every morning for about three weeks and then two men stopped in the same week without either of them saying so.

“*I am going to give my own figure because about four of you have noticed that nobody has been giving it and I have noticed it more than about four of you.*”

“*A hundred and sixty-six days, and that is eight hundred and eight less six hundred and forty-two, and it is a hundred and seven mornings since I came up that cart road, and that is eight hundred and eight less seven hundred and one.*”

“*I have not given a name this morning and nobody is going to make me. I have been on this bank a hundred and sixty-six days and I have never been asked for it and I have not offered it, and it is not a refusal, because I have not decided anything about it. I have simply not got round to it and I am not going to pretend that is a decision I made.*”

“*And here is what I would like, and it is the only thing I have come up here for. A man of sixty-one gave my figure of days out loud every morning for about three weeks and stopped on a Sunday. A man of thirty-one gave it out loud every morning for about three weeks and stopped on a Sunday. Neither of them told me they had stopped and I found out from about four of you.*”

“*And in three weeks I have had my figure of days read out loud by two men about nine times each and I have been asked one question. So I would like one of you to ask me what I check.*”

“*And if nobody asks me then I am not going to say it, and that arrangement has worked for a hundred and sixty-six days and it is the only one I have ever had that worked, and I am not going to be asked to say that it is a refusal, because I have not been refused anything and I have not refused anything and I have been left alone, and I know what I am.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts, and a boy of thirteen gave two of his own and one of a man of forty-three's, and a man of thirty-one gave the want and the mistake and the cost.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter sixty-four mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter fifty-eight mornings with a number on them and nothing to write a name in and no names on them.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-five days, a hundred and fifty-five being eight hundred and eight less six hundred and fifty-three, and he has been counting the mornings about four of us do not say a figure.

“*A hundred and fifty-five days, and it is one more than yesterday, and I counted it in the lane.*”

“*And seventy is the figure for the thing a man of forty-three is keeping, and seventy is eight hundred and eight less seven hundred and thirty-eight, and he has still not written the day down, and I have still not given him the figure twice, and I am not going to.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight has just asked about four of us to ask her one question. And I am thirteen and I have three questions and I am not going to be the one, because if a boy asks then it is a boy asking and she has been here a hundred and sixty-six days waiting for somebody who counts.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-four days, and the cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-three days old, and he has asked about it eight times in about a fortnight and nobody has said, and he did not ask this morning.

“*A woman of forty-four had that figure ready since Monday and I took it out of her mouth ten minutes before she got to it. That is the twenty-fourth of the same mistake and it is mine and I made it at about the seventh hour in the ordinary voice, and the reason I made it is that I have been carrying it since the fourth morning of this volume and I wanted it said.*”

“*That is the whole of the mistake and it is the first one this week that I did not make in a hurry. On Saturday I looked at a wheel. On Sunday I looked at a boy. On Monday I looked at a road. On Thursday I looked at a woman. And this morning I looked at a figure of mine and got to it first.*”

“*A man of fifty-four has said out loud this morning that three things have failed in the same week and none of them knows about the other two, and he is right, and I have been able to see it since Wednesday and I have not said it because saying it is putting it in a list, and a list is a thing about four of us do not say a figure on.*”

“*The want is the same and I said it out loud yesterday for the first time in eleven days and I am not going to say it again this week. I want somebody who can be asked who says no and comes back the next morning and is asked again. And the wanting to be checked is inside that and I said it yesterday and about four of you heard it and I do not know what a person does with having said a thing like that in front of people.*”

“*A hundred and eight days, and a hundred and eight is eight hundred and eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer, and seventy-five weeks is not an answer either, and a man of fifty-four being right about three things is not an answer, and I am leaving all of them alone.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of twenty-eight came up that bank this morning and asked about four of us to ask her one question, and I did not ask it, and I did not ask it because I had taken a figure off a woman of forty-four four minutes earlier and I did not think I was fit to ask anybody anything else before lunch.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of any of this morning. Nobody thanks me for saying the fever out loud. And the right of refusal is not restored this morning and it is given to nobody, and a woman of twenty-eight has just said out loud that she has not been refused anything and has not refused anything and has been left alone, and she is right, and about four of you would like her not to be.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Saturday with sixteen inches of water on that cut, the third morning of a full bank, and a wheel of iron still in the mud at the foot of a stair nine feet below them.
