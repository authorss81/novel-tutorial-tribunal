# Chapter 816: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Eighth Week, And A Sunday, And About Four Of Them Agree In Front Of The Rest That They Will All Say The Same Thing About The Lime, And A Fifth Person Is Not Asked, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says That Agreeing Is Not Being Asked And Is Not Anybody Refusing Anything, And Nothing Is Refused And Nothing Is Agreed

Sixth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week. His five hundred and sixty-sixth morning. Four hundred and sixteen days after the settlement. The fever seventy-six weeks and one day. Three hundred and seventy days since the division. Three hundred and sixteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was a full bank and it held there for the third morning out of four. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and said something about a woman of twenty-four that nobody else on that bank had said for five days.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years and has said three things on it every morning of them. He is sixty years old next and he has not told anybody that either.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is a full bank, and it is six days of the coming back.*”

“*And this is the third morning out of four where nothing happens on that bank, and there is one more of them tomorrow.*”

“*And I am going to say a thing about this morning and it is not about the water and it is the only thing I have got.*”

“*A woman of twenty-four stood at the bottom of that cut on Tuesday morning at about the ninth hour while I was giving three things, and she did not come up.*”

“*She stood there for about half an hour.*”

“*And about nine of you have been talking since Saturday about a woman of twenty-eight four miles up that road, and about four of you have not once said the word twenty-four.*”

“*I am not asking anybody about her. I have given three things every morning for nineteen years and I am not starting to ask about a woman at the bottom of a cut on a Sunday.*”

“*And I am saying her age out loud because about four of you have stopped saying it, and a figure of a person is a figure of a person and not a figure of a bank.*”

Then about four of them agreed, in front of the rest, that they would all say the same thing about the lime. A fifth person was not asked, and he said out loud that he had not been asked and was not going to pretend he had.

“*I am going to say this so that it is said.*”

“*About four of us worked it out in the lane on Saturday morning without saying it to anybody.*”

“*About nine of us are going into a room four hundred miles off and every one of us has a different reason and about nine of us are going to come out of that room saying different things about the same cart.*”

“*And the cart is two and a bit barrow-loads. That is the one thing we agree about.*”

“*And we are going to say it the same way, all of us. That is allowed and it is not a lie.*”

“*And about nine of you are going to hear four people say the same true thing and it is going to sound like five.*”

“*And I am going to stop there and I am not going to agree to the rest of it.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been on that bank since before it had about nine of us on it telling us that a figure of a thing we all said is worth less than a figure of a thing one of us said.*”

“*He has been right every time he has said it.*”

Renn Oakes is nineteen and drove the cart up that road on Thursday morning and stood on that bank with a piece of paper that said four barrow-loads on it.

“*I was not asked.*”

“*I am not saying anybody has done anything wrong.*”

“*I am saying that four people in a lane on Saturday morning decided what nine of us are going to say, and I was the one they did not ask.*”

“*And I am the only one who was in that lane who knows there is a piece of paper.*”

“*And it is my paper. That is not a small thing. About nine of them have agreed to say the same thing about my cart and I am nineteen and I am the one holding the only bit of paper there is.*”

“*So I am going to say two and a bit barrow-loads out loud this morning, in about nine ears, whatever any of you said in a lane on Saturday.*”

“*And that is me refusing to be agreed with, and it is not a big thing.*”

“*I would rather do it in the open than have somebody come and tell me afterwards that four of them had already said it, and ask whether I want to change mine.*”

Then a man of thirty-four in an ash said what he thought about that, and said it was the right shape and not the right size, and that he had done the same thing on Friday to a woman of sixty-four.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four and has a day against his name which is not a day of this week. He has been in an ash, about twenty feet off the edge of that drain and a hundred yards along it, for a hundred and eighty-six days.

“*A hundred and eighty-six days. That is eight hundred and sixteen less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*And I am going to say two things and stop at two, which is the same rule I have used twice.*”

“*One. He is right.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about two and a bit barrow-loads of lime. And if four of us say it the same way then the four of us are the only thing anybody can check it against, and I do not want that.*”

“*Two. I did exactly that on Friday morning to a woman of sixty-four, and I told about nine of you I was going to.*”

“*And I want it noticed that it is the same shape and it is not the same size.*”

“*On Friday I used a woman. This morning four men used a boy of nineteen with a piece of paper in his hand.*”

“*And I do not know which of those is worse and I am not going to be the one who says.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two checked three figures out loud and stopped at three, and said which three, and said what he would not check.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He has stopped checking figures out loud after three mornings, more than once.

“*Three, and I am going to say which three so that nobody has to wonder whether I stopped at three because I could not see.*”

“*One. Sixteen inches over the top step. I can put my hand on that and I did.*”

“*Two. Seventy-six weeks and one day. That is five hundred and thirty-three days, and that is eight hundred and sixteen less two hundred and eighty-three, and I can do that in my head.*”

“*Three. Two and a bit barrow-loads of lime. I can hear it. That is not the same as being able to check it and I am not going to pretend that hearing a thing out loud in about nine ears is a line under me at the top of that cart road.*”

“*And I am not going to check a fourth and I am not going to say why, because the why is the same one I gave on the Thursday of last week and about four of you have stopped hearing me give it.*”

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He stood at that bank at the ninth hour on six mornings running and then stopped, and said on Monday that he was not going to look down again.

“*I am not standing at the ninth hour this morning and about four of you have been treating me as a man who cannot bear it.*”

“*I have got a board in the inside of this coat. I have not opened it in the week before this one and I am not going to open it this week, and I stopped saying how old it was on purpose, and a man of thirty-eight said out loud on the Saturday that he had stopped.*”

“*And I am not going to start again this morning because about four of you have been standing near me at about the ninth hour as though there was going to be something at the ninth hour.*”

“*There is not going to be anything at the ninth hour. There is a bank with sixteen inches on it and about nine of us on it and I am one of them, and nobody is to ask me what the thirty-two notches are for.*”

Then a woman of forty said that she had done it twice this week and had told about nine of them both times, and gave the two things she had done.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years. She will not name a room. She counted the number of times a man of thirty-one had said he did not know in nine days, out loud, and then asked him what he wanted.

“*I have done it twice and I am going to say both of them.*”

“*A man of thirty-four has just said the right shape and not the right size. He is talking about himself and he is talking about me.*”

“*On Friday I stood at about the ninth hour and made sure that when a man of thirty-four said three things, about nine of us had heard all three.*”

“*And I had been at the ninth hour every morning this week.*”

“*And on Saturday I stood nine feet off a woman of twenty-eight and let her decide whether to come up that bank.*”

“*And about four of you said that was the hardest thing anybody had done on that bank in a week.*”

“*And neither of those was asked of me by anybody, and neither of those is a thing a person can be sent for.*”

“*And both of them are me deciding where about nine of us stand.*”

“*And I am not going to stop doing either of them and I am not going to be thanked for them.*”

“*And I am saying them out loud because a boy of thirteen worked out on Tuesday that a thing said in a lane is not a thing anybody can check.*”

“*And because a man of thirty-four has just said out loud that what I did on Friday was the same shape as what four men did in a lane on Saturday, and I would rather hear that from him than work it out on my own at about the eleventh hour.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost. He gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that about four of them agreeing in a lane is not anybody refusing anything and not anybody consenting to anything either.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-two days, two hundred and sixty-two being eight hundred and sixteen less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-one days old, a hundred and thirty-one being eight hundred and sixteen less six hundred and eighty-five. He has asked seven times whether it will keep, nobody has said, and he is not going to ask an eighth time.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in ten days. It was nine days yesterday, and I am giving you the run so that about four of you can work it back to the Sunday it stopped.*”

“*And I am going to say something this morning that I have been not saying since about the ninth hour on Friday, and about four of you have noticed that I have been not saying it.*”

“*On Saturday a man of forty-four said it would be easier and safer if one person could say yes to a road. A woman of sixty-four said what that is from the inside.*”

“*And I said I had wanted the same thing since before that bank had about nine of us on it.*”

“*And this morning about four of them agreed in a lane to say the same true thing about a cart, and did not ask the one man who was holding the paper.*”

“*A man of nineteen got up this morning and said so. A woman of forty said she has done the same thing twice this week and said both times out loud.*”

“*And here is what I have been not saying. That is the thing I have wanted since before that bank had about nine of us on it, and I did not know it until Saturday, and it is not a person at the end of a road.*”

“*It is four people deciding in a lane what nine of us are going to say, and all of them being right.*”

“*And me being the person who has been arranging for about nine of them to be in the same place at about the same hour since before that bank had about nine of us on it.*”

“*The mistake is the twenty-eighth of the same mistake and it is about two hours old and a man of nineteen gave it to me at about the ninth hour in front of about nine of you and he did not know what he was doing.*”

“*I have wanted somebody who can come back the next morning and tell me I am wrong.*”

“*And about four of you agreeing in a lane about a thing I have not been asked about is the closest anybody has come to that since I came to that bank.*”

“*And it is not that, and about four of you are going to say I am being careful again, and I am going to say the rest of it before you do.*”

“*The cost is that a boy of nineteen stood on that bank this morning and said the words I was going to use.*”

“*And about nine of us heard him use them first.*”

“*And I have had every morning since I came to that bank of getting there first, and I would not have wanted to get there first this morning.*”

“*A hundred and sixteen days. A hundred and sixteen is eight hundred and sixteen less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning. Nothing was refused on that bank on Saturday and nothing was refused on it this morning.*”

“*About four of us agreeing in a lane is not a refusal. It is not being asked.*”

“*And a person who is never asked has not refused anything, which is a different thing and is not the right of refusal either.*”

“*And that right is not given to anybody on this bank by anything that happened on Saturday or this morning.*”

“*And about four of them agreeing is not consent either.*”

“*They agreed about a figure of lime and not about whether to go. A man of nineteen was not asked.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-four was standing at the bottom of that cut on Tuesday morning, and nobody has asked her anything, and nobody has been refused by her.*”

“*And a boy of nineteen said no to four people this morning and was thanked for it by nobody, and a man of thirty-four said that a man who has to be asked has already been asked, and I want both of those said out loud in the same breath as the four of them in a lane.*”

“*And a man of forty-three has just given her age out loud because about four of us had stopped saying it.*”

“*That is not a refusal and it is not a rule, and it is the only thing that happened on that bank this morning that I would put a name to.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of nineteen who was not asked, or of a woman of twenty-four at the bottom of a cut.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-four in an ash who can see himself.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to me for a Sunday morning, and nobody is to be told that anybody on that bank was brave about any of it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Sunday, with a full bank about sixteen inches over the top step above them. Nobody on that bank had refused anything, nobody on it had agreed to anything either, and a woman of twenty-four had not come up.

---

The fever was seventy-six weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and sixty-two days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and thirty-one days old, and a hundred and sixteen days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

About four of them agreed in a lane to say the same true thing about two and a bit barrow-loads of lime, and did not ask a boy of nineteen who was holding the only piece of paper there is.
And the boy of nineteen said he was not asked, and refused to be agreed with in the open, and a woman of forty said she has done the same thing twice this week and told about nine of them both times.

A man of forty-three said out loud the age of a woman who stood at the bottom of that cut on Tuesday morning, because about four of them had stopped saying it.
And a man of thirty-one said that agreeing in a lane is neither a refusal nor a consent, and that the right of refusal is given to nobody by anything that happened on Saturday or this morning.
