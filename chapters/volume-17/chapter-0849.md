# Chapter 849: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Third Week, And A Friday, And Sixteen Inches And Seven Days Of The Coming Back And It Holds And Tomorrow At About The Sixth Hour It Comes Off For The Twenty-Third Time, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Has Been Away For Fifty Mornings Names Two Dead Persons Out Of Eleven And Says They Are Dead, And A Man Of Thirty-One And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Renegotiate What Each Of Them Carries, And A Man Of Thirty-Four Is Asked About A Bridge

Fourth day of the hundred and forty-third week. His five hundred and ninety-ninth morning. Four hundred and forty-nine days after the settlement. The fever eighty weeks and six days. Four hundred and three days since the division. Three hundred and forty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was seven days of the coming back, and it holds. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time. He said that tomorrow at about the sixth hour about ninety steps would be walkable, and that he had been giving the number of the time for twenty-two mornings, and that nobody had ever once asked him to stop.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives three things every morning. The fourth thing is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and it has been nothing for seventeen days.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is seven days of the coming back, and it holds. That is the seventh day it has held, and I have said the word seven times this week and I am not going to apologise for the number.*”

“*And tomorrow at about the sixth hour about ninety steps can be walked, and that is the twenty-third time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week. I have given that number for twenty-two mornings, and nobody has ever asked me to stop giving it.*”

Then at about the ninth hour a woman of forty-four who keeps twenty-nine chairs came up that cart road for the first time in fifty mornings. She named two dead persons out of eleven, gave the working, and refused to have either of them thanked for or carried.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and had not come up that cart road since the Thursday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week until this morning, and has a question she asked three times that nobody on that bank has answered. Eleven of her chairs have a name against them in a book and nobody in them.

“*Two of the eleven are dead. I have said that out loud in a room four hundred miles off once in nineteen years and I am saying it now, and about four of you have never heard it, and that is not anybody’s fault except mine.*”

“*One of the two has been dead since before the last flood came up, and nobody wrote the day down, because there is no form in this town that asks anybody to write a day down. I have had that day in my own head for nineteen years, and I have not put it on the paper, and I am not going to put it on the paper this morning.*”

“*And the other one went away in a lighter about eleven years ago and has not been heard of since. His name is on a chair at the other end of the book, and a chair is not a grave, and I am not going to use the word.*”

“*They are dead. Neither of them is to be carried as a cost, neither of them is to be thanked for, and about four of you are not to say that the settlement was for them.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the figure of about four of her own out loud, and a woman of twenty-nine said what it was and what it was not, and refused it by name in about nine ears.

Orla Fennimore, forty-four, gave the figure and would not give it as anything else. A woman of twenty-nine in a reed had been told about it on Wednesday and had not been asked for anything since.

“*And here is the other figure and I am going to give it whole and I am not going to dress it. It is about four of my own. Not mine. Four people whose names are in my book and who have said they will stand up and say the same thing at the same time if somebody asks them a question.*”

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about twenty feet off the low side of that landing, a hundred yards along the same drain, for two hundred and twenty days. The figure is eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and twenty-nine.

“*About four of them agreeing to say one thing at one time is not consent, and I am not going to let it go into a record as consent. I have refused that word for it four times in the last book and twice in this one, and this is me refusing it in front of you.*”

“*Four people who will speak together is a thing that is easy to arrange. Four people who will be asked separately is a thing that is hard to arrange. Only one of those two is worth having, and a woman of forty-four has offered me the first, and she is the best keeper of chairs I have ever met.*”

Orla Fennimore, forty-four, heard the refusal, and said she was not going to argue with it, and that she had made the same refusal herself in a room four hundred miles off.

“*Then it is not consent, and it goes in the book as four people who will speak together and nothing else. I am not going to be cross with a woman of twenty-nine in about nine of your ears, because she said a thing I have said myself in a colder voice.*”

Then a man of thirty-one and a woman of twenty-eight divided what each of them was carrying, out loud, on a bank, in about nine ears, and gave the working. She said it was not a third no and it was not a yes.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for in this county or in the one she came from, and has been in this county two hundred and seven days, and the figure is eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and forty-two. She has said no twice.

“*Two hundred and seven days, and the working is eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and forty-two, and I am going to use it for something other than a day count this morning.*”

“*On the Wednesday of last week I told about nine of you that my two things were a man of thirty-one and a line on a leaf about nine hundred feet from that landing. They are not the same thing, and I had had them the same for four days, because it is easier to be frightened of one thing than of two.*”

“*They are still not the same thing. They are two hundred and seven days old and nobody has made them one, and I have come to say which of them I am putting down and I want it in a mouth that is not his.*”

“*I put down the man. From today I do not carry a man of thirty-one, and I have not carried him since I was asked to in the first place, and I have been pretending the pretending was work.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and ninety-five days, two hundred and ninety-five being eight hundred and forty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and sixty-four days old, a hundred and sixty-four being eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*And I am picking up the man, and I am not picking up the leaf. A man of about fifty-two on Sunday worked out that a day is a day, and that the two things cannot stand in the same place. I am applying that to two people, and it is a different job, and about four of you should be suspicious of it.*”

“*Here is the cost and I am naming it before she has to ask, because she named hers first on Sunday and I am not going to be a person who learns that from other people.*”

“*If I carry the man, then I am a man with a thing attached, and the thing is a leaf four hundred miles off that says a day in a gap is answered, and a person who carries that can be used with it. She carries the leaf alone from today and I do not know what that costs her and I am not going to ask her here.*”

“*And this is not a third no and it is not a yes. It is two people moving a load each, and I have asked nobody’s permission and been given none, and that is the whole of the working.*”

Then a man of thirty-four who lives in an ash a hundred yards along that drain was asked about a bridge four hundred yards from where he is standing, for the first time. He said yes, wrote a day against his own name in his own hand, and said what that had cost.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four and has been in an ash two hundred and nineteen days, and the figure is eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and thirty. Line fourteen of the fifth leaf said that a bridge four hundred yards from where he stands would open and he would not be asked about it.

“*Two hundred and nineteen days, and that is eight hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and thirty. Nobody has ever given me that figure in a mouth until this morning, and I have counted it myself every day since the second day of the hundred and thirty-first week.*”

“*Yes. I want the bridge. I have wanted it in about nine of your ears since a man of thirty-one read line fourteen out on the Thursday of last week and said I would not be asked about it. I have not said so out loud until this morning, and you are asking me now. That is the whole difference and I would like it written down.*”

“*It is mended. Not by an arrangement. By about four men and a woman of thirty-three with a trowel, and two days of work, and nobody voted on it. I am going to be the man who wanted a bridge and got one out of a settlement I did not have to agree to.*”

He wrote it on the page of a local book at about the eleventh hour, and the working was said out loud while he wrote it, and he did not stop to talk in the middle of it.

“*A day against my name, in a book that a woman of sixty-four does not keep and that nobody four hundred miles off can read today. That is a different place, and a man of thirty-four has been in an ash for two hundred and nineteen days and nobody could compute a day against my name because I never wrote one.*”

“*So I have made myself computable, in about nine of your ears, with a pencil. A woman of sixty-four did the same thing on Wednesday, and I am not going to say we are a pair, and I am not going to say I am glad for her.*”

Then a boy of nineteen and a man of thirty-nine argued about the first appeal, and the boy said the whole of the terms out loud, and a man of thirty-one did not tell him what to do.

Bram is nineteen and is the first apprentice of the public cartography office, and refused a route on Thursday because the four places on it had not been asked, and has been told that refusing it was correct and has not enjoyed being told that.

“*The first appeal. It is about a cart road and about whose names go on a sheet when a cart goes down it in a hard year, and a man of thirty-nine wants the sheet to carry the region, and I want it to carry the four houses.*”

“*And here are the terms, and I am going to read them all out because a man of thirty-nine asked me yesterday whether I had read them and I had not, and I am not going to be a person who is asked that twice.*”

“*And I am asking the man who put the appeal in to say whether he agrees with the terms, out loud. He says he does not agree with the fourth one. I have written that down, and I am not going to argue him out of it in about nine of your ears.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, said that he had one thing to say about an appeal and that it was not advice, and said it in the ordinary voice, and then stopped talking about it.

“*The one thing. A boy of nineteen has just read four lines of terms and told a man of thirty-nine that he disagrees with the fourth one. I have been on this bank for eleven days handing about nine of you figures, asking for things, opening things. Today I have got nothing to add to that.*”

“*Check the terms. That is all. Read them and see if they are the terms you thought you were agreeing to, and if they are not, say so, and that is not disloyalty and it is not a boy’s trick, and about four of you should try it this week.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the fourth hour of a Friday with a full bank holding over the top step. Two dead persons were named out of eleven. A load was divided between two people in about nine ears, and a bridge four hundred yards off that is mended has a day against a name in a book nobody can read today.

The fever was eighty weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and ninety-five days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and sixty-four days old, and a hundred and forty-nine days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A woman of forty-four who keeps twenty-nine chairs came up the cart road for the first time in fifty mornings and named two dead persons out of eleven, and said the first has no day written down because there is no form that asks for one, and the second went away in a lighter eleven years ago, and neither of them is to be carried as a cost or thanked for.
She gave the figure of about four of her own, and a woman of twenty-nine refused it as consent by name for the fifth time in two books, and it went into the book as four people who will speak together and nothing else.

A man of thirty-one and a woman of twenty-eight divided what each of them was carrying, and she put down the man of thirty-one and he picked him up, and he named the cost before she was asked, and it was not a third no and it was not a yes.
A man of thirty-four in an ash was asked about a bridge for the first time and said yes out loud. He wrote a day against his own name in his own hand, in a book nobody four hundred miles off can read today, and said he had made himself computable with a pencil.

And a boy of nineteen read the whole of the terms of the first appeal out loud, said that a man of thirty-nine disagreed with the fourth one and wrote that down too, and a man of thirty-one said the only thing he had, which was to check the terms, and then stopped talking about it.
