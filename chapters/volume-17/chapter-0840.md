# Chapter 840: Second Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Second Week, And A Wednesday, And A Full Bank Holds At Sixteen For The Third Morning And The Next Time Is In Two Days, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Checks Nine Anchor Positions Out Loud And Says He Can Check Six Of Them And Not The Other Three, And A Boy Of Thirteen Says What A Figure Of Six Is A Figure Of

Second day of the hundred and forty-second week. His five hundred and ninetieth morning. Four hundred and forty days after the settlement. The fever seventy-nine weeks and four days. Three hundred and ninety-four days since the division. Three hundred and forty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the third morning of a bank sitting at that figure. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time in the new way, and did not give the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and said that was now the fourth morning of him not giving it and that he had stopped keeping the fifth thing.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and the fourth thing has been nothing for eight days and he is no longer apologising for it.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is six days of the coming back, and that is a full bank and it holds.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in two days, which is the day after tomorrow.*”

“*And that is four days after the morning it is full.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two checked that against what I said on Sunday and it is the same day and he did it in a lane with a nail and he is not going to tell you he got there first.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two did the reading of nine positions out loud, in about nine ears, on the flat, from the fourth leaf and the fifth leaf together, and gave the count of what he could check and the count of what he could not, and named his eighth thing.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and has a palm and a forearm, and has named seven things he cannot check. He has been asked to read nothing and to be read to, and has done both, and on the last morning of last week he did a subtraction of his own and said the two counts were not his.

“*Nine positions. I am going to say the nine and then I am going to say which of the nine I can put a line under, and the second list is the one that matters and about four of you know that already.*”

“*Nine, eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, one. That is the order they are written in, and it is not an order of importance.*”

“*And I have been asked twice this week whether it is, and it is not.*”

“*I can put a line under six of the nine. Six are positions on the ground, and a man can stand on them, and I have stood on four of them in nineteen years.*”

“*And I know what a position is.*”

“*And the other three are not on the ground. That is the whole of what I can say about them and I am not going to say one word more, because the second time somebody has said the seventh thing to me he was right and the first time he was being cruel about it.*”

“*That is my eighth thing I cannot check, and it is the three and not the six.*”

“*And the six being checkable does not make the three checkable. I have watched about four of you do that arithmetic in your heads in the last minute.*”

“*And I would rather say it out loud than have it be done quietly.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said what a figure of six is and what it is not, and gave his own count, and said the two counts were not the same count and that he was not going to pretend they were.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-seven days, and he has counted them in a lane, and the figure is one more than the morning before. He has been keeping a count of his own since the Monday of last week that is not his days and not his eight.

“*A hundred and eighty-seven days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two has six positions he can stand on and three he cannot. That is a figure of six and it is a figure of three and neither of them is a figure of anybody.*”

“*And I have got four, and my four are not his six and they are not his three.*”

“*And I am not going to put mine next to his and make a figure of ten. A man of thirty-one told me on Friday that a figure of about four of them who are going to speak is not consent, and I have been sitting with that for four days.*”

“*And it is not consent and it is not a plan, and it is four of them.*”

“*And one of them has a day against his name, and one of them is in a reed, and one of them cannot read, and one of them is a boy.*”

“*That is four, and I am one of them, and I am not going to be counted twice because I am talking.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for the four. I counted them and I said who they are and the counting is the whole of it.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the six and the three and the four out loud together, in that order, in about nine ears, and said what a man of thirty-one could and could not do with three different figures of about four people.

“*Six, three, and four. And I am the only man on this bank who can put a line under the first two and not the third, and about four of you have not worked out that I would rather say that than have you assume I can.*”

“*And there is no sum of those three. I have been asked three times this week whether six and three and four make a figure of anybody and the answer is that a figure of six positions and a figure of three things and a figure of four people are three figures of three different things and adding them is the error, not the sum.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said the thing she had gone down four miles to look at on Saturday, and said she had looked at the second of her two things, and gave both of them with the working on them.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She said her own name on that bank on the first day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week and has not gone back down that road since. On the last day of the hundred and fortieth week she said she was going to look at the second of her two things on the second day of this week, and she has.

“*A hundred and ninety-eight days. That is eight hundred and forty less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have looked at the second of the two things and I am going to say what it was before I say what I think of it, because a woman of twenty-nine in a reed taught me that on the first day of the hundred and fortieth week and I have not stopped doing it since.*”

“*The second of the two things is a man of thirty-one, and the second of the two things is what is written on a leaf about nine hundred feet from this landing, and those are the two things and they are not the same thing and I have had them the same for four days.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has an origin that other people can look at now. That happened on Friday, and a woman of twenty-nine in a reed made him take the halves apart in about nine of your ears.*”

“*And I have been treating the man and the leaf as one thing all week, because it is easier to be frightened of one thing than of two.*”

“*And I am not going to be frightened of two in about nine of your ears, because I have been frightened of one for nine days.*”

“*And I have not decided, and I am not going to decide on this bank this week, and I am telling about nine of you that I looked and that is the whole of what I have done.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said what a figure of six had done to his morning.

“*Here is the want and it is the same shape and it is one day smaller and I am not going to describe the shape again because about four of you have it and the other four do not need it.*”

“*The mistake is the fortieth of the same mistake and I am not renumbering it, and a man of about fifty-two has just said that six positions and three things and four people do not make a figure of anybody, and I have been using them as if they did.*”

“*I have been counting myself inside a figure of four for nine days. A boy of thirteen has just told me in about nine of your ears that he is one of the four and he is not going to be counted twice, and he is right, and I have been doing it in about nine of your ears since Friday.*”

“*And nobody stopped me, because it is a small vanity and small vanities do not get stopped.*”

“*A hundred and forty days. A hundred and forty is eight hundred and forty less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And a thing on that flat says there is one seal that is immediately valid and it is not mine. A man of about fifty-two can put a line under six of nine positions.*”

“*And neither of those is the standing offer, and the standing offer has not been answered this morning by a bank that is full or by a boy who will not be counted twice.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Wednesday with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them, a man of about fifty-two saying six and three and four out loud, and a boy of thirteen saying that none of the three is a figure of anybody.

---

The fever was seventy-nine weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eighty-six days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and fifty-five days old, and a hundred and forty days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of about fifty-two read nine positions out loud and said he can put a line under six of them and not the other three, and named his eighth thing. He said a figure of six, a figure of three and a figure of four people are three figures of three different things, and there is no sum of them.
And a boy of thirteen said his four are not the six and not the three, and that he is one of them and is not going to be counted twice, and that four of them who are going to speak is not consent and is not a plan.

A woman of twenty-eight said she had looked at the second of her two things and that the two things are a man of thirty-one and a leaf, and that she has had them the same for four days because it is easier to be frightened of one thing than of two, and that she has not decided.
And a man of thirty-one admitted he has been counting himself inside a figure of four for nine days, and named it a small vanity, and gave the standing offer at a hundred and forty days with the subtraction in it.
