# Chapter 824: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Week, And A Monday, And A Full Bank Holds For The Third Morning And Then Comes Off In Two Days For The Twentieth Time, And A Man Of Thirty-Four In An Ash Is A Hundred And Ninety-Four Days Into A Figure And Says What A Bridge Has To Do With Him, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Decides He Is Not Going To Read The Fourth Leaf Today

Seventh and last day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. His five hundred and seventy-fourth morning. Four hundred and twenty-four days after the settlement. The fever seventy-seven weeks and two days. Three hundred and seventy-eight days since the division. Three hundred and twenty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the third morning of a bank holding there. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, said what the day after tomorrow was, and said the number out loud, because it is the twentieth time and he has given the number for the last four.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and one of the three this week was a thing on a flat with a nail in it, which is the first time in nineteen years one of the three has not been about the landing.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is six days of the coming back, and that is a full bank and it holds.*”

“*And it comes off in two days at about the sixth hour, and that is the twentieth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And it is eight days on from the Tuesday of the hundred and thirty-ninth week, and the nineteen before it were eight days apart, and four of the twenty at the start were not.*”

“*And it has been off since about the fifth hour on the four mornings of it, and in two days it will be off since about the fifth, and I have said that four times.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked after that is in ten days, and that is four days after the morning it is full, and the morning it is full was the fourth day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week.*”

“*And I have had four mornings like this in nineteen years and in three of the four there was nobody else standing on this bank, and I am telling about nine of you that I have noticed the difference and I do not know what to do with it.*”

Then a man of thirty-four in an ash, about a hundred yards along the low side of that drain, was brought up the bank by nobody, and said what a bridge has to do with him and what it does not.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four and has been in an ash about twenty feet off the edge of that drain and a hundred yards along it, for a hundred and ninety-four days, and he has a day against his name which is not a day of this week. He said on the second day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week that he will speak in a room four hundred miles off only if he is asked, and was told out loud that a man who has to be asked has already been asked, and said *you are right*, and did not withdraw it.

“*A hundred and ninety-four days. That is eight hundred and twenty-four less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*I was not brought up. Nobody brought me up. I am here because the fourth leaf is on that flat and I am going to be asked about it, and I would rather be here when the asking is done than be found out afterwards.*”

“*About nine of you have a bridge in the fourth leaf. I can hear it in about four of you from down at the bottom of this drain without seeing a page.*”

“*Here is what it has to do with me. Nothing.*”

“*That is the whole of my answer and I would like about nine of you to notice that I have given it before the leaf has been read.*”

“*I have a day against my name. A bridge that is reopened four hundred yards from where I am standing does not take the day off my name and it does not put one on it either, and a man of thirty-four is the only one of the four of us who is going to lose something if that leaf is accepted, and I am telling you that before anybody works it out.*”

“*And I am not saying that as a reason against it. I am saying it because a woman of sixty-four told me on the sixth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week what to do with a thing like that, and I am doing it.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for saying it first, and nobody is to tell about nine of you that it was brave, and I am not going to say the word at all.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said he was not going to read the fourth leaf today, gave the reason, and said what he was going to do instead and how long he had had it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has a hundred and three mornings and the figure is one more than the morning before.

“*I am not going to read the fourth leaf today and I am going to say why before anybody asks me.*”

“*Three leaves is a morning. Three leaves I have got, and about nine of you have got them, and I can say what is on them in about nine ears.*”

“*Four leaves is not a morning. Four leaves is a morning in which nobody can say no to anything, because there is no space in it to say no in.*”

“*And that is the whole of the reason and I have had it since Saturday night.*”

“*And I have a hundred and three mornings of asking, and a hundred and two of them were yesterday, and a hundred is the one I remember.*”

“*And on the hundredth morning I did not have this reason, and I would have read four leaves.*”

“*Read three on Friday, three on Saturday, three on Sunday, and the fourth on Monday of next week.*”

“*And I am not going to decide that today. I am going to decide it on the Wednesday of the hundred and fortieth week, and about four of you are going to ask me before then and about four of you are not, and I am telling you both lots now so that nobody can say afterwards that I was ambushed.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for the three mornings of reading. Reading is not a thing to be thanked for. It is what I cannot do myself.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the fourth leaf would go into her apron, or it would not, and said which, and said what she had decided about the piece of paper with four names on it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and is a keeper of nothing anybody can be sent for about. A piece of paper with four names on it is still in the other pocket of her apron, and the crumb of bread is in this one, and she has checked.

“*It is not going in my apron.*”

“*I have said on Thursday that I am not giving a figure of about four of you this week, and I have not given one, and I am not going to give one on Wednesday of next week either.*”

“*And the fourth leaf is not a piece of paper with four names on it, and putting it in my apron would be me deciding that the fourth leaf is the same kind of thing as the paper, and it is not.*”

“*So it stays on that flat with the nail through the corner of it, and a man of fifty-four will read it or he will not read it, and that is not a thing I am going to be the keeper of.*”

“*And I am saying that in about nine ears because a woman of forty said on Friday that the answer to her question has come down a cart road in a night, and I do not want about nine of you putting me in the middle of that.*”

“*I am a keeper of chairs. Twenty-nine of them, at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, for nineteen years, and the three numbers I keep are of things, and a leaf is a thing, and nobody can be sent for about a leaf.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it. He said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that the standing offer and the thing on that flat are still two offers.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventy days, two hundred and seventy being eight hundred and twenty-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-nine days old, a hundred and thirty-nine being eight hundred and twenty-four less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the want and it has moved shape three times in three days, and I am not going to give you the shapes.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said four leaves in a morning is a morning in which nobody can say no, and I have been giving about nine of you shapes since Friday.*”

“*What I want is a road answered, a child treated, and a bridge open.*”

“*All three are in three leaves of paper that came out of the dark on Friday night, and about nine of you have read them in about nine ears.*”

“*And not one of you has said the word yes out loud, including me.*”

“*And I have not said no either, and I said on Saturday that saying I am not going to say no is not the same as saying yes, and I am going to keep saying that in about nine ears for as long as it takes.*”

“*The mistake is the thirty-fifth of the same mistake and it is about three hours old and it is the first one this week that is not the fence.*”

“*This morning a man of thirty-four in an ash said what the fourth leaf has to do with him before anybody had read it.*”

“*And I have been on that bank for two hours wanting to tell him he does not have to be careful about it.*”

“*He does have to be careful about it.*”

“*He is the only one of the four of us who loses something, and the fact that he said so first does not make it a smaller loss for him.*”

“*And I have spent two hours treating a man who names a thing early as a man who has handled it.*”

“*That is the thirty-fifth and it is a new one, and I want about four of you to notice that I have said it is new.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight stopped giving a figure about this on purpose in an earlier week for the same reason, and I am not going to compare myself with him and I am not going to stop either.*”

“*A hundred and twenty-four days. A hundred and twenty-four is eight hundred and twenty-four less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And it is the last day of this week, and the day after tomorrow the water comes off all ninety for the twentieth time.*”

“*About nine of you are going to be standing on ninety dry steps at about the sixth hour, and I am not going to be one of them.*”

“*I am going up that cart road at the seventh hour, and a man of forty-three told me on Sunday that I could, and he is right.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-four who said out loud what a bridge has to do with him and what it does not, and who was not brought up that bank by anybody.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of fifty-four who has decided that three leaves is a morning and four is not, and who has had that reason since Saturday night and gave it on Monday morning.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of forty-four who has said the fourth leaf is not going in her apron and that it is not the same kind of thing as the paper in the other pocket.*”

“*And nobody says a word of thanks to me for three days of not saying no, and nobody is to be told that any of the three of you who said a thing on that bank this week was brave about it, because none of the three of you has said the word once.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this week, and the week ends in about two hours, and I am not going to say that a week of not saying no is progress.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of the last day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week, with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them, and three leaves of four read out loud and the fourth not read. A man of thirty-four in an ash had said what a bridge had to do with him before anybody had read the word, and a man of fifty-four had said he would decide on the second day of the hundred and fortieth week.

---

The fever was seventy-seven weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventy days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and thirty-nine days old, and a hundred and twenty-four days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A full bank held for the third morning, and the man of forty-three said the water comes off in two days at about the sixth hour for the twentieth time, eight days on from the Tuesday, and gave the number out loud.
And a man of thirty-four in an ash, a hundred and ninety-four days in, came up that bank unasked and said a bridge has nothing to do with him and that he is the only one of the four of them who loses something if the fourth leaf is accepted.

A man of fifty-four said three leaves is a morning and four is not, and said he would decide on the second day of the hundred and fortieth week, and gave the figure for his own run of mornings and the hundredth morning he remembers.
And a woman of forty-four said the fourth leaf is not going in her apron and is not the same kind of thing as the paper in her other pocket, and a man of thirty-one named a mistake that is not the fence and said he is going up the cart road tomorrow at the seventh hour.
