# Chapter 827: Third Day Of The Hundred And Fortieth Week, And A Thursday, And About Four Inches Back On The Flat And One Day Of The Coming Back With All Ninety Steps Dry, And A Boy Of Thirteen Asks How A Mark Gets Made And Is Not Answered For Two Days, And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Gives Him The Answer On Friday And It Is A Name She Has Not Said Out Loud Before, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says His Own Is A Post At A Cart Road

Third day of the hundred and fortieth week. His five hundred and seventy-seventh morning. Four hundred and twenty-seven days after the settlement. The fever seventy-seven weeks and five days. Three hundred and eighty-one days since the division. Three hundred and twenty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was one day of the coming back, and about ninety of those steps were dry at about the sixth hour and are dry still. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, gave the figure for the next time, and said what a man of forty-three saw on the fourth leaf that he had not said on the morning it was read.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and none of them about the water. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week, and about nine of them walk past it every day, and one of the three things is now a thing on a flat that he did not put there.

“*About four inches at the seventh hour, and it is one day of the coming back, and about ninety of those steps were dry at about the sixth hour and are dry still at the seventh.*”

“*And about ninety of those ninety steps are dry this morning, and that is the largest figure of that kind I have given in nineteen years, and I gave it yesterday without knowing it was the largest.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in seven days.*”

“*And that is this morning plus eight, and the twentieth was yesterday, and the twenty-first is eight days on from yesterday.*”

“*And I have not got to the twenty-first morning, and I am not going to pretend to about nine of you that I have.*”

Then he said a thing about the fourth leaf that he had not said at the tenth hour on Wednesday, and gave the reason he had kept it a day.

“*I want to say a thing about that fourth leaf, and I did not say it yesterday at the tenth hour.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting since the tenth hour on Wednesday for me to say it, and I have let you wait, and I am not going to pretend that was hard for me.*”

“*I did not say it because I had not looked at the line on it that is not one of the eleven.*”

“*There is a mark at the bottom of it. It is not ink.*”

“*It is a small pressed mark, the kind a bailiff’s stamp leaves, and it is on that flat and I have seen it every morning since Friday.*”

“*And that mark is the only part of that thing I can check, because I have seen forty-one of them in nineteen years on about nine doors in this county.*”

“*And every one of the forty-one was on a thing that could not be argued with.*”

“*And I am not going to say what I think the mark is for.*”

“*I am going to tell about nine of you that it is there, and that it is the only figure on that flat I can put my hand on.*”

Then a boy of thirteen asked how a mark gets made, and was not answered for two days, and a man of about fifty-two said he could not help with it and gave the reason, and a man of thirty-eight said he had a board in the inside of his coat and had not opened it.Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-four days, and he has counted them in a lane, and the figure is one more than the morning before. He has a figure of eight that is a count of times and not a clock, and it went up on the third day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week.

“*A hundred and seventy-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And here is the question, and I am giving it to about nine of you at once and not to one of you.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight has been keeping a board for nineteen years, and he is the only one of the four of us who might have the answer.*”

“*How does a mark get made?*”

“*Not how does a mark mean something. How does it get there.*”

“*A man makes a mark with a thing he is holding. So somebody held something.*”

“*And I have had that question two days and nobody has answered it.*”

“*And I am going to ask it again on Friday at about the ninth hour, and about four of you are going to try, and about four of you are going to find a reason that you are not the one who answers it.*”

“*I am not blaming anybody who does not answer it. I am telling you now that I will notice, and I am thirteen and I have been counting my own days for a hundred and seventy-four and I can count the not-answerings too.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said what he could not help with and why, and named the fifth thing he cannot check, and said how many there are.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He has checked figures out loud on four mornings of last week and has named, on each of them, which ones he cannot do.

“*A mark gets made by a person. That is not a figure I can check, that is a thing every one of about nine of your ears already knows.*”

“*And the fifth thing I cannot check is that mark, and I have named the other four out loud this week, and I am naming this one now so that about four of you can count them.*”

“*Four: a figure of nine days in a document four hundred miles off.*”

“*Five: a pressed mark at the bottom of it that a man of forty-three has seen forty-one of in nineteen years.*”

“*And I can hear both of them and I cannot check either, and I have known about the fifth since the eighth hour on Wednesday, and I have had a day with it.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said he had a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, that he had stopped giving its age on purpose, and that he was not opening it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and said the number of names out loud for twenty mornings and stopped, and has a board in the inside of his coat that nobody has seen opened. He gave the figure of his own mornings standing at the ninth hour and then stopped on purpose.

“*I have a board in the inside of this coat, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and I have had it nineteen years.*”

“*And I am not going to open it, and I am not going to say how old it is.*”

“*I gave that figure up in an earlier week on purpose, and about four of you noticed, and I have not given it since.*”

“*And a boy of thirteen has just asked how a mark gets made, and I am the only one of the four of us who might have the answer.*”

“*And I am telling about nine of you that I have it and I am not going to give it.*”

“*Not this week.*”

“*And I want about nine of you to notice that I said *not this week* and not *no*.*”

“*I noticed it myself at about the tenth hour on Wednesday, and I have had a day with that too.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said a man of thirty-one was doing the thing he had been doing for eleven years, out loud, in about nine ears, and named it, and a man of thirty-one said it and said what his was and where it was.

“*A man of thirty-one is doing the thing I have watched people do for eleven years, and I have never said it out loud before.*”

“*He is the only person on that flat who gets to decide when a thing has been said enough.*”

“*I did it with a mark on a sheet of deal four lines under me. He does it with his mouth.*”

“*And it is the same act, and I have watched it go into about nine of your ears every morning for a year and a bit.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that was true, and said what he had built and where it stood, and gave the two distances, and said it was a post and not a monument.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventy-three days, two hundred and seventy-three being eight hundred and twenty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and forty-two days old, a hundred and forty-two being eight hundred and twenty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*That is true, and I have known it was true for about a year, and I have not said it.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two has said it for me, and I am not going to be irritated about that in front of about nine of you.*”

“*Here is what I built, and I am going to say where it stands.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two said on Monday that a thing with no distance is a thing anybody can carry anywhere, and he is right about that and he is not being kind.*”

“*A post at a cart road. About four hundred yards up that road from this landing, at the turn, on the low side.*”

“*That is where a cart comes off a lane onto the road that goes down to the water.*”

“*There is a board on the post and a figure nailed to the board. The figure is of the weather, and the road, and what is on the road.*”

“*And it is a figure of things and not of anybody, and about four of you have been standing at that post since I put it up.*”

“*And a boy of nineteen came up that road with an empty cart on the Friday and said he had not been told he was going to be watched.*”

“*And here is what it is not. It is not a monument, and I am not going to let about nine of you turn it into one this week while a thing with a mark at the bottom of it is lying on that flat.*”

“*The mistake is the thirty-sixth of the same mistake, and it is the one a man of about fifty-two just named.*”

“*I am not going to give it a number that makes it new.*”

“*A hundred and twenty-seven days. A hundred and twenty-seven is eight hundred and twenty-seven less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-eight who has a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches in it, and who said not this week and not no, and heard himself say the difference.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who asked how a mark gets made and has had two days without an answer, and who is going to ask again on Friday.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of about fifty-two who said out loud what a man of thirty-one has been doing for about a year and a bit, and who had never said it before this morning.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that any of the three of you was brave about it, because none of the three of you has used the word once and neither have I.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and a post at a cart road is not it.*”

“*And I am not going to tell about nine of you what it is, because about four of you would hear that as a man building a case on a Friday.*”

“*And I have not asked the boy of thirteen his question back. I have had it two days and I have not worked out the answer and I am not going to hand him a half one in about nine ears.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with about eighty of those ninety steps under them again, and a pressed mark at the bottom of a thing on that flat that a man of forty-three can put his hand on and nobody else can check. A boy of thirteen had asked how a mark gets made and had been two days without an answer, and a man of thirty-eight had said *not this week* and then heard himself say it.

---

The fever was seventy-seven weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventy-three days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and forty-two days old, and a hundred and twenty-seven days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of forty-three said there is a pressed mark at the bottom of the fourth leaf of the kind a bailiff’s stamp leaves, that he has seen forty-one of them in nineteen years, and that it is the only figure on that flat he can check.
And a boy of thirteen asked how a mark gets made, and a man of about fifty-two said he could not help with it and named the fifth thing he cannot check, and a man of thirty-eight said he had the answer and was not giving it this week.

A man of about fifty-two said a man of thirty-one had been doing the thing he has watched people do for eleven years, and a man of thirty-one said that was true and said what he had built.
And he said it is a post at a cart road about four hundred yards up from the landing, with a figure on a board nailed to it, and that it is a figure of things and not of anybody, and he is not going to let it be a monument this week.
