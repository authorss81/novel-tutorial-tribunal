# Chapter 831: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Fortieth Week, And A Monday, And A Boy Comes Down A Cart Road With A Thing He Was Not Asked For, And A Man Of Forty-Three Declines To Give A Figure He Does Not Trust, And Two Remedies Are Set Out On The Same Forty Yards And Neither Of Them Can Have The Last Fifteen

Seventh day of the hundred and fortieth week. His five hundred and eighty-first morning. Four hundred and thirty-one days after the settlement. The fever seventy-eight weeks and two days. Three hundred and eighty-five days since the division. Three hundred and thirty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour. It was the second morning of a bank sitting at that figure. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time. Then he did not give the fourth thing, and he said why before anybody asked him.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and a fourth thing which is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under. On this Monday he would not give it.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is the second morning of it.*”

“*And it is five days of the coming back, and that is a full bank, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days, which is three days after the morning it is full, and the morning it was full was yesterday.*”

“*And here is the thing I am not going to say. I would a good deal rather say that I am not going to say it than say one I do not trust.*”

“*I have given you how many of those ninety steps are under on most mornings for nineteen years.*”

“*On the Saturday I gave you one figure. On the Sunday after it, which was the morning the bank was full, I gave you a smaller one. I have not been down on that flat since that Sunday because it has been under.*”

“*I do not know which of the two is right. I have had the weekend on it and I have not found out, and I am not going to give you a third and let it be the one you remember.*”

“*About four of you have worked out that I have not said that in nineteen years. About four of you have worked out that I have said the other thing instead. That is the same thing, and I would rather it were said.*”

Then a boy of thirteen and a man of forty-four came down that cart road at about the eleventh hour. The boy had asked his question at a low gate. He had been given something. He gave his own count first, in his own mouth.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-eight days, and he has counted them in a lane. He went up the cart road at about the sixth hour with a man of forty-four. He came down at about the eleventh. He did not say what he had been given on the way down.

“*A hundred and seventy-eight days. It is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I asked it at the gate and not at the house above it, because the man at that gate is the man who stopped the cart, and he is the only person on that road who has ever been in the way of a mark. He is right, and I am not going to argue with about nine of you about it.*”

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years. Its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. He has a handcart with three barrow-loads of gravel standing on the flat of that bank, and a socket with two lengths of ash and a wedge and an old nail in it.

“*Four terms, and I have kept all four, and I am going to say so in about nine ears.*”

“*He asked and I did not. I did not ask him what he said, and he did not tell me, and we came down four hundred yards of that road not talking, and I chose that and he chose it and it is not a sulk.*”

Then the boy said what he had been given, and he said it slowly, and he said it was a man’s and not his.

“*A mark can be made by a person who is not making it.*”

“*That is his, not mine. I am carrying it up a bank and I am not claiming it. I have not put it on a counter, and I have not written it in the lane, and I am going to write it in the lane tonight, because I am not going to lose it.*”

“*Here is the whole of it, and I am not going to say it twice.*”

“*On the Tuesday, eleven years ago, a person came down that cart road and told him to stop the cart. He did. He did not ask who the person was. He has never been able to say what they looked like, and he has thought about that for eleven years.*”

“*And he said the reason he has never gone up and asked whether it mattered is that if he went up and it had mattered for nothing, then he would have stopped a cart for nothing on the only morning anybody ever asked him to.*”

“*He said it in that order. I am not going to put it in a better order. A woman of twenty-nine made a man of thirty-one say two things in the right order on Friday, and I am not going to do that to anybody.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything for about a minute. Then a woman of forty-four said what about nine of them had been doing on a flat was not a room, and named the thing that it was instead.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Her three numbers are all of things. She has declined three times in a week to put a thing on her counter.

“*I have been standing here since that boy came down, waiting to say this.*”

“*This is not a hearing. I have been in four of them. I know the floor of one and this is not the floor of one.*”

“*If about nine of us stand on a bank and one of us is right and one of us is wrong, then that is two of us in a room. A room is a thing with a door in it. A thing with a door in it is a thing that can be shut, and I have been shut in one, and I am not going to stand in one in the open air and have it called the same thing.*”

“*There is a string. That is what this is. A stone on a string and two stakes, and one of you holds one end, and the other end is going to be somewhere it is not.*”

“*And I will say one more thing, because I am a keeper of chairs and this is my trade.*”

“*Two of you have been getting on with each other for a week. I want to see whether you can do it with a figure between you. That is the whole of the test, and it is not fair, and I do not care.*”

Then they set it out. The two things they set out on that ground were both wanted and both real. A man of forty-four said his first. A woman of twenty-nine in a reed said hers. About nine of them stood on a flat four hundred yards up a cart road and let a string be put down.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and wants a bore under the cart road at the turn, so that the water on that road goes under it instead of down it. He has been on that road for twenty-six years and he knows where it stands.

“*My remedy. A hole under the road at the turn, four feet down, with a head wall either side. The water goes through it and out on the low side and stops lying in the ruts.*”

“*That is nine feet of hole and four feet of depth and nine days of two men. It is a figure of a remedy and not a figure of a person.*”

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about twenty feet off the low side of that drain, a hundred yards along it, for two hundred and two days. She can read. She has said that us is four people. She wants that drain taken deeper.

“*Two hundred and two days. That is eight hundred and thirty-one less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*My remedy is his with the water turned round. That drain is eighteen inches too shallow at the top end, and it is eighteen inches too shallow for the whole first stretch, and every year it stands in the reed.*”

“*The reed does not care and the water does not go away.*”

“*I am not asking for the road and he is not asking for the reed, and about four of you have been waiting all week for one of us to call the other one wrong.*”

“*Neither of us is saying it. I said on Friday that the game was a bridge and not a source, and about nine of you are still working on that.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said he would work the figure out himself, and a man of about fifty-two said he would do it, and the man of thirty-one did it anyway and got it wrong, and the man of about fifty-two did it and got it right, and said the whole of it out loud in about nine ears.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He named a fifth thing he cannot check on the third day of last week.

“*The man of thirty-one is going to work it and I am going to let him. Then I am going to do it. About nine of you are going to find something out about me from the order of those two things.*”

“*Two yards to a pace of mine. I have given that figure on this bank and it is the only pace in this county I have got.*”

“*The ground falls one in thirty over that stretch. That is four feet in forty yards. Forty yards is a hundred and twenty feet. A hundred and twenty feet over four feet is thirty, and there it is.*”

“*His bore has to be four feet under the road at the top stake, and it has to be level the whole way. A bore that falls is a hole, and water in a hole does what it likes.*”

“*Her drain is eighteen inches under its own bed at the top stake, and it falls with the ground. A drain that does not fall with the ground is a different drain.*”

“*Her drain is one foot six under the road at the top stake. His bore sits four feet under the road at the same stake.*”

“*So her drain has two foot six to come to reach him, and it falls one in thirty, and two foot six at one in thirty is seventy-five feet, and seventy-five feet is twenty-five yards.*”

“*In twenty-five yards of forty, her drain is level with his bore. After that it is above it. And a drain above a bore takes the bore’s water. His water comes back out on his own side.*”

Nobody on that cart road said anything for a while. Then a man of forty-four said what had just happened, in about nine ears, and it was not what anybody had been waiting eleven days for.

“*It is not him and it is not her and it is not you either, and I want that said before anybody on this flat goes looking for a person to blame for a piece of ground.*”

“*Fifteen yards. The last fifteen yards of that stretch cannot belong to both of them. That is a figure of a remedy, and about nine of you can go and look at it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he had wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it had cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that this was the eleventh morning of a run that has gone eleven mornings.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventy-seven days, two hundred and seventy-seven being eight hundred and thirty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and forty-six days old, a hundred and forty-six being eight hundred and thirty-one less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the want, and I have wanted it since Thursday, and I am going to say it out loud before about nine of you work it out of my face. I want the two of them to be able to stand in the same place.*”

“*And the reason I want it is that a challenge which cannot let two good things stand in one place is not a challenge. It is a queue. And a man of about fifty-two said on Thursday what I am, and I did not take it, and I am not going to argue with him about it on a cart road.*”

“*Here is what I got wrong. It is small and it is mine and I am not going to give it to the ground.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two said he would do that figure. I said I would do it instead, in about nine ears, and I gave a figure.*”

“*I said about twenty yards. It is twenty-five. I was out by five yards on a straight fall and I did not check, and I did not check because I have been doing this for two hundred and seventy-seven days and about four of you have stopped noticing.*”

“*That is the cost of it, and it is not going to be let go for me, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and about four of you have just heard a man of thirty-one use the word myself out loud for the second time in a week.*”

“*A hundred and thirty-one days. A hundred and thirty-one is eight hundred and thirty-one less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And this is not the answer either. A string on a flat is not the answer, and a woman of forty-four said that before I did, and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who came down that road with a thing he was not asked for and who has put it nowhere.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of forty-three who did not give a figure this morning and who has not done it in nineteen years before.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of about fifty-two who did a figure in about nine ears and who said the whole of the working, including the part where it fails.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that a man of thirty-one being out by five yards was honest, because it was not honest. It was quick.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning. A thing that only works if you say yes is not it. A stretch of ground that will take one remedy and not another is not it. A man of thirty-one who is out by five yards is not it either.*”

About nine of them were on a cart road four hundred yards up from that landing at about the eleventh hour of the last Monday of the hundred and fortieth week, with sixteen inches of water over the top step behind them. A string had been put down on about forty yards of the low side. In about nine ears it had said that the last fifteen of them could not belong to two people at once.

The fever was seventy-eight weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventy-seven days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and forty-six days old, and a hundred and thirty-one days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and did not give the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and said he did not know which of two numbers he had given was right and would not give a third.
And a woman of forty-four said that about nine of them standing on a bank with one of them right is a room with a door in it, and that what they were doing instead was a string and a stone and two stakes.

A boy of thirteen came down that cart road with a thing he was not asked for and gave it in a man’s words and not his own, and a man of forty-four kept all four of his terms and said so out loud.
And a man of about fifty-two worked a fall of one in thirty out loud in about nine ears and said where two remedies meet, and a man of forty-four said it was fifteen yards of ground and not a person.
