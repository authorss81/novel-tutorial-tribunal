# Chapter 821: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Week, And A Friday, And A Thing Is Found On The Flat Of That Bank That Nobody Saw Come In, And A Woman Of Forty Says It Is The Answer To A Question She Asked Twenty-Eight Days Ago And She Does Not Want It, And The First Leaf Is Read And It Is A Road

Fourth day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. His five hundred and seventy-first morning. Four hundred and twenty-one days after the settlement. The fever seventy-six weeks and six days. Three hundred and seventy-five days since the division. Three hundred and twenty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour. It had been that since the middle of the night. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and gave the figure for the next time, and said the bank would be full tomorrow and would hold there for the three mornings after that.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and none of them about the water. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week.

“*About twelve inches at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back.*”

“*And about five of those ninety steps are under this morning.*”

“*It is a full bank tomorrow at the seventh hour, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in five days.*”

“*That is four days after the morning it is full, and the morning it is full is tomorrow.*”

“*And that is the first figure for the next time I have had for three days that I did not have to look at the water-stone to get.*”

Then he said there was a thing on the flat of that bank, that he had found it at about the sixth hour, and that nobody had seen it come in.

“*It is about the size of a folded cart road map.*”

“*There is a nail through the corner of it, and the nail has a head on it, and there is rust on the head.*”

“*It was lying at the low end of the flat, where the water comes over. The water has not been on that spot since Wednesday night.*”

“*Nobody has been on this landing since about the ninth hour on Wednesday night.*”

“*I was on it at about the sixth hour this morning and the water was on that spot.*”

“*So it came in during the night. I did not see it come in and neither did anybody else.*”

“*And I am not going to tell about nine of you a reason for it.*”

Nobody gave a reason for it.

Then a man of fifty-four said he wanted that thing a leaf at a time, gave the reason, and gave the figure for his own run of mornings out loud before anybody had worked it out.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He put two lengths of ash across that handcart socket on the Tuesday of the hundred and thirty-ninth week and has not taken them off.

“*A hundred mornings this morning.*”

“*That is one more than ninety-nine, and it is the eleventh morning of that run.*”

“*The run began on the first day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week, and I am giving you the working because a man of about fifty-two will ask me for it.*”

“*And I have got it before about nine of you for eleven mornings, and about four of you have stopped being surprised, and that is the right order for that to happen in.*”

“*I cannot read it. I have said that on this bank in about nine ears more often than any other thing I have said on it, and I am not going to stop.*”

“*And I want that thing read a leaf at a time, and I want to say why before anybody asks me.*”

“*Because if a man of sixty-one reads four leaves in one go, then about nine of you are going to nod at the first one and go quiet afterwards.*”

“*And I would not know which leaf you nodded at.*”

“*And I have asked for a thing for a hundred mornings and I have never once been given it.*”

“*The reason I am asking for this in pieces is that I would rather be slow than be nodded at.*”

“*And I am not saying a man of sixty-one cannot be trusted with four leaves. I am saying I cannot tell you which one you agreed to.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said he would read it, said what he would not do with it, and read the first leaf out loud in about nine ears.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years. He has refused since the second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week to write a bench’s notice in it, and gave that figure out loud once, on the second day of this week, and is not going to give it again. He wrote a woman of twenty-eight’s name on a page in a column nineteen years ago and has not gone back to that page since.

“*I will read it in the open and I will not write one line of it in my book.*”

“*And I am not going to write down what any of you says after it. I told you that on the Wednesday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week and I have not stopped.*”

“*There are four leaves. I counted them twice, because I have a book of heads and carts and a habit.*”

“*Here is the first one. I am going to read it the way it is written and not the way it sounds.*”

“*Where it says a figure I am going to say the figure and its working.*”

“*It says: an exception.*”

“*And the next line says it is not a warning, and that nobody sent it, and that it has no face and no voice of its own.*”

“*It says three things can be done, and here they are, and here is the working of each one, and the working of all three is the same working.*”

“*It says a refusal under this arrangement is entered in a book.*”

“*And the next line says a book can be corrected.*”

“*And the next line says a correction is not a punishment and is not sent for and is not a cruelty, and that it is a correction.*”

“*That is the first leaf, so far. I am not going to say what I think of it, because a man of fifty-four asked for it a leaf at a time and that is the whole of the reason.*”

Then he stopped and started again, because there is a working at the foot of that leaf, and the working has a figure of a person in it, and he wanted about nine of them to have the working before the name.

“*There is a working at the foot of it and it is a working about a road, and I am going to give it whole.*”

“*It has a person in it, and that is why I stopped.*”

“*It says three miles and six hundred yards up the cart road from this landing is a gate, and there are four houses above the gate.*”

“*And one of those four houses has had nothing come down that road since the Tuesday before a man of fifty-eight walked down it and stood on this bank and said so.*”

“*It says the working is eleven years.*”

“*And it says the cause is that a man who is not one of the four houses stopped a handcart on that road on a Tuesday morning and has said so to nobody since.*”

“*It says a road is a road.*”

“*And the next line says that a road which cannot be answered is not a road. It says it is a piece of ground with two ruts in it and eleven years on it.*”

“*And it says the arrangement can answer that road in nine days.*”

“*The nine is in the document. I am not going to do the working for it, because the working is not in the document.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said it back, and said what it was going to cost, and said it before about nine of them had worked out that it was going to cost him.

“*Say the last part again, and say the name at the end of it.*”

“*I have it. Three miles and six hundred yards, four houses, one handcart on a Tuesday, eleven years, nine days.*”

“*And at the end it says the fourth house has a man in it who cannot read.*”

“*And it says his name is on the ninth line of a book, and there is no day against it, and this arrangement supplies one.*”

“*And it gives my name. It gives my name on a thing that came out of the dark on a night when that bank was under.*”

“*Nobody has said no to me in a hundred mornings. And nobody has said yes to me either.*”

“*And I have got used to that, and it is the only thing I have got.*”

“*A yes from four hundred miles is not a yes. A yes is the man who could have said no.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything for a while. Then a man of forty-three said the water had not been on that spot since Wednesday night, and that he was not going to be asked about it again.

Then a woman of forty said that the thing on the flat was the answer to a question she had asked on that bank, and that she did not want the answer, and gave the date of the asking out loud.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has not been given one. She asked on that bank, on the Friday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week, whether anybody in this county had a way of refusing a thing a person four hundred miles off can put in front of them. She has not named the room since.

“*The fourth day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. That is a Friday, and this is a Friday, and it is four weeks back, and it is twenty-eight days.*”

“*Twenty-eight days is eight hundred and twenty-one less seven hundred and ninety-three. I did that one in my head before I said it, because I have been asked for a figure I could not do in my head eleven years ago and I am not going to do it in about nine ears this week.*”

“*I asked it in about nine ears and about four of you heard it, and it has been twenty-eight days.*”

“*And that thing on the flat is the answer to it. It came out of the dark and it is the answer.*”

“*I have been asking for eleven years whether there is a way of saying no to a thing that arrives. And nobody has answered me, and that has been the answer for eleven years, and I knew that.*”

“*And this morning the answer has come down a cart road in a night and put itself in a book, and it says a book can be corrected.*”

“*I am not going to say that it is a good answer or a bad one. I am going to say that I did not want it to arrive today, and that is not a reason against it.*”

“*And I am not going to name the room. I have been asked three times in eleven years and I am not going to name it on a Friday because a thing came out of the dark.*”

“*And nobody is to thank me for saying that out loud, and nobody is to tell about nine of you that I have been asking for eleven years, because I have been saying it every time and saying it every time is not the same as saying it louder.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said which of the figures on that first leaf he could put a line under, and which one he could not, and named both.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He has checked figures out loud on four mornings of this week and has named every morning which ones he cannot do.

“*Three miles and six hundred yards is five thousand eight hundred and eighty yards.*”

“*The cart road from this landing to the low gate is two yards to a pace of mine, so that is two thousand nine hundred and forty paces, and I have walked it and it comes out.*”

“*Eleven years I can hear and I can put a line under.*”

“*A man of forty-four has said that figure out loud twice this week and a woman of forty-four has given a figure of about four houses, and both of those are about the same eleven years.*”

“*And nine days is not mine.*”

“*There is no line under me at the top end of that cart road, and there has not been one for eleven years.*”

“*And a thing that came out of the dark last night is not going to put one there.*”

“*And I can hear a figure of a person and I cannot check one.*”

“*Those are two different things, and about four of you keep handing me the first one when I am asking for the second.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost, and he gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that none of it was an answer.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and sixty-seven days, two hundred and sixty-seven being eight hundred and twenty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirty-six days old, a hundred and thirty-six being eight hundred and twenty-one less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the want, and I am saying it before about nine of you have worked it out of my face.*”

“*I have had it since Thursday morning.*”

“*I want somebody else to carry the figures.*”

“*Not a woman of forty at the ninth hour. Me, at about the eleventh hour, with a figure and a subtraction, on this bank, every morning.*”

“*And about nine of you coming up that cut to hear it.*”

“*And that thing on the flat would take the eleventh hour off me, and it would take it off me this morning, and it has not asked me for anything yet.*”

“*That is what I want. I have not said it out loud on this bank before.*”

“*About four of you have known it for a year and about four of you did not, and it is out now.*”

“*The mistake is the thirty-third of the same mistake, and it is about two hours old.*”

“*I came up that bank at about the eighth hour and I asked about nine of you what you wanted to do about a thing none of you had read.*”

“*I had not read any of it myself.*”

“*That is me deciding when the question is finished. It is the same thing I did on Tuesday, and a man of thirty-four told me on Wednesday that it is the same fence.*”

“*The cost is that a man of fifty-four has got an offer for the thing he has asked for a hundred mornings, and about nine of us did not do one thing about it.*”

“*A hundred and twenty-one days. A hundred and twenty-one is eight hundred and twenty-one less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And I have not read the second leaf.*”

“*And I am not going to say no to the first one this morning, and saying I am not going to say no is not the same as saying yes.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of fifty-four who has been asked for a hundred mornings and has been given it by a night.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of forty-four who asked out loud on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-eighth week for one person who could say yes to that road, and who is going to want it.*”

“*And nobody says a word of thanks to me for wanting somebody else to carry the figures, and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and it is given to nobody by a thing with a nail through the corner of it.*”

“*And I am not going to call that thing by its name on this bank this morning.*”

“*A man of sixty-one read four leaves off it and did not use the name, and that was his choice, and I am not going to take it off him.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Friday, with twelve inches of water over the top step above them and a nail through the corner of a folded thing at the low end of the flat. A man of fifty-four who cannot read had asked for it a leaf at a time, one of the four leaves had been read out loud, and a man of thirty-one had said out loud that he wanted somebody else to carry the figures.

The fever was seventy-six weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and sixty-seven days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and thirty-six days old, and a hundred and twenty-one days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A man of forty-three found a thing on the flat of that bank at about the sixth hour and said nobody had seen it come in, because the bank was under twelve inches of water all night and nobody had been on it.
And a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for it a leaf at a time, and gave the reason, and said that a yes from four hundred miles is not a yes, because a yes is the man who could have said no.

A man of sixty-one read the first leaf in about nine ears, and it was a road, and the working at the foot of it had a name on it, and a man of fifty-four said that name back before about nine of them had worked out that it was his.
And a man of thirty-one said he wanted somebody else to carry the figures, and that a thing with a nail through the corner of it would take the eleventh hour off him and had not asked him for anything yet.
