# Chapter 830: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Fortieth Week, And A Sunday, And A Full Bank Holds For The First Morning And The Next Time Is In Four Days And A Man Of Forty-Three Says The Figure Is Derivable From The Rate, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Nobody On That Bank Has Answered The Offer And He Is Not Going To Stop Saying The Figure, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says The Nineteen Years Are Hers And Not A Leaf

Sixth day of the hundred and fortieth week. His five hundred and eightieth morning. Four hundred and thirty days after the settlement. The fever seventy-eight weeks and one day. Three hundred and eighty-four days since the division. Three hundred and thirty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the first morning of a bank sitting at that figure since the water came off. The man of forty-three gave the reading, the working, the figure for the fever, and the figure for the next time, and said that the figure for the next time was derivable from the rate and did not need him.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and on this Sunday the fourth thing was that he does not have to be asked, which is the first time the fourth thing has been nothing in nineteen years.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is four days of the coming back, and that is a full bank, and about sixty of those ninety steps are under this morning.*”

“*And I have said about sixty, and I am not going to say a smaller figure to be kind to anybody.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days, and the morning it is full is today, and four days after today is Thursday.*”

“*And I want that said in about nine ears, because that figure is not mine any more. It is four inches a day and a full bank holds, and anybody here can work it out without me.*”

“*I have said that once in nineteen years and I have had a day with it, and I am not sure I like it, and about four of you have been waiting since Friday for me to say it.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the nineteen years were hers and not a leaf, and said what she was going to do about a decision she had said on Monday she would make on the Wednesday, and did not make it.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line of a book four hundred miles off and has never had a day against it. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and was forty-five when she worked out that the thing asking was not a person. She said the word twice and refused nine people, and her protection for nineteen years was a gap and not a wall.

“*A man of fifty-four said on Monday that he would decide today, and today is the Sunday of the week after, and he is not on this bank and I am not going to decide for him.*”

“*What I am going to say is about the nineteen years and not about a leaf, and I want about nine of you to notice the difference and about four of you will not.*”

“*On the fifth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week I gave the whole of nineteen years away in front of about nine of you, and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be.*”

“*The nineteen years are mine. They are not evidence. They are not a thing the arrangement can hold or not hold, and I will not have them read out on the Wednesday of next week as a thing that has been settled.*”

“*Line nine says no person is named in any book of the arrangement. I believe line nine and I am going to keep believing it, and I have believed a thing for nineteen years and it has held.*”

“*And what I have is that nobody in this county can be sent for about anything, and that is not on a leaf. That is nineteen years of about nine of us being careful on a bank, and a woman of forty-four keeping chairs, and a man of fifty-four holding gravel he cannot read a figure of.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what the ten days had been, and gave the figure for the standing offer for the tenth morning running with its own subtraction, and said he was not going to stop saying it, and said what he was going to do on Monday.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventy-six days, two hundred and seventy-six being eight hundred and thirty less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and forty-five days old, a hundred and forty-five being eight hundred and thirty less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the tenth morning and I am going to say the same thing I have said on ten mornings, and a man of fifty-four said on Monday that about four of you were waiting for me to stop, and you are going to be disappointed.*”

“*A hundred and thirty days. A hundred and thirty is eight hundred and thirty less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And it has not been answered by anything that came out of the dark on Friday night, and it has not been answered by a road, or a child, or a bridge, or a woman of sixty-four who is not frightened of the eleventh line.*”

“*And it has not been answered by a man of thirty-four in an ash who said that the eleventh line means there is no such thing as being wrong about this.*”

“*And I have said on two mornings that the standing offer and the thing on that flat are two offers, and they are, and nobody on this bank has confused them, and I have said that out loud twice because about four of you were going to.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said what he was going to do on Monday, and that he was going to ask the question in a different place, and gave the figure of his own days and did not give the eight.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-six days, and he has counted them in a lane. He has a figure of eight that is a count of times and not a clock, and it went up on the third day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. He asked at the ninth hour on that Thursday and again on the fourth day of the hundred and fortieth week how a mark gets made, and was answered on the Friday.

“*A hundred and seventy-six days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I am not going to give you the eight again, and a man of thirty-one is not going to ask me for it, and about four of you have been wanting to know which eight mornings they were since Thursday.*”

“*Here is what I am going to do on Monday and I am saying it on Sunday so that nobody can say I did not give notice.*”

“*I am going to take the question to the low gate. Not here, and not at about the ninth hour, and not to about nine of you.*”

“*Four houses at the low gate have not had a cart down that road in eleven years, and there is a man there who stopped one on a Tuesday and has said so to nobody, and he is the only person on that road who has ever been in the way of a mark.*”

“*And I am thirteen and I am not going alone, and I am not going to ask a man of thirty-one to come, because if he comes then it is a man of thirty-one asking and not a boy of thirteen asking.*”

Then a man of forty-four said he would go with the boy and on what terms, and a man of thirty-one said the terms were right and he was not going to add himself.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years, and its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. He has a handcart with three barrow-loads of gravel in it standing on the flat of that bank, and a socket with two lengths of ash and a wedge and an old nail in it.

“*I will go with him on Monday, and here are my terms and they are not negotiable on the flat.*”

“*One. He asks the question and I do not.*”

“*Two. If the man at that gate says no, we come back down, and we do not stand there.*”

“*Three. Nobody thanks me for going and nobody is told that going was brave, and I have not used that word once in eleven years and I am not starting on Monday.*”

“*And four. He does not tell me the answer on the way up, and I do not ask him for it, and if he has worked it out he keeps it until the man at that gate has had his turn.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the terms were right, said he was not adding himself, and said what he would be doing on Monday while the boy and the man of forty-four were up that road.

“*Those terms are right and I am not going to add myself to them.*”

“*On Monday morning I am going to be at that cart road at the turn, at the post, and I am going to be doing the thing I do, which is saying a figure out loud with the subtraction in it, so that about four of you can check it.*”

“*And about four of you will want me to go up the road instead, and I am not going to, and the reason is that a boy of thirteen has asked for the question to be his and I am not going to take it off him on the Monday.*”

“*And I am not going to stand at the low gate and watch, because a man of thirty-four in an ash told me on Wednesday that being watched is a thing he has to be careful about, and I have been on the wrong side of that once already this week.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said he was going to read the eight of the eight mornings out loud himself, in about nine ears, and named it, and said he could not check them and was going to read them anyway.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He put a mark on one of two sheets of deal, four lines under him, eleven years ago, because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. He checked figures out loud on four mornings of last week and named the fifth thing he cannot check.

“*A boy of thirteen has a figure of eight and will not say which eight mornings, and he has told about nine of you it is a count of times and not a clock.*”

“*And I am going to say something on Monday that I have not said in eleven years, and I am saying now that I am going to say it, so that nobody can say I sprang it on about nine of you.*”

“*I am going to read out the eight mornings on which my own sheet of deal had a mark put on it, and I know all eight, and I have known them for eleven years.*”

“*And that is not the same as his eight and I want about four of you to notice that it is not the same and not to put them together.*”

“*His eight is eight mornings on a bank. Mine is eight mornings over eleven years on a sheet of deal four lines under me. Those are two figures of eight and neither one is a figure of anybody.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it for the tenth morning running.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in six days and I am not going to pretend it has.*”

“*I want somebody else to carry the figures. I said that on Friday of last week in about nine ears and about four of you had known it for a year and about four of you did not, and it is still the want and it is the same size and it has not become a better want by being said.*”

“*And here is the cost and it is a person and not a figure. A man of thirty-eight has a board in the inside of his coat and about four of us now know he has the answer to a boy of thirteen’s question, and he has said not this week twice, and about four of you are going to be gentle with him about it, and that is the second time in a week somebody has been handled.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of sixty-four who said the nineteen years are hers and are not a leaf, and who is going to keep believing line nine, and who is not going to let them be read out on the Wednesday as settled.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a boy of thirteen who is taking a question to the low gate on Monday and who is not going alone and who is not going to be thanked for it.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of about fifty-two who is going to read out eight mornings of his own on Monday that he has not said in eleven years, and who can check none of them.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that any of the three of you was brave about any of it, and none of the three of you has used the word, and I am not going to use it either.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and a leaf with a mark at the bottom of it is not it, and a full bank at sixteen is not it, and a man of thirty-one not adding himself to four terms is not it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Sunday with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them, and a boy of thirteen had said he was taking his question to the low gate on Monday with a man of forty-four and not a man of thirty-one, and a man of about fifty-two had said he was going to read out eight mornings of his own that he had not said in eleven years. Nothing was decided. The next time about ninety steps could be walked was in four days and the figure was derivable from the rate.

The fever was seventy-eight weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventy-six days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and forty-five days old, and a hundred and thirty days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A woman of sixty-four said the nineteen years are hers and not a leaf, and that she is going to keep believing the ninth line, and that nobody in this county can be sent for about anything and that is not on a leaf either.
And a man of forty-three said the figure for the next time is derivable from the rate and does not need him, and said he is not sure he likes that, and a boy of thirteen said he would take his question to the low gate on Monday with a man of forty-four and without a man of thirty-one.

A man of thirty-one said the offer is at a hundred and thirty days and has not been answered by a road, or a child, or a bridge, or a woman who is not frightened of the eleventh line, and that he is not going to stop saying the figure.
And a man of about fifty-two said that on Monday he is going to read out eight mornings on which his own sheet of deal had a mark put on it, and that those eight are not the boy of thirteen’s eight, and that neither one is a figure of anybody.
