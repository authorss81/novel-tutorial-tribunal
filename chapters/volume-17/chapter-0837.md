# Chapter 837: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-First Week, And A Sunday, And Twelve Inches And Three Days Of The Coming Back, And A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Says The Well Has Gone Off And Says What That Costs A Person Four Times A Day, And A Man Of Thirty-One Puts A Question Under The Tenth Line And Five People Are Asked Before It Is Put, And Two Of Them Say No And Lose Nothing

Sixth day of the hundred and forty-first week. His five hundred and eighty-seventh morning. Four hundred and thirty-seven days after the settlement. The fever seventy-nine weeks and one day. Three hundred and ninety-one days since the division. Three hundred and thirty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and said that the three failures he named on Wednesday had now had one of them, and did not say which, because he had not been told.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has not given the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under since Monday, and on this Sunday he gave a different reason for not giving it, and the reason was that nobody had asked him for it since Monday.

“*About twelve inches at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back, and it is eight inches higher than it was on Friday morning, and I am not going to count the steps for you this morning.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in five days, and that is five forward with nothing counting down, and the boy does not like it and he is right.*”

“*And I have not given you the figure of how many of those steps are under since Monday, and I am not going to give it this morning, and the reason is that nobody has asked me for it since Monday and I have noticed that and I am not going to supply it on my own account.*”

“*A man of thirty-one told about nine of you on Wednesday that a bridge might fail, a well might fail, and a thing about books might fail. I have no figures on any of the three. I have got a water-stone and a rate of four inches a day and that is the whole of what I am.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine in a reed came up that bank at about the sixth hour and said the well had gone off, and said what that costs a person, and gave the figure for her own days with the subtraction in it, and did not ask anybody for anything.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about twenty feet off the low side of that drain, a hundred yards along it, for two hundred and eight days, and she can read, and she has said that us is four people, and she drew water at that well on Friday and drew it again on Saturday.

“*Two hundred and eight days. That is eight hundred and thirty-seven less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*The well is about six hundred yards along the edge of that drain from your landing and about a hundred yards below the bridge, and I gave you that figure on Tuesday and it has been in the zone since Tuesday.*”

“*It went off in the night. It is four foot higher in the shaft than it was on Saturday and it is not cold and it is not clear and it has lime in it, and a man of forty-four has told me that a floor is lime and gravel and a floor cannot be laid with lime alone.*”

“*Here is the cost and it is a person and not a figure of a well.*”

“*It is six hundred yards there and six hundred yards back, four times a day, and it is a bucket, and I have been doing it since yesterday, and I am going to be doing it on Monday.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four in an ash has been carrying it up for me since yesterday, and he has a day against his name, and he has not asked for anything for it, and I am not going to thank him for it in about nine of your ears because he would have to say something.*”

“*That is a well. That is what a well is when it goes off, and it is not a figure and I am not going to give you one.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said he wanted to put a question to the arrangement, and a woman of sixty-four told him which line of the thing on that flat he was going to have to use, and he said so out loud, and five people were asked before it was put, and two of them said no.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and eighty-three days, two hundred and eighty-three being eight hundred and thirty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and fifty-two days old, a hundred and fifty-two being eight hundred and thirty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Line ten of the fourth leaf. Any person in this county may at any time put a question to the arrangement, and the arrangement shall answer it, and the answer shall be given in the open.*”

“*That is the door. It is the only door into any field of the arrangement from this bank, and it is a door that a thing with a nail through the corner of it put there, and I have been standing here since the sixth hour working out that I have to use it.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four told me the door was theirs and not mine. She is right and I am not going to argue with her about a door.*”

“*Here is the question and I am going to say it now so that about nine of you can stop me.*”

“*Show the interval between the first entry ever made in any linked field and the second. Not a summary. The interval itself, with the two counts on either side of it.*”

“*And here is the part that is why I have to ask five people first. The fields of this ground are linked to that interval. If the fields of this ground are in it, then every one of you whose name is in a field of this ground is going to see what comes back.*”

“*And nobody in this county is going to be shown it who has not agreed to it beforehand. I am not going to be the one who puts a thing in front of a person and then tells them afterwards what it was.*”

Then five people were asked, one at a time, in about nine ears, and each of them was told what the question was and what could come back, and three of them said yes and named what it cost them, and two of them said no and lost nothing at all.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line of a book four hundred miles off and has never had a day against it. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago. On Sunday of last week she said the nineteen years are hers and are not a leaf, and that she is not afraid of the eleventh line and is also tired of it.

“*Yes, and I am going to say what it costs before he asks me twice, and it is the second time I have given those nineteen years away in about nine of your ears in twenty-two days.*”

“*The first time was on the fifth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week and I said they were mine and not evidence, and a man of thirty-four has been careful with me about it since and I have noticed him being careful.*”

“*If that interval comes back with what I think it comes back with, then those nineteen years are not a gap I have. They are the arrangement’s own way of doing things, done once before, and it can be closed by the same arrangement without entering a single thing about me.*”

“*And I have relied on it for nineteen years. Every morning. That is the cost and it is not a figure and I am not going to give you one.*”

“*And I am saying yes anyway, and I am not going to be thanked for saying yes, and I am not going to be told it was brave, and if about four of you are about to say either of those things I have said them first and I would rather they did not.*”

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has a hundred and sixteen mornings and the figure is one more than the morning before.

“*Yes, and I want it said in about nine ears that I said yes before I knew what was coming back.*”

“*A hundred and sixteen mornings. One more than the morning before, and the twenty-seventh morning of that run.*”

“*Here is the cost and I have had it since the second day of this week and I have not said it out loud.*”

“*I have found out whether there is ever going to be a day against the ninth line for me. Either it is in that interval and it has been there all along and nobody gave it to me, or it is not in there and it is not going to be.*”

“*And if it is the second one, then I have got to get up on this bank on Monday morning and ask again, and I will be a hundred and seventeen.*”

“*And I have got used to a hundred and sixteen and I am not going to pretend I have not. That is the cost. It is not a figure.*”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and he was on the top of those ninety cut steps with a palm and a forearm, and he has named six things he cannot check, and he was asked from where he was sitting.

“*Yes, and my part is going to be the worst one on that bank and I would rather say so now.*”

“*I cannot look at the answer. I will hear it in about nine of your ears and I will not be able to see a figure in it, and I am going to ask for it read twice anyway because the reading is the only check I have got.*”

“*And I want about four of you to know that asking to be read is not the same as being able to check. I have said that before this week and I am saying it on a Sunday with a wet hand and about four of you have stopped hearing it.*”

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has not been given one. She asked on that bank on the Friday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week whether anybody in this county had a way of refusing a thing a person four hundred miles off could put in front of them, and she has not named the room since.

“*No.*”

“*And I want it said in about nine ears that I have lost nothing, so that about four of you do not go looking for what it cost me.*”

“*Eleven years of being asked this and eleven years of not being given it. A gap is not going to give it to me either.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four’s nineteen years and a man of fifty-four’s hundred and sixteen mornings are about nine of your business, and not mine, and I would rather they were.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you what I am protecting either. I have been asked three times in eleven years, and today is the fourth.*”

“*There is a room I will not name, and there is a thing this morning I will not name, and you may have both of those.*”

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She said her own name on that bank on the Tuesday of the hundred and thirty-ninth week. She is at a hundred and ninety-five days, and the figure is eight hundred and thirty-seven less six hundred and forty-two.

“*A hundred and ninety-five days, and that is eight hundred and thirty-seven less six hundred and forty-two, and I am going to give the working because a boy of thirteen made it a rule on that bank on the third day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week.*”

“*No, and mine is a small one and I am not going to dress it.*”

“*I have no day. That is the whole of it. I have no day and no name in ink and nothing about me in any book of the arrangement, and that is the only thing I have got that is mine.*”

“*And whatever comes back in that interval will come back through fields, and fields are what I do not have, and I am not going to find out this morning whether I would be in one.*”

“*And nobody loses anything by me saying no. Nobody. That is worth saying out loud on a Sunday when about nine of you have just watched two people pay for a yes.*”

“*And I am not going to be called brave for it, and I am not going to be called careful either, because I have been careful for a hundred and ninety-five days and it has not been a virtue once.*”

“*Here is the other thing and it is owed and it is not settled. On the Wednesday of last week I said I had two things to look at and that the second one was whether a man of thirty-one has said one true thing this week that he did not have to say.*”

“*On Monday he was out by five yards and he told about nine of us in about nine ears. That is a thing he did not have to do, and I am not going to say it is the thing, and I am not going to say it is not.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked this week. I have been asked that question twice and refused twice, and a woman of forty-four asked it herself on Wednesday and was not answered, and I am not going to be the second person this week to be not answered about it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he had wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it had cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that the question had been put at about the eleventh hour in about nine ears.

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in six days.*”

“*I want somebody else to put it. I have said that want on eight mornings and it is the same want and I said on Friday that I was not going to be the one who put a rod in a drain, and I was not, and about four of you worked out what that meant and about four of you did not.*”

“*Here is what I got wrong and it is the thirty-ninth of the same mistake and I am not numbering it.*”

“*I thought for about an hour this morning that two people had said no to me. They had not. They said no to a thing on a flat, and I had put the thing between their no and the only reason there was to say one.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four told me on the sixth day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week what to do with a thing like that and I have now done it twice, and the second time I did it without noticing for about an hour.*”

“*The cost is that a question which ought to belong to about nine of us is going to be put by me, out loud, in about nine ears, using a line written by the thing I am standing here to argue with.*”

“*A hundred and thirty-seven days. A hundred and thirty-seven is eight hundred and thirty-seven less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer, and a question is not an answer, and I have got two of them said no to me on a Sunday morning.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of twenty-nine in a reed who said what a well costs when it goes off, which is six hundred yards there and six hundred yards back four times a day, and who is not going to thank a man of thirty-four for carrying it.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of sixty-four who said yes to a question before she knew what was coming back and named the cost out loud, and who has now given those nineteen years away twice in twenty-two days.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of twenty-eight who said no and lost nothing, and who is not going to be called careful for it, and who has still not said the second of her two things.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that the two of them who said no were the only ones who were free this morning. I have not been free this morning. I have been standing at a gate I did not want to use.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and it is not what a man of thirty-four said on Thursday, and it is not what a woman of twenty-eight just said, and I am going to keep saying that at about nine of you until somebody gives me a reason to stop.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Sunday with twelve inches of water over the top step above them, and a well six hundred yards along that drain had gone off in the night with lime in it.
Five people had been asked about a question under the tenth line of a thing on a flat. Three of them had said yes and named what it cost them. Two of them had said no and lost nothing.
A man of thirty-one put the question out loud in about nine ears at about the eleventh hour, and said that it would be answered in the open.

The fever was seventy-nine weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and eighty-three days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and fifty-two days old, and a hundred and thirty-seven days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A woman of twenty-nine in a reed said the well had gone off in the night and was four foot higher in the shaft and not cold and not clear, and said what it costs a person, which is six hundred yards there and six hundred yards back four times a day.
And a woman of sixty-four said yes to a question before she knew what was coming back, and named the cost, which is that nineteen years of a gap she had relied on every morning may turn out to be the arrangement’s own way of doing things.

A man of thirty-eight was not asked this week, by a decision a man of thirty-one made out loud, and a man of about fifty-two said yes from the top of ninety cut steps with a wet hand and said that asking to be read is not being able to check.
And a woman of forty said no and said she had lost nothing so that about four of them would stop looking for what it cost her, and a woman of twenty-eight said no because she has no day, and lost nothing either.
