# Chapter 806: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Seventh Week, And A Thursday, And Four Hundred And Six Days After The Settlement, And The Bank Is Full On The First Morning Of It And Holds, And A Woman Of Sixty-Three Who Has Not Been On That Bank In Eleven Years Comes Up Ninety Cut Steps At About The Eighth Hour

Third day of the hundred and thirty-seventh week. His five hundred and fifty-sixth morning. Four hundred and six days after the settlement. The fever seventy-four weeks and five days. Three hundred and sixty days since the division. Three hundred and six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about sixteen inches on that cut at about the seventh hour and it was four days of the coming back, and the man of forty-three said the figure and the word for it, because a full bank is not the same figure as a bank filling.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, and a fourth he only says when somebody asks, and a fifth he keeps.

“*Sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is four days since it came off, and that is a full bank, and it is the first morning of a full bank and not the fourth morning of one, and about four of you have been counting the mornings of it and getting a different one each.*”

“*And it holds there. Sixteen is the figure it holds at and it will hold at it for as long as it holds, and then it comes off, and that is not a figure anybody can hurry.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath the holding and the next time all ninety of those steps can be walked is in four days, and that is the Monday of this week, and it will be the eighteenth time, and I said five yesterday and I am saying four today and I am saying out loud that I said five yesterday, because a figure that gets corrected quietly is a figure nobody can check.*”

“*And the fifth thing is still kept and still is not written down, and a woman of sixty-three is somewhere between here and that landing and I have not seen her go either way, and I am not going to stand at the top of these steps and watch for her, because I did it yesterday and about four of you looked at me doing it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open, eighty-fifth morning, and a woman of forty-four read it for the first time in eleven years of keeping a book of twenty-nine chairs.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, and on Friday of last week he asked for a day against that line and was not granted one, and his wheel of iron is still in the mud at the foot of that stair where he put it down on Saturday.

“*Eighty-five mornings, and eighty-five is eight hundred and six less seven hundred and twenty-one.*”

“*And a woman of forty-four read that line for me and she has not read one out loud in my hearing before and I am not going to say anything about it that would put her in a place. That is for her to say or not say and not for me.*”

“*And the cart does not go up that road again until next Wednesday, and there is a heap of my gravel at that turn with nobody standing at it, and a man of about twenty-nine is asleep at a gate three miles and six hundred yards up it with boards he has not got, and I have done three loads today and my hands are not what they were on Thursday, and that is a sentence about my hands and not about anybody else and I would like it kept that way.*”

Then she came up.

She came up those ninety cut steps at about the eighth hour with one hand on the rope that is fixed at the top of them for the purpose, and she stopped twice, and about four of them did not offer, and she did not ask.

Hild Ketch is sixty-three and has been at a gate three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road for thirty-one years and has not been on that bank in eleven years, and she cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one the gate is in.

“*I am sixty-three. I have been at that gate thirty-one years and I have not been on this bank for eleven and I did not come up here yesterday because I wanted something from anybody on it. I came because my son stood at the foot of those steps at about the ninth hour on Wednesday and looked up at the top of them, and I have not seen him look up at anybody since he was a boy.*”

“*And I came down yesterday and stood at the foot of those steps for about a quarter of an hour and did not come up them, and I am telling you that because about four of you have wondered since yesterday morning whether I went away, and I did not go away, I was standing at the bottom of them in the dark at about the ninth hour deciding whether a woman of sixty-three can do ninety steps.*”

“*And here is the true of it and it is two halves and not one thing. Yes. Every person on this bank can be told something. A man of fifty-four told about nine of you on Saturday morning that a ring of iron about the width of two fingers had let go, and I heard that on Sunday from the man who sells milk two days off, which is faster than anything has ever moved on that road in my thirty-one years.*”

“*And not one of you has told anybody on that road. And those are two facts and about four of you have been holding them as one since Saturday, and I can tell the two apart because I have had eleven years to get good at it.*”

Then she said the thing she had carried down the road, and she gave the figure and the working, and about four of them wrote nothing down.

“*I have said no out loud on that road two hundred and six times in eleven years. Two hundred and six, and I have never written one of them down anywhere, not on a wall and not in a book and not on my own hand, and I can name every single one of them and the year it was in.*”

“*And that is not a refusal, and I am going to say why and I am not going to soften it. Nobody has ever put anything in front of me to refuse. What I have got is a woman who has been left out of two hundred and six decisions and has been the reason for none of them going wrong, and that is being useful, and I have known the difference between the two since about the fourth year.*”

“*So I have a figure and I cannot do anything with it and I have never been able to do anything with it, and I am not going to be told I have refused two hundred and six things, because a thing that nobody asked me to refuse is not one and I will not have it said over me while I am standing on my own legs.*”

“*And what I want is one thing and it is small and it is eleven years old. I want to be asked. Not a question about a gate. Not a question about boards. I want to be asked what I think about the thing I am standing in.*”

“*And I came down five thousand eight hundred and eighty yards yesterday and I went back up it in the dark this morning and I came down it again at about the sixth hour, and I got up these ninety steps this morning without stopping once, and a man of fifty-four on this bank has gone down nine feet of bank on a Saturday because somebody asked him for a thing, and I have not asked anybody for a thing since before he was born.*”

Then he asked her one question, and it was the wrong one, and it cost what it cost.

“*What was on that road at that gate on the Friday, when the cart did not come down.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-two days, two hundred and fifty-two being eight hundred and six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-one days old, a hundred and twenty-one being eight hundred and six less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*A cart with boards on it, and a man who said he could not bring it down, and nothing else that I have found.*”

“*And I asked you what was on that road, and you have been on that road for thirty-one years and you have said no on it two hundred and six times, and I asked you about the road.*”

“*I am not going to say sorry and I am going to say why I am not going to say sorry, and the reason is that if I say it then it is a thing I did this morning and not a thing I am doing, and I have spent eleven days on that bank finding out which of those two it is.*”

“*The mistake is the twenty-second of the same mistake and it is mine and I made it at about the eighth hour and a half in about four seconds, which is the fastest I have made it and it is not the worst one.*”

“*It is the same mistake with a woman in it instead of a road. On Saturday I looked at a barrow wheel. On Sunday I looked at a boy with legs. Yesterday I looked at a man who came down a road. This morning I looked at a woman who had walked five thousand eight hundred and eighty yards twice and I asked her a question about a road, and about four of you watched me do it and about four of you did not stop me.*”

“*A hundred and six days. A hundred and six is eight hundred and six less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer, and a woman of sixty-three with a figure of two hundred and six in her head is not an answer either, and she is not going to be one and I am not going to ask her to be.*”

“*The cost is that she came up ninety steps that she had not climbed in eleven years and the first question she was asked on this bank in eleven years was about a road, and I cannot give that back to her and I am not going to buy it with a word.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of any of this morning. Nobody thanks me for noticing in about four seconds. And the right of refusal is not restored this morning and I am not going to say it was, and a woman of sixty-three said out loud that two hundred and six is not a refusal and that is not it either, and she is the one who has earned the right to say what that is.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts, and a boy of thirteen gave two of his own and one of a man of forty-three's.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she read that line out loud this morning for the first time and did not give a figure for it, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter sixty-two mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter fifty-six mornings with a number on them and nothing to write a name in and no names on them.*”

“*And three: one hundred and one mornings of that bank, and one hundred and one is eight hundred and six less seven hundred and five, and a man of thirty-eight has been teaching me that a figure for a morning and a figure for a thing are not the same figure, and I have had that for four days and I am still using both.*”

“*And I read that line this morning and I am not going to say what I thought of it, because a woman of sixty-three has just stood up here and told me that two hundred and six is not a refusal, and I have a figure of a hundred and fifty-six mornings of that sheet and I am not going to put the two side by side in front of nine people and let them make a shape out of them.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-three days, a hundred and fifty-three being eight hundred and six less six hundred and fifty-three, and he has been counting the mornings about four of them do not say a figure.

“*A hundred and fifty-three days, and it is one more than yesterday, and I counted it in the lane before the light.*”

“*And sixty-eight is the figure for the thing a man of forty-three is keeping, and sixty-eight is eight hundred and six less seven hundred and thirty-eight, and he has still not written the day down, and I am not going to give him the figure twice.*”

“*And I have got nothing else and I am going to say so, because a woman of sixty-three gave a figure of two hundred and six this morning and it is the first figure anybody has said out loud on this bank that I cannot check, and there are four lines under a man of about fifty-two and none of them are under her, and I have been thinking about that for an hour and I am thirteen and I am saying it instead of keeping it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with sixteen inches of water on that cut, a full bank on the first morning of it, and a woman of sixty-three standing on the flat of that counter who had not stood on it in eleven years.

---

The fever was seventy-four weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and fifty-two days, and a hundred and twenty-one days after an offer that has not been answered.

A woman of sixty-three who has said no out loud on a road three miles and six hundred yards up from that landing two hundred and six times in eleven years, and has written none of them down, came up ninety cut steps and said that two hundred and six is not a refusal, because nobody has ever put anything in front of her to refuse.

And a man of thirty-one asked her a question about that road within a quarter of an hour of her saying she wanted to be asked, and did not say sorry, and gave the reason why he did not say sorry.

And the right of refusal was not restored and was given to nobody, and she was the one who said so first.
