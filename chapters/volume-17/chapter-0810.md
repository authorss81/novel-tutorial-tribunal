# Chapter 810: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Seventh Week, And A Monday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Eighteenth Time, And A Man Of Thirty-One Goes Down Those Ninety Steps And Is Asked To Come Back Up And Says He Will And Does Not Make It A Thing, And A Boy Of Thirteen Gives One Figure And Says What It Is For

Seventh day of the hundred and thirty-seventh week. His five hundred and sixtieth morning. Four hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever seventy-five weeks and two days. Three hundred and sixty-four days since the division. Three hundred and ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The bank was off all ninety steps at about the sixth hour and had been off since about the fifth hour of it, and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the ordinal out loud because it is the eighteenth time and he said on Friday that he would be here to say so.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the week before last and about four of them were here when he said a whole number of weeks out loud on Saturday.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour, and it has been off since about the fifth, and that is the eighteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*The last two have been eight days apart and the one before them was eight days and the seventeen before that one goes back to the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week.*”

“*And fourteen of those were eight days apart and three of them were not, and I have not found out why and I am not going to.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working, and it goes on now at about four inches and it will be about four and eight and twelve and sixteen on Wednesday.*”

“*And it comes off again eight days after tomorrow, which is a Monday the week after, and that will be the nineteenth time and I have not got to it yet.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, eighty-ninth morning.
And said that about four of you have been giving him his own figure before he asks and that he wanted to be the one to say it this morning.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. And asked at the top of a cut on Sunday for the second time. And walked two hundred yards along the edge of that drain on Wednesday and got up half an hour earlier every morning since.

“*Eighty-nine mornings, and I gave it before you this morning for about a week and yesterday I decided to stop.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one said on Saturday that a whole number of weeks is not a line, and about four of you thought that was about him and it was about me.*”

“*I have been giving my figure before anybody asked it for me and I have decided that is me standing behind a figure the way a man of thirty-eight said on the Monday of last week.*”

“*And I have got eleven years of not being able to read and I am not going to spend them standing behind a number.*”

“*And I am still getting up half an hour earlier. I said on Sunday that I was not going to put a figure on it because a figure would be a rate and I did not know what the rate was for. It is for this. It is so that I can say the number myself and be wrong about it in my own mouth instead of hearing it in yours.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said one thing about what a man of thirty-one was doing at about the eighth hour and had been doing since Saturday.
And it was the sixth morning in a row, and he said he was not going to say anything about the seventh.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and he has stood on that bank at the ninth hour for six mornings.

“*A man of thirty-one has been going down those ninety steps at about the eighth hour every morning since Saturday and about four of you have not looked down and I have looked down every one of the six and I am not going to look down tomorrow and that is the whole of what I have to say this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to say anything about the seventh and I am not going to say why and I am aware that about four of you are going to decide that the not saying is the saying.*”

Then a man of thirty-one went down those ninety cut steps at about the eighth hour of a Monday with the water off all ninety for the eighteenth time.
And was asked by a man of forty-three to come back up, and said he would, and did not make it a thing.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-six days, two hundred and fifty-six being eight hundred and ten less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-five days old, a hundred and twenty-five being eight hundred and ten less six hundred and eighty-five. He has asked seven times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said, and it is a hundred and twenty-five days old this morning. And he is not going to ask an eighth time and about four of them will notice that too.

“*I have been going down these steps at about the eighth hour every morning since Saturday because there are about nine of us on this bank and there have been about four for nineteen years.*”

“*And I worked that out on Saturday in about nine lines and I have been doing it since and nobody asked me to.*”

“*And I am going to say that out loud one more time before I go down them, because a woman of twenty-eight said on Sunday that about four of you were building a thing in the open and standing in it.*”

“*And I am one of the four and I have been doing it for six days and I am not going to stop today out loud and then keep doing it.*”

“*That is the want. It is not about this bank and it is not about a piece of ground two hundred yards off it and it is not about a woman of twenty-eight who walked four miles and said no. It is that about nine of us are standing on a place that held four people and about four of us have been watching about nine of us do that for a week and finding it out at about the ninth hour of each morning.*”

“*I want to be down there when that is happening. That is the whole of the want and I have not got a use for it and I have not got a figure for what it is worth and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years.

“*Come up at about the tenth hour. Not at about the ninth and not at about the eleventh. The tenth, and I will be here.*”

“*And I have three things to give and I will give them at the tenth hour like I have every morning for nineteen years.*”

“*That is not an instruction. You do not have to do it and I am not going to be a man who says it in a way that makes it one. If you come up at about the ninth or about the eleventh or at about the seventh I am going to give the three things then.*”

“*And if you do not come up at all I am going to give them at about the seventh hour and go home.*”

“*And I am going to say the last of that and it is the only thing I have got. About nine of you have been coming up here for a week and I have given three things every morning for nineteen years and I have not had to repeat them since Wednesday and I want you to know that I noticed.*”

“*A man of forty-three does not mind saying that in front of about nine of you. It is not a favour to any of you. It is a fact about a landing and I have got to keep it in the morning somewhere and it is not going to be on the counter because I am not that kind of keeper.*”

Then about four of them were asked to come up to the top of that cut and went up, and a woman of forty-four said a figure of about four of them out loud for the second time and said what the two of them are.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has put her crumb of bread in her apron and her pencil in her sleeve and is not giving the third figure this morning or any morning after it.

“*I gave a figure of about four of you on Friday and I said it was the only one I was going to give.*”

“*And I am going to give a second one this morning and I told a man of thirty-four on Friday that I was going to and he did not stop me.*”

“*On Friday it was four of you: a man of forty-four ignored twice, a man of forty-three with a knee, a man of fifty-four nobody has asked anything of.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one who is going to use it as a fence. Those were the four and I gave them and that was Friday and it is Monday.*”

“*This morning it is four of you and not one of them is the same four and that is the only thing I am going to say about it. A woman of twenty-eight. A woman of twenty-nine. A man of thirty-four. And a man of fifty-four who got up half an hour early so that he could be wrong about a number in his own mouth.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one who said on Sunday that he wanted somebody who could come back the next morning and tell him he was wrong.*”

“*And has been going down ninety cut steps six mornings in a row without being asked.*”

“*And about four of you are going to say on Sunday that he found that and none of you is going to be able to show me the morning he found it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave one figure and said what it was for, and a man of about fifty-two said he was not going to check it because he could not see it from where he was standing.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-five days, and he counted them in the lane this morning and it is one more than yesterday.

“*A hundred and fifty-five days and I counted them in the lane.*”

“*And one figure and I am going to say what it is for, because about four of you have been letting me give figures for a week and a man of thirty-eight said on Saturday that a man of fifty-four has not been thanked for anything and I have not been thanked either and I have been giving them anyway.*”

“*Four. And it is about four of you. Not a figure of a bank. Not a figure of the world. Not a figure of anybody who is not standing on this bank this morning.*”

“*And it is for a thing I am going to do tomorrow morning and I am telling you what it is for so that about four of you can check it, because that is what a figure is for. I am going to ask about nine of you something tomorrow morning and about four of you are not going to like it and I am doing it anyway and I am thirteen.*”

“*And I am not going to say tonight what the question is, because a man of forty-four said his bad reason out loud on Saturday morning and got a worse morning for it.*”

“*And I have thought about that for two days and I am not going to do the thing where I put something out there so that I can watch what it does to people.*”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well and put a mark on one of two sheets of deal four lines under him eleven years ago because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for. And he stopped checking figures out loud on Tuesday after three mornings and refused on Saturday to check a figure of a boy of thirteen that he could not see from where he was standing.

“*No, and I am going to say the reason in one sentence and then I am finished.*”

“*I cannot see it from where I am standing and I said on Thursday that I got a woman of sixty-four wrong by one for two mornings because I could not see her.*”

“*And I am not going to check a figure of a boy of thirteen at nine feet off this bank and get it wrong a third time.*”

“*A boy of thirteen is standing there telling me to leave a number alone and I am going to leave it alone.*”

“*And that is a man of about fifty-two being told something by a person of thirteen and doing it, and I do not know what that is and about four of you do.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it had cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and gave four hundred and ten days out loud and said what it is not.
And gave the figure for an offer that has not been answered, and said what four hundred and ten days is and is not.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-six days, and the cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-five days old and nobody on that bank has said whether it will keep.

“*Four hundred and ten days after the settlement. That is the number and I am giving it once at about the eleventh hour of a Monday morning and about four of you have been waiting for me to say something about it since Saturday night, because it is a round figure and about nine of us have been living on round figures for a week.*”

“*And I am going to say what it is not. It is four hundred and ten days. Nobody in this county can be sent for about it and nobody in this county can be given it.*”

“*And it says nothing whatever about a case, nothing whatever about this bank, and nothing whatever about the nine of you standing on it.*”

“*And I have been in this county two hundred and fifty-six days and I worked that out off eight hundred and ten less five hundred and fifty-four this morning.*”

“*And I am going to say the number out loud on a Monday, out of the way of the roundness, because it is my last whole number of mornings this week and in about four weeks it will be two hundred and seventy and none of the roundness will be there.*”

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in four days and I am not going to be able to improve on it by Monday. I want somebody who can come back the next morning.*”

“*And here is the mistake and it is the twenty-second of the same mistake and it is nine days old and it is the one a man of about fifty-two said on Thursday about a day being where a thing was on the day somebody wrote it.*”

“*I have been giving two hundred and fifty-six as though it were how long I had been here. It is not. It is how long ago I came. And about nine of us on this bank have been treating every figure we have said this week as a figure of how we are.*”

“*And a man of forty-four has had nine years of standing about two hundred yards off and a boy of thirteen has a hundred and fifty-five days in a lane and a man of thirty-four has a hundred and seventy-nine days in an ash.*”

“*And about four of us on this bank have been here about four weeks.*”

“*The cost is that this morning a man of forty-three said come up at about the tenth hour and not at about the ninth or the eleventh.*”

“*And about nine of you heard that as a man of thirty-one being given a time.*”

“*And it was a man of forty-three telling me he would give three things when I was on the bank and not at an hour I was doing something at.*”

“*And about four of you were right and about four of you were wrong and nobody on that bank said either.*”

“*And I am not going to be the one who asks about it, because a woman of forty-four said on Friday that she was going to give a figure and let it be the only one.*”

“*And I have watched her do that and I am not going to do it back to her.*”

“*A hundred and ten days. A hundred and ten is eight hundred and ten less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And a hundred and ten is the same figure as four hundred and ten less three hundred and I am giving both of them on the same morning on purpose and about four of you have noticed.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of anything. Nobody says a word of thanks to me for going down ninety cut steps six mornings in a row or for saying the same want four days running. Nobody is to be told they were brave about any of it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four came up that bank on Wednesday and nobody thanked him and nobody will.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning and I am not going to say it was. A man of fifty-four has not been granted and has not been refused. A woman of twenty-eight was told she could not attend and has not said what the word means. A woman of sixty-four has not said why for nineteen years. A man of thirty-four has a day and does not live in this county. A man of thirty-eight has not been asked what his board in his coat says for one hundred and twelve days and he has not opened it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one who spent a fortnight on this bank making sure nobody on it had to refuse anything.*”

“*And then spent a week being one of about nine of us who had a morning, has not been refused anything either. And that is the closest I have come this week and I am not going to walk down ninety cut steps at about the eighth hour and call it that.*”
