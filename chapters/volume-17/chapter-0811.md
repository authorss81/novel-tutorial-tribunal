# Chapter 811: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Eighth Week, And A Tuesday, And A Boy Of Thirteen Who Gave A Figure On Monday So That About Four Of Us Could Check It Asks It, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Says His Was For One Person And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says His Was For Both

First day of the hundred and thirty-eighth week. His five hundred and sixty-first morning. Four hundred and eleven days after the settlement. The fever seventy-five weeks and three days. Three hundred and sixty-five days since the division. Three hundred and eleven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour. It had been that since the middle of the morning. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the next time in seven days. Then he said that he had given the number for the next time on Monday and was not going to give it twice.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. About nine of them walk past it every day now and he has not asked one of them to stop.

“*About four inches at the seventh hour and it is one day of the coming back.*”

“*And it went off all ninety on Monday for the eighteenth time at about the sixth hour and had been off since about the fifth.*”

“*About a dozen of those steps were under and I have said a dozen because that is what I know and not what I could guess at.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it. A full bank is about sixteen inches over the top step, and about ninety steps can be walked again about four days after that.*”

“*That is in seven days from this morning.*”

“*And I gave you the number for it on Monday morning in front of about nine of you and I am not going to give it to you twice this week.*”

“*It is written down on about four of you and I do not mind.*”

Then he said that a boy of thirteen had asked him something on Saturday and he had had two days with it and had not worked out how to answer it.

“*He asked me whether the water is a clock. And I said no, it is a bank.*”

“*And I have kept that landing nineteen years and I have said three things on it every morning and I have got nothing in that which does not come off.*”

“*And I have thought about it for two days and I have not worked out how to answer him.*”

“*And I am going to tell him that, instead of handing him a figure of my own so that he can check it against a thing he cannot see.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said his own number out loud with the working in it, and gave four names, and said why each of them could check what he was going to ask.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-eight days. He counted them in the lane this morning and it is one more than yesterday.

“*A hundred and fifty-eight days.*”

“*And that is eight hundred and eleven less six hundred and fifty-three, and I counted it in the lane.*”

“*I am giving you the working because that is the thing I said I would do on Monday and I have not done it since.*”

“*Four. And here are the four.*”

“*And I am going to say why each one of them can check it, because a figure is for people to check, and I said that on Monday.*”

“*A man of fifty-four who cannot read, and who has been saying his own number in his own mouth since Saturday morning so that he can be wrong about it in about nine ears.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well, and who checked three figures out loud on the Saturday before that and stopped at three.*”

“*And who told us on Monday morning that he could not check a figure of mine from where he stands.*”

“*A man of forty-four who said his own worst reason out loud on the Saturday before that, and knew it when he gave it.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight four miles down that cart road, who heard about nine of us from the bottom of a cut on Sunday morning.*”

“*That is the four and that is what a figure is for, and nobody is to thank me for giving it, because I have not given anybody anything.*”

Then he said what he was going to ask, and said that he had had it since Monday night, and said what he was not going to do with it.

“*And here is the question and I am going to say it before anybody asks me why.*”

“*One thing you said on this bank since Saturday that was for one person and not for the rest of us.*”

“*That is all of it.*”

“*And about four of you are not going to like it, and I told you that on Monday night, and I am thirteen, and I am not taking it back now you have all heard it.*”

“*And I am giving it to about nine of you at once and not to four of you one at a time.*”

“*A man of forty-four said his reason out loud in front of about nine of us on Saturday morning and got a worse morning for it. I did not want to hand anybody a thing to hold and watch.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight said on Saturday that he has got nothing and is not going to pretend he has.*”

“*A woman of forty-four gave me a figure of four of you twice in one morning and neither four had one of the same four in it, and she would not say why.*”

“*So about four of you can check this one. That is why it is four and not nine.*”

“*And if I had made it nine, then nine of you would have been able to correct me, and nine of you agreeing would have been a figure and not a check.*”

Then a man of fifty-four gave his ninety, and said that his was for one person, and gave the reason he has carried for nineteen years without knowing it was there.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He asked at the top of a cut on Sunday for the second time and was told that nobody had refused him. He has got up about half an hour earlier every morning since Wednesday.

“*Ninety.*”

“*And ninety is one more than eighty-nine and I had it in my own head this morning before anybody on that bank said it.*”

“*And that is the reason I got up half an hour early. I told you the reason was so that I could be wrong about a number in my own mouth.*”

“*And here is mine, and it is for one person, and every one of you was standing here when it happened, so check it.*”

“*I gave you my number before you did it for as long as I have been getting up half an hour earlier than you.*”

“*And I stopped it on Saturday morning because a man of thirty-one said he had noticed it happening.*”

“*That was for him. Not for the four of you and not for that bank. For him.*”

“*And here is the reason, and I am not going to make it small.*”

“*I have been asked for nothing in nineteen years and I have not said no to a person in nineteen years, and I worked that out on Saturday night.*”

“*And then I worked out that if I was the only one on that bank with a figure in his mouth, about nine of you would come to me for yours.*”

“*And I have not had to turn one of you down in nineteen years and I have not known whether I could.*”

“*I have not found out.*”

“*And it is a Tuesday morning and I have said it in front of about nine of you and it is not better for having been said.*”

“*And I am going to go on getting up half an hour early.*”

“*And nobody is to say a word of thanks to me for this morning and nobody is to be told I stood up here and said a true thing about myself.*”

Then a man of forty-four said his was for a boy of thirteen, and said that he had known it was a bad reason when he gave it.

Tomas Elrick is forty-four and pushed a handcart up that cart road for twenty-six years. Its near shaft let go of its socket on the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. There are three barrow-loads of gravel in it standing on the flat of that bank.

“*Mine was for the boy.*”

“*I said the worst reason anybody has had on that bank out loud on Saturday morning. I said it because a boy of thirteen had not said his on the Saturday night.*”

“*And I knew that at the time and I said it anyway.*”

“*And it is not for you nine and it is not for the man of thirty-one.*”

“*It is for a boy of thirteen who told us on Monday that he was not going to say tonight what his question was.*”

“*And I am not going to tell him I was thinking about him.*”

“*He is thirteen and he did not ask me, and if I say it then he has to answer it, and he would have to answer it in front of us.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said his was for himself and for about four of them and refused to have either of the two taken off, and a boy of thirteen had nothing to do with it and said so.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He stood on that bank at the ninth hour for six mornings running. On Monday morning he said he was not going to say anything about the seventh, and he has not stood there since.

“*Mine was for me and it was also for about four of you, and that is the whole of it.*”

“*I told you last Monday that I was going to stand at the ninth hour, and I told you before I did it and not after.*”

“*And the reason I told you before is that I wanted about four of you to notice me doing it.*”

“*And that is a thing I did for me. And it is also a thing I did for about four of you.*”

“*And I have been sitting on this bank for nine mornings working out which of those two it was, and it is both.*”

“*And I am not going to have one of them taken off.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked which one was the real one, and if anybody asks me I will say it was both and nothing else.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-eight days.

“*I have got nothing for that and I am not going to pretend I have got something for it.*”

“*I said I would give the question to about nine of you at once so that about four of you could check it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has said his was for him and for about four of us, and that is one answer and it is not the one I built the four for.*”

“*I am not going to tell him he is wrong, because I do not know that he is wrong.*”

“*And I am not going to work out a new figure this morning, because I would be making one up.*”

“*I have told this bank twice already that a figure of mine is a figure of a bank and not of anybody.*”

“*So I have asked about nine of you a thing and I have got four answers, and one of the four is not the shape, and I am going to carry that about and not fix it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said that she was not going to answer, gave the reason in one sentence, and did not give a figure.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has put her crumb of bread in her apron and her pencil in her sleeve. She is not giving the third figure this morning or any morning after it. She gave a figure of about four of them on Saturday morning and a different figure of about four of them on Monday morning, and not one of the two fours was the same four.

“*I am not going to answer, and I am going to say why in one sentence, so that about nine of you know it was a decision and not me forgetting.*”

“*I gave you two figures of four of you this week and the two of them did not have one of the same four in them.*”

“*That is the answer to the question that boy asked, and I am not going to say it out loud at about the ninth hour.*”

“*Not because it is mine. Because it is a figure of four of you.*”

“*And he has just spent a morning making about four of you into the people who can check him, and those are not the same four, and he is thirteen.*”

“*And nobody is to say a word of thanks to a boy of thirteen for asking a question in front of about nine of us.*”

“*He is not to be thanked and he is not to be told he was brave, and if anybody does either of those things he will stop.*”

Then a man of thirty-one came to the end of it and said what he wanted and what he had got wrong and what it cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it, and said that four of them agreeing to say the same thing is not anybody refusing anything.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and fifty-seven days, two hundred and fifty-seven being eight hundred and eleven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and twenty-six days old, a hundred and twenty-six being eight hundred and eleven less six hundred and eighty-five. He has asked seven times whether it will keep, nobody has said, and he is not going to ask an eighth time.

“*And a boy of thirteen has handed me a morning and I have got three things to say about it, and two of them are about me.*”

“*The first is that I said the want out loud on Friday for the first time, and about four of you did not believe it.*”

“*And this morning a boy of thirteen has gone and asked about nine of you for the thing a man of thirty-eight has been doing for six mornings.*”

“*That is the whole of the want. I said on Friday that I want somebody who can come back the next morning and tell me I am wrong.*”

“*And it is about that boy at about the ninth hour asking one thing of nine people so that four of them can check it.*”

“*Here is the want and it has not moved in five days. It was three days on Sunday and two days on Saturday.*”

“*And I am saying that every morning now, so that about four of you can check I am not changing it in the morning and putting it back in the afternoon.*”

“*And here is the mistake, and it is the twenty-third of the same mistake, and it is about eleven hours old.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight gave it to me at about the ninth hour in front of about nine of you, and he did not do it for me.*”

“*He said it was for him and it was also for about four of us.*”

“*And I have been on this bank since before it had about nine of us on it, treating those as two different things and using the one I liked.*”

“*I have been doing that since a man of thirty-four came up here and gave three things and stopped, and I called that being careful.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-nine told me on Thursday of last week, in four sentences, that it was a fence. She was right and I have had five days with it.*”

“*The cost is that about nine of you now know that I have wanted that, and about four of you are going to be careful with me about it.*”

“*A hundred and eleven days. A hundred and eleven is eight hundred and eleven less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the thing that is going to look like the most this week is not going to be the boy, because a boy asking a question is a question.*”

“*It is going to be about four of us agreeing to say the same thing in a room four hundred miles off.*”

“*And that is not a refusal and it is not the right of anybody. Agreeing is not being asked.*”

“*A person who is asked and says no is not the same as a person who was never asked, and neither of those is the right of refusal.*”

“*And that right is not restored this morning and it is not given to a boy of thirteen, or to a man of thirty-eight, or to me.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a barrow-load of gravel, or of a floor in a bakehouse four miles up that road, or of a man of thirty-four in an ash.*”

“*Nobody says a word of thanks to me for this morning and nobody is to be told that anybody on that bank was brave about any of it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Tuesday with four inches of water over the top step above them. A boy of thirteen had asked about nine of them for one thing, and four of them had answered, and one of the four had said it was for both.

The fever was seventy-five weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and fifty-seven days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and twenty-six days old, and a hundred and eleven days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

A boy of thirteen counted his days in a lane and gave his working out loud, and named four people who could check what he was going to ask.
And a man of fifty-four said the thing he had been giving before anybody asked it for was for a man of thirty-one and not for the four of them, and gave the reason he has carried for nineteen years without knowing it was there.

A man of thirty-eight said his was for himself and for about four of them and refused to have either of the two taken off.
And a man of thirty-one said he had wanted, since before that bank had about nine of us on it, the thing a boy of thirteen did at about the ninth hour, and that four of them agreeing to say the same thing is not anybody refusing anything.
