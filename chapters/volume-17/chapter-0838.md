# Chapter 838: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Forty-First Week, And A Monday, And Sixteen Inches And Four Days Of The Coming Back And A Full Bank, And The Answer Is Given In The Open And It Is A Gap Of Two Hundred And Six Days In A Figure Of A Book, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Is Told Her Nineteen Years Are The Same Shape And Gives Them Away A Third Time In Twenty-Three Days, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says There Is A Third Of These

Seventh day of the hundred and forty-first week. His five hundred and eighty-eighth morning. Four hundred and thirty-eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy-nine weeks and two days. Three hundred and ninety-two days since the division. Three hundred and thirty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was four days of the coming back, and that was a full bank. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time, and said that a full bank holds at sixteen until it comes off, and that a man of thirty-one had asked him on Friday when that would be and he had said he would not be asked.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has not given the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under since Monday, and about four of them have stopped asking him for it, which he says is the correct number of times to be asked for a figure he has not got.

“*About sixteen inches at the seventh hour, and it is four days of the coming back, and that is a full bank.*”

“*And a full bank holds at sixteen until it comes off, and the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days, which is the fourth day of the hundred and forty-second week.*”

“*And I have given you the inches and the day of the coming back and the next time, and I have not given you the figure of how many of those ninety steps are under since Monday, and about four of you have stopped asking, and I noticed.*”

Then at about the ninth hour the answer was given in the open, and a man of sixty-one read it, and said what reading it had cost him, and gave the two counts on either side of it, and a man of about fifty-two did the subtraction and named what he could not check.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has a book of heads and carts and two numbers in it in eleven years. He has refused since the second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week to write a bench’s notice in it. He read all four leaves of a thing on a flat in about nine ears on four mornings, one leaf a morning. He has never read an answer out loud in about nine ears before this morning.

“*It came at about the eighth hour. I am not going to say how, because I do not know how, and I have been on this bank eleven days saying out loud what I do not know. This is the largest one yet.*”

“*Here is the cost before the answer, because a woman of sixty-four asked for that on Wednesday in about nine of your ears.*”

“*Eleven years I have refused to write a bench’s notice in that book. This morning I read one out of a page of the same kind, in about nine of your ears, and I did not write it in.*”

“*Not writing it is a choice. It is mine and it is not a rule and nobody has given it to me. I am not going to stand here and be praised for a choice I made in about a second.*”

“*Here is the answer, and it is short, and I am reading it the way it is written.*”

“*It says: the interval between the first entry ever made in any linked field and the second is shown, and the two counts on either side of it are given, and no summary is offered.*”

“*It says: the first entry is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days before this morning. The second is one thousand and forty days before this morning.*”

“*And it says: in that interval the arrangement entered no day against any person, and no name, and answered every question put to it, and recorded none of the answers.*”

“*That is the whole of it. There is no third sentence and there is nothing on the back and I have turned it over and the back is blank, and a man of fifty-four will tell you that I turned it over because he heard the paper.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two did the subtraction out loud in about nine ears, and said the subtraction was his and the two counts were not, and named his seventh thing, and a boy of thirteen said what the two counts were not.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and he has a palm and a forearm, and he has named six things he cannot check. He has been asked to read nothing and to be read to, and he did both.

“*The subtraction is mine and I am going to do it twice.*”

“*One thousand two hundred and forty-six, less one thousand and forty. That is two hundred and six.*”

“*Two hundred and six days. That is twenty-nine weeks and three days, and I am not going to give it to you in weeks.*”

“*A man of thirty-one told me on Friday that a figure of weeks with nothing under it is the figure that has caught about nine of us out in sixteen volumes. I have been on this bank long enough to believe him.*”

“*Two hundred and six days is a figure of a gap in a figure of a book. No place is counted by it. No person is counted by it. This county is not counted by it either.*”

“*And it is not a figure of how many people were asked anything, and about four of you were waiting for me to say that, and I have said it twice because one was not enough.*”

“*And that is my seventh thing I cannot check, and it is the two counts and not the subtraction. One thousand two hundred and forty-six and one thousand and forty came back out of a page and I cannot put a line under either of them, and I have not pretended otherwise at any hour of this morning.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-five days, and he has counted them in a lane, and he has been keeping a count of his own since Monday that is not his days and is not his eight and is not the houses.

“*A hundred and eighty-five days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And here is the other count and I have been keeping it since Wednesday and I have not given it to anybody.*”

“*It is how many of you have said yes to a thing this week. Not a promise, and not a yes to a person. A yes to a piece of work.*”

“*It is four. A man of fifty-four. A woman of sixty-four. A man of about fifty-two, from the top of the steps, with his hand. And a man of forty-four, who gave up nine feet of hole on the second day of this week and said so himself.*”

“*And that is four of you and it is not four of this bank and it is not a figure of anybody who was not here, and I am not going to let it be one, and I made that rule myself at the fourth hour on Tuesday and I am using it.*”

“*And I am not counting the two who said no. They are not part of it.*”

“*And I am not counting a man of thirty-one, because a man of thirty-one saying yes to his own work is not a yes and I have been carrying that since Friday too, and I am not going to argue with him about it on a Monday.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four was told in about nine ears what the gap was the same shape as, and paid for it a second time in one week, and refused in the same sentence an offer of thanks that was made to her out loud.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line of a book four hundred miles off and has never had a day against it. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and was forty-five when she worked out that the thing asking was not a person. She said on Sunday of last week that the nineteen years are hers and are not a leaf, and that she is not afraid of the eleventh line and is also tired of it.

“*Read me the part about me, and read it in about nine ears, and do not do it in a voice.*”

“*There it is.*”

“*I gave you a gap. I have given you a gap every morning for nineteen years, and this morning the answer says that the arrangement entered no day and no name in that gap, and answered everything and recorded none of it.*”

“*So the gap is not mine. It never was mine. It is the arrangement’s own way of doing things, done once before, and it can be shut by the same arrangement without a single thing being written about me, and I have relied on it every morning of nineteen years.*”

“*That is the third time in twenty-three days I have given those nineteen years away in front of about nine of you, and the first time I said they were mine and not evidence, and this morning they are not mine and they are not evidence either. They are the arrangement’s, and I have been living in them.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine in a reed said out loud that she wanted to thank her, and a woman of sixty-four refused the thanks in the same sentence, and a woman of twenty-nine said she had said it and was not going to unsay it and was not going to say it again.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about twenty feet off the low side of that drain, a hundred yards along it, for two hundred and nine days, and she can read, and she has said that us is four people.

“*Two hundred and nine days. That is eight hundred and thirty-eight less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*I want to thank you. I am saying it in about nine of your ears and I am not going to take it back and I am not going to say it again.*”

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has a name in ink on that ninth line and has never had a day against it, and she heard it, and she said the rest of the sentence herself.

“*Do not thank me for handing nineteen years over a second time, because the second time is not a gift, and there is nobody in this county to give a gift to, and you are the one who asked me for it by asking him to ask me.*”

“*And I want that said in about nine of your ears in that order, and I want it said on Monday and not remembered as a thing I said kindly on a Sunday.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked, and I am not going to be told that it was brave, and a woman of twenty-nine has just paid for my saying no in about nine of your ears and I would like about four of you to notice that she is the one who paid and not me.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said that today was the Monday he had named on Friday and that he was still not opening what is in the inside of his coat, and gave the reason, and gave a day, and worked the day out loud.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and it is not opened. He said on Friday that the reason he had not given on Thursday was that he did not know what he would do with it, and that he had known that since the Saturday of the hundred and thirty-ninth week.

“*I said Friday that I would open it on Monday if I had the part where I would know what to do. It is Monday. I have not got that part.*”

“*And I have got a different reason today and I am not going to give you Friday’s, because Friday’s is still true and a true reason does not stop being true because a second one arrives.*”

“*Here is the second one. On Saturday a man of about fifty-two went into a drain on his own and came out with a palm. A man of thirty-four said in about nine ears that the drain he asked for twice found a bridge out. A woman of twenty-nine said what a well costs, which is six hundred yards there and six hundred yards back four times a day.*”

“*About nine of you have had three things this week that were not anybody’s fault and that all of us will be carrying. I am not going to be the fourth thing that happens to this bank on a Monday, and I am not going to decide anything today about a boy of thirteen and a coat.*”

“*And the day. The seventh day of the hundred and forty-second week, and that is seven days from today, and I have worked it out from the week this bank is in and not off a calendar, and a man of about fifty-two can check it and I would rather he did.*”

“*And I am not saying next week, because I said not this week last week and that turned out to be a way of saying nothing, and I have heard myself do it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one named a third of these, and said they were not doing it today, and gave the reason out loud, and said what he wanted, what he had got wrong, and what it had cost, and gave the figure for the standing offer in his own mouth with the subtraction in it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and eighty-four days, two hundred and eighty-four being eight hundred and thirty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and fifty-three days old, a hundred and fifty-three being eight hundred and thirty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*There is a third of these, and I am naming it because I have had a week of two and a band that spends both has spent two, and about four of you have been waiting since Monday for me to say there was a third.*”

“*The first was whether two remedies can stand in one place, and a string said no about fifteen yards. The second was whether a remedy survives being compared, and a bridge said no.*”

“*The third is whether a remedy that belongs to a person can stand in the same place as the answer of an arrangement. A gap. A name in ink with no day. A day that nobody in this county can be sent for. Whether one of those can be kept while the arrangement answers the ground underneath it.*”

“*And we are not doing it today, and here is the reason, and it is a reason I gave on Wednesday and I am not breaking it to get to a third.*”

“*It needs a person to be put at the edge of it. It needs somebody to agree to have their own protection looked at by about nine of us, on a Sunday, in about nine ears, with the cost named before the question is asked.*”

“*On Wednesday I described that and then I said I was not going to ask one of those four people this week. It is Monday. I am not going to start a week by breaking the only thing I said out loud in it.*”

“*And when it is done, it will not be me asking, and I am saying that now so that about four of you can hold me to it in about a week.*”

“*Here is the want and it is nine mornings old and it is the same want and I have not improved it.*”

“*I want somebody else to carry the figures, and on the fourth day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week I said out loud that a thing with a nail in it would take the eleventh hour off me.*”

“*This week it has taken the eleventh hour off me twice. On Saturday I gave a figure about a bridge that a man of about fifty-two did better. On Sunday I put a question under a door I did not want to use.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is the fortieth of the same mistake and I am not numbering it and I am not going to keep saying that I am not numbering it.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight said on Friday that he would open what he has in his coat on Monday. He did not. And I have had six hours today in which I could have said something to him, and I did not, and I have been thinking about it since the ninth hour and I have not done anything about it.*”

“*I decided last night that it would be at the seventh hour that a man of about fifty-two went into a drain. I decided it about a bridge and the man of thirty-four has had that out of me in about nine of your ears already and I am not going to say it twice, and about four of you have noticed that I have said it twice.*”

“*The cost of that is a man of about fifty-two with a palm, and it is a figure of a day and not a figure of a person, and I am the only person in this county it belongs to.*”

“*A hundred and thirty-eight days. A hundred and thirty-eight is eight hundred and thirty-eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And an answer given in the open settles nothing. Two hundred and six days of nothing settles nothing. A woman of sixty-four finding out her nineteen years were never hers settles nothing. A man of thirty-eight keeping a coat shut settles nothing.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning. Not by the zone, and not by eighty yards of lime, and not by a woman of sixty-four paying twice, and not by me putting a question under a line that a thing on a flat wrote.*”

“*I do not know what will restore it and I have stopped saying that I do, because I have said it nine mornings running and about four of you have worked out that saying it is a way of not deciding.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of sixty-four who was told in about nine ears that a gap she had relied on every morning for nineteen years was the arrangement’s own, and who gave those years away a third time in twenty-three days.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a woman of twenty-nine in a reed who paid for that in about nine of your ears by saying out loud that she wanted to thank her, and who is not going to say it again.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of thirty-eight who had a day named and did not use it, and who gave a reason and a second reason, and who said the seventh day of the hundred and forty-second week and worked it out loud.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of a man of sixty-one who read a bench’s answer out of a bench’s page for the first time in eleven years and did not write it in his book, and who says that not writing it is a choice and not a rule.*”

“*And nobody is to be told that any of the four of them was brave about it, and I am not going to be thanked either, and a woman of sixty-four has just told about nine of you in about nine ears what happens to a thank you in this county and I am not going to argue with her.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Monday, with sixteen inches of water over the top step above them and a full bank sitting there, and an answer read out loud in about nine ears that had a gap of two hundred and six days in it.
A woman of sixty-four had been told that the thing she had relied on for nineteen years was the arrangement’s own. A boy of thirteen had said that four of them had said yes to a piece of work this week, and that it was four of them and not four of that bank.

The fever was seventy-nine weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eighty-four days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and fifty-three days old, and a hundred and thirty-eight days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

An answer was given in the open and a man of sixty-one read it, and it said that between the first entry ever made in any linked field and the second there are two hundred and six days, and that in those days the arrangement entered no day and no name, and answered every question, and recorded none of the answers.
And a man of about fifty-two did the subtraction out loud and said the subtraction was his and the two counts were not, and called them his seventh thing he cannot check, and refused to give the gap in weeks.

A woman of sixty-four was told that her nineteen years were the same shape as that gap and were not hers, and gave them away a third time in twenty-three days.
A woman of twenty-nine said out loud that she wanted to thank her, and she refused the thanks in the same sentence and said the woman of twenty-nine was the one who had paid.

And a man of thirty-eight said that today was the Monday he had named on Friday, that he had not opened what is in the inside of his coat, and that he had a second reason as well as the first.

He named the seventh day of the hundred and forty-second week and worked it out loud.
