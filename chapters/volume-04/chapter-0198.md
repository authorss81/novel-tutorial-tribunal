# Chapter 198: What The Account Says

A reeve of fifty-four walked six miles up a road that goes north on the sixth day of the forty-ninth week with a bundle in a basket, the day after a draft went into a room four miles off, because a sheet of the district’s own does not go into a book by itself and a person of the district is the only person who may carry a thing out of that district, and a man of thirty-one from nowhere is not a person of anything and a woman of twenty-eight with a recordwright’s licence is not either, and both of those facts had been said out loud in that room the day before and neither of them had been argued with.

There is about two hours of light in the middle of that towpath and it is six miles in each direction, and she got there at about the ninth bell and put the basket down on a step and went away from it, because the man who opens the post in that building is not there every day and nobody has asked him to be and she is not going to start this week.

What was on the outside of the bundle was a number, and it was not a name, and it was the number that is at the top of one of a hundred numbered sheets, and she had copied it off one in a yard off a lane nine days ago while nine people stood in that yard and not one of them knew she was doing it, because a bundle of paper with no number on the outside of it is a bundle a man cannot find again, and she has been putting numbers on the outside of things for nine years and there has never been anywhere for them to go.

A boy of nineteen took it at about the fourth bell the next day and turned it over once, which is what he does with everything, and read the four lines on the face of it, and did not ask a single question about any of them, and that is the thing he is going to be asked about in about a year by a person who is not him.

He is two years in that office. He is entitled to take depositions for eleven months and not entitled to anything else. He entered a number for that district nine days ago at the request of a person of the district and wrote down a true thing she said in a lane at the same counter, and he has not told anybody in that building that he did it and is not going to until the day he has to. This is the second time and it is going to be the last of them that anybody had to ask for.

“Second time,” said Marda Fen.

“Second time I have had a person of a district stand at that counter with something that is not a claim and not a return,” said Amos Kellock. “In two years. And I have about nine thousand returns behind me and every one of them has a claim on it, and the first one had a hundred loads on it and no claim and nothing asked for, and this one has a hole in the third line about the size of a hand.”

He squared the bundle on the counter and did not open it further.

“And the line that goes in against the account is not yours,” he said. “A person of the district is a party to this matter. You are the party. A burden cannot go on a party’s page by a stranger’s number, and this is your page and the third line of that sheet has nobody’s name in it, so it goes against the number on the outside of the bundle or it does not go in at all.”

“That is what I came to find out,” said Marda Fen.

“That is what I have been in two years finding out and it is the first time it has come to the front of the counter.” He turned the form about a quarter of a circle. “And I am going to say the rest of it before you ask me, because you are going to ask me, and because I would rather you had it from me than worked out on a towpath with a basket.”

---

Here is what the account’s own book says for the district of Ryefold, second mouth, on the seventh day of the forty-ninth week, and it is a page of a book in a building six miles up a road that goes north and the account is a party and has never been asked to show it in a room. It is seven lines long and four of them are the four lines a boy of nineteen read out in a room four miles off on the first of the forty-eighth week, in the district’s own words, on a page the district paid sixteen pence for, and those four were the whole of the page then, and the last time a person of that district stood in that building she was standing at this counter nine days ago with an envelope and nothing asked for it.

> RYEFOLD, SECOND MOUTH.
> LOADS ENTERED 3,400. AT THE COUNTY’S RATE OF 2/8 THE LOAD.
> CARRIAGE PAID AGAINST THE ACCOUNT, 3,000 LOADS AT 3/0. SHORT 100 LOADS.
> CLAIM OF A CARTER, 406 LOADS, 54/2/8. PAID 9/0. THE REMAINDER TO A YEAR.
> LOADS WEIGHED FOR THE DISTRICT AT THE COUNTY’S RATE, 140, 18/13/4. NO CLAIM ENTERED. NOTHING ASKED FOR.
> A REPAYMENT DRAFT, FOUR LINES, THE THIRD A HOLLOW. PUT BY A PERSON OF THIS DISTRICT. FOUR WITNESSES NAMED ON A PAGE IN THE DISTRICT’S OWN ROOM, NOT COPIED.
> THE BURDEN STATED AGAINST THE PARTY IS ENTERED AGAINST NO. 63 OF ONE HUNDRED, WHICH IS A NUMBER UPON A SHEET AND NOT A NAME. THE PERSON WHO HAS TAKEN IT IS NOT A PARTY, IS NOT OF THIS DISTRICT, AND COULD NOT BE ENTERED BY NAME, THERE BEING NO LINE IN THIS BOOK FOR A PERSON WHO IS NOT A PARTY. THE BURDEN IS A YEAR OF ONE MAN’S MEMORY AND IT IS PAID. NO LINE IN THIS BOOK SAYS WHICH YEAR, AND THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR ONE, AND THE PERSON WHO PAID IT IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO COULD PUT ONE THERE.

Nobody read it out loud in that building. Amos Kellock read it twice, standing up, with a finger on the line, and then he put the book down flat and did not say anything for as long as it takes a carter to decide something, and there were nine people in that lane.

“You have entered a year against a number,” said Marda Fen.

“I have entered what came in against the number on the outside of the bundle,” said Amos Kellock. “And I did not enter the year. I entered what was in the sheet and the sheet said a year and so the line says a year, and the office enters what comes in, and there is nothing in this building that lets me put a man’s name in a page he is not party to and there is nothing in this building that lets me take a number off a line, and there is no form for either.”

“Then somebody else could.”

“Nobody could, and not by anybody, and not ever, and there is no form for asking.” He put his hand flat on the counter, which is a thing he does. “A name is a person and a person can be asked whether that is his name and he will say it is or it is not. A number cannot be asked anything. I said that to a man in this lane on the third day of the forty-eighth week and it is in a minute in a room four miles off and it is the same thing that is on this page, and I have had a week with it and I have not been able to make it smaller and I have stopped trying.”

Before he said them, the reeve asked the boy one question and it was about the third line of a page she had paid sixteen pence to copy herself, and she asked it in her own mouth and she had not asked anybody anything about a number for nine years.

“Read me the third line again.”

“There are three thousand four hundred loads entered and three thousand of them paid for and there is a hundred short,” said Amos Kellock, “and a claim of four hundred and six loads at fifty-four pounds two shillings and eightpence, of which nine pounds was paid on the sixth day of the forty-third week and the remainder of forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence waits to a year.”

“Whose claim.”

“A carter’s. Fifty-one, with a boy, and he is not of this district, and he is eleven miles off, and he was owed it before the cut and he has not had it, and the minute of the remainder is in a room four miles off in a book, and the book is not in this building and this building has never had it.” He put the book down flat. “And nobody in this building can pay it and nobody in this building can produce him and there is no form for a person named in a claim to ask for it to be entered, and he will be back in about a year, and he is right, and I have not met him and I am not going to pretend to have.”

“Two shillings and eightpence is the county’s rate and three shillings is the register’s,” said Marda Fen. “I have known both of those since the sixth day of the forty-third week and I have never known what the fourpence was, and I have worked it out four times in a fortnight and it is fourpence a load, and fourpence is the price of copying one sheet at that counter, and a sheet is one shilling and fourpence, so a sheet is four times the difference, and I do not know why it is four times and not once, and nobody has told me and I have stopped asking in a room.”

“You have not stopped asking in a building,” said Amos Kellock. “That is a difference and it is the only one I have found in two years.”

Ansel Cork came out from the back of that building at about the fifth bell and said one thing, and the one thing was the thing he says, and Marda Fen had heard it five times in about a year and did not answer it and let him finish, and he has never once said it in a building, and she had wondered about that for a fortnight and did not ask.

And then he said the other thing, and it was about the line, and it was two sentences, and it is the only time in nineteen years he has said anything to a reeve about a page in that book that the account did not put there itself.

“Nothing comes off,” said Ansel Cork. “I have said that in a lane and in a room and I have said it a third time a day ago and I am not going to say it a fourth time, and I am telling you instead that I have been nineteen years in that post and I did not know until about nine minutes ago that the book had a fourth column, and it has not got one, and what it has got instead is a line, and a line is not a column and a line is what a thing turns into when nobody is going to give it a place.”

Then he went back inside and the door was shut behind him and it is not his door to shut.

---

And then the lane got quiet for about a minute and a half, which is not long, and the thing that nobody in that lane said is the thing that about nine weeks of this district have been walking towards since the second day of the fortieth week, and it was in the book on the seventh day of the forty-ninth week, and every person in that lane knew it and not one of them said it.

The book has a line that pays a year to somebody. The book does not say who. The number on the outside of the bundle is in a sacking bag in a yard in a district of six hundred people with ninety of the other numbers in it and a bit of string on the mouth of it, and it is not going anywhere until there is a road to take it on, and the bag has been in that yard since the seventh bell of the third day of the forty-eighth week and about four hundred carts have been standing in about nine hundred yards with nothing to do for three weeks and not one of them has been asked to carry ninety-one sheets a day to a door.

And the person who would know which year it was is standing in that lane.

Nobody asked him. Nobody in that lane has ever asked him a question that they did not already have the answer to, which is the whole of what that lane has been for about a month, and there are nine people in it and every one of them has watched something happen to a man in it since the second day of the fortieth week of this year and not one of them has ever mentioned it.

The white nothing did not put anything up. It has put four things in front of him in about nine weeks and this was the day a year of his own went out of him in a room four miles off with four witnesses in it, and it said nothing whatever about a year then and it said nothing whatever about a year now, and it is not a thing that warns and it is a thing that records, and this is going to be in a book six miles up a road that goes north, and in about nine months a clerk in a counting-house in another city four miles from that one is going to have to enter one line against the district of Ryefold against whatever is in the fourth of the fourth sheet, and the district cannot say, and there is no form for not saying it, and it is not for ever yet.

---

Then the reeve said the other thing that was in that counter, and she said it in her own mouth, and about a month ago she had said a version of it in a room above a chandler’s shop and had been told by a convenor of sixty-one that the whole of what she wanted out of it was one line.

“There is a name in that book that I put there in a different ink with no authority anywhere in this county for a reeve to do it,” said Marda Fen. “Nesh Arrad. She is thirty-nine. She came into this district in the twenty-second week of last year with four children and a stove and no paper at all, and I put her at the bottom of my own list in my own ink, and then a woman of thirty-nine who reads things walked her up this road six miles on the second day of the forty-seventh week with a form and a sheet I paid for, at a shilling and fourpence, and put one name on one sheet in a second hand, and it has been in that book ever since, and it is in there now, on the same page as the hundred loads and the year.”

“About nine months,” said Amos Kellock.

“About nine months, and it is the office’s own nine months and it is the time the thing takes and it is not a delay and it is not a punishment, and there are two nines in that building and neither of them is a punishment, and one of them is about five hundred and thirty people who did their jobs properly, of whom about one hundred and sixty never came back at all, and I have carried both of them up that path and I have never read either of them.” She put her hand on the counter and left it there. “And if they do not send a letter then it is for ever, and that was the first one, and it is not shut.”

Nobody in that lane said anything.

“And there is a box,” said Marda Fen, “that is not the box I am talking about and is the other one. There is a box of about nine hundred and forty forms in a run of four thousand, of which about three thousand and sixty were taken up, and it is not in this district, and it has not been in this district for about a year, and the man with the key is six miles off and is there two days out of seven and sweeps a passage and cannot read what is in his own nineteen shelves, and nobody in this district can get a message to him in a day.” She took her hand off the counter. “I have wanted that box for about nine years. It is not coming up a towpath and it is not coming in a basket and there is no form for asking for it, and that is a second nine months in the same building and it is not the same nine months and I have stopped being able to keep them apart in my own head.”

At about the seventh bell a basket went up the towpath with the district’s own papers in it and the reeve’s own hand on a number on the outside of them, and a basket that is not full weighs about eleven pounds, and the woman who carries it is paid three shillings a week in a cut and nothing the rest of the year and has carried it for nine years and has never read a thing in it and has never asked and says so before anybody asks her.

“Ninety-one of them are in a yard in a sacking bag and I am not asking about that either,” said Sena Ollet, at the bottom of the path, weighing it on a steelyard first, which is what she does. “About eleven pounds. That is light for the top of that path.” She shouldered the basket and went up, and she said one thing over her shoulder and it was about her own work and not about the basket, because that is the only thing she has ever said about it in nine years.

“I go nine days in a cut and once a month the rest of the year, and whatever is in that book today I will be on this path in about a month with a basket that weighs forty pounds and I will not have read one page of any of it, and that has been the arrangement for nine years and nobody has ever offered to change it and I would not let them.”

And at about the ninth bell a man of thirty-one was in a cart shed behind a granary with about seven thousand loads in the building and four hundred carts in the yard, and he sat down on an upturned pail with his back against a cart wheel and did not tear a page out of the exercise book, because there was nothing in that day that was not already in one of the three, and a page is the only instrument he has.

He did not write down one word about which year it was, and he could not have, and the week ended there, and there was nothing on a board in that lane but a name and a figure that had not moved since the sixth day of the forty-third week.
