# Chapter 197: The Year

Four persons of the district of Ryefold walked four miles on the sixth day of the forty-ninth week and got into a room up nine steps off a lane in a city of about ninety thousand people at about the second bell, and the reason they could do it is that there have been three weeks running in which about four hundred carts in this district have had nothing to do, and the week before that was the first such week in seven years, and no person in that county had ever thought to put anything on a list of the days a carter is free.

There was no sitting. There has not been a sitting in that room since the first of the forty-eighth week, and the next one is in the other half of the year, and everybody in that room knew both halves of that and nobody in it had said the second one out loud until a clerk of the counter said it at about the second bell with a tray in his hand and no weight on it.

“This is not a hearing,” he said. “Nothing is decided this morning and nothing can be decided this morning, because a draft is not a decision and a decision is not taken on the day a thing is heard. What happens this morning is an entry. Somebody in this room puts a page in front of the account and the account either enters it or it does not, and if it enters it then it goes into a book six miles up a road that goes north and it is in that book in about nine seconds and it is in this room in about nine months.”

“And if it does not enter it,” said Nevin Hake.

“Then we have all walked four miles,” said the clerk, “and I would rather that were said now than at the fifth bell.”

Ansel Cork came in from the other end of the room with no file under his arm, which is the first time anybody in that building has seen him come in without one, and he is fifty-one and has been in that post nineteen years and he is the man entitled to speak for the account, and the account is a party, and a party is the only thing in that room that can put a burden down.

He read the four lines twice and he did not read them a third time and he put them face up on the table.

“The third line is a hole,” he said.

“The third line is a hole,” said Sera Quill, from the side of the room, where she had been standing since the second bell with her own book shut. “A person of that district was asked to fill it and said no, and the reason she gave was said out loud in a room in that district in front of about nine people, and the person holding the pen in that room is not in this room, and I am not going to repeat it, because it is hers and not mine.”

“Then the account cannot enter it,” said Ansel Cork, “and I have said that in nineteen years in a room and I have never said it about a page with a hole in it, and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this room that I have thought about it.”

“You do not have to fill it,” said Ilyan Vester. “A party can put a burden down. It cannot put a burden on nobody, and there is no form anywhere in this county for a burden with no name in the middle of it, and the third line of that page is the only place in this county where a person can stand inside a sentence at all, and it has been a blank about the size of a hand for about a year, and it is empty, and I am the only person in this room who is not of the district it is about, and that is not a qualification. That is the whole of my use.”

“Four witnesses,” said the clerk of the counter, from the tray. “You cannot put a burden on a person on a party’s word. It takes four persons of a district who have each said a thing in their own words and can be asked about it afterwards. The account’s own book has got a column for the witness and it has not got a column for the person, and that is the whole of the form, and I have been at this counter two years and I have never once had anybody use it.”

---

The four were asked one question each and the question was the same one and it took about four minutes for all of it, and it is the only work a witness quorum has ever done in this county.

The four were there because a bench of four in its own room in a district six miles off had appointed them on a day the district chose, and a person cannot be compelled to testify and cannot be produced, and a bench’s own motion in its own room is the only thing in this county that can put four people in a chair.

The clerk asked them what they were answerable for. Not what they had seen, which is a question that was put to them four days ago in a room four miles off and is on a nail there in a second hand, but what they were answerable for.

A convenor of sixty-one said: nineteen years of a book, the thirty-four names he wrote into it on the sixth day of the forty-fifth week, a page with a question in it and no answer under it, and a nail with five things on it.

A carter of forty-six said: a hundred and forty loads of this district’s own grain that went over a beam in this district and into a book six miles up a road that goes north with nobody’s name on the line, a hundred loads of hire that were never paid for at anything, and four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year on nine thousand seven hundred pounds.

A man of thirty-three said: about nine hundred doors, a hundred days at nine a day, three pounds fifteen, and a list of about nine hundred names that nobody in this district is able to walk, and the four hundred and sixty-third form.

A reader of thirty-nine said: two hundred and forty names in her own hand, a dead man in the fourth line, a woman at the bottom of it in a different ink with no authority behind her, and a page on the fourth sheet that says none of the four has agreed with any of the others, and she said that last one twice, because it was the one that had been asked about and it was hers to answer for.

Nobody in that room said anything about the fact that she is a person in this matter, and she had said twice that she is, and the reason nobody said anything is that a witness is not a party and the third line of the fourth sheet in this county has been a hole for about a year and the only difference between a person in a matter and a witness in a matter is which of the two of them is holding the pen.

---

Then he said the price out loud, in the ordinary way, standing up at the end of a table with nine people in the room and four of them having come four miles for it.

“I am going to say what this costs and I am going to say it once and nobody is to answer it,” he said. “One year of my own memory, out of thirty-one. It is a specific year and not a fog and not a wound and nobody is going to tell me afterwards what was in it. It is the last of the years in which I was in a room with a person I had not yet met, and I have never written it down and I am not going to write it down, and the reason it is the last one is in a book in this district and it is that the not was available and I took it, and about a dozen years of those rooms were all the same room and I went into all of them because there was nobody else to go, and this morning there are four people in this room who have each said a thing in their own words and can be asked about it afterwards and one of them can read and three of them cannot and I have said that out loud in front of nine people and it went into a minute and it is the only reason I am standing here.”

Nobody said anything.

“The person who named that price is not in this room and is not one of the nine of you, and is a long way from here, and I have never found out whether the naming was fair and I am not going to ask anybody this morning,” he said.

“Not one of you can check what I have just said,” he said, “and that is not a trick of mine. It is how a person becomes a cheap thing to name, and I have been the cheapest thing in this county to put a name on for about a month and I am going to stop being it by paying it, and I am not going to be relieved of it and nobody is to tell me the price was fair, because a man relieved of a price is not a man who paid one.”

Then the thing came up in its own two plain lines, in a lane, in a city six miles off a district of six hundred people, in the flat white nothing that has put four things in front of him in about nine weeks, and it did not say one word about any of the nine people in that room.

**THE BURDEN STATED AGAINST THE PARTY OF THE DISTRICT OF RYEFOLD IS ENTERED AGAINST A PERSON, AND ITS SOURCE IS A RULING AND NOT A PERSON, AND IT IS DUE AT THE CLOSE OF EVERY GRAIN YEAR, AND A PERSON WHO HAS TAKEN A BURDEN UPON HIM MAY NOT BE COMPELLED TO GIVE IT BACK.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A WITNESS WHO CANNOT SAY WHAT A THING SAYS IS A WITNESS TO THE THING.**

He read it twice. It said nothing whatever about a year, and it did not take anything, and nothing came out of him in that room, and the entry it was about had not been made yet and was made an hour later by a man with nineteen years in a post, and about nine people in this district have watched something happen to him in the last ten weeks and not one of them has ever mentioned it.

And while he was reading it the rendering came up, at the height and at the distance it has been at since before he came off a boat in this county, in a city four hundred miles behind him: the boards, the table the size of a table a hand could go on, the lamp with the wick down, and four people at it, and one of them about forty-four with her back to him.

It did not move. It did not explain itself. His leg was what his leg has been since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him and it did not do anything at all, and there is a reason for that and he had it in about four seconds and the reason is that a year is not a thing anybody is carrying and the only thing that has ever cleared that leg in his life was a piece of paper somebody else was going to have to read, and there was nothing in that room he was carrying for anybody.

Nobody in that room saw anything, and he has never in this district told anybody that he had expected it to, and that is the whole of it.

---

Ansel Cork entered it at about the fourth bell and it took about nine minutes, and there is no copy of it, because a copy is fourpence a sheet and this district is at nothing, and the account entered it, and the account is a party, and a party is the only thing in that room that can put a burden down, and a person cannot be compelled to take one and cannot be produced.

He said three things when it was done, and the first of them had been standing in a minute since the thirty-fifth day.

“The cost of the notice of four sheets remains with the district,” said Ansel Cork. “Five shillings and fourpence. It is the fourth line of that page and it is the only line of it the account cannot do anything about, and it has been on that minute since the thirty-fifth day, and this district has never paid any of it and is not going to, and I have said it out loud in a room twice this morning and I am not going to say it again.”

Nobody in that room argued with him about it.

“And a forbearance may be applied for once a year,” he said, “and the district applied for one on the thirty-fifth day of this year and it was set aside and not refused, and once a year is once a year, so the next one this district can put its hand on is fifty-two weeks off, and there is no form for applying for one and there has not been one since before I came. That is the only door in this county that this thing came through, and nobody in this room can hold it open and nobody in this room can shut it.”

“Will the carrying come off the record,” said Perrin Gant, who had been a convenor in that district for nineteen years and had been asked for nothing in all of them.

“No,” said Ansel Cork. “The line is on the party and it is nineteen years old and it is in the account’s own book and the account’s own book has three columns in it and one of them is a claim and a line that is not a claim is not a thing that comes off. I have told you that in a lane and I have told you that in a room four miles off six days ago and I am telling you it a third time because a man has paid a year for it and I would like the three of those to be the same three.”

---

And afterwards, at about the sixth bell, in the corridor outside, on a bench that is not for sitting on, in a nine-foot corridor about nine yards long, four persons of a district stood about and one of them said the only thing anybody said that afternoon which was about a wheel.

Dace Ferrow was fifty-seven and he had come four miles with the other three and he had a wheel in his shop and a boy of sixteen who cannot turn a wheel without him, and he had not been paid for a day in the forty-sixth week of this year and he was not going to be paid for this one, and he said that in about nine words and then went down the lane.

“It is a day and a day,” said Dace Ferrow, “and neither of them is going to be paid and that is the correct arrangement and I am not going to be asked about it in a corridor.” He looked at the man with the basket. “You have paid a year and you have still got the thing you were carrying, have you not.”

“I have still got it,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Then that is the right way round and I would not have it any other,” said the wheelwright, “and I am going down four miles to a shop with a wheel in it and I have to be at that wheel at the second bell tomorrow, and you are not going to tell me that a man who cannot read ought to be helped with it, because I have had that off a bench of four and I have had it in a lane and it does not work.”

The four went down the lane at about the seventh bell, four miles, in about an hour and a half, and not one of the nine people who watched them go out of that lane knew where the four of them had been or what any of them had said in that room, and the room emptied the way that room empties, which is four people at a time and no farewells.

And a man of thirty-one sat down on the bench that is not for sitting on, in a corridor about nine yards long, and found that he could not remember which year it was.

Not the year in general. He could say what the year was: the last of the years in which he was in a room with a person he had not yet met, and the not was available and he took it, and there were about a dozen of them and they were all the same room. He had said that out loud twenty minutes ago in front of nine people and he could still say all of it, and it was the best-known thing he had.

He just could not put a year on it. He went through them the way a man goes along a handrail in the dark, four at a time, and there was nothing there at all. He had named this one out loud twenty minutes ago, in a room, in front of nine people, and had never written it down and had told nobody which it was, and the sentence about why it is the last one was the best sentence he has ever said in a room, and the sentence did not have a year in it and never had, and this morning was the morning he found out what that was worth.

He sat on the bench that is not for sitting on for about four minutes and he did not tell anybody, because there was nobody there, and at about the ninth bell he got up and went and took a penny exercise book out of his coat and sat down on a step in the lane with his back against the wall of a building with a counter in it, and wrote on the seventeenth page, and the two lines at the end of it are the two lines that are on the other sixteen.

*I have paid it. It is a year of my own out of thirty-one and it is gone in the ordinary way in a room with four witnesses in it and nobody was relieved of anything and nobody said the price was fair and nobody has told me what was in it and I am not going to be told. It is the last of the years in which I was in a room with a person I had not yet met. I can say all of that.*

*I cannot say which year it was.*

*That is the whole of what has happened and there is nobody in this city who can put it back and there is nobody in this district who can put it back and I am not going to ask the four of them, because they were in the room with me and I said it out loud and what they heard was a shape, and a shape is not a year, and I asked for a shape because a shape is the only part of it that is not just me.*

*It is mine to write down and it is not mine to decide.*
