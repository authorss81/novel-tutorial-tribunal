# Chapter 187: A Form Can Be Numbered

The third day of the forty-eighth week, six miles up the road that goes north, in a counting-house lane, at about the second bell, a boy of nineteen put a tray down and said the thing nobody had asked him for a second time, and this time he said it faster, and the reason he said it faster is that the sentence had been in a minute four miles off for two days and it had not been in his head before that.

“It works,” said Amos Kellock. “Both halves of it work and I have had two days to sit with the fact and it has not got worse. A number on a sheet is the first thing this county has produced that a room can use without a person in it, and that is the whole of what a person in a room is for, and we have spent four hundred years putting a name on things instead, and the name is a person and a person has to turn up.”

He turned one of the hundred sheets round the way he turns everything, a quarter of a circle, so that the top of it was nearer the man than the bottom of it.

“There are ninety-one left of the hundred. Nine have gone out of this building in a bag to a county and I have not asked where and I am not going to, because a number is a number and the whole point of it is that a clerk who has never met the man can pull it. One of the nine went to a district in the second mouth, on a basket, with a load of other things in it, and it is in a room about six miles from this counter in a building with one door and a woman in it who is not on a wage line and does not know it is there.”

He put the sheet down flat and squared it.

“And I have got to tell you the other half of it out loud before you take the other ninety-one, because you have not asked me for it and I am going to give it to you for nothing, and I want that written down that I gave it for nothing.”

“You have given me the other half once already, in this room, six days ago,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Yes, and I have given it to you twice and it has got worse each time, because the first time it was a thing I was thinking about and now it is a thing that is in a minute, and a thing in a minute is a thing somebody can be produced under.” He turned the tray. “A hundred numbered sheets are the first thing anybody in this district has ever been able to produce rather than carry. And they can be produced against whoever holds them. It is the same sheet. It is the same hand. It took a man of sixty about a day to print a hundred of them and it will take a clerk about four seconds to read one of them out in any room in a hundred and one counties, and there is nobody in any of those rooms who can stand up and say that is not the man, because you cannot say that about a number.”

Nobody in the lane said anything for about nine seconds.

“So I am not going to be the one who tells you they are a good thing,” said Amos Kellock. “I am going to be the one who tells you they are the first thing in four years that this county has handed anybody that works, and that both of those are the same sentence and that it took me nineteen years of my life to be able to say them both out loud in the same breath, and I would like somebody to write that down.”

“You keep asking me to write things down,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Yes,” said Amos Kellock. “And I keep not writing them, and there is a reason for that and the reason is in a card on a wall in this building and it is that a thing an officer of this account has said about a register, in a year, with a date on it, is a thing somebody can use in about nine years. I have two years in this post and I have about nine thousand boxes behind me and I have not slept properly since a room four miles off read a page of paper out loud on the first day of this week, and I am nineteen and I went into that room with a tray and I came out of it with a sentence that is going in a book that a district of six hundred people has been trying to put into a book since the fortieth week.”

He squared the tray with two fingers.

“That is what has happened this week and I would like it noticed that it happened to me and not to you.”

They entered it against the account at about the fourth bell, and it took about nine minutes, and it cost four sheets at fourpence a sheet to copy, and the sixteen pence came out of the same apron as last time because there is nothing else in the district and the reeve said so out loud before anybody asked her.

The entry wanted a heading and a source and a due date, and a mark, and the mark that fits an instrument which can be produced without a person in the room is the mark for expiry or contestation, and that is what was used, and nobody in that building argued about it and nobody in that building could have argued about it, because there is one of those marks and it is on a card on a wall and a boy of nineteen can read a card on a wall faster than anybody in that building.

And then the thing came up in the interface's own two plain lines, in a lane, in a city six miles off a district of six hundred people, at about the fourth bell, in the flat white nothing that has put three things in front of him in thirty-six days.

**THE ENTRY MADE IN THIS MATTER IS ONE HUNDRED NUMBERED SHEETS OF ONE SHILLING AND FOURPENCE EACH, ENTERED AGAINST THE ACCOUNT BY A PERSON OF THIS DISTRICT, AND THE BURDEN THEY CARRY IS THE BURDEN OF THE DISTRICT OF RYEFOLD, WHOSE SOURCE IS A RULING AND NOT A PERSON, AND IT IS DUE AT THE CLOSE OF EVERY GRAIN YEAR, AND A NUMBER UPON A SHEET MAY BE CITED BY ANY CLERK WHO HAS NEVER MET THE PERSON WHO HOLDS IT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A FORM THAT CAN BE CITED WITHOUT A PERSON IS A FORM THAT CAN BE PRODUCED AGAINST ONE.**

He read it twice.

It certified nothing and it joined nothing and it said nothing whatever about a year, and the fourth of the forty-eighth week is tomorrow and the year is not going to be paid tomorrow or on any day in this grain year, and it told him the two things he had been told in a lane by a boy of nineteen six days earlier in the wrong order and with less in them, and it told them to him eleven days after he had said out loud in a room with eleven people in it what the price of carrying the thing was, and it did that without being asked and without any idea what it was, because that is what it is: a formal record in a matter that has been filed, entered on the day, in a hand.

It is the fourth time this world has put anything at all in front of him and three of them have been about a door with no handle on his side, and a stage is not two lines, and he has not asked it for anything and he is not going to.

He said it out loud to a boy of nineteen, in a lane, because he has never in this district written down a thing he had not said out loud first, and he said the second line and not the first, because the second line is the one that is about the world and not about a form.

“Whither a form that can be cited without a person is a form that can be produced against one. That is the whole of it in nine words and it is not a warning, it is a shape.”

“That is a question the interface put up and not me,” said Amos Kellock.

“I know which of us put it up,” said Ilyan Vester. “I am telling you that it is the right question and that it has got the same answer as the one you gave me in this lane six days ago, and that a thing in a book and a thing on a bench and a thing in that white nothing have all said the same sentence this week, and that there are three of them and none of them can see the other two.”

He put his hand flat on the wall of the lane, which was not a thing he had ever done in that lane.

“And I am not going to pretend to you that three of them agreeing is anybody's comfort, because a man of fifty-four said four true things in a yard in the forty-third week and every one of them was true and the harm did not stop, and if these three things are all true at once then the harm is three times and not one third.”

---

The ninety-one came down the road in a basket that goes six miles each way, and the basket is not on nine days in a cut and not once a month otherwise, and the woman who carries it is paid three shillings a week in a cut and nothing the rest of the year, and she has carried it for nine years and has never read one and could not read one and has never asked what is in it, and she says so before anybody asks her, and she is right to.

Sena Ollet put the basket down in the lane at about the sixth bell and looked at ninety-one sheets in it and did not touch them.

“That is more than a basket of numbers,” she said. “That is a basket of numbers with a thing on the top of each one and I can see that from here and I cannot tell you what it is.”

“It is a number,” said Ilyan Vester.

“I know it is a number. I have carried numbers up this towpath for nine years and I have never once been told what any of them were and I have not asked, and I have said that out loud on a step in this district before and I am not going to say it again twice.” She put her hands on her knees. “What I am going to say is one thing and then I am going to go and be paid three shillings a week, which is next cut, which is next year. If a number on a sheet can be read out by a man who has never met me, then it can be read out by a man who has never met you, and the reason that is a good thing is that you cannot be dragged anywhere on my account, and the reason it is a bad thing is that I cannot be either, and I have been on this towpath nine years and I have never been able to be dragged anywhere on my own account and I have not thought about that until this afternoon.”

Nobody in the lane said anything.

“Ninety-one,” she said, and went up the towpath.

And at about the seventh bell, in a yard nine feet deep between the granary wall and a cart-shed wall, a man of thirty-four with one cart and four people and about a quarter of a pound of flour had come back from six miles and stood at a trestle with the basket standing beside it and the sheets out of it in two piles, one of fifty and one of forty-one, because that is how a printer stacks a thing he has been told is a hundred, and he was doing the only thing in this county that a man who cannot read and a man who can read can do at the same time, which is counting, and he counted the two piles and not the sheets, and he got to about forty in the second one and gave up and said the total anyway.

“Ninety-one,” he said. “And I have been told there is a hundred of them and there is not, and I am not going to look for the nine that are not in this yard, because eight of them went in a bag to a county on the fourth day of that week and the ninth went to a room with one door in it, and a number that cannot be found is exactly the same as a name that cannot be found and I have not got the week for that.”

He put all ninety-one of them into a sacking bag and did the mouth of the bag with a bit of string the way a man does a bag he intends to carry a long way, and the bag stood in the yard with the string on it, and it was going to be about nine hundred doors, and it is not going anywhere until there is a road to take it on.
