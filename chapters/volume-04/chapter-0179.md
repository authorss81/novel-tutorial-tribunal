# Chapter 179: What Four Hundred Private Things Are

The question came from a carter and it was a small question, and it was asked at the second door on the second day of the forty-seventh week at about the third bell with the beam working behind him, and it is the last question anybody in Ryefold asked for a long time.

Marda Fen read the book to the man in the lane, the way she reads everything, and then the man in the lane stood in the second door and said what was in it, out loud, in the working voice, because a convenor of sixty-one cannot read his own hand and has never once pretended otherwise, and because the alternative is a man reading his own record in a doorway, and this district has not done that yet and is not going to.

“Can it be put back,” said the carter. “Any of it. The last four weeks.”

And Perrin Gant said, “No,” and then made himself say the whole of it, and it took him about twenty minutes, and about nine people stood in that doorway and got back to the weigh afterwards in the ordinary way, and nobody thanked him and nobody is going to, and this is what came out of the twenty minutes and it is the finding of four weeks and it is a working finding and it is not a moral.

> THE SECOND DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. A PAGE, FOUR SHEETS, SWORN BY FOUR PERSONS OF THIS DISTRICT IN THEIR OWN HAND, IN THEIR OWN ROOM, ON A DAY THEY APPOINTED, WITH A STRANGER’S NAME IN IT AS A PERSON AND NOT AS HANDS. CORRECT.
>
> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. A NOTICE, FOUR SHEETS, FIVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, PAID OUT OF AN ACCOUNT THAT HELD NINE POUNDS, CARRIED UP A TOWPATH BY A BOY OF THIRTEEN WHO WAS NOT TOLD WHAT WAS IN IT. CORRECT.
>
> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. THE CONVENOR SIGNING AN APPLICATION HE COULD NOT READ, AND SAYING SO OUT LOUD IN THAT ROOM BEFORE HE SIGNED IT, AND BEING ANSWERABLE FOR THE WHOLE OF IT FOUR MILES OFF ON ANOTHER PERSON’S WORD. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIFTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. A SHEET READ ALOUD IN A YARD TO FOUR PEOPLE WHO CANNOT READ IT, BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY-NINE, AFTER A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR SAID THE PRICE OF IT OUT LOUD BEFORE THE FACT. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIFTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. THE STATEMENT, BY THE CONVENOR, IN THE SAME YARD, THAT A RECORD IS A THING ABOUT A MAN WHETHER HE LIKES IT OR NOT, AND THAT THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT A RECORD IS. CORRECT.
>
> THE SIXTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. A CLAIM SATISFIED OUT OF THE MONEY IN THE ACCOUNT INSTEAD OF OUT OF THE GRAIN, ON THE BACK OF THE CLAIMANT’S OWN SHEET, READ OUT TWICE BEFORE IT WAS SIGNED, WITH A WHEELWRIGHT IN THE DOORWAY ASKING WHETHER IT WAS RIGHT. CORRECT.
>
> THE SIXTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. THE LAST NINE POUNDS OF THIS DISTRICT GIVEN AWAY TO A CARTER WHO IS OWED FIFTY-FOUR POUNDS TWO SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE DISTRICT AT NOTHING, AND FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX LOADS OF ITS OWN GRAIN LEFT IN A BIN. CORRECT.
>
> THE SEVENTH DAY OF THE FORTY-THIRD. A FORM OF DEPOSITION, ONE SHEET, ONE SHILLING AND FOURPENCE, TAKEN AT A COUNTER SIX MILES UP A ROAD GOING NORTH, WITH A NAME IN THE LAST BOX, IN A BOY’S HAND, OF A MAN WHO COULD NOT READ IT. CORRECT.
>
“That one is not correct,” said a carter at the back of that doorway, and about nine people turned round.

“It is not and I am not going to pretend it is and I am not going to take it out of the book either,” said Perrin Gant. “I wrote it down and I wrote the other two things next to it. Two children and no fault in either of them and a number that is gone, and I have been the convenor of this district for nineteen years and I did not know either of those things was in this lane until about the sixth day of the forty-third week, and if you want to know the whole of what I have learned in four weeks it is that and not the money. Go on.”

He went on, and here is the rest of it, and the second half of it is worse than the first.

> THE THIRD DAY OF THE FORTY-FOURTH. A NUMBER WIPED OFF A WAGE BOARD BY A GIRL OF SIXTEEN WHO WAS TOLD TO DO IT AND WAS NOT TOLD WHY, FOUR DAYS AFTER A BOY OF THIRTEEN HAD PUT IT THERE WITHOUT BEING ASKED. CORRECT, AND NOT CORRECT, AND IT IS NOT ANYBODY’S.
>
> THE FIRST DAY OF THE FORTY-FIFTH. A BOY OF NINETEEN ENTITLED TO TAKE DEPOSITIONS ENTERING A TRUE DOCUMENT INTO A MATTER FOUR MILES OFF, AFTER WALKING SIX MILES TO ASK ONE QUESTION, AND BEING THANKED BY NOBODY, AND NOT EXPECTING TO BE. CORRECT.
>
> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-FIFTH. TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY NAMES READ ALOUD IN A BUILDING WITH THE DOOR OPEN, IN NINE MINUTES OF READING AND FORTY-TWO OF WAITING, BY A WOMAN WHO HAD NEVER READ ONE OUT BEFORE, WITH A MAN IN THE DOOR WHO READ NOTHING. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIFTH DAY OF THE FORTY-FIFTH. A REEVE ANSWERING QUESTIONS FROM HER OWN QUAY FOR HALF AN HOUR IN FRONT OF ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY PEOPLE, OUT LOUD, IN FIGURES, HAVING DONE IT ONCE IN TWENTY-TWO YEARS. CORRECT.
>
> THE SIXTH DAY OF THE FORTY-FIFTH. THIRTY-FOUR NAMES IN THE BENCH’S OWN BOOK IN A MAN’S OWN HAND, SEVENTEEN THOUSAND LOADS OF CARRIAGE GIVEN UP IN NINE COUNTIES, AND THE FINDING THAT A PAGE CANNOT GO TO NETHERGILL ON ONE DAY AND STAND IN A LANE ON THE NEXT. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIRST DAY OF THE FORTY-SIXTH. A HUNDRED LOADS OFF A QUAY IN A DAY AND THREE QUARTERS BY ONE CART AND ONE CARTER WHO SAID THE PRICE OF IT OUT LOUD TWICE, A NAME ON THE BACK OF A WAGE BOARD IN CHALK, AND THE FACT THAT A CARTER’S DAY IS EIGHT POUNDS ELEVEN AND THE ONLY PRICE OF A DAY ANYBODY IN THAT DISTRICT WRITES DOWN IS NINEPENCE. CORRECT.
>
“Say that one again,” said the same carter.

“A notice going out on the day it was due, unpaid for, to four parties, and not to one carter in about nine hundred yards,” said Perrin Gant. “It is four sheets at a shilling and fourpence a sheet, which is five shillings and fourpence, and the district has had nothing since the sixth day of the forty-third week, and it went out anyway, because a notice goes out on the day it is due. I have been a convenor for nineteen years and I have never known what I was going to say to a room about that until this morning and I have now said it and it is the worst thing on the list and it is mine.”

> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-SIXTH. A NOTICE GOING OUT ON THE DAY IT WAS DUE, UNPAID FOR, TO FOUR PARTIES, AND NOT TO ONE CARTER IN ABOUT NINE HUNDRED YARDS. CORRECT.
>
> THE SIXTH DAY OF THE FORTY-SIXTH. A LETTER FROM A CLERK IN A CITY FOUR MILES OFF, ASKING A BENCH OF FOUR A QUESTION ABOUT THE FOURTH SHEET OF A PAGE THEY SWORE, WHICH IS NOT A QUESTION ANYBODY ASKED. CORRECT.
>
> THE SEVENTH DAY OF THE FORTY-SIXTH. THREE PEOPLE ASKED OUT LOUD IN A LANE IN NINE WORDS WHETHER THEY WOULD LIKE TO BE THE ONE STANDING THERE. TWO SAID YES. ONE SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE NO WAS CORRECT, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED AGAIN. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIRST DAY OF THE FORTY-SEVENTH. A CLAUSE READ ALOUD AT LAST BY THE ONLY PERSON WHO COULD READ IT, WHICH HAD BEEN FOLDED UP IN A BOOK FOR A YEAR, AND WHICH IS WRONG, AND FOUR LINES WRITTEN INSTEAD WITH A BLANK IN THE THIRD OF THEM ABOUT THE SIZE OF A HAND. CORRECT.

He read it with his finger on the page and he could not read a word of it and he had it in front of him because Osa Trin stood behind the chair with her hand on the back of it and read it to him in a low voice, twice, and did not correct one word, and was not thanked for it, and did the same thing at the fourth sitting with an application in her hand for about four hours.

“Every one of those is correct,” said Perrin Gant. “I have been through them twice and I have had the reading done and I am going to say the thing I have got to say and then I am going to go up that lane and shut a door.”

Nobody in that doorway said anything.

“Sum it,” he said. “Not the money. The other thing.”

“There is no other thing to sum,” said Nevin Hake. “There is not a sum of that.”

“There is,” said Perrin Gant. “Here it is, in one sentence, and I have had about nine days to get it down to one sentence, and it is the only sentence in nineteen years that I have wanted in that book more than the page, and I am going to say it in a doorway with nine people in it and I am sixty-one.”

He said it in the working voice, at about the fourth bell, with the beam working behind him.

“Every single thing anybody in this district did in the last four weeks was correct, and the sum of the correct things is a district that is a hundred loads short and cannot prove it.”

---

And then he took half of it back, in the same doorway, about four seconds later, and nobody in that doorway had ever seen a convenor do that.

“I have got to take half of that back and I have been a convenor for nineteen years and I have never taken half of a sentence back in this lane in my life.” He put his hand flat on the shut book. “The half I have to take back is the *is*. Not the hundred. The hundred is exactly right and the hundred is the whole of what I have got. A hundred loads at three shillings a load is fifteen pounds and this district had nothing in the account on the sixth day of the forty-third week and has had nothing since. On the first day of the forty-sixth week four carts took a hundred loads off that quay in one day, and it was correct, and it was the right thing done in the right way by four men who were not owed it and were not paid for it. And after it was done this district was not a hundred loads short. It was fifteen pounds short, which is the same money.”

“Then where is the hundred,” said Nevin Hake.

“The hundred is on paper,” said Perrin Gant, “and that is the whole of it and it is the finding and I have been nine days getting to it. There is nowhere in the world that records that this district was ever a hundred loads short. Not on the account, because the account holds a fund and a fund does not have a shortage in it. Not in this book, because a bench’s book is a bench’s book. Not on a wage board, because that is wiped every week, and the one that mattered was on the sixth day of the forty-third week in a hand of a boy of thirteen who could not read, in six chalked words, and it was wiped four days later by a girl of sixteen who was told to do it and was not told why, and neither of those children will ever be told by anybody who was in that building. The four carters are in a list in this book and the list says what they did that morning and not one penny, and if any clerk in this county asks this district what it did on the first day of the forty-sixth week, then the answer is a page, and a page is not a person, and the page is in a room.”

Nobody said one word, and a carter at the back said, “Then somebody is off.”

“Nobody is off,” said Perrin Gant. “That is the thing I have been trying to say for about nine days and I have not got it and I have got it now, and it is about nine seconds long and I am going to say it and then I am going to go up that lane.”

He said it at about the fourth bell, in the working voice, to nine people in a doorway.

“The thirty-four men who gave up a second cart gave up seventeen thousand loads of carriage in nine counties and not one load of it was in this one. Ormond Ketch is owed forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence to a year by a district that gave him its last nine pounds, and he is not a thief, and he is right, and he will be back. The nine hundred and forty forms are in a box somewhere that is not in this district. A woman of thirty-nine is going to be asked a question in about nine months about the fourth sheet of a page and there is no form anywhere for not answering it. A boy of nineteen walked six miles with a file in his hand to hand a man his own words and was thanked by nobody, and he is nineteen, and he is going to get better at it. And every one of those is correct, and every one of those is the right thing done in the ordinary way by somebody who was not thanked for it, and the sum of the correct things is that a district of six hundred people is fifteen pounds short in the hand and a hundred loads short on paper and cannot prove either, and has not been robbed by anybody.”

He shut the book and put the key in his pocket.

“Nobody in that sum is a villain and nobody in it is redeemed and nothing in it is undone,” he said. “I have been waiting nine days for somebody to come in here and undo one thing and nobody has and nobody was going to. A page is the only instrument I have ever been able to get anybody to use and a page cannot go to Nethergill on one day and stand in a lane on the next, and I said that in this room ten days ago, and I am saying it in a doorway, and it is still true, and it is going to be true on the first of the forty-eighth week.”

And then Marda Fen put the chalk in the box on the wall and picked up her own, which is what she does at the end of a day, and went back to the beam, because there are about four hundred carts in this district and about seven thousand loads in that building and one pair of hands, and a reeve answers a question about the weigh and then everybody gets back to the weigh.
