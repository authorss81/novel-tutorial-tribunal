# Chapter 173: A Matter That Has To Be Held Open

The room above the chandler’s shop has a fire in it in the forty-fourth week and not before, and on the second day of the forty-sixth week it was the second day of the cut and the room was empty except for four people who had not gone down to the quay, and they were there because the cut is a thing that happens at the end of a quay and not in a room, and the four of them had nothing to do for about four hours a day for seven days.

He had been up since the second bell and he had done it alone, and doing it alone had taken him until about the fourth bell, and it had not worked, and the reason it had not worked is the plainest thing in this world and he had known it for about six weeks.

He wrote it out on a page torn out of a penny exercise book, standing up, at the table, with the fire behind him, and it is worth setting the whole of it down because it is the only document this district has ever had from a person who is not of it, and because it failed.

> WHAT I AM ASKING FOR. That a matter be listed for a day in which the people who pay for it are in the room. That is the whole of it and I have written it out four ways and it is four words long every time.
>
> WHY THE DAY MATTERS. The fourth sitting was on the thirty-fifth day. The cut began on the forty-second. Those are seven days apart and they are both in the same grain year and there is no form in this county that will hold a matter open across either of them, and a matter that is listed for a day and taken on that day is a matter that is taken while four hundred carters in about nine hundred yards are in a cut.
>
> WHAT I CANNOT WRITE. The name of the person who applies for it. A person of the district applies. I am not a person of this district and there is no form anywhere in this county for a man of no district to be a party to a thing about a district, and I have checked that twice at a counter four miles off and the man at the counter said it in the ordinary way and he was right.

That is the whole of the page. It is six lines and one of them is a sum in a man’s own arithmetic and the rest of it is an explanation of why a sum is the answer, and no clerk in this world is going to be helped by it.

He read it back to himself twice, which is a thing he can do with a document he has written, and then he folded it in four and put it in his coat, and that is the sixth time in his life he has put a piece of paper in that coat that he wrote himself and cannot read.

---

The walk is four miles and it takes a man with a bag and a leg about an hour and a half, and he went at about the fifth bell with the cut still going behind him, and the lane off the court is four hundred yards long and there is nowhere in it to stand out of the rain, and there is no bus to the court.

Roald Tern was at the counter. He is twenty-six and he has been in that post two years and he has read the same eleven hundred forms about nine hundred times, and he is better at the counting than at the room, and he was at the same counter in a better coat, and he looked up, and it took him about two seconds to work out who was standing there, and he did not say anything about it.

“I am not here about a party that does not come,” said Ilyan Vester.

“That is a relief,” said Roald Tern, “and it is the second time you have told me which question you are not asking, and I have noticed it both times and I have not decided what to do about noticing it.”

He put his pen down, which he did not have to do, and which he had never done for anybody else in two years.

“Ask.”

“Who can open a matter.”

“A person of the district. That is on the sheet and I read it out to you in the second week of your being in this county and you wrote it down, and you have written it down again, and it is still the whole of it.” He turned the sheet round and did not push it, and did not read it, which was a thing he had learned to do about four minutes after the first time. “A person of the district. Not a district. Not a bench. Not an account. A person, with a name, who is in the district, and the name goes on the sheet and the name is answerable for it and cannot be got out of it afterwards by anybody including me.”

“A bench of four is four people.”

“A bench of four is four people and it is not one of them and there is no line on this sheet for it and I have had this counter for two years and I have never once entered one and I have not gone looking, and I want to be exact with you, because you are the only person in this county who has ever stood at this counter and not asked me for a date, and the exactness is: I have never gone looking because nobody has ever asked me whether a bench can be a party, and if you had asked me in your first week I would have told you the same thing, and you did not, and I do not know why.”

Nobody asked him why and he was not asked why, and there is a reason for that and it is the same reason there has not been anybody alive in this county who wanted to know what a clerk of twenty-six thought of a sentence he entered in a room four miles off six weeks ago, and it is not going to be anybody, and he did not say one word about the thirty-fifth day and he was not asked and he was not going to be.

---

Then he gave the thing that made the day, and he gave it in about four sentences, in the working voice, and he did not know when he had worked it out and he said so.

“Held open,” he said. “That is not a thing anybody asks for. Nothing is held open. A matter is listed for a day and the parties come on the day and the thing is done on the day, and if a party is not in the room on the day then it is not heard, and not being heard is the same as not coming, and that is the word on the wall and the word is right.”

“So a decision cannot be put off.”

“A decision cannot be put off by asking. No.” He turned the sheet round again. “But a matter is not a decision. A matter is a list of things and a day. And a list of things and a day is made by the person who carries it, and the person who carries it is a person of the district, and the person of the district chooses the day. Not the account. The person who applies chooses the day out of the days that are sitting days, and there are two sittings a year, and the next one of this kind is the first of the forty-eighth week, which is in thirteen days from today, and I have looked it up, and it is for other matters, and other matters can be added to it by a person.”

“So the day is the whole of it.”

“The day is the whole of it, and it is not a trick and it is not a thing anybody has ever used, and here is why, and it is the plainest thing in this world and it has been in front of me for two years.” He put his hand flat on the counter. “Nobody in this county has ever chosen a day for a matter. They take the day the sheet says. And the day the sheet says is a day in the middle of a cut, because the year runs so that a cut and a sitting are the same fortnight, and that is not anybody’s design, and I have never once in two years been asked why. And a person who chooses the first of the forty-eighth week for a matter about a cut has chosen the first week after the cut ends, and every carter in about nine hundred yards is free to walk into a room four miles off on that day, and the whole of what your district lost on the thirty-fifth day is that six hundred people were in a cut and the room was four miles away and the notice cost five shillings and fourpence to say they were still poor.”

He stopped, and it was the first time in two years he had said more than a date and a price at that counter to anybody, and he noticed himself doing it and did not take it back.

“Then the cost of that day,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Four sheets at a shilling and fourpence, on notice to every party, which is five shillings and fourpence, and you paid it once in the forty-third week and I have the slip, and it is five shillings and fourpence the second time.” He looked at the slip and then at the man. “And one person, all day, and the person is a person of the district and not you, and there is no form that makes you one. And after the notice has gone out the district is represented by that person, and if that person is not in the room on the day then the district is carried as uncooperative, again, and this time nobody in the world will be able to tell it from the last time, because it will be the same word in the same book about the same district.”

---

He walked back the four miles and the leg went on the last of them and he was his own again by the time he got to the top of the lane, and the room above the chandler’s shop had four people in it and the fire was burning down, and he put the six lines on the table face up, and four people in that room looked at six lines they could not read, which is what four people in that room have been doing since the sixth day of the forty-second week.

Then he said it out loud, in the ordinary way, standing at the end of the table, and it took him about a minute and a half, and there is no version of this in which anybody in that room was surprised, because the four of them have known since the sixth day of the forty-second week what a stranger with a bad leg was going to do about a thing a stranger could not read.

The six lines were: that he was not a person of the district; that there is no form for a man of no district to be a party; that a person of the district applies and chooses the day; that the first of the forty-eighth week is a sitting and is in thirteen days; that the cut ends on the last day of this week and every carter in about nine hundred yards could walk four miles on the first of the forty-eighth week; and that the cost was five shillings and fourpence for four sheets of notice and one person and one whole day, and the district has nothing, and it paid five shillings and fourpence once already in the forty-third week, so that the two notices come to ten shillings and eightpence.

Then he asked the bench whether it would be a party, and he asked it in the plainest words he had, and he had asked four people to be the one standing there in a yard once already and had got a price out of it in about nine seconds, and this was not that, and he said so before anybody could mistake it.

Perrin Gant said no, and he said it fast, and it was the right no, and it was the right no for a reason that had nothing to do with the case, which is the only kind there is.

“No,” said Perrin Gant. “And I am going to give you the reason and I would like the room to have it before anybody argues with me, because I have been convenor nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years put a thing on that table that I did not know was true.”

“You cannot read it.”

“I cannot read it,” said Perrin Gant. “And that is not the reason. I could get somebody to read it to me in about four minutes and I have got somebody who reads and I have been asking her to read things to me for nineteen years, and if the reason were the reading I would have had it read and signed it this morning and slept tonight.”

He put his hand flat on the bench’s own book, which was shut.

“The reason is the twenty-fourth day of the forty-third week,” he said. “I signed an application in this room that I could not read, and I said out loud in this room that I was going to be answerable for the whole of it in a room four miles off to a man I had never met, on her word, and I have not slept properly about it since, and I did it because nobody else would, and eight days later a boy of nineteen read a piece of paper out in a room and a district of six hundred people had its year set aside on the word *decided*, and it was not the word that did it. It was the signatory. It was a man who could not read signing in a room with nobody in it who could.”

He took his hand off the book.

“So if I sign this one I am doing the same thing twice, and the second time I will have known exactly what the first one cost, and there is a bench of four in this district and three of us cannot read and one of us can, and the one that can is going to be the one that signs it whether I like it or not, because she is the only person in this district who can read a form, and that has been true for nineteen years and it is the only thing about this room that I am ashamed of.”

Wil Tamworth said yes, and he said it before Gant had finished, and it was the wrong yes, and he was thirty-three and he had wanted for four years to be given something to do that mattered.

“Yes,” said Wil Tamworth. “And I want it noticed that I have said yes and that my reason is not a reason. My reason is that I have been the fourth of four in this room for two years and I have asked four times for something to do that mattered and I have been given notices, and a notice about the lease, and a page that is for being legible, and a list of thirty-four names in a good coat’s handwriting, and I would like to be able to say that I did one thing that mattered before I am old. I have not got a better reason and I have looked for one since the second bell and there is not one, and I would rather say that in a room than have somebody find it out in a year.”

Nobody said anything to him about it, which is a kindness, and he did not want one.

And Nevin Hake, who was forty-six and had been in that bench eleven years and who said out loud in this room in the forty-first week that he could not read a form and had never said so, said the thing that made the afternoon into something, and he said it in a carter’s mouth, and he said it to Gant and not to the stranger.

“You are both wrong about the same thing and you have been wrong about it in opposite directions for an hour,” he said. “The bench is not a party. Nobody said the bench was a party. Gant, listen to what the man said. A person applies. A person is represented. Four sheets on notice to every party. That is the whole of the sheet and you had it read out to you in this room in the forty-third week and Osa read it out twice with her thumb in two places. Four people is four persons. One of us makes it. One of us stands in the room on the day. The other three are not the district and are not asked to be. They are witnesses, and we have got four witnesses and we swore them on a page on the twenty-second day and it went on a nail and the Chancellor of this account stood in a yard and said what it was for.”

He put his own hands flat on the table, which are a carter’s hands and are forty-six years old.

“So it is not the bench,” he said. “It is one of us. And it is not a thing four of us can agree to. It is a thing one of us has to walk four miles with, alone, on a day in thirteen days, and be asked questions about six hundred people, and be responsible for every word of it. And three of the four of us cannot read what is being applied for. So we have got one name and not four, and we have had one name since the forty-third week and we have all four known it and not one of us has said it in this room in about a month, and I am saying it now because a carter from a yard two streets off that quay said a price out loud in front of nine people and got nine people to listen, and that is the whole of what a person in this district can do and he is thirty-four.”

And then Perrin Gant stood up, and he is sixty-one and he stood up the way he does, and he did not ask anybody anything, and he did not look at the stranger, and he said a woman’s name out loud in a room, which is the thing a bench that has decided something does.

“Osa Trin,” he said.

Nobody answered him.

“Sitting is the first of the forty-eighth week. That is in thirteen days. Notice goes out eleven days before, which is the fourth day of this week and is two days from now, and it is four sheets at a shilling and fourpence, which is five shillings and fourpence, and we paid that once in the forty-third week and it is five shillings and fourpence again, and the two of them are ten shillings and eightpence, and there is nothing in the account and there has not been since the sixth day of the forty-third week, and I am not asking that question this morning because I have not got the answer to it and I am not going to invent one in front of a stranger.”

He sat down.

“That is the name and that is the day and that is what it costs,” he said. “And nobody in this room has asked her, and I have not asked her, and you have not asked her, and the reason none of us has asked her is that she has been the person who reads things in this district since she was nineteen years old and nobody in four hundred years has thought to put a question to her instead of a document.”

It was not a ruling. Nothing was decided. It was a name, and a day, and ten shillings and eightpence, and a woman of thirty-nine who was not in the room, and a bench of four which had been asked whether it would be a party and had answered no and yes and not yet, and a form that one sheet of it and a shilling and fourpence has to be written on by somebody who can read, and there is one person in that district who can.
