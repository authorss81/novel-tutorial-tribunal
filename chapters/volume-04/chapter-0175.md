# Chapter 175: The Five Hundred And Thirty

He had been carrying it since the twenty-second day of the forty-third week and it is about a quarter of a pound of paper, which is nothing, and it went up six miles in somebody else’s pocket on the same morning, and a man of fifty-four had stood in a yard and asked him a fair question at the gate, and he had said the one word that was in him, and the one word was *nothing*.

Five hundred and thirty.

It is a number in a district of six hundred people and it is made of about nine hundred of them — nine hundred district clerks in this county — who were sent one sheet by post in the thirty-first week of last year, in the middle of a working week, with no threat in it and no offer in it, and who did their jobs.

Nobody in Ryefold had done anything about it in twenty days, and the person who had said *nothing* at a gate had said it in the ordinary way and had not been asked for anything else since, and on the fifth day of the forty-sixth week he tried.

---

He tried in the wrong order first, which is his habit and which is now on the record in four hundred and sixty-three forms in a district eleven miles off a bridge.

He went down to the second door at about the second bell with a page torn out of the exercise book and asked for the room above the chandler’s shop at the fourth bell, and Marda Fen said that the room was hers on the days she swept the floor out, which is most of them, and that a man who wanted to say a thing in it should come and say it, and that she was not going to carry a page up a lane for him.

That was fair. He took it.

Then he stood in the lane for about half an hour and tried to write the second application, and this is the whole of what he got, and it is four lines and one of them is a figure:

> WHAT I AM ASKING FOR. That the account be required to enter, for every sheet it has issued, the name of the person it was issued to.

And then nothing, and the nothing was not writer’s block and it was not fear and it was four hundred words that did not come.

The reason, and he wrote the reason down because he writes the reasons down and does not write the things, is this. A man who is carrying a thing cannot open a second one. It is not a rule and it is not a wall and there is no form about it anywhere in this county, and it is not a thing that can be argued with, because there is nobody on the other side of it. It is a pair of hands. He had been carrying the matter for the first of the forty-eighth week since the second day of this week and it was in his coat and it was six lines long and it was going to need a person in a room on a day in thirteen days, and while it was in his hands there was room for one thing and not for two, and he had spent the half hour in that lane trying to make room and there was no room, and the reason there was no room is that the reason there is no room is that his hands are full, and that is the whole finding and it cost him half an hour and it will cost this district a great deal more and it has not been paid.

---

The room above the chandler’s shop had a fire in it and about nine people in it at the fourth bell on the fifth day of the forty-sixth week, and there were four of the bench, and there was a reeve who had come up because it is her room, and there was a man of thirty-eight in a good coat who had said in a yard that he would be in this district until the cut and who was in it and who was the only person in the room who had ever said the price of his own trade out loud.

He said it standing up, at the end of the table, in about eleven seconds, and he did not sit down afterwards, and there is a version of this in which he says it to make a clerk comfortable and there is not one in which he says it for any reason at all.

“A man who puts nothing on a sheet and sends it back has not refused anything,” he said. “And there is no column anywhere in this county for a man who did the work properly.”

Nine people in that room did not move, and one of them wrote it down, which is the thing that happened and it is worth saying exactly what happened: nobody asked him what the category was, and nobody said that it was a rule, and nobody entered it anywhere.

“What is the category,” said Perrin Gant.

“It is not a category. That is the whole of what I am saying and you have just asked me the question everybody asks.” He put his hands flat on the table. “It is a set of about nine hundred people who were each sent one sheet in a working week and who each did the work in front of them and each put nothing on it and sent it back, and not one of them has a name in this district and not one of them has ever been asked, and there is no column for them anywhere, and the count of them is a figure that this district has been carrying for about three weeks and I would like to be able to tell you the figure and I have to do arithmetic first and I am not good at it in a room.”

Perrin Gant turned his hand over and gave him the chalk and Gant did the sum himself on the back of his own hand, slowly, twice, and let him look at it, which is a thing that has happened in that room before and about which nobody has ever said a word.

> NINE HUNDRED SENT. THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY CAME BACK WITH A NAME ON THEM. FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY CAME BACK WITH NOTHING ON THEM OR DID NOT COME BACK. OF THOSE, ABOUT THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY CAME BACK WITH NOTHING ON THEM, AND ABOUT ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY DID NOT COME BACK AT ALL.

“So a hundred and sixty went into a drawer,” said Ilger Rusk, from about six feet off, in the good coat.

“One hundred and sixty is the honest figure for the ones that never came back at all,” said Ilyan Vester. “And it is not the figure this district has been carrying, and the figure this district has been carrying is four hundred and thirty, and that figure is in a book in a room in this city and it was said out loud in a yard on the twenty-second day of the forty-third week by a man who did not lie to me once in about eleven minutes, and I am not going to say a word about that man this morning, because I have not checked it against anything but his memory and a yard is a bad place in which to do arithmetic.”

“It changes nothing,” said Rusk.

“It changes nothing at all,” he said. “And that is the part I have not been able to get past since the twenty-second day of the forty-third week. Three hundred and seventy or five hundred and thirty, blank or in a drawer, one hundred and sixty or four hundred and thirty, they are all the same people and they all did the work. The number is wrong by a hundred and seventy clerks and the wrongness is in a figure and it does not have an author this morning, and the harm is identical in every one of the four figures, and if I had come here this morning with the right number I would have had exactly the same nine minutes and exactly nothing.”

---

Then Osa Trin said the thing that nobody in that room wanted said, and she said it because she is the only person in that district who can read a list and has been asked what any of it is for exactly once in six weeks.

“Then say what you want done about it,” she said, “and not what it is.”

He said what he wanted done about it. It took him about four minutes and it is the plainest thing he has said in a district of six hundred people, and it is a sentence about a person’s feet.

“Nine hundred doors,” he said. “One at a time. Somebody who can read, saying to a man with a cart: here is the sheet, here is the word at the top of it, here is what it does and here is what it does not do, and you may send it back and you have not refused anything, and here is where the register is and it is not in this district. That is all. It is not a form and it is not a ruling and it is not a page. It is a person going to nine hundred doors and reading one sheet out loud nine hundred times.”

“Whose feet,” said Osa Trin.

“That is what I have been trying to work out since the second bell and I have not worked it out, and you have asked me the question in nine words and I have been in this district forty-six days and nobody has ever asked me a question in nine words before, including you until the fourth day of the forty-fifth week.”

“You have,” said Osa Trin. “You have had me in a doorway since the first day of this week and you have had me in a list for about a week and you have worked out that I am the only person here who can read one. And you have had four minutes just now to work out that if the person who goes to nine hundred doors is me then I am a person in the matter, and you are carrying a matter.”

He did not answer that for about nine seconds and he has not been able to answer it since.

---

And then Perrin Gant did the arithmetic, because the arithmetic was his and it was not a finding and it was not a moral, and he did it on the back of his own hand below the other sum, and he said it out loud as he went, which is what he does.

“Nine hundred doors,” he said. “At about nine a day, which a carter in a lane of this size gave me in a yard two days ago without being asked and who was right, that is a hundred days. And a hundred days of this district’s only written price for a day is ninepence, and a hundred days is three pounds fifteen. That is the wage and we have not got it.”

“And the rest of it,” said Marda Fen.

“The rest of it is a year and a cart,” said Perrin Gant. “A hundred days cannot be done by a man with a cart, because the hundred days are inside a cut, and a man with a cart in this district moves about four hundred loads in a cut, and four hundred loads at the register’s own three shillings is sixty pounds, and that is his year. So the price of nine hundred doors is one man’s year, or one man’s cart, and it is not both, and about nine hundred yards of this district own the cart, and not one of them is going to give it up for a form with a word at the top of it, because they have not been asked and they do not know there is a room.”

Nobody said anything, and the fire went on, and the wind was doing something to the shutter on the lane side, and about nine people were in a room and the finding was that the remedy for a category is not a better form and is not a ruling and is a person, and the price of the person is a year and a cart and three pounds fifteen, and the district is at nothing.

“Write it down,” said Ilger Rusk, from about six feet off. “I have a good coat and I have four years of wanting a list and you have just described the only thing in this world that produces a list and it is not expensive, it is a person, and I would put the price of it on the back of that reeve’s hand and send it to four hundred addresses tomorrow and it would be the only honest letter anybody in this county has sent in nine years. Three pounds fifteen, a year, a cart. That is a thing a man can decide about.”

“No,” said Osa Trin.

He turned round.

“I am not going to refuse it because it is expensive and I am not going to refuse it because it is my feet,” she said. “I am refusing it because there is nobody to refuse it with, and because on the first day of this week I read two hundred and forty names out loud in a building with the door open, in front of about two hundred and forty people, for the first time in nineteen years, and about nine of them watched a man who cannot read standing in that door and worked out why, and I have not worked out yet this morning whether I am going to be able to stand up after it, and I am not going to have nine hundred doors in front of it.”

Nobody argued with her, and about nine people in that room agreed with her, and one man of thirty-eight in a good coat agreed with her and did not say so, and wrote four figures on the back of a form and put the form in his bag.

At about the sixth bell Marda Fen went back down the lane to the granary, because there are about four hundred carts to weigh in a cut and about four hundred loads had gone over the beam that day and there were about a thousand in the bin that wanted to go out, and she has four hundred carts and one pair of hands and the whole of what she said in that room was about a quarter of a pound of paper and the price of nine hundred doors.
