# Chapter 174: What He Cannot Do

The notice went out on the fourth day of the forty-sixth week, which was the day it was supposed to go out, eleven days before a sitting in a city four miles off Ryefold. It is four sheets. It is served on every party. The parties are the account, the chamber, and two other bodies whose names are on a slip in a city and not in this district, and the boy who carries notices up the towpath carried it to the top of the lane and to a cart shed behind a granary and to a yard in Nethergill, and the cost of it is one shilling and fourpence a sheet, which is five shillings and fourpence, and the district has had nothing in the account since the sixth day of the forty-third week.

It went out anyway. That is worth one line and no more: a notice goes out on the day it is supposed to go out, and nobody in this county has ever in four hundred years stopped one because there is no money, and the people who stop notices are not the people the notices are for.

And it went to four parties, and not one of the three hundred and thirty-nine.

---

The sentence he had learned in a yard on the twenty-second day of the forty-third week and had written down in a book that night in a hand he could not read, with the date, was the whole of what the matter wanted, and he said it out loud three times in one afternoon in three doorways, in the ordinary way, standing up, to three people, because he has learned that in this district in about six weeks and it is the only thing he has learned that is any use.

The sentence is this: that the account be made to show, in a room, on a day, the register’s own entries for this district, being the loads and the rate and the names of the holders of sheets, and that the people who bought the sheets be in the room when it is shown.

He said it at about the second bell in a yard two streets off the quay, to a man of thirty-four with a hub on his knee.

“That is a thing with about nine pieces in it,” said Iven Brack, “and four of the nine are you asking a man in a good coat for something he has already told you he cannot do, and I want to take them in order.”

“Go on.”

“One. The loads and the rate.” The hub turned a quarter and stopped. “You have got them. The loads and the rate are eleven thousand loads in a granary and three thousand four hundred of them going out in this cut, and the rate is three shillings a load because that is what the register says a load costs to move, and a hundred loads short is fifteen pounds and I moved a hundred loads off your quay on the first day of this cut for three shillings that nobody has got. That is one of the nine and you have had it since a woman said it out loud on that quay in front of two hundred and forty people and you were in the crowd.”

“Two.”

“Two is the names, and the names are the whole of it, and there are not any.” He put the hub down. “Three hundred and thirty-nine people in this county have one cart and bought a sheet. Three hundred and thirty-nine. I have worked that out from a good coat’s arithmetic and I believe it and I cannot check it. I have been one of them for two years. I do not know the name of one of the other three hundred and thirty-eight, I have never met one of them, and if you put me in a room with that register open in front of me I would not be able to tell you whether a man in it was me.”

“Three.”

“Three is the day, and the day is yours, and a day is the only one of the nine you have got.” He picked the hub up again. “Four is the room, and a room is a room, and everybody who has ever wanted anything in this county has wanted a room. Five is the showing, and the account will show what it likes, and what it liked in the last four weeks was a line in a book with a word in it, and nobody made it show that. Six, seven and eight are the account, the chamber, the register and the form, and there is no form, and I have been a man in this county six weeks and I have looked, and there is no form, and that is not an insult to anybody. It is nine years of nobody in a district of six hundred people wanting to put anything in a room.”

He looked at the gate.

“Nine is the one that is the problem,” said Iven Brack. “And you have not asked me about nine, and you have not asked me about any of them, and I have worked out nine on my own in a yard in about four minutes, which means nine is not a thing that is hard. Nine is: four hundred men in about nine hundred yards, and every one of them did their job, and not one of them knows there is a room. And you cannot show a man a room. You can only show a man his own door, one at a time, and there are nine hundred of those, and it takes a day to do about nine of them in a lane this size, and that is a hundred days, and there is no one hundredth person in this district to do it.”

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then Iven Vester said the thing that made the afternoon, and he said it out loud, and he said it in a yard to a man with a hub in his hands, and it is worth setting down because it is the finding and it is not a moral and it is arithmetic.

“Then I have got the answer and it took a carter four minutes to give it to me and I have been carrying it about since the second bell,” he said. “The only thing anybody in this district can reach is a person. Not a body, not a book, not a number, not a room, not a form. A person, at a door, one at a time, about nine a day, a hundred days, and there is not one hundredth person.”

“No,” said Iven Brack. “And you will not find one, and I will tell you why, and it is not a complaint and I have thought about it for about an hour and a half. Every man in those nine hundred yards has a cart. A cart is the whole of what he has got and it is worth about four hundred loads in a cut and about sixty pounds, and he is going to be out on it from the first bell to the ninth and he is going to be under it, in the weather, on somebody else’s road, and he is going to come home at the ninth bell and eat what his wife has got and he is going to do that for nine weeks. And you are asking him to stop and listen to a stranger for a minute about a form. And I would do it. I would stop. I stopped for a man in a lane on the sixth day of the forty-second week and it cost me a day and I am still thinking about it. And there are four hundred of me and one of you, and that is the arithmetic and it is the only arithmetic.”

---

The second door of the granary, at about the fourth bell, and a reeve with a chalk in her hand and about nine people behind her, and the same sentence said again standing up.

“Two hundred and forty names,” said Marda Fen. “I have two hundred and forty. One hand. Nine years. And there is a second list in a drawer that is nineteen years old and it is the hundred and one who did not come, and neither of those is yours and I want that said before you ask me for it.”

“I was not going to ask you for it.”

“You were going to ask me for it in about four minutes,” said Marda Fen, “and the reason you were is that you have been in this district forty-five days and you have found the one person in it who can read a list and the one person in it who keeps them, and those are two different people, and one of them is me and one of them is in a room above a chandler’s shop and neither of them has been asked anything by anybody in nine years, and I am the one you are standing in front of, so it is me you will ask.”

She put the chalk in the box.

“Here is what is in this district and it is all of it. Two hundred and forty names, and about four hundred carters in about nine hundred yards, and a form with a word at the top of it that has been printed four thousand times and about three thousand and sixty of them taken up and nine hundred and forty of them in a box somewhere that is not in this district, and I have never wanted that box until the eleventh day of the forty-fifth week, and I have not been able to find out where it is, and there is one man in this county who could have handed a hundred of them out at a counter to any person who walked in, and he is six miles off and he sweeps and he is there two days out of seven and nobody knows which two.”

“What is his name.”

“Corrin Vask,” said Marda Fen. “It is on a page in a book in a room above a chandler’s shop and four of us know it and not one of us can get a message to him in a day, and I have been a reeve in this granary for twenty-two years and I cannot get a message to him in a day, and I did not know that was the case until a man in a good coat stood in this doorway and said his name out loud in front of nine people.”

---

And then six miles up the road that goes north out of Ryefold, on the same afternoon, at about the seventh bell, at a counter on the ground floor of a counting-house with a window, where a boy of nineteen was counting a tray and put it down when he saw who it was, and did not say anything about it, and went and got a file.

Amos Kellock is entitled to take depositions and has been for eleven months, and he is not entitled to anything else, and he said so before anybody asked him anything, which is the ordinary thing, and it is a thing that nobody has ever been allowed to improve on in this county, and the reason is about four hundred years old and is in nobody’s book.

“Three of the four I can do,” he said. “I will do them now, in order, and you did not ask and I am going to do them anyway because you are going to find out in about nine days and I would rather you found out in about nine seconds.”

“The loads and the rate.”

“We hold a book. Every district, every load, every claim, at the county’s entered rate, which is two shillings and eightpence a load and it is the county’s rate and not the register’s and they are two different numbers and both of them are right.” He turned the book round and did not open it, which is a thing he does. “We will produce it. We are a party. A party produces, on the day, whatever a person of the district asks for, and we will produce it in a room, and it will be four sheets at fourpence a sheet to copy and sixteen pence, and the district has nothing, so that is the first sixteen pence of a thing that costs five shillings and fourpence to start.”

“The names of the holders of sheets.”

“No.” He said it flatly and he did not soften it, and he had had this answer ready since about the second bell of the day before. “The register is not in this building. The register is kept by one man in this city and he keeps it himself and there is no clerk on it and there is no form for asking to see it and there is nobody to ask. That is not a rule anybody made. There is one man and a book and if he dies it goes to a successor who has never been in a yard, and he said that out loud in a yard to you on the twenty-second day of the forty-third week and he said it because you asked him and he has not stopped saying it since, and I have read about nine thousand returns in this tray and I have never in two years been asked for the register by anybody and I would not have known how to say no until about nine seconds ago.”

“The carrying.”

“The carrying is a line in a book about a party,” said Amos Kellock. “It is not a line in a book about the district. That is the whole of what a carrying is and it took a man of nineteen to work it out on the fourth sitting and he was in the room and I was in the room and nobody has un-said it since, and I have looked for a way to say the same thing in fewer words for nine days and there is not one.”

He put the tray back and squared it with two fingers.

“And the fourth thing you have not asked me about, which is the only one of the four that is any good to you and which I am going to give you for nothing, and I want it written down that I gave it for nothing.” He looked up. “You can have the loads and the rate, in a room, on a day, in four sheets, at sixteen pence. You cannot have the names, because a person is not printed. There is no column anywhere in this county that will hold the name of a man who bought a sheet and put it in a queue that has not happened, and there is no form that will make one, and the reason is not that anybody is protecting him. The reason is that a name on a sheet is a holder and a holder is a thing a register carries, and a register carries things and not people, and the whole of your district’s trouble is that a register is about eleven thousand loads and three shillings a load and a clerk’s fee of one shilling and fourpence a sheet, and there is nowhere in it for four hundred men, and that is not a conspiracy. It is a register.”

He picked the tray up again.

“Now go away, because the post comes on this day and it is about nine hundred of them in a sack and I am the one who opens the sack, and nobody in this building reads them as a set of anything, and I am not going to start.”

---

And the finding is not a finding about anybody, and it is the whole of what six weeks has come to, and it is arithmetic.

The thing that has been doing this to about nine hundred yards for two years is not a man. It is a number: three hundred and thirty-nine people in this county who have one cart each and bought a sheet, and thirty-one who have more, and about nine hundred district clerks who were asked a question by post and did their jobs. A number cannot be written to. A number cannot be asked whether it meant it. A number has to be got at through a person, and a person can only be got at at one door, and there are about nine hundred doors, and this district is about six hundred people and about four hundred carters, and there is no office in it and there has not been one for nine years, and the account is six miles up a road that goes north and the register is in a city that is not this district and is not kept in it, and the only line anybody in this district has about where the register is, is a sentence in a bench’s own book in a convenor’s hand that a man said in a yard and nobody wrote down anywhere else in the world:

> THE REGISTER IS KEPT IN THIS CITY AND IS NOT KEPT IN YOUR DISTRICT.

That is the fourth line of a circular of one sheet. Four went out for every one that came back, and the district has the sentence and not the paper, and the paper is in a bag in a county, because a factor of about fifty picked it up off a table in a yard, folded it once, put it in a bag, and did not read it, and asking him for it would be a production, and this district does not produce people, and that is the one thing it has done right for four hundred years.

And the thing he wanted, at the seventh bell, on the fourth day of the forty-sixth week, with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles behind him, was a person.

He did not have one, and he had not had one since the sixth day of the forty-second week, when a woman with a slate on a three-legged frame had told him there is not a third thing that is not carrying.
