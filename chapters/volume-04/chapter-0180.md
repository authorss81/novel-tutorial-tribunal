# Chapter 180: The First Of The Forty-Eighth Week Is In Six Days

Nothing can be put in front of the first of the forty-eighth week, and everybody in that room knew it by about the fifth bell on the second day of the forty-seventh week, and it is worth listing what is in the way, because the list is short and it is the whole of what a district of six hundred people has got.

The cut is over. It ended at the ninth bell two nights ago on the seventh day of the forty-sixth week, and about three thousand four hundred loads of that district’s grain went out of that granary in seven days, and three thousand of them went on carts that somebody else owned, and the account is nine thousand seven hundred pounds and its charge of four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year is due at the close of every grain year and does not stop, and the account holds nothing, and it has held nothing since the sixth day of the forty-third week.

The lease is undecided, and it is worse than undecided, and nobody in that room said so out loud until about the fourth bell. The tenancy ended on the first day of the forty-second week. The renewal is at the discretion of the account. There is no line on that sheet saying how long a discretion runs, and Osa Trin read that sheet four times in the forty-third week and found nothing, and the account held that granary in its pocket until the cut was over because after the cut it knows exactly what is in the granary and before the cut it does not, and she said at the time that she would have done the same and had thought about it for about a minute. The discretion ran to the cut. The cut ended two nights ago. There is nothing on the nail about it, and the nail has got three things on it, and it is a nail.

And the carrying is on the account’s own record, in a yard six miles up a road that goes north, in a file that is not in a locked room, and nobody has asked for it to be taken off, and there is no form for a person named in a deposition to ask for a thing to be out, and a forbearance may be applied for once a year and this was the year.

And the first of the forty-eighth week is in six days, and there is in that room a form that is one sheet and a shilling and fourpence, and a woman of thirty-nine who can fill it in, and a matter that wants a person in a room four miles off, and the fourth of those four is the sitting itself, and the sitting is six days away, and the reason there is nothing to put in front of it is not the money and not the lease and not the carrying. She has not been asked, and he is not allowed to ask, and he has known that since the seventh day of the forty-sixth week.

He said none of that out loud. He was in the room and he carried a basket he was not going anywhere with, because a basket is the only thing in that lane that puts his name in a place where anybody can find it, and the wage line is read before it is written and is written at the seventh bell.

---

What happened instead happened at about the fifth bell and it was not a remedy and it was not a victory and nobody asked for it.

Osa Trin put her sleeve back down, which she does about nine times a day, and said, “I am going up the road.”

“To the account,” said Perrin Gant.

“To the account. Six miles. There and back and a counter in between, and I have to be there before the second bell or the man who opens the post is not there and the next one is not there either, because I have watched him do it and I have watched him not do it and he does not care which, and I have not asked him to care and I am not going to start this week.” She was already moving. “It will take the day. I have told Marda and she is paying the sheet.”

“Which sheet,” said Nevin Hake.

“The one it is on,” said Osa Trin.

And nobody in that room asked her what it was, and nobody asked her, and it is worth being exact about that, because in about four hundred years in this district there has never been a thing done in that room that nine people in it did not ask about, and the whole of what has happened since the second day of the fortieth week is that this district has been getting worse at asking and better at not asking and has reached, on the second day of the forty-seventh week, a point where a woman of thirty-nine gets up in a room and says she is going six miles and nobody asks.

---

The finding, which is the bridge out of this and into the next ten chapters, was said by the man in the basket, in the lane, at about the sixth bell, to a woman of thirty-nine who was walking six miles up a road with sixteen pence that was not hers, and neither of them wanted it and it is the only true thing anybody in this district has said out loud since a woman of about sixty-one stood on a quay and said it and went home and whose name nobody ever found out.

Then what was it for,” he said. “I have been in this district fifty days and I have watched every single thing anybody in it has done and I could not tell you one single one of them that was wrong, and I have been carrying a matter for seven days and it has cost me nothing and bought nothing, and I have not had a part in this and I am not going to have one and I would like to know what the difference is between the two.”

She did not stop walking, and she did not slow down, and she said it to the road.

“The difference is the length of it,” said Osa Trin. “A harm that is one person long is a thing a person can fix. You go six miles, you pay a shilling and fourpence, you write one name on one sheet in a second hand, and it is done, and it is legal, and nobody has to be asked twice, and it takes a day and the day is yours. I have done about nine years of that in pencil on the back of a reeve’s sheet and I never once found out that it was supposed to be done in a hand anybody else could check.”

“And the other kind.”

“The other kind is a page,” said Osa Trin, “and a page cannot undo anything. A page is the only instrument there is for a harm that is four hundred people long and there is not one other, and that is not a complaint about the page, that is a fact about the length. A page can be made in a day by a bench of four. A page can be read by a stranger four miles off in about a month. And it cannot go to Nethergill on one day and stand in a lane on the next, and it cannot stand in a door, and it cannot be argued with, and it cannot be un-sworn, and it is the only thing in this world that can hold four hundred private things and it holds them the way a net holds fish, which is to say it holds them and they are still in the water.”

She got to the end of the lane and turned up the road that goes north, and she walked away from something, and nobody watched her do it except the man with the basket, and he has not been able to put it down since.

“One more thing,” she said, over her shoulder. “About nine months, and it is the office’s own nine months and it has nothing to do with any other, and if they do not send us a letter then it is for ever, and I have wanted a column for a form that was sent and nothing came back of it for about nine years and I am not going to get it this week. And I am walking six miles up a road with a form in my hand and a sheet I did not pay for, and I would like the man who has carried a basket for fifty days without being asked what it is for to notice that the two of us are the same arrangement and one of us has a form and one of us does not.”

---

At about the seventh bell, Perrin Gant came down the lane and read the board, because he does, and a reeve read it before she wrote it and did not say anything about it and did not correct it, and it is nine pence and it is the same nine pence that has been on that wall since the fourth day of the forty-first week, and nobody in that lane has any idea why a man who cannot read has a line on a wage board.

“How long before anybody in this district is told what she did with it,” said Wil Tamworth, who is thirty-three and asks what a number means.

“About nine months,” said Perrin Gant. “If the office answers at all. That is how long a letter takes to that office and it is their own nine months and it is a different nine months from the one a man of fifty-four said in a yard in the forty-third week, and there are two nines in that book now and I am sixty-one and I do not like it and I have written both of them down with the dates.”

“Nobody in this district can read the list it is on except two people,” said Marda Fen, from the second door. “It is my sheet, and it is hers now, and that is the two of us. And neither of us is going to know what the account did with it until somebody sends us a letter, and if they send us a letter in nine months then it is nine months, and if they do not send us a letter then it is for ever, and there is no column anywhere in this county for a form that was sent and nothing came back of it, and I have been in this granary twenty-two years and I have wanted that column for about nine years and I am not going to get it this week.”

Then she shut the second door, at the seventh bell, having read the board before she shut it, which is what she does.

At about the ninth bell he was in the cart shed behind the granary with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and he wrote on the fourteenth page of a penny exercise book, and the two lines at the end are the two lines that are on the other thirteen.

*Fifty days on this shelf. I have carried one thing for seven days and it has cost me nothing and bought nothing, and on the second day of this week a woman of thirty-nine walked six miles up a road with a form and sixteen pence that was not hers, and nobody asked her what it was for, and I was not asked and was not allowed to ask, and that is the whole of the difference between carrying a thing and doing one.*

*The lease discretion ran to the cut. The cut ended two nights ago. There is nothing on the nail about it and the nail has three things on it and it is a nail.*

*A harm that is one person long is a day, and a shilling and fourpence, and a second hand. A harm that is four hundred people long is a page, and a page cannot undo it, and there is not one other instrument in the world, and I have not found one, and a boy of nineteen has been inside eleven buildings since the fortieth week and there is no form anywhere that will take a line about a person out of a book.*

*It is mine to write down and it is not mine to decide.*

And the book on the table in the room above the chandler’s shop was shut on a page with four lines on it and a blank in the third of them about the size of a hand, and the first of the forty-eighth week is in six days, and in this district there is one person who can read and at that hour she is six miles up a road, and there is no second thing to put in front of it, and nobody asked anybody anything, and that is where the second day of the forty-seventh week ends, and it was a working day like every working day there has been in this district, and it was nine pence, and it went on the board in chalk with a name against it, and the name was read before it was written.
