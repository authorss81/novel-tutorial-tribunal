# Chapter 191: The First Sitting Of The Next Half

The corridor outside the chamber at Orison is nine feet wide and eleven yards long and has a bench in it that is not for sitting on, and on the seventh day of the forty-eighth week there were eleven people in the room at the top of the nine steps and none of them were there for a sitting.

There was not one. That is the whole of the morning and it is worth being exact about, because six days ago a man stood up in that room and asked a room for a day and the chair gave him one, and the district has been carrying the fact of it ever since, and the day is not this week’s day and is not next week’s either, and eleven people were in the room to be told so by a clerk of the counter who had it in a book in front of him.

It is the first sitting of the next half of the year. That is what the room calls it and that is what the four sheets of notice call it and what the minute says, and the clerk said it once, standing up, in the flat voice that people use for a thing they have read in a book, and then he said what it was in weeks, because nobody in that room had said it in weeks.

“Not this half,” he said. “The other half. It is not a date and I am not going to give you a date, because I do not have the date and the chair does not have it either and there is no form in this building that has a day on it if nobody has written one.”

Nobody in that room said a word for as long as it takes a carter to decide something.

“Somebody write that in a book,” said Nevin Hake, from the back.

“It is in a book,” said the clerk. “It is in two. One of them is yours and one of them is mine and mine is the one that has the day on it when somebody writes it, and nobody has written it, because a day is written when a party is entered and a party is entered when there is a person to enter, and there is no person on the paper this morning.” He squared the tray. “That is not a refusal. That is a form with a hole in it and I have been behind this counter two years and I have not seen the hole filled.”

---

What was on the paper this morning was four lines and a signature, and the signature was his, and Perrin Gant had filled the rest of it in with a man of thirty-one standing at the end of the table who could not read one word of it and did not say so at the time, seven days before, in a room above a chandler’s shop with a fire in it.

It was nobody’s doing. The district had come four miles on its own account, three of them, because a man had said in a room above a chandler’s shop in the forty-seventh week that he would rather be wrong out loud in a room four miles off than be right about a thing on a sheet he cannot read. The man with the basket had come four miles because there was nothing else to do with a morning and a basket is the only thing in that lane that puts his name in a place where anybody can find it. He was not in the room. Nobody sent for him and nobody was going to go and find him, and what he had said in the forty-seventh week about the price of it had been said before the fact and the price was this.

Hake read his own four lines out loud, from the paper, at the top of the nine steps, in about nine seconds, and he got them right, because he had said them to himself on a towpath and in a lane and in a room above a chandler’s shop and he has been a carter for twenty-two years and he can hold nine hundred loads in his head and four sentences is nothing.

Then he stopped, and looked at the paper, and said the thing.

“I cannot read this,” he said. “I want it said in this room, now, before anything else is entered, in front of the people it is going to be said to. I cannot read what I have signed. I signed it seven days ago and I did not say so then, and I have thought about it every day since, and the reason I did not say so then is that I did not want to be asked to sign it again, and that is a reason about me and not about the paper.”

The clerk squared the tray against the edge of the table and left his hands flat on it.

“Go on,” he said.

“There is a thing about that and I would like it put in the minute while I am standing here saying it, and it is not a complaint.” Hake turned the paper about a quarter of a circle, the way a man turns a thing round at a counter. “Six days ago a man stood up in this room and I read four sentences out of a sheet I had written and the chair read them back to me, and a boy of nineteen read a page of a book out in numbers, and then I said three figures out loud and not one of the three was entered, and the reason is that there is no column in this room for a figure and there is no column in this county. I have been told it is all in that minute. Nobody has sent me a copy of any part of it and a copy is fourpence a sheet and this district is at nothing.”

“That is right,” said the clerk. “I have wanted to enter them all morning and there is nowhere to put a figure. It is not in the chair’s gift and it is not mine, and I have been at this counter two years and nobody has ever asked me whether there was a place for one, and I have looked, twice, this morning.”

---

Then Ansel Cork came in from the other end of the room, and he is fifty-one and has been in that post nineteen years and he is the man entitled to speak for the account, and he had a file under his arm, and it was watched being opened, because a file is always watched being opened in that building.

He did not say the four things. Everybody in that room waited for the four things and they did not come, and what came instead was two sentences and a look at the paper, and it took about a minute.

“The account has been heard and the account’s position has not altered,” said Ansel Cork. “And the account has no objection to the day that has been given, and no objection to it is not the same as agreeing with it, and I have said the difference out loud once in a lane six miles up that road and I am not going to say it twice in a building.”

Nobody in that room believed that and everybody in that room let it go, and about two of them noticed that he had come into a room and not a lane, which is the first time in nineteen years that anybody could put a date to.

Then he said the thing that was actually on the paper, and he said it flatly, and it was about a line and not about a day.

“There is a line in the account’s own book against this district that says the district is carried as a party that does not co-operate,” said Ansel Cork. “It is nineteen years old and it has never been argued and it is not argued today, and I am going to say the reason I have said it, because I have never said that reason in nineteen years and somebody has now paid for a page out of a reeve’s apron to be here. The line is on the party. It is not on a person. And there is no form in this building or in that building or in this county for a person named in a deposition to ask for a thing to be out.”

The room was quiet for a length of time.

“Then how does a person get it out,” said Wil Tamworth.

“They do not,” said Ansel Cork. “That is not my answer and it is not the account’s answer. It is what the form does. I have been in this post nineteen years and I have watched it do that to about nine parties and I have never once been asked to explain it and I am not going to start, and I would like it noticed that I have just started.”

---

And then the chair said the part of the morning that was going to be the whole of the year, and he said it in about eleven seconds, and it was the only thing anybody said all morning that nobody in that room had heard before.

Aldis Fenner came out of the room at the back, which is not a thing he does, and stood behind the last chair.

“A person of the district applies and stands in the room on the day,” he said. “That has been the rule here for nineteen years and it is older than nineteen years, and it is the rule I have sat under twice a year for the whole of this chair, and I have watched it do two things and I have never once put them together in a sentence and I am going to put them together in a sentence now because a man in this district has asked me for a day and I have given him one and there is nothing else in this room that can be done with a year.”

He put his hand on the back of the chair.

“First. A person of the district signs a thing and stands in a room and answers for it, and if he cannot read what he has signed then he is a man who has said in a room that the four sentences are true, and there is nothing anywhere in this county that requires him to have read them, and every word he says in that room goes into a minute.” He looked at Nevin Hake and did not go through it quickly. “And that is not a weakness. That is the only way this kind of district is ever going to be a person in a room, and this room has been producing the harm for nineteen years off exactly that arrangement and not off anything else.”

“And second,” said Perrin Gant, from the front, and he had not been told to go second and he did it anyway.

“There is no other arrangement,” said Aldis Fenner. “There is no other one in this county and there is no form for the other one. And so what happens to a man like him is that a room can use him, once, and there is nothing whatever in the paper that stops the room using him again.”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“That is not a finding and it is not a rule and I am not making it one,” he said. “I have said it out loud and I am going back to my chair. If the district wants a man to stand in a room on a day six months from now then the district has to decide before that day what is going to happen to him in it, and there is exactly one thing in this building that can be decided about it, and it is not mine and it is not the account’s and it is not the chair’s. It is a witness.”

---

And a witness is four, and this district had four, and three of the four could not read what they had sworn on the twenty-second day of the forty-third week, and the fourth could, and had never been asked.

Nobody in that room knew that there was a question about it. It was in a book in a city four miles off, on a page after thirty-four names, in a hand of a convenor's that the only person who could read in that building had read back to him twice, and it had been there since the sixth day of the forty-sixth week.

What the room did about it was nothing at all, and what a lane four miles back did about it at about the second bell the next morning was the whole of the fourth thing that happened in this district in ten weeks.

---

At about the seventh bell a clerk of the counter copied the minute out, because a clerk of the counter copies the minute out on the day, and it was one sheet, and the district had paid sixteen pence for four sheets of the other one out of a reeve’s apron six days ago and is at nothing, and so this one was copied and put in a drawer in that building, and nobody in the drawer has a name on it and nobody has ever asked for it.

Here is the whole of it, and it is one sheet, and it is the only copy of it in the world, and the district has not paid for it and does not know that it has it:

> THE MATTER. THE CLAIM OF THE DISTRICT OF RYEFOLD TO THE ACCOUNT OF THE SHELF GRANARY, LISTED, NOT DECIDED, FOR A DAY IN THE OTHER HALF OF THE YEAR.
> THE CARRIER. NIL. A CARRIER IS A PERSON OF THE DISTRICT AND NO PERSON OF THE DISTRICT HAS BEEN ENTERED AND NO ONE IS ASKED TO BE ENTERED.
> THE PARTY RECORD. THE DISTRICT IS CARRIED AS A PARTY THAT DOES NOT CO-OPERATE. THE LINE IS ON THE PARTY AND NOT ON A PERSON. NO FORM EXISTS FOR TAKING IT OUT.
> WHAT WAS SAID IN THE ROOM AND NOT ENTERED: A CARTER OF THIS DISTRICT STATED THAT HE CANNOT READ WHAT HE HAS SIGNED; THAT THREE FIGURES SPOKEN BY HIM ARE IN A MINUTE THAT HAS NOT BEEN COPIED; AND THAT THE CHAIR STATED THAT A ROOM CAN USE A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ WHAT THEY HAVE SIGNED, ONCE, AND THAT NOTHING IN THE PAPER STOPS A ROOM USING THEM AGAIN.
> THE CARRIER IS TO BE ENTERED BEFORE THE DAY. A WITNESS IS FOUR PERSONS OF A DISTRICT AND THE DISTRICT HAS NOT APPOINTED FOUR.

And in the corridor, at about the seventh bell, on a bench that is not for sitting on, a man of thirty-one with a basket he was not going anywhere with read none of it and was told none of it and was not asked for anything, because nobody in that building owed him anything and nobody was going to go and find him, and about nine people in this district have watched something happen to him in the last ten weeks and not one of them has ever mentioned it.
