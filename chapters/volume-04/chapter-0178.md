# Chapter 178: The Voluntary Restraint Clause, Still

The room above the chandler’s shop had a fire in it on the first day of the forty-seventh week, and it was the day after a cut ended, and about nine people were in it, and the quay outside was full of carts that had nothing to do until the next cut, and the bench was trying to write one sentence.

That is the whole of what it was trying to do and it took the whole morning and it took two men who had already tried once, in the autumn before last, and had given up, and had folded a sheet up and put it in the bench’s own book and not told anybody, and it is in the book still, and it should never have been folded up, and the reason nobody ever read it out loud is that it was about a rent, and a rent is a thing the account does to a district, and there is nothing in a room above a chandler’s shop that can be done about a rent.

Osa Trin took it out of the book at about the second bell. It is one sheet, folded in four, and it is in her hand and not in his, because he wrote it and could not read what he had written, and there are three people in this district who cannot read a form and two of them are on the bench, and so she copied it out for him in the autumn before last, and that is the only reason it has ever been legible to anybody, and she has never mentioned it, and nobody ever asked her for it.

“Wil,” she said. “This is yours.”

“It is mine,” said Wil Tamworth, who is thirty-three and who put *I said the word* out of his own mouth in the forty-third week and never did it again. “I wrote it in the autumn before last and Nevin and I spent about two evenings on it and we could not get past the second line and then I folded it up and I have not opened that book in front of anybody for about a year. Read it out.”

He said read it out. He could not read it. Nobody in that room said anything about that either, and it is the fifth time in six weeks, and it is not going to be the last, and that is its own small loss and it is not a large one and he is not going to be told about it again.

And she read it out, standing up, with her thumb under the line, and this is the whole of it:

> That a district which cannot pay a fortieth in grain may pay it in labour.

“That is wrong,” said Perrin Gant, immediately, and he was right, and he was the only one of the four who said so and he said it in about a second and a half, and the reason he was right is that there is no such thing in this county as paying a rent in labour and has not been for four hundred years and the four hundred years are not an accident.

“It is wrong,” said Gant. “And I have known it was wrong since the second evening and I folded it up instead of saying so, and I have been carrying that for a year, and I would like the room to have the reason, and the reason is that I did not want to be a man who had been wrong for two evenings.”

“That is not the reason it is wrong,” said Nevin Hake. “That is the reason you folded it up. There are two things wrong with it and you have found one of them and I have found the other and neither of us found the right one or we would not have folded it up.”

---

They went at it for about three hours and it is worth setting down exactly what came of it, because three hours in that room is the largest single effort that has been made in it in nineteen years and the whole of what it produced is six lines and a blank.

The thing they had to write was not about a rent. That was the first wrong thing, and Wil Tamworth found it at about the third bell and he found it by asking what the sentence was for, and the answer was that it was for the matter that goes to a room four miles off on the first of the forty-eighth week, and the matter is about a district’s protection and the price of it, and the price of it is a fortieth of everything in the granary, and a clause about a rent is a clause about the wrong end of the same stick, and a bench in a district of six hundred people has got one matter and it is about the grain and not about the shed.

“So it is not a rule about a rent,” said Nevin Hake. “It is a rule about a person. And I have been writing it as a rule about a rent for a year because a rent is the only thing on the table and because a rule about a person is the one thing that has not been on the table in four hundred years.”

“Then what is the rule about a person going to say.”

And that is the moment the whole morning turns on, and it happened in about eleven seconds, and it was a carter’s mouth, and the carter is forty-six and has been on that bench eleven years and is out of his depth in grain and has said so.

“It is going to say what happens to a man with one cart in a bad week,” said Nevin Hake, “and that is the sentence the two of us could not get past, and the reason we could not get past it is not that it is hard. It is that there is a blank in the middle of it.”

“What blank.”

“There has got to be one,” said Nevin Hake. “Because a bad week is a week in which the district cannot pay, and when the district cannot pay somebody does not get paid, and the person who does not get paid is the man with one cart. And if the sentence does not say who eats that, then it is a sentence about a queue, and we have all been writing it as a sentence about a queue for two years, and the whole of the difficulty in this county is that the queue is the only instrument anybody has ever been able to name.”

He put a finger on the paper.

“So the sentence has a name in the middle of it. *And where a person cannot be paid, — bears it.* That is the sentence. And there is a blank in it about the size of a hand.”

Nobody in that room said anything for about nine seconds.

“And I am going to say the thing that stops it,” said Nevin Hake, “and I have been a carter for twenty-two years and I know it in my hands and not in my head. A burden with a name on it is a thing that can be sold. That is not a theory, that is a thing that happened to this district and I have watched it happen and I can tell you the day it happened, which is about three weeks ago, and it happened in a room four miles off, and there is a line in a book about a party and the party is a district and the district is not the user of it. And the whole of that happened because somebody wrote a name in a blank, and the name was a category and not a person, and the category could be used by anybody who held the paper.”

He took his hand off the paper.

“So the moment we fill that blank in, we have written the clause. And the moment we have written the clause, the clause can be sold, and it can be sold to whoever is holding the paper at the time, and that is not a fear and it is not a warning. It is how a rule about a rent became a rule about a cart in about four hundred years and nobody did anything wrong in any of the four hundred of them.”

“Whose name goes in it,” said Wil Tamworth.

“That is the blank,” said Nevin Hake. “And that is the whole of the finding and I have had it since about the fourth bell in the autumn before last and I have not said it in a room because I could not think what to do with it.”

---

The four of them went through the names, out loud, in a room with about nine people in it, and it took about an hour, and it is worth setting the whole of the list down because it is the real argument and it is a list of nouns.

They could not put the account in it, because the account is the other side and the account is a body and it puts a figure in a register and it is not on this side of anything.

They could not put the register in it, because the register is kept in a city six miles off and not in the district, and it is a book and a book cannot bear anything, and a page cannot stand in a door, and the man who keeps it is a man of fifty-four who has said four true things in a yard and a fifth thing that was his, and he is not the villain in this and he is not going to be.

They could not put the bench in it, because a bench is four persons and the fourth person it would be is the reader, and a bench that bears a burden in a matter it is a party to is a bench that is on both sides of it.

They could not put the granary in it, because a building is not a bearer and the grain in it is the district’s and the district’s own grain did not go out on the sixth day of the forty-third week and four hundred and six loads of it stayed in a bin for nine pounds and nobody in this county is going to be told that twice in the same breath as a clause about a bad week.

And they could not put a person, and that is the one that matters, and here is the finding and it is not a villain and it is not the price of a sheet of paper.

“You cannot put a person in a blank,” said Osa Trin. “Not without asking them. And you cannot ask four hundred men in about nine hundred yards, and you cannot ask them this month, and there is no form anywhere in this county for a person to be entered as a bearer of anything, and the reason there is no form is that in four hundred years nobody has ever needed one, because the bearers have all been districts and accounts and bodies, and a name that is not a person is easy to fill in and that is the whole of what the difficulty is.”

---

And then Marda Fen said the thing that had been in the room for about three hours without anybody noticing it, and she said it from the doorway with her sleeve rolled up, because she had come up to say one thing about the granary and had stayed, and what she said was not about a clause and it was not about a blank.

“You have all been talking about a bad week,” she said, “as though it has not happened nine times.”

Nobody said anything.

“I have been a reeve in this granary for twenty-two years,” she said. “I have carried forty-one loads out of my own authority in nine years, and forty-one loads at three shillings a load is six pounds three shillings, and I have told two people that figure in the last month and I have never been asked for it by anybody who is not one of them. I have put a woman with four children on the bottom of that list in a different ink with no authority in this county, and I have carried the fourth line for nine months, and a man on that line died in the thirty-first week of last year and his wife is in this district and she does not know, and I have thought about it every week for nine months. And I did all of it out of my own authority, for nothing, without a form, and I have never once been asked to, and I have never once been asked whether I minded.”

She put her hand flat on the door frame.

“That is a bad week,” she said. “That is what a bad week is in this district. It is not a queue. It is about nine years of a woman of fifty-four deciding things on her own in a room that has not had a clerk in it since before I came, and there is no clause in this county that covers it and there is not going to be one, and if there were one it would have a name in it, and the name would be mine, and I am not going to stand in a room four miles off and be entered as the bearer of a thing I have been carrying for nine years with a pen borrowed off a bench.”

Nobody in that room said one word for about a minute.

“I am not saying no to a clause,” she said. “I am saying the blank in that sentence is not a hole and it is not a difficulty. It has got a person in it who is standing in the doorway and has been in it for nine years, and the reason none of you can write the sentence is that writing it means putting her in a book, and she has not asked to be in a book, and the man who said out loud in a yard two streets off that quay on the fifth day of the forty-third week that a record is a thing about a man whether he likes it or not, and that that is the whole of what a record is, is the reason.”

And Ilyan Vester, who had said that, and who had said it in this room in the forty-first week, did not say anything at all, and did not look at the door, and did not try to repair it, and the reason he did not is the reason he did not think about Roald Tern in a yard a fortnight ago, and he has never in his life written that reason down.

---

They finished at about the fifth bell and what they finished with was this, and it is the whole of what the morning produced, and Perrin Gant wrote it in the bench’s own book in his own hand with the date and then shut the book, which is a thing he does.

They wrote the question and not the answer, which is the first time in nineteen years that anything in that book has been a question, and it is four lines long, and there is a blank in the third of them about the size of a hand, and the four lines are these:

> The voluntary restraint. That a district which cannot pay may — .
> And where a person cannot be paid, — bears it.
> And the name of the person who is to bear it is not yet anybody’s, and the bench has asked nobody, and the bench will not write it down until somebody has answered yes.

“Read it back to me,” said Perrin Gant, when it was done, and he said it to the only person in that room who could, and she read the four lines back to him twice and did not correct one word of them and did not put a finger under any of the lines, and neither of them said one word about the fact that a convenor of sixty-one has been getting his own book read to him for nineteen years and that this is the first time he has ever asked for a page with a question in it.

At about the sixth bell Marda Fen went back down the lane to the granary, and there were about seven thousand loads in that building and about four hundred carts and one pair of hands, and nobody in this district has ever entered her in a book and nobody in this district is going to, and the last line of that page is the closest thing to a rule about a bad week that four hundred years has produced, and it is a page with a blank in it, and the blank has a woman in it who is carrying, and she is not going to stop.
