# Chapter 162: Two Shillings and Eightpence

A bench of four walked up a lane to a yard at the sixth bell on the fifth day of the forty-third week because a carter with one cart had said in that yard that he was going to put a sheet of paper on their table, and nobody had asked him to, and the reason the bench walked instead of sending is worth putting down, and Perrin Gant put it down, standing at a gate, in about eleven words, before he came in.

“A man with a cart out in a cut cannot come to a room for nothing,” he said. “That is a day. We would be asking a day off him. And I have never in nineteen years asked one of this district for a day for nothing, and this is the first time and I have noticed.”

The yard is nine feet deep between the granary wall and a cart-shed wall and there is a trestle in it and a wheel off the trestle and a man of thirty-four on a stool with the hub on his knee and his hands in it.

He did not stop.

“I know all four of you,” said Iven Brack, to the hub. “And the one at the back with the leg I do not know and I am not going to pretend I do. Sit on the wall. Do not touch the wheel. If you touch the wheel I will put the hub through your hand and it will be an accident and I will not be sorry about it and there will be no proof.”

Eleven people came in over about four minutes and eleven of them stayed, and about six of the eleven were the bench and one was the bench’s stranger, and four were from two streets off and stood at the gate because that is what four men do at a gate.

Then Brack put the hub down, and put a folded sheet on the trestle beside it, and did not smooth it out.

“Before anybody reads it,” he said. “I want the price said where all of you can hear it, and I want it said first, and the reason I want it said first is that after it has been read out there will be about nine reasons for nobody to say it, and every one of the nine will be a good reason, and I have thought of six of them and I do not want to be talked out of this by the other three.”

“Go on,” said Perrin Gant.

“If that sheet comes off me, I have not got a second cart by the thirtieth.” He put his hand flat on the trestle, not on the sheet. “Not me. And not a man in this lane. And not a man in this county who has one cart this week and would have had two by the end of the week, and there are three of those within a mile of this gate and one of them is in that shed and he knows it and he does not know I know.”

Nobody said anything.

“And about four hundred loads of ours will be standing on that quay on the last day of the fortysixth week with nobody to move them, because there is no second cart, and they will go into the ground or they will go into the river and it makes no difference which and I have not worked that out and I do not intend to. That is the price. It is not mine, it is the district’s, and I am not being brave about it and I am not being clever. I have worked it out on the back of this trestle with a nail and I would like somebody to say it back to me and then I would like nobody to say it again.”

Nevin Hake said it back to him.

Nobody thanked Nevin Hake.

---

Then Osa Trin picked the sheet up off the trestle, and nobody asked her to, and she turned it round so it faced the yard, and she held it at the height of her apron and read it standing up, and here is the whole of it, because it is two sheets and the price is the same in a hundred and one counties and the second one is the same shape in four hundred and thirty-nine yards.

> WHERE A FORBEARANCE IS RUNNING IN A DISTRICT, THE HOLDER OF THIS MAY CARRY GRAIN IN IT AND BE PAID OUT OF THAT DISTRICT BEFORE ANY OTHER CLAIM IS ENTERED AGAINST IT.
>
> ENTERED ON THE SHEET: THE HOLDER’S NAME, THE DISTRICT, THE DATE, AND THE PLACE, IN THE HOLDER’S OWN HAND. TWO SHEETS. ONE SHILLING AND FOURPENCE A SHEET.

“That is all of it,” said Osa Trin. “There is nothing on it about the seven days, and there is nothing on it about a bad week, and there is nothing on it about what a queue is, and there is nothing on it about anything at all except a place in a line of people, and the line has not happened and may never happen, and the sheet does not say so.”

“It is the same one,” she said, turning to the yard rather than to the bench, “that a man sold at the third counter in Nethergill in the thirty-eighth week of last year, and it is word for word the same, and I have seen two more like it and I could not tell you which county they were in, and I can read.”

Iven Brack said, “I could not read it.”

“No,” said Osa Trin. “You could not. That is the whole of what is wrong with that yard and it is not the yard.”

---

And the second sheet had his own hand on it, and Osa Trin turned it over and read it out loud, and it was four lines, and they were in a carter's hand, and the hand was better than anybody expected.

> IVEN BRACK. RYEFOLD. THE TWENTY-EIGHTH DAY OF THE FORTY-FIRST WEEK. THE YARD, RYEFOLD.

“That is a man of thirty-four writing his own name in a lane,” said Wil Tamworth, who was thirty-three and the youngest of the four, “and he got the date right, and I say that because in this district nobody gets the date right and I have been one of the four of them for two years and I am including myself.”

“Anything else on it,” said Perrin Gant.

“No,” said Osa Trin. “That is the four lines and there is no fifth. It wants the place, and he put the place, and it does not want a county, and it does not want a number of loads, and it does not want him to say how many loads he intends to move, and I would like it noticed that a man with one cart was not asked by this paper how much he meant to carry, and that is the first time in four years that a piece of paper in this district has not asked me something about myself.”

Then the bench's stranger was holding it.

He had asked for it, two days earlier, in that yard, and what he had asked for was to be the one person in the room who had read nothing and could still hold a piece of paper, and there was no room, and there was a yard, and the paper was in his hands.

And he could not read it, and he was holding it the way a man holds a plate that is hot, with the corner of it in a downward tilt, and he had been doing that for about four minutes.

There was one man in that yard who knew why, and Nevin Hake had said out loud in a room above a chandler's shop on the third day of the forty-first week that he could not read a form and had been in this bench eleven years and had never once said so, and he had said it because a stranger had been honest in that room, and he had said the price of saying it, and the price was that he would never be able to sit in that room and not know it.

Nevin Hake watched a man hold a sheet of paper he could not read for about four minutes and said nothing at all, and looked at a wheel instead, and did not enjoy it and did not think about it afterwards.

---

“Nine pounds and threepence,” said Osa Trin.

Nobody had said anything about money for ten minutes and she said it into a yard with about eleven people in it.

“The use of a forbearance on a load is worth what a load costs to move. That is the register’s own scale and it is three shillings. He moved sixty-one loads under it, and sixty-one loads at three shillings is nine pounds and threepence, and he paid two shillings and eightpence for the privilege of moving them first.”

“Nine pounds and threepence,” said Iven Brack.

“You did not know that,” said Perrin Gant.

“No,” said Iven Brack. “I did not know that. I knew what the second cart cost. Nobody has ever told me what the first one was worth and I did not ask, and I would like it noticed that I did not ask and not that I could not have.”

---

And then there is the part of a yard nobody thanks anybody for.

Wil Tamworth said that a remedy that takes the sheet off a man with one cart would leave a district with no second cart by the thirtieth, and that the district had four carts, and that four carts in a cut of three thousand four hundred loads is nothing, and that taking three hundred and thirty-nine sheets off three hundred and thirty-nine men with one cart each would not produce a single additional cart in this district before the cut, and possibly not this year.

Nevin Hake said that the loads would not be moved.

Marda Fen was not there and had sent nothing and had not been asked, and the reason she was not there is that she was in the granary with sixty carts standing on a quay, and the reason she was not asked is that nobody in that yard thought of her, and Perrin Gant noticed that at the time and wrote it in the bench's book that night, in his own hand, with the date, which is the fourth thing this district has ever made about itself and the second one that is about a person.

And then nobody refused, because there was nothing to refuse, and nobody was thanked, and Perrin Gant asked a carter with one cart whether the bench had his leave to put his sheet in the bench's book and the carter said it could go in the book and it was not to be a *thing about him*, and Perrin Gant said that a record is a thing about him whether he likes it or not and that is the whole of what a record is, and the carter said he had not been asked that before and did not answer.

---

And then the yard was quiet for a bit, and the carter said the question.

It was not about the sheet and it was not about the second cart and it was not about the thirty-fifth day. Everybody in that yard had been talking about the thirty-fifth day for two hours without once asking anything about it, which is a thing that happens wherever a group of people are all avoiding a thing, and Iven Brack asked it at about the ninth bell with the hub in his hands and a wheel off a trestle and eleven people against a wall.

“Has anybody in this district ever been up there?”

He did not say up there. He tipped his head at the top of the lane, where the road goes to the court.

“On our behalf,” he said. “Not for a notice. For this. Has anybody.”

“Once,” said Perrin Gant, “in the thirty-ninth week of last year, a reeve of this district signed a piece of paper in a room in a city and the signature of the reeve was in a room she was not in.”

“Was a person there.”

“No,” said Perrin Gant. “A paper was there. And the paper is what has been coming back ever since, and it is the only thing in four hundred years that this district has ever put in a room anywhere, and every one of the four of us can tell you that a paper does not answer a question.”

The carter turned the hub a quarter of a turn and did not look up.

“Then what is she,” he said. “Osa. If a person goes up there on the thirty-fifth day and stands in a room and they ask her something and there is nobody in this district who knows the answer, then she stands there and says she does not know, and that is the truth, and she says it for six hundred people. And that is the first time anybody in this district has ever been able to say a true thing out loud in a room four miles off.”

Nobody answered him, and he did not appear to want one, and he put the hub in and hit it once and it went true.
