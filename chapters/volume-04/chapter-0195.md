# Chapter 195: The Blank Has To Stay A Blank

The room above the chandler’s shop had nine people in it on the fourth day of the forty-ninth week and the fire was out again, and on the table was a sheet of the district’s own with four lines on it in a second hand, and three of the four lines were finished and the third of them was not.

That is the whole of the morning and it took four days to arrive at and it took about nine minutes to write, and the writing was done by a woman of twenty-eight with a pen and her own book open beside her because she had brought the book and not the sheet.

She had walked four miles with it and had not been paid for the day and had not asked to be, and she said that in the room before she said anything else, and nobody thanked her, and she had not expected to be thanked and said so too.

“I am not of this district,” said Sera Quill. “I am a recordwright of a public record and I write down what people say in their own words, and I cannot act for you and I have told him so in a city four miles off that room and I am telling this room so that there is no part of this morning that anybody in it can be told later on was arranged.”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“Now tell me what the draft says, and say it as four things, and do not say the fifth thing yet.”

---

It went in a hand that was not the hand of any of the four of them and it is on a nail in that room and it is the fifth thing on it, and Perrin Gant said so out loud before it went up, because there have been three things on that nail since the forty-third week and there are five on it now and the fifth is a sheet with a blank in the third line and a page with a blank in the third of its lines is eleven pages further on in a book in the same room.

> A REPAYMENT DRAFT, PUT BY A PERSON OF THIS DISTRICT, TO THE ACCOUNT OF THE SHELF GRANARY, ON THE MATTER LISTED IN THE OTHER HALF OF THE YEAR.
> ONE. THAT THE PAGE OF THE ACCOUNT’S OWN BOOK FOR THIS DISTRICT BE READ AGAIN IN THE ROOM, ON THE DAY, AND THAT THE HUNDRED LOADS ON IT BE NAMED IN THE MINUTE AS A HUNDRED LOADS.
> TWO. THAT A YEAR OF THE PROTECTION OF THIS DISTRICT, SPENT UPON CARRIAGE BELONGING TO OTHER PEOPLE, BE REPAID TO THE ACCOUNT IN THE MANNER THE ACCOUNT ITSELF DIRECTS, AND THAT THE DISTRICT BE TOLD WHAT THE MANNER IS BEFORE IT IS AGREED TO.
> THREE. THAT THE BURDEN NOW STATED AGAINST THE PARTY BEARER BE — .
> FOUR. THAT THE COST OF THE NOTICE OF FOUR SHEETS REMAINS WITH THE DISTRICT, AND IS FIVE SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THE DISTRICT HAS NEVER PAID IT.

The third line is a blank about the size of a hand and it is the same blank as the one in the book eleven pages back, and it has been a blank since the first day of the forty-seventh week, and everybody in that room knew what was in it and nobody in that room had said so in a room since the seventh day of the forty-seventh week, when a woman of fifty-four said it from a doorway with her sleeve rolled up.

Then a reeve of fifty-four said it again, and she had come up to say one thing about the beam and had stayed, and it is the second time in ten weeks that she has said it in that room and it is the second time she has said it from the same doorway.

“You are all about to write my name in the middle of a sentence,” said Marda Fen.

Nobody in that room said anything.

“I said in this doorway in the forty-seventh week that I would not be entered in a book as the bearer of a thing I have been carrying for nine years with a pen borrowed off a bench, and nothing has happened since then, and nothing is going to happen, and I am not going to be asked twice.” She put her hand on the door frame. “So that line has to go down the way it is, or the district has to find somebody else, and there is nobody else, and I have been the only person in this district carrying anything without authority since before the four of you were on this bench, and you know that, and Gant wrote a question about it into a book a year ago and I have watched that question sit in there.”

“Then it stays blank,” said Wil Tamworth.

“Yes, it stays blank,” said Marda Fen. “And I want everybody in this room to know what that costs, because you all know and not one of you has said it, and I have been in this lane for about nine minutes and I am the only person in this room who has been inside a bad one.”

She came away from the frame.

“A draft with a name in the middle of it is a draft. A draft without a name in the middle of it is a sheet with a hole in it that a man of thirty-one from nowhere has to put himself into, and he is the cheapest thing in this county to put a name on, and I have watched that happen from the second door for about four days and I have not said anything to him about it either and that is mine. So write the hole. Write it and leave it and let it stand there, and then do not go about this county telling anybody that a bench of four has got a remedy, because what you have got is a sheet with a gap in it and a carter who cannot read standing in the gap for you on a day six months from now.”

---

Nobody argued with her and nobody contradicted her, and it is worth saying exactly what happened next, because in a district of six hundred people the whole of what a room can be used for is a thing that gets said in a doorway and then everybody goes back to a beam, and about four hundred carters in about nine hundred yards have had nothing to do for three weeks running.

A man of thirty-one said the thing that was not in anybody’s mouth, and he said it standing up at the end of the table with his back to the shutter on the lane side, and he said it in the working voice, and he had it worked out in about nine seconds and the working was his and nobody in the room could check it.

“The second line,” he said. “Read it back to me.”

Sera Quill read it back to him, because it is her sheet and he cannot read, and she read it twice, and neither reading changed a word.

“That says the district repays the year, and the year was worth four hundred and sixty-two pounds, and the carts cost four hundred and sixty, and the difference is two pounds.” He had his hand flat on the table. “So the second line asks the account to be paid two pounds, and the account’s charge is four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year on nine thousand seven hundred pounds, and a hundred loads of hire were never paid for at anything, which is fifteen pounds, and this district is at nothing and has been since the sixth day of the forty-third week, and a reeve’s wage of two pounds ten a year is eleven weeks out of it, which is ten shillings and sevenpence.”

“Then you are asking the account for two pounds,” said Nevin Hake.

“I am asking the account for a manner,” said Ilyan Vester. “And the reason I have written it that way and not with a figure in it is that I have had four days of doing it with figures in it and a man of fifty-four is right about every one of them, and I am not going to stand in a room six months from now and be a man who came to a county with two pounds.”

Nobody in that room filled that.

“Then what does it do,” said Perrin Gant.

“It asks a question,” said Ilyan Vester. “In a form, in a hand, with four lines and a hole in the third of them, and a question in a form is the only instrument this district has ever had that nobody in this county can answer with a no. And in about nine months a clerk in a counting-house four miles off is going to have to enter one line against this district whatever is in the fourth of these hands, and there is no form for not answering it, and I would like the thing that is standing in that book when he does it to be a hole with a question in it.”

He took his hands off the table.

“And there is a second thing and it is mine and I am going to say it before anybody in this room works it out and says it kindly. I am the third line of this sheet. I have been in this county nine weeks and a day and there is a hole in the third line the size of a hand and the only person in this district who could be put in it has said no in a doorway, and I am a man of no district with a page torn out of a penny exercise book in his coat and I am the cheapest thing in this county to put a name on, and I have known that since a room with eleven people in it in the forty-sixth week and I have not been able to do one single thing about it.”

He looked round the table.

“Which is why the third line is the one I have written last and written worst and it is the reason it is a hole. I would rather be a hole in this county than a name in it, and I want that in the book, and I want whoever reads the book in about nine months to have a hole to write against and not a man.”

The room was quiet for about a minute, and there were nine people in it, and a reeve of fifty-four stood at the door of a room in which nothing had been fixed and said the last thing before she went.

“I have been in this building twenty-two years,” said Marda Fen, “and I have never once seen a thing written down in this district that cost anybody anything, and that is not a compliment to the four of you and it is not a complaint about them either. It is the price of a sheet in this county and you all know what that is. Nine pence a day is what it is. That is what it costs a man to be a person in a record in the district of Ryefold for one day, and he has been on that board in chalk in my hand every working day since the fourth day of the forty-first week and about nine people in this lane have worked out that a man who cannot read has got a line on my wall and not one of them has ever asked me.”

She went down the lane.

---

At about the seventh bell the sheet went on the nail, and a nail in that room has had three things on it since the forty-third week and has five on it now, and the fifth of them is a repayment draft in a second hand with a blank in the third line about the size of a hand, and the blank is the same blank, and it is going to be a blank on the day.

And at about the ninth bell a man of thirty-one with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him was in a cart shed behind a granary, and a woman of twenty-eight was going back four miles with the sheet on her own time and he was not going with her because he is not a person of this district and a person of this district is the only one who may carry a thing out of this room, and nobody in that lane has ever said that out loud and she had said it in a doorway of wet wool four miles off before she came, and about nine people in this district have watched something happen to him in the last ten weeks and not one of them has ever mentioned it.
