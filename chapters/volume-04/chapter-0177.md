# Chapter 177: What Carrying Is

He tried to do it alone first, on the seventh day of the forty-sixth week, and it took him a whole day, and the day was one of nine he had, and what it cost was not a feeling.

The thing he had carried for five days was six lines long and it wanted a person in a room in a city four miles off, on the first of the forty-eighth week, and there is no form anywhere in this county for a man of no district to be that person. He had known that for about six weeks. He went and found out whether there was a way round it that did not involve asking anybody, and there is not, and the not is not a rule and is not a wall and there was nobody on the other side of it to be unkind.

The only way to carry it alone was to be the one who carried it, and the only way to be the one who carried it was to be produced into the matter as a person and not as somebody’s hands, on one sheet at a shilling and fourpence, and that is a thing he said out loud, in a room above a chandler’s shop, on the third day of the forty-first week, to four people, before anybody had agreed to anything: *being asked and being produced are not the same act, and every one of the five times I have been produced was a thing that was simply the next thing along, and I am not going to be produced a sixth time.*

So the way round it was a form, and the form was the thing he had sworn not to use, and he was carrying it, and he put it in his coat, and he went and found out about a hundred exception forms instead, which took a day, and there are about nine hundred and forty of them in a box somewhere that is not in this district.

He came back at about the seventh bell on the towpath with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and a cart was going into Ryefold, and the carter lifted the shafts for a man with a bag because that is what is done on a towpath, and he got in, and about two miles up the path the rendering came up on him the way it comes up, at the height and at the distance it has been at since the third of the second month in a city four hundred miles behind him: the boards, the table the size of a table a hand could go on, the lamp with the wick down, the window with weather on the outside of it that was not this weather, and four people at the table and one of them about forty-four with her back to him.

It did not move. It did not explain itself. The leg went at the same moment from the hip down and the carter said nothing about it and did not slow down and asked him whether he was going as far as the granary, and he said he was, and that was the whole of what happened.

---

The room above the chandler’s shop had about eleven people in it at about the eighth bell on the seventh day of the forty-sixth week, and it was the last day of a cut, and the bench had not called a meeting and the room was full anyway, and that is a different thing and everybody in the lane knows the difference.

He asked three people, out loud, in the open, in the lane outside the second door, before he went in, with nine people standing about, and he asked all three the same question and he did not explain it and he did not persuade anybody afterwards and he has not come back with it in different words.

The question was nine words long: *Would you like to be the one who stands in the room.*

The first was Nevin Hake, forty-six, who had been a carter before he was a bench, and he said yes before the end of it.

“Yes,” said Nevin Hake. “And I would like it noted that I have said yes and that I have not worked out whether I know what it is, and I have not asked, and I am saying yes to a thing in a lane in front of nine people and if you tell me what it is I shall go cold. So do not tell me what it is.”

“I am not going to tell you what it is.”

“Then I am still yes, and that is the most honest thing anybody in this lane has done since the spring, and I would like it put down that I said yes to a question and not to a plan.”

The second was Dace Ferrow, fifty-seven, a wheelwright two streets off the quay, forty-one years building carts, who had a wheel in and had had it in since the second day of the cut, and who has been owed money for a job since the twenty-second week of last year.

“Yes,” said Dace Ferrow, “and I have got a price and it is not a noble one and I am going to say it out loud so that nobody in this lane can be surprised.”

“Say it.”

“One day. My shop is shut and my boy is sixteen and he cannot turn a wheel without me and he has not done it since his father died and I have not asked him to learn. A day is a day and there is no hire in this district to put against it.” He looked at the stranger. “And I have not been paid since the twenty-second week of last year for a different job and there is a wheelwright in this town who has been waiting a year and has not stopped working, so I can stand one more day in a room and be owed for it. That is the whole of me and it is not much and I am not going to pretend it is more.”

“I cannot pay you,” said Ilyan Vester. “There is nothing in the account and there is no fund and I am not an officer of anything and I have ninepence a day. I cannot pay you and I cannot promise to and I am not going to pretend to.”

“I did not ask you to,” said Dace Ferrow. “I asked for the day and you have given me the day. I have a wheel in and I will tell the boy.”

The third was Iven Brack, thirty-four, one cart, a yard two streets off the quay, four people, about a hundred loads still to go on his own account, and the cut ends at the ninth bell tonight, and he said no.

He said it in about four seconds and he gave the reason and the reason is the reason and it is not going to be improved on by anybody including the man he said it to.

“No,” said Iven Brack. “And if you ask me again in different words I am going to say the same word, and I want it understood that I am not being frightened and I am not being careful. You want somebody who is not in it. I am in it.”

“You are in it.”

“I am one of the three hundred and thirty-nine. I have been one of them for two years and I bought a sheet off a man in a good coat and I did not read it, and I have worked out since what it is worth on the day it is worth anything, and it is about sixty pounds, and sixty pounds is a second cart, and if I stand in a room in a city four miles off on the first of the forty-eighth week then I am a man in a room about four hundred private things and I am one of the four hundred private things. And then that room has got a man in it who is speaking about himself, and that is the whole thing you have been trying to stop since you came off that boat.”

He put his hand on the shaft of his own cart.

“And there is a second thing and I am going to say it because you will find it out in about four minutes and I would rather you had it from me. There are about a hundred loads on that cart behind me and the district has not got the hire money and by the first of the forty-eighth week I have not got a cart unless I have got a day of somebody’s, and there is nobody in this district who has got a day of somebody’s, and I have been looking at this quay for nine days. So if I go four miles off on the first of the forty-eighth week, I have not got a cart in the first week of the year and there is no way to get one, and the difference between me in that room and me on that quay is about four hundred loads and about sixty pounds, and I have got four people and a quarter of a pound of flour.”

“That is correct,” said Ilyan Vester.

He did not argue. He did not ask again. He did not put it to her as a duty, or to him as a carter, or to the bench as a bench, and he did not go up the lane and come back with it in different words, and he has not asked anybody to mend it and he is not going to.

“Yes,” he said. “It is correct and you have said it in four seconds and I asked you in nine words and I am not going to make you pay for having said it.”

“Good.”

And that is the practice, and it is the same practice as on a coast four hundred miles behind him, and it has now been used twice, and it is a sentence of nine words that a man says out loud in a lane to three people in front of nine other people, and it costs the man who asks nothing and it costs the man who says yes a day he will not be paid for, and one of the three said no and the no was correct, and the whole of what a practice is, is that you cannot arrange it in advance and you cannot pay it back.

---

And then there was the fourth person in that lane, and he did not ask her, and that is the thing he is actually carrying.

Osa Trin was at the second door with the list against her sleeve and about nine people behind her, and she is thirty-nine and she has been the person in this district who reads things for other people since she was nineteen and she has been asked what any of it is for exactly once in six weeks, and the once was him, on the fourth day of the forty-fifth week, in about nine words, in a doorway, in front of about eleven people, and the answer she gave was that there were two hundred and forty names and one of them was a dead man and one of them was not on it at all.

He did not ask her.

He has thought about not asking her for about ten days and he has gone through the reasons in order and the reasons are as follows and none of them is a good one. He is carrying a matter. If he asks her, the person who carries it into a room on the first of the forty-eighth week is a person who is already in the matter — a person the matter is about, in the sense that the fourth line of a list of two hundred and forty names is in it, and a woman at the bottom of the same list in a different ink is in it, and both of those are the reeve’s, and both of those are nine years of nobody asking anybody anything. He cannot ask her. He heard a convenor of sixty-one say out loud in a yard two streets off that quay on the fifth day of the forty-third week that a record is a thing about a man whether he likes it or not and that that is the whole of what a record is, and a carter said he had not been asked that before, and he has been trying for six weeks not to arrange people into records and asking a woman of thirty-nine to give up a year in order to be a person in one is arranging her into a record, and he knew it, and he asked the other three.

And that is what the relationship costs, and it is not a quarrel and it is not a refusal and nobody has said no, and it is this: she is a person in the matter and he cannot ask her anything, and that is the same shape as the fourth reason a woman of twenty-eight gave him in a room in a city four miles from this room, and he already knows exactly what that costs, and he paid it once, and he did not get an argument.

---

At about the eighth bell he was in that room, and the fire was down, and eleven people were in it, and the last hours of a cut were going on outside, and he said the last thing out loud because he has never in this district written anything down that he had not said out loud first, and because the practice is nine words and the truth is nine more, and because there were eleven people in the room and every one of them would have to carry it and none of them could check it.

“A burden with a name on it is a thing that can be sold,” he said, “and the name on this one is the name of a district and the district is not the user, and the whole of the last six weeks is that the name is the only part of it that is on a piece of paper. And I am going to carry this one. Not for a week. Past its review. Until the people who are paying for it are in the room, and if that is the first of the forty-eighth week then it is the first of the forty-eighth week, and it is in eight days and I have not got a person and I have asked three and one of them said no and the no was correct.”

Nobody in that room said anything, and he went on, and this is the part he has not been able to get past since and has not tried to get past since.

“And it costs me a year,” he said. “That is the price. That is what it is and I am not going to have it in a paper in this room, I am going to say it out loud, and it is mine, and I am the only one of the eleven people in this room who can tell you what it is, and none of you can check it, and that is not a trick of mine. That is how a person becomes a cheap thing to name.”

He said it in the ordinary way and then he said the rest of it, and it took him about a minute, and Perrin Gant did not interrupt him once and Osa Trin did not move.

“It is not a fog and it is not a wound and nobody has taken it. It is one year of my own out of thirty-one and I know which year and I have never written it down and I am not going to write it down tonight.” He put his hand flat on the shut book, which is a thing he has done twice. “It is the last of the years in which I was in a room with a person I had not yet met. There was a year of them and most of them were like the others and every one of them was the same shape: I was the nearest thing to hand, and I did not know whether it would help, and I went in anyway. That is the shape of the whole of it and there is nothing noble in it and it is not a gift and it is not a curse. It is a price that a person in a room a long way off from this one named out loud to me once, and I have never found out whether the naming was fair, and I have not asked anybody since, and I am not going to start tonight, and that person is not the woman of twenty-eight and I am not going to mix them up in a room. And it is the last one. And that year is the last one because of what happened in a lane at about the second bell this afternoon, and not because of anything in me. The thing that makes those years is that nobody else is in the room. I go into a room because there is nobody else to go, and the going is the shape and the shape has been the same for about a dozen years, and this afternoon there were eleven people in a room in this district and three of them were asked out loud in a lane in nine words whether they would like to be the one standing there, and one of them said no, and the no was correct. There is not going to be another year of that because the not was available and I took it, and a thing that is available is not a burden and is not a year and is not anything except a thing you did or did not do. A door is not a matter and a person is. That is the whole difference and it took me forty-eight days.”

He stopped.

“That is the year,” he said. “It is the last one. And I am not paying it this morning and I am not paying it on the first of the forty-eighth week. I am going to carry it, and it is going to be carried past a review, and if I stop carrying it the thing stops being visible, and if it stops being visible then about four hundred people never find out what their protection was spent on, and that is the whole of the arrangement and I have understood it at about the eighth bell on the last day of a cut in a district of six hundred people and I would like somebody to write that down in a better sentence than that.”

Nobody wrote it down. Perrin Gant put his hand on the shut book and said, “I cannot write down what I have not read and I have not got anybody to read it to me and I am not going to ask one of the three of us and I am not going to ask her,” and he did not say her name, and it was the second time in five days.

And the thing he had asked for came up, in its own two plain lines, and it did not say one word about any of the eleven people in that room.

**THE BURDEN CARRIED IS THE MATTER OF THIS DISTRICT LISTED FOR THE FIRST OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH WEEK, AND ITS SOURCE IS A RULING AND NOT A PERSON, AND IT IS DUE AT THE CLOSE OF EVERY GRAIN YEAR, AND NOBODY WHO IS NOT A PARTY TO IT MAY CONTEST IT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A MATTER HELD OPEN TO A LATER DAY IS A MATTER THAT HAS BEEN HAD.**

He read it twice. It told him what he had known at the second bell. It certified nothing and it joined nothing and it said nothing whatever about a year, and it is the third time this world has put anything at all in front of him, and every one of the three has been about a door with no handle on his side, and a stage is not the two lines and a stage is not a number and a stage is a duty with a consequence, and the consequence is in the room with eleven people in it and one of them is a wheelwright who has given a day and will not be paid for it and one of them is a carter who said no in four seconds and was right, and he is carrying one thing, and while he is carrying it he has no room for a second one, and that is not a rule he gets round, and that is the cost, and it is structural, and nobody in that room is going to soften it and he is not going to ask them to.

At about the ninth bell he was in the cart shed behind the granary and he wrote on the thirteenth page of a penny exercise book.

*Forty-eight days on this shelf. I asked three people in a lane in nine words and two of them said yes and one of them said no in four seconds and the no was the correct one and I am not going back with it in different words and I did not. A wheelwright of fifty-seven has given me a day and I cannot pay him and I have not promised to and I am not going to pretend to, and that is the first time in this world that asking a question out loud has cost a person something I could not put right, and I am not sorry about the asking and I am not going to be able to forget the day.*

*Osa Trin is a person in the matter and I cannot ask her anything, and I have not, and that is what it costs, and I knew what it cost before I did it, and it is the fourth reason a woman of twenty-eight gave me in a city four miles from this room and I paid it once and I did not get an argument, and she is in that room and she did not hear me say it and that is correct.*

*The year is the last of the years of the last times, and I said it out loud in front of eleven people, and nobody in that room can check what it is, and that is not a trick. It is the shape of the whole thing. I am the only witness to my own payment and I am the cheapest thing in this county to put a name on.*

*It is mine to write down and it is not mine to decide.*
