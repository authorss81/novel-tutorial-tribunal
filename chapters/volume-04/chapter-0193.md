# Chapter 193: Four People Who Have Each Said A Thing

The room above the chandler’s shop was cold on the second day of the forty-ninth week, because a fire in that room is lit when somebody in it is cold and nobody in it was, and there were about nine people in it and four of the nine were the bench.

They were doing the thing a chair had come out of the back of a room to give them two days before and had not been asked to do in nineteen years, and the reason they were doing it in a cold room was that a quorum is four and this district had four and none of the four had been asked.

Perrin Gant had the book open on the table at the page after the thirty-four names, because Osa Trin had put her thumb in it on the sixth day of the forty-sixth week and he had not moved it since, and the letter was face up on the page and there was a clerk’s name at the foot of it, and about nine people in that room could not read one word of it.

“Read it out again,” he said.

It is the second time in nineteen years that he has asked for a thing in that room to be read out again, and the second time is on the page after thirty-four names and is a letter, and the first time is a page with a question in it which is in the same book eleven pages further on.

Osa Trin read it out, standing up, with her thumb under the line, twice, and she did not correct a word of it, and Perrin Gant followed it with his finger on the page and could not read a line of it and has never been able to, and nobody in that room said one word about that, and it is the fifth time in six weeks and it is not going to be the last.

And then he shut the book, and put his hand flat on it, and said the thing that turned the morning.

“I am not going to answer it,” he said. “I am sixty-one and I have been a convenor for nineteen years and I have answered about four hundred things in this room and I have got very good at it, and I am going to sit down and tell you what I am not going to do and then somebody else can say the rest of it.”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“The letter asks which of the four hands carries the burden. I know which four. It is in the book and I have had it out twice and I have not read it and I have had it read to me and I know the four and I am not going to say them in this room.” He turned the hand over. “And I am not going to say the fourth one’s name either, and the reason is that the fourth one is standing in this room and has been for nineteen years and the three of us have spent nineteen years being careful about that, and I am not going to stop being careful about it in the twelfth week of a year in which a clerk in a city four miles off has asked us a question in a letter we did not answer.”

“Then we cannot answer it,” said Nevin Hake.

“We cannot answer it, and I am going to give this room two reasons, because there are two, and this room has only ever been given one.” Gant turned round in the chair. “The first is about the letter. A bench of four cannot un-swear a thing. We swore it. All four of us. And we would not want to. That is the reason that is about the letter and it is the whole of it and it is not a mystery, it is nineteen years of a room being comfortable.”

“And the other one,” said Wil Tamworth.

“The other one is that the fourth of the four has never been asked,” said Perrin Gant. “Not in nineteen years, not once, and I have sat in that chair for all of them and I have never asked her, and the reason I have never asked her is that I did not want to find out, and I am going to hand that to this room now because the letter is going to be answered in about nine months by a clerk in that office whether we answer it or not, and I would rather it was not filled in by a stranger about a person who has been in this room for the whole of it.”

---

The finding of the morning took about four seconds to say and is going to be true of for about a year, and a carter of forty-six said it standing up with his back to a cold grate.

“A witness is not four signatures,” said Nevin Hake. “That is what a bench thinks a witness is, because a bench is four people and four people sign things. I have been in this bench eleven years and I have watched four people sign a page and go out of the door, and that is not four witnesses. That is four hands.”

He put his hand on the cold grate and took it off again.

“A witness is a person who has said a thing in their own words and can be asked about it afterwards. That is the whole of it. That is the only difference between a signature and a witness, and it has got nothing whatever to do with reading, and I would like that said in this room this morning before anybody in this room uses the word reading again as though it were a way of saying frightened.”

Nobody contradicted him.

“We swore four of them on a page on the twenty-second day of the forty-third week and it went on that nail, and it is four sheets, and the fourth of them is in a hand that is not the bench’s, and a man of thirty-four in a counting-house four miles off has read all four and has written to us about that fourth one only, and he is right. The fourth one says the burden is named in four hands and four places and that none of the four has agreed with any of the others, and that is true, and it is the only true line on the whole of it, and it is the reason a stranger has written to us, and it is going to be the reason a clerk in that office enters one line against us in about nine months whether we like it or not.”

“Which we cannot stop,” said Osa Trin, from the door.

“Which we cannot stop. And that is the reason we cannot get a quorum by getting four people in this room and standing them up.” Hake put his hands in his coat. “Because three of the four of us would be saying the same thing we said in a book, and the fourth thing is in a stranger’s hand, and the man in that hand has not been asked what his hand was doing on our page, and he is not a person of this district, and there is no form anywhere in this county for asking a man who is not of a district what he wrote about a district that is of one.”

---

So they did it the other way round, and it took from about the second bell until about the fifth, and the fire stayed out and about nine people were in the room and four of them were the bench and none of the four wrote anything.

Hake said what he had seen on the first day of the forty-eighth week, in his own words, in about nine sentences. Wil Tamworth said what he had seen in this room nine days ago, and what he had not understood at the time, and why. Perrin Gant said what he had seen at the top of nine steps two days ago and why he had been the only one of the four who had not said anything at the time.

And Osa Trin was not asked, and came in off the door, and said what she had seen, and it is the fourth time in nineteen years she has said a thing in this room that nobody asked her for and the second time in a fortnight.

And then it was written down, and it is on a page of its own, in a second hand, and it is the fourth thing on that nail. There have been three things on that nail since the forty-third week — the lease notice, the notice of the fourth sitting, and the page the four of them swore — and there are four on it now, and Perrin Gant said so out loud before anybody put it up, because a nail in a room where three of the four people cannot read is the only clock in this district that anybody can keep.

> WITNESSED IN THE DISTRICT OF RYEFOLD, ON A DAY THE DISTRICT APPOINTED, BY FOUR PERSONS OF THE DISTRICT, EACH OF WHOM HAS SAID A THING IN THEIR OWN WORDS AND CAN BE ASKED ABOUT IT AFTERWARDS.
> I SAW A MAN OF THIS DISTRICT STAND IN A ROOM FOUR MILES OFF AND BE READ FOUR SENTENCES HE HAD WRITTEN AND NOT READ, AND HE TOLD THE ROOM HE COULD NOT READ THEM, AND THE ROOM ENTERED THAT HE HAD NOT CHECKED THEM.
> II SAW A FACTOR IN A GOOD COAT STAND IN A YARD IN THIS DISTRICT AND SAY THAT A ROOM DOES NOTHING WITH A TRUE THING, AND FOUR HUNDRED CARTS STANDING IN THIS DISTRICT AT THE TIME WITH NOTHING TO DO.
> III SAW A CHAIR COME OUT OF THE BACK OF A ROOM FOUR MILES OFF AND SAY THAT A ROOM CAN USE A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ WHAT THEY HAVE SIGNED, ONCE, AND THAT NOTHING IN THE PAPER STOPS A ROOM USING THEM AGAIN.
> IV SAW A MAN WALK SIX MILES UP A ROAD THAT GOES NORTH WITH A FORM IN HIS HAND AND A SHEET NONE OF US PAID FOR, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT ROOM ASKED HIM WHAT IT WAS FOR.
> NONE OF THE FOUR HAS READ THIS PAGE. THE FOURTH HAND NAMED ON THE FOURTH SHEET SWORN IN THIS ROOM HAS NOT BEEN ASKED WHICH HAND IT IS.

The hand that wrote it was the hand that reads out of this book and has never once written into it, and the sixth line is the only line in nineteen years that this room has put on a nail about itself that is a true thing it did not know.

---

At about the sixth bell the room emptied the way that room empties, which is four people at a time and no farewells, and the fire was still out and nobody thanked anybody.

And on the towpath at about the seventh bell, at the bottom of a path that is six miles in each direction, a woman of thirty-one put a basket down and said the one thing she had come to say, and then went up.

“When there was one of them there was one of them and I carried that one,” said Sena Ollet. “I did not know what was in it and I did not ask and I have said that out loud on a step in this district before and I am not going to say it a second time, so I am going to say the new one and it is one sentence. Whatever the four of them turn out to be, they are not going to be in a room on a day I am not going to. I go nine days in a cut and once a month the rest of the year. The day this matter has been given is in the other half of the year, and I will be on that path with a basket in it, and that is the whole of what any of them can say about me, and it is exactly the same as what was true when there was one of them instead of four.”

She shouldered the basket and went up the towpath.
