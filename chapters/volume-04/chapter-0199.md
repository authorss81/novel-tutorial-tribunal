# Chapter 199: He Says The Number Himself

The account sent its Chancellor again, and he came for the same reason he came in the forty-eighth week, and a district of six hundred people spent a fortnight deciding that he had not, and that fortnight is the only thing that has happened in this district that was not somebody’s work.

He did not come about the register. He came about a granary, and the granary is the thing a lease is priced on, and the lease’s discretion has run out and has not been exercised and has not been refused, and the account is nine thousand seven hundred pounds and its charge of four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year is due at the close of this grain year, and that is the same reason as last time and it is the only reason there is.

He is the office. The register is the office, and he has been both of them for nine years, and there is no clerk on the register and no form for asking to see it and no person in either of them that can be sent to a yard off a lane in a district, which is the whole of why he came himself in the forty-eighth week and the whole of why he came himself this morning, and it is not modesty and it is not a courtesy. It is that the account has never in nineteen years put a person anywhere near a district that is asking questions, and the only person it had to put there was the one who could not be sent anywhere else.

He was in that yard at about the second bell for about three hours and Marda Fen weighed what went over the beam and he wrote nothing down, and then he went up the lane and into a room above a chandler’s shop, which is the first time in nineteen years that a Chancellor of that account has been in that room, and there were about nine people in it.

Nobody sent for him. He came, and the district did not ask him to and could not have, and about four hundred carts in about nine hundred yards have had nothing to do for three weeks running and every one of them could have walked four miles off a road and not one of them was sent for anything.

He stood up in the middle of the floor and said the whole of it, in terms, with the figures, in about nine minutes, and he did not deny one of them, and Perrin Gant took out the bench's own book at some point and did not open it.

“Four lines,” said Ors Halm. “I am going to say four lines and then I am going to say what I have brought with me, and I am going to say the numbers slowly, and the man who found most of them is not in this room and I am going to say his name because he has not asked me not to.”

“Go on,” said Perrin Gant.

“One. There is a form. It is two sheets and it costs one shilling and fourpence a sheet, so two shillings and eightpence, and it is the price of the sheets and not the price of the thing, and it is the same in a hundred and one counties, and I did not set it, and I have argued against it twice in nine years and lost twice, and the second time I was told by my own office that I was not the office.

“Two. Nine hundred of them went out in this county. Three hundred and seventy came back with a name on them. Five hundred and thirty came back with nothing on them or did not come back at all, and of those five hundred and thirty, about three hundred and seventy came back blank and about one hundred and sixty did not come back at all. And every one of those five hundred and thirty is a person who did their job properly.”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“Three. Three hundred and seventy prizes are in issue in this county in this year and about nine thousand are in issue on the Shelf, and three hundred and thirty-nine of the three hundred and seventy are a man with one cart and thirty-one are a man with more, and every one of the three hundred and seventy has got a cart. That is the Chancellor's own number and I keep the register myself and I have no clerk on it and there is no form for asking to see it and there is no other count of it in the world.”

“Four. A sheet is valued at what it saves you and what it saves you is a load, and I have never in nine years been able to make that sentence mean anything, and a man in that district's own book has printed a load at two shillings and eightpence and a sheet costs two shillings and eightpence, so the number the register puts on a piece of paper is the number that district pays for a piece of paper, and the load is not in it. On my own scale a man with one cart moves about four hundred loads in a cut, which is sixty pounds, and sixty pounds is four hundred and fifty times two shillings and eightpence. Every one of the three hundred and thirty-nine pays the same, and five hundred and thirty of the nine hundred have got nothing at all for it, and a man with no cart is not in the scale in the first place, because the unit is a load and a load is a thing a cart does.”

He stopped, and there were nine people in that room and not one of them had written anything down, because three of the four of them cannot read what is written and the fourth can and has not been asked.

“And since I came about a granary I am going to say the other thing about it, because nobody in this room has asked me and I have been in it about two hours.” He did not put his hands in his pockets. “The discretion on that lease has run out. Nobody has exercised it. Nobody has refused it. It ran to the end of your cut and it stopped and nothing happened, and your district is still in that building, and the account has not moved on it in a month, and I am going to tell you why, and it is not kindness and it is not a favour and it does not help you this winter.

“To take a granary off a district the account has to put a person in the district. I have spent nineteen years making sure that it never does that, because a person who can be asked a question is a person, and an account that can be asked questions is a thing that can be refused. There is no clerk on the register. There is no form for sending anybody to look at a granary. There has not been one since before I came into this post, and nobody in that office has ever been asked to invent one, and I have not invented one either, and I have been asked twice in nine years and I have said no twice and I was right twice.” He looked at the door frame and then at the room. “So a reeve of fifty-four has been holding a granary and a bank of the second mouth for about a month on a word of her own and a door that shuts at the seventh bell, and the whole of the account’s protection of her in that is that nobody has ever had to look at her. That is not a right. There is no form for it and there is no line for it and I could not produce it in a room if you asked me to.”

Nobody in that room said anything, and a reeve of fifty-four, who had come up to say one thing about the beam and had stayed, said the thing she had come up to say and it was one sentence.

“Then put it in a book, because it is the only thing about this lease that has ever been said out loud anywhere and it has been said in a room by the man who could take it off me and not in a bin-board by me,” said Marda Fen, “and nobody is going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to be ruined by it, and I have had a granary for twenty-two years and I would like the reason it is still mine to be written down by somebody who is not me.”

“Now what I have brought with me.”

---

He put a register on the table. It was a book and it was about nine inches thick and it had been in a building in a city six miles off for nine years and nobody in this district had ever seen it.

“I have brought the register and I am giving it up, and I am saying the number out loud because nobody else in this county will, and there are about nine thousand of them in issue and about nine thousand of them stay in issue.”

Nobody in that room said anything at all.

“About nine thousand,” said Ors Halm. “In a hundred and one counties, at two shillings and eightpence, at the same price, in the same form, entered in a register I have kept myself for nine years with no clerk on it and no form for asking to see it. I have given you the book. I have not given you the nine thousand and I am not going to pretend to you that I have, and I am not going to be a man who hands over a book and lets a room think that something has been undone.”

“Where are the other nine thousand,” said Wil Tamworth, who is thirty-three and asks what a number means.

“In the same places they were yesterday, in a hundred and one counties, at a clerk's counter, in a book, in a hand.” He put his hand flat on the register. “And here is the part of it that I want said out loud in this room while there are nine people in it, and it is the reason I have not been redeemed by anything anybody has said to me in nine years: what I have given you is an office. It is one office. It is mine, it is the only one I have ever held, and I have held it for nine years because there is no clerk on it, and the reason there is no clerk on it is that a thing with no clerk on it cannot be asked a question by a person who is not in it. That is not a scheme. That is a shelf and a man and a habit, and I said that in a yard in the forty-eighth week and every word of it was true then and it is truer now that I have brought the book into this room.”

“And the price,” said Nevin Hake. “Two shillings and eightpence.”

“The price is a table of fees,” said Ors Halm, “and the table is not mine, and I cannot change it and I have never been able to change it, and I have argued against it twice and lost twice. I have brought you the register and I have not brought you the table, and I would like everybody in this room to understand that those are two different objects and that I have given you one of them and that the other one is in about nine hundred rooms in about a hundred and one counties and is a printed sheet that costs fourpence to copy.”

---

And then he said the last thing and it is the only thing he said in that room that was about himself rather than about a form, and he said it standing up, in the ordinary way, and nobody thanked him and nobody has ever thanked him and he is not going to be thanked and is not counting on it.

“I am not a good man and I am not going to become one this morning,” he said. “My first scheme is the two sheets and two shillings and eightpence and about nine thousand people who did their jobs properly, and it is on the page in this room in terms, and I have not denied a figure, and you may do whatever you like with it. And about thirty-four men in nine counties have given a second cart back in the last month, which is seventeen thousand loads, and your district was a hundred loads short of a cut that is over, and thirty-four is not a list, and there are about nine hundred men in this county and no list of any of them exists, and a man in a good coat has wanted one for four years and has thirty-four names off a trestle and I have wanted one for nine years and have never asked him for it, and there is no form for asking.”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“And here is the thing that everybody in this district knows and has not said, and I have read it out of a book in this county and I am not going to be a man who leaves it in a book.” He looked round. “If the sheets go and the price stays, then about three hundred and thirty-nine men in this county who have one cart each are still men with one cart each, and a load of carriage is still worth three shillings to a man who has a cart and nothing at all to a man who has not got one, and there is still no column anywhere in this county for a man who has not got one, and a remedy that undoes the scheme and does not undo the price produces a district where nobody can get a cart.”

He took his hands off the table.

“I did not say it in the yard in the forty-eighth week either, and about nine people were in that yard, and every one of them knew it, and I was one of the nine. I am saying it in a room this morning because I have got a book on the table, and because a thing is a different thing when it has been said out loud by the man it belongs to, and I do not know that it is, and I am not going to find out today.”

Then he said the thing that closed it, and it took about four seconds.

“Nobody in this room has said the other thing and I am not going to say it for you,” said Ors Halm. “The man who said it first said it in a yard in the forty-third week with a hub on his knee and about eleven people round him and he was not thanked then either, and he is not in this room, and he is in this district, and I have not sent for him and I would not have been entitled to.”

---

Nobody in that room said the other thing for about a minute and a half, which is not long, and then the bench of four did the only thing a bench of four can do with a room like that, which is that Perrin Gant shut the book he had not opened, and put the key in his pocket, which is a thing he does, and said that the register would go on a nail in that room and that a nail in that room had had three things on it since the forty-third week and would have six.

“It is not a letter,” said Osa Trin, from the door, where she had been standing for about nine minutes, which is a thing that happens in that room. “It is the sixth thing on that nail, and it is the first thing on it in nineteen years that anybody in this county is going to be able to put a name to, and I would like it read into a record this evening by a recordwright who is not of this district and cannot be a party to it, and I am not going to say whether I have asked her, and I have not, and she would come if she were asked and so would I, and that is the whole of what is useful about either of us and nobody in this room will get any more out of it tonight.”

At about the sixth bell a man of fifty-four went down the stair and out of a lane in a district of six hundred people, and he did not say one word about nine hundred doors, or a boy of nineteen, or a carter of thirty-four and a pound and five shillings, and nobody asked him about any of them, and that was correct.

And a reeve of fifty-four went back down the lane to a beam, and there were about four hundred carts in that yard with nothing to do and about seven thousand loads in the building and one pair of hands and about nine thousand pieces of paper in issue in a hundred and one counties, and the figure on the bin-board was the same figure it was yesterday.

And at about the seventh bell a register went on a nail in a room above a chandler’s shop, and it is the sixth thing on that nail, and there is a beam in the yard in the morning.
