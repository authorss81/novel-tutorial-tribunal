# Chapter 159: A Letter On a Coast Four Hundred Miles Away

Two hundred miles up the Saltglass coast there is an office with a printer in it, and in the office there is a clerk called Peth Ollam, and she is thirty-four, and she has been in the post nine years, and about half of what she does is putting things into other people’s bins.

The bundle came up the coast road in a cart on the seventh of the ninth tide, with a load of oil and nothing else in it, and it is still in the office because nobody has asked for it, and the date on the outside of it is the only thing on the outside of it.

It is three sheets. They are not on top of each other. There is nothing at the head of any of them saying that the three of them are about the same water, because there is no such sentence, and about a minute and a half was spent on each attempt to write one and about a minute and a half was spent stopping.

There is a name at the foot of the third sheet, in the first sheet’s writer’s own hand, saying that he asked three people for one thing each and was given three, and that he is not a party to any of them, and has no standing on that coast, and has no office.

Peth Ollam read the third sheet on the day it came and read it again about a month later and has read it about nine times since, and there is a question on it that she cannot answer out of the three sheets, and she has known that since the day it arrived.

It is this: **the third sheet says the three accounts are not joined. A person in this building who has never been to this coast is entitled to one account. He is going to be entitled to the other two the moment he has the first, because that is what the three of them are for and because nobody wrote a sentence saying they must be kept apart, only a sentence saying they were given apart. And three accounts of one wall in one bundle is one thing and the writer has told us in his own hand that it is not, and he is four hundred miles away and cannot be got at and did not say in the sheet what a person should do about that.**

She put the bundle in a bin on the day it came. She took it out about a month later and put it back. She has taken it out four times since the second of the fourth tide, which is the date on the third sheet, and it is not sealed and it is not in a locked room, and there are four people in that building and any one of them can ask for it and be handed it and copy it and send the copy to a coast.

And there is one more thing about the bundle that only she knows, and it is the reason she has taken it out four times and put it back four times.

**On the third sheet, at the foot, under the stranger’s name, there is a line that says a thing was asked of a person and was not asked, and that is the only line in the three sheets about anybody who is not one of the three.** It does not name the person. It does not say what was asked. It says the asking did not happen, and it says it in the middle of a record about eleven yards of a wall, and it is the only sentence in the whole of the three that was not written by one of the three people about their own work.

She worked out in about four days that she could not ask about that line, because the person it was about had said no, and a person who has said no is not a person a clerk may go and ask again.

Nobody in that building knows this. There is no entry anywhere saying that anybody declined. The three sheets are in a bin and the third one is a page about a wall and there is no column on it for a person who said no, and there was not one to write it in.

So she wrote a letter, and it is the whole of the fourth of the ninth tide’s work, and it went up the coast road in a cart in the morning with a load of oil, and the clerk who took it out on the shelf said the address was a shed and she said yes, and he said a shed is not a person, and she said no, and he said that is not my department, and she said that is exactly why it is going to the shed.

What she wrote was about nine lines and it was to a glassmaker of sixty-one at a bench in a shed two miles along that coast, and it said that a record had come into this office, and that the record was in three parts and the three parts were not joined, and that the writer of it had said so, and that the office was entitled to one account and would be glad of the other two, and that the office was not asking anybody to say that they had agreed with anybody, and that the office did not need any of the three of them to travel and would pay fourpence a sheet for a copy of anything anybody chose to send.

And it said, at the end, in the last line, which she wrote twice and then did not take out:

*If anybody in that building does not wish to answer this, they need not answer it, and it would help me if you would say so in one line, so that I can put it in this office’s book and stop asking.*

She read that last line four times before she sealed it.

Nobody in this world will ever know what was in that letter, or that it was written, or that the woman who wrote it understood precisely what she was doing and did it anyway. There is no System on that coast. There is no panel. There is no record of the four times she took the bundle out of a bin, and the bin is not locked, and the office is one floor above a printer and the printer is a man of about fifty who does not come out.

And in about two months a man with a brush in his cuff will come into a working shed two miles along that coast, because that shed is where the thing is, and everybody on four hundred miles of that coast would go to that shed and not to the woman at the second fire in it, and he will ask his question at a bench and he will be answered by a man of sixty-one who has been at that fire for forty-one years and who was asked on the fourth of the ninth tide of a tide before last whether he would like to be the one standing there, and who said no first and then said yes, and who has never once in his life been asked to hold a piece of anybody’s paper.

And she at the second fire will put the bar into the fire and will not turn round, and will not be asked anything, and will not be on anybody’s list of people who can be produced.

She is not on anybody’s list. Nobody put her on one. She was never on one.
