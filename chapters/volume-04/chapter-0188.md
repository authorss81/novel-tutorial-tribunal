# Chapter 188: He Is Right About It

The account sent its Chancellor.

That is the whole of why a man of fifty-four was standing in a yard off the lane at the near end of the quay, which is the yard the weighers stand in and is about thirty feet across and has a beam at one end of it, at about the fourth bell on the fourth day of the forty-eighth week, and it is worth saying exactly, because a bench of four in this district had spent three days working out what a run-out discretion looks like and the answer turned out to be a man in a good coat with a book under his arm and a reason nobody in that district had thought of.

There is no clerk on the register. There has never been a clerk on the register. The account does not send anybody to look at a granary; there is no form for it and nobody in that office has ever been asked to invent one. And the granary is the thing the lease is priced on, and the lease's discretion has run out, and about seven thousand loads are in it, and if the account sends a clerk then the clerk is a person who can be asked a question by a district of six hundred people, and the account has spent nineteen years not putting a person anywhere near a district that is asking questions.

So it sent the one man in that building who cannot be sent anywhere else, because he is the office, and he came six miles up a road that goes north in a carriage and he was in the yard at about the fourth bell, and Marda Fen gave him the number and he wrote it down himself with a pencil, and about nine people were in the yard because about nine people are always in that yard.

He did not come about the register. He did not come about the three hundred and thirty-nine. He was in a yard off the lane at about the fourth bell on the fourth day of the forty-eighth week to look at seven thousand loads of grain, and everything that was said in that yard for about an hour came out of a question about a load, and that is how it happened, and nobody in that yard planned it and one man in it did the planning and is not going to say so.

---

Marda Fen asked him the question at the beam, standing up, with the lamp in her hand, and she asked it in the ordinary way because she has not in her life kept a figure back off a quay.

“What is a load worth.”

“Three shillings,” said Ors Halm.

“That is the register's figure. I have had it off a man in a good coat in this lane twice and I have had two and eight off a book six miles up the road that goes north, and those are two numbers and I have known both of them since the sixth day of the forty-third week and I have never once known what the fourpence is.” She did not take her hand off the beam. “Are they both yours.”

“Neither of them is mine,” said Ors Halm. “The three shillings is the register's published scale of carriage and it is a number about what a load costs to move, and the two and eight is the county's entered rate and it is a number about what the county pays, and they have been different for longer than this account has been an office and I did not set either of them and I cannot change either of them. If you want the fourpence, it is the difference between what a load costs and what a load is entered at, and there is a difference in every county I have ever been in and it is not a secret and it is not a crime and it is not in anybody's pocket.”

He put the pencil in his coat.

“I will say the four things I say, because I say them everywhere and I have never once been asked to stop, and I have said four of them in a yard in this district once and every one of them was true then as well. I am not going to pretend to be a different man because somebody has read a page out in a room.”

---

“One. I did not write the register and I did not set the price, and the price is a clerk's fee for two sheets and it is the same in a hundred and one counties, and I have argued against that price twice in nine years and lost twice, and the second time I was told by my own office that I was not the office.

“Two. Your district applied for the year and got it, and the year was worth four hundred and sixty-two pounds, and the carts to move a cut in a hard season cost four hundred and sixty, and the district spent the year on the carts, and I could not have made that year do anything else and neither could any man in that yard, and the difference between the two figures is two pounds, and two pounds is not a crime and it is not even a mistake, and I have not said one word to anybody in this district about that difference until this morning and I am not going to now.

“Three. The man who bought a sheet bought a place in a queue, and there is no queue, and the queue is the first bad week. There has not been a bad week. If you take the sheets away in a bad week then the bad week is still a bad week, and six hundred people are in it together instead of one at a time, and the one at a time is the man who could not get a cart, and I have met him and he is in this county and he is not a criminal and he has four people.

“Four. I put nine thousand people into a winter meal last year, and I did it with about four hundred carts I hired at a rate I set myself, and nobody has been able to argue me out of that in about four years.”

He took his hands off the trestle.

“Every part of those four is true and I have never said a false one in a room and I am not going to start. And I am going to say a fifth thing, which is the only one of the five that is mine, and I have said it once before in a yard in this district and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to soften it because somebody has read a page out in a room four miles off and got a sentence in it that I have not read.”

He looked round the yard.

“Nine hundred sheets went out in this county. Three hundred and seventy came back with a name on them and about nine thousand are in issue on the Shelf. Five hundred and thirty came back blank or did not come back at all, and every one of those five hundred and thirty is a person who did their job properly, and the whole of it is standing on top of correct pieces of work by people who had no way of knowing what they were part of. And I keep the register myself because there is no clerk on it, and there has not been a clerk on it in nine years, and there is no form for asking to see it, and there is nobody to ask. That is not a secret. It is a shelf and a man and a habit, and I have never once pretended it is anything else.”

Nobody in that yard said anything, and about four hundred carts were standing in it doing nothing, and a dog came round the wall the way a dog does.

---

Then the man with the basket asked the only question anybody asked him all morning, and it was nine words, and it was not a good question, and it was the right one.

“If it is all true, what do we do.”

Ors Halm looked at him for about four seconds and then said the thing that made the afternoon, and he said it flatly, and he was not being cruel and he was not being clever and he was not enjoying it, and nine people in that yard heard it and not one of them repeated it afterwards.

“You do nothing about the sheets,” he said. “That is my answer and it is the answer of the account and you may write it down. Take the sheets away in a bad week and the bad week does not go away, and six hundred people in this district are in it together instead of one at a time, and one at a time is the only thing that has ever kept a district like this one alive, and I have watched it keep about nine thousand people alive in this county in one winter with four hundred carts.” He put his hands behind his back. “I am not telling you the account is right. The account is a thing that holds money and I have never in nineteen years been able to make it be right about anything. I am telling you that I have thought about the question you have just asked me for nine years and the answer I have is that the price is the price and the sheets are the sheets and a remedy that takes the price away and leaves the sheets is not a remedy, it is a change of weather.”

Nobody asked him what the remedy was that would do both. He was not asked it and he would have answered it and neither of those things happened, and the reason neither of those things happened is in a minute four miles off and in a yard in the forty-third week and it is not going to be settled in a yard off a lane in this district in one afternoon.

---

And that is the shape of it, and it is the shape of the whole of this, and it is worth putting down flat because about nine people were in a yard and every one of them understood it at the same moment and not one of them could do anything with it.

He is right.

About the price. About the two pounds. About the queue and the bad week and the man who could not get a cart. About four hundred carts hired at a rate he set himself. About the five hundred and thirty clerks who did their jobs. About the register being a shelf and a man and a habit, and about there being no clerk and no form and nobody to ask. He is right about all of it, in a yard, in the ordinary way, and he has never said a false thing in a room, and he is fifty-four, and he did not write the register and did not set the price, and he is not the villain in this and he is not going to be.

And it is still a harm.

Those two things are not in tension. That is the finding, and it is not a victory and it is not a defeat and it is not a man being shown to be wrong, and it can be stated in one sentence by anybody who was in that yard: a price can be correct and a transfer can still happen, and the correctness of the price is not a defence of the transfer, and the transfer is not an argument against the price. He is right and it is a harm and the room cannot make them one, and no room in this county is going to make them one, and the reason is not stubbornness. The reason is that they are two different questions and only one of them has a person in it. *Is the price right* has a man in it and he is in the yard and he answers it. *Who is the price right for* has nobody in it, because the price is right for a man with a cart and there are about three hundred and thirty-nine of those in this county and every one of them has one, and the answer to the second question is not a sentence. It is a column, and there is no column.

That is the whole of what a yard off a lane in a district of six hundred people can do with the truth, and it did it in about an hour, and it is a working finding and it is not a moral, and it is going to be in a minute or it is not going to be in anything.

---

Nobody said the next thing. That is worth saying exactly, because there were nine people in that yard and the next thing was in every one of them.

The next thing is this. If the sheets go, and the price stays, then about three hundred and thirty-nine men in this county who have one cart each are still men with one cart each, and the district is still a district where a load of carriage is worth three shillings to a man who has a cart and nothing at all to a man who has not got one, and there is still no column anywhere in this county for a man who has not got one, and the four hundred men in about nine hundred yards have not got a second cart between them this week, and three of them within a mile of that gate had one cart in the forty-third week and would have had two by the end of it. And a remedy that undoes the scheme and does not undo the price produces a district where nobody can get a cart, and everybody in that yard knew it, and not one person said it, and the man who said it first said it in a yard in the forty-third week with a hub on his knee and about eleven people in the room and he was not thanked then either and he is not in this yard and he is in a house off the Nethergill road with about two hundred carts and a bag of forms and no list.

Iven Brack is in this district. He was not in that yard. He was at the weighbridge, with a cart, on the fourth day of the forty-eighth week, doing about forty loads of his own, and he has been one of the three hundred and thirty-nine for two years and he paid a pound and five shillings on the fourth day of the forty-seventh week for a hundred numbered sheets in a yard, and he is not in this yard, and he is not going to be told any of this, and he will be told none of it and will not ask, because that is the arrangement.

And at about the seventh bell a man of fifty-four got into a carriage in a yard off a lane in the district of Ryefold and went six miles back up the road that goes north, and he was in this district for about three hours and he did not say one word about a hundred sheets or a hundred doors or a boy of nineteen or a sentence in a minute, and nobody asked him about any of them, and that was correct, and nobody in that lane has any idea how to write down what he came for, and nobody asked him, and he did not say.

And the reeve went back to the beam. There were about four hundred carts in the yard with nothing to do and about seven thousand loads in the building and one pair of hands, and the figure on the bin-board was the same figure it was yesterday, and a carter came off the weighbridge at about the seventh bell and asked her whether she would weigh by the second bell in the morning, and she said she would not, and he said he would be back at the first, and that is what happens at the end of an afternoon in a yard off a lane in this district about nine times a week, and the man who asked was doing his work and the woman who answered was doing hers and the answer was no and it was not a complaint.
