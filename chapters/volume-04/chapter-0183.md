# Chapter 183: The Book Of Entries

The account's own office is on the ground floor of a building in a counting-house lane in a city six miles up the road that goes north out of Ryefold, and it has a counter with a window in it, and behind the counter there are nineteen shelves of returns that nobody in that building reads as a set of anything.

The book of entries is not on the shelves. That is the first thing to say about it and it took Amos Kellock about four minutes to say it standing up at a counter on the fifth and sixth days of the forty-seventh week, with a leg gone from the hip down and his weight on the sill, and nobody in that building said one word about the leg then or afterwards, which is the fifth time that has happened in this district.

“It is in the other room,” he said. “It is a book and it is about nine feet long and it is kept by district, and every district in this county that has ever had a claim against the account has a page in it, and the page has three things on it and there are no more than three things on it anywhere in the book and there is not a fourth column and there has not been one since before I came.”

He turned round one of the books and did not open it, which is a thing he does.

“Three things. The loads. The rate. The claim. And the rate is the county’s entered rate, which is two shillings and eightpence a load, and I am going to say the next thing in this room and then I am not going to say it again this year. The register has a published scale of carriage on it. That is three shillings a load. Those are two different numbers and both of them are right and about four hundred people in this county have been shouting at each other about them for two years and neither of them has been shouting at the other one.”

“Because the county enters it at what the county pays and the register values it at what it saves,” said Ilyan Vester.

“I do not know why and I have stopped trying to know why,” said Amos Kellock. “I have a card on the wall that says the rate is the county’s entered rate and that is the end of what the card says. I have been in this building two years. I have read about nine thousand returns. Nobody in this building has ever been asked why there are two rates and I have asked nobody because I did not know there was a question.”

---

They got it into a room on the sixth day of the forty-seventh week, and it took four sheets at fourpence a sheet to copy, which is sixteen pence, and the sixteen pence came out of a reeve's apron because the account had been at nothing since the sixth day of the forty-third week.

Here is the page, and it is the whole of it, and it is a page of a book in a building six miles up a road that goes north and the account is a party and has never been asked to show it in a room:

> RYEFOLD, SECOND MOUTH.
> LOADS ENTERED 3,400. AT THE COUNTY’S RATE OF 2/8 THE LOAD.
> CARRIAGE PAID AGAINST THE ACCOUNT, 3,000 LOADS AT 3/0. SHORT 100 LOADS.
> CLAIM OF A CARTER, 406 LOADS, 54/2/8. PAID 9/0. THE REMAINDER TO A YEAR.

Four lines. Kellock read them out in a lane on the second day of the forty-seventh week and did not read them twice, and the finding came in the middle of the second line and it took him about nine seconds to say and about four days to be sure of.

“The loads are three thousand four hundred,” he said. “That is what went out of that granary in the cut. The rate is two and eight, that is the county’s, that is what the county pays. And the carriage that was actually paid for was three thousand loads at three shillings, which is the register’s figure and not the county’s, and there is a hundred loads on the second line that were never paid for at anything, and they are the only hundred loads in this book that anybody has ever been short.”

He stopped and started again, and he did it in the flat voice he uses for a card on a wall.

“Now. The book has three columns. Loads, rate, claim. And in about nine hundred pages in that book there is not one word about a sheet. Not one. No sheet, no holder, no queue, no use, no bad week, no grant, no priority, no name of any man who has ever been given a piece of paper with a word at the top of it in this county. The register and this book are about eleven feet apart in this building and the man who keeps one of them has never asked to see the other and I have never been asked to bring them together and there is no form anywhere for asking.”

Nobody in the lane said anything for about eleven seconds.

“That is the whole of what I have got,” said Amos Kellock, “and I have been carrying it for four days and I could not get it into a sentence and I have now got it into a sentence and the sentence is that a register and a book of entries are in the same room and one of them has never heard of the other, and that is not anybody keeping anything from anybody. That is two books.”

---

And then the other half of it, on the sixth day of the forty-seventh week, in the lane outside the same building, with about nine people standing in it and the reeve's sixteen pence in the reeve's apron and a man of thirty-four who had walked six miles to be in it.

Kellock did the comparison out loud. He did it because he is the only person in that building who has ever been asked a question by a man he had just met and had answered it, and he did it slowly, and he said the working as he went, and he got it right the first time and said that he had got it right the first time.

“Here is the comparison and it is one comparison and I am not going to add anything to it. A sheet costs one shilling and fourpence. Two sheets cost two shillings and eightpence, and that is the price of the thing that lets a man carry grain in a district and be paid out of it before anybody else, and it is the same in a hundred and one counties and it is the price of the sheets and not the price of the thing, and the man who set it did not set it and has argued against it twice and lost twice and says so out loud.”

“That is three shillings a load,” said Nevin Hake.

“No. Wait.” Kellock put his thumb on the sill. “I have got it and I want to do it properly. A sheet is valued at what it saves. That is what it is for. A sheet lets you carry a load in that district ahead of everybody else, and a load is worth what a load costs to move, and the register prints that figure and the figure is three shillings. So the register's own number for a sheet is one load. Not two shillings and eightpence. One load, at three shillings, because that is what it saves you.” He looked up. “And a load in this district's own book is two shillings and eightpence, and a sheet costs two shillings and eightpence. So the register's own valuation of a piece of paper, translated into this district's own book, comes to exactly the price of the piece of paper. And the load is not in it.”

He put that on the sill and left it there for about four seconds.

“And the two rates differ by fourpence a load. Four pence. Which is the price of reading and copying one sheet at that counter. I have worked that out twice and it is right both times and I have no idea whatever what it means and I have been sitting with it for two days and I have not been able to leave it, and I am putting it on the page because if I do not put it on the page it will be the only thing I have thought about in two years that has not gone into a book.”

Nobody wrote it down. Gant wrote it down, in the bench's own book, in his own hand, with the date, and could not read what he had written and did not ask for it to be read back, which is a thing he has not done before.

---

Then the other half, said by a carter, in a lane, standing up, to about nine people, and it took about four minutes and nobody interrupted him and that has not happened in this district before.

“Now the part that is not a sum,” said Iven Brack. “Listen, because I have been one of the three hundred and thirty-nine for two years and I am the only man in this lane who can say the next part and say it properly.

“On the register's own scale a load is three shillings. My cart does about four hundred loads in a cut. Four hundred loads at three shillings is sixty pounds. And the sheet cost me two shillings and eightpence.” He put his hand flat on the wall. “Sixty pounds is two shillings and eightpence, four hundred and fifty times over. Four hundred and fifty. Not four hundred and forty-nine and not four hundred and fifty-one, and I have done it twice on the back of this wall with a nail and got the same figure. A sheet is worth four hundred and fifty sheets to me and nothing at all to a man who has not got a cart, and we both paid the same two shillings and eightpence, and the reason is not that one of us was cheated. The reason is that the thing I can use and the thing he cannot use are priced in the same unit, and the unit is a load, and a load is a thing a cart does.”

He took his hand off the wall.

“And this district spent a year of its protection on somebody else's carts, and I have said the number of that out loud in this yard twice and I am not going to say it again. And the person who paid for it is six hundred people. And the people who got the carts are four hundred men in about nine hundred yards, and about three hundred and thirty-nine of them have one cart each and thirty-one have more, and every one of the three hundred and seventy has got a cart, and there is not one of them in that number who has not got one.” He looked round. “So the register's own scale has priced a piece of paper in loads, and a load is a thing a cart does, and a man with no cart is not in that scale at all, and there is nobody standing in this lane who is a man with no cart, and that is why nobody in this lane has ever been able to say the next sentence.”

He said the next sentence.

“A piece of paper in this county is priced at what it is worth to a man who already has a cart. That is the whole of it. That is not a complaint about anybody in this lane and it is not a lie and nobody has to go round and be told. It is a fact about how a register is written, and it would be exactly the same fact in a hundred and one counties, and there is not one man in this county who is allowed to say it is a crime.”

---

The agent came out at about the seventh bell to say four things, and he said them the way he has said them four times in a year, standing in a doorway with his hands at his sides, and he is fifty-one and has been in that post nineteen years and is the man entitled to speak for the account at a sitting, and he is not a villain and he did not lie and he conceded nothing and he was not a bad man for any of it.

Ansel Cork said that the account kept a book and produced it on the day, and that the book was the account's own and was not privileged, and that any party might ask for any page of it on the day of a sitting at fourpence a sheet, and that the district had asked for a page and had got it and had paid sixteen pence for four sheets of it, and that this was the arrangement and had been the arrangement for nineteen years.

He said that the register was not in that building and was not the account's, and that the account had no power over the register and had never claimed any, and that the price of a sheet was a clerk's fee and was the same in a hundred and one counties and had nothing to do with the account and nothing to do with the scale of carriage and nothing to do with this district.

He said that Ryefold's account was nine thousand seven hundred pounds and that its charge of four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year was due at the close of every grain year and did not stop, and that it had not stopped through the cut and would not stop for this, and that the district had never been credited with the year it had been given and never would be, because the year had been spent and a spent year is not a thing that comes back.

And he said that the account's position had not altered, and that the account was not a party to any ill will, and that the account would prefer that district to apply again and be heard, and that the account had put nine thousand people into a winter meal last year and had the receipts, and that it was the fifth time it had said that in about a year and it would keep saying it.

Then he said the thing he had not said on the three previous occasions and he said it flatly and without any weight on it at all, and it was the fourth true thing, and it is the only one of the four that was about the book.

“I have read the four lines your boy has read,” he said. “And I have had the page in my hand and I have had about four minutes with it, and I am not going to tell this lane that the hundred loads are not in the book, because they are in the book, in your district's own words, on a line the district has paid sixteen pence to copy.” He looked at the four of them. “And I am not going to tell this lane that it matters, and I want it noticed that I have said that in that order and that I have thought about the order for nineteen years of saying things in rooms. It is in the book. Whether it is in a column is a different question and that question is not mine and the account has never been asked it and I am not going to be the one to start asking things about a book that is not the account's.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody said he was a villain and nobody has ever said either of those about him and it is not likely that anybody ever will.

---

At about the ninth bell he was in a cart shed behind a granary with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and there is a thing worth putting down about what he did with the four days, because it is the fourth thing in a row this district has done that was correct and it is the first one of the four that was his.

He had known on the second day of the forty-fifth week, on a towpath at the first bell, in about nine seconds, standing at the top of a milestone with his weight against it, that the whole of what was wrong with the three hundred and thirty-nine was that a piece of paper was priced in a unit that only four hundred men in this county could use. He had said it in a lane the day after and nobody had put it in a book, and it had taken a boy of nineteen four days and four lines and a thumb on a sill to put it into a sentence that would go into a room four miles off on the first of the forty-eighth week, which is in two days — and it was not his sentence and it was not his comparison and the working was not his, and he had spent four days not being able to help, and that was the correct way round, and there is no part of this in which anybody did anything wrong.

And he did a thing at about the seventh bell that nobody had asked him for, which is that he asked the boy at the counter how long the account's own office takes to get a thing into a book in a building this district cannot reach and back out of it again, and Kellock said nine months and gave the reason, and the reason was that it is the time the thing takes and it is not a delay and it is not a punishment and it is nine months, and there are two nines in that book now and he is not going to be the one who sorts them out.

---

At about the ninth bell Marda Fen was on the beam with a lamp and about four hundred carts in the yard behind her doing nothing, because the cut is over and the next one is not for weeks, and she had seven thousand loads in that building and one pair of hands and a bin-board with a figure on it that was the same figure it had been since the sixth day of the forty-third week, which is nothing.

“Vester,” she said, without stopping. “Hold that end and do not talk to me for about nine minutes.”

He held that end for about nine minutes. It is a beam and a weight goes on it and comes off it and the number goes into a book that has three columns in it, and the number that went in at about the ninth bell on the sixth day of the forty-seventh week was a hundred and forty loads at the county's entered rate of two shillings and eightpence, which is eighteen pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence, and the reeve said the figure out loud as it went on, the way she says every figure, and the figure is in a book in a building six miles up a road that goes north and it will be there in nine months and in nine years and nobody in this district will ever be told it is there.
