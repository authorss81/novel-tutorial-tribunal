# Chapter 157: The Difference Between Those Two Things

Iven Brack had one cart, a yard at the top of a lane two streets off the quay, four people, and a wheel off.

He was thirty-four and he had had the cart nine years and the yard eleven, and he was doing the wheel himself on a trestle at the fourth bell on the third day of the forty-second week, with the hub on his knee, and he said, without looking up, that if the man on the towpath was the one who had carried Sena Ollet’s basket twice then he was welcome to sit on the far bench and not to touch anything.

“Thank you.”

“Ilger,” he said, and put his hand up without turning round. “Ilger Rusk. Sit there. I am going to say nine things to you in about four minutes and eight of them are going to be about me and the ninth one is going to be about your book, and I do not know that you have got a book.”

Ilyan Vester sat on the far bench and did not say that he had one.

“I have been in this county about three weeks,” said Ilger Rusk, “and I sell carriage priority, and I have a licence for it and a book for it and I am not a criminal and I have never once been in a room where anybody shouted, and I would like to say the rest of that out loud, because I have said the first half of it in my own head about four hundred times this season and it has not once helped.”

Nobody had asked him to.

“No,” said Ilger Rusk. “Nobody has. That is what I would like you to take away from this yard. Nobody in this county has ever asked me what I do.”

He was thirty-eight and he had a good coat and a bag of forms and a way of standing in a yard that made the yard smaller. He put the bag down on the wall.

“Here is what you have bought, and you have bought it from me, and it is in your own hand, and here it is.” He took a folded sheet out of the bag and gave it to the man on the trestle, who turned it round on his knee, and then put it down on the trestle beside the hub, and neither of them read it.

> WHERE A FORBEARANCE IS RUNNING IN A DISTRICT, THE HOLDER OF THIS MAY CARRY GRAIN IN IT AND BE PAID OUT OF THAT DISTRICT BEFORE ANY OTHER CLAIM IS ENTERED AGAINST IT.
>
> ENTERED ON THE SHEET: THE HOLDER’S NAME, THE DISTRICT, THE DATE, AND THE PLACE, IN THE HOLDER’S OWN HAND. TWO SHEETS. ONE SHILLING AND FOURPENCE A SHEET.

“That is the whole of it, and it is two sheets, and it is two shillings and eightpence, and that is the price in every county in this Shelf and it is the price of the sheets, and there is no discount and there is no negotiation, and there is no other man in nine counties who will sell it to you cheaper, because there is no other man in nine counties who is allowed to sell it to you at all.”

“Why did I want it?”

“Because you have one cart and I have two hundred and the district has three thousand loads to move in a cut, and a man with two carts is a man with two carts in a bad week, and I said so to you at the weighbridge on the eighth day of the forty-first week and I said it for nothing because I wanted to sell you a piece of paper, and you said you would think about it, and you thought about it for a day and came back, and that is the whole of how this happened and nobody did anything to you.”

“I did not know what I was buying.”

“No,” said Ilger Rusk. “You did not. I will tell you exactly what you were buying, because I am not going to be the one who says it afterwards. You were buying a *use*. The district got a year — that is what a forbearance is, it is a year of the account not running — and that year is worth four hundred and sixty-two pounds to about six hundred people, and it is in a form and a form is a thing nobody in that district can read, and the year came out about a fortnight ago, and by the time anybody found out it was gone the carts had all been hired and the hiring had gone on the account and the account is nine thousand seven hundred pounds and the interest on it is four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year and will be four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year next year, and the same, and the same, and the same.”

Nobody said anything at the end of that sentence and the wheel was still off.

“Now your sixty-one loads,” said Ilger Rusk. “You moved them under it. The register does not know about that and I do not know about that and nobody knows about that but you and the man who weighs you. And if anybody ever did know, then this sheet says you get paid out of that district before anybody else does, and what your sixty-one loads cost them is nothing at all, because the use of a forbearance on a load is worth three shillings, which is what a load costs to move, and sixty-one loads at three shillings is nine pounds and threepence.”

“Nine pounds and threepence,” said Iven Brack, from the trestle, not moving the hub.

“That is what I sold you for two shillings and eightpence, and I have never had anybody ask me that sum before, and you have just said it out loud in your own yard.”

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and a dog came round the wall.

And then Iven Brack said, “Say the rest of it.”

“There is no rest of it.”

“There is a rest of it and you have had it ready since you got here because you have had it ready since the eighth day of the forty-first week.” He put the hub down. “Say the part where you tell me what happens to me when that sheet is worth nothing.”

Ilger Rusk looked at a cart wheel for about four seconds.

“Nothing happens to you,” he said. “That is the part I have never been able to say out loud in a yard. Nothing happens to you. The sheet is a true sheet and you paid for it and it is in a book in a building four miles from that court and it is a legal sheet and nobody in this world can take it off you, and the district cannot go to court and say the register sold a thing the district did not know it had, because the district did know it had, in the sense that the district applied for it, and the district cannot say the register sold a thing without telling anybody, because that is what a sale is.”

He took the folded sheet off the trestle and put it back in the bag.

“You will be first in the queue in a bad week. That is what you have bought and there is no bad week coming that anybody knows of, so you will be first in a queue that has not happened, and I have sold about three hundred and seventy of those in this county this year and about nine thousand on the Shelf, and I have never once in fourteen years had a man come back at me and say *you cheated me*, and I would like somebody in this yard to understand that that is not because I am careful. It is because I did not need to be.”

---

Brack got the wheel back on about twenty minutes later and put the hub in with the heel of his hand and stood looking at it.

“I did not buy the paper,” he said.

“No.”

“I bought the winter.” He said it the way a man says a thing he has been carrying about four hundred yards. “That is what that man said to me at the weighbridge. He said, you want the winter, and I said I want the winter, and I did not know there were two words for it.”

“There are not two words for it. There is one word and it is *priority* and I will not pretend to you that I said winter on purpose. I said winter because it is the word that works on a man at a weighbridge in a bad week and I have been selling at weighbridges for fourteen years and that is the whole of my trade and the whole of my education.”

“You did not have to lie to me once,” said Iven Brack.

“Not once,” said Ilger Rusk. “I want that said in this yard by you and not by me, because in about a year somebody is going to make a speech about what men like me do, and the speech will be about lying, and I would like the yard to have heard me say the true thing first.”

He picked up the bag and put it over his shoulder and stopped at the wall, because there was a man sitting on a bench who had said nothing for twenty minutes.

“You have not asked me a single question,” he said. “You have been sitting on that bench for twenty minutes and I have just said the price of my entire life to a yard and you have not asked me one thing. Why not.”

“Because I already knew what it was and I have been waiting to see whether you would say it,” said Ilyan Vester.

“That,” said Ilger Rusk, “is the most frightening thing anybody has said to me in a yard, and I am going to walk away now, and I am going to come back through this district in about three weeks, and I am going to knock on that door — the second one, the reeve’s — and I am going to ask her for a list. And I want you to have known that I was going to say that in this yard and not in an office, so that you cannot tell anybody afterwards that I only said it to be difficult.”

He went out through the gate.

---

Brack sat on the bench for a while with the wheel off again, because he had put it in wrong.

“Two hundred and ninety-four loads left,” he said. “I have got one cart and I have twenty-six days and I have got about four people at that house and I have got a quarter of a pound of flour and a quarter of a pound of meal, and I am not asking you for anything and I am telling you because there is nobody in this district I would tell.”

“Why me.”

“Because you are a stranger and because you have been sitting on that bench for twenty minutes and you did not once look at that gate to see what came in.” He turned the hub. “And because you have got the look of a man who does not have a district, and I know what that costs, because I have got no money and I have got one cart, and those two are nearer the same thing than anybody will tell you.”

He said, “Here is what I am going to do, and I am telling you so that you cannot tell me I did not say it. I am going to take that sheet to that bench at the start of next week and put it on the table in front of those four people, and I am going to say that I paid two shillings and eightpence for a piece of paper that made my loads get paid first out of a district that got a year of protection and spent it on carts, and that the year is over.”

He put the hub in and hit it once and it went true.

“And I know what happens after that. Sixty loads of mine come out of that district’s account first, and there are about six hundred people in it, and sixty loads at three shillings is nine pounds and threepence, and that is not money to anybody. And if I go to that bench and put that sheet on their table, then either they take it off me or they do not, and if they take it off me then I have not got a second cart by the thirtieth and neither has anybody else in this district, and a man with a second cart in a cut is a man who can take nine hundred loads and a man who cannot is a man who can take four hundred, and the four hundred are the ones that rot.”

He looked at the gate.

“So I am going to do it, and I want you to be in that room, and I am not asking you to be useful. I am asking you to be the one person in that room who has read nothing and can still hold a piece of paper.”

Ilyan Vester sat on a bench in a yard in a district of six hundred people and had a piece of paper in his coat that he could not read, and had had one for four days, and had been asked to hold it by a man he had met once, and he said:

“All right.”
