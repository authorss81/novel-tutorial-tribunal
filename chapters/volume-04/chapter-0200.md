# Chapter 200: A Person Doing Their Work

The first day of the fiftieth week was a working day like every working day there has been in this district since the second day of the fortieth, and nothing in it was a resolution, and the only thing that happened that had not happened before was said in a yard off a lane at about the fourth bell by a carter who did not know he was doing anything.

The beam was working at the second bell because there are about four hundred carts in this district and about seven thousand loads in that building and the carts are not going to wait for a year, and a reeve of fifty-four was calling loads off it in a voice that about two hundred and forty people in this district heard her use once in the forty-fifth week of this year and are not going to hear again, and the figure on the bin-board was the same figure it was yesterday and it will be the same figure tomorrow and it will be a different figure at the close of this grain year, when four hundred and eighty-five pounds falls due on nine thousand seven hundred pounds and the district is at nothing and has been since the sixth day of the forty-third week.

A bench of four had been in the room above the chandler’s shop since the fourth bell and had come out of it at the sixth, four at a time and no farewells, and a convenor of sixty-one went down the lane first again, which is the second time in ten days and was noticed by four people and was not asked about.

---

The turn of the day was at about the fourth bell and it took about eleven seconds and it was this.

A carter of about thirty stood at the second door with a cart behind him and asked the reeve a question he had never asked anybody, which is that why a man who cannot read has a line on her wall.

“Because a basket is the only thing in this lane that puts his name in a place where anybody can find it,” said Marda Fen, without stopping, and she said it to the carter and not to the man, and the man was four feet away and heard all of it.

Nobody in that yard said anything for about four seconds, which is not long.

“Right,” said the carter, and went and got his cart weighed.

And the reeve went back to the beam, and about nine people were in that yard, and about four hundred carts in about nine hundred yards had had nothing to do for three weeks running and had not been sent for anything by anybody, and a man with a basket was in a cart shed behind the granary with a leg that had not been his own since a morning four hundred miles and some weeks behind him, and it had not done anything at all in a week, and he had expected it to do something the day a year went out of him and he has not told anybody that he expected it to and he is not going to.

---

The bench had a register on the table in the middle of the morning and nobody read it out, because a register is a thing a county keeps and not a thing a bench does, and the woman who read things for that district read four lines of it out of a book of her own at about the fifth bell into a record that a woman of twenty-eight was keeping on a table in another room four miles off, and it took about nine minutes, and the woman of twenty-eight wrote down what was said and not what was in the register, and both of them knew that was the difference and neither of them said it.

The one thing that happened in that room about the register is that a convenor of sixty-one asked for one line of it read back to him.

“Which one,” said Osa Trin.

“The one about the two hundred and forty-first.”

“It is not in that book,” said Osa Trin. “That book is a register of sheets. Every line in it is a number and a rate and a date and a heading, and there is no column in it for a person and no column in it for a household, and the two hundred and forty-first name in this district is in a reeve’s hand at the bottom of a list of her own in a different ink, and the other one of the two is in a book six miles up a road that goes north, and neither of them is here.”

“So read me the first line of it,” said Perrin Gant.

She read him the first line of it and it took her about four seconds and it was a number and a rate and a date and a heading, and there were nine people in that room and not one of them could have told her afterwards what the date was.

“Thank you,” said Perrin Gant, and shut the book, and put the key in his pocket, which is a thing he does. “That is three times in nineteen years that I have asked for a page in this room to be read back to me and the third one has cost nothing, and I am sixty-one, and I have asked for a thing since the forty-seventh week and this is the first one that has come back with nothing in it, and I would like it written down that I asked.”

Nobody thanked him and he did not expect to be and he had said in this room ten days ago that nobody does, and that is a sentence about a bench of four and not about a register, and it is the whole of what happened in that room between the fourth bell and the sixth.

At about the sixth bell four persons of a district came down the lane from a room they had not been in, three of whom had come four miles the day before and one of whom had come on a day of his own with a wheel in his shop, and none of them was thanked, and in about nine months a clerk in a counting-house in another city four miles off is going to have to enter one line against this district and this bench is not going to be able to say which of the four of them it belongs to, and the fourth of them has never once been asked by anybody what any of it was for, and she is the only one of the four who could have told him and she is not going to be asked again before then.

At about the seventh bell a basket went up a towpath that is six miles in each direction, with the district’s own papers in it and a number on the outside of it in a reeve’s hand, and a basket that is not full weighs about eleven pounds, and a woman of thirty-one carried it up and did not read one word of it and has not asked what is in it and says so before anybody asks her, and what is in it will be in a book in about nine months if the office ever puts it in one, and there are two nines in that building and neither of them is a punishment.

And at about the same hour a reeve of fifty-four read the board before she shut the second door, which is what she does, and the figure on it was the same figure it was yesterday and there was a name against it that has been on that wall in chalk since the fourth day of the forty-first week, and there is a man in this district who has not worked out what it is for, and a carter in that yard asked her about it this morning out of nothing, and she answered him, and the man was four feet away and heard the whole of it, and not one person in that lane has said a word about it since and there is not going to be one.

Behind her the second door of the granary came to, and there were about seven thousand loads in it and one pair of hands, and a lease at a fortieth of what was in that building in the forty-third week, which is about a hundred and seventy-five loads, and which nobody has renewed and nobody has refused and which nobody has been told is there, and a sheet with a blank in the third line about the size of a hand went back four miles in a woman’s coat in the first half of this week and is in a book six miles up a road that goes north, and a register about nine inches thick went on a nail in a room in this lane this morning and is the sixth thing on it, and not one of those five things is a person and four of them are in this district and one of them is not and cannot be.

And in a lane four miles off, on a step with his back against a wall, a man of thirty-one wrote on the eighteenth page of a penny exercise book, and the two lines at the end of it are the two lines that are on the other seventeen.

*This district ate this winter and about six hundred people in it are not going to be hungry and about four hundred and eighty-five pounds a year is going to fall due at the close of this grain year on an account of nine thousand seven hundred pounds and the district is at nothing. Nobody is going to be ruined. That is the good news and the person who arranged it did not arrange it for us and has said so himself in a room in his own words with the figures, and he has given up a register and there are about nine thousand of them in issue in a hundred and one counties and he said the number out loud himself, and a hundred of a thing cost a carter of thirty-four a pound and five shillings in a yard nine weeks ago and is the reason about nine hundred doors are not going anywhere, and the ninety-one of them are in a bag in a yard with a bit of string on it and about four hundred carts in about nine hundred yards have had three weeks to pick them up and not one of them has been asked and he has said out loud in a room that he is not a good man and is not going to become one this morning, and he is not, and nobody in that room believed the second half of it and he did not ask them to. A remedy that undoes a scheme and does not undo the price produces a district where nobody can get a cart, and a Chancellor said that in a room yesterday in his own mouth, and nobody in that room had said it before him, and the man who said it first said it in a yard in the forty-third week with a hub on his knee and was not thanked then and was not in the room.*

*There is a line in a book six miles up that road which pays a year to somebody and does not say who, and I have paid it, and I can say what it was and I cannot say which year, and the person who would know is the only person in the world who is standing in a lane four miles from a counter and has not got it. Nobody has asked. Nobody in that building has ever asked a person a question they did not already know the answer to and that is the whole of what that office is, and it is not a conspiracy and it is not a villain, it is a counter with a window in it and a boy of nineteen in it who is two years in the post and will not write a sentence down unless somebody in a good coat makes him.*

*And it is on a nail, and the nail has six things on it, and one of them is a page with a hole in the third line about the size of a hand, and I am the one who put the hole there and I could fill it and I did not, and I will not fill it, and nobody is going to ask me to, and a woman of fifty-four said no in a doorway ten weeks ago in front of about nine people and she was right and I have not gone near it since.*

*It is mine to write down and it is not mine to decide.*

And then a book was shut on a name, in a room above a chandler’s shop, on a page with four lines on it and a blank in the third of them about the size of a hand, and the blank stayed a blank, and the book was shut. And six miles up a road that goes north there is another book, and it is not shut, and it has a line in it that pays a year to somebody and does not say who, and two people have read it in this county and not one of them has said a word about it out loud, and nobody in this district is going to be told what is in it for about nine months, and there is a form that goes up a towpath in a basket and there is no column anywhere for a form that was sent and nothing came back of it, and a woman of fifty-four has wanted that column for nine years and has not got it and is not going to this week.

And a basket went up a towpath, and a door was shut at the seventh bell, and a beam was there in the morning.
