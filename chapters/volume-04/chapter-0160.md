# Chapter 160: Four Lines in a Yard

The room above the chandler’s shop has a table in it, four chairs, a fire that is lit in the forty-fourth week and not before, and about nine people in it at the fourth bell on the second day of the forty-third week, which is a day the bench appointed.

Perrin Gant wrote the page. That was the arrangement and nobody argued about it, because there is a form for a thing like this and it is one sheet, and the person who swears a page for a bench is a person of the district and there are four of those and one of them can write.

He wrote it in about an hour and a half, standing up, at the table, in his own hand, and he would not have anybody help him, and Osa Trin read it back to him twice before he put his name to it and did not correct one word of it, and when he asked her whether it was right she said, “It says what you want it to say,” and he said, “Then it is right, because I cannot read it, and you can, and you have told me the truth twice, and I am sixty-one and I have got a bench to run.”

The page went on the nail at about the sixth bell with a nail through the top left corner, and there is a copy in the bench’s own book and a copy going down the towpath in a hand, and neither of them is a copy in a building four miles from a court, and nobody in that room said a word about that and everybody in that room knew it.

Perrin Gant came down off the chair and stood at the table and put his hand flat on the bench’s own book, which was shut, and said, “That is the first thing this district has ever made about itself. Not about a road and not about a rate and not about a man. About itself. I have been convener nineteen years and we have made notices and we have sent for things and we have written to a city twice in my life, both times asking for money, and neither of those is a page.”

He said, “It will not go anywhere. I know that. It is going to a counter in a city and the man at the counter is going to read it in about a minute and put it in a bin, and it is going to be a thing in a bin for four years, and I would like this room to understand that I know that and that I have done it anyway, and that the reason is not courage. The reason is that if nobody ever writes one down then in about four years the only account of this district in the whole of Veyra is a number in a register, and a number in a register has got a name on it and it is not ours.”

He shut the book and put his hand off it.

“I will say one more thing and then I am going to sit down, because I am sixty-one and there is a fire in this room and four chairs and about nine people in it, and a man of my age who has been standing up for an hour and a half has earned a chair. Nobody has to agree with it and nobody has to write it down. I have been convener nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years put a thing on a table that I did not know was true, and that is not a virtue, it is a habit, and habits are the only thing any of us actually has, and this district has had four of us for about four hundred years and three of the four have never read anything and one of the four has read everything and been paid nothing, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of this room and there is nobody in it to fix it and there is nobody in it that it is anybody’s fault but mine.”

He sat down.

Ilyan Vester said the number out loud once, under his breath, to a fire, and about two people heard it and neither of them said anything about it then or afterwards.

Twenty-two days on this shelf. A page, and four pence a sheet, and a bench of four in a room above a chandler’s shop, and about nine people, and the cut in about three weeks, and no money in the district at all.

The page is four sheets and it is the first page that district has ever made about itself, and it says this, in a reeve’s and a convenor’s hand, and in a stranger’s hand at the foot of the fourth:

> THE SUBJECT. A forbearance granted to the district of Ryefold in the thirty-ninth week of last year, one grain year, and its use and the sale of its use.
>
> THE CONDITION. Where a forbearance is in force in this district, a person may carry grain in it. A holder of a printed sheet called a prize may carry grain in it and be paid out of this district before any other claim is entered against it. The district applied for the year. The district did not know it had ended until the tenth day before this page. The sheets were sold in this district by a licensed factor, and about three hundred and seventy are in issue in this county and about nine thousand on the Shelf.
>
> THE BURDEN. Named on the fourth sheet, in full, at the foot, and the naming is the whole of what is asked.
>
> THE WITNESS. Four persons of this district, a granary reeve, a woman who carries the numbers of this district to a counter six miles away and has not been asked what they are, and a carter with one cart. The last of these has a sheet and has offered to put it on this bench’s table and has not been asked to and has not.
>
> THE REMEDY. That the use of an exception granted to a district may not be made the consideration of a loan, and may not be sold, and where it has been, the use is void and the loan stands and the loan is not taken out of the district by the loss of the use.
>
> THE EXIT. A person who has bought a sheet may have the money back, or keep the sheet, and a person who has not bought one loses nothing, and this is written on the fourth sheet in the hand of the man of twenty-six who wrote it, and the bench has asked him whether he will stand behind it, and he has said yes, and his name is on it.

And it does not say one word about what happens to a man with one cart in a bad week, and that is because nobody in that room could write it, and two of them tried.

At about the seventh bell the light went and the page was on the nail and the room was warm and about nine people were in it, and the fourth bell of the day was over and the ninth was two hours off, and the System put a thing in front of Ilyan Vester that he had asked for and could not have got any other way.

**THE FOURTH SHEET. THE BURDEN OF THE CLAIM IS NAMED IN FOUR HANDS AND FOUR PLACES AND NONE OF THE FOUR HAS AGREED WITH ANY OF THE OTHERS. THE PAGES SAY SO IN THEIR OWN WORDS AND THE PAGES ARE RIGHT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER AN EXCEPTION THAT HAS BEEN SOLD IS A THING THAT CAN BE SOLD BACK.**

He read it. It told him what he already knew and it certified nothing and it joined nothing, and it had taken him nineteen days and four people and nine pence a day to get a line out of the interface he has been carrying since a city four hundred miles behind him, and there is no part of him that is satisfied, and about nine people in this district have watched something happen to him on a quay, on a shingle, on a towpath and in a cart, and not one of them has ever mentioned it.

---

The third day he walked six miles up the road that goes north out of Ryefold, to a yard behind the granary account’s own office, and there were about nine people in that yard and a table and a man of fifty-four standing at the far side of it with his hands flat on it.

Ors Halm did not sit down for the first eleven minutes and Ilyan Vester found out afterwards that this was the ordinary thing and that about four people in the county knew it and that all four of them were factors.

“I have got four lines and I am going to say them and I am not going to argue with you about any of them,” said Halm, “and then there is a fifth thing I am going to have to say to you and I am not going to like saying it, and I would like it said while there is still daylight in case either of us turns out to be a coward about it.”

“Say them.”

“**One. I did not write the register and I did not set the price, and the price is a clerk’s fee for two sheets and it is the same in a hundred and one counties, and I have argued against that price twice in nine years and lost twice, and the second time I was told by my own office that I was not the office.**”

“That is true.”

“**Two. Your district applied for the year and got it, and the year was worth four hundred and sixty-two pounds, and the carts to move a cut in a hard season cost four hundred and sixty, and the district spent the year on the carts, and I could not have made that year do anything else and neither could any man in that yard, and if you had asked me in the thirty-ninth week of last year what I thought they would do with it I would have said a cart, and I would have been right, and I would have been as disappointed as you are now.**”

“That is true as well.”

“**Three. The man who bought a sheet bought a place in a queue, and there is no queue, and the queue is the first bad week. There has not been a bad week. If you take the sheets away in a bad week then the bad week is still a bad week, and six hundred people are in it together instead of one at a time, and the one at a time is the man who could not get a cart, and I have met him and he is in this county and he is not a criminal and he has four people.**”

“Also true,” said Ilyan Vester, “and I have written it down, and I want you to know that I have written it down, and I am not going to pretend I have not.”

Halm looked at him for about two seconds.

“**Four. I put nine thousand people into a winter meal last year, and I did it with about four hundred carts I hired at a rate I set myself, and nobody has been able to argue me out of that in about four years, and I would like somebody in this yard to put that in front of the three hundred and seventy cartering men standing on this quay before anybody in this yard uses the word *free* about one single thing I have done.**”

He took his hands off the table.

“That is the four lines and every part of them is true and I have never said a false one in a room and I am not going to start.”

Then the rendering came up on him, standing in a yard, with his hand on a bench that was not his, and it came up the way it comes up, at the height and at the distance it has been at since the third of the second month in a city four hundred miles behind him: the boards, the table the size of a table a hand could go on, the lamp with the wick down, the window with weather on the outside of it that was not this weather, and four people at the table and one of them about forty-four with her back to him.

It did not move, and the leg went at the same moment from the hip down, all of it, and there were about nine people in that yard and about four of them were looking at him, and a man of fifty-four on the far side of a table watched a man of thirty-one put his weight against a bench and stay there for about four minutes and did not say one word about it, and had a factor’s memory of about nineteen years of people doing things on a bench.

Nobody mentioned it then or afterwards.

---

“Five,” said Ors Halm, when the four minutes were up. “Now the part I do not like.

“In the thirty-first week of last year this office wrote to about nine hundred district clerks in this county. One circular. One sheet. And it said, and I am going to read it to you because I have it in my own coat and I have not sent it to anybody in two days, and that is because I sent it in the thirty-first week of last year and I have been not sending it to anybody ever since and that is a sentence I am not proud of.

“It said this, and that is all of it, and every word of it is true, and there was no threat in it and no offer in it, and I have had it looked at by two men who hate me and they could not find a lie in it.

> TO THE CLERK OF THE DISTRICT NAMED ABOVE. A DISTRICT WHICH HAS A FORBEARANCE RUNNING MAY CERTIFY A HOLDER OF ITS PROTECTION. PLEASE RETURN THIS SHEET FILLED IN, OR NOT, AS THE CASE MAY BE. THE ACCOUNT IS AT THE DISPOSAL OF ANY HOLDER NAMED HERE. THE REGISTER IS KEPT IN THIS CITY AND IS NOT KEPT IN YOUR DISTRICT.

“Nine hundred went out. Three hundred and seventy came back with a name on them. Five hundred and thirty came back blank, or did not come back at all.

“And here is the thing, and it is the fifth line and it is the only one I have ever had to say in a yard and I have never said it in one before. Five hundred and thirty clerks did that piece of work properly. They got a sheet, they did not have a district that had ever heard of it, they had a bench of four and no office and nobody to ask, and they put nothing on it and sent it back, and in four hundred and thirty cases they did not send it back and simply put it in a drawer. And every one of those five hundred and thirty is a person who did their job. None of them did anything wrong. Not one. And the whole of my scheme is standing on top of five hundred and thirty correct pieces of work by people who had no way of knowing what they were part of.”

“Then you did it,” said Ilyan Vester.

“I did it,” said Ors Halm. “There is a minute. There is a date. There is my name on it and there is a name under mine and that one has left the office, and I am not going to pretend to you that the fact that a person is under mine helps either of us, and I have watched about nine hundred men in this county get away with things on exactly that argument and I have never once accepted it and I am not accepting it from myself this morning.”

He put the circular down on the table between them, face up, and did not push it across.

“That is the first thing I have done that anybody in your bench could have found out in about four years if anybody had asked the right question in a county clerk’s room, and nobody did, and the reason nobody did is on that yard behind you and it is four hundred years old and it is in nobody’s book, and your friend at the counting-house with the bad hand has been in a building with no name on the door for nine years with every printed word in this county on nineteen shelves and not one person has ever walked in and asked to read one, and a man of thirty-four walked in a fortnight ago because he could not read a slip and was too proud to go back.”

He straightened up.

“Now you are going to ask me what I am going to do about it, and I am going to tell you the true answer and I am telling you in a yard on a day like this because in about three weeks there are going to be nine hundred of my circulars in a new edition with a different question on them and I have not decided what the question is and I have had eleven days and I have not slept much and I have nobody in this county I can ask, because the men who can read my circulars are the same five hundred and thirty and they are all still there and they are all still right.”

He said, “There are three hundred and seventy in this county and about nine thousand on the Shelf, and I am one man in a building with a window, and I am not going to tell you I am going to stop, and I am not going to tell you I am going to carry on, and I am going to give you the one number that is the whole of my trade and I would like it written down in whatever you are writing in.

“Three hundred and thirty-nine of the three hundred and seventy are in the hands of a man or a woman with one cart. Thirty-one are in the hands of a person with more. And the reason I know that to the person is that I keep the register myself and I do not have a clerk on it, and if I die it goes to my successor and my successor has never been in a yard.

“That is the whole of Ors Halm and I have said it in one place in about eleven minutes and I have not said one word of it that is not true, and you are welcome to take all of it into a county that has never had it in a yard, and I would like to know what you are going to do about the five hundred and thirty.”

“Nothing,” said Ilyan Vester.

“Right,” said Ors Halm. “Good. I have been waiting since the second of the third tide for a man to say that to me and I did not know it was going to be a man from a coast with a bad leg and a page I cannot read. Do you want to know what I think the answer is?”

“Yes.”

“I think the answer is that the district stops applying for years,” said Ors Halm, “and I think the district will not stop, because a district with a year in it can put nine hundred people into a winter meal and I have the receipts and you do not, and that is not a threat, it is the reason I am not frightened of you.”

He picked up his coat.

“And one more thing and it is the last and it is not for the book.” He got as far as the gate and turned round. “Your bench has sworn a page. In nineteen years that district has never made one. Do you know what it is for?”

“To be owed something.”

“It is not,” said Ors Halm. “It is for being *legible*. Your district has just become the first thing in this county anybody can read from end to end, and about nine months from now a clerk in my office will read your page and write to a clerk in another county and ask whether your district knew about the year, and you will not have to answer, because the page will answer for you while every one of your four are in a room. That is what the page is for. I did not want it and I am not going to pretend I do not know what it is.”

He went out through the gate and left the circular on the table, face up, and nobody in that yard picked it up for about four minutes, and then a factor of about fifty picked it up, folded it once, and put it in a bag, and did not read it.

---

At about the fifth bell he was at the top of the lane with a leg that had not been his own since the fourth of the third tide at Thornglass, and there were about nine people on the quay and a man unweighing a cart, and nobody in that district has ever mentioned it to him and nobody ever will.

Sena Ollet went up the towpath at the sixth bell with a basket and a sheet in it, and the sheet was the third copy of a page sworn in a room above a chandler’s shop, and it would be in a building four miles from a court in about three days, and the district was paying fourpence a sheet out of nine pounds, and she was paid three shillings a week in the cut and nothing the rest of the year, and the cut was in about three weeks and there was no money in the district at all.

She had not been asked what was in it and nobody was going to ask her, and she said so to nobody, and she carried it, because carrying it was the whole of what she was for, and she had been carrying numbers up that towpath for nine years and had never once been told what one of them was, and the basket was about eleven pounds and she carried it up for eleven hours and she got back at the ninth bell and the leg that was in his was not hers and she did not know he was sixty feet behind her for the first two miles.
