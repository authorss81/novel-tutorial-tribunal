# Chapter 172: Three Shillings

The first day of a cut in Ryefold is a morning in which about sixty carts stand on about eleven hundred yards of stone quay and about forty of them do nothing at all, and this has been true every cut in this county for as long as anybody in it has been alive, and the reason is not a shortage of carts. It is that the carts are there before the grain is, and the grain is not there yet, and the men who own the carts stand in the rain with their hands in their coats and wait for a reeve to call a cart, and the waiting is the day.

Nobody has ever put that in a book. It is the ordinaryest thing in the district.

At about the fourth bell on the first day of the forty-sixth week, Marda Fen had about a hundred and forty loads over the beam and about two hundred and sixty waiting in the granary, and four of the carts on that quay were the district’s own, and three thousand four hundred loads had to go out of that building before the last day of the forty-sixth week, and about three hundred of them were going on the four, and about three thousand one hundred were going on hired carts, and three thousand loads of that hire were paid for, and a hundred loads were not.

She said all of it out loud, standing at the beam, in the working voice, because four carters and a boy were standing there and she has not in her life kept a figure back off a quay.

Then she said the two figures that have been said on this quay before and are going to be said on it again, in this order, because she has decided they will be said in that order from now on: “A hundred loads at three shillings a load is fifteen pounds, and there is nothing in the account, and the cut began at the second bell this morning, and I have said it and I am not going to pretend I have not.”

---

The hundred loads were in the granary and not on the quay, which matters more than it sounds. A hundred loads of the district’s own grain sitting in a bin in a building at the end of a quay is not a shortage of anything. It is grain. It has a value and the register’s own scale sets it at three shillings a load, which is fifteen pounds, and the district owes fifteen pounds of hire it has not got for exactly those loads and about nothing at all for the other three thousand.

And then a carter said the thing that made the whole of the day, and he said it without raising his voice, standing at the back of his own cart with a strap in his hands, and he was thirty-four and he had said a price before a fact in this district once already and had not been thanked for it then either.

“A hundred loads is a quarter of a cart,” said Iven Brack. “That is all it is and every man on this quay knows it and not one of you has said it. A cart in a cut does about four hundred loads and this cut is seven days, so a hundred loads is a quarter of a cut, and a quarter of seven days is a day and three quarters. You have four carts of your own and they have got three hundred loads between them and you have not got a day and three quarters to give them, and I have got a cart and I have not got anything on it this morning, and there are about forty carts on this quay standing in the rain doing nothing. One of us could have that bin empty before the seventh bell and it would be the whole of a day and three quarters out of his seven.”

“Say it as a day,” said Marda Fen. “I want it as a day and not as loads. I have had a hundred loads described at me four times this morning and I have a bin with a hundred loads in it and I have not got a day anywhere.”

“Right,” said Iven Brack. “A cart does about four hundred loads in a cut of seven days. That is about fifty-seven loads a day. The register says a load is worth three shillings, and that is the register’s own published scale and it is the only price of a load in this county that anybody has ever printed. Fifty-seven loads at three shillings is eight pounds eleven a day, and that is what my day is worth, and I have never once had it in my hand or in my mouth or in a book, and I have worked it out twice in a yard in about four minutes this morning and I got the same figure both times and I would like the room to know that I have never had anybody check it and I have never had anybody tell me I am wrong.”

Nobody said anything.

“A day and three quarters of mine is fifteen pounds,” he said, “because a hundred loads at three shillings is fifteen pounds and a quarter of seven days is a day and three quarters, and those are the same sum twice and I did not know they were the same sum until I said them out loud in the order I have just said them. That is what a day of mine is. Fifteen pounds. And the only price of a day anybody writes down in this district is ninepence a day, in chalk, on the outside wall of the second door, and it is there for a man with a basket, and it is two hundred and twenty-eight to one. That is the figure. I have said it in a yard and I would like somebody to write it down and I would like it written down wrong if nobody can do it better.”

---

He did not want to give the day, and he said so, and then he gave it, and the two of those are in the same four minutes and about nine people heard both of them and nobody has put either in a book.

“I do not want to give it,” said Iven Brack. “I am not being noble about it and I would like it noticed that I have said that out loud before I have said anything else, because after it has been said the other way I will not be able to make anybody hear it. I am saving for a second cart. A man with a second cart takes about nine hundred loads in a cut and a man with one takes about four hundred, so a second cart is about five hundred loads and about seventy-five pounds, and I have been saving for one since the spring before last and I have about a quarter of it, which is a hundred and twenty loads and about eighteen pounds, and I have counted it more times than any man in this district has counted anything. And there is no money in this district to finish it with and there is not going to be any this year, so the only way a man in this lane gets a second cart is that somebody in this lane gives him a hundred and twenty loads for nothing. And I have not got a hundred and twenty loads for nothing. I have got a day and three quarters. If I give it this morning then a quarter of my second cart goes on a bin of the district’s grain at the end of the first day of the cut, and I will be a hundred and twenty loads short of a cart in the second week of the forty-seventh instead of in the first, and that is the whole of what is being asked of me and I have said it out loud.”

“Then do not give it,” said Perrin Gant, from about thirty feet off, in the voice he uses for a bench, which is not much louder than the voice he uses for a man on a towpath. “Nobody in this lane has ever asked you for a day and this is not the morning we start.”

“I know,” said Iven Brack. “It is a day and three quarters and it is the first morning of a cut I have had this month and I am not asking for a day off, I am asking for a day on, and there is a difference and there is not one person on this quay who knows the difference, including me, until this morning.”

He put the strap over his shoulder.

“Right,” he said. “Twenty-five loads. And I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to stop talking, and I have thought about it and I am not going to pretend I have not thought about it.”

Nobody stopped him.

“Keep the list,” he said. “Of who did this morning. Put it somewhere a person can read it and not somewhere a person has to be able to read it. Because in about a month there is going to be four hundred men in this county who gave a morning to something and there is not going to be one sheet of paper in the world that says so. And I have been one of the four hundred for two years, because I bought a sheet off a man in a good coat, and I did not read it, and I do not know what it says, and I have worked it out since that it is worth about sixty pounds to me on the day it is worth anything, and sixty pounds is not here, and the list of who gave a day and a three quarters this morning is going to be one name long, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it and I have said it.”

Gant looked at him for about nine seconds and then went and got the board off the wall and turned it round, and chalked on the back of it, and the front of that board is a wage line and what went on the back of it on the first day of the forty-sixth week was not a wage.

> WHO GAVE A DAY. THE FIRST DAY OF THE FORTY-SIXTH. ONE HUNDRED LOADS OFF A QUAY IN A DAY AND THREE QUARTERS. ONE CART. NOT PAID.

One name went on it in about four minutes, and it took four minutes because Gant asked her twice whether the day was a day and three quarters and because she had to read his own figure back to him off the back of his own hand. Perrin Gant wrote it out standing up on the step of the second door and he could not read his own hand, and Osa Trin read it back twice and did not correct one word, and when he asked her whether it was right she said, “It says what you have got,” and he said, “Then it is right, because it is all I have got,” and he copied it into the bench’s own book with the date, which took him about nine minutes and which he has never once done for a list before.

And the list is one name long, and there are about four hundred carters in about nine hundred yards, and that is the whole of the arithmetic of the first day of the forty-sixth week.

He did it because a carter asked him to, out loud, in front of about nine people, in the ordinary way, and Gant is a convenor of sixty-one who has been convener nineteen years and who has never once in nineteen years had a person in his district ask him for a thing and then not take it back.

---

The hundred loads went over the beam between the fifth bell and about the seventh, off one cart, and it was done inside a day and three quarters by a man who is not on the district’s board and is not paid by the district and is not owed anything by anybody for it, and the district’s four carts went on doing the district’s three hundred, and about forty other carts stood on that quay in the rain the whole of the time and went home and came back and did not do anything about it, and not one of them was asked and not one of them has ever been asked for anything by anybody.

And the district is fifteen pounds short instead of a hundred loads short, and that is the whole of what happened on the first day of the forty-sixth week.

The day’s number, and it is the same number as yesterday’s and it will be the same number tomorrow and it is a wall with a chalk on it, and it is the only instrument in that district that has ever made anybody a person in a record:

> VESTER. NINE PENCE. THE FIRST OF THE FORTY-SIXTH.

A hundred loads, a day and three quarters, eight pounds eleven a day, fifteen pounds for the day, two hundred and twenty-eight to one, and one name, on the back of a wage board in chalk, and a page in a book in a room above a chandler’s shop that three of the four people in that room cannot read, and that nobody has asked to have it read, and that there is nobody in the world who can be asked whether any of it is right.
