# Chapter 189: What The Book Says Now

The fifth day of the forty-eighth week was a working day like every working day there has been in this district, and about four hundred carts were in the yard doing nothing, and a bench of four met in a room above a chandler's shop at about the fourth bell because it is the day the bench meets and it has met on that day since before anybody alive, and about nine people were in it and two of them were not of the district and had come for the noise, and four of the nine were the bench.

He asked for the book to be read out and Perrin Gant said no, and the reason he gave is worth setting down because it is the first time in nineteen years that a convenor of sixty-one has refused a thing in that room and given a reason that is not about the reading.

“You read it,” said Perrin Gant. “And I am not going to sit in front of it. I have had that book read to me in that chair for nineteen years and I have never once asked for it, and the reason I have never asked is that a convenor is a person who is responsible for what is in the book and does not need to see it, and if I ask to see it then I am a man in a room being shown his own handwriting, and there are four people in this room who can read my handwriting and three of them would not say a word about what they found and I would spend the rest of my life not knowing which of them was being kind.”

He put his hand flat on the shut book.

“You read the last six weeks. All of it. And read it out of the book and not out of your head, because a number out of a head in this room is how a book came to say *no advice was given* about four hundred decisions in nine counties, and I am not going to have that done in this room in my nineteenth year.”

So Osa Trin read it out, standing up, with her hand on the back of the chair, and she read it twice, and she did not correct a word of it, and about nine people in that room got back to the weigh afterwards in the ordinary way, and she was not thanked, and she has not expected to be in nineteen years, and that is the arrangement of this room and it is not going to change this week, and what came out of about twenty minutes is this, and every line of it is correct.

> THE THIRD DAY OF THE FORTY-SEVENTH. THE LEASE DISCRETION FOUND TO HAVE RUN OUT, AND NOBODY HAVING EXERCISED IT, AND THE GRANARY HELD ANYWAY ON THE WORD OF A REEVE OF TWENTY-TWO YEARS AND ONE DOOR. NOBODY EXERCISED IT AND NOBODY REFUSED IT AND NOTHING WAS TAKEN. CORRECT.
>
> THE THIRD DAY OF THE FORTY-SEVENTH. A PERSON OF THE DISTRICT OFFERING, IN A LANE, WITHOUT BEING ASKED, TO MAKE AN APPLICATION HE CANNOT READ AND ANSWER FOR SIX HUNDRED PEOPLE IN A ROOM FOUR MILES OFF, AND THE PRICE OF IT NAMED BY HIM BEFORE THE FACT, AND THE READER OF THE DISTRICT DECLINING TO SIGN ON THE GROUNDS THAT A DISTRICT WITH ONE READER IN IT IS NOT A DISTRICT. CORRECT.
>
> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-SEVENTH. A HUNDRED NUMBERED SHEETS PRINTED AT THREE HUNDRED PENCE, PAID FOR BY A CARTER OF THIRTY-FOUR OUT OF HIS OWN, ON THE GROUNDS HE GAVE OUT LOUD AND WHICH WERE A PRICE HE ALREADY KNEW, AND A FIGURE OF TWO POUNDS FIVE THAT WAS SAID IN A YARD IN THE FORTY-THIRD WEEK REPEATED IN THAT YARD AND CORRECTED IN IT, IN FRONT OF NINE PEOPLE. CORRECT.
>
> THE FIFTH AND SIXTH DAYS OF THE FORTY-SEVENTH. THE ACCOUNT'S OWN BOOK OF ENTRIES READ, BY DISTRICT, BY LOAD, AND BY CLAIM, AT THE COUNTY'S ENTERED RATE OF TWO SHILLINGS AND EIGHTPENCE, AND THE FINDING THAT IT HAS THREE COLUMNS AND THAT NONE OF THEM IS A SHEET, AND THAT THE REGISTER IS ABOUT ELEVEN FEET AWAY IN THE SAME BUILDING AND HAS NEVER BEEN ASKED TO SEE IT. CORRECT.

“Go on,” said Nevin Hake, who was in that room as a person of the district and not as a carter, and who had been in a room four miles off four days earlier and had not slept.

> THE FIRST DAY OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH. THE PAGE READ OUT IN NUMBERS IN A ROOM FOUR MILES OFF, AND A SENTENCE ENTERED IN A MINUTE THAT A PIECE OF PAPER IN THIS COUNTY IS PRICED AT WHAT IT IS WORTH TO A MAN WHO ALREADY HAS A CART, AND THE MATTER LISTED FOR A DAY IN THE NEXT HALF OF THE YEAR AT THE REQUEST OF THE PERSON CARRYING IT, WHICH NOBODY IN THIS COUNTY HAS EVER DONE. CORRECT.
>
> THE SECOND DAY OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH. THE SECOND WEEK RUNNING IN WHICH FOUR HUNDRED CARTS IN THIS DISTRICT HAD NOTHING TO DO, THE WEEK BEFORE IT BEING THE FIRST SUCH WEEK IN SEVEN YEARS, AND THE FINDING THAT A NUMBER CAN BE READ OUT IN A ROOM AND CANNOT BE HANDED TO ANYBODY, AND THAT THE ONLY THING A NUMBER CAN BE TURNED INTO IS A LIST OF PEOPLE, AND THAT A LIST OF PEOPLE IS NINE HUNDRED DOORS. CORRECT.
>
> THE THIRD DAY OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH. ONE HUNDRED NUMBERED SHEETS ENTERED AGAINST THE ACCOUNT BY A PERSON OF THIS DISTRICT, AND A BOY OF NINETEEN SAYING OUT LOUD, TWICE, IN A LANE SIX MILES UP A ROAD, THAT THEY ARE BOTH THE FIRST THING IN FOUR YEARS THAT WORKS AND THE SAME THING AS WHAT THEY COST. CORRECT.
>
> THE FOURTH DAY OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH. THE ACCOUNT SENDING ITS CHANCELLOR TO A YARD TO LOOK AT SEVEN THOUSAND LOADS BECAUSE THERE IS NO CLERK ON THE REGISTER AND NO FORM FOR ASKING, AND FOUR THINGS SAID IN THAT YARD, EVERY PART OF WHICH IS TRUE, AND A FINDING THAT A PRICE CAN BE CORRECT AND A TRANSFER STILL HAPPEN, AND THAT THE TWO ARE NOT IN TENSION AND THAT NO ROOM IN THIS COUNTY IS GOING TO MAKE THEM ONE. CORRECT.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and the fire was out and the shutter on the lane side was doing what it does, and a bench of four and five other people sat in a room above a chandler's shop and had got to the end of it.

“Every one of those is correct,” said Perrin Gant, “and there are eight of them and I have counted them twice.”

“I have had it read twice,” said Ilyan Vester, “and I have been through it in my own head, and I am going to say the thing I have got to say and then I am going to shut the book.”

He put his hand on it and did not open it.

“Ten days ago I read a list of four weeks out of that book in a second door and every line of it was correct, and then I took half of a sentence back in a doorway four seconds after I had said it, and Gant has never done that in nineteen years and I have not been right about anything since.” He turned round in the chair. “I am not going to take half of this back. I have been looking for half of it since the fourth bell and there is no half, and I want the room to know that I looked, and that is the first time in nineteen years I have gone looking for something in a sum of mine and not found it, and I would like that put in the book, because I do not think it means I was right and I think it means I have run out of things to take back.”

He put both hands flat on the cover.

“Here is the sum, and it is short, and it took a convenor of sixty-one ten days to get to it and I am going to say it in a room and not in a doorway because the doorway was the last time.”

He said it in the working voice, at about the fifth bell.

“Every single thing anybody in this district did in the last six weeks was correct, and the sum of the correct things is a district that is owed nothing and has paid everything.”

---

Marda Fen said one thing from the doorway and it was about four seconds long and it was the only thing anybody said in that room that was not a sum.

“No it is not,” she said.

Nobody turned round. She had come up to say one thing about the beam and had stayed, which is what she does, and she is fifty-four and she has been in that granary for twenty-two years and there were about four hundred carts in the yard behind her and about seven thousand loads in the building and one pair of hands.

“There is a hundred loads on a line in a book six miles up the road that goes north,” she said, “in the account's own column, in the account's own hand, and the line says short, one hundred loads, and that line is two days old and it is true, and it is the first line anybody has ever entered in a book about this district that was not written by one of us. And you have all just read out eight items and every one of them was correct and every one of them was done by a person in this district, and the sum of them is that this district has written nine correct things about itself in six weeks and has been owed nothing and has paid everything and has a hundred loads in a book that a reeve paid sixteen pence out of her own apron to copy.”

She came into the room, which she does not do.

“So the sum is not a sum of what we did. The sum is what is in a book, and the book is eleven feet away from the register in a building six miles off, and the hundred is in one and the sheet is in the other, and it took a boy of nineteen four days and a carter four minutes to put them side by side, and it has taken the four of you about eight weeks and a hundred doors that we cannot walk to.” She put her hand on the table. “And I will tell you the part that is mine. Nine years. Two hundred and forty names in my hand and two hundred and forty-one, and forty-one loads carried out of my own authority, and a dead man in the fourth line and a woman at the bottom in a different ink, and I have never once been asked whether I minded and I am not asking now and I want it in the book that I did not ask.”

Nobody asked her to mind and nobody said anything for about a minute.

“That is what is in that book and it has not been in it before,” said Marda Fen, “and I have put it in it and I have not put it in a book and I am not going to, and if the four of you want to read it out at the end of every month for the next year then that is your business and not mine.”

She went down the lane.

---

And then the convenor of sixty-one said the last thing of the afternoon and it was not a sum and it was not a discovery and it is in that book in his own hand under the nine lines, and it is one line, and it is the only line in nineteen years of that book that is not a thing this district did.

“I have got a line to put in and I am going to say it out loud before I put it, because that is what this room started doing in the forty-seventh week, and Osa is not to read it back to me tonight.”

Nobody said anything.

“A page cannot stand in a door,” said Perrin Gant. “I wrote that down in this room on the sixth day of the forty-fifth week, in a different hand, and I have said it in a second door and I have said it in a lane and it is still true, and it is going to be true at the end of this year. And every one of the eight lines you have just had read out was done by writing, and one of the lines on the list I read out of this book in a second door ten days ago was a man standing in a door reading nothing at all, and that one is the only line on either list that anybody in this room can still say the whole of without a sheet in front of them. I have not worked out what that means and I am sixty-one and I have been a convenor for nineteen years and I am not going to work it out this week.”

He shut the book and put the key in his pocket, which is a thing he does.

“Nobody in that sum is a villain and nobody in it is redeemed and nothing in it is undone, and I said that ten days ago in a second door and I am saying it again in a room because I have now had ten days to find the one that was wrong with it and there is not one, and that is the whole of what a person doing their work looks like from the inside, and it is not the same thing as it being finished.”

At about the sixth bell the room emptied the way that room empties, which is four people at a time and no farewells, and Perrin Gant went out of the door and down the stair and did not stop at the second door on the way, and about four of the nine people who were in that room noticed that he had not stopped, and it was the first time in nineteen years that a convenor of this district has gone down that lane before the building shut, and he had said in the forty-third week that he would not, and he had a reason then and the reason was that a convenor does not go down a lane first, and he did not give a reason at the sixth bell and nobody asked him for one.

He went to the weigh and stood at the end of the beam for about nine minutes and did not say anything to anybody, and Marda Fen weighed four carts in that time and did not ask him why he was there, and then he went back up the lane and shut the room, which he does, and the key went in his pocket, which he does.

And at about the seventh bell a reeve of fifty-four shut the second door of a granary after reading a board that has said nine pence against a name since the fourth day of the forty-first week, and did not correct it, and there were about four hundred carts in that yard with nothing to do and about seven thousand loads in the building and one pair of hands and a beam, and the door was shut and the beam was there in the morning.
