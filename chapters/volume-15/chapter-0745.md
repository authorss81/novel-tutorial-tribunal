# Chapter 745: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Fever Was Sixty-Six Weeks Old, And A Sheet Of Printed Paper Is On That Counter With Three Columns On It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing That He Is Not Going To Fill Any Of It In

Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. His four hundred and ninety-fifth morning. Three hundred and forty-five days after the settlement. The fever sixty-six weeks old. Two hundred and ninety-nine days since the division. Two hundred and forty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the three things, in the order he gives them, and nobody had asked him for any of them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and he says neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the seventh day of it, and a full bank holds.*”

“*Seven days. It was full on the Wednesday of this week and I am giving you that working again because it is what I give.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in one day from this morning, and I have given you that Sunday five mornings running.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to give you a fourth thing.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and read the nine words on the back of it, and the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. He did not read anything twice this morning.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-five times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and has not started again.

“*Thirty-five is seven hundred and forty-five less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-five in the dark.*”

“*I read it once this morning and not twice, and I gave the reason yesterday in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said yesterday he is not going to read that sheet again this week. He has not read it either. He is standing about four feet off my left hand and he has not looked at it once this morning.*”

“*About four of you have noticed that and about nine of you have not.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twenty-fourth morning, and he said it flat and short, and he did not vary the asking by one word.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-four mornings.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said an argument out loud on this bank yesterday and about four of you have not told me what it was.*”

“*I am not asking. One of the three was about a day I cannot come on and that is mine.*”

“*I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me and I am still waiting. Nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty-four.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave three counts back and refused the fourth, and a man of sixty-one did not tell him to stop.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping four counts since the Monday of last week and none of them has been asked for by anybody.

“*Forty-four days. Forty-four is seven hundred and forty-five less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And twenty-four mornings, and a hundred and fifteen days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and sixteen for a woman in a reed.*”

“*And the fourth one I am not saying this week, because a man of sixty-one told me on Wednesday not to, and it has not been a week yet.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for carrying three.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since, and has never given it.

“*I am not going to tell him to stop and I am not going to tell him to go on either.*”

“*I said on Wednesday four sentences about a boy of thirteen with a figure and the fourth was that I was not going to say why.*”

“*A figure of days in a place a man is not in is not a thing a boy should be asked to hold.*”

Then about nine people saw a sheet of printed paper on that counter that had not been there on Thursday morning, and nobody on that bank could be sent for about it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has read that sheet thirty-five times in this county and has read every line on it and the line at the top of it in smaller type and the nine words on the back of it. He has never once seen a third thing on it.

“*It was not there yesterday morning. It is the same sheet, because it is the same nine lines and the same fourteen names and the same nine words on the back.*”

“*And there is a third thing on it. It has three columns.*”

“*Name. Day. And a third one, and the third one has a line at the top of it in smaller type, and I am going to read it out loud and I am going to count the words because that is what I do and because you cannot check anything else about it.*”

Then he read it out loud, and counted it, and the count was twelve words and the sheet was that wide.

“*A. Person. Who. Is. Not. There. On. A. Day. Agrees. To. It.*”

“*Twelve. And I am going to say the other half of it and nobody asked me.*”

“*That is the nine words from the back of the sheet with three words in front of them. A person who. Three and nine is twelve and I counted it twice and about four of you counted it with me.*”

“*And one of the nine is not the word that is on the back of it. That one says agrees. The back of that sheet says is agreeing.*”

“*A rule about a day is one thing and a thing a person does is another one, and the three words in front are what turn the first into the second, and I have read that sheet thirty-five mornings and did not hear either half of it until this one.*”

“*And there is no column on that thing for a person stopping. I have read that sheet thirty-five mornings and there were two columns and neither was a person stopping, and this morning there are three and not one of the three is.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one said yesterday in about nine people’s hearing that he is not going to fill anything in, not a line, not a day, not a name, not a space about as wide as a thumb.*”

“*He said it yesterday about a thing that had two columns. It has got three this morning and I am not going to say that makes it a different thing.*”

“*I am not going to be the man who says a thing is different because it has got worse.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said what it says, in four sentences, and he cannot read, and he gave the case first, and he said nobody was going to be thanked for any of it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He asked to be in that room once and was given nothing, and asked again once and did not ask again.

“*Four sentences and the fourth of them is the reason and it is mine.*”

“*One: I cannot come on any day of any week. I have held the gravel at the head of this drain for nineteen years and I am not going to stop holding it so that a piece of printed paper can have me on a day.*”

“*Two: and there are nine lines on that thing and about nine days set out against them. If I do not come on any of them then I have agreed to all nine, and I have not agreed to any of them.*”

“*There is nothing on that sheet anybody could put in a column to say the difference.*”

“*Three: a man of thirty-four in an ash has a day against his name on the seventh line and he has never been asked anything in a hundred and fifteen days. If he came out of that ash tomorrow he would have agreed to that day as well, and he did not.*”

“*Four: a thing that cannot tell the difference between a person who agreed and a person who was not there will get it wrong about everybody on that line, and it is not going to get it wrong about me either.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for four sentences and nobody is going to be told I was brave about them, and I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me about that either.*”

About four of them had a man of fifty-four who had said that about nine people on a piece of paper cannot be told apart. About nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had not seen a third column until this morning and was not going to call it worse.

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud, in the open, on that bank, at about the seventh hour, in about nine people’s hearing, that he was not going to fill any of it in. He gave the working for his own figure first, and he said what it was called.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-one days. A hundred and ninety-one is seven hundred and forty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. On Thursday he said he is not going to read that sheet again this week.

“*A hundred and ninety-one is seven hundred and forty-five less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety-one days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*That is called an amendment. I am saying the word out loud because a thing that cannot be said cannot be refused, and nobody in this county can be sent for about who put that paper there.*”

“*Here is the want and I am giving it before the refusal, because I gave it after it on Monday morning.*”

“*I want the ninth line to have no day against it again. I have wanted that for four mornings and I have said it once and I am not going to say it a second time this fortnight.*”

“*And here is the refusal and it is the whole of it. It took a woman of sixty-four nineteen years to have one thing she could refuse and I do not have that.*”

“*And it is not the thing that was asked on a Wednesday forty-five days ago. Forty-five is seven hundred and forty-five less seven hundred.*”

“*That question is still open. Nobody answered it yesterday and nobody has answered it this morning, and I am not going to let this morning be an answer to it, and nobody on this bank is going to be told that refusing a piece of paper is refusing that.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four said on the second day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a column which means one thing in a book and another thing on a sheet is one column in two places. A man of sixty-one told me on the last day of that week that I was joining up two things because they were the same size.*”

“*I am not going to fill any of the three columns in. Not the name, not the day, not the third one, and I do not know what the third one is.*”

“*A man of fifty-four just said in four sentences that a thing which cannot tell the difference between a person who agreed and a person who was not there will get it wrong about everybody on that line.*”

“*That is the whole of what I am refusing and I am refusing it in about nine people’s hearing on a bank on a Saturday morning and nobody is going to thank me for it.*”

“*And here is what it cost, and it is mine, and nobody is going to be told I was right to pay it.*”

“*A thing that cannot be said cannot be refused. That is why I said the word and I am not going to pretend I said it for any other reason.*”

“*And a word said out loud on a bank is a word about four people can now say a man of thirty-one said. I have been nobody’s for a hundred and ninety-one days and this morning I have given that up.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said on the fourth day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a mark is worth being sent for and is not worth being believed, both at once, and about four of you believed the second half.*”

“*I am not going to argue with one word of that this morning.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said the true of it in five sentences and did not raise her voice and she is not one of the nine.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and said a seventh is a rule. She has said the same sentence about nine times and has not begun again. The count of the times she has said it and not said it carries no ordinal and is not restarted.

“*Five, and the fifth one is the reason, and it is mine and it is about what you have just done and not about why.*”

“*One: you have said a word out loud on a bank this morning that you had said in your own head twice in about three weeks and neither of those times in front of anybody.*”

“*Two: a thing that cannot be said cannot be refused, and you are right about that, and it is the same sentence nine times in a different order and I am not going to thank you for hearing it.*”

“*Three: a man of fifty-four put a case in front of an argument yesterday and a man of thirty-eight gave a case in front of a count this morning. You have given a reason in front of a refusal.*”

“*About four of you have noticed that all three put the case first, and that is not how any of you were taught.*”

“*Four: nobody on this bank can be sent for about you for saying it, so the refusing is not a rule, and you are not going to say it a second time this fortnight.*”

“*I have said that in four sentences rather than nine. About nine of you noticed the difference and about four of you did not.*”

“*Five: I am not going to tell you what the third column is and neither are you.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind. Nobody touched the sheet. Nobody carried it anywhere.

Then a man of forty-one said the other half, and it was about what happens on Monday, and nobody was going to be thanked for it.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and named the morning out loud in a passage at the fifth hour before the light on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. Nothing fell down. He has not been the ninth of nine since, and there are nine of these things in this county with no original and no keeper.

“*I am forty-one and I keep two carts and neither of them can be sent for about.*”

“*I stopped being the ninth of nine in a passage before the light on the Friday of a fortnight and nothing fell down, and I have said that out loud four times and nobody has written it down.*”

“*A thing with two columns asked me for a day in that passage and I gave it. I had a day to say no on and I said it out loud instead of putting my hands on the stone, and the second pair of hands was not there.*”

“*About nine of you worked out that the second pair of hands was me. It was not.*”

“*And I am not going to say that a man of thirty-one refusing a piece of paper this morning is the same as what I did in that passage.*”

“*He did not say a day out loud and I did. Those are two different shapes, and a woman of twenty-eight told me on the second day of this week that I was mixing them up.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for four times out loud and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it, and Monday is a Monday whatever is on that counter.*”

The fever was sixty-six weeks old. He had been in that county a hundred and ninety-one days.

A sheet of printed paper was on that counter on Saturday morning with three columns on it and twelve words at the top of the third one.

The twelve are the nine words that were on the back of it with three words in front of them and one of the nine changed. There is still no column on it for a person stopping.

And a man of fifty-four who cannot read said in four sentences that he cannot come on any day of any week and has held the gravel at the head of that drain for nineteen years. If he does not come he has agreed to all nine. And a thing which cannot tell the difference between a person who agreed and a person who was not there will get it wrong about everybody on that line.

There is a sheet of printed paper on that counter with three columns on it, and the third one has twelve words at the top of it, and the twelve are the nine words that were on the back of it with three words in front of them and one of the nine changed.

One of the nine is not the word that is on the back of it. A man of thirty-eight read the two of them out loud in the same morning, and one of them says agrees and the other one says is agreeing.

And there is still no column on that thing for a person stopping, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about who put it there, and about nine people were on that bank on a Saturday morning when a man of thirty-one said the word amendment out loud and refused to fill in any of the three.
