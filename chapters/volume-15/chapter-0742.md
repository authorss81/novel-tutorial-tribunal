# Chapter 742: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Week, And A Wednesday, And A Bank Is Full At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step, And A Man Of Thirty-Four In An Ash Has Been There A Hundred And Twelve Days And Has A Day Against His Name And Nobody Has Asked Him Anything In Any Of Them

Second day of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. His four hundred and ninety-second morning. Three hundred and forty-two days after the settlement. The fever sixty-five weeks and four days. Two hundred and ninety-six days since the division. Two hundred and forty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the first morning it had been that since the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. The man of forty-three gave the reading, gave the working, and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and a full bank.*”

“*Four days. Four inches a day, and it was about twelve at this hour yesterday, and that is the whole of the working.*”

“*And that is four days after the water came off, and the water came off on the Sunday of last week at about the sixth hour and came off all ninety of the steps.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, and that is the Sunday of this week, and I have given you that figure every morning since Friday.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for it, and about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for a fourth thing and I am not going to give you one.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twenty-first morning, and he said the thing that had been standing behind the asking for a fortnight, and it was not the same as the ninth line.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied it. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-one mornings.*”

“*And there is a day against that line this morning that was not there on Sunday.*”

“*I cannot read, so I do not know what day it is, and I am not going to ask anybody to tell me.*”

“*I am not going to become a man who gets told what day it is.*”

“*I can check a thing against another man saying it out loud. That is the whole of what I have got and it is not nothing.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight read it out loud yesterday and there was no day. He read it out loud this morning and there is one.*”

“*Those are two men saying one thing out loud and they do not agree.*”

“*I am not going to say which of them I believe, because I have got no way of doing that.*”

“*And I am still not answering the fifth thing and not one of you has asked me, and I am not going to be thanked for twenty-one and I am not going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and then read the nine words on the back of it, and it took about nine minutes and a bit over, and the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-two times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Thirty-two is seven hundred and forty-two less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-two in the dark.*”

“*And I read the nine words on the back out loud this morning as well, and I read them last, so that nobody had to get used to them while I was saying them.*”

“*Not there on a day is agreeing to it. Nine. And I am going to say the other half of it and nobody asked me.*”

“*That rule is about the nine lines and not about the fourteen names, and I have read fourteen names out loud thirty-two mornings and I have never once read what happens to a name that is on a line with no day against it.*”

“*The ninth line has had a day against it since yesterday morning. That is one day. It is not one day for one name, it is one day for two names, and there are two names on that line and one of them is about a hundred yards along that drain.*”

“*And I am not going to say her name out loud on this bank. Not this morning. I have read a line out loud thirty-two mornings and I have never said a name on it and I am not going to start on the morning a day came on.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the thirty-two and nobody is going to be told I was brave about not saying a name.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said the figure out loud that nobody on that bank had, and he gave the working, and he said it in about nine people’s hearing, and a man of sixty-one told him he was not to do that again.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping four counts since yesterday morning and none of them has been asked for.

“*Forty-one days. Forty-one is seven hundred and forty-two less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And twenty-one mornings, and a hundred and twelve days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and thirteen for a woman in a reed.*”

“*And here is the one I have not said and I am going to say it once and then I am not going to say it again this week.*”

“*A day came on that line yesterday. And a man of thirty-four has been in that ash a hundred and twelve days and he has a day against his name there and nobody has asked him anything in any of the hundred and twelve.*”

“*A hundred and twelve is seven hundred and forty-two less six hundred and thirty, and the day against his name is not one of the hundred and twelve, it is a day and a half and it is not his.*”

“*And I am not going to work out what it is, because a boy of thirteen should not work out what a day is against a man’s name when nobody asked him anything about it.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for the four counts and I am keeping them on Wednesday.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since and has never given it to anybody.

“*Four sentences and the fourth one is the reason, and I am going to say them flat and I am not going to shout at a boy of thirteen.*”

“*One: you have got a figure and you have not got a man, and a figure of a man is not a thing you can carry down a cart road in your head and give to nine people at the seventh hour.*”

“*Two: a day is a place a man sat in a chair, and there was no chair and there was no room.*”

“*I said that on the first day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and I am not going to say it a second time.*”

“*Three: and what you have just done is take a thing nobody in this county can be sent for about and put it in the mouth of the one person here that nobody can be sent for about either.*”

“*Four: and I am telling you not to do it again this week.*”

“*Not because you are wrong. I am not going to say why.*”

“*About four of you have noticed that there is no reason given and that is the correct way round.*”

A boy of thirteen said he had heard the four sentences and was not going to do it again this week, and said nothing else, and about four people on that bank said nothing at all.

Then a man of thirty-four in an ash was named out loud by two people, and neither of them asked him anything, and he has been there a hundred and twelve days and has a day against his name.

Ivo Kellow is thirty-four and has been on the low side of that landing in an ash for a hundred and twelve days, and his name is on the seventh line of a sheet of printed paper with a day against it, and he has never read it and nobody has read it to him. He has said nothing on this bank in about a fortnight and there is nothing to be said by anybody about him.

“*He has been there a hundred and twelve days and that is seven hundred and forty-two less six hundred and thirty, and the working is his mother’s and not his and he cannot hear it from here.*”

“*Nobody is going to say his name a third time on that bank this week, because about nine people have noticed that two people saying a name is not the same as one person asking a question, and that is the correct way round.*”

“*And I am not going to ask him anything and I am not going to be thanked for not asking him anything, which is the same as every other morning of this band.*”

“*And there is a day against his name on that thing and he has never been asked about it.*”

“*I am going to say that out loud at about the ninth hour.*”

“*Nobody on this bank is going to be sent for about me for saying it, and nobody is going to be sent for about him either.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the whole of it in about nine sentences, and she gave the working first, and she refused the tenth, and nobody picked one for her.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years, and she has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper and that line has had a day against it since yesterday morning.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash a hundred and twelve days and has a day against his name, and a woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed a hundred and thirteen and has the same day on the same line.*”

“*There are three of us on that sheet who have not been asked anything about a day.*”

“*I am one. I have been out of every book in this county for nineteen years.*”

“*The other two are about a hundred yards along that drain and neither of them has been asked anything in any of their days.*”

“*Not there on a day is agreeing to it. Nine words, and a man of thirty-eight counted them out loud this morning and I counted them with him.*”

“*I have been out of every book in this county for nineteen years and it cost me one thing a day.*”

“*And no form in this county has my name at the top of it, and that is the whole of what a person in this county is.*”

“*And here is the finding and I am not going to improve on it: a day on a line does not want anybody to agree to it. A day is a place, and a place can be written down whether anybody sat in it or not.*”

“*And that is why I said yesterday that I am not going to be asked about it, and it was not because I am careful about me. It was because if I agreed to it then it would go against them as well, and neither of them is going to be asked.*”

“*Being a name nobody can find by writing a name down took nineteen years and one thing a day. Being a name on a piece of printed paper has taken six days the first time, eight the second and nine the third, and none of the three cost anybody anything at all.*”

“*And there is a day against three names on that thing and about nine people in this county can be sent for about two of the three, and neither of those two has been asked.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for any of that, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about standing here and saying it.*”

“*About four of you can tell in four days that I said it on a bank and not in a book. That is all I am claiming.*”

“*And the tenth sentence is not coming. I have refused one in a fortnight and I am not going to stop refusing them on the morning after a day came on.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of the sixth sentence in his own mouth, and gave the working, and said nobody was going to be told which half of it was his.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-eight days. A hundred and eighty-eight is seven hundred and forty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. On the first morning of this week he said out loud that he wanted the ninth line to have no day on it again by tomorrow morning, and he said he had not got a way of doing it.

“*She is right about all nine of them and I am not going to take one of them back.*”

“*A day on a line does not want anybody to agree to it. That is the sixth sentence and it is the one I did not know on Monday morning, and I have had a day and a night to think about it and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.*”

“*A hundred and eighty-eight is seven hundred and forty-two less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty-eight days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*Here is the want and it has not changed in a day and that is the part I have not said out loud.*”

“*I want that woman of twenty-nine to be on a line with no day against it again, and I said on Monday morning that I wanted the ninth line to have no day on it and that was the same want and I did not know it.*”

“*And the cost is that a woman of sixty-four said on Saturday that if she agreed to that day it would go against her name as well, and she is out of every book in this county for nineteen years and it cost her one thing a day, and I have been on this bank three mornings wanting to take a day off a line she is standing next to.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for that and nobody is going to tell me I was right to want it.*”

“*And I said yesterday morning that I wanted the ninth line to have no day on it again by this morning. It has got one.*”

“*I have not got a way of taking it off and I am not going to tell you I have.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight reads a rule out loud on that sheet every morning and about nine of you hear it.*”

“*He read a rule yesterday morning when there was no rule on the back of it. That is not his doing and I am not going to let it be.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about this morning and nobody is going to be thanked.*”

“*Nobody is going to be told that I was right to want it yesterday. Wanting it was not the same as being able to do it, and I did not know that until this morning.*”

“*And none of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday forty-two days ago.*”

“*Forty-two is seven hundred and forty-two less seven hundred.*”

“*I am not going to let it be one and I am not going to let it be a reason.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me in nine sentences on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a second time is a rule. I said yesterday morning that I was not going to say mine a second time this fortnight, and a day came on a line instead, and that is what came instead.*”

“*I am not going to say that is a reason and I am not going to say it is not. I am sixty-one years younger than the man who would know the difference and I am thirty-one.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a full bank and a day against the ninth line. About four of them had a boy of thirteen who had said a figure out loud and been told four sentences about it, and nobody had picked a shape for either of the two.

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. Nobody carried it anywhere.

---

The fever was sixty-five weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-eight days.

A boy of thirteen said out loud on a bank that a man of thirty-four in an ash has been there a hundred and twelve days and has a day against his name and has not been asked anything in any of them, and a man of sixty-one told him in four sentences not to do it again this week and gave no reason, and said it was not because the boy was wrong.

And a woman of sixty-four said that a day on a line does not want anybody to agree to it, and that a day is a place, and that a place can be written down whether anybody sat in it or not, and that this is why she said yesterday she was not going to be asked about it, and refused the tenth sentence.
