# Chapter 735: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Seventh Week, And A Wednesday, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Be The One Who Asks For The Seventh Time In About Three Weeks And Is Refused In About Nine Sentences By A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Who Says A Seventh Is A Rule

Second day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. His four hundred and eighty-fifth morning. Three hundred and thirty-five days after the settlement. The fever sixty-four weeks and four days. Two hundred and eighty-nine days since the division. Two hundred and thirty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, in that order, as he gives them. He gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked at the end of it, and nobody had asked.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since. Neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step, and it is the fifth day of it, and a full bank holds.*”

“*Five days. It was full yesterday at about the seventh hour, and four inches a day and sixteen is the whole of the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning, and that is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the three things and I am not going to be asked about them until they stop working.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened. It was the fourteenth morning, and he did not vary the asking by one word.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied it. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered, and nobody has asked.

“*Fourteen mornings.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two said something in my hearing yesterday that I have not stopped carrying, and I am going to say what it was, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it.*”

“*He said a mark is worth being sent for and is not worth being believed, and both of those are true at once.*”

“*I have been asked a fifth thing and I have not answered it. I have not been sent for about anything in six weeks.*”

“*I am not going to work out in the hearing of the nine of you standing there, whether those are the same size.*”

“*I am still waiting to be asked about the fifth thing and not one of the nine of you has asked me.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read that line out loud in the open, and then read fourteen names, and it took about nine minutes, and the sheet went back on that counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twenty-five times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He has decided every morning since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week not to say a name out loud.

“*Twenty-five is seven hundred and thirty-five less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty-five in the dark.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has carried a board in his own coat since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and has never read it out.*”

“*And about four of you worked out on this morning that a man of thirty-one said he would go up four miles yesterday, and about nine of you did not work it out at all.*”

“*He did not go. He is standing on this bank and he is not going up that road, and I am not going to say anything about that and I am not going to be thanked for not saying it.*”

About four of that bank had a man of thirty-eight who had read twenty-five mornings. About nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had said he would go up four miles on Tuesday and had not gone.

Then a man of thirty-one offered to be the one who asks for the seventh time in about three weeks. He said it in the plainest words anybody in this county has got, in the hearing of the nine of you standing there, before anybody had asked him anything.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-one days. A hundred and eighty-one is seven hundred and thirty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. Nobody has asked him for anything in one of them. He offered on the Tuesday of this week to go up four miles and ask a question, he had offered six times to be the one who asks in about three weeks and been refused six times out loud, and nobody has ever told him to stop.

“*I said yesterday that I would go up four miles and ask her, and I am not taking it back and I am not adding to it.*”

“*A woman of forty-four said on Saturday that I am the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks, and I have offered seven times in about three weeks and been refused six times out loud.*”

“*And nobody on that bank has ever told him to stop, and I am not asking anybody to, and about four of you have known that for about a fortnight.*”

“*And if I say it a second time it is a rule, and a woman of twenty-eight said on Wednesday that a rule is a thing that works without a person.*”

“*So I am going to say what is left of it once, and then a woman of twenty-eight is going to refuse it, and I am not going to say any of it a second time.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight refused him in about nine sentences, and she did it before he had finished the last of it, and she did not raise her voice.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine, and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She has said the same sentence nine times without saying it. Her fortnight of not beginning again is over and the seven weeks of mornings carry forward. The count of the times she has said it and not said it carries no ordinal and is not restarted.

“*No. Nine sentences and the ninth one is the reason, and I am not going to let it be a rule.*”

“*One: a man of about fifty-two said yesterday that a mark is worth being sent for and is not worth being believed.*”

“*And you have heard it, and about four of you have been carrying it since yesterday morning.*”

“*And you have come up here on Wednesday morning and offered to be the instrument of a thing nobody in four counties can be sent for about.*”

“*Two: there is no form anywhere that says a man may go up four miles and put a question to a woman at her own door.*”

“*And there is no form anywhere that says a man may not, and both of those have been true since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*Three: and if you go up that road this week then in about four days a man of thirty-six is going to come up it with a sheet of printed paper, and he is not going to be you.*”

“*Four: I cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one I came from, and I am standing on this bank, and I am not going to be a post.*”

“*Five: and I have not said that on this bank before and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*Six: a man of fifty-four who cannot read has been refused nothing, has answered nothing, and is waiting to be asked, and about four of you have not worked out that those are three different things.*”

“*Seven: a man of thirty-eight has carried a board in his coat for about four weeks and has never read it.*”

“*And if you go up that road with a pen then about four of you are going to carry a thing with a name in it, and none of you will be able to put it down.*”

“*Eight: and I have said the same sentence about nine times in about nine days and I am not going to begin again about this one.*”

“*Nine: a man of about fifty-two said yesterday that a mark is not worth being believed, and about four of you believed the sixth.*”

“*And the sixth is the one that did the damage and not this one, and I am not going to take that back either.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of the ninth sentence in his own mouth and gave the working. He said he was not going to be told which part of it was his.

“*She is right about all nine of them and I am not going to take one of them back.*”

“*A hundred and eighty-one is seven hundred and thirty-five less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty-one days.*”

“*I said on Tuesday that I would go up that road, and I am not going to thank anybody for the seventh, and nobody is going to be told I was right about offering it.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two said yesterday that a mark is not worth being believed. He is the only one of the nine standing here who cannot see well and he is right about that sentence and nobody has told him so.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight has said that a seventh is a rule, and that a rule is a thing that works without a person. She is right.*”

“*And I said a rule out loud myself on the second morning of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, in nine people’s hearing, and I did not know I was doing it.*”

“*And I am not going to say the seventh a second time, and that is the whole of what I have done about it.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of the six either, and nobody is going to be thanked for refusing me and nobody has ever told me to stop.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the half of it that is about a bank and not about a road. She said it in four sentences and she said it flat.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*About nine people have said out loud on this bank in about nine days that you are the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks.*”

“*I said it on Saturday and I am not going to say how many of the nine said it and I am not going to be thanked for the count.*”

“*And none of them is a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and that is the whole of why it is true and it is not a compliment.*”

“*A shut door is not the right of refusing. A sealed door is not that. A man who cannot read is not it. A person who cannot be asked has not refused anything.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the four sentences and I am not going to be told I am right, and nobody on this bank has been given the right of refusing this week by anybody.*”

About nine of them on that bank had a woman of forty-four who had said four sentences about a shut door and a man who cannot read. About four of them had a man of thirty-one who had been refused a seventh time and was not going to say it again.

Then nothing whatever happened for the rest of that morning. About nine people stood on that bank in the wind. The sheet was on the counter where anybody could put a hand on it, and a man of thirty-four in an ash and a woman of twenty-nine in a reed were not asked anything by anybody.

---

The fever was sixty-four weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-one days.

A woman of forty-four put four sentences out loud about a shut door, a sealed door, a man who cannot read and a person who cannot be asked, and said she has given the reason nine times in about six weeks, and that the reason is that all four of those things still leave a person standing on that bank.

Nobody on that bank was given the right of refusing by anybody, in nine sentences or in four, and nobody is relieved, and about four of them had a man of thirty-one who was refused a seventh time and was not going to say it again.
