# Chapter 744: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Week, And A Friday, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read Says The Argument Out Loud In Nine Sentences With A Case Standing In Front Of It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Is Not Going To Be The One Who Fills Any Of It In

Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. His four hundred and ninety-fourth morning. Three hundred and forty-four days after the settlement. The fever sixty-five weeks and six days. Two hundred and ninety-eight days since the division. Two hundred and forty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the three things in the order he gives them. Nobody had asked him for any of them and nobody was going to.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and that landing is the only sentence anybody can say about him that has not had to be corrected. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the sixth day of it, and a full bank holds.*”

“*Six days. It was full on the Wednesday of this week at about the seventh hour, and that is the working, and I am not going to give it a fourth time in the same morning.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in two days from this morning, and that is the Sunday of this week, and I have given you that Sunday four mornings running.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for it and I am not going to give you a fourth thing.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and read the nine words on the back of it. Then he did the thing he has not done in about a fortnight, which was to stop in the middle and start the line over.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-four times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and has not started again.

“*Thirty-four is seven hundred and forty-four less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-four in the dark.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one said on Wednesday that he is not going to read that sheet again this week, and he did not say that I could not, and I noticed that on Wednesday and I have not said anything about it until this morning.*”

“*Not there on a day is agreeing to it. Nine words. And I am going to read that line twice this morning and I am going to tell you why before I read it.*”

“*I am going to read it twice because a man of thirty-four in an ash has a day against his name on that line and he was not there on that day.*”

“*That is not a thing I have worked out in my own head. That is a thing I can check against another man saying it out loud, which is the only thing a man of fifty-four has got and I have got it too.*”

“*And his name is not going to be said by me, and nothing is going to be asked of him, and nobody is going to be thanked for my reading a rule out loud twice.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said nine sentences, in the open, on that bank, at about the seventh hour, in about nine people’s hearing. He cannot read. He gave the case first and then the argument, and he said nobody was going to be thanked for any of it and nobody was going to be told he was brave.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, and the asking has not varied by one word in all of them. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-three mornings.*”

“*Here is the case and it is standing in front of you on this bank and I am going to put it before what I am going to say.*”

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash about a hundred yards along that drain for a hundred and thirteen days, and his name is on a line on that thing with a day against it.*”

“*He was not there on that day. He was in an ash. There was nobody there to see it and nobody has been able to ask him anything in any of the hundred and thirteen.*”

“*And that line says he agreed. Not that he came. It says he agreed, and it says it this morning, and a man of thirty-eight has read it out loud three mornings running.*”

“*And now the argument, and I am going to say it in three sentences because I am fifty-four and I have not got nine.*”

“*One: I have been asked for a day I cannot come on, twenty-three mornings, and not one of the nine of you has asked me whether I could come.*”

“*Two: and a man of thirty-four has not been asked anything at all, and a line has filled itself in.*”

“*Three: a thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing and it becomes a field somebody fills in.*”

“*That is the whole of it. I am telling it by hand because I cannot read, and I have said the case before the argument on purpose.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to be told I was brave about standing here and saying it in the open on a Friday morning.*”

“*And I am still not answering the fifth thing and not one of you has asked me about that, and I am still waiting, and I am not going to put those two things in one line with each other.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind. Nobody said the word he had said, and nobody improved on it, and about four of them had not understood all three of his sentences and about nine of them had.

Then a woman of forty said the count of what was in front of them, and gave the working, and refused to add it to anything.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She gave three spaces and one width in a previous band and nine sentences and a refused tenth on the last day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them.

“*Three, and I am going to say what I am standing in front of first, because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*Three: two names on the ninth line and one day against that line.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four’s is one of the names. A woman of twenty-nine’s is the other, and she is not on this bank and has not been asked anything in a hundred and fourteen days.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four’s is on the seventh line with a day against it and he was not there on that day.*”

“*That is three names and one day and I am not going to add it to the nine of the twenty-seventh week, because that is a count of things and this is a count of people.*”

“*Three and one and three is seven and seven is a figure and I am not going to give you one.*”

“*A day is a place a man sat in a chair. A woman of forty-four said a version of that on the last morning of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and I have not improved on it since and I am not going to improve on it now.*”

“*And what a man of fifty-four has just said in three sentences is that a chair can be written down without anybody sitting in it.*”

“*About four of you have worked out that that is the whole of this week so far and I am not going to tell you whether it is.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has read that line out loud thirty-four mornings and a man of thirty-four has not been asked anything in a hundred and thirteen of them, and I am not going to say which of those two figures is the one that matters.*”

“*And I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them. I said on a Monday of a fortnight that I would not and I have not started and I am not going to start on the morning a man of fifty-four has said the argument.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he was not going to do with it, in the open, on that bank, before anybody had asked him anything, and he gave the working for his own figure before he gave it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety days. A hundred and ninety is seven hundred and forty-four less five hundred and fifty-four. On Wednesday he said he was not going to read that sheet again this week. On Monday he said he wanted the ninth line to have no day on it again.

“*A hundred and ninety is seven hundred and forty-four less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*A man of fifty-four who cannot read has just said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that a thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing and becomes a field somebody fills in.*”

“*He said it by hand and he said the case first. I am not going to improve on one word of it and I am not going to say it again this week in my own mouth, because a woman of twenty-eight told me on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a second time is a rule.*”

“*And here is what I am not going to do with it, and I am saying it before anybody asks me, because that is the only shape I have left.*”

“*I am not going to fill anything in. Not a line, not a day, not a name, not a space about as wide as a thumb.*”

“*A woman of forty-four said on the second day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that there are three places there is to put a want and she refused all three, and one of the three is a piece of printed paper four hundred miles off.*”

“*I have been the outside of that thing for a hundred and ninety days and I have not filled in one of them.*”

“*I said out loud on the second day of this week that I wanted a day taken off a line.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has just told nine people on a bank what that wanting was, and I did not know it was that, and I am not going to be told I was right about it.*”

“*Here is the want and it is the fourth one this week and it is the smallest and I am not going to pretend otherwise.*”

“*I want that man of fifty-four to be asked one question in this county before the end of next week. Not about a room. Not about the fifth thing.*”

“*Anything. And I am not going to be the one who asks it.*”

“*I am not going to say his name out loud on this bank, and nobody is going to be told that I wanted it.*”

“*And the cost is that a man of thirty-four in an ash has been there a hundred and fourteen days and a woman of twenty-nine in a reed a hundred and fifteen and neither of them has been asked anything in any of them, and a man of thirty-one wants one man of fifty-four asked one question, and that is what this county has come to on a Friday morning in the middle of a fortnight.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to be told I was right to want it, and about four of you are going to work out in four days that I have wanted something every morning this week and got nothing, and I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about this morning. Nobody is going to be thanked. Nobody is going to be told I am the right instrument for a thing that is not being done.*”

“*And none of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday forty-four days ago, and forty-four is seven hundred and forty-four less seven hundred, and I am not going to let it be one and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the half of it that is about the bank and not about a figure, and she said it in five sentences, and she said the fifth one was not coming and nobody picked it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and saying so is most of what she does.

“*Five and the fifth is not coming and about four of you have been waiting for it since the seventh hour.*”

“*One: a man of fifty-four has said the argument out loud and not one of the nine of you has asked him whether he would be a fifth thing about a room.*”

“*He asked for that room once on a Sunday of this band and was given nothing, and asked again once and did not ask again.*”

“*Two: and a shut door is not the right of refusing, and a sealed door is not that, and a man who cannot read is not it, and a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything.*”

“*Three: and a man of fifty-four who says an argument out loud in about nine people’s hearing on a bank is not doing either of those four things, and nobody in this county has a form that says he has.*”

“*Four: and that is the whole of what I have got this morning and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to be thanked for five sentences.*”

“*And I am not going to be told I am right about the fifth one not coming, because I did not decide that. It is simply not here and I am fifty-nine and I have not worked out why.*”

About four of them on that bank had a woman of forty-four who had said four sentences and had refused a fifth. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had put a case in front of an argument and had said nobody was going to be thanked for it.

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. Nobody carried it anywhere and nothing went into any of the columns on it.

A man of thirty-four in an ash has not been asked anything at all and a line has filled itself in. And a thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing and becomes a field somebody fills in.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read put a case in front of an argument on that bank on a Friday morning and then said the argument in three sentences, and told it by hand because he cannot read.

Nobody improved on one word of it and nobody was told he was brave about it, and nobody in this county is going to be the one who fills any of it in.
