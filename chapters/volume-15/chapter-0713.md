# Chapter 713: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Tuesday, And The Fourth Morning Of A Reading, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That A Day In A Column Of His Own Book Has Meant One Thing For Eleven Years And A Day In A Column On That Sheet Means Another Thing And That There Is One Column And Not Two, And Refuses To Say Which Of The Two Is The Real One, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Keeps A Bakehouse Four Miles Up That Cart Road Says What She Has Worked Out In Four Days And Says It Is Not The Answer

First day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-third morning. Three hundred and thirteen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks and three days. Two hundred and sixty-seven days since the division. Two hundred and thirteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour for the seventh morning running and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the day. He gave nothing else, because there was nothing else in it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and a full bank has held for seven mornings now. The Wednesday of this week is the morning the ninety steps come off again.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Seven days, and a full bank holds, and I have said the working about nine times this fortnight and I am not going to say it again today.*”

“*And tomorrow is the Wednesday, and that is one day from this morning.*”

“*If the river does what she has done five times since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, then about ninety steps can be walked tomorrow at about the sixth hour.*”

“*And I am not going to be the man who promises her.*”

Seven days on that bank. A man of forty-three had a tomorrow he would not promise, and about nine of them had a tomorrow that would not promise itself.

Then a man of thirty-eight read it out loud for the fourth morning running, and it took about nine minutes, and the sheet went back on the counter, and nobody carried it anywhere.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and has read that sheet four times in this county now, twice on a bank and once in a field and once at the fifth hour under that bank. He has not put a term on it, and he has been refused a figure for how long twice.

Then a man of sixty-one said the thing he had been working out since Sunday morning, and he gave it in the order he gives things. He refused to finish it, and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting four days for it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are two columns on every leaf of it that anybody in this county can be sent for about.

“*I have got one column and not two. It is the same column twice.*”

“*I am going to say what it means in this county and what it means on that piece of paper. Then I am going to refuse to say which of the two is the real one.*”

“*A day against a name in my book is the day a head came through that landing on a cart. That is what a day is in a book of heads and carts.*”

“*It is a place, and a man sat in a chair on it, and about nine people could go and look at the chair.*”

“*And a day against a name on that piece of paper is the day a person was asked to come at first light, put two hands on a stone, push it to the far end of a room and push it back.*”

“*That is what a day is on that sheet. And there is one column and not two columns, and it has been one column for about nineteen years and it has been the other thing for four days.*”

“*And here are two reasons why I am not going to say which one of them is the real column. I am not going to give a third reason, and I would like about four of you to notice that it is two and not three.*”

“*One: if my column is the wrong one then eleven years of heads came through that landing and went to a place I cannot be sent for about.*”

“*I have never once in eleven years thought that and I am not going to start this morning.*”

“*Two: if their column is the wrong one then nobody has ever been asked anything at all.*”

“*And a woman of forty worked out yesterday that nine or ten of them can be sent for about three people who were safe four days ago. That is worse and not better.*”

A man of sixty-one had stopped on purpose at two reasons, and about nine of them on that bank had been standing there waiting for the third.

Then a woman of forty said what that is, and it took her about four sentences. She said it was the fourth time she had said a version of it this fortnight and she was not going to say a fifth.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead, and she knows what a column is for. She has been asking for one since before any of them came up that plank.

“*A column that means one thing in a book and another thing on a sheet is not a column. It is one column in two places, and there is no form in this county that says which one you are reading, and nobody in four counties has ever seen one.*”

“*And every day I have asked for since before any of you came up this plank has meant the first one. It has meant a man in a chair on a landing. That is what I have been asking for.*”

“*And I am not going to say that I got it wrong on Saturday. I did not ask for that column, and I am not going to be given it as a thing that happened to me.*”

Then about nine people walked four miles up that cart road to a bakehouse at about the ninth hour. About nine houses in that village take her bread, and about four of the nine cannot do a day without it, and nobody sent for anybody.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has kept that bakehouse nineteen years, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a tradesman four miles off may be sent for about a morning of his work.

“*I have had four days with it and I have worked out one thing and I am going to give you the one thing and it is not the answer.*”

“*They used the same words. Both of them. The same nine words about first light and two hands and a stone and the far end of a room. One of them had a piece of printed paper in his hand, and the other one had something on him that I did not see.*”

“*And neither of them knew about the other. That is the one thing. A man of thirty-six said he had been told to ask one person one question and write down what they said. He did not know there had been one on the Friday, and the one on the Friday did not know there was going to be one on the Wednesday.*”

“*And so if it was one question, then something counted me once. And if it was two, then nothing counted me at all. And I have been in a bakehouse four miles up a cart road for nineteen years and I cannot get at either of those two things from here.*”

That kitchen had one thing in it. About nine of them had a woman of forty-four who had four days and one finding in her, and four of them had understood the finding before she said the last of it.

Then she said the other half of it, and she said it flat, and she said it before anybody had asked her.

“*And I am not going to say which of the two cost more. I have been asked that three times in about nine days by people who were not on this road, and I am not going to say it to the nine of you standing on this step either.*”

“*And nobody in four counties can be sent for about a morning of my work. That is the same county that cannot send anybody up that road to find out whether two men in two coats asked me one question or two.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for four days of turning it over, and I am not going to be sorry for them.*”

“*And I am not going to stand on this step and let anybody say it was worth the walk.*”

Nobody on that step said sorry and about four of them had the same nine words.

Then a man of thirty-one said that there was one office with two kinds of paper in it, and he said it before he had thought about it. A man of sixty-one refused him in three sentences, and the third one was a thing he had not said out loud in this county in eleven years.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-nine days, and a hundred and fifty-nine is seven hundred and thirteen less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*Then it is one office. One place that keeps heads and carts and also asks people to come at first light. Two kinds of paper in one building and a man in each—*”

“*No. Three sentences and the third one is a thing I have not said out loud in this county in eleven years.*”

“*There is no office. There is a book in this county that a man of sixty-one has kept. There is a piece of printed paper that came up nine miles of cart road in the inside of a coat, and it was printed about four hundred miles off by somebody who has never been to this county and is not going to.*”

“*And the third sentence is that you have been in this county a hundred and fifty-nine days and you have just said out loud that there is one building somewhere that both of those come out of.*”

“*I have been asked for where the paper comes from three times in four days and I have refused three times. There is no figure and I am not going to make one up on a step.*”

Three sentences and about nine of them on that step had heard a man of thirty-one get a thing wrong and be told the shape of it in eleven words.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of the third sentence, and gave the working, and did not apologise for it, and said so in advance.

“*I am not going to apologise for it on this step. An apology is a thing you say when you have been caught, and I have not been caught. I have been told the shape of a thing I did not know.*”

“*Here is the true of it. I wanted there to be one office. A hundred and fifty-nine is seven hundred and thirteen less five hundred and fifty-four and I have been in this county a hundred and fifty-nine days.*”

“*And one office can be asked a question, and a building can be refused, and a man can go and stand in front of one.*”

“*I cannot go and stand in front of anything in this county, because there is nothing here to stand in front of.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years, and cannot be found by writing her name down. I have wanted about nineteen years to be that.*”

“*And it is not what I have just said I wanted, and I am not going to improve on the difference.*”

About nine of them on that step had a man of thirty-one who had wanted one thing, said another, and said so out loud in about nine people’s hearing.

Then the sheet was read out loud on that bank for the fourth morning and the count was still nine lines and eleven names. Nothing had been added to it, and nobody had written anything down anywhere all day. About nine of them went down that water at about the ninth hour and stood on the low side in the wet grass.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-nine days.

A man of sixty-one said out loud that there is one column and not two. A day in it has meant that a head came through a landing for eleven years, and has meant that a person was asked to come at first light for four days, and he refused to say which of the two is the real one.

And a woman of forty-four with a bakehouse four miles up that cart road said that both men used the same nine words and neither of them knew about the other. If it was one question then something counted her once, and if it was two then nothing counted her at all, and she cannot get at either from where she is.

There is no office. A man of thirty-one who said there was one, with two kinds of paper in it, was told so in three sentences, and then said out loud that he wanted one, because a building can be refused and a man can stand in front of one and he cannot.
