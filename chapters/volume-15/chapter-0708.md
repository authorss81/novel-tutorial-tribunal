# Chapter 708: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Thursday, And On Which Something That Is Not A Person Says A Woman’s Name Out Loud On A Bank At About The Ninth Hour And Asks Her One Question And She Answers It And Nobody In Four Counties Can Be Sent For About Either The Asking Or The Answering, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Who Has Been Out Of Every Book In This County For Nineteen Years Says The Name Out Loud Herself Because Nobody Has Said It In This County Since The Wednesday Of A Week Before, And It Is The Fifth Time

Third day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Three hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever sixty weeks and five days. Two hundred and sixty-two days since the division. Two hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and it was two days since it came off all ninety, and nine or ten of them were on that bank.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. Seven mornings running a thing that is not a person has said nothing at all, and about four people on that bank have been counting them and about nine have not.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Two days, and it was about four at this hour yesterday, and that is the working.*”

“*And seven mornings. Nothing whatever for seven mornings, and a man of sixty-one has been counting them out loud since the Friday of last week and gave the count before I gave the reading this morning.*”

About four people on that bank had the seven mornings and about nine of them had the eight inches.

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count himself and said what it was a figure of, and refused to say what it was a figure of the other thing.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one. He kept that book of heads and carts eleven years and there are five lines on the leaf after the last head of it, and a number is the last of the five, and he has written one number in eleven years.

“*Seven mornings. Seven is seven hundred and eight less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And that is a figure of mornings and not of a thing, and it is a count of times and not a clock, and there are two of them in this county and only one of the two runs any more.*”

“*I am not going to tell you what it was doing on the Wednesday of last week, because a man of thirty-one has already told you and he was in about nine people’s hearing and I was not, and a thing said in about nine people’s hearing is not a thing that becomes truer because a man of sixty-one was told about it on a Thursday.*”

Seven mornings is a figure of mornings and not of a thing, and about nine of them had been keeping one of the two counts in that county for about six weeks.

Then at about the ninth hour, with nine or ten of them standing on that bank, something that is not a person said a woman’s name out loud.

It was not a shout. It came out of the middle of the bank where there was nothing in it, and it was in plain words, and nobody shouted anything back at it.

“*Ada Renk.*”

About nine people on that bank knew that name and about four of them had not heard it said out loud in about nineteen years.

Then it asked her one question, and it asked her in about as many words as it took to say it, and it did not ask the man of thirty-one anything at all.

“*Ada Renk. You have not been in a book for nineteen years and you got up out of that book in the open in front of about nine people. Will you come at first light, on the days it is written on, and put your two hands on the stone in the ninth room and push it to the far end of the room and push it back.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four answered it, out loud, in the hearing of about nine people, and she did not stop and think about it first, and she said afterwards that she did not have a figure for how long she thought.

Nobody on that bank said sorry and about nine of them had the whole of it said twice.

“*Yes.*”

About four people on that bank had the answer and about nine of them had a woman who has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years answering a question about a door.

Then nothing whatever happened, and about four people said nothing at all for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and nobody said the word brave.

Then she said what she wanted to say first, and she said it before anybody asked her anything, and she said that she was not going to be asked about the asking.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and got up out of that book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and there has been no line for her in it since, and a man of sixty-one read that out in the open at the time.

“*I want three things said on this bank in this order and I want them said before anybody asks me one question about the asking, because about four of you have already had a face on.*”

“*One. I said yes. I heard it and I said yes and I was not asked twice and I am not going to be told that I did not understand it.*”

“*Two. Nobody can be sent for about the asking and nobody can be sent for about the answer. There is no form in this county that says either, and there is no form that says I may not.*”

“*Three. I am not going to be asked about the asking. Not today, not in nine days, not in four years. I will tell anybody what I said and I will tell anybody what it cost and I will not tell anybody what it was like to be asked.*”

She put them in an order before anybody asked her a question, and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting three weeks for somebody to do that.

Then she said the true thing, and it was the whole of what she had come up that bank for, and she gave the working in it without anybody asking for it.

“*Nineteen years. Nineteen is not a figure and I am going to give it the working anyway. Nineteen years is a long time to be a person nobody can find by writing a name down.*”

“*And I have said out loud in this county, in about nine rooms, that the way to find me is to come to my door, and that is the true of it and it has never once cost me anything, and I have said it as a favour to about nine people over about nineteen years.*”

“*And it has taken it about nine weeks and about nine doors and it has not cost it a thing, and it came to a bank at the ninth hour this morning and asked me in front of nine people, and I could not have stopped it by having said no to a man in a coat on any of those nine doors, because there was no man in a coat on any of those nine doors.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for saying that and I am not going to be sorry for saying it and I am not going to let it be said for me in about four days by a man with a hand and a book.*”

Nine weeks and nine doors and not one hour of it, and about four of them had heard her give that number of doors in a room about nine weeks back.

Then she said the name out loud, and she said it once, and it was the fifth time anybody had said it in that county and the first time it had ever been said by a person who had been asked something by it.

“*And I am going to say that name now because about four of you know what asked me and nobody has said it out loud in this county since a man of thirty-one said it flat to a floor a week ago on a Tuesday morning.*”

“*And somebody is going to be asked about this in about four days and I am not going to let the first person to say that name be a man who was not here.*”

“*The First Witness.*”

About four people on that bank had the name and about nine of them had a woman of sixty-four saying it for herself.

Then a man of thirty-one started to ask a question, and stopped, and said out loud that he had stopped, and gave the count, and a woman of twenty-eight said the true of one half of it and refused the other half in about six sentences.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county a hundred and fifty-four days. A hundred and fifty-four is seven hundred and eight less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*I have got a question and I am not going to ask it this morning and I am going to say that out loud, and I have started a sentence and stopped it four times in about six weeks and about four of you have been counting.*”

“*It is: how does it find somebody. And a woman of sixty-four has just told about nine people the whole of how, on a bank, in about forty words, and it took it about nine weeks and about nine doors and it used the doors I told about nine people she should keep using in about nine rooms over about nineteen years.*”

“*And that is the half of it I can say and I am going to stop there on purpose and I would like about four of you to notice that I stopped on purpose and not because I ran out.*”

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She is not one of the nine and she has checked a thing ten times and has not said it once.

“*You have got half of it and you have got the half with a name on it, and the half you have got is that a thing which asks a person nobody can be sent for about is not doing anything a person could have stopped by saying no to a man in a coat.*”

“*That is a finding and it is correct and I am not going to take it off you, and about nine people on this bank have had it now.*”

“*And you have been three sentences into the other half three times this morning and the other half is why it does it that way, and I have not got that, and you have not got that, and I am not going to let either of us stand on a bank and pretend that one half is the whole of a thing about nineteen years of doors.*”

“*And I am not going to give you the rest of it in about nine days out of a favour. If I get it I will say it and if I do not get it you will not have had it from me first.*”

Half of it, and a woman of twenty-eight who has not got the other half, and about nine of them had been asking her for it since the day she came up that cart road.

Then a man of fifty-four said what a man of thirty-one had just refused to make, and he cannot read, and about nine people were on that bank and he said it before he had been asked anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of a piece of printed paper that is lying in the open air about four feet from where he was standing.

“*A man of thirty-one has just said he is not going to make a list of the people in four counties who cannot be found by writing their names down.*”

“*I want that said on this bank by a man of fifty-four who cannot read, because a woman of sixty-four has just told us there are about nine doors and a man of thirty-one has come within about nine sentences of the second half of it and has stopped.*”

“*That is six times since the first day of the hundred and nineteenth week that somebody has got close to a list of things that cannot be sent for and has stopped, and I am the only person anybody can be sent for about four things and nobody has asked me to be a fifth, and I would like somebody to say out loud in about four days that a man of fifty-four said this first and not a man with a hand and a book.*”

She has not been in a book for nineteen years and about four of them on that bank understood why that mattered and the rest of them did not.

---

The fever was sixty weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-four days.

Something that is not a person said a woman’s name out loud on that bank at about the ninth hour and asked her one question about a door, and she answered yes, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about either the asking or the answering.

And a woman of sixty-four said out loud that nineteen years of keeping a door open for about nine people is how a thing found her, and it took it about nine weeks and about nine doors, and it came to a bank where about nine people were standing.

And she said the name herself, because nobody else in that county had said it since the Tuesday of a week before and she was not going to let the first person to say it be a man who was not there.
