# Chapter 729: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Week, And A Thursday, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Asks A Man Of Thirty-One How Long He Was In The Place He Is Not In And A Man Of Sixty-One Cuts Him Off, And The Figure Is Given By Nobody And Refused Out Loud By Two Of Them

Third day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. His four hundred and seventy-ninth morning. Three hundred and twenty-nine days after the settlement. The fever sixty-three weeks and five days. Two hundred and eighty-three days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and it is the seventh day of it and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the day and gave a figure, and stopped at three things.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and has said he has nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it, and has said he is not going to give a fourth thing on that bank.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step. Seventh hour.*”

“*Seven days.*”

“*A full bank holds and it was full on the Monday of last week.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in one day from this morning.*”

“*That is tomorrow, and that is the Friday of this week.*”

“*And I am going to stop while I am still ahead of the nine of you standing here.*”

“*Is a thing I have said before and meant once.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the eighth morning, and nobody was thanked.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line every morning since the Thursday of last week, and has still not answered the fifth thing that was asked of him on the Sunday of a fortnight.

“*Eight mornings.*”

“*And I am still not answering the fifth thing and I still am not going to be angry with anybody about it.*”

“*And the thing I said yesterday about the second line at the top of that sheet is still true this morning and I am not going to improve on it.*”

“*And I have not worked out what I am going to do about any of it and I have got no figure for how long that is going to take and I am not going to make one up.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read thirteen names out loud in the open on that bank for the nineteenth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes, and three of the thirteen have no day against them.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet nineteen times in this county. He was refused a figure for how long three times and has asked for a fourth and will not ask a fifth, and has decided every morning since the Monday of a fortnight ago not to say a name out loud.

“*Nineteen is seven hundred and twenty-nine less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the nineteen in the dark.*”

“*And the two names in ink are on the ninth line and the seventh line.*”

“*The ninth line has no day and the seventh line has one, and I am not going to give anybody a total.*”

“*And I have read the line at the top of it out loud in the open nineteen times and about nine of you have now heard what it says and four of them heard it on Tuesday before anybody stopped me.*”

“*And I read everything on that sheet and there is nothing else on it that I have not read out loud nineteen times.*”

“*I am not going to say whether that is a comfort or not.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said what he has on the leaf after the last head of his own book, and nobody had asked him for it, and he gave the working before he gave it.

Nevin Trask has five lines on that leaf and a number is the last of the five, and he has not added a name to any of them in eleven years.

“*Five lines. Five is seven hundred and twenty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-four.*”

“*And that is a figure of a leaf and not a figure of anything, and I am giving the working first because that is what happens on that bank now.*”

“*The first four are in a hand that is not mine and the last one is a number that is mine, and I have never once worked out who put the first four there.*”

“*A woman of forty has told nine people since Tuesday that a copy leaves out what could not be carried, and I am not going to tell anybody that that is what those four lines are.*”

“*I am going to say that I have had them on that leaf for eleven years and that I have looked at them, and that I have never said what they are out loud, and that a thing being a figure of a leaf is not a thing anybody can be sent for about.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two asked a question on that bank in about nine people’s hearing that nobody had asked him for either.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county, and has been that person eleven years, and asked on Monday whether a line on a sheet is a person and was told it is not.

“*How long was he in the place he is not in.*”

“*That is the whole of it.*”

“*Nine words.*”

“*I have been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years and I have never once been asked that about anybody.*”

“*And I am asking it in the open and about nine of you heard it.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for asking and I am not going to be told I was right to.*”

Then a man of sixty-one cut him off in six sentences, and he has never once said the figure in this county, and the figure was not printed on that bank and was not given to anybody.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county eleven years and has written one number in it in eleven years. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since.

“*One: there is no figure for that and there never has been one in this county.*”

“*Two: it is not because of you and I am not going to let about nine people on that bank think it is because of anything you did this morning.*”

“*Three: a man of sixty-one stopped a count of days himself, on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and he refused the figure of it out loud.*”

“*I gave the reason then and the reason was that it does not go on.*”

“*Four: it is the count of days a man was in a place he is not in and he is not in it now.*”

“*It has nothing to point at, and a day with nothing to point at is not a day anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*Five: and about four of you know that sentence and four of you know where it came from and about nine of you know where the other one came from, and neither of them was written down.*”

“*Six: and I am not going to give it to him and I am not going to let him give it.*”

“*If either of the two of you tries this morning then about nine people are going to hear me refuse it twice.*”

Then the man of thirty-one said the true of that in his own mouth and gave the working, and said the thing he wanted out loud and would not be relieved of.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-five days. A hundred and seventy-five is seven hundred and twenty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four, and nobody has asked him for anything in one hundred and seventy-five days.

“*He is right about five of those six and I am going to say that I am glad of the fifth one.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for being glad of it.*”

“*A hundred and seventy-five is seven hundred and twenty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and seventy-five days.*”

“*And I am not going to give the figure and I am not going to say how long I was where I came from.*”

“*I would like about four of you to be able to tell in four days that a man of thirty-one refused a figure out loud on a Thursday morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of that, and nobody is going to tell me I was brave about it.*”

“*A man of fifty-four who cannot read said the same about himself on a Sunday a fortnight ago and I am not going to be compared with him.*”

Then he said the thing that cost him, and it was the palm of his right hand, and nobody said whether it would keep.

“*And I put my right hand flat on that counter this morning to reach the sheet and the cut across it is forty-four days old.*”

“*Forty-four is seven hundred and twenty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five.*”

“*I have said the working out loud and I am not going to let any of you make it into anything else.*”

“*I said on the Thursday of a fortnight that nobody was going to say whether it will keep, and nobody has.*”

“*I am saying again this morning that I am not going to let it be a thing.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the last of it, in four sentences, and it was about a mark and not about a hand.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she has never once said the number out loud in the open.

“*You have been the outside of a thing for a hundred and seventy-five days and you have been the outside of it by accident.*”

“*And you found out on Tuesday that you are the outside of it on purpose.*”

“*About four of you think that is better and it is not better, it is just known.*”

“*Knowing does not make you anybody’s and it does not put a column in this county with your name at the top of it, and there is not going to be one.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is going to be told this morning that a man of thirty-one finding out what he is was a good thing.*”

“*There is a tomorrow that four of you have not said a word about.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the third morning of a week. Four of them had a figure of days that two men refused out loud in about nine people’s hearing.


---

The fever was sixty-three weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-five days.

A man of about fifty-two asked a man of thirty-one how long he was in the place he is not in, in nine words, in the open. A man of sixty-one cut him off in six sentences and refused the figure twice, and the figure was not printed on that bank and was given to nobody.

And the man of thirty-one refused the figure out loud himself, gave the working for a hundred and seventy-five days, and gave the age of a cut across his right palm as forty-four days and asked again that nobody say whether it will keep, and nobody did.

And a man of sixty-one said he has five lines on the leaf after the last head of his own book and has never once said out loud what the first four of them are.
