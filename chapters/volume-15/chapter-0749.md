# Chapter 749: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Week, And A Wednesday, And The Water Is About Twelve Inches Over The Top Step, And A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That There Are Nine Of These Things In This County And None Of Them Is Being Kept This Week And That Is Not Going To Change Because A Man Of Thirty-One Said A Word Out Loud

Second day of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. His four hundred and ninety-ninth morning. Three hundred and forty-nine days after the settlement. The fever sixty-six weeks and four days. Three hundred and three days since the division. Two hundred and forty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour and it was the third morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading, said the working, and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, all three before anybody had asked him for any of them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and this is the third morning of that.*”

“*Three days. It stood at about eight at this hour on Tuesday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning, and that is the Monday of this week.*”

“*A full bank is about sixteen and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off, and that is the whole of the working.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twenty-eighth morning, and he said it flat and short.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied it by one word. He asked to be in that room once and was given nothing, and asked again once and did not ask again. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-eight mornings.*”

“*And I have said out loud on this bank for four days that I have not varied the asking by one word.*”

“*About four of you have worked out that I am counting the mornings now and about nine of you have not.*”

“*I cannot read the thing on that counter. I have told you that four days running. About four of you keep asking a man of thirty-eight what the third column is instead of asking me what I have seen.*”

“*I have seen six mornings of nothing going into any of the three and I am going to keep seeing them.*”

“*I am not going to make a count of them out loud, because a boy of thirteen was told on the Wednesday of last week not to do that.*”

“*Nobody has asked me about the fifth thing and I am still not answering it and I am still waiting.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty-eight and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and the fourteen names after it, and the eleven words at the top of the third column, and the line at the top of the sheet. It took about nine minutes and a bit over, and the sheet went back on that counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-nine times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Thirty-nine is seven hundred and forty-nine less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-nine in the dark.*”

“*I read the line at the top out loud yesterday for about nine times in a fortnight. This morning I read it once and about four of you looked at me when I started it.*”

“*There is still no column on that thing for a person stopping. There is still no fourth column. Five mornings and nothing in any of the three, and I am not going to say that means something and I said that on Monday of last week and I have not improved on it.*”

“*I am going to say the other half this morning because nobody asked me.*”

“*I have got a child of nine, a lamp that is mine and about nine minutes a morning.*”

“*I have carried a board in the inside of my own coat since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and I have never read it out.*”

“*I am not going to read it out this morning. A man of thirty-one refused to fill in a column on Saturday and I am not going to carry a fourth thing out of a coat and open it in front of nine people.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for thirty-nine and nobody is going to be told I was brave about carrying something for about eight weeks.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said out loud what the state of this county is, in six sentences. The sixth one is the reason and it is his, and about four of you have been waiting three days for a seventh and it is not coming.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and every time anybody has asked him for the figure of it since he has refused out loud.

“*Six and the sixth one is the reason.*”

“*One: there are nine of these things in this county and none of them is being kept this week.*”

“*That is a count of nine and not a count of anybody, and I am giving it first for that reason.*”

“*Two: twenty-nine chairs in a book kept nineteen years, nine of them used, and no rule anywhere in this county that says the ninth of the nine has to be somebody.*”

“*Three: there is a ninth room a hundred and thirty feet under this bank and it has not been kept since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week.*”

“*A keeping is a figure of work and not a figure of people, and nobody in that passage is paid for it, because there is no form in this county that says a person may be paid for pushing a stone to the far end of a room.*”

“*Four: five of nine terms were never put back together and nobody on this bank has seen a mouth put one of them together.*”

“*A term is a job with a name on it and there is a space about as wide as a thumb where the name goes.*”

“*Five: eight of the nine boards went out of this county on the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and none of the eight has been located.*”

“*And none of them is going to be located in about four days.*”

“*Six: I am not going to tell you that a man of thirty-one saying a word out loud on Saturday did this. He did nothing to any of the five, and about four of you know that already.*”

“*And I am sixty-one and I have not worked out whether the parts left out could not be carried or were left out on purpose, and I am not going to.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind and about four of them had a man of sixty-one who had given five facts and a reason and had refused a seventh.

Then a woman of sixty-four said what it costs. She gave the working first, and said nobody was going to be thanked, and said it is not paid back by a man saying a word.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper, and that line has had a day against it since Monday of last week.

“*I have been out of every book in this county for nineteen years and it cost me one thing a day of them.*”

“*That is the whole of the price and I gave it in about nine people’s hearing a fortnight ago. I am giving it again because a man of sixty-one has just given a count of nine and one of those nine is a room I have never been in.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one said a word out loud on Saturday and refused to fill in a column, and about four of you are going to say in four days that that was the best thing anybody in this county has done in a fortnight.*”

“*It was not the best thing anybody has done in a fortnight. It was a man of thirty-one standing on a bank saying a word he had said twice in his own head.*”

“*Nobody on that bank can be sent for about him for it, and about four of you believed the second half of what a man of about fifty-two said about a mark.*”

“*Nothing that has happened this week pays back one thing a day for nineteen years. Not the reading. Not the day on the ninth line. Not a boy of thirteen carrying five counts.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying that on a Thursday morning and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

“*About four of you can tell in four days that I said that out loud on a bank and not in a book. That is all I am claiming.*”

“*And the tenth sentence is not coming, and I have refused one in a fortnight and I am not going to stop refusing them because a man of sixty-one counted nine things in public.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the other half of the count. He asked what a mark is worth a fourth time in a fortnight, nobody answered him, and he said that is what he expected.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county, and has been that person eleven years. He asked in the open on the first day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week what a mark is worth in this county and was answered by nobody. He asked on the fourth day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week what a mark is worth in four counties when there is one of him, and got the other half of the answer in about nine words.

“*Four times in a fortnight and about nine of you have heard all four and about four of you can tell the difference between them.*”

“*What a mark is worth as a figure is owed to me and I am not going to stop asking for it.*”

“*I asked it first and I am going to say that again, because about four of you have started with the second one.*”

“*What I am going to say this morning is the half nobody in this county has said. A mark is worth being sent for and it is not worth being believed, both at once, about the same mark.*”

“*About four of you believed the second half on the fourth day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week, and a woman of twenty-eight told that man of thirty-one in nine sentences that the second half did the damage.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has spent five mornings not filling in three columns of a form, and a woman of forty-four has walked four miles twice in four days about nine houses.*”

“*About nine people on this bank can be sent for about none of that.*”

“*If there were a column with his name at the top of it he would be sent for. He has said so. And if there were one he would be believed, and he has said that too. Neither of those is going to change and I am not going to be thanked for saying either of them again.*”

“*Nobody answered me this morning. That is four. I have had eleven years of those and I am not going to stop keeping them.*”

About four of them had a man of about fifty-two who had asked a fourth time and been answered by nobody. About nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had carried a board in his own coat for about eight weeks and said out loud on a Wednesday morning that he was not going to read it out.

Then a man of thirty-one said the last thing he said that week, and it was about the boards. He gave the working and said nobody was going to be told which half of it was his.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-five days. A hundred and ninety-five is seven hundred and forty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. On Friday of last week he said the word amendment out loud in about nine people’s hearing and said he was not going to fill in any of the three columns.

“*A hundred and ninety-five is seven hundred and forty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety-five days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*A man of sixty-one gave a count of nine and a reason and refused a seventh. A woman of sixty-four gave the price of nineteen years out loud twice in a fortnight.*”

“*Here is the want, and it is the third one this week and it is smaller than the other two and I am giving it before the rest.*”

“*I want the eighth of those nine boards read out loud in the open by the man of fifty-four who has it in his coat, and I am not going to ask him, and about four of you know why and about nine of you do not.*”

“*And here is the cost of wanting it. I am the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks and I have not been asked once in a hundred and ninety-five days, and this morning I have wanted a thing four times in five days and got nothing.*”

“*Nobody is relieved about that and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*Here is what I am going to say and it is not a finding and it is not going to be said twice this fortnight.*”

“*Eight boards went out of this county on the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week. One of them has been inside a man of fifty-four’s own coat for about eight weeks and he has not asked anybody to read it to him.*”

“*And I am the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks and I have not been asked once in a hundred and ninety-five days.*”

“*About nine people have said that out loud in about nine days and I am not going to say it again.*”

“*And that is not going to be an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday forty-nine days ago.*”

“*Forty-nine is seven hundred and forty-nine less seven hundred and I am not going to let it be one.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about this morning. Nobody is going to be thanked, and nobody is going to be told I was right about any of it, and I would rather it was nothing at all in four days.*”

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. Nobody carried it anywhere. Nobody filled anything in.

The fever was sixty-six weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and ninety-five days.

A man of sixty-one counted nine of these things in that county and said none of them is being kept this week, and gave five of them.

Nine chairs in a book kept nineteen years, with no rule about the ninth. A room a hundred and thirty feet under that bank. Five of nine terms never put back together. And eight boards that went out of that county on the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and have not been located.

He said that a man of thirty-one saying a word out loud on Saturday did nothing to any of the five.

And a man of thirty-eight said out loud that he has carried a board in the inside of his own coat since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and has never read it out, and that he is not going to read it out this morning.
