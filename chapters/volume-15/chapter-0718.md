# Chapter 718: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Bank Is Full Again At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step And The Next Time About Ninety Steps Can Be Walked Is In Four Days From That Morning, And Something That Is Not A Person Says A Man Of Fifty-Four’s Name Out Loud At About The Ninth Hour And Asks Him One Question In The Same Nine Words, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Cannot Be Sent For About Either Refuses It In About Nine Sentences And Gives Nobody The Right Of Refusing

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-eighth morning. Three hundred and eighteen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and one day. Two hundred and seventy-two days since the division. Two hundred and eighteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full again at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. He gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked before anybody asked him for it, and about nine of them were on that bank in the wind.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and has said he has nothing to say about a night five times, then stopped and gave two figures for a night instead. He says neither of them is his.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Four days, and it was about twelve at this hour yesterday, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, and that is four days after this bank is full.*”

“*And I gave that same figure wrong on the Monday of last week and I am not going to say by how much, and this morning I have given it right.*”

About four people on that bank had the four days, and about nine of them had a man of forty-three who had given that same figure wrong four days ago and said so.

Then a man of thirty-eight read twelve names out loud in the open for the eighth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes. Three of the twelve have no day against them, and the sheet went back on the counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet eight times in this county and had it in his own hands on about eight of them. He has been refused a figure for how long three times.

Then at about the ninth hour, with about nine people standing on that bank, something that is not a person said a man’s name out loud. It came out of the middle of the bank where there was nothing in it, it was in plain words, and nobody shouted anything back at it.

“Barnaby Crove.”

About nine people on that bank knew that name and about four of them had never said it out loud in that county.

Then it asked him one question, in about as many words as it took to say it, and it did not ask anybody else anything at all.

“*Barnaby Crove. You have held the gravel at the head of that drain for nineteen years and nobody has sent for you about it. Will you come at first light, on the days it is written on, and put your two hands on the stone in the ninth room and push it to the far end of the room and push it back.*”

Nobody on that bank said sorry, and about nine of them had heard a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked the same question in the same words.

Then a woman of forty-four refused it, in her own mouth, and she did it before he had opened his. Nobody could be sent for about her either.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*He has not answered you yet and I am going to use nine sentences before he opens his mouth.*”

“*One: he is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for about four things, and you have just made him a fifth, and I am not going to tell you that is nothing.*”

“*Two: he cannot read, and a man who cannot read cannot refuse you, he can only be spoken for.*”

“*Three: I have kept a book of twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years and not one of those chairs has ever answered me, and I have never once said that was a refusal.*”

“*Four: he has said out loud on that bank about nine times in about six weeks that nobody had asked him to be a fifth thing. About nine of you heard him say it every time he said it.*”

“*Five: a thing that asks a person nobody can be sent for about is not asking a person.*”

“*Six: nobody in four counties can be sent for about the asking, and so there is nothing to appeal it to.*”

“*Seven: I am not going to pretend that stopping the asking is the same thing as stopping you, and about four of you have wanted that to be true this morning.*”

“*Eight: I am not going to give him the right of refusing and I am not going to take it off him. I am not a person you can be sent for about either, and this is my own mouth and not yours.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine sentences, and about nine of them had a woman of forty-four who had refused a thing and given nobody the right of refusing.

Then a man of fifty-four said what he had, before anybody had asked him anything. He said that it was not a refusal, and he cannot read.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on the counter or of either board that has been on it. He is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about four things, and nobody has sent him.

“*I am not going to answer it this morning and I am going to say why. The why is not a refusal, and I am going to say that out loud as well, so that about four of you can tell in four days.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said on the Saturday of last week that if he is asked again his answer will be the same as it was on the Wednesday, which is nothing at all.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight told him in about nine people’s hearing that that is a rule and not an answer.*”

“*And I am not going to use his rule, because I have not got one and I have not had one for about six weeks.*”

“*Here is the true of it. I cannot read. There is a thing in front of me with printing on it, and there are nine words on that thing, and about four of them are about my own two hands.*”

“*And I cannot tell you whether the nine words I have been given are the nine words that are on it.*”

“*And I am not going to stand on this bank and say that I cannot read as though it were an answer. It is not an answer. It is the reason I have not got one.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is going to be thanked for the nine sentences a woman of forty-four just said. I would like somebody to say in about four days that a man of fifty-four did not thank her for them either.*”

About four people on that bank had the reason, and about nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had not answered and had said why and had said it was not a refusal.

Then a woman of forty said what there are now. It took her about four sentences, and she refused to pick one of them.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead, and worked out on the Monday of last week that five of the eleven are in no book in this county.

“*There are three now, and there were two about nineteen years ago, and about nine of us have said out loud that neither of the first two is the right of refusing.*”

“*One: a door that will not open. Two: a door that is shut and held. Three: a man who cannot read.*”

“*And I am not going to say the third is the same as the first two, and I am not going to say it is different.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting all morning for me to pick one and I am not going to.*”

“*And it is a figure of three and it is not a figure of anybody and I am not going to add it to anything.*”

About four people on that bank had the three, and about nine of them had a woman of forty who had not picked one of them.

Then a man of thirty-one offered to ask him the same thing himself, out loud, in the plainest words. He said the whole of it before anybody had finished being quiet.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-four days. A hundred and sixty-four is seven hundred and eighteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and he has offered five times in about three weeks to be the one who asks and been refused five times out loud.

“*I will ask him. One person at a time, in the plainest words anybody in this county has got, standing on his own step, and I will write down what he says and I will bring it back down here and put it on that counter.*”

“*No. Nine sentences, and the ninth one is the reason, and I am saying all of them as a man who cannot read.*” The man of fifty-four did not raise his voice. “*One: you have been refused five times in about three weeks for offering to be the one who asks, and about nine of us said no out loud every time.*”

“*Two: the sixth is about nine seconds late. Something that is not a person asked me on this bank, and you offered to come and ask me the same question before I had opened my mouth.*”

“*Three: that is not a reason to refuse you. That is the reason I am going to say the rest of this in a hurry.*”

“*Four: a woman of forty-four has just used nine sentences on this morning to keep a thing from asking me, and about nine of you heard her do it.*”

“*Five: if you go up that road and ask me the same question in the plainest words, then in about four days somebody is going to come up that road and ask a man of thirty-six, and then a man of thirty-four in an ash.*”

“*And every one of you knows where that ends.*”

“*Six: I do not know where that ends, and I have said that out loud, and I am not going to stand here and work it out in front of about nine people.*”

“*Seven: I am the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about four things, and nobody has sent me, and I want that said.*”

“*I have said it about nine times in about six weeks and not one of you has written it down.*”

“*Eight: nobody on this bank is going to be thanked for anything that happened this morning and I am not going to be thanked for refusing you.*”

“*Nine: and four of you noticed that I gave a count and the rest of you did not, and that is the correct way round, and it is not going to change.*”

Nine sentences, and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting six weeks for a man who cannot read to refuse somebody out loud.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it and did not improve on it, and gave the working. He said out loud that nobody was going to be thanked for him not going up a road.

“*He is right about five of those nine and I am not going to argue with about four of them. The one I want to argue with is the fifth, and I am not going to argue with it either.*”

“*A hundred and sixty-four is seven hundred and eighteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and sixty-four days.*”

“*And I have just offered to be the sixth asking, about nine seconds after something that is not a person did the asking.*”

“*And I am not going up that road. I am not going up it this week, and four of you can tell in four days that I said that in about nine people’s hearing before the light went.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for me not going, and nobody is going to be told I was right, and if about nine of you are relieved about it in about four days I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

About four people on that bank had the five sentences, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-one who was not going up a road he had offered four times.

Then nothing whatever happened for the rest of that morning. About nine people stood on that bank in the wind, and the sheet was still on the counter, and the man of fifty-four had not answered anything.

---

The fever was sixty-two weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-four days.

Something that is not a person said a man of fifty-four’s name out loud on that bank at about the ninth hour and asked him the same question in the same nine words it has asked about four other people. A woman of forty-four who cannot be sent for about either refused it in about nine sentences, and gave nobody the right of refusing.

And a man of fifty-four said he was not going to answer it and said why. The why was that he cannot read, and cannot tell whether the nine words he has been given are the nine words on the sheet, and he said out loud that that is a reason and not an answer.

And a woman of forty said there are three of those things now and refused to pick one of them. A man of thirty-one offered to ask him the same question himself and was refused in nine sentences, and a bank that was full at about sixteen inches said the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from that morning.
