# Chapter 728: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Week, And A Wednesday, And A Man Of Thirty-Four In An Ash Has Been There Ninety-Eight Days And Is Named By Two People Without Being Asked Anything, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud That None Of It Is An Answer To A Question Asked On A Wednesday Of A Fortnight Ago

Second day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. His four hundred and seventy-eighth morning. Three hundred and twenty-eight days after the settlement. The fever sixty-three weeks and four days. Two hundred and eighty-two days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and he gave all three of the things he gives and stopped at three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, and has kept that landing nineteen years, and told nine people on Tuesday that he was not going to give them a fourth thing out of the four he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step, at the seventh hour.*”

“*Six days.*”

“*A full bank holds, and it was full on the Monday of last week, and that is the whole of the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in two days from this morning, and that is the Friday of this week.*”

“*And I said on Tuesday that I would not give you a fourth thing and I am not going to.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting two days for one anyway.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the seventh morning, and he said the thing that had been standing behind the asking since the Sunday of a fortnight.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of last week, and he has still not answered the fifth thing that was asked of him on the Sunday of a fortnight.

“*Seven mornings and I have not varied the asking by one word.*”

“*And here is what has been standing behind it and I am going to say it in the open on a Wednesday morning in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight read a line out of that sheet on Friday morning in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under this bank and he read a second thing as well.*”

“*And I have been read that second thing every morning since, in the open.*”

“*I did not know it was there until yesterday.*”

“*I cannot read, so nobody has been able to tell me whether I heard it or not.*”

“*And I am not going to be angry with him about it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for my working it out and nobody is going to be told I was brave about working it out.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said the true of it in five sentences and did not improve on it and asked for a figure for how long for the fourth time and was refused in one.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet eighteen times in this county, and asked for a figure for how long three times and been refused, and has decided every morning since the Monday of a fortnight ago not to say a name out loud.

“*He is right about all five of those and I am not going to take one of them back.*”

“*I read it on Friday because I read everything on that sheet out loud and I have never once left anything off.*”

“*If I had left it off this time he would never have known.*”

“*And I am not going to be forgiven for that.*”

“*I am not going to be told I did the right thing in a passage in the dark.*”

“*And I asked a man of thirty-one for a figure for how long a fourth time on the Tuesday of this week, and he did not give me one.*”

“*That is three refusals and an answer that was not a figure, and I am not going to ask a fifth time.*”

“*I would like about four of you to be able to tell in four days that I said that.*”

Then a boy of thirteen counted two things aloud that nobody had asked him for, and one of them was about a man in an ash, and he gave the working.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping two counts since the Saturday of a fortnight and has not handed either of them to anybody.

“*Ninety-eight days.*”

“*Ninety-eight is seven hundred and twenty-eight less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*Nobody asked me for that.*”

“*I have got it off a man of sixty-one giving two counts out loud on the Friday of last week, and I have been carrying it since.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for carrying it and I am not going to be told I am brave.*”

“*I am also not going to say what he does in that ash.*”

“*Nobody has asked me what he does in there and a boy of thirteen should not invent it.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four took the shape a man of thirty-one had put on her, and it was four sentences, and she said the two of them were not the same size.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years, and has a name in ink on the ninth line of that sheet with no day against it, and said the name of the thing that is not a person herself, three weeks ago, in about nine people’s hearing.

“*You have just put me next to a person nobody in four counties can be sent for about.*”

“*You have done it in nine people’s hearing, and I am not going to be lined up with them.*”

“*I said yes on a Thursday at about the ninth hour and I have said four times since that I am not going to be asked about the asking.*”

“*I have never once worked out who built the thing that asked me and I am not going to work it out this week either.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four out of every book in this county for nineteen years and a dissident in another county are two different sizes of a person and I am not going to put them in a line.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said one thing about the count she is not keeping, and she said it before anybody had asked her anything.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She checked a thing once at the seventh hour on Tuesday morning and said the count carries no ordinal, and she has not begun again.

“*I am not going to check it again this morning and I am not going to tell you what I am going to do instead.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying that, and I am not going to be the second person this week to say a thing out loud that nobody asked for and that costs somebody.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it and gave the working and then said the other thing, which was the only reason anybody on that bank would remember the morning.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-four days. A hundred and seventy-four is seven hundred and twenty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*She is right about four of those four sentences and I am not going to improve on what I said.*”

“*A hundred and seventy-four is seven hundred and twenty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and seventy-four days.*”

“*And I wanted about a week ago to have a thing like yesterday on me that I could say out loud and put on a counter and ask nine people to look at.*”

“*And I have got it, and it is not mine.*”

“*About four of you on this bank know exactly what I wanted it for and what it is.*”

“*And here is the other thing and I am going to say it before anybody says it for me.*”

“*None of yesterday is an answer to the thing that was asked on the Wednesday of a fortnight ago.*”

“*I am not going to let it be one, and I am not going to let it be a reason.*”

“*Nobody in this county has answered it and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*I am not going to arrange to be asked, and I am not going to say the shape of it a second time.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two asked the last question of the morning, and a woman of forty-four did not answer it, and gave the reason in four sentences.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county, and has been that person eleven years, and was told on the Monday of last week by a woman of forty-four that a line on a sheet is not a person and that it is not him.

“*A mark is a thing somebody puts next to a rule to say the rule has not been checked.*”

“*And can a mark say no.*”

“*That is all of it.*”

“*Nine words, and I have been a mark for eleven years.*”

“*Nobody in this county is going to be sent for about me either.*”

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and she has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and neither is the book.

“*I am not going to answer that, and I am not going to pretend I did not hear it.*”

“*About four of you have noticed that I did not answer.*”

“*Here is the reason and it is the reason I have given about nine times in about six weeks.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about a man of about fifty-two either.*”

“*An answer from me would be nine people agreeing on a thing.*”

“*And the last thing nine people agreed on this week cost a man of fifty-four a word he has not got back.*”

“*I would rather a question went unanswered in the open than answered in a lump.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the second morning of a week. Four of them had a mark that had been asked whether it could say no, and nobody had given it an answer.

---

The fever was sixty-three weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-four days.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read said out loud that he has been read a second thing off a printed sheet every morning for eight mornings and did not know it was there. A man of thirty-eight said he reads everything out loud and has never left anything off.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud that none of the finding of the day before is an answer to a question asked on the Wednesday of a fortnight ago, and is not going to let it be one.

And a man of about fifty-two asked whether a mark can say no, in nine words, and a woman of forty-four did not answer him, and gave the reason that an answer from her would be nine people agreeing on a thing.
