# Chapter 706: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which The Water Comes Off All Ninety Of The Steps In The Night For The Fifth Time Since The Saturday Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week And About Nine People Go Down About Ninety Steps For The First Time In This Volume, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says The Light Under The Eighth Door-Stone Is The Width Of His Own Hand And It Was The Width Of His Own Thumb Eleven Days Ago, And There Is A Chair In The Eighth Room And Nobody Has Ever Been In It, And Nobody Goes In

First day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and fifty-sixth morning. Three hundred and six days after the settlement. The fever sixty weeks and three days. Two hundred and sixty days since the division. Two hundred and six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps at the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and nine or ten of them went down at about the ninth hour.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years, and it is the only sentence anybody can say about him that has not had to be corrected. A bank fills in about four days and empties in about three, and the next time about ninety steps can be walked is about four days after that bank is full.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off in the night, and it was full on the Friday of last week, and four days is the working, and it came in the night and I was on that landing at the second hour because there is nothing else to do at the second hour.*”

“*And that is the fifth time it has come off in the night since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I am not going to tell you how many days that is because there is no figure for a river and I have given you four numbers this week already.*”

About four people on that landing had the five times and about nine of them were going down.

They went down at about the ninth hour, and the man of forty-four bailed for about two hours and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his, and the man of thirty-one went fifth with his hands and nothing else in them.

Then they went past the last of the beams and about nine feet past the place where the gravel is walked, and along about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage at the same fall, and up the step.

There were nine openings in the wall with two hands cut over eight of them and one hand over the ninth, and there was a lamp on a hook in the roof of the ninth of them about a quarter full of oil, and it was burning, and it was not lit and it was not put out.

And in the ninth room the shelf cut in the wall about the height of a hand was empty, and the door-stone about five feet high and about two feet across at the top was shut where it had been put, and a man of fifty-four put his own hand down into the gap under it and said there was water in it and said how much and said that it was an inch and about the thickness of his own finger.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of anything any of them have found.

“*An inch and about my own finger. Same as it was on the Thursday of last week and same as it was on the Sunday before that.*”

“*And there is a man of fifty-four in this passage whose whole week has been that figure and I am not going to be asked about it again and I am not going to be thanked for it, and about nine of you know exactly what it is and about four of you do not and that is the correct way round.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight went along the wall to the eighth opening and stopped, and said nothing for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and then gave a figure with the working, and it was not the figure anybody had been carrying about for about seven weeks.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, and went down a hundred and thirty feet of passage with his own lamp. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark.

“*The light under the eighth door-stone is the width of my own hand at the knuckles.*”

“*On the Friday of the hundred and twenty-first week I put my own hand flat on that stone and said about a thumb’s width, and I said it in front of about nine people, and I gave the working then and I am giving it now.*”

“*Eleven days. Eleven is seven hundred and six less six hundred and ninety-five. And it was a thumb then and it is a hand now, and it is not my thumb and it is not my hand that has got wider.*”

“*And the eighth of these nine has been shut since before I was in this county and about four hundred years before that, and I have not been in it, and nobody in this county has been in it, and I have been carrying a thumb’s width of light under that door in my own head for about seven weeks.*”

About four people in that passage had the eleven days and about nine of them had a hand.

Then a man of forty-one gave one reason why nobody was going to go in and he had given a version of it before, and a man who cannot see well gave a second, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read gave a third, and none of the three was about the light.

“*I am not going in and I have said that twice and I am going to say it a third time and it is not going to be about the water.*”

“*If I go and look at it on a morning when the water is off, then I will go and look at it, and then I will go in. And the way in is two people before the light and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether they will still be doing it.*”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well and is the fifth line of one of the two sheets of deal in this county.

“*And the second reason is mine and it is about two feet and it took about nine seconds to work out.*”

“*That gap was a thumb and it is a hand and that is not a door settling and that is not water under a stone. A door-stone that has been shut since before any of us came here does not come open a thumb’s width in about four hundred years because of anything anybody has done this fortnight.*”

“*So there is something in that room that is about as big as a hand across and about as thick as a hand, and it has come close to the door in about eleven days, and I am not going to guess what it is with a lamp in my hand in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under a bank.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said the third reason, and he cannot read, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

“*Nobody has ever asked me whether I would be one of the two hands in that room and I am not going to find out by being in it.*”

“*That is the whole of my reason and I have been in this passage about a quarter of an hour and I have put my own hand under two door-stones this morning and I am not going in either of them.*”

“*I want that said on this passage floor by a man of fifty-four who cannot read, because somebody is going to say in about four days that a man of fifty-four had a chance to see it and did not take it, and I am not going to let that be the first sentence about me.*”

Nobody in that passage asked the third reason twice, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-four in an ash who had not been asked anything in seventy-one days.

Then they put the lamp on the floor of that gallery and put it flat against the stone and looked through the gap, and it took about four of them a while, and about nine of them were in that passage, and nobody went in.

And there was a chair in that room.

“*There is a chair.*”

“*That is all of it and that is a stupid thing to say and I am saying it anyway. That is a chair.*”

Nobody in that passage said anything at all and about four of them had a chair and about nine of them had not believed it for about four seconds.

“*And it is not cut in the bank. Everything in these nine rooms is cut in the bank and there is not one stick of wood in a hundred and thirty feet of passage.*”

“*And it is about as high as the knee of a man of forty-one and it has a back on it. And I am not going to say where it came from because I do not know and about nine of you are standing in a passage guessing and I can hear the guessing.*”

“*I am going to say the only thing I know and it is the plainest thing I have said since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*Somebody has sat in it. And nobody has ever been in that room.*”

About four people in that passage had somebody has sat in it and about nine of them had a room that has never been opened.

Then a woman of forty-four said what a chair is and what a sheet is, and it took her about four sentences and she said she was not going to say either of them again.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, has kept that book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years, and has never once been able to make anybody answer one of them.

“*A chair is not a form. That is the whole of what I am going to say and I have got a book of twenty-nine of them and I have never once been able to make anybody answer one of them.*”

“*A form does not make a chair. A form goes to the end of what it was made for and stops. A person makes a chair, and a person is in the room, and that is the difference and there is no figure of it.*”

“*And I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am aware that I have said four sentences to a lamp-lit floor and about nine of you cannot see what I am looking at.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and did not get, and said it in his own mouth, and nobody finished it for him.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and is standing a hundred and thirty feet under a bank with his hands empty. He has been in this county a hundred and fifty-two days, and a hundred and fifty-two is seven hundred and six less five hundred and fifty-four, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it and there is no form that renews him.

“*I want to go in there and I am going to say it out loud so that about four of you can hear me say it and be able to tell in nine days that I said it and did not do it.*”

“*And nobody is going to let me and none of the three reasons was about me and I am not going to pretend any of them was.*”

“*There is a man of forty-one who has said he will not go in because nobody has ever been asked whether they will still be doing it. There is a man about fifty-two who cannot see well who has said he will not guess. And there is a man of fifty-four who cannot read who has said he is not going to find out by being in it.*”

“*I have got nothing behind any of those three and I am nobody’s, and I am not going to put a man of thirty-one’s want against a man of forty-one’s nineteen years to see which one weighs more, and I would like about four of you to be able to tell in nine days that I said that.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for not going in and I am not going to be told I was right, and if about nine of you are going to say anything about it in about four days I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

A chair about as high as a man of forty-one’s own knee, and a man of thirty-one standing in a passage saying out loud that he wanted to go in and not doing it.

Then they went up, and the man of thirty-eight carried the lamp up last and did not say a word about the chair on the way past it, and nine or ten of them stood at the head of that flight in the wind.

---

The fever was sixty weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-two days.

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night and about nine people went down about ninety steps for the first time since the first day of this week, and nothing fell down.

The light under the eighth door-stone is the width of a man’s own hand at the knuckles and it was the width of his own thumb eleven days ago, and nobody has ever been in that room, and there is a chair in it that is not cut in the bank and that somebody has sat in.

And nobody went in, for three reasons, and none of the three was about the light.
