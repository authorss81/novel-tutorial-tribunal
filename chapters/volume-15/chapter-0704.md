# Chapter 704: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Second Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Sheet Of Printed Paper That Was Left On The Counter Of A Landing Is Read Out Loud On A Bank Nine Feet Above That Landing In The Open With Nine Lines And Eleven Names In It And Two Of The Eleven Are The Same Name Twice And Two Of Them Have No Day At All, And A Woman Of Forty Asks For The Day Against Each Name And Gets It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Calls Eleven On Nine Lines Too Many Hands And Is Taken Off In Seven Sentences

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-second week. His four hundred and fifty-fourth morning. Three hundred and four days after the settlement. The fever sixty weeks and one day. Two hundred and fifty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the sixth day, and then he said the other half of it, which was new, and nine or ten of them heard it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. He found a sheet of printed paper on the counter of his own landing on the Saturday morning and he did not bring it inside and he did not take it anywhere.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Six days, and it is a full bank and it is holding at sixteen, and there is no more working to give you than that.*”

“*And there is a sheet of printed paper on that counter and it was not there when I shut the door on Friday night and it was there when I opened it on Saturday morning, and a man of thirty-six put it down and went down that road and he was not gone more than about four hours.*”

“*And I have said for about four days running that I am not going to be the man who brings it in. I have not brought it in. It is on my counter and it is in the open air and anybody can put a hand on it and I have not picked it up.*”

“*Nobody thanks me for that and nobody is going to forgive me for having had it on that counter since Saturday morning, and nobody is going to be permitted to tell me afterward that it was not my fault.*”

About four people on that bank had the six days and about nine of them had a sheet of printed paper lying in the open about four feet from where they were standing.

Then a man of thirty-eight asked to read it out loud on that bank, in the open, and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him to, and he said out loud why he wanted it done there and not indoors before he did it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he reads anything put in front of him and has done so in this county for about six weeks.

“*I want it read out loud on this bank in the open air and not in a room, and I want the reason said before I start and not afterwards.*”

“*A thing that is read out loud in the open in front of about nine people is a thing about nine people have heard. A thing that is read out loud indoors in front of two people is a thing two people have heard, and there is no form in this county that says two is enough.*”

“*Nobody thanks me for it, and the man who thought of it is a woman of forty who has been asking for a day against a name in every room in this county since before I came up that plank, and I have watched her do it, and I am not going to take it off her.*”

Then he read it, and he read it in the order it was written, and he counted twice out loud and gave the working, and nine or ten of them stood in the wind on a bank nine feet above a landing and listened.

“*It is about as long as my own forearm and about as wide as both my hands, and it is printed and not written, and there are nine lines ruled across it and nothing above the first and nothing below the last.*”

“*Nine lines. Nine of them. And on each line there are four things and I am going to name the four before I read any of them out, because a man of thirty-one has already made one shape out of this today and I am not going to let him make another one that sticks.*”

“*A place. A mark cut into it about the size of a thumb and square. A name. And a day.*”

“*Nine lines and eleven names. Nine and eleven. That is the whole of it and I counted it twice and the second time I counted it out loud a second time so that about four of you could hear me do it.*”

“*And two of the eleven are the same name twice. Not two names that are alike. The same name, written twice, on two different lines, on two different days.*”

About four people on that bank had the eleven and about nine of them had the two.

Then a man of forty-one said one of the two was a man who has been dead about thirty years, and he said where he had the name, and he said he had not written it down anywhere.

“*One of them is a man who has been dead about thirty years, and I am the only person in four counties who has heard that name out loud, and I heard it from a woman of about sixty in a village about four days on this road.*”

“*I asked her his name on the fourth day of the hundred and twentieth week, which was a Friday, and that is sixteen days ago, and she gave it to me and I have not written it anywhere and there is no column in any book in this county that a name like that could go in.*”

“*And it is the same name. I have heard one name read out this morning and it is the name she gave me, and I am going to say that out loud on this bank because a man of thirty-eight read it out in the open and if I do not say it then about nine people are going to be standing here holding two things that are the same thing apart.*”

“*And a name on a piece of printed paper is not a person being able to say no. That man has been dead about thirty years and he cannot say no and I am the only person who can tell you that and I am telling you it about eleven seconds too late.*”

Nobody on that bank improved on him and about four of them had eleven seconds and about nine of them had a name that cannot answer.

Then a woman of forty asked for the day against each name, and she opened with the three words, and he gave it to her one at a time, and she wrote nothing down because she does not write.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She is on two sheets of deal in this county and she knows what a day on a line does and what a day on a line does not do.

“*Say what you are standing in front of, and say the day against each one of them, and I am not asking for a reason and I am not asking for a name twice.*”

“*The days. All nine of the eleven, in the order they are on the sheet, and where there is no day say there is no day and do not put anything in the place where the day would be.*”

Then he read the days out loud, and two of the eleven had no day, and a woman of forty said what that is.

“*Nine lines. Eleven names. Seven days, five days, four days, four days, five days, seven days, nine days, and then two blanks.*”

“*And the two blanks are not old. They are not rubbed out. They are not on the same kind of line as the others. They are set apart about a finger’s width from the day column, and there is a space between them and the day column on every other line, and a man of thirty-eight says the two of them are about the same place as everything else and I am going to say they are not.*”

“*A day is not a judgement. I have said that on a plank in this county and I am saying it on this bank and it is still true.*”

“*A name with a day against it is a name somebody can be sent for about, because the day is a place a man sat in a chair and about nine people can go and look at the chair. That is the whole reason I have asked for days since before any of you were in this county.*”

“*And a name with no day on it is a name nobody can be sent for about. Not because it is secret. Because there is nothing to point at.*”

“*And there are two of them and I want that said in front of about nine people, and I do not know who wrote them and I have decided in about four seconds that I am not going to find out today.*”

Two blanks, and a name nobody can be sent for about, and about nine of them on that bank had been standing next to a woman of twenty-nine for about four seconds without knowing it.

Then a man of thirty-one said what eleven names on nine lines meant, and he said it as a figure of a crowd and got the wrong end of it, and a man of thirty-eight took it off him in seven sentences in the open air.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county a hundred and fifty days, and a hundred and fifty is seven hundred and four less five hundred and fifty-four, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it.

“*Eleven names for nine rooms is two too many. That is over-committed. Somebody is going to be going down there on a morning when nine people have already been and one of them is going to find out late that it was done, and that is a figure of too many people and we should say so now.*”

“*Eleven is not a figure of anybody and I have been on this bank about four minutes and you have got it wrong and I am going to take it off you in seven sentences.*”

“*A chamber that is a day is kept by two hands once a day before the light, about a minute, about four hundred years. There is no such thing as too many hands on a keeping. If fifty people came every morning it would still be two hands and about a minute and it would still be one day.*”

“*So eleven is not eleven people doing too much. Eleven is a number on a piece of paper and it is a figure of the paper.*”

“*And the two that are the same name twice is not one person being given two jobs. It is one question put twice, or one person put in twice by somebody who did not know, and either way the work is the same and the writing is the difference.*”

“*You have been in this county a hundred and fifty days and you keep making people into numbers because a number is the only thing you can check. A number of hands is not a thing. A number of names is not a thing. And I have just watched a woman of forty read two blanks out loud and get a figure nobody in four counties has.*”

That was the plainest correction anybody had made on that bank in about six weeks, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody argued with him.

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count of the whole of it out loud, in the order it was said, and then refused to write any of it, and then said the one thing he wanted said, and nine or ten of them heard it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. He wrote one number in that book in eleven years and announced it three days before he wrote it.

“*One sheet. Nine lines. Eleven names. Two of the eleven the same name twice. Two of the eleven with no day against them. One of the eleven a man dead about thirty years.*”

“*And one of the eleven is a woman of forty-four who said yes twice on a step four miles up that road on a Friday and on a Wednesday about two different doors, and she is the one who is in this county and who can hear me and who has not said a word this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to write one of these eleven in this book and here are three reasons and I have given them before.*”

“*And I am not going to be the man who says that a form is the worst thing that has ever come up that road either. I have been keeping a book eleven years and I have had a Crown put over the top of it and taken off it and a man does not stand on a bank in this county and call that the worst thing, because he has not got a worse one, and nothing is improved by a man improving on what has come up that road.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for any of this, and in particular the man of thirty-eight is not going to be thanked for reading it out in the open, because a thing read out in the open in front of about nine people is a thing about nine people have heard and that is not a small thing to be handed to somebody in front of nine other people without saying so first.*”

Eleven names on a counter four feet behind that bank and nobody keeping it, and about nine of them stood there on a Sunday morning having heard every one of them read out loud.

---

The fever was sixty weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty days.

A sheet of printed paper that was left on the counter of a landing on Saturday morning was read out loud on a bank nine feet above that landing on Sunday morning in the open air, and nobody kept it and nobody brought it inside.

Nine lines and eleven names, and two of the eleven are the same name twice and one of those is a man who has been dead about thirty years, and two of the eleven have no day against them at all, and a name with no day on it is a name nobody can be sent for about.
