# Chapter 730: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Week, And A Friday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Of The Steps In The Night And About Nine People Go Down At The Fifth Hour Because A Keeping Is Done Before The Light, And A Man Of Forty-One Says The Day He Has Named Out Loud In A Passage And Nobody Puts Their Hands On The Stone

Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. His four hundred and eightieth morning. Three hundred and thirty days after the settlement. The fever sixty-three weeks and six days. Two hundred and eighty-four days since the division. Two hundred and thirty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps at the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working, and he had been on that landing at the second hour because there is nothing else to do at the second hour.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and about ninety steps of the work are under the bank he was standing on, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour.*”

“*It came off in the night.*”

“*It was full on the Monday of last week and four days is the working.*”

“*The eighth time it has come off in the night since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you how many days that is.*”

“*There is no figure for a river and I have given you about nine numbers this week already.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the ninth morning.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He asked for the ninth line on the Thursday of last week and has asked every morning since, and he has still not answered the fifth thing that was asked of him on the Sunday of a fortnight.

“*Nine mornings.*”

“*And I am going to go on asking on Monday and I am not going to be thanked for the ninth one any more than for the first.*”

“*And I have still not answered the fifth thing, and nobody has asked me, and I am still waiting.*”

“*About four of you have not worked out that those are two different things and that is the correct way round.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read thirteen names out loud in the open on that bank for the twentieth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes, and two of the thirteen names are in ink and not in the print.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet twenty times in this county. He was refused a figure for how long three times and asked for a fourth and will not ask a fifth, and has decided every morning since the Monday of a fortnight ago not to say a name out loud.

“*Twenty mornings.*”

“*Twenty is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty in the dark.*”

“*Thirteen names, two of them in ink, and three with no day against them.*”

“*I am not going to give anybody a total of any of that.*”

“*And this morning is a Friday and about four of you have not said a word about it since Monday.*”

“*I am not going to say a word about it either, because I am not the one who is saying it.*”

“*The sheet went on that counter and nobody carried it anywhere and it will be on that counter tomorrow.*”

Then about nine people went down about ninety steps at about the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light, and the water was off all ninety of them and nothing fell down.

They went down at about the fifth hour and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his, and the man of forty-four bailed for about an hour and said nothing about it, and about nine of them stood in a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage with a lamp on the floor of a gallery.

Then a man of forty-one said the day he had named, in the passage, at the fifth hour, before the light, in about nine people’s hearing, and nobody asked him a question about it.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine and has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years. He told about nine people on the Wednesday of last week that there were two mornings in the next fortnight when that water would be off all ninety before the light, and that he was not going to say which.

“*Nine days.*”

“*Nine is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and twenty-one, and that is the Wednesday of last week.*”

“*I told about nine of you standing in this passage that I would say it in a passage.*”

“*This is the Friday of this week and it is the last of the two and I did not name it on that bank.*”

“*I am not going to be asked about that either.*”

“*I am not going to write it down and there is not one of you who is going to write it down.*”

“*If anybody writes it down I will know, because one of you cannot read and he will tell me.*”

“*And here is what I said I would do and I am saying it before I do it.*”

“*A man of sixty-one has said about nine times in about six weeks that the working goes before the figure.*”

“*I am the ninth of nine and I have been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and I am going to stop being the ninth of nine on this morning at about the fifth hour before the light.*”

“*And I am not going to stop in the open, and I am not going to stop on a Wednesday.*”

“*I did not move it to a Thursday because a Wednesday is easier.*”

About nine of them stood in that passage and nobody said a word, and about four of them had a day that was not in a book and not on a sheet and not in anybody’s keeping.

Then nobody put their hands on the stone.

It took nobody about a minute. The stone is about as big as a door and it goes about nine feet along a groove and then back again, and it was not moved at all, and nobody in that passage put a hand on it, and the second pair of hands that has been in that room a hundred and thirty feet under this bank for nineteen years was not there.

And nothing fell down.

Then a man of thirty-eight said what he had seen in the ninth room in about nine seconds, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and went down a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage with his own lamp, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark.

“*There is a chair in the eighth room and I have not been in it and nobody in this county has been in it.*”

“*There is a lamp burning on a hook in the ninth of the nine rooms and it was not lit and I did not put it out.*”

“*And there are two worn places in the floor of that room.*”

“*One of them is about as deep as the thickness of my own finger, and that one has not got any shallower this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to put my hand in it and I am not going to be thanked for saying that.*”

“*About four of you would like me to and about nine of you would not.*”

Then a woman of forty gave the count of the things there are, and refused to pick one again, and nobody picked one for her.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead, and gave a count of three on the Sunday of a fortnight and refused to pick one of them.

“*There were three of those things and I gave the count and I did not pick one.*”

“*I am not going to pick one this morning either.*”

“*And I am not going to say that a man of forty-one not doing a thing at the fifth hour before the light is a fourth of them.*”

“*The first three have been in this county about nineteen years and this one is about a minute old.*”

“*And I am not going to say what a minute old thing is.*”

“*I have said that about nine times in about six weeks and four of you are waiting for me to.*”

“*A man of forty-one has a day now that nine people heard and nobody can be sent for about.*”

“*And I have asked for a day against a name for about nineteen years and I am not going to be told this morning that I have got what I asked for.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it in about nine sentences, and said out loud that nobody was going to be thanked for him not putting his hands on the stone.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-six days. A hundred and seventy-six is seven hundred and thirty less five hundred and fifty-four, and he offered on the Wednesday of a fortnight ago to be one of the two hands and was refused in nine sentences.

“*I offered on the Wednesday of a fortnight ago to be one of the two hands in this room, and I was refused in nine sentences.*”

“*The ninth one was that a man of forty-one did not want the last thing his two hands did to have a man of thirty-one in it that nobody can be sent for about.*”

“*And this morning there are no two hands at all.*”

“*The first thing in a hundred and seventy-six days that has made an offer of mine unnecessary.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of it, and nobody is going to tell me I was right about offering.*”

“*About four of you are going to say in about four days that a man of thirty-one was right about something and I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

Then a boy of thirteen read two counts back that he had been keeping since the Saturday of a fortnight, and gave the working for both, and a man of thirty-eight told him to keep them.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about, and he has been keeping two counts since the Saturday of a fortnight and has not handed either of them to anybody.

“*Twenty-nine days.*”

“*Twenty-nine is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and one, and I counted it on the road coming down and nobody asked me.*”

“*And twenty readings.*”

“*Twenty is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and ten, and there was not one of the twenty in the dark.*”

“*And I am not going to hand either of them to a man of thirty-eight like a present.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for counting them, and I am keeping them on Monday.*”

Then they went up, and the man of thirty-eight carried the lamp up last and did not say a word about the light on the way past it, and about nine of them stood at the head of that flight in the wind.

The fever was sixty-three weeks and six days old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-six days.

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night and about nine people went down at the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light.

And a man of forty-one said the day he had named, in that passage, at the fifth hour, before the light, in nine people’s hearing. It is the Friday of this week, and he said he was going to stop being the ninth of nine at about the fifth hour before the light, and he did not move it to a Thursday because a Wednesday is easier.

And nobody put their hands on the stone, and the second pair of hands was not there, and nothing fell down.

And a woman of forty said she is not going to say what a minute old thing is, and a man of thirty-one said the offer he made a fortnight ago has become unnecessary and that he is not relieved.
