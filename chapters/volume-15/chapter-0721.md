# Chapter 721: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Wednesday, And The Last Morning Of A Fortnight Said Out Loud Before Anybody Had Asked, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says The Day In The Open To Take It Off A Man Who Said He Would Not

Second day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventy-first morning. Three hundred and twenty-one days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and four days. Two hundred and seventy-five days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour for the seventh morning running and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the day and a figure. A wind came up off that water and went down again while he was doing it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has stood on that landing nineteen years. He has said he has nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it and gave two figures for a night instead, and he says neither of the two is his.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Seven days, and a full bank holds.*”

“*And there is no more working in that than there has been since the Sunday of last week.*”

“*The next time about ninety steps can be walked is one day from this morning, and that is the Thursday of this week.*”

“*It came off in the night the last two times and it is not coming off this morning.*”

“*And I gave you that same figure wrong on the Thursday of last week and I am not going to say by how much.*”

“*And there is a boy of thirteen on this bank who keeps two counts that nobody has asked him for.*”

“*Neither of the two is about water, and he did not offer me either of them, and I am not going to ask.*”

Four of the people on that bank had the one day. The rest of them had a Thursday of this week.

Then the man of forty-three said the other half of it, and nobody had asked him for it.

“*A bank that is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth.*”

“*That is four days holding and not four days filling.*”

“*I have been saying four days to you for a fortnight and I have never once said which four.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked about it until I have given you all three of the other things first.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the sheet out loud in the open for the eleventh morning running. It took about nine minutes, and there were twelve names on it, and there had been twelve yesterday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has read that sheet eleven times in this county and has been refused a figure for how long three times and has asked for a fourth.

“*Twelve names, and three of the twelve have no day against them, and that has not changed since the Friday of last week.*”

“*I said on that Friday that I would read the new one with the other eleven, and I have read it every morning since.*”

“*And four of you have worked out why I read it, and the reason is that the reason somebody wrote it is not a reason anybody in this county can do anything about.*”

“*And I have not got a figure for when the next one comes.*”

“*The only two people in this county who have given a figure for that have each said out loud that they gave it early on purpose, and neither of them asked me.*”

“*About nine minutes. Eleven mornings.*”

“*Eleven is seven hundred and twenty-one less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*The tenth of them was yesterday and the seventh was the Saturday of last week.*”

“*And one morning of those eleven has nothing read on it and it is the Wednesday of last week, and I am not going to let about four of you work it out between you and think they have caught me.*”

The eleven went into a few heads on that bank. Four of them had a man of thirty-eight who had asked for a fourth figure and had been refused three.

Then a man of forty-one said the day out loud, and it was the last morning of it, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine. He has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and he is nobody’s, and he has been asked about a day three times and has never once been asked to move it.

“*This is the last morning of a fortnight.*”

“*I said it in a room like this one a week ago, at the fifth hour, before the light, and I said it to the nine of you standing there and not to the room.*”

“*Seven days. Seven is seven hundred and twenty-one less seven hundred and fourteen.*”

“*And nothing is going to happen on this bank this morning.*”

“*I am not going to be the reason a bank has nine fewer people on it, and about four of you have been waiting a week to be told one way or the other.*”

“*A fortnight is a count and a count is not a place.*”

“*There is no form in this county that says a fortnight ends and there is no form in this county that says it does not.*”

“*I have not got a column and I have not got a book, and I am not going to write my day on a piece of printed paper four hundred miles off.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word, and the whole of it stood there.

Then he said the part that had been standing behind the rest of it for a week, and he gave the reason, and it was not the reason anybody had expected.

“*I am not going to name the day on this bank, and I am not going to name it on a Wednesday.*”

“*And I am not going to name it on a Thursday, because a Thursday is a Wednesday that has been moved to make it fit.*”

“*I said yesterday that I would not do that, and I am not doing it this morning in front of you.*”

“*Here is the reason, and it is a man of fifty-four’s sentence, and I am giving it back with his words because he gave it to me first and I have been carrying it about for two days.*”

“*A thing about nine people have is a thing about nine people can be sent for about.*”

“*So a day said out loud on this bank is about nine people.*”

“*And about nine people can be sent to stand on a step and ask me about it.*”

“*Nobody in four counties can be sent for about me, and there are a great many who could be sent for about a day I said out loud.*”

“*And I have been the ninth of nine for nineteen years, and every morning of the nineteen years has been said out loud by somebody and written down by nobody.*”

“*I have found out in about two days what a mouth is for.*”

Then he said where he was going to say it. He said it in the plainest words he had, and he did not say which morning.

“*I am going to say it in the passage, at the fifth hour, before the light, on a morning when that water is off all ninety and the nine of us can walk down.*”

“*There are two of those mornings in the next fortnight and I am not going to tell you which.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked about it and I am not going to be written down.*”

“*If any of you write it down I will know, because one of you cannot read and he will tell me.*”

Four people on that bank had the two mornings. Nobody on that bank had either of them.

Then a woman of twenty-eight said what that was, in four sentences, flat, and before anybody had finished being quiet.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine. She cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from, and she has said the same sentence nine times without saying it, and she said yesterday that she was not going to begin again.

“*That is a rule, and you have built it in front of nine people in about four sentences.*”

“*I told a man of thirty-one what a rule is, in nine people’s hearing, on the Saturday of a fortnight.*”

“*A rule is a thing that works without a person.*”

“*You have made a thing that works without you, and you have made it out of two mornings, and neither of the two mornings knows what is coming.*”

“*And the fifth sentence is not coming and I am not going to say why.*”

“*I said the same about nine sentences from a man of sixty-one two mornings ago and I am not going to improve on it.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the day out loud in the open air to take it off him, before anybody had asked him for anything. It was the morning of the following day.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-seven days. A hundred and sixty-seven is seven hundred and twenty-one less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*Tomorrow. It is tomorrow, and that water will be off all ninety at the sixth hour.*”

“*I am going to say it now so that a man of forty-one does not have to carry it a week with nobody helping him.*”

“*That is not a favour and I want you to hear what it is.*”

“*It is the fourth one of those I have done on this bank in about nine days.*”

“*I have wanted for a hundred and sixty-seven days to be on a piece of paper somewhere, and I have wanted for a hundred and sixty-seven days for somebody to ask me one question.*”

“*Neither of those is a reason to open my mouth about a man’s day.*”

Then the man of forty-one said the true of it in four sentences. He did not raise his voice and nobody improved on it.

“*You have just made it about nine people who can be sent for about a morning I have not said.*”

“*And you have made it on a Wednesday, which is the thing I said I would not do.*”

“*That is the same mouth twice, and I am not going to be angry with you about it.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight has said what your own mouth is, and you have said the true of it every time she has said it, and I am not going to thank her for it in front of you.*”

“*And there is nothing on this bank now that can be put back, including the part where nobody knows which of the two mornings it is.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for not being angry, and nobody is going to tell me I was right about not moving it.*”

The whole of that bank had a man of thirty-one who could not keep a thing in his own mouth on a Wednesday morning. Four of them had a woman of twenty-eight who had said what it was in four sentences and had not enjoyed saying it.

Then the man of thirty-one gave the working for the figure that is his and said the true of the rest of it. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong.

“*A hundred and sixty-seven is seven hundred and twenty-one less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and sixty-seven days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*And this morning I made a man’s unsaid morning into a day that nine people could be sent for about.*”

“*Nobody asked me to. I would like somebody to say in four days that I did it on a Wednesday and not on a Monday.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the last of it, in four sentences, and said she had been keeping the other half of it since the Saturday of last week.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and nineteen years of it have been a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*You have told us there are two mornings and you have not said which.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has said one of them out loud in the open, so there is one morning now, and it is a Thursday, and he has said it in nine people’s hearing.*”

“*And you are not going to be asked about that and nobody is going to thank him for it, and I have been keeping that half since he said it.*”

“*I have been waiting about nine seconds for somebody else to say it first.*”

“*And I am not going to say that a man of forty-one has lost a fortnight. He had one and it has ended and there is no column anywhere in four hundred miles that would have held it.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is relieved and nobody is going to be told this morning was a good one.*”

Six or seven of them on that bank had a Thursday of this week that had not existed when they came up it. All of them had a fortnight that had ended without anything having been done about it.

Then they went up the water from that landing in the wind, and the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it, and nobody carried it anywhere.

---

The fever was sixty-two weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-seven days.

A man of forty-three gave the reading and the day and a figure, and then said out loud that a bank which is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth, and that he has been saying four days for a fortnight without saying which four, and that he gave the same figure wrong on the Thursday of last week and will not say by how much.

A man of forty-one said the last morning of a fortnight out loud before anybody asked him, and said nothing was going to happen. He is not going to name the day on that bank and not on a Wednesday and not on a Thursday, and he gave the reason in a man of fifty-four’s words: a thing about nine people have is a thing about nine people can be sent for about.

And he said he is going to say it in the passage at the fifth hour before the light on one of two mornings in the next fortnight, and would not say which.

And a woman of twenty-eight said that is a rule and that a rule is a thing that works without a person. A man of thirty-one said the morning out loud in the open to take it off him, and the man of forty-one said that made it about nine people who could be sent for about a morning he had not said, on a Wednesday, which is the thing he said he would not do.
