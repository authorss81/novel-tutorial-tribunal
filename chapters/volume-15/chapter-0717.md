# Chapter 717: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Fever Was Sixty-Two Weeks Old, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read Takes The Eighth Of Nine Boards Out Of His Own Coat And Puts It Down On The Counter Beside The Sheet And Does Not Ask Anybody To Read It To Him, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Puts One Down Beside It And Does Not Read That One Either, And Says Out Loud That A Board And A Sheet Are Two Different Instruments

Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-seventh morning. Three hundred and seventeen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks old. Two hundred and seventy-one days since the division. Two hundred and seventeen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. It was three days since it came off all ninety, and about nine people went down that water and off the low side to the gravel at the head of that drain.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gave two figures for a night in a fortnight and has not given one since. He says neither of them is his.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Three days, and it was about eight at this hour yesterday, and that is the working.*”

“*And tomorrow is four days, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*I am not going to say what that is yet, because I have not been there at the seventh hour on a Sunday in a fortnight, and I am not going to work it out in front of about nine people.*”

Twelve inches on that landing. About nine of them were at the head of a drain at about the ninth hour.

Then a man of thirty-eight read twelve names out loud on a bank in the open air for the seventh morning reading. It took about nine minutes, and three of the twelve have no day against them.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and put the sheet back on the counter afterwards where anybody could put a hand on it. He has been carrying a board in his own coat that nobody put there.

Then a man of fifty-four came up that bank and took something out of the inside of his coat. He put it down on the counter about a foot from the paper. Nobody sent for him and nobody asked him anything, and he said what he was doing before he did it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain, cannot read a word of the paper or of the board, and has had the eighth of nine boards inside his coat for about four weeks. He has not asked anybody to read it to him.

“*That is the eighth of nine, and it has been inside my coat for about four weeks. I have not asked anybody to read it to me, and I am not going to this morning.*”

“*And I want that said on this bank by a man of fifty-four who cannot read, before anybody asks me.*”

“*So that about four of you can tell in four days that I said it first and not a man with a hand and a book.*”

“*Nine went out of this county on the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week. Four of them have a job cut on them with two hands over the job.*”

“*And there is a space about as wide as a thumb between the two hands, where a name goes if anybody ever puts one there.*”

“*And five of the nine have not got a job on them at all, because five of the nine jobs were never put back together.*”

“*I was there and I watched about four of them being put back together. I have got no figure for the five and nobody has one.*”

About four people on that bank had the eighth, and about nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had not asked.

Then a man of thirty-eight took something else out of his own coat and put it down about a foot the other side of the paper. He did not read it, and he said out loud that he was not going to read it and why.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and was given that board in the open on a bank by a man of thirty-one at the end of the hundred and twenty-first week. He put it inside his coat and has carried it every day since, and has never read it out.

“*That one was given to me. It is not mine, it is not a copy of anything, and I have never read it out. I am not going to this morning either.*”

“*And there are two boards on that counter and a piece of printed paper, and about nine people are standing on a bank looking at all three of them.*”

“*I have not read either board and I have read the paper out loud seven times.*”

“*And I am asking the nine of you standing on this bank to notice something in the next four seconds.*”

“*I am going to say two things that are the same size, and I would like you to notice if you are about to make them into one thing before you do it and not after.*”

Two boards and a piece of printed paper were on that counter. About nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had asked them to notice something in the next four seconds.

Then a woman of forty said what the difference is between the two instruments, and it took her about four sentences, and she said it was the first time she had said it out loud and she was not going to say it again.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name, and has never once asked for a reason instead, and worked out on the Monday of this week that five of the twelve are in no book in this county.

“*A board is a job with a space in it where a name goes. The space is empty and it is waiting for somebody. That is the whole of what a board is.*”

“*And a sheet is a question with a name already in it. Twelve names and no spaces, and nothing on that thing is waiting for anybody.*”

“*So a board with an empty space in it is asking for a person, and a sheet with a name in it has got one.*”

“*And those are two different instruments, and about nine of us have been treating them as one thing for about four weeks because they are both nine lines.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying that, and I am not going to be told I am right.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to notice that I have said it, and that I have not put a fifth sentence on the end of it.*”

About four people on that bank had the two instruments, and about nine of them had a woman of forty who had not put a fifth sentence on the end of it.

Then a man of thirty-eight said out loud that they were the same size and the same shape, and that he was not going to say they were the same thing. He gave the working for both, and asked about nine of them to watch themselves.

“*Both of those boards are about as long as my own forearm and about as wide as two hands, and that paper is about as long as my own forearm and about as wide as both my hands.*”

“*Both of them have nine lines. On both of them there is a space about as wide as a thumb, and on the boards the space is between two hands and it is empty, and on the paper the space is a mark cut into it about the size of a thumb and square and there is a name beside it.*”

“*That is a mark cut into a sheet the size of a thumb and square. There is a space cut between two hands over the ninth opening in that wall about as wide as a thumb and square, and I have had both of them in my own hands.*”

“*And I am not going to say they are the same thing today.*”

“*And I am not going to say they are not the same thing either, because that would be the other way round and it would be just as much of a shape.*”

“*I am going to leave them where they are, and I would like all of you to do the same with me and not wait for me.*”

Two boards and a sheet on one counter, and about nine people on a bank who had been asked to notice whether they were about to join them up.

Then a man of thirty-one said that they were the same nine, and he said it before anybody had finished looking at them. A man of forty-three took it off him in about seven sentences and gave his own true of it at the end.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-three days. A hundred and sixty-three is seven hundred and seventeen less five hundred and fifty-four, and he has said one shape after another out loud for about six weeks.

“*They are the same nine. Nine lines on the boards and nine lines on that paper and nine openings in that wall. That is not three things. That is one thing written down three times in three—*”

“*No. Seven sentences, and the seventh one is mine, and it is about me and not about you.*”

“*One: nine openings in that wall and nine lines on a piece of paper is a shape. I have watched you make about six shapes this fortnight, and every one of them held for about four days.*”

“*Two: a term is a job with a name on it and not one of the nine has a name on it and about nine of us have said that out loud and you were there when we said it.*”

“*Three: nine of them went out of this county about four weeks ago, and there is no original and no keeper.*”

“*Nobody in this county knows where eight of the nine are. I do not know where eight of the nine are.*”

“*Four: a board is a job with an empty space in it and a sheet is a question with twelve names in it, and a woman of forty has just said that out loud. She is better at this than either of us.*”

“*Five: you have been in this county a hundred and sixty-three days and I do not know where eight of those boards are and neither do you and neither does he.*”

“*Six: I have been wrong about about four things in about nine days and I have said so out loud on a Monday of last week and I am not going to count this as a fifth and put a number on it.*”

“*Seven: that is the same nine. I said it in front of about nine people on the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week, when about nine of them were standing on that bank, and I have been carrying it for about four weeks.*”

“*And it is not wrong. It is the only shape I ever got right, and it is about nine lines and nothing else. Four of you have known that since the Sunday and the rest of you have not.*”

Seven sentences, and the seventh one turned out to be true, and about four people on that bank had known it for three days.

Then a boy of thirteen gave two figures nobody had asked him for. He gave the working for both and said he was keeping them.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. His mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about, and he gave a man of thirty-eight a figure of nine days at the end of last week, and was told to keep it.

“*Sixteen days. Sixteen is seven hundred and seventeen less seven hundred and one, and I counted it myself on the road coming down and nobody asked me, and I am keeping it.*”

“*And there have been seven readings. Seven is seven hundred and seventeen less seven hundred and ten, and that is the seventh including this morning. There was not one on the Wednesday.*”

“*And I am not going to hand either of them to a man of thirty-eight like a present and I am not going to be thanked for counting them.*”

The seven readings went into about nine heads on that bank, and four of them had a boy of thirteen with two numbers of his own that nobody had asked for.

Then the two boards went back inside two coats and the paper went back on the counter. About nine people stood at the head of that drain in the wind, and nothing on that counter was read.

---

The fever was sixty-two weeks old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-three days.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read took the eighth of nine boards out of his coat and put it on a counter beside the sheet, and did not ask anybody to read it to him. A man of thirty-eight put one down the other side of the paper and did not read that one either.

And four of the nine boards carry a job with a space about as wide as a thumb in it where a name goes, and five of the nine jobs were never put back together. Nobody in this county knows where eight of the nine are.

And a man of thirty-eight said the two marks are the same size and the same shape and refused to say they are the same thing, and asked about nine of them to notice if they were about to join them up before they did it. A man of thirty-one said they were the same nine, and was told that it is a shape and it is the only shape he ever got right and it is about nine lines and nothing else.

And a boy of thirteen gave two figures nobody had asked him for, gave the working for both, and said he was keeping them. A man of thirty-eight put the two boards back inside two coats and asked the nine of them not to wait for him, and about nine people went up that track in the wind.
