# Chapter 719: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Man Of Forty-One Gives Three Ways He Has Tried To Get The Day He Stops On Written Down And None Of Them Is A Form In This County, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Name Out Loud To The Ground In About Nine People’s Hearing For The First Time In This County By A Person Who Was Asked Something By It, And It Costs A Woman Of Sixty-Four The One Thing She Said On Thursday She Would Keep

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-ninth morning. Three hundred and nineteen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and two days. Two hundred and seventy-three days since the division. Two hundred and nineteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour for the fifth morning running and the man of forty-three gave the reading and gave the day. He said nothing else, because there was nothing else in it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has kept that landing nineteen years, and has said he has nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it. He has given two figures for a night instead, and he says neither of them is his.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Five days, and a full bank holds, and the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning.*”

“*And that is four days after this bank is full, and this bank was full yesterday.*”

“*There is no more working in it than that, and about four of you have had it three times this week.*”

About four people on that bank had the five days, and about nine of them had three days and a Thursday of next week.

Then a man of thirty-eight read twelve names out loud in the open for the ninth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes. Three of the twelve have no day against them, and nothing was carried anywhere afterwards.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet nine times in this county and has been refused a figure for how long three times. There is a name on the ninth line that was not in the print on Friday morning.

Then a man of forty-one gave three ways he has tried to get one thing written down, and he gave all three. None of them was a form in this county, and he said the last of it before anybody asked him anything.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine for nineteen years, and has a fortnight and a Wednesday and no day, and told nine people about it in the dark a week ago.

“*One: I asked whether my day could go on that piece of printed paper. A man of thirty-eight says a column is a question that gets asked the same way every time so that the answer can be found again.*”

“*And there is no column on that thing that gets asked that. He worked that out in about four days and said it out loud on a Sunday morning.*”

“*Two: I asked whether it could go in a book. A man of sixty-one says a man who writes it is a man who knows it, and a man who knows a thing alone is a man who will say it in about four years when somebody asks him whether it was true.*”

“*And he has refused nine spaces and two names and a first of the nine in about a fortnight.*”

“*Three: I asked whether it could go on one of the nine boards. And about four of us worked out on Saturday morning that there are eight of those nine in four counties, and none of them is going to be found in about four days.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one offered to go and find them, and there is no form that says he may and there is no form that says he may not.*”

“*And so there is nowhere in four hundred miles to put it. That paper was printed four hundred miles off by somebody who has never been to this county and is not going to.*”

“*And I am not going to put my day on it, and the reason is a man of thirty-eight’s. I am going to give it in his words, because he has got it and I have not.*”

“*I would be asking it once. And a column that gets asked once is not a column. It is a favour, and I have been the ninth of nine for nineteen years, and I have had enough of favours.*”

Three ways went out of a man of forty-one on that bank. About nine of them had heard him say he was not going to move his day to a Thursday, and four of them had been waiting six weeks for him to move it.

Then a woman of forty-four who keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road said the third thing she has worked out about two men in two coats, and she said it on a bank instead of on her own step, and nobody asked her for the other two.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has asked three times and answered twice, and she gave the count out loud on the Saturday of a fortnight and has not added anything to it since.

“*The third thing is that I do not want to know. I have had four days and I have turned it over every one of them and what I have come to is that I would rather not be told.*”

“*And that is not a thing anybody has said on this bank in about six weeks and I am saying it about two men in two coats and a door four miles on and a stone in a room nine miles under a river, and I am not going to work out why those are the same.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be told it is honest, and I would like somebody to say in about four days that a woman of forty-four said it first and not a man with a hand and a book.*”

About four people on that bank had the third thing, and about nine of them had a woman of forty-four who had come down the road to say a thing nobody had asked her for.

Then a man of thirty-one said a name out loud. He said it to the ground, once, in plain words, and nobody shouted anything back at it. Nothing whatever answered him, and about nine people were on that bank.

It was not a shout, it did not come from anywhere in particular, and it was the first time in this county that a person who had been asked something by it had said it out loud at all.

“The First Witness.”

Four of them on that bank had heard it before. Nobody said a word and nothing whatever happened, and about nine of them had not heard it before that morning.

Then a woman of sixty-four gave what it cost, and it was hers. She said it in about nine sentences and she did not hand it to anybody.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and got up out of that book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and there has been no line for her in it since, and she said that name herself on the Thursday of last week.

“*I said it first. I said it on the Thursday of last week in about nine people’s hearing because I was not going to let the first person to say that name be a man who was not there.*”

“*And he was there. And that is the whole of what I said out loud on that bank on the Thursday of last week.*”

“*It has just stopped being true in about nine seconds, and nobody on this bank is going to be able to put it back.*”

“*And here is what it cost, and I am going to say it in my own mouth. I am not going to hand it to him and I am not going to hand it to about nine of you.*”

“*About nine people on this bank have got that name in their mouths this morning, and they did not have it on Monday.*”

“*And one of the nine is a man of thirty-eight who has to stand on this bank before anybody goes anywhere and decide, every morning, whether he says it out loud.*”

“*That is a figure of mornings and I have not got one and I am not going to make one up.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the nineteen years, and I am not going to be told it was worth it, and I am not relieved and nobody here is relieved.*”

About four people on that bank had the cost, and about nine of them had a woman of sixty-four who had lost the one thing she had said she would keep.

Then a man of thirty-one said why he had said it, in his own mouth, before anybody asked him. He said what it had cost about nine people, and a woman of twenty-eight refused him for the third time this fortnight.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-five days. A hundred and sixty-five is seven hundred and nineteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and he has had four people take a thing off him in four days.

“*I said it to find out whether it would answer. That is the whole of it and I am not going to put it in a better shape than that.*”

“*And I used about nine people on a bank to find out. That is the thing I have done, and it is the second time this week I have used about nine people as the instrument of a question that was mine.*”

“*And it did not answer. And about four of you heard it not answer, and that is a figure of about nine people and not a figure of the thing itself.*”

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She checked a thing ten times on the Monday of a fortnight and has not begun it again.

“*That is three times this fortnight and every one of them handed about nine people something to hold. I stopped two of them and I am not going to stop this one.*”

“*And I have not got a fourth thing to say, and I am not going to make one up at about the ninth hour on the last morning of a week. I am not going to be the person who holds it.*”

“*I said that on the Saturday of last week and I am saying it again because it is still true, and not because I have not got anything else.*”

“*And about four of you are going to say in about four days that she said the same thing twice.*”

“*I would like them to say also that the second one was about a different thing, and that I have never said otherwise.*”

About four people on that bank had the three times, and about nine of them had a woman of twenty-eight who was not going to stop it a third time.

Then nothing whatever happened for the rest of that morning. About nine people stood on that bank in the wind, the sheet was on the counter, the two blanks and the third one were on it, and nobody had said another word about the name.

---

The fever was sixty-two weeks and two days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-five days.

A man of forty-one gave three ways he has tried to get the day he stops on written down, and none of them is a form in this county. He said he is not going to put his day on a sheet of printed paper because he would be asking it once, and a column that gets asked once is a favour.

And a man of thirty-one said a name out loud to the ground in about nine people’s hearing, for the first time in this county by a person who had been asked something by it. Nothing whatever answered him.

And a woman of sixty-four who said that name herself on the Thursday of last week said what it cost, and it is hers. About nine people on that bank have got that name in their mouths who did not have it on Monday, and one of the nine has to stand on that bank every morning before anybody goes anywhere. She is not relieved, and nobody there is relieved.
