# Chapter 711: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Reads Nine Lines Out Loud For The Second Morning Running And Says It Took About Nine Minutes And Was The Same As Yesterday, And A Woman Of Forty Says Out Loud That It Is A Practice And Not A Settlement And Gives The Reason In Four Sentences, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Would Answer If He Were Asked And Is Taken Off In Six Sentences By A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Says He Is The Only Kind Of Man In This County That Is Worth Anything To Anybody

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and sixty-first morning. Three hundred and eleven days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks and one day. Two hundred and sixty-five days since the division. Two hundred and eleven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the day. Then he gave a figure nobody had asked him for, before he had finished saying the working.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and there is a piece of printed paper lying in the open on the counter of it four feet below that bank. He has been on that landing at the seventh hour every morning this week and the bank came off in the night on the Tuesday and this is the fifth morning it has not.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Five days, and it was about sixteen at this hour yesterday, and that is a full bank and a full bank holds.*”

“*The next time about ninety steps can be walked is on the Wednesday of this week. That is three days from where I am standing this morning.*”

“*And the working is four days after a bank is full. This one was full on the Saturday of last week.*”

“*That is the second figure I have given you for how long. Both of them are four days after a full bank and not four days from the morning I am standing in.*”

“*And I would like four of you to notice the difference.*”

Four of them had the five days. About nine of them had a figure they had not asked for.

Then a man of thirty-eight went down four feet and picked the sheet up off that counter. He came up with it and stood on that bank in the wind and read it out loud for the second morning running.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and read it once on a Sunday in the open and once at the fifth hour under that bank. He put the sheet back on the counter afterwards both times. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has not put a term on it.

“*Nine lines. Eleven names. Two of the eleven the same name twice. Two of the eleven with no day against them, and they are the eighth and the ninth.*”

“*And the fifth line is not one of the two blanks. The fifth line is a woman of twenty-nine in a reed and she has got a day against her.*”

“*I am going to read it the same as the other eight. I am not going to leave it out.*”

“*It took about nine minutes and it took about nine minutes yesterday, and I am giving you both so that about four of you can tell that I have not been learning it.*”

“*Nobody asked me to do this and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*I have been told by two people on this bank that it is not a good idea, and I have done it anyway. I am not going to improve on what either of them said.*”

Four of them counted the nine minutes on their own two hands. The rest of them watched a man of thirty-eight stand in the wind with a piece of printed paper in his two hands.

Then a woman of forty said what it was, and she opened with the three words she opens every room with, and she said it before anybody had asked her what it was.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She is on two sheets of deal in this county and she knows what a day on a line does.

“*Say what you are standing in front of.*”

“*A practice is not a settlement. That is the whole of it and I have not got a second half.*”

“*A settlement is a thing that holds when the person who was doing it stops. That is what makes it one. And nobody on this bank has stopped anything, so you have not got a settlement, you have got a thing about nine of you are doing.*”

“*And I am not going to say what would make it one.*”

“*About nine of you have been putting a finger at that since the Thursday. Not one of you has put it on the same one, and I would rather say that than have about four of you find out about it from a man with a hand and a book.*”

Nobody on that bank worked out the second half of what a woman of forty had said, and the woman of forty had only got one of the two.

Then a woman of forty-four asked the question nobody had asked. It was what happened on a morning when the man of thirty-eight was not on that bank, and she asked it in about four sentences and did not raise her voice.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and nineteen years of it have been a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*What happens on a morning when the man of thirty-eight is not standing here with that thing in his hands.*”

“*And I am asking it as a woman of forty-four who keeps chairs and not as a person who thinks something is wrong.*”

“*About four of you have been thinking something is wrong since the Thursday and none of you have said it.*”

“*There is no column on that sheet for a morning when the person who does it does not come. A woman of twenty-eight read that out loud on the Saturday morning in this county in the open air and it was the second of seven.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight gave the answer himself, and gave it in the plainest words he had, and nobody improved on it and nobody said he was right.

“*About four of you can read it. I read it on the Friday before I said I was going to do it every morning.*”

“*That was at the fifth hour, in the dark, a hundred and thirty feet under that bank. Nobody sent me and nobody asked me, and about nine people were not in there.*”

“*And if I am on a bed on a morning then it is a piece of paper with about nine words on it lying on that counter with nobody keeping it.*”

“*That is not a catastrophe and I am not going to stand on this bank and make it one.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for that either, and I am not going to be told I was right.*”

“*If about nine of you are going to be pleased about it in about four days, I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

Four of the people standing on that bank could read it. All the rest of them had a man of thirty-eight who had read it in the dark.

Then a man of thirty-one said something he had been carrying since the Thursday. He said it out loud and it was the wrong thing, and a woman of forty-four took it off him in six sentences before he got to the end of it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-seven days. A hundred and fifty-seven is seven hundred and eleven less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*There ought to be one person on this bank who can be stopped. That is what I have been carrying since Thursday. If there is one person who can be stopped then there is one thing here with a person in it and not—*”

“*You have just asked a bank of about nine people to make you somebody’s, in about as many words, on a Sunday morning, in the open air.*” She did not raise her voice. “*That is six sentences and I am going to use all six of them.*”

“*One: nobody on this bank asked you a question, and I have been counting what gets asked on this bank for four days and you were not on the list.*”

“*Two: you are not the thing that can be stopped. A thing that can be stopped is a thing somebody is doing, and you are not doing this, and you said so yourself on the Friday.*”

“*Three: what you actually said is that there ought to be somebody in it. That is a want and not a finding.*”

“*I have listened to about four men on this bank say a want out loud this fortnight, and every one of them wanted to be asked.*”

“*Four: you have been in this county a hundred and fifty-seven days and nobody has asked you for anything in one of them.*”

“*About nine of us have known that since the second morning you were here.*”

“*Five: and you have said the true of it about four seconds too late, and I would have said it anyway, and nobody else on this bank was going to.*”

“*Six: nobody on this bank is going to be your somebody, and I am not going to let about nine people do it out loud this morning.*”

“*That is a refusal and not a kindness. It is the last one I am going to give you on this bank.*”

Six sentences and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting three weeks for somebody to say that out loud.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it, and he gave the working first, and nobody improved on it and nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong.

“*She is right and I said it wrong and I am not going to improve on it, because improving on it would be the seventh sentence and she did not give me a seventh.*”

“*Here is the working. A hundred and fifty-seven is seven hundred and eleven less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and fifty-seven days and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them. And I have just asked a bank of about nine people to ask me, in the open air, on a Sunday.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying the true of it, and I am not going to be forgiven for the six sentences.*”

“*I am not going to stand on this bank on Wednesday as a man who was right about it on Sunday.*”

Then the sheet went back on the counter where anybody could put a hand on it, and nobody carried it anywhere. About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind, and about four of them had the second morning of a thing with no term on it.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-seven days.

A piece of printed paper with nine lines and eleven names on it was read out loud in the open on that bank for the second morning running. It took about nine minutes, and the same as the morning before, and it went back on the counter afterwards and nobody carried it anywhere.

And a woman of forty said that is a practice and not a settlement, and gave the reason. A settlement is a thing that holds when the person who was doing it stops, and nobody on that bank has stopped anything.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted there to be one person on it who could be stopped. A woman of forty-four took it off him in six sentences and said he was the only kind of man in this county that is worth anything to anybody, and nobody made him somebody’s.
