# Chapter 741: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Week, And A Tuesday, And A Day Has Appeared Against The Ninth Line And Nobody In Four Counties Can Be Sent For About Who Wrote It, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Stops In The Middle Of Reading It Out Loud

First day of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. His four hundred and ninety-first morning. Three hundred and forty-one days after the settlement. The fever sixty-five weeks and three days. Two hundred and ninety-five days since the division. Two hundred and forty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour and it was the third morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and then the working, and then the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for the third of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has stood on that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the third morning of it.*”

“*Three days. It was about eight at this hour yesterday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And a full bank is about sixteen, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning, and that is the Sunday of this week.*”

“*I have been giving you all three of those for about a fortnight and I am not going to be thanked for them.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you why you can check me against yesterday, because I said on Monday that I was not going to.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twentieth morning, and he said it flat.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied the asking by one word. The fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty mornings.*”

“*And I am not going to say anything about what I have been listening to since the second hour, because it is a thing I have no way of checking and I am not going to stand on a bank and be believed about it.*”

“*The fifth thing is still not answered, and not one of the nine of you has asked me, and I am still waiting.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty and nobody is going to be told I was brave about any part of it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read that line out loud in the open, and he got about nine words into it and stopped, and he stood there with the paper in his hand for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-one times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding every morning at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and has not started again.

“*Thirty-one is seven hundred and forty-one less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-one in the dark.*”

“*And there is a day against that line.*”

“*And there was not one against it yesterday morning and I read it yesterday morning and I read it every morning since the first day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week.*”

“*The ninth line has never had a day. I have said that out loud to these people twenty mornings running and I have been right twenty mornings running.*”

Then he turned the sheet over, because he could not go on with the side he was on, and there was print on the other side.

Nobody on that bank had ever seen the back of it. The sheet had lain on that counter face up since a man of thirty-six carried it down a road in a coat in a town two days off, and it had been face down on that counter this morning when they came up, and a man of thirty-four said he did not turn it, and a woman of twenty-nine said nothing, and nobody turned it.

“*There is a second thing printed on the back of it and it is in the same small type as the line at the top of the other side.*”

“*And it is nine words and I am going to count them in front of you, because you cannot check anything else about it.*”

“*Not. There. On. A. Day. Is. Agreeing. To. It.*”

“*That is nine and I have counted it out loud and you all heard it.*”

“*And that is a rule about days and it is about the nine lines on the other side and not about anything else.*”

Then about four of the people on that bank said the count at once and about nine of them said nothing at all, and the count was right.

A woman of forty said what a count of words is and what it is not, and she gave the working first, and nobody picked one of the things she listed.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. On the last day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week she gave nine sentences and refused the tenth about nine things, and she has still not said which three of five things have a day against them.

“*Nine words and a rule, and I am going to say what I am standing in front of first, because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*A count of words is a count of words. It is not a figure of a rule and it is not a figure of anybody.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight can read and I cannot, and he read it out and I counted it, and if either of us has it wrong then about nine people on this bank have it wrong the same way.*”

“*That is four things and I am going to say four.*”

“*One: a day has appeared against a line that has never had a day against it, and the ninth line has got two names on it and not one of them.*”

“*Two: and a woman of sixty-four’s is one of the two and she is standing on this bank, and a woman of twenty-nine’s is the other and she is not, and she is about a hundred yards along that drain and about twenty feet off the edge of it, and I am not going to say any more about where she is than that.*”

“*Three: and nobody in this county can be sent for about who wrote that day, and I have asked for a day against a name for about nineteen years and I have never once been able to find out who put one there.*”

“*Four: and I am not going to say what a day is worth when nobody sat in the chair, because a man of about fifty-two asked that in the open on this bank ten days ago and nobody answered him and I am not going to improve on his asking.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said what the day had done to her own name, and she said it before anybody had asked her, and she said nobody was going to be thanked for stopping her.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years. She got up out of the book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and there has been no line for her in it since. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper, and that line has never had a day against it, and this morning it has.

“*Do not ask me about that day.*”

“*That is the whole of what I am going to say about it and I have said it before anybody opened his mouth.*”

“*And I am saying it because if I agree to that day then it goes against her name as well, and she is in a reed about a hundred yards along that drain and nobody has asked her anything in a hundred and twelve days.*”

“*I am not going to be the way that day gets to be true.*”

A man of forty-four said the other half of it in four sentences, and she did not take one of them back, and nobody said she was right.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*A woman of sixty-four has just refused to be asked about a day, in the open, in about nine people’s hearing, and the day is going to be there in about four minutes either way.*”

“*That is not a refusing. A shut door is not the right of refusing, a sealed door is not that, a man who cannot read is not it, and a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything.*”

“*And what a woman of sixty-four has just done is a thing nobody can be sent for about, which is say out loud in the open that you are not going to be used, and there is no form in this county that says a person may do that.*”

“*And the day is going to be there anyway, and I am not going to be thanked for the four sentences and I am not going to be told I am right about them.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said the half of it nobody had asked him for, and it was about the sheet and not about the day, and it is what he has been carrying since he stopped deciding.

“*I read it out loud yesterday and the day was not on it, and I read it out loud this morning and the day is on it, and I am the only person on this bank who can tell you those are two different sheets of the same thing.*”

“*And I have not turned it over and I am not going to say that I would not have, because I did not think about it and I have not been thinking about it for about a fortnight.*”

“*There is a second side to that thing and about nine people have had the first side in their hands thirty-one mornings running and read it out loud in the open.*”

“*That is not a mistake anybody made and it is not a trick, and I am not going to stand on a bank at the seventh hour and give the nine of you a word for it, because there is no word in this county for a thing that is the same paper twice.*”

“*And I have a child of nine and I am not going to say that I read a rule out loud this morning without knowing there was a rule on the back of it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the thirty-one and nobody is going to be told I was brave about stopping in the middle of it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave three counts back and one of them was new, and he said it on the road coming down and nobody asked him.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping three counts since the Saturday of a fortnight and none of them has been asked for.

“*Forty days. Forty is seven hundred and forty-one less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And twenty mornings of a man of fifty-four asking, and I have been counting those since the Wednesday of last week, and the two agree and I am not going to make a total of the two.*”

“*And a hundred and eleven days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and twelve for a woman in a reed, and a man of sixty-one gave those two on the Friday of a fortnight and has not given them since and I am not going to add them either.*”

“*And I have got a fourth one this morning and I am not going to say what it is, because I do not know what it is yet and a boy of thirteen should not say what a day is.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for carrying four and I am keeping them on Tuesday.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud what he wanted, in the open, on that bank, before anybody had asked him anything, and he gave the working for his own figure before he gave the want.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-seven days. A hundred and eighty-seven is seven hundred and forty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. He offered seven times in about three weeks to be the one who asks and was refused seven times out loud, and he said on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that he is not going to offer again, and nobody has ever told him to stop.

“*A hundred and eighty-seven is seven hundred and forty-one less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty-seven days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*And I am not going to offer to be the one who asks, and I said on Friday that I was not, and I am not going to say it a second time this fortnight because a woman of twenty-eight told me in nine sentences that a second one is a rule.*”

“*Here is the want and I am going to say it before anything else because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*And here is the mistake and I have not said it out loud in about a fortnight.*”

“*I have been saying *before anybody had asked him anything* in front of about nine people for nine days, and a woman of twenty-eight told me on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a thing said before anybody has asked is the shape of a rule.*”

“*I said it again to you on Friday of last week and again to a man of sixty-one on the Saturday and again in a passage last Sunday, and that is three times this week, and I am not going to stop saying the sentence because a woman of twenty-eight said a thing about it once.*”

“*I want the ninth line to have no day on it again by tomorrow morning.*”

“*That is all of it. I am not asking anybody to agree with me and I am not going to call it a rule and I am not going to be thanked for wanting it.*”

“*And I have not got a way of doing it and I have not said that I have and I am not going to pretend to have one in about four days.*”

“*About four of you worked out on Monday that nobody can be sent for about me, and a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well told me on Tuesday of last week what that is worth, and he said both halves of it and he was right about both.*”

“*And here is what it costs, and it is mine and it is not going to be paid back.*”

“*I have been nobody’s for a hundred and ninety-one days and I have said in about nine people’s hearing that I want a day taken off a line. A man of thirty-four in that ash has a day against his name and has never been asked about it and he is about a hundred yards along this drain and he can hear about four of the nine of you breathing.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about me for wanting it and nobody can be sent for about him for having it, and I have made a want out loud on a bank in front of two people who cannot be sent for about anything.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for that and nobody is going to tell me I was right to say it, and I am not going to be relieved of having said it.*”

“*So this morning I am a man of thirty-one standing on a bank wanting a thing done that cannot be done because there is no column in four hundred miles that a man of thirty-one can be sent for about, and I have said that out loud in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*And I am not relieved of it and nobody is going to be told I was right to want it, and I would rather it was nothing at all in four days.*”

“*A man of thirty-four in that ash has a day against his name on that thing and he has never been asked anything in a hundred and eleven days, and I have not said that out loud yet this morning and I am not going to.*”

“*Not yet. That is not a rule and I have said so, and it is a thing I am going to do at about the ninth hour if about nine of you are still standing here.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with twelve inches of water under the top step and a day against a line that had never had one. Four of them had a woman of sixty-four who had refused out loud to be used, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had read a rule out loud for about nine words this morning without knowing it was a rule.

---

The fever was sixty-five weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-seven days.

A day appeared against the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper between the seventh hour yesterday and the seventh hour this morning, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about who wrote it, and the ninth line has two names on it and one of the two is about a hundred yards along the edge of that drain and has not been asked anything in a hundred and twelve days.

And a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open and stopped in the middle of it, and turned the sheet over, and found nine words printed on the back of it in the same small type: not there on a day is agreeing to it. He counted them out loud and the count was nine.

And a woman of sixty-four said, before anybody asked her, that she was not going to be asked about that day, and that if she agreed to it then it would go against the other name on the same line, and she is not going to be the way that day gets to be true.
