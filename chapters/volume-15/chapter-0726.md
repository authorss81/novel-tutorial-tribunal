# Chapter 726: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Monday, And A Bank Is Full Again At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step And The Next Time About Ninety Steps Can Be Walked Is In Four Days From That Morning And Is A Friday Of Next Week, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Says He Has Been The Fifth Line Of Two Sheets Of Deal For Eleven Years And Cannot Find Himself On Either

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventy-sixth morning. Three hundred and twenty-six days after the settlement. The fever sixty-three weeks and two days. Two hundred and eighty days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked before anybody asked him for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, and that landing is the only sentence anybody can say about him that has not had to be corrected. He gave two figures for a night a fortnight ago and has not given one since, and he says neither of them is his.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour.*”

“*Four days.*”

“*It was about twelve at this hour yesterday, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*And I have given you the two halves every morning this fortnight and I am going to give you the two halves tomorrow morning as well.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning.*”

“*That is four days after this bank is full.*”

“*And this bank is full now, and that is the first time I have said a Monday out loud since the first day of the hundred and twenty-second week.*”

“*And that is a Friday of next week, and I am not going to say it twice and I am not going to be asked what I think of it.*”

About four people on that bank had the Friday and the rest of them had a full bank and a Monday that had never been said out loud before.

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and a man of thirty-eight read it, and did not read anything else that morning without saying why.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter, and has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of this week. He has also had a fifth thing asked of him on the Sunday of a fortnight and has still not answered it.

“*Five mornings.*”

“*And I am going to keep asking and I am not going to say what I do with it.*”

“*And the bank is full again and that is four days and it has not changed me by one half of a nail.*”

“*And I have heard about the Friday of next week and I am not going to say a word about it.*”

“*Nobody asked me.*”

“*And one of the people who has to make a decision about it has told me he is not going to be asked.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two came up that bank on the last morning of a week and said something he had been carrying for eleven years, and nobody had asked him for it, and he gave the working before he gave the thing.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county. He has been that person eleven years without knowing it was a thing to be, and he refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences a fortnight ago and gave the reason in the ninth.

“*Eleven years.*”

“*Eleven is seven hundred and twenty-six less seven hundred and fifteen.*”

“*I have been the fifth line of one of those two sheets since the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-first week.*”

“*And I have never once read either of them, and I cannot read them now.*”

“*I have said out loud on that bank about nine times that I cannot see well and not one of you has written it down.*”

“*And here is the thing and I am giving the working first because that is how it is done on that bank now.*”

“*I have got eleven years on two sheets and I cannot find myself on either one.*”

“*I have looked, and looking took about nine seconds because there are not many lines.*”

“*And I would like somebody to say out loud in front of these nine people whether a line on a sheet is a person or not.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for asking, and I am not going to be told I am right either way.*”

“*If nobody answers me that is an answer and I have had eleven years of those.*”

Then a woman of forty-four answered him in four sentences and she said the answer was not going to be free.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and keeps a book of twenty-nine chairs. She has kept it nineteen years and there are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*A line on a sheet is not a person.*”

“*I have kept twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years and a chair is not the person who sits in it.*”

“*And I know that better than anybody on this bank because I have been the one who counts them.*”

“*I have been wrong about it once and I have not been wrong about it since.*”

“*And here is the half of it that is going to cost you and not me.*”

“*I am not going to say what a line on a sheet is.*”

“*I am going to say what it is not, and it is not you.*”

“*You have asked me to say that out loud in front of nine people on a Monday morning.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is going to say that a man of about fifty-two has had eleven years of a good life because of it.*”

About nine of them on that bank had a man of about fifty-two who had been a line on two sheets for eleven years and had asked the only question anybody had asked him in a fortnight, and four of them had a woman of forty-four who had answered it and made it cost something.

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that a sheet of deal and a sheet of printed paper are the same instrument, and the man of about fifty-two took it off him in four sentences and gave his own true of it at the end.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-two days. A hundred and seventy-two is seven hundred and twenty-six less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*Two sheets of deal in this county and a sheet of printed paper four hundred miles off.*”

“*All three of them have lines in them, and a man is a line on two of them.*”

“*No.*”

“*Four sentences and the fourth one is mine and it is about me and not about you.*”

“*One: two of those three are in this county and one of them is printed four hundred miles off by somebody who has never been here.*”

“*Two: a sheet of deal is a thing you can put your thumb on.*”

“*A sheet of printed paper is a thing you cannot.*”

“*About nine of you have been treating them as one thing for a fortnight because they both have lines in them.*”

“*Three: and the third one is that you have said it out loud on the first morning a man asked a question out loud in eleven years.*”

“*You have used a man to get at a thing, and I have watched that four times.*”

“*Four: that is the same nine as last week and there is nothing else.*”

“*I am not going to improve on it, and nobody is going to thank me for saying it.*”

Then the man of about fifty-two said the true of the fourth sentence in his own mouth, and said he would not be told which part of it he was right about.

“*He is right about three of the four and I am not going to argue with the fourth either.*”

“*A man of thirty-one used me this morning to get at a thing.*”

“*I have been a line on two sheets for eleven years without a day in it.*”

“*Those are the same shape and I am not going to pretend one of them is bigger.*”

“*And the third one is the one I am not going to argue with, because he is right.*”

“*I am going to be a man who was used this morning and who said so himself.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for that and nobody is going to be told I was right about the two sheets.*”

“*I am not relieved and nobody on this bank is relieved.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read thirteen names out loud in the open for the sixteenth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes, and the sheet went back on that counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet sixteen times in this county. He asked a man of thirty-one for a figure for how long three times and has not been given one, and he decided again this morning and has not said a name out loud.

“*Thirteen names.*”

“*Sixteen is seven hundred and twenty-six less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And three of the thirteen have no day against them and there have been three for four mornings.*”

“*That is not a figure of anybody and I am not going to make it one.*”

“*And there is a man of thirty-four in an ash on the low side of that landing who has not been asked anything in ninety-four days, and he is one of the reasons I do not read that line out loud twice.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two has just told nine people that he cannot find himself on either of two sheets and I read this one out loud sixteen times and there is a name on it that nobody in four counties can be sent for about.*”

“*I am not going to say anything about that.*”

“*I am going to put it down on that counter and four of you can do what you like with it.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the last morning of a week, and about four of them had a Friday of next week, and nobody said anything at all about it.

The fever was sixty-three weeks and two days old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-two days.

A bank was full again at about sixteen inches over the top step, and the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from that morning, and that is a Friday of next week, and a man of forty-three said it out loud for the first time since the first day of the hundred and twenty-second week.

And a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said he has been the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county for eleven years and cannot find himself on either one, and asked whether a line on a sheet is a person.

And a woman of forty-four said it is not, and that it is not him, and that she was not going to say what it is.
