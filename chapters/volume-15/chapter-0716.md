# Chapter 716: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Goes Down Four Feet To A Counter And Picks Up A Sheet Of Printed Paper And Finds Twelve Names On Nine Lines And Not Eleven, And Gives The Working Out Loud Before He Reads One Of Them, And Says That He Did Not Read It On The Wednesday Because He Was Under That Bank With A Lamp, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Who Has Been Out Of Every Book In This County For Nineteen Years Has Got A Name On A Thing Four Hundred Miles Off That She Cannot Put A Hand On

Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-sixth morning. Three hundred and sixteen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks and six days. Two hundred and seventy days since the division. Two hundred and sixteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. It was two days since it came off all ninety, and about four of the nine people who went down that water were wet to the knee before the reading.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and there is a counter on it about four feet below that bank. He gave a figure for a night on the Saturday of last week and has not given one since, and he says neither of them is his.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Two days, and it was about four at this hour yesterday, and that is the working.*”

“*And a bank that is full is sixteen inches and it takes four days, and it holds until it comes off.*”

“*I have given you that about nine times this fortnight and I am not going to give it an eleventh time.*”

Eight inches on that landing, and about nine of them were up the bank looking at a piece of printed paper that had not been the same shape on it a day before.

Then a man of thirty-eight went down four feet, picked that sheet up off the counter, and stood on that bank with it in his two hands. He counted, and then he said a number out loud, and it was not the number he had said on five mornings running.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet five times in this county, and had not read it for six mornings, because on the sixth of them he was a hundred and thirty feet under that bank with his own lamp.

“*Nine lines. Twelve names. That is what is on it and I am going to say the working before I read one of them, and about four of you can check it.*”

“*On the Friday of last week it was eleven. On the Saturday it was eleven. On the Sunday it was eleven. On the Thursday of this week it was eleven, and I gave the count of eleven that morning.*”

“*I did not read it on the Wednesday. There are seven mornings from the Saturday of last week to this one and there is a reading on six of them. Seven is seven hundred and sixteen less seven hundred and nine.*”

“*And I am going to say which one of the seven has not got a reading on it, rather than let about four of you work it out.*”

“*I was under that bank with my own lamp at the fifth hour on the Wednesday with about nine people, and I did not come up it with a piece of printed paper in my hands.*”

“*I am not going to say that is the reason, and I am not going to say it was a coincidence either, and nothing is going to make me the man who says one of those two things in the open air.*”

A man of thirty-eight gave the working out loud on that bank. About nine of them had a man who had said out loud that he left a thing alone for one night and would not say what that meant.

Then he read it out loud in the open in the order it was written. The twelfth name was the last one on the ninth line, and it was not in the print.

“*And the twelfth name is on the ninth line, under the other name on that line. It is in ink and it is not the print, and the ninth line has no day against either of them and never had.*”

“*And I am going to read it out loud, because I read everything on that sheet out loud every morning and I am not going to be the man who decides which of them to leave off.*”

“*Ada Renk.*”

“*That is the twelfth of the twelve. Ada Renk, and she has got no day against her.*”

“*She got up out of the book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and there has been no line for her in it since.*”

About four people on that bank had the twelfth, and about nine of them had a woman of sixty-four hearing her own name read out loud in the open air for the second time in this county.

Then a woman of sixty-four answered for herself. She said four things in the order she said them, and she gave the working inside the third one without being asked.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and is out of every book in this county, and said yes at about the ninth hour of the Thursday of last week in about nine people’s hearing, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about either the asking or the answering.

“*One: I said yes on the Thursday, and I am not going to be asked about the asking. Not today, not in nine days, not in four years. I said that then and I am saying it now, and it is the same sentence.*”

“*Two: I have been a name nobody can be sent for about for nineteen years. I still am. There is no day against my name.*”

“*And a name with no day on it is a name nobody can be sent for about, because there is nothing to point at. A woman of forty said that on a bank on the Sunday of last week, and she was right.*”

“*Three: and I am still a name on a thing. Those are two different things, and about four of you want them to be the same one.*”

“*Being a name nobody can be sent for about took me nineteen years, and it cost me one thing a day for about nineteen years.*”

“*Being a name on a piece of printed paper has taken six days and it has cost nobody anything at all, and I am not going to stand on this bank and thank anybody for the difference.*”

“*Four: nobody in this county can be sent for about who wrote it. Not because anybody arranged it. Because there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a night.*”

About nine of them had a woman of sixty-four who was not going to thank anybody for the difference between two things, and four of them had wanted them to be one thing.

Then a woman of forty gave the whole of it in the order it went on that sheet. She gave the working, and gave the count before the count, and refused to give anybody a total.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name, and worked out on the Monday of last week that five of the eleven are in no book in this county. She refused to say which three of them have a day.

“*Nine lines. Twelve names. Two of the twelve the same name twice, which is the same two as before. It is not a third thing.*”

“*Three of the twelve have no day against them. That is three and not two, and three is two and one, and one is the twelfth of the twelve and it went on this week.*”

“*Six is seven hundred and sixteen less seven hundred and ten. That is six days, and it is a figure of a gap and not of a person.*”

“*And I am not going to add it to the five I worked out on Monday, because the five is about people and the six is about a sheet.*”

“*And I am not going to say which three of the five now have a day against them and I said on Monday that I would not, and I have not started.*”

About four people on that bank had the three, and about nine of them had a woman of forty who had not started.

Then a man of thirty-eight said what a person ought to do about a name that appeared on a sheet in one night. He gave four things in the order he had them, and did not say a fifth.

“*One: I left it on a counter in the open and I said out loud on the Saturday of last week that I was going to do it every night, and I did it every night.*”

“*Two: about three of us said it out loud and about nine of us heard it, and nobody improved on it.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four who cannot read said the same morning that nobody in this county is going to find out who wrote it. He was right about that, and he was not right about when.*”

“*Three: I am not going to carry it into a room, because the moment it is in a room somebody is keeping it, and I said that on the Saturday and I am not going to unsay it over a name.*”

“*Four: and I am not going to stop reading it either. I would like every one of you to notice that I have put those two things next to each other on purpose, and four of you are going to want me to have put them further apart.*”

A man of thirty-eight put two things next to each other on purpose, and about nine of them had him on that bank knowing he had done it.

Then a man of thirty-one said he would find out who wrote it, and he said it out loud. A woman of forty-four refused him in about nine sentences, and the ninth one was about finding and about being sent for.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-two days. A hundred and sixty-two is seven hundred and sixteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and he said four wrong words on the Thursday of this week without looking at a sheet of paper.

“*I will find out who wrote it. Give me a week. There are about nine people in four counties who cannot be sent for about and one of them is me and I am the only one who would—*”

“*No. Nine sentences and the ninth one is the only one I have never said.*”

“*You cannot find out who wrote it. That is not because anybody arranged it, and it is not because you are nobody’s.*”

“*And I would like all of you to hear the reason before four of you decide it is an insult.*”

“*A person who cannot be sent for about is not a person who gets found. That is the whole of it.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four told about nine people on the Thursday of last week that it took about nine weeks and about nine doors to come to her. Nobody found her.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may ask somebody anything, and there is no form that says a person may not, and both of those have been true since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*And the sixth sentence is that you said four wrong words on this bank yesterday morning and a woman of sixty-four paid for them. This morning you have offered to pay for hers with a week of your own time.*”

“*And that is the same move in the same week, and I have watched it twice.*”

“*And the seventh is that nobody is going to thank you for offering, and I am not going to be the one who tells you that you are the best instrument for it in four counties.*”

“*A man about fifty-two said that about a different thing last week, and it nearly broke him.*”

“*And the eighth is that you have been in this county a hundred and sixty-two days, there is no column with your name at the top of it, and there is no form that renews me.*”

“*I am not going to say the ninth one in a voice. I am going to say it flat: there is no figure of a man of thirty-one in this county, and there is not going to be one made out of this morning.*”

“*And the ninth is that a name on a piece of paper is not a wound and nobody on this bank is going to be told they have been done one by somebody else.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine sentences, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had offered a week of himself and been told there is no figure of him to give.

Then the sheet went back on that counter, and about nine people stood on that bank in the wind. About four of them had a count of twelve, a count of three, and a count of one night, and none of the three had been written down.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks and six days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-two days.

A man of thirty-eight went down four feet to a counter and found twelve names on nine lines and not eleven. He gave the working out loud before he read one of them, and said that he did not read the sheet on the Wednesday because he was under that bank with a lamp. He would not say that it was the reason, and would not pretend it was a coincidence.

And a woman of sixty-four who has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years heard her own name read out loud on the ninth line with no day against it. She said she was still a name nobody could be sent for about, and that being a name on a thing had cost nobody anything at all in six days, and she is not going to thank anybody for that difference.

And nobody in this county can be sent for about who wrote it, because there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a night. About nine people were standing on that bank, and none of those three counts was written down.
