# Chapter 707: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Man Of Forty-One Says At About The Fifth Hour That He Wants To Stop And Wants The Day Of It Written Down Where About Nine People Can Hear It, And A Woman Of Forty Says A Name With No Day Against It Is A Name Nobody Can Be Sent For About, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Can Get A Column For It And Is Wrong In Six Sentences

Second day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and fifty-seventh morning. Three hundred and seven days after the settlement. The fever sixty weeks and four days. Two hundred and sixty-one days since the division. Two hundred and seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and it was one day since it came off all ninety, and about four of the nine people who went down that morning were already wet to the knee before the reading.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. He counted out of the bed at the second hour this morning because a man of forty-one told him to at about the fourth hour on the Monday, and he did not say why and he was not asked.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. One day, and it was off all ninety yesterday at the sixth hour, and that is the working.*”

“*And I got up at the second hour this morning because a man of forty-one told me to and he did not say why and I did not ask him and he knows I did not ask him.*”

About four people on that landing had the four inches and about nine of them had been on the water before the reading.

They went down at about the fifth hour because a keeping is done before the light, and about nine people went down about ninety steps at about the fifth hour of a Wednesday, which is not a thing anybody in that county has done in about nineteen years.

At about a quarter of an hour before the light they were standing in the ninth room, and the lamp on the hook was burning and about a quarter full of oil, and the shelf cut in the wall about the height of a hand was empty, and the door-stone was shut.

Then a man of forty-one said the thing he has been not saying for about six weeks, and he said it flat, and he gave the whole of it in one go and nobody got a question in.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine. He has a burnt board under his own coat with about four inches of one end gone in it and he was in a passage nineteen days ago about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank and said he was not going to go in, three times, and meant it three times.

“*I have been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and I have opened that door twice a day every one of them except about four in the whole nineteen and I know those four to the day.*”

“*I am the ninth opening of nine. That is what the ninth of nine means and I have never once been told what any of the other eight are and I have not asked, and a man of forty-one who asked has been at eight doors in about a fortnight and got one name out of them and that name is a man who has been dead about thirty years.*”

“*I want to stop.*”

Nobody in that room said anything and about nine of them were in a room a hundred and thirty feet under a bank a quarter of an hour before the light.

“*Not today. Today is a Wednesday and I have said I would not do it on a Wednesday and about nine of you know that and none of you asked me about it.*”

“*What I want is the rest of it in a fortnight. And what I want with it is that it be written down that I stopped, with a day on it, where about nine people can hear it and where somebody can point at the day afterwards.*”

“*And I have been putting that want in about four different shapes for about six weeks and every time I have put it in the shape of a person and not in the shape of a line, and I have worked that out this morning about four days ago and I did not tell anybody.*”

About four people in that room had the want and about nine of them had a man of forty-one who had worked it out four days ago.

Then a man of thirty-one said he could get it, and he said it before anybody had finished being quiet, and a man of sixty-one took it off him in about six sentences and gave the reason in the last one.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county a hundred and fifty-three days, and a hundred and fifty-three is seven hundred and seven less five hundred and fifty-four, and there is no form in this county that says a person may hold a thing.

“*There is a column for it. I can get a column for it. There is a printed sheet with nine lines on it and about nine columns of nothing across the top of each, and a name and a day are two of them, and there is room for the rest of what a person is and—*”

“*No. And here is the whole of it and there are six sentences in it.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. He refused nine spaces and two names and a first of the nine in about a fortnight.

“*That sheet was printed about four hundred miles off by somebody who has never been to this county and has never met any of us and is not going to.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says a person may add a column to it, and there is no form in this county that says a person may not.*”

“*And both of those have been true since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week, and they are true of everything, and they have been the reason I have not written nine spaces in this book in about a fortnight.*”

“*And the sixth sentence is the one I would like about four of you to be able to tell in nine days, and it is that you have just told a man of forty-one that there is a column for it because you have been in this county a hundred and fifty-three days and you have never once been in a county where there is not a column for it.*”

Six sentences, and the sixth one was about a man of thirty-one who has been in this county a hundred and fifty-three days and has never once been standing in a county where there is not a column for it.

Then a man of thirty-eight said the plainest thing in that room and it was about a column, and he took about nine seconds over it and nobody improved on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has read a sheet of printed paper out loud on a bank once in this county.

“*A man of forty-one has asked for a day on which he stopped. And a man of sixty-one has said there is no column for it.*”

“*Here is the thing about columns and it is the only thing I know about columns and I have worked it out in about four days.*”

“*A column is not a place to put something. A column is a question that gets asked the same way every time so that the answer can be found again.*”

“*A name column gets asked: who is here. A day column gets asked: when. And there is no column on that sheet that gets asked: who has stopped, because nobody has ever written down a question that has no answer on it yet.*”

“*And a man of forty-one asking for one is not asking for a place. He is asking somebody who has never heard the question to write down that he asked it.*”

He stood about a quarter of an hour from a door and said nine seconds of work out loud, and about nine of them in that room had come down at the fifth hour and not before it.

Then a woman of forty said what a name with no day on it is, and she had said it on a bank the day before and she said it again in a different shape and she said why.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead.

“*I said this yesterday on that bank and I am saying it in this room because a man of forty-one has just asked for a day and the two of them are the same sentence with the ends round about.*”

“*A name with a day against it is a name somebody can be sent for about. That is what a day is for. You point at the day and about nine people can go and see whether the man was in a chair on it.*”

“*And a name with no day on it is a name nobody can be sent for about, because there is nothing to point at, and I said that yesterday about two of the eleven and nobody has written it down since and it is still true this morning.*”

“*And a man of forty-one has just asked for a day on which he stopped, and that is not the same as a day on which he did it, and a sheet with columns in it cannot tell the two apart, and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to be thanked for having got it.*”

About four people in that room had the two days and about nine of them had two blanks on a piece of printed paper four hundred miles off.

Then a woman of forty-four said the whole of what a form is, and it took her four sentences, and she said it was the fourth time she had said a version of it and she was not going to say a fifth.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and nineteen years of it have been a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and there are nine of those chairs and not ten.

“*A form is a form because it asks the same question every time and it does not know who it asked.*”

“*That is all of it and there is nothing else in it. Not the paper and not the print and not the seal if there is a seal. A form is a question that cannot notice the answer.*”

“*You have all been asked the same question. That is the good news and it is the good news and I want it said as good news by a woman of forty-four because nobody has said it as good news yet and about four of you are leaving this room worse than you came into it.*”

“*And the bad news is that you are all going to be asked it again on the same day of the week in about nine weeks, and there is no form in this county that says you may not be, and there is no form in this county that says you may.*”

Then the man of forty-one opened the door and did the keeping, and it took about a minute, and two people put their hands on a stone about as big as a door and pushed it nine feet along a groove and pushed it back, and then the light went out.

And nobody said the word brave and nobody said the word sorry and nobody was thanked.

And a man of thirty-one said he was not going to answer anything about a question that had been asked on the bank on the Wednesday of a week before, and about nine people let him, and about four of them had been expecting him to say it and had not said so.

Nobody in that room asked him what his answer would be, and about four of them had been expecting him to say something and had said nothing about expecting it, and a man of thirty-eight said out loud that a man of thirty-one keeping a silence in front of nine or ten of them is a thing nine or ten of them can be sent for, which is not the same as a thing they can be asked about.

---

The fever was sixty weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-three days.

A man of forty-one who has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years said out loud at about the fifth hour that he wants to stop in a fortnight, and wants the day of it written where about nine people can hear it, and nobody asked him a question about it.

And a man of thirty-one said there is a column for it, and a man of sixty-one took it off him in six sentences, and a man of thirty-eight said that a column is not a place to put something but a question asked the same way every time.

And the keeping was done, and it took about a minute, and nobody was thanked.
