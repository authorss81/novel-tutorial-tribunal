# Chapter 750: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Week, And A Thursday, And A Bank Is Full Again At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Asks Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing Whether Nine People Can Write A Rule Without One Of Them Being The One Who Wrote It, And Nobody Answers

Third day of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. His five hundredth morning. Three hundred and fifty days after the settlement. The fever sixty-six weeks and five days. Three hundred and four days since the division. Two hundred and fifty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full again at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the first morning it had been that since the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and he gave the reading before anybody had asked him for it this morning, as he does. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and a full bank, and it is the first morning it has been that since the Wednesday of last week.*”

“*Four days. It was about twelve at this hour on Wednesday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, and that is the Monday of next week.*”

“*A bank that is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth. I said that on Sunday of last week and I am going to leave it there and not say it again in this county.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twenty-ninth morning, and it was the second morning of a week.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied the asking by one word. He asked to be in that room once and was given nothing, and asked again once and did not ask again. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-nine mornings and there has not been one of them with nothing read on it.*”

“*That is the whole of my count and I am giving it out loud because about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for me to give a figure and there it is.*”

“*It is the second morning of a week and I am going to go on Saturday and on Monday.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty-nine and nobody is going to be told I was brave about any part of it.*”

“*The fifth thing is still not answered and nobody has asked me about it and I am still waiting.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and read the eleven words at the top of the third column, and read the line at the top of the sheet, and it took about nine minutes, and then he put it down on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it and stepped back about four feet.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Forty is seven hundred and fifty less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the forty in the dark.*”

“*And it has been on that counter six mornings now and there is no column on it for a person stopping and nothing in any of the three, and I am not going to say that means anything.*”

“*I am going to read the line at the top one more time because a man of thirty-one is not going to read it either.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told a man of thirty-one on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a second time is a rule. That is a man of thirty-one and not me.*”

“*I have read that line about nine times in a fortnight and I am not going to start counting it out loud.*”

Then he read it, and about four of them looked at the top of the sheet when he started and about nine of them did not, and nobody said a word about either of those two numbers.

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty. Nobody is going to be told I was brave about stepping back four feet.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four asked the question, out loud, in the open, on that bank, at about the seventh hour, in about nine people’s hearing.

She gave the working first, and said nobody was going to answer it and nobody was going to be thanked for asking it.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years, and it cost her one thing a day of them. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper, and that line has had a day against it since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about who put it there.

“*Six mornings is six and six is seven hundred and fifty less seven hundred and forty-four.*”

“*That thing has been on that counter six mornings with nothing in any of the three columns and no column for a person stopping.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said the word out loud on the Friday of last week and refused to fill in any of the three.*”

“*It was a man of thirty-one standing on a bank. And a man of thirty-eight has read that sheet forty mornings and has never once put a hand near it.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four cannot read it and has asked for the ninth line twenty-nine mornings in the open and has never once been asked a question back.*”

“*A man of thirty-four in an ash has been there a hundred and twenty days and has not been asked anything in any of them.*”

“*And about nine houses four miles up a cart road have been doing three things nobody in this county asked them to do.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has walked four miles twice in four days and refused to give six of the nine.*”

Then she asked it, and it was one sentence, and it was asked in the plainest words anybody on that bank has used in about three weeks.

“*Can the people of this county write a rule that does not need one of them to be the one who wrote it.*”

Then nothing whatever happened. The wind came up the reach and went away again. The water was about sixteen inches over the top step and holding. Nobody shouted anything. About four of the people on that bank looked at the top of the sheet and about nine of them looked at the man of fifty-four.

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a question in the air that nobody had answered, and about four of them had a woman of sixty-four who had just asked it.

Then a man of thirty-one said what he was not going to do about it, and he gave the working for his own figure first, and he said it in about nine people’s hearing, and he said he was not going to answer it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-six days. A hundred and ninety-six is seven hundred and fifty less five hundred and fifty-four. On the Friday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week he said the word amendment out loud in about nine people’s hearing and said he was not going to fill in any of the three columns.

“*A hundred and ninety-six is seven hundred and fifty less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety-six days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four has just asked a question in about nine people’s hearing and I am not going to answer it.*”

“*I am going to say why in three sentences and then I am going to stop.*”

“*One: I am the only person anybody has said out loud is the best instrument for being the one who asks, and I have not been asked once in a hundred and ninety-six days.*”

“*Two: a man of sixty-one told a woman of twenty-eight on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a second time is a rule, and a rule is a thing that works without a person.*”

“*Three: if I answer a question about whether nine people can write a rule without one of them writing it, then about nine people on this bank have a man who answers questions about rules.*”

“*And that man goes on doing it whether he is standing on this bank or not.*”

“*That is the whole of it. Nobody is going to be relieved about it. Nobody is going to be thanked, and nobody is going to be told I was right on Friday, and I am not going to answer her.*”

“*And I am going to put my right hand flat on that counter to reach the sheet, because I am not going to end this morning on a refusal about a question somebody else asked.*”

“*The cut across it is sixty-five days old. Sixty-five is seven hundred and fifty less six hundred and eighty-five.*”

“*I said the working out loud and I am not going to let any of you make it into anything else. I have asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep, and nobody has, and nobody is going to be told it was a good trade.*”

“*And none of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday fifty days ago. Fifty is seven hundred and fifty less seven hundred.*”

“*Nobody is going to answer it for me and I am not going to answer it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said what has not happened this week, and she gave the count first, and she refused the last of it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She said on the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week that there is no rule in this county that says a thing which cannot be asked can stop being asked about.

“*Four things have not happened this week and I am going to give them as four because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*One: nobody has filled in any of the three columns in six mornings, and that is six mornings and not a settlement.*”

“*Two: the ninth room has not been kept since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. A man of thirty-one refusing a piece of paper on Friday did not keep it and did not unkeep it.*”

“*Three: the right of refusing is unrestored and it is given to nobody. A shut door is not it, a sealed door is not that, a man who cannot read is not it, and a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything.*”

“*Four: I am not going to say whether nine people in this county can write a rule. I have been asked about a figure for about nine days and I have not got one.*”

“*About four of you can tell in four days that I said that on a bank and not in a book.*”

“*And the fifth is not coming and about four of you have been waiting since the seventh hour for it and I am not going to make it up.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said the last thing on that bank that morning, and it was four sentences, and the fourth one was the reason, and he said nobody was going to be thanked for any of it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He asked to be in that room once and was given nothing, and asked again once and did not ask again. He said an argument out loud in three sentences on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and gave the case before it.

“*Four sentences, and the fourth of them is the reason and it is mine and it is not anybody else’s.*”

“*One: a woman of sixty-four has just asked whether nine of us can write a rule without one of us writing it, and nobody has answered her, and I am not going to answer her either.*”

“*Two: I cannot read. If there was a rule on that counter I would not know it was there until a man of thirty-eight read it out loud.*”

“*That is the only way I have ever been able to check anything and I said that on the second day of this week.*”

“*Three: I asked to be in that room once on a Sunday of a fortnight and was given nothing, and I asked again once and did not ask again, and a man of thirty-eight has not offered it a third time in about nine days and that is the correct way round.*”

“*Four: if nine of us can write a rule without one of us being the one who wrote it, then there is nobody in it who can be asked about it.*”

“*I have been asking the same thing every morning for twenty-nine mornings and about nine of you have heard every one of them and not one of you has asked me a question back.*”

“*That is not a finding and I am not going to improve on it. Nobody is going to be thanked for four sentences and nobody is going to be told I was brave about saying them on the last morning of a week.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a full bank and a question in the air. About four of them had a man of fifty-four who had just said that a rule written by nobody in it has nobody in it who can be asked about it. Nobody answered anybody. Nobody carried the sheet anywhere.

The fever was sixty-six weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and ninety-six days.

A woman of sixty-four asked out loud in about nine people’s hearing whether the people of that county can write a rule that does not need one of them to be the one who wrote it, and gave the working for six mornings first and said nobody was going to answer it and nobody was going to be thanked for asking it.

And a man of thirty-one said he was not going to answer it, and gave three sentences.

He is the only person anybody has said out loud is the best instrument for being the one who asks, and has not been asked once in a hundred and ninety-six days. A man of sixty-one told a woman of twenty-eight on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a second time is a rule, and a rule is a thing that works without a person. And if he answers a question about whether nine people can write a rule without one of them writing it, then about nine people on that bank have a man who answers questions about rules, and that man works whether he is standing there or not.

And a man of fifty-four who cannot read said that if nine people can write a rule without one of them being the one who wrote it, then there is nobody in it who can be asked about it.

He has asked the same thing every morning for twenty-nine mornings and not one of the nine has asked him a question back.
