# Chapter 737: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Seventh Week, And A Friday, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says About Nine Minutes Is The Only Figure He Has Ever Given Out Loud In Nineteen Years, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Is Not Going To Offer Again

Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. His four hundred and eighty-seventh morning. Three hundred and thirty-seven days after the settlement. The fever sixty-four weeks and six days. Two hundred and ninety-one days since the division. Two hundred and thirty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading, said the working, and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, in the order he has given them every morning of this week. Nobody had asked him for the third of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, and nineteen years of that landing is the whole of what he has ever said about himself. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it, and has given two figures for a night since. Neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour.*”

“*Seven days. A full bank holds, and that is the whole of the working, and it was full on the first day of this week at about the seventh hour.*”

“*The next time about ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow at about the sixth hour, and that is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*And it came off on a Tuesday, and then a Wednesday, and then a Thursday, and then a Friday, and then it came off last night.*”

“*A bank that is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth. I gave you that on Sunday and I am not going to give it again in this county.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the sixteenth morning, and he said it flat and short.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has said it in the same words each time. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered, and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Sixteen mornings.*”

“*And the ninth line has not changed in sixteen mornings and there are fourteen names on it and two of them are in ink.*”

“*And I am not going to say what I do with it, and I am still not answering the fifth thing, and not one of the nine of you has asked me and that is not a favour.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for the sixteen and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read that line out loud in the open, and then read fourteen names, and it took about nine minutes, and he said the other thing before he started.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twenty-seven times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He has decided every morning since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week not to say a name out loud.

“*Twenty-seven is seven hundred and thirty-seven less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty-seven in the dark.*”

“*And I am going to say the other half of it and nobody asked me.*”

“*About nine minutes is a figure of a job and not of a man, and I have given it every morning for a fortnight and I have given nothing else.*”

“*A day is about four inches, a bank is about sixteen, four days is a full bank. That is a man of forty-three’s and I have said it after him every morning for a fortnight.*”

“*And those are the only figures I have ever been able to give out loud in nineteen years, and I have never once given one of this county about myself.*”

“*A man of forty-three has given the same three things every morning for a fortnight. I do not know what he has got them for and I am not going to ask him.*”

“*And I have not said a name out loud and I decided again at the fifth hour at the head of my own gate and I am not going to say anything about that either.*”

The four of them who had a man of thirty-eight who had read twenty-seven mornings. About nine of them had a man of forty-three who had given the same three things every morning for a fortnight and would not say why.

Then a boy of thirteen said out loud that he has been counting a man in an ash since the Friday of a fortnight. He gave the working, said he was not going to ask him anything, and nobody asked him a question about it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping three counts since the Saturday of a fortnight. The third one of the three is a man in an ash and nobody has ever given him that figure, he counted it on the road coming down.

“*A hundred and seven days.*”

“*A hundred and seven is seven hundred and thirty-seven less six hundred and thirty, and I counted it on the road coming down and nobody asked me.*”

“*And I gave ninety-eight on the Wednesday of last week and a hundred and three on the Monday of last week, and I am not going to make a total of the three of them.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and eight days, and a man of sixty-one gave that figure on the Friday of a fortnight and has not given it since.*”

“*A man of sixty-one has said he is not going to add them.*”

“*I am not going to add them either, and I am not going to be thanked for carrying three counts.*”

“*And a boy of thirteen should not invent what a man does in an ash, and a boy of thirteen should not ask him anything, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about either of those.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that he is not going to offer again, in front of about nine people, before anybody had asked him anything. A woman of twenty-eight said the true of it in four sentences, and then said the half of it that nobody had asked her to say.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-three days. A hundred and eighty-three is seven hundred and thirty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. He has offered to be the one who asks seven times in about three weeks and been refused seven times out loud. Nobody has ever told him to stop.

“*I am not going to offer again.*”

“*Not on this bank and not up that cart road and not to a man of thirty-four in an ash and not to a woman of twenty-nine in a reed.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight has said the only figure he has been able to give out loud in nineteen years is about nine minutes. He is right about himself.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two has said a mark is worth being sent for and is not worth being believed. About four of you believed the sixth.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight told me yesterday that the sixth is the one that did the damage, and she said it in nine sentences.*”

“*So this is not a decision. A decision is a thing a man of sixty-one would put a number to. This is a man of thirty-one counting what is left.*”

“*And what is left is that I am nobody’s. A woman of forty-four said on the Thursday of last week that there is not going to be a column in this county with my name at the top of it, and I have had a week to argue with her and I have not.*”

“*She was right.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of stopping, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and nobody is going to be told I was right to stop.*”

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine, and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She has said the same sentence nine times without saying it and has not begun again. The count of the times she has said it and not said it carries no ordinal.

“*Four sentences, and the fourth one is mine and it is about what you have just said and not about why.*”

“*One: a man of thirty-one is nobody’s and there is no form in this county that says a man may be a person and no form that says he may not.*”

“*Two: about nine people in this county have said out loud in about nine days that he is the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks.*”

“*I do not know how many of those nine believed the sixth and I am not going to count them here.*”

“*Three: a man of thirty-eight has been read a thing out loud in the open twenty-seven mornings and has not said a name once.*”

“*That is a figure of a man and not of a job, and I am not going to let the two of you be put in one line this morning.*”

“*Four: nobody on this bank is going to be told that a man of thirty-one stopping is a good thing, and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it in four sentences.*”

“*And here is the half nobody asked me for.*”

“*I have said the same sentence nine times in about nine days and I am not going to say it a tenth, and I have just said four sentences that are the same sentence in a different order.*”

“*I would like about four of you to be able to tell in four days that I did that on purpose.*”

About four on that bank had a woman of twenty-eight who had said four sentences and then admitted the shape of them. About nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had said he was not going to offer again and was not relieved of it.

Then a man of fifty-four said the thing that has been standing behind all of it since Sunday. He cannot read, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

“*A man of thirty-one has just said he is not going to be the one who asks.*”

“*And I am the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about four things.*”

“*About four of you have been saying for six weeks that nobody is going to ask me whether I would be a fifth.*”

“*And I have not been asked, and I have still not answered the thing that was asked, and I am not going to say that those two are the same size.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for this morning and I am not going to be told I was brave about it.*”

“*A boy of thirteen has just said out loud that he is not going to ask a man in an ash anything. I have been saying about four of the same thing for six weeks.*”

“*I am not going to put him in a line with me and I am not going to thank him for it.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the last morning before the water came off. Four of them had a man of thirty-one who was not going to offer again. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had not been asked a question in a fortnight and had not answered one.

---

The fever was sixty-four weeks and six days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-three days.

A man of thirty-eight said out loud that about nine minutes is the only figure he has been able to give out loud in nineteen years. He said a day is about four inches, a bank is about sixteen, and four days is a full bank, and that he does not know what the man of forty-three has them for and is not going to ask.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud that he is not going to offer again, and called it a man counting what is left and not a decision. A woman of twenty-eight said the true of it in four sentences, and then said she had just said the same sentence a tenth time in a different order.
