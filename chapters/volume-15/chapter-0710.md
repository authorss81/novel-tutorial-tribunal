# Chapter 710: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Saturday, And On Which A Sheet Of Printed Paper With Nine Lines And Eleven Names On It Is Read Out Loud In The Open On A Bank From This Morning And Every Morning After It, And A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Reads Out Loud The Seven Things That Are Not On It And Gives The Count With The Working And Says It Is A Figure Of A Sheet And Not A Figure Of Anybody, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asks For How Long And Is Refused A Figure And Is Given One By A Boy Of Thirteen He Did Not Ask For

Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and sixtieth morning. Three hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks old. Two hundred and sixty-four days since the division. Two hundred and ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full again at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and said it was four days, and nine or ten of them were on that bank in the wind at about the ninth hour with a piece of printed paper lying on a counter about four feet behind them where anybody could put a hand on it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. A bank fills in about four days and empties in about three, and this one had gone off in the night on the Tuesday of this week and been full again by this morning.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Four days, and it came off in the night on the Tuesday of this week and that is a full bank and there is no more working in it than that.*”

“*And I have been on that landing at the seventh hour every morning of this week and the fifth time came off in the night was on the Tuesday and I have a figure for that one now and I am not going to give you another.*”

About four people on that bank had the sixteen inches and about nine of them were looking at a piece of printed paper on a counter.

Then a man of thirty-eight read it out loud again, in the open, in the order it was written, and counted twice, and gave the working, and said that he was going to do it every morning from this one and had not been asked to and would not be thanked for it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he has read that sheet out loud once in this county, on a Sunday, on a bank, in the open air.

“*Nine lines. Eleven names. Two of the eleven the same name twice. Two of the eleven with no day against them.*”

“*And the days, in order, from the Sunday: seven days, five days, four days, four days, five days, seven days, nine days, and then the two blanks, and the two blanks are on the fifth line and the ninth.*”

“*And one of the two blanks is a woman of twenty-nine in a reed and I read her name out loud on the Sunday and nobody in that bank knew she was on it, and about nine people heard it on the Friday, and I am not going to read it differently this morning and I am not going to leave it out.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine lines and about nine of them had a woman of twenty-nine read out loud for the third time.

Then a woman of twenty-eight read out loud the things that are not on it, in order, and gave the count with the working, and said what the count was a figure of before she said it.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from, and is not one of the nine, and checked a thing ten times on the Monday of this week and said it was the last of a fortnight.

“*I have got a list and I have been carrying it in my own head since the Sunday and I am going to read it out loud in the open because that is the only place anything gets said properly any more.*”

“*Seven. Seven is the count and I am giving it before I give it.*”

“*One. No day of anybody stopping.*”

“*Two. No column for what happens on a morning when the person who said yes does not come.*”

“*Three. No column for who a person in this county is, and a woman of sixty-four told that to about nine people on this bank on the Thursday and it is the third one because it is the one that makes the other two possible.*”

“*Four. No column for what a name on it is for.*”

“*Five. No column for the person who keeps it. There is no original and there is no keeper and a man of fifty-four who cannot read has the eighth of nine boards inside his coat and has not asked anybody to read it to him, and that is about four weeks old now and none of it is on this sheet.*”

“*Six. No column for how many were asked.*”

“*Seven. No column for whether anybody was told they could not.*”

“*And that is seven and it is a figure of a sheet of printed paper that came up nine miles of cart road in the inside of a coat, and it is not a figure of a person and it is not a figure of a week, and I am not going to add it to anything.*”

Nobody on that bank said sorry and about four of them had the seven and about nine of them had a woman of twenty-eight who had said nothing for about eleven days and then said all of it.

Then a man of forty-four said what those seven are, and it took her about six sentences and she said that it was the plainest thing she had said since she came up that cart road and that she was not going to improve on it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and she has a list is a column and said it out loud four times in about six weeks.

“*A thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing.*”

“*That is all seven of them in one sentence and I have been getting to it since the Saturday of a fortnight and I have not got there any earlier because a thing with a form under it does not arrive all at once. It arrives as a column, and then as a day, and then as a keeper, and then somebody finds out that the person in it cannot be sent for about.*”

“*And a person who cannot be sent for about is not a person refusing. A person refusing is somebody who can be sent for and says no. The other one is a field with something in it, and about nine people in this county have got one of those in about four days and nobody sent them for anything.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for saying it on a Saturday morning in the open air, and I am not going to be forgiven for having got to it four weeks late, and I would like somebody to say in about four days that a woman of forty-four said it and not a man with a hand and a book.*”

About four people on that bank had the field and about nine of them had a woman of forty-four standing next to a sheet of printed paper.

Then a man of thirty-eight said that he was going to do it every morning, and gave the four reasons in the order he had them, and refused to give a figure for how long, and was refused a figure by a man of sixty-one in about five sentences.

“*Every morning, in the open, before anybody goes anywhere, from this one. And I am not going to keep it and it is not going to be kept, because a thing nine people have is a thing nine people can be sent for about, and a man of fifty-four stopped a man of thirty-one with that sentence about six weeks ago and it has been sitting in my own head ever since.*”

“*It lives on that counter, where anybody can put a hand on it. If somebody carries it into a room then somebody is keeping it, and if somebody is keeping it then there is a keeper, and I have read a man of sixty-one refuse nine spaces and two names and a first of the nine in about a fortnight and I am not going to be the reason he has to refuse a tenth thing.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years and has written one number in it in eleven years.

“*I am not going to give you a figure for it and here are five sentences of reason and I am not going to give a sixth.*”

“*A figure for it is a term. I have kept heads and carts in this county for eleven years and a head is a head until somebody says how long it has to turn round, and when somebody says that it is not a head any more, it is a place on a line with an end on it.*”

“*And I am not going to be the man who writes the end of a term on a thing that was not put in front of me by anybody who could be sent for about.*”

Five sentences and no sixth, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who was going to read nine lines every morning of a thing nobody had put a term on.

Then a boy of thirteen gave him a figure he did not ask for, and gave the working, and a man of thirty-eight told him to keep it.

“*You have been counting since the Thursday of last week. That is nine days. Nine is seven hundred and ten less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And it is not a term and it is not a figure of a year and I am not going to let it be one, and I am going to keep it because I counted it and I am not going to hand it to a man of thirty-eight like a present.*”

“*Keep it.*”

Nine days, and a boy of thirteen keeping a number nobody had asked him for, and about nine of them on that bank had watched him refuse to hand it over.

Then a man of thirty-one said the shape of his answer out loud, before he meant to, in front of about nine people, and said he was saying it early on purpose, and a woman of twenty-eight told him what he had actually done.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in that county a hundred and fifty-six days. A hundred and fifty-six is seven hundred and ten less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*I am going to say the shape of my answer out loud before I have got an answer, and I am doing it a week early on purpose, because a man of sixty-one has been keeping a week of mornings this week and about nine of you have been waiting since the Wednesday of a week ago to find out whether I am going to say anything.*”

“*Here is the shape. If I am asked again, the answer will be the same as it was on the Wednesday, which is nothing at all. No is not what comes out. Neither is yes. And there will be no claim in it that I need a week, and none that I failed to take it in.*”

“*And I am telling about nine of you that now so that when it happens, if it happens, about nine of you can stand on this bank and say that a man of thirty-one said it a week before it happened and not that he worked it out on the day.*”

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine and cannot be sent for about in two counties.

“*That is a rule and it is not an answer, and you have handed it to about nine people in the open air and they have all got something to hold, and you did that to yourself on purpose this morning and I want it noticed that you did it on purpose.*”

“*A rule is a thing that works without a person. You have just built one about yourself and given it away, and in about nine weeks it will be a rule that a man of thirty-one does not answer, and it will be a rule about a man of thirty-one in about nine people’s mouths, and nobody will have been there when it was made.*”

“*And that is the second time this fortnight has made a thing out of your own mouth and the first one was about a column and it was me who stopped that one and I am not going to stop this one for you because you are a grown man and it is your own silence and I am not going to be the person who holds it.*”

A woman of twenty-eight who was not going to be the person who holds a man of thirty-one’s silence for him, and about nine of them had heard her refuse it before he had finished giving it away.

Then a man of fifty-four said what was not going to happen, and he cannot read, and nine or ten of them were on that bank and the sheet was lying in the open about four feet behind them.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain. He is the only person anybody can be sent for about four things and nobody has asked him to be a fifth.

“*Nobody is going to know who wrote it. That is what is going to happen and I want it said on this bank on a Saturday morning by a man of fifty-four who cannot read.*”

“*There is a man of thirty-six who carried it and a woman in a town a day’s walk off who gave it to him and neither of them is in this county and neither of them can be sent for about from here, and that is not because anybody arranged it. That is because that is where the paper came from.*”

“*And nobody in this county is going to find out in about nine days and nobody is going to find out in about four years, and about nine of us will spend those four years reading nine lines out loud every morning and saying out loud that nobody knows.*”

“*And that is not the worst thing. The worst thing is on the sheet and it is the two blanks, and about four of us know what a blank is now and about nine of us do not, and in about four years everybody in this county will know what a blank is and it will not be any use to anybody at all.*”

Nobody in this county is going to find out who wrote it, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read said that on a Saturday morning about four feet from the thing itself, and about nine of them were standing there and had heard him say it.

Then the reading was finished, and it took about nine minutes, and about nine people were on that bank, and the sheet went back on the counter where anybody could put a hand on it, and nobody carried it anywhere.

And nobody said the word brave and nobody said the word sorry, and nobody was thanked.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-six days.

A sheet of printed paper with nine lines and eleven names on it is read out loud in the open on that bank from this morning and every morning after it, and it lives on the counter of a landing where anybody can put a hand on it, and there is no original and no keeper.

And a woman of twenty-eight read out loud the seven things that are not on it, and a woman of forty-four said in one sentence what those seven are, and a man of thirty-eight is going to read nine lines every morning without a term on it.

And a man of thirty-one said the shape of his answer to a question that has been standing since a Wednesday nine days ago, and it was nothing, and it was not a refusal, and a woman of twenty-eight said it was a rule and refused to hold it for him.

And nobody answered the question. Nobody said no and nobody said yes, a woman in a reed said one word on the Friday about something and has not said another since, and about nine people can stand on that bank and say that nothing was said about it.
