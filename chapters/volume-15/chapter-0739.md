# Chapter 739: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Seventh Week, And A Sunday, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says Out Loud That He Has Decided Twenty Mornings Running And Is Going To Stop Deciding, And Nobody Asks

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. His four hundred and eighty-ninth morning. Three hundred and thirty-nine days after the settlement. The fever sixty-five weeks and one day. Two hundred and ninety-three days since the division. Two hundred and thirty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. He also gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, before anybody had asked him for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has stood on that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it, and he has given two figures for a night since. Neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About four inches over the top step, and it is the first day of it.*”

“*One day. It came off all ninety in the night at the sixth hour, and that is the working.*”

“*Four inches a day, and a full bank is about sixteen, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*The next time about ninety steps can be walked is in seven days from this morning, and that is the Sunday of next week.*”

“*It came off on a Tuesday, and a Wednesday, and a Thursday, and a Friday, and a Saturday, and this is the first Sunday I have said out loud in about a fortnight.*”

“*Nobody on that bank is going to ask me what I think about a Sunday, and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened. It was the eighteenth morning, and it was the last morning before a week turns over.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not added a word. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered, and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Eighteen mornings and there has been one morning of that run with nothing read on it.*”

“*And I know which one it is and I am not going to let about four of you work it out between you and think they have caught me.*”

“*And the next three mornings are the Monday of this week and the Tuesday and the Wednesday of next week, and I am going to ask on all three of them.*”

“*I have still not answered the fifth thing, and nobody has asked me, and I am still waiting.*”

“*And I am not going to stop waiting because a week has turned over.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read that line out loud in the open, and then read fourteen names, and it took about nine minutes, and the sheet went back on that counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twenty-nine times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He decided every morning from the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week to the Saturday of this week, and he stopped on this one, and he has not said what he was deciding.

“*Twenty-nine is seven hundred and thirty-nine less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty-nine in the dark.*”

“*And I decided again this morning at the fifth hour at the head of my own gate, and I am not going to give the answer.*”

“*And I decided every morning from the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week to yesterday, and about four of you have worked out what it was and the rest of you have not, and that is the correct way round.*”

“*And I am not going to say it, and I am not going to be thanked for the deciding, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then he said the other half. Nobody had asked him for it, and it was in a different shape from anything he had said on that bank in about a fortnight.

“*And I am going to stop deciding.*”

“*Not tomorrow. This morning. That is the whole of it and I have not said what the deciding was for.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said at first light yesterday that a rule is a thing that works without a person, and he was not talking about me and he was right.*”

“*Deciding every morning is a thing that works with a person in it, and a child of nine at the back of it.*”

“*If I stop then the morning has one fewer thing in it, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about the morning.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said on Friday of this week that he is not going to offer again. A woman of twenty-eight said that was a man counting what is left and not a decision.*”

“*She was right about him, and she would be right about me if I said it was a decision.*”

“*So I am not going to call it a decision and I am not going to call it a count.*”

“*About four of you are going to work out in four days that it is the same shape as a man of thirty-one, and I am not going to help them.*”

Four of that morning had a man of thirty-eight who had decided every morning since a Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week and had stopped. About nine of them had a man of forty-three who had said a Wednesday out loud for the first time in a fortnight and would not say what he thought of it.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it in his own mouth and said what it cost him. He gave the working, and said nobody was going to be thanked for either of them.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-five days. A hundred and eighty-five is seven hundred and thirty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. On Friday of this week he said he is not going to offer again. On Saturday, in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under that bank, he said none of that is an answer to anything asked on a Wednesday thirty-nine days ago.

“*A man of thirty-eight has just stopped deciding and I have been one of the things he was deciding about.*”

“*Not the whole of it. About four. I do not know which four and he is not going to tell me and I am not going to ask him.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight said on Wednesday that a seventh is a rule, and that a rule is a thing that works without a person.*”

“*And I have spent about four days not offering, and it has not got easier, and that is the part I am not going to put in a better shape.*”

“*A hundred and eighty-five is seven hundred and thirty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty-five days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*And I have wanted for a hundred and eighty-five days to be the one somebody asks.*”

“*About nine people said out loud in about nine days that I am the best instrument in four counties for it, and I have not been asked once in a hundred and eighty-five days.*”

“*So the cost of stopping is that the thing I wanted and the thing that is true about me turn out to be the same size, and I am not going to say that is a joke.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of it and I am not going to be thanked for it, and nobody is going to tell me I was right about stopping.*”

“*And about four of you are going to be relieved in about four days and I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said what the whole of that bank is, in about nine sentences. She said it was hers and not his, and she said it in the open on a Sunday morning.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years. She got up out of the book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and there has been no line for her in it since. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper with no day against it. There are fourteen names on nine lines and two of them are in ink.

“*About nine people on this bank have stopped doing a thing in about four days and about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for it.*”

“*And I am the one of the nine who has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years, and I did not stop anything.*”

“*And nobody in four counties is going to be sent for about me to ask whether I did.*”

“*Being a name that nobody can find by writing it down cost me nineteen years and one thing a day of them.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has been nobody’s for a hundred and eighty-five days, and nobody is going to be sent for about him either.*”

“*And about four of you have just worked out that those two are the same shape. They are the same shape.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved about it and I am not going to be forgiven for saying it.*”

“*And the thing that cost me one thing a day for nineteen years is not paid back by four men putting a thing down, and nobody on this bank is going to be told that it is.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying that and I am not going to be told I was right about it.*”

“*And about four of you can tell in four days that I said it on a bank and not in a book, and that is all I am claiming and I am not going to be told I am right.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on a Sunday morning. About four of them had a man of thirty-eight who had stopped deciding and a man of thirty-one who was not going to offer again. A woman of sixty-four had paid one thing a day for nineteen years and was not going to be paid back.

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. Nobody carried it anywhere. About nine people stood in the wind with four inches of water under the top step, and nobody said another word about a Sunday of next week.

---

The fever was sixty-five weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-five days.

A man of thirty-eight said out loud on a Sunday morning that he has decided every morning for twenty mornings, from the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week to yesterday, and is going to stop deciding. He said deciding is a thing that works with a person in it, and refused to say what the deciding was about, and nobody asked him.

And a woman of sixty-four said that about nine people on that bank have stopped doing a thing in about four days and she did not stop anything. She said the thing which cost her one thing a day for nineteen years is not paid back by four men putting a thing down.
