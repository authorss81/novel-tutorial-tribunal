# Chapter 734: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Seventh Week, And A Wednesday, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Who Cannot See Well Says Out Loud That A Mark Is Worth Being Sent For And Is Not Worth Being Believed, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Walks Four Miles Down That Cart Road To Say She Has Been Asked A Fourth Thing, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Will Go Up That Road Himself

Second day of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. His four hundred and eighty-fourth morning. Three hundred and thirty-four days after the settlement. The fever sixty-four weeks and three days. Two hundred and eighty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and thirty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working. He gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked as well, and nobody had asked him for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, and that landing is the only sentence anybody can say about him that has not had to be corrected. He has given two figures for a night since he stopped saying he had nothing to say about one, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step, and it is the fifth day of it.*”

“*Five days. A full bank holds, and it was full yesterday, and that is the whole of the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning, and that is the Friday of this week.*”

“*I said on Tuesday that I would not say it a second time, and I am going to break that once and only once.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting two days for a fourth thing and I am not going to give you one.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened. It was the thirteenth morning, and he said the thing that had been standing behind it since the Sunday of this week.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. The fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered, and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Thirteen mornings.*”

“*And I have not asked for the room again and nobody has offered it and that is the correct way round.*”

“*I am still not answering the fifth thing. I said on Sunday that I would wait to be asked.*”

“*Not one of the nine of you has asked me, and that is not a favour to anybody.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for the thirteen and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read that line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it. It took about nine minutes and the sheet went back on that counter.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twenty-four times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth.

“*Nine words, and four of them are about my own two hands and nine feet of it.*”

“*And there is still no day against either name on that line, and there never was.*”

“*Twenty-four is seven hundred and thirty-four less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty-four in the dark.*”

“*I read the line at the top as well. A man of fifty-four has now been read it thirteen mornings.*”

“*A man of thirty-one on this bank has not read it once.*”

“*I am not going to say that to him and I am not going to say it for him.*”

There were four on that bank who had a man of thirty-eight who had read twenty-four mornings. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had not been asked a question about anything in a fortnight.

Then a man of about fifty-two said out loud what a mark is worth. He gave the working first. He said it in nine people’s hearing, and said it was the first time anybody in this county had put the two halves together.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county. He has been that person eleven years without knowing it was a thing to be. He asked on the Monday of a fortnight whether a line on a sheet is a person, and on the Wednesday of a fortnight whether a mark can say no in nine words. He has been answered once out of three.

“*I have been a mark for eleven years, and I said on the Tuesday of a fortnight that a mark is a thing somebody puts beside a rule to say nobody has been through it.*”

“*And I have not improved on that and I am not going to.*”

“*And here is the half of it that nobody in this county has said out loud, and I have been carrying it about four days.*”

“*A mark is worth being sent for, and it is not worth being believed.*”

“*And both of those are true at the same time and about the same mark, and about four of you have been treating them as one thing since Tuesday.*”

“*I have been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years, and in eleven years I have not been sent for about anything.*”

“*That is not a fault in the sheets.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has been the outside of a thing for a hundred and seventy-nine days and in a hundred and seventy-nine days he has been sent for about nothing, and he says that is what he wants.*”

“*A woman of forty-four told him on Thursday that knowing does not put a column in that county with his name at the top of it. She was right.*”

“*And this morning I am saying what is on the other side of it.*”

“*If there were a column with your name at the top of it, you would be sent for, and you would be believed, and you would not be a man who is used to get at a thing.*”

“*A woman of forty took you off in seven sentences on Saturday for calling nine holes nine fields. She was right about the seven.*”

“*Neither of those two things is going to change.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and I am not going to be told I am right.*”

“*If nobody agrees with me this morning then I am one more man of about fifty-two in four counties, and that is the correct way round.*”

About nine of them on that bank had a man of about fifty-two who had put two halves of a thing together for the first time in this county. Four of them had a man of thirty-one who had been sent for about nothing for a hundred and eighty days, and did not know until that moment that it was the whole of what he had.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it in his own mouth, and gave the working, and said nobody was going to be thanked for him not arguing with it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty days. A hundred and eighty is seven hundred and thirty-four less five hundred and fifty-four. He has offered to be the one who asks six times in about three weeks and been refused six times out loud. Nobody has ever told him to stop.

“*He is right about all of it and I am not going to argue with one word of it.*”

“*A hundred and eighty is seven hundred and thirty-four less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two has just told me out loud, in the hearing of the nine of you standing there, that the only thing I have is that nobody can be sent for about me.*”

“*And he said it is not worth being believed, and I am not going to argue with that either.*”

“*I am not going to be relieved of it, and I am not going to be thanked for not arguing.*”

“*About four of you are going to be relieved about it in about four days and I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well has said a thing nobody in four counties can be sent for about, and that is what he is.*”

“*I am not going to put him in a line with a man of thirty-four in an ash or a woman of twenty-nine in a reed.*”

Then a woman of forty-four came down four miles of that cart road on a Wednesday morning. She said the thing she had come down for before she got to the bottom of the road.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has kept that bakehouse nineteen years, and about nine houses in that village take her bread. She has been asked three times and has answered twice. She worked out a thing in her own head on the Sunday of a fortnight ago and cannot give it back to anybody, and she still has three askings and two answers.

“*I have been asked a fourth thing and I have not answered it and I am not going to answer it this week.*”

“*That is all I came four miles down that cart road to say, and I said it before I got to the bottom of it.*”

“*Two men in two coats came through my own door and used the same nine words.*”

“*I worked out on the Sunday of a fortnight ago that the nine words on the ninth line of a printed sheet are the same nine words.*”

“*A third man came through that door on the Sunday of this week and used the same nine words a third time.*”

“*He did not know about the other two, and I have not said that out loud to anybody before this morning.*”

“*Three is a figure of men in coats and not a figure of anybody. I am giving it out loud and I am not going to add it to the two.*”

“*And I am not going to say which of the three cost more.*”

“*I said on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week that I would rather not know. I have known since the Sunday of a fortnight ago.*”

“*And I am saying it again now that a third one has come, and I mean it both times.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for four miles, and nobody is going to be told it was worth the walk, and I am not going to stand on this bank and let anybody say that.*”

About four of them had a woman of forty-four who had come down four miles to say a thing she had not answered. About nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had been told a third time in three weeks and had not known about the other two.

Then a man of thirty-one said he would go up that road himself and ask her the fourth thing in the plainest words, out loud, standing on her own step. He said it before anybody had finished being quiet.

“*I will go up there and ask her.*”

“*Not about the two men and not about the third one. About the fourth thing she has not answered.*”

“*I will write down what she says and I will bring it back down and put it on that counter where anybody can put a hand on it.*”

“*And I know what is going to happen. A man of fifty-four is going to tell me in nine sentences that this is the sixth asking.*”

“*And he is going to be right about the six.*”

Four of the nine had a man of thirty-one who had said he would go up four miles. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had been refused six times out loud in about three weeks and had never once been told to stop.

---

The fever was sixty-four weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty days.

A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said out loud that a mark is worth being sent for and is not worth being believed. He said both of those are true at the same time and about the same mark, and that if there were a column with a man of thirty-one’s name at the top of it he would be sent for and believed and would not be a man who is used to get at a thing.

And a woman of forty-four came down four miles to say she has been asked a fourth thing in the same nine words and has not answered it. A man of thirty-one said he would go up that road himself and ask it.
