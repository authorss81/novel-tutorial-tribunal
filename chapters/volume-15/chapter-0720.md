# Chapter 720: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which The Tenth Reading Of Twelve Names Is Done In The Open On A Bank And A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That A Day About Nine People Have Heard Read Out Is Not A Day In A Book And Not A Day On That Sheet And Not A Day Anybody Can Be Sent For About, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says Somebody Is Going To Write Another Name On It In About Four Days, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Be Standing On That Bank On Wednesday

First day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventieth morning. Three hundred and twenty days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and three days. Two hundred and seventy-four days since the division. Two hundred and twenty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour for the sixth morning running and the man of forty-three gave the reading, gave the day, and gave a figure. About nine of them were on that bank and about four of them had heard all three of those things.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. About ninety steps of the work are under the bank he stands on, he has said he has nothing to say about a night five times, and he has not said it since. He says the two figures he did give are neither of them his.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Six days, and a full bank holds, and the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in two days from this morning.*”

“*That is four days after this bank is full, and this bank was full on the Sunday of last week. There is no more working in it than there has been since the Friday of last week.*”

“*And I am going to go on giving you all three of those every morning, and I am not going to give you a fourth thing.*”

“*If anybody standing on this bank wants a term on it, ask a man of sixty-one. He will not give you one.*”

Two days on that bank. About nine of them had a man of forty-three who had given the same three things six mornings running and stopped at three.

Then a man of thirty-eight read twelve names out loud in the open for the tenth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes. Three of the twelve have no day against them, and one of the twelve was not in the print five days ago.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet ten times in this county, and has been refused a figure for how long three times. On the Friday of last week he said he was going to read the new one with the other eleven.

“*Twelve names. And I said on Friday that I was going to read the twelfth with the other eleven, and I am not going to leave it off because it is in ink and not in the print.*”

“*And nobody has asked me to and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*And about four of you have not worked out that the reason I read it is that the reason somebody wrote it is not a reason anybody can do anything about.*”

“*And about nine minutes. Ten mornings. I have got no figure for how long and I have been refused one three times and I am going to ask a fourth time about a week from now.*”

About four people on that bank had the ten, and about nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had been refused a figure three times and had asked again.

Then a man of sixty-one said what a day is that about nine people have heard read out, and he gave it in about nine sentences. Then he refused to write any of it, and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting a fortnight for it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, keeps a book of heads and carts, and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head of that book, and a number is the last of the five, and he has not added a name to any of them.

“*Here is the thing, and it is the first thing in about four weeks that has been any use to me.*”

“*I am going to give it out loud and then I am going to refuse to put it anywhere.*”

“*A day about nine people have heard read out is not a day in my book. It is not a day on that sheet.*”

“*It is not a day anybody in four counties can be sent for about, and it is not a day anybody in four counties can point at either. Those are two different things, and they have been getting confused all week.*”

“*There is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a day. And there is no form anywhere that says a person may not.*”

“*And a man of forty-one has been asking for one of those days since the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-third week, and there is nowhere in four hundred miles to put it.*”

“*So the only thing he has got is about nine people who heard it.*”

“*And that is the same thing a man of fifty-four who cannot read said on that bank nineteen days ago, and I did not take it from him then and I am not taking it from him now.*”

“*A thing about nine people have is a thing about nine people can be sent for about. That is his sentence.*”

“*And it cuts both ways, and four of you have been working out that for two days, and the rest of you have not.*”

“*The fifth sentence is not coming, and none of the nine is going to be written down anywhere.*”

“*I have given three reasons for that about nine times in about six weeks and there is not going to be a fourth.*”

About four people on that bank had the thing, and about nine of them had a man of sixty-one who had given nine sentences and stopped at nine on purpose.

Then a woman of sixty-four said what is going to happen next. She said it before anybody asked her, and she said she had not got a figure for it and was not going to make one.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and is out of every book in this county. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a piece of printed paper with no day against it, and nobody in this county can be sent for about who put it there.

“*Somebody is going to write another name on that thing. I am telling the nine of you standing here now, on a Tuesday morning, in the open air, and nobody is going to be sent for about it.*”

“*Not because anybody has arranged it. Because there have been four nights on that counter, and about nine of us said out loud on the Saturday of a fortnight that we were leaving it there.*”

“*And a name came on it, and it cost nobody anything to come.*”

“*I have not got a figure for when. I am not going to say it will be me, and I am not going to say it will not be me either, and nobody is going to stand me on this bank and ask whether I am frightened of it.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it out loud a week early on purpose.*”

“*That is a thing a man of thirty-one did on the Saturday of a fortnight, and nobody on this bank has stopped talking about.*”

About nine of them had a woman of sixty-four who had said a thing out loud a week early on purpose about four nights on a counter, and not one of them was going to be thanked for it.

Then a man of fifty-four said what he had done with a question asked on a bank two days ago. He cannot read, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter, and is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about four things. He was asked a fifth thing on the Sunday of last week in about nine people’s hearing.

“*Two days. I have had it two days and I have not answered it and I am not going to answer it this week either.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for not answering it, and nobody is going to be told I have been brave about it.*”

“*Four of them know what that is and the rest of them do not, and that is the correct way round, and it has not changed since the Sunday of last week.*”

“*And I want it said that a man of fifty-four who cannot read has not answered a question that about nine people heard asked, and that nobody on this bank has asked me about it since.*”

“*I said I would wait to be asked and I am still waiting.*”

About four people on that bank had the two days, and about nine of them had a man of fifty-four who was still waiting to be asked.

Then a man of forty-one said one thing about Wednesday. He said it flat, and nobody asked him a question about it and nobody improved on it.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine for nineteen years and has a fortnight and a Wednesday and no day, and told nine people about it in the dark a week ago. He has not moved it.

“*I have not moved my day. I am not going to do it on a Wednesday and I am not going to do it on a Thursday because a Wednesday is easier.*”

“*And that bank is going to have about nine people on it on Wednesday morning, because there are nine lines on that counter and they get read out loud every morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be the reason a bank has nine fewer people on it.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is going to be asked a question about that by me, and nobody is going to say anything about it to me.*”

“*And if any of you want to say something to me about it, then I will not have asked you and I will not have heard it.*”

About four people on that bank had the Wednesday, and about nine of them had a man of forty-one who had told them not to say anything to him about it.

Then a man of thirty-one said he would be standing on that bank on Wednesday. He said it before anybody had finished being quiet, and a woman of forty-four took it off him in six sentences and gave the reason in the last one.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-six days. A hundred and sixty-six is seven hundred and twenty less five hundred and fifty-four, and he has said the shape of his own answer out loud once this fortnight and was told it is a rule and not an answer.

“*I will be standing on that bank on Wednesday morning. Not to say anything. I will be standing on it because there is a man of thirty-eight who has been standing on it every morning for ten mornings, and nine or ten of them can be sent for about him if he is not—*”

“*No. Six sentences, and the sixth one is the reason, and I am going to say it flat.*”

“*One: he is nobody’s, and he has refused a figure for how long three times, and he has asked for a fourth time about a week from now.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one does not get to stand next to that and make it a thing with two people in it.*”

“*Two: about nine people can be sent for about about nine things. That is a man of fifty-four’s sentence from the Friday of a fortnight and it has not stopped being true because a name went on a piece of paper.*”

“*Three: you have been in this county a hundred and sixty-six days and nobody has asked you for anything in one of them, and you have just offered to be a person standing in a place on a morning that is not about you.*”

“*Four: that is the same shape as a rule. Everybody standing here heard the last one of those on the Saturday of a fortnight, and a woman of twenty-eight told him what it was in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*Five: I am a woman of forty-four who keeps a book of twenty-nine chairs, and I am not going to be the second person this week to tell a man of thirty-one that the thing he has just built is a thing he has built.*”

“*Six: and he has not been asked again. Nothing has asked him anything since the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-second week.*”

“*And if you stand on that bank on Wednesday you will be standing in front of about nine people waiting for something to happen to you, and everybody standing on that bank will be able to tell.*”

Six sentences, and about nine of them on that bank had been waiting a week for somebody to say the last one out loud.

Then a man of thirty-one gave the true of it, and said the standing question had not been asked again and was not going to be answered. Nobody answered it for him.

“*She is right about five of them, and the fifth one is the one I would have argued with, and I am not going to argue with it.*”

“*A hundred and sixty-six is seven hundred and twenty less five hundred and fifty-four and I have been in this county a hundred and sixty-six days and nothing has asked me anything since the Wednesday of a fortnight ago.*”

“*And I am not going to arrange to be asked, and I am not going to say the shape of my answer a second time.*”

“*I am not going to say it a second time because a woman of twenty-eight told me on the Saturday of a fortnight that a rule is a thing that works without a person, and I have had one of those out of my mouth already this fortnight.*”

“*And nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*Any of you can stand on that bank on Wednesday and say that nothing was said about it. That is the same sentence you have all been saying for a fortnight, and I am not going to improve on it.*”

The standing question went across that bank one more time and nothing came of it. A man of thirty-one said he was not going to arrange to be asked, and about nine of them had heard him say it.

Then a boy of thirteen gave two figures nobody had asked him for. He gave the working for both, said he was keeping them, and a man of thirty-eight told him to keep them again.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. His mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about, and gave a man of thirty-eight a figure of nine days ten days ago, and was told to keep it.

“*Nineteen days. Nineteen is seven hundred and twenty less seven hundred and one, and I counted it myself on the road coming down and nobody asked me, and I am keeping it.*”

“*And there have been ten readings. Ten is seven hundred and twenty less seven hundred and ten, and that is the tenth including this morning. There was not one on the Wednesday.*”

“*And I am not going to hand either of them to a man of thirty-eight like a present, and I am not going to be thanked for counting them.*”

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it, and nobody carried it anywhere. About nine people stood on that bank in the wind, and about four of them had a day that was not in a book and not on a sheet and not in anybody’s keeping.

---

The fever was sixty-two weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-six days.

A man of sixty-one said out loud that a day about nine people have heard read out is not a day in a book and not a day on that sheet, and that there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a day. He refused to write any of the nine sentences down.

And a woman of sixty-four said somebody is going to write another name on that sheet in about four days, and nobody is going to be sent for about it, and that she is not going to be thanked for saying it a week early on purpose.

And a man of forty-one said he has not moved his day, and is not going to do it on a Wednesday, and is not going to do it on a Thursday because a Wednesday is easier. He said that bank is going to have about nine people on it on Wednesday morning, and that nobody on it is going to say anything to him about it.

And a man of thirty-one said he would be standing on that bank on Wednesday and was taken off in six sentences. He said nothing has asked him anything since the Wednesday of a fortnight ago, and he is not going to arrange to be asked.
