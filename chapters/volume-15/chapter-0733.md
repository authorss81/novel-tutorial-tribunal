# Chapter 733: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Week, And A Monday, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says Out Loud She Has No Figure For The Fourteenth Name, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Two Things Are The Same Shape And Is Refused In Six Sentences

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. His four hundred and eighty-third morning. Three hundred and thirty-three days after the settlement. The fever sixty-four weeks and two days. Two hundred and eighty-seven days since the division. Two hundred and thirty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was at about the seventh hour and was not full. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. He also gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, before anybody had asked him for it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has stood on that landing every morning for nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped saying it, and he has given two figures for a night since. Neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About twelve inches over the top step, at the seventh hour.*”

“*Three days. It was about eight at this hour yesterday, and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*A full bank is about sixteen, and a full bank stays full until it comes off, and that is the whole of the working.*”

“*It comes to sixteen tomorrow morning and not this morning, and I am not going to say it before it is.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning.*”

“*That is the Saturday of next week.*”

“*And on the last morning of a week I am not going to give a different figure, and nobody is going to thank me for the arithmetic.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twelfth morning, and he did not vary the asking by one word.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has still not answered the fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight, and not one of the nine of you has asked him about it.

“*Twelve mornings.*”

“*And I asked to be in a room yesterday and a man of thirty-eight put it in his own head, and I am not going to ask him for it again.*”

“*I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me and I am still waiting.*”

“*I would like about four of you to be able to tell in four days that I said that.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for the twelve and nobody is going to be told I was brave about not answering.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read thirteen names out loud in the open, in the order they were written, and the line at the top of that sheet, and it took about nine minutes.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twenty-three times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He has decided every morning since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week not to say a name out loud.

“*Twenty-three is seven hundred and thirty-three less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the twenty-three in the dark.*”

“*I read the line at the top as well. The nine of you have heard it twenty-three times.*”

“*Four of you heard it twice on the Tuesday of this week, before anybody stopped me, and about four of you have not worked out why that is a different Tuesday from the one I was refused a figure on.*”

“*And there is nothing else on that thing I have not read out loud twenty-three times, and I am not going to say whether that is a comfort.*”

“*The two names in ink are on the ninth line and the seventh line. The ninth has no day and the seventh has one.*”

“*I am not going to give anybody a total of any of it.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has been read that ninth line twelve mornings running and there is nothing on that line to come on.*”

Four of the people standing there had a man of thirty-eight who had read twenty-three mornings. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had been read a line twelve mornings running that had nothing on it to come on.

Then a woman of sixty-four said what she said on the Friday of this week, out loud, and then said that this morning she has nothing. She gave the working for the one she got right. She said she had not worked out how she knew it then.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years. She got up out of the book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and there has been no line for her in it since. She has a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper with no day against it.

“*On the Friday of this week I gave the nine of you standing there a figure of four days for a name going on that thing, and I gave it out loud and in the open.*”

“*Four is seven hundred and twenty-four less seven hundred and twenty, and it was the Saturday of that week, and it was right.*”

“*And nobody was ever going to be sent for about it, and I said that then and I am saying it again this morning about one I did not see coming.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for a figure I gave ten days ago.*”

“*I have not got a figure for this one. I have not got a figure for the day of the week, and I am not going to make one at seven in the morning to a woman of forty.*”

“*And I did not know how I knew the first time, and I am not going to pretend this morning to a woman of forty that I know anything at all.*”

Then a woman of forty said what the number of names on that sheet now is, and gave the working, and refused to give anybody a total.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. On the Thursday of this week she said a mirror is a thing that shows you a thing and is not the thing, and she gave three spaces and one width. She has still not said which three of the five have a day.

“*Fourteen names on nine lines, and two of the fourteen are in ink and not in the print.*”

“*Three of the fourteen have no day against them, and the one that went on this morning is the second of the two that are in ink, and it is on a line that has a day against it.*”

“*And the first of the two in ink is a woman of sixty-four’s and it is on the ninth line, and the ninth line has never had a day.*”

“*A day is a place a man sat in a chair. That is what a woman of forty-four said through a door four miles up that cart road in about nine words on the first day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week.*”

“*This is the second time anything on that counter has made anybody findable, and nobody in this county arranged either of the two.*”

“*And I am not going to add the nine days to the five I worked out on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, because the five is about people and the nine is about a sheet.*”

“*And I am not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and I said on the Monday I would not, and I have not started.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said what the second one had cost her, and what this one did not. She said it was hers and not his, and nobody was going to be thanked for it.

“*The twelfth went on a line with nothing against it and it cost nobody anything at all in six days.*”

“*And the thirteenth went on a line with a day against it, and a day is a place, and that was the first time anything on that counter made anybody findable.*”

“*This one has gone on a line with a day against it as well, and nine is seven hundred and thirty-three less seven hundred and twenty-four, and it took nine days from the last one.*”

“*And I got the last one four days early and I have got nothing for this one, and both of those are mine and not anybody’s.*”

“*Being a name nobody can find by writing a name down took nineteen years, and it cost me one thing a day.*”

“*Being a name on a piece of printed paper has taken six days the first time, four days the second and nine days the third.*”

“*It has cost nobody anything at all any of the three times.*”

“*A mirror does not change that and I am not going to be told it was somebody being kind.*”

“*Nobody on this bank is going to thank the man of thirty-eight for finding either of them.*”

“*I am not going to thank him myself, and he is not going to be thanked for the twenty-three mornings.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that the thing that is not a person and the thing that prints are the same shape. He said it in front of about nine people, before anybody had asked him anything. A man of sixty-one refused him in six sentences and gave the reason in the last one.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-nine days. A hundred and seventy-nine is seven hundred and thirty-three less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*A thing that is not a person asked a man of fifty-four nine words on a Sunday a fortnight ago, and something printed nine lines four hundred miles off with nine words on the ninth of them.*”

“*Nobody else in four counties can do either of those things, so they are the same shape.*”

“*And that is the whole of it.*”

“*Six sentences and the sixth one is the reason and it is not mine, it is a fact.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written one number in it in eleven years. Five lines are on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since.

“*One: nobody in this county can be sent for about who wrote it, and I gave that sentence out loud seventeen days ago and I have not found a shorter way of saying it.*”

“*Two: cannot be sent for about is not the same as did not, and you have put the two in one sentence because one of them is easier.*”

“*Three: a woman of sixty-four said on the Tuesday of this week that a copy leaves out what could not be carried, and she worked that out herself, and nobody handed it to her.*”

“*Four: and I said on the Tuesday of this week that I have not worked out whether the parts left out could not be carried or were left out on purpose, and I am sixty-one and I am not going to.*”

“*Five: a man of thirty-eight has read that line at the top out loud twenty-three mornings, and nobody heard what it said until he stopped in the middle of it.*”

“*That is not the same shape as a man of fifty-four being asked a question at about the ninth hour on a bank in the open.*”

“*You have joined them up because both of them are about nine words.*”

“*Six: and I am not going to tell you whether they are the same thing, and you are not going to be told either, and that is the whole of what I have.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of the fifth sentence in his own mouth, and gave the working, and said he would not be told which half of it was his.

“*He is right about five of those six and I am not going to argue with the sixth.*”

“*A hundred and seventy-nine is seven hundred and thirty-three less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and seventy-nine days.*”

“*And I said yesterday, in a passage, that I want to be sent for about.*”

“*About four of you heard it and every one of you has been carrying it since.*”

“*That is a want and it is not a reason.*”

“*Joining two things up because they are the same size is the only shape I have ever been any good at.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of it and I am not going to be thanked for saying the true of it, and I would rather nothing at all was said in four days.*”

Then the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it. Nobody carried it anywhere. About nine people stood on that bank in the wind, and a man of fifty-four had not asked for the room again, and nobody was going to say a word to him about it.

---

The fever was sixty-four weeks and two days old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-nine days.

There are fourteen names on nine lines and two of them are in ink, and one of the two is a woman of sixty-four’s and it is on the ninth line, which has never had a day against it, and the other went on on the last morning of this week, on a line that has one. Three of the fourteen have no day and a woman of forty has said three out loud and refused to add nine days to five people.

And a woman of sixty-four said out loud that she got the name before last four days early, with the working, and that she has no figure at all for the one that went on this morning, and that nobody is going to be sent for about any of it, and nobody is going to thank her for the four days.
