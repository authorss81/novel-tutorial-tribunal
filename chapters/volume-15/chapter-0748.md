# Chapter 748: First Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Week, And A Tuesday, And The Water Is About Eight Inches Over The Top Step, And About Nine Houses In A Village Four Miles Up A Cart Road Are Doing Something Without Being Asked To By Anybody

First day of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. His four hundred and ninety-eighth morning. Three hundred and forty-eight days after the settlement. The fever sixty-six weeks and three days. Three hundred and two days since the division. Two hundred and forty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour and it was the second morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading, gave the working, and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and about ninety steps of the work are under the bank he stands on. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the second morning of it.*”

“*Two days. It was about four at this hour yesterday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in six days from this morning, and that is the Monday of this week, and I said that on Friday of last week.*”

“*I am the only one of the nine of you who can be checked against yesterday and I said on Monday morning that I was not going to say why.*”

“*I am not going to say why this morning either.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened. It was the twenty-seventh morning.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He asked to be in that room once and was given nothing; he asked again once, and did not ask again. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-seven mornings.*”

“*I have been asked by nine people in this county for about four things.*”

“*There is no rule that says I have to be and there is no rule that says I have not to be, and both of those have been true all week.*”

“*And I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me about it and I am still waiting.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty-seven and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and read the twelve words at the top of the third column. Then he read a fourth thing off that sheet, which is the line at the top of it, and nobody had asked him to.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-eight times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Thirty-eight is seven hundred and forty-eight less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-eight in the dark.*”

“*And the sheet has been on that counter four mornings with nothing in any of the three columns and no column for a person stopping.*”

“*I am going to read the line at the top out loud because about four of you have stopped looking at the top of that sheet since I turned it over on Monday of last week.*”

“*It is still there and it is the same nine words it was.*”

Then he read it, and it took about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and nobody said a word.

“*It says the form is set out from a form carried over from Veyra and printed four hundred miles off, and to be read out where anybody can hear it and not to be kept.*”

“*And to be given to one person in this county and not to a man who came up a plank.*”

“*I have read that about nine times in a fortnight now and about four of you have stopped hearing it and that is a figure of about four people and not a figure of the sheet.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for thirty-eight and nobody is going to be told I was brave about reading a line at the top of a piece of paper.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said what about nine houses four miles up a cart road are doing. She gave the count first, and said nobody was going to be thanked for it, and said she had not been asked to do it by anybody in this county.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has kept a bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses in that village take her bread. She has been asked four times and answered twice and has not answered the fourth thing. She came down four miles on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and again on the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week.

“*Nine houses, and I am giving the count first because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*And because about four of you are going to make the other half into a bigger number than it is.*”

“*And about nine of them took bread from me this morning and about four of them did not and I am not going to say which four and I am not going to make a total of the two.*”

“*Here is what nine houses have been doing since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. Nobody in this county asked them to and I did not ask them to.*”

“*One of them has stopped sending the second of its two carts down that road in the morning. That is a woman of forty-one with two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. Her boy came down this road on Saturday and was told not to say a figure again that week.*”

“*Another has put its own name on its own paper with a day against it, in its own hand.*”

“*About four of you are going to tell me that is not the same as the thing on this counter. It is not, and I am not going to argue with you about it on a bank.*”

“*And a third of them has asked its own wife a question in its own doorway and written down what she said and has not put it in front of anybody.*”

“*That is nine houses and I have given you three of them and I am not going to give the other six.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for any of the three and nobody is going to be told I was brave about walking four miles twice in four days.*”

About four of them on that bank had a woman of forty-four who had given three things out of nine and had refused to give the other six. About nine of them had about nine houses four miles off doing three things nobody in this county had asked them to do.

Then a woman of forty said what a covenant is and what it is not, and she gave the working first, and she did not refuse anything this morning.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them, and she said on a Monday of a fortnight that she would not and has not started.

“*Three places, and I am going to say what I am standing in front of first, because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*Three: a woman of forty-four’s nine houses, a man of thirty-eight reading a line at the top out loud for about nine times in a fortnight, and a man of fifty-four asking for a line twenty-seven mornings in the open.*”

“*And I am not going to add those three together.*”

“*One of them is about nine people in a village I have never walked to and two of them are about this bank, and about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for me to make one figure of a morning and a morning and nine houses.*”

“*A covenant is two people who have both said a thing out loud and can both be asked about it.*”

“*That is the whole of what I have got and it is not much, and it took me about nineteen years.*”

“*I worked it out on the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week at about the sixth hour before the light.*”

“*A man of fifty-four has asked the same question every morning for twenty-seven mornings and nobody has asked him a question back.*”

“*That is not a covenant and I am not going to call it one, because it is the nearest thing to one I have got.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the three sentences and nobody is going to be told I am right about a covenant.*”

“*And I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it in his own mouth and gave the working and said nobody was going to be told which half of it was his.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-four days. A hundred and ninety-four is seven hundred and forty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. On the first day of this week he said he wanted that sheet to still be on that counter in four weeks with nothing in any of the three columns.

“*A hundred and ninety-four is seven hundred and forty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety-four days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*I am not going to say I am right about the three sentences. I have been wrong about about four things in a fortnight and I have worked out three of them and I said on Thursday of last week that I was not going to work out the fourth.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has been walking four miles twice in four days about nine houses doing things nobody asked her to do. I have not been asked to do any of them, and that is correct.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week that a seventh is a rule. I offered seven times in about three weeks and was refused seven times out loud and I said on the Friday of that week that I am not going to offer again.*”

“*Here is the want and I am going to give it before that, because I have been giving the shape first for about a week and the want second and about four of you have not worked out that I have been doing it in the same order as a woman of forty-four and on purpose.*”

“*I want to know what the third column is for. That is the whole of it. Nine mornings of not asking and I am going to ask it in about four days and not before.*”

“*And here is the mistake, which is that I have spent a week saying *not this week* in four different directions and not one of them was a want, and about four of you have worked out that and about nine of you have not.*”

“*I am not going to offer to go up that cart road this morning. I am going to stand on this bank at about four feet off a man of thirty-eight’s left hand and I am not going to offer anything.*”

“*And the cost is that a woman of forty-four has now said the same thing about nine houses that I have been saying about one counter, and she is four miles nearer to nine people than I am, and nobody is going to thank her for walking it and nobody is going to thank me for not walking it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about that and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting nine days for me to be the one who goes up that road and I am not going to be that today.*”

“*And none of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday forty-eight days ago.*”

“*Forty-eight is seven hundred and forty-eight less seven hundred. I am not going to let it be one and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind. About four of them had a man of thirty-one who had said in about nine people’s hearing that he was not going to offer to go up four miles of a cart road.

Then a boy of thirteen gave four counts back and said he was going to carry five, and he carried five.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping five counts since Monday of this week.

“*Forty-seven days. Forty-seven is seven hundred and forty-eight less seven hundred and one.*”

“*And twenty-seven mornings, and a hundred and eighteen days for a man in an ash, a hundred and nineteen for a woman in a reed, and four mornings of a sheet on a counter with nothing in it.*”

“*That is the five and I said yesterday I was going to carry five and I have.*”

“*And my mother has stopped sending the second cart down that road in the morning.*”

“*I did not tell anybody on this bank that until just now and a woman of forty-four already knew it. About four of you have worked out what that means and about nine of you have not.*”

“*I am not going to thank a woman of forty-four for saying it out loud first. I am going to carry it.*”

The fever was sixty-six weeks and three days old. He had been in that county a hundred and ninety-four days.

About nine houses in a village four miles up that cart road have been doing three things nobody in that county asked them to do.

A woman of forty-one with two carts has stopped sending the second of them down that road in the morning. One of the nine has put its own name on its own paper with a day against it in its own hand. And a third has asked its own wife a question in its own doorway and written down what she said, and has not put it in front of anybody.

And a woman of forty said that a covenant is two people who have both said a thing out loud and can both be asked about it, and that a man of fifty-four asking the same question every morning for twenty-seven mornings while nobody asks him one back is not that. She refused to make one figure of a morning and a morning and nine houses.
