# Chapter 725: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Sunday, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Walks Four Miles Down A Cart Road To Say She Has Worked Out A Thing She Told Nine People She Would Rather Not Know, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Decides Again And Says Nothing

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventy-fifth morning. Three hundred and twenty-five days after the settlement. The fever sixty-three weeks and one day. Two hundred and seventy-nine days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working.

He gave the other half of it as well, because he has given both halves every morning this fortnight.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gave two figures for a night, one of them on the Saturday of a fortnight ago, and he says neither of those two figures is his, and he has not given one since.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Three days.*”

“*It was about eight at this hour yesterday, and that is the working.*”

“*And tomorrow is four days, and a bank that is full stays full until it comes off.*”

“*And I am not going to say what four days is yet, because I have not worked it out in front of about nine people since the first day of the hundred and twenty-second week.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, as he has asked every morning since the Thursday of this week.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He asked for the ninth line on the Thursday of this week and said he would go on asking while there is a reading on that counter.

“*Four mornings now and I have not varied the asking by one word.*”

“*About four of you noticed that on the fourth and the rest of you did not, and that is the correct way round.*”

“*The ninth line has not changed in five mornings. Neither has the seventh line, and there are thirteen names on it now.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight told me on Friday that a name went on a line with a day against it.*”

“*I have thought about that for about two days and I am not going to say what I thought.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read thirteen names out loud in the open on that bank for the fifteenth morning reading, and it took about nine minutes, and he said the other thing before he started.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and has read that sheet fifteen times in this county, and has been refused a figure for how long three times and has asked for a fourth.

“*Before I start. I decided again this morning.*”

“*I decided it at the fifth hour at the head of my own gate, and I decided it again at the seventh hour on this bank.*”

“*And both times the answer was the same as the Monday of last week.*”

“*Nine of you know what that is. Four of you have known since the Monday and the rest of you worked it out this week.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for either.*”

“*And I am not going to say it, and nobody is going to be relieved about it, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about not saying it.*”

“*Thirteen names. About nine minutes. Fifteen is seven hundred and twenty-five less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And three of the thirteen have no day against them, and one of the three is on a line that has a day against a different name, and I am not going to improve on that either.*”

Then about nine people walked four miles down that cart road to a bakehouse and back, and the woman who keeps it was already at the bottom of it when they got there.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has kept that bakehouse nineteen years, and about nine houses in that village take her bread. She has been asked three times and has answered twice, and on the Monday of last week she said in nine people’s hearing that she would rather not know.

“*I have worked it out. That is all I came down four miles to say and I said it before I got to the bottom of the road.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight stood at my own door on Friday morning and read me that ninth line out loud, and there are nine words on it, and about four of them are about my own two hands.*”

“*And they are the same nine words. Both of them used to me through a door four miles on, on the Friday of a fortnight and then on the Wednesday of the week after, in the same nine words.*”

“*So one of those men was given them off that line, or both of them were, and there was never anybody in this county standing in a room going to say nine words.*”

Then she said what it had cost and she did not hand it to anybody, and she said the thing she had said on the Monday and meant.

“*I said on the Monday of last week that I would rather not know.*”

“*I have said it in front of nine people and I meant it, and I am saying it again now that I know.*”

“*I have known for two days. Two is seven hundred and twenty-five less seven hundred and twenty-three.*”

“*The man of thirty-eight counted it and I counted it and we were not in the same room when we counted it.*”

“*And I cannot give it back. There is nobody in four counties I can hand a thing I have worked out to, and I have tried about nine ways of doing that since Friday and I am not going to say what they were.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for four miles, and nobody is going to be told it was worth the walk, and I am not going to stand on a bank and let anybody say that.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that she was the only safe place in four counties to put it, and said it out loud, in front of about nine people, before anybody had asked him anything.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and seventy-one days. A hundred and seventy-one is seven hundred and twenty-five less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*You are not one of the nine. You cannot be sent for about from this county or from the one you came from.*”

“*And you have the nine words in your own head now and there is nobody in four counties who can be sent for about you to take them out of it.*”

“*So you are the only place left to put a thing, and I am saying that on a bank in about nine people’s hearing, and I am not going to improve on why I am saying it.*”

Then she said what he had done, in three sentences, and she did not raise her voice, and she said it flat.

“*You have handed me a thing again.*”

“*You have done it three times in about nine days and a woman of twenty-eight told you the third time what it was, and you said the true of it, and here is the fourth.*”

“*I have not been thanked for a fortnight and I am not going to be thanked now and I am not going to be told that I am the safe place for anything.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the other half of it, in four sentences, and it was not about the woman of forty-four at all.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and nineteen years of it have been a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. She is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and she has said so on that bank about nine times.

“*She came down four miles to say a thing she told nine people she would rather not know, and a man of thirty-one has turned that into a post.*”

“*And the post is safe, and that is the whole of the trouble with it.*”

“*I have been a safe place for about nine people’s sentences for nineteen years.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about either of us, and that is not a qualification and it is not a use.*”

“*And I am not going to say what a safe place is, and I am not going to be thanked for saying that a man of thirty-one has made one.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave two figures nobody had asked him for, gave the working for both, and said he was keeping them, and a man of thirty-eight told him to keep them again.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has been keeping two counts since the Saturday of a fortnight and has not handed either of them over.

“*Twenty-four days. Twenty-four is seven hundred and twenty-five less seven hundred and one, and I counted it on the road coming down and nobody asked me.*”

“*And fifteen readings. Fifteen is seven hundred and twenty-five less seven hundred and ten, and there was not one of them in the dark.*”

“*And I am not going to hand either of them to a man of thirty-eight like a present, and I am not going to be thanked for counting them.*”

Then a man of forty-one said one sentence, and he said it flat, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine for nineteen years. He has a fortnight and one morning and has not named it.

“*I have said I would not name the day on a bank and I have not named it, and about four of you have worked out that a bank with nine people standing on it is the same as the open air.*”

“*There is one morning left and I know what I am going to do with it, and I am not going to say that out loud, and nobody on this bank is going to ask me.*”

Nine of them on that bank had a woman of forty-four who had come down four miles to give away a thing she could not put down anywhere. Four of them had a man of thirty-one who had found out in about four seconds what she had come down to say.

Then they went back up the cart road in the wind, and the woman of forty-four went back up it ahead of them, and nobody walked beside her, and the sheet stayed on that counter.


---

The fever was sixty-three weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and seventy-one days.

A woman of forty-four walked four miles down a cart road to say that the nine words on the ninth line of a printed sheet are the same nine words two men in two coats used through her own door.

She said one of them was given them off that line, and that there was never anybody in this county standing in a room going to say nine words.

And she said she had known for two days and cannot give it back to anybody, and that she said on the Monday of last week that she would rather not know, and meant it, and is saying it again now that she knows.
