# Chapter 722: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Thursday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps In The Night For The Seventh Time, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Puts Two Sets Of Nine Words In One Room Before The Light And A Man Of Fifty-Four Says He Can Only Check One Of Them Against A Man

Third day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventy-second morning. Three hundred and twenty-two days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and five days. Two hundred and seventy-six days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps at the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working.

He had been on that landing at the second hour, because there is nothing else to do at the second hour.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and about ninety steps of the work are under the bank he was standing on.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off in the night.*”

“*It was full on the Sunday of last week and four days is the working, and that is the seventh time it has come off in the night since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you how many days that is, because there is no figure for a river and I have given you about nine numbers this week.*”

About four of them had the seven times and the rest of them were going down.

They went down at about the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light. Nine of them went down about ninety steps and then about a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage. The man of forty-four bailed for about an hour and said nothing about it, and the man of thirty-eight went first with the lamp that is his.

Then a man of about fifty-two said the nine words, and he said them twice, and the second time was not the same as the first time.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal in this county. He has been that person eleven years without knowing it was a thing to be, and he refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences a fortnight ago and gave the reason in the ninth.

“*One: I am going to say this and then I am going to say it again, and it is not going to be the same twice.*”

“*And if you cannot tell me what the difference is, then we have both had a morning and neither of us has had an answer.*”

“*Two: you are the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about four things.*”

“*And four of you worked out in the last week that you are about to be a fifth, and I am not going to pretend I worked it out this morning.*”

“*Three: a man of fifty-four who cannot read cannot refuse a thing that is not a person. He can only be spoken for.*”

“*And nine of us have said that out loud and one of us said it in nine sentences on Sunday.*”

“*Four: and I am going to say the words anyway, twice, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and if you answer me I am not going to be told you were right.*”

“*Five: and if you do not answer me I am not going to say that you refused me either, and that is not mine to give and not yours to take.*”

“*Six: I cannot see well. I have said so out loud on this bank about nine times and nobody has written it down and I am not going to start this morning.*”

“*Seven: and the man of thirty-four in that ash has not been asked anything in more than ninety days and there is a great many of us who would take his ninety days over either answer you give me.*”

“*Eight: I am the fifth line of a sheet of deal and I have never once read it.*”

“*And I am not going to pretend I am a better instrument for asking than a woman of forty-four who has asked three times and answered twice.*”

“*Nine: here is the reason and it is the only reason.*”

“*You said on Sunday that you cannot tell whether the nine words you have been given are the nine words that are on the thing.*”

“*So I am going to put the two of them in one room at the fifth hour before the light in nine people’s hearing, and that is the whole of what a man who cannot read can be given, and it is not nothing.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud off the sheet in his own lamp, and read it a second time, and then said out loud that the two things were not the same.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark, and has been asked for a figure for how long four times now.

“*Nine words, and they are on the ninth line in the print, and about four of them are about two hands and about nine feet along a groove.*”

“*And there are nine lines on that sheet, and the ninth one has got no day against either name on it, and the days are on the other eight.*”

“*And the words I read off it were the words to come on the days it is written on.*”

“*So a man of fifty-four has been asked about the ninth line, and the ninth line has not got a day on it, and there is nothing on that line to come on.*”

“*That is the fourth of the four reasons and it is the only one of the four that is about the thing that asked him rather than about the two of us.*”

“*And I have read that sheet out loud eleven mornings in the open, and I have read it twice more down here.*”

“*There is not one person in this county who has ever asked me for a line with no day on it. Not one in nineteen years of asking.*”

“*And I am not going to work out at the fifth hour whether that is because nobody wanted to know.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for noticing it and I am not going to be the man who says it means anything.*”

That sat in that passage for about as long as it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading. Nobody said a word about it and nine of them had a ninth line that had never once been read out to anybody who could use it.

Then a boy of thirteen gave a count that nobody had asked him for, and gave the working, and said he was keeping it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. His mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about, and he has been keeping two counts since the Saturday of last week and neither of them has been asked for.

“*Four. There are four people in this passage who cannot see well and four things a man of fifty-four can do that he has never once said he would do.*”

“*Four is not five and five is not nine, and I am not going to add them together and I am not going to be thanked for counting.*”

“*Four is four and I counted it and nobody asked me, and I am keeping it the way I keep the other two.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said what he had done, and he cannot read, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper or of either board that has been on it. He has been the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about four things and nobody has sent him, and he was asked a fifth thing on the Sunday of last week and has not answered it.

“*I heard the two versions. They are not the same and I am not going to tell you which one is the one it said to me.*”

“*Here is what I can do and it took me about nine seconds and I do not read, so I have not had a book to do it out of.*”

“*A man who cannot read cannot check a thing against the paper. He can check a thing against another man saying it out loud, and that is all he has got, and that is not nothing and it is not a refusal either.*”

“*And I am going to ask for it every morning from now on while there is a reading on that counter, and I am saying that out loud now, in this passage, before the light, in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for asking for it and I am not going to be told I was brave about having not answered a question on Sunday.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the other half of it, and she said it flat, and she said she had been keeping it since the Sunday.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and nineteen years of it have been twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year. There are nine of those chairs and not ten, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about.

“*Four of them can hear the difference between two sets of nine words and five of them cannot, and one of the five is you, and I am not going to say that makes him better at it.*”

“*And you have just asked a man who cannot read to lean on a man of thirty-eight the way you have leaned on him for eleven mornings.*”

“*I do not think you meant to do that and I am not going to let it pass without saying it.*”

“*And nobody in this passage is going to say that is the right of refusing, and nobody in this passage is going to say that it is not.*”

“*I have been waiting six weeks for somebody to say either and I am going to go on waiting.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight put two boards either side of a sheet on Saturday and asked nine people to notice if they were about to join them up.*”

“*The three things on that counter are three because nobody joined them up, and that is a thing I would like nine people to notice on a morning when nothing is happening.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud what a man of fifty-four was doing, before anybody had asked him anything, and he said it in front of about nine people.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-eight days. A hundred and sixty-eight is seven hundred and twenty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. He has offered to be the one who asks five times in about three weeks and been refused five times out loud, and a sixth on Sunday was refused in nine sentences, and nobody has told him to stop.

“*He is not refusing because he cannot read. He is refusing because nobody can be sent for about him to tell him when to stop, and he has worked that out and I have not.*”

“*And that is the shape of it and I am saying it out loud before anybody has asked me, which is what I do, and about four of you have been listening to me do it for a hundred and sixty-eight days.*”

Then a man of fifty-four took the whole of it off him in nine sentences, and he cannot read, and the ninth one was the cost.

“*No. Nine sentences and the ninth one is the cost and it is mine.*”

“*One: the four things I am sent for about are gravel, and a drain, and a cart road, and a man’s right to be asked whether he is going to move it.*”

“*Two: none of the four is about a room that is a hundred and thirty feet under a bank, and there is no shape that joins them and I have been looking for one for about six weeks.*”

“*Three: you have said a shape out loud about a man in the dark, and you said it before anybody had asked you.*”

“*That is the same thing you did on Friday, when you said four words about a woman of sixty-four without looking at a piece of paper.*”

“*Four: I am not going to say you are wrong about it. I do not know whether you are right and I have not got the two days it would take me to find out.*”

“*Five: I have said for about six weeks that nobody has sent me and nobody has asked me whether I would move it, and I have not moved it.*”

“*Six: nobody has asked me whether I would do a fifth thing.*”

“*A thing that is not a person asked me on Sunday at about the ninth hour on a bank in the open, and you have just made that into a thing about me.*”

“*Seven: there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a man who is refusing, and there is no form anywhere that says a person may not.*”

“*Eight: there is one of us in this passage who cannot read, and four of us who cannot see well, and a man of thirty-one who is nobody’s.*”

“*Not one of the three of us can be sent for about any of it.*”

“*Nine: this morning a man of thirty-one has said out loud in nine people’s hearing that I am refusing, before I was asked anything.*”

“*I cannot take it back, and nine people now have a word for me that they did not have on Sunday.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said the thing about the word, in four sentences, and did not raise her voice.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She has said the same sentence nine times without saying it, and she said a fortnight ago that she was not going to begin again, and she has not begun.

“*You have a word for him now.*”

“*He had a name, and nine of you had a name, and not one of you could be sent for about him.*”

“*And now nine of you have a word for him, and there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a word either.*”

“*And I am not going to be the one who says that is the same as a name.*”

“*I have said that a thing eleven times and not said it once, and I am not going to begin again to say it about this.*”

“*I am going to say what it is.*”

“*Nine people in a passage have just agreed on a description of a man, and that is the first time in three weeks that anybody in this county has agreed on anything.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for noticing it and I am not going to say it was the ninth sentence and it was not.*”

The whole of that passage had a man of thirty-one who had cost a man of fifty-four a word in the dark. Four of them had a woman of twenty-eight who had said it in four sentences and would not say it was the ninth.

Then two people put their hands on a stone about as big as a door and pushed it nine feet along a groove and pushed it back. It took about a minute, and then the light went out, and the man of forty-four bailed for about an hour and said nothing about it.

And a man of forty-one who keeps two carts came up out of that passage at about the seventh hour and stood on the bank in the wind, and he did not say anything at all about a morning.

The fever was sixty-two weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-eight days.

A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well said the nine words twice in that passage and the two versions were not the same.

And a man of thirty-eight read them off the ninth line of a printed sheet and gave four reasons why they were not the same. The fourth of them is that the ninth line has no day against either name on it, and a man of fifty-four has been asked about the ninth line, and there is nothing on that line to come on.

And a man of fifty-four said he can check a thing against another man saying it out loud and not against the paper, and asked for it every morning while there is a reading on that counter.
