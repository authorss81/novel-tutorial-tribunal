# Chapter 743: Third Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Week, And A Thursday, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read Says Out Loud That He Has Been Asked For A Day He Cannot Come On And Has Never Been Asked For One He Can, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Is Not Going To Read That Sheet Again This Week

Third day of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. His four hundred and ninety-third morning. Three hundred and forty-three days after the settlement. The fever sixty-five weeks and five days. Two hundred and ninety-seven days since the division. Two hundred and forty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the three things in the order he gives them, and nobody had asked him for any of them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since, and neither of those two is his. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning, and he has not given one this week.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the fifth day of it, and a full bank holds.*”

“*Five days. It was full on the Wednesday of this week at about the seventh hour, and four inches a day and sixteen is the whole of the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning, and that is the Sunday of this week, and I have given you that same Sunday three mornings running.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for the three things and I am not going to be asked about them until they stop working.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open before anything else happened, and it was the twenty-second morning, and he said it flat and it was four sentences long.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He has asked for the ninth line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not varied the asking by one word. The fifth thing asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight is still not answered and nobody has asked him about it.

“*Twenty-two mornings and I have not varied the asking by one word.*”

“*And I have had a day read to me along with the line for the first time this week and I am not going to be thanked for that either.*”

“*Here is what I have been standing here since Wednesday and I am going to say it in four sentences and I am not going to say a fifth.*”

“*One: I have been asked for a day I cannot come on. I have not been asked for one I can come on.*”

“*Two: I do not know what day it is because I cannot read and I have said I am not going to become a man who gets told.*”

“*Three: about nine of you have been asking me the same question every morning for three weeks and not one of you has asked me whether I could come.*”

“*Four: that is not a complaint and nobody is going to be thanked for the twenty-two and nobody is going to be told I was brave about any part of it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open, and read the fourteen names after it, and read the nine words on the back of it. It took about nine minutes, and the sheet went back on that counter where anybody could put a hand on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet thirty-three times in this county. He has been refused a figure for how long three times, asked for a fourth, and will not ask a fifth. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Thirty-three is seven hundred and forty-three less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the thirty-three in the dark.*”

“*And a day came on that line on Monday morning and I have read it out loud two mornings since.*”

“*Both of those mornings the day was on it. That is two mornings, and I am not going to make it a third kind of figure.*”

“*A man of fifty-four has just said in four sentences that he has been asked for a day he cannot come on and never for one he can.*”

“*About four of you heard all four and about nine of you heard three.*”

“*And the fourth one was the reason and it is his.*”

“*He said it in about nine people’s hearing on a Thursday morning and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to thank him for it.*”

“*The ninth line has two names on it and one day against it.*”

“*I have read that line out loud thirty-three times and I have never once said a name on it, and I am not going to start this week either.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the thirty-three.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said the true of the four sentences in four sentences of her own, and she did not raise her voice, and she is not one of the nine.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She refused a man of thirty-one in nine sentences on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and said a seventh is a rule. She said the same sentence about nine times and has not begun again. The count of the times she has said it and not said it carries no ordinal.

“*Four sentences and the fourth one is mine and it is about what he has just said and not about why.*”

“*One: a man of fifty-four has been asked for a day he cannot come on.*”

“*About nine people on this bank have asked him for it every morning for three weeks and not one of them has asked him whether he could come.*”

“*Two: I have been in the hearing of all four of those sentences. I have been in the hearing of that every morning since Wednesday and I have not said a word about it on any of them.*”

“*Three: I said the same sentence about nine times in about nine days and I am not going to say it about this one, because this one is not about a rule, it is about a chair.*”

“*Four: a day is a place a man sat in a chair. A woman of forty said that. And I have been thinking about the chair for two days and I am not going to say what I have been thinking, and nobody is going to be thanked for four sentences that do not finish.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that he is not going to read that sheet again this week. He said it before anybody had asked him anything, and he gave the working for his own figure first.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and eighty-nine days. A hundred and eighty-nine is seven hundred and forty-three less five hundred and fifty-four. On the first morning of this week he said he wanted the ninth line to have no day on it again, and on the second morning he said he had not got a way of doing it.

“*A hundred and eighty-nine is seven hundred and forty-three less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and eighty-nine days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*I am not going to read that sheet again this week. Not in the open and not in the dark and not at about the ninth hour.*”

“*And I am going to say the shape of it before I say the want, because the want is the easy part and I want about four of you to have the shape first.*”

“*I am a man who reads things to people. That is the whole of what I have got.*”

“*About nine of you said out loud in about nine days that I am the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks. A woman of twenty-eight told me on Wednesday of last week that a second time is a rule.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight has read that sheet out loud thirty-three mornings. I have read it out loud twice in about a fortnight and both of those times I was a man reading a thing I had not been asked to read.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four who cannot read has just said in four sentences that he has been asked for a day he cannot come on.*”

“*If I get up on that bank at about the ninth hour and read that sheet out loud, then about nine people on this bank have a man who reads things to them.*”

“*And such a man keeps working whether he is on that bank or not.*”

“*Here is the want and I am putting it after the shape on purpose, because a woman of forty-four put her reason last on the last morning of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and I have been thinking about that for two days.*”

“*I want to be asked about one thing in this county. Not to be the one who asks. Asked.*”

“*That is a different want from the one I said on Monday morning and about four of you have noticed that and about nine of you have not, and that is the correct way round.*”

“*And the cost of saying it in about nine people’s hearing is that about nine people now know it, and about nine people can say a man of thirty-one said he wants to be asked, and there is nothing anywhere in this county that a man can be sent for about what he wants.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for any of that and nobody is going to tell me I was right to say it on a Thursday morning.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four in that ash has a day against his name and has never been asked about it.*”

“*I have not read his name out loud in about a fortnight. About four of you have noticed that and about nine of you have not, and that is the correct way round.*”

“*And none of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday forty-three days ago.*”

“*Forty-three is seven hundred and forty-three less seven hundred. I am not going to let it be one and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*I am not relieved and I am not going to be thanked, and I would rather it was nothing at all in four days.*”

Then about nine people stood on that bank in the wind and nobody read anything out loud, and a man of fifty-four said it was the correct way round.

About four of them had a man of thirty-eight who had read thirty-three mornings and had not read the back of it until Monday. About nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had said on Wednesday morning that he wanted a day taken off a line, and who has now said he is not going to read the sheet again this week. Nobody on that bank said either of those things was the right thing to do.

Then a man of thirty-one said one more thing before anybody had finished being quiet, and it was about a room, and it was the last thing he said that day.

“*About nine of you are going to keep asking that man of fifty-four for the ninth line for another week, and that is not a rule, it is what has been happening for three weeks.*”

“*And I am not going to stand on this bank and say to any of you that a thing which works whether he is standing here or not is the same as nine people asking a man for a thing.*”

“*It is not the same and I know it is not the same.*”

“*Those are two different shapes and I have been mixing them up for about four days and I am not going to mix them up this week.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for that either and I am not going to be told I am right about it.*”

“*I have been wrong about about four things in four days. I have worked out three of them and I am not going to work out the fourth.*”

---

The fever was sixty-five weeks and five days old. He had been in that county a hundred and eighty-nine days.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read said out loud in four sentences that he has been asked for a day he cannot come on and has never been asked for one he can, and that about nine people have asked him the same thing every morning for three weeks. He refused a fifth sentence.

And a man of thirty-one said he is not going to read that sheet again this week, in the open or in the dark, and gave the shape of it before the want. A man who reads things to people is a man who works whether he is standing on that bank or not.
