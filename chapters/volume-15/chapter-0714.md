# Chapter 714: Second Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which The Water Comes Off All Ninety Of The Steps In The Night For The Sixth Time Since The Saturday Of The Hundred And Nineteenth Week And About Nine People Go Down At The Fifth Hour Because A Keeping Is Done Before The Light, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Gives A Figure For The Light Under The Eighth Door-Stone That Is The Same Figure He Gave Eight Days Ago, And A Man Of Forty-One Says Out Loud In The Dark That He Has Got A Wednesday And No Day

Second day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. His four hundred and sixty-fourth morning. Three hundred and fourteen days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks and four days. Two hundred and sixty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and fourteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was off all ninety of the steps at the sixth hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working. He had been on that landing at the second hour, because there is nothing else to do at the second hour.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and about ninety steps of the work are under the bank he was standing on. A bank that is full stays full until it comes off.

“*Off all ninety at the sixth hour. It came off in the night and it was full on the Saturday of last week, and four days is the working.*”

“*And I gave you that figure three mornings running, and this morning is the morning it is.*”

“*And that is the sixth time it has come off in the night since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*I am not going to tell you how many days that is, because there is no figure for a river and I have given you five numbers this week.*”

The six times went round that landing and nine or ten of them were going down.

They went down at about the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light. About nine people went down about ninety steps at about the fifth hour of a Wednesday, and the water was off them and nothing fell down.

Then a man of thirty-eight put his lamp on the floor of that gallery and gave a figure for the light under the eighth door-stone. It was the same figure he had given eight days ago, and he said so out loud before anybody asked him for it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and went down a hundred and thirty feet of dry passage with his own lamp. He is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark.

“*The light under the eighth door-stone is the width of my own hand at the knuckles.*”

“*That is the figure I gave on the first day of this week, and it is the figure I am giving now. It is the first time in about seven weeks that I have given the same figure twice.*”

“*Eight days. Eight is seven hundred and fourteen less seven hundred and six. And it was a thumb on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-first week, and it was a hand on the first day of this week. It has been a hand for eight days, and it has not gone back to a thumb and it has not got wider.*”

“*I am not going to say what that means and I am not going to say what it does not mean.*”

“*About four of you have already worked out one of those two, and I would rather you worked it out between you than had it from me in the dark.*”

Eight days in that passage. About nine of them were looking at a width that had stopped, and four of them had been carrying a different figure about for about seven weeks.

Then a man of forty-one said the day he has worked out. He said it in the dark about a quarter of an hour before the light, and he said he was telling them and not asking them anything.

The man is forty-one and keeps two carts and is the ninth of nine, and he has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years. He has said he wants to stop in a fortnight and nobody has asked him a question about it.

“*The Wednesday of next week. That is the last day of a fortnight I said out loud in a room like this one seven days ago, and seven is seven hundred and fourteen less seven hundred and seven.*”

“*And I am telling you the day and I am not asking you anything. I would like about four of you to be able to tell in three days that I said that and did not ask.*”

“*And here is the other half of it and I am saying it in the dark before the light because I cannot say it on a bank.*”

“*On that Wednesday I said I would not do it on a Wednesday, and everybody here heard me say that. None of you asked me about it, and I have not been asked about it since.*”

“*So I have got a fortnight and a Wednesday and no day. And I am not going to move it to a Thursday to make it fit, and I am not going to do it on a Wednesday. I am not going to be the man who says he has changed his mind in the dark a hundred and thirty feet under a bank.*”

“*And I worked the day out on the Monday of last week and told nobody. Nine is seven hundred and fourteen less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And I have been carrying that number about for nine days in the shape of a person every time I picked it up.*”

About nine of them were in that passage a hundred and thirty feet under a bank before the light, and every one of them had a man of forty-one telling them a day in the dark.

Then a man of thirty-one offered to be one of the two hands. He said it before the light and he said it out loud, and the man of forty-one refused him in about nine sentences and gave the reason in the ninth.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty days. A hundred and sixty is seven hundred and fourteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and he is standing in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under a bank with his hands empty.

“*I want to be one of the two hands. Tonight and the night after and the night after that. I know it is about a minute, I know it is about nine feet along a groove, and I have watched two men do it and I could do it.*”

“*No. Nine sentences and the ninth one is the reason, and I am saying all nine. I said it out loud in a room like this one a week ago, and nobody asked me a question about it then either.*”

“*One: a keeping that is a day is done by two hands and there is nobody in four counties who can be sent for about whether they will still be doing it.*”

“*Two: a man of thirty-one who is nobody’s has no way of telling anybody he has stopped, and that is the exact thing I have just said out loud in the dark.*”

“*If my hands are on that stone then in a fortnight there are two of us who have done it, and one of us has a reason.*”

“*Three: a hundred and sixty is seven hundred and fourteen less five hundred and fifty-four and nobody has asked you for anything in one hundred and sixty days.*”

“*Four: five times in about three weeks you have offered to be the instrument of somebody else’s work, and four men and a woman have refused you out loud.*”

“*And you have never once looked like a man who had been told no.*”

“*Five: a man about fifty-two who cannot see well said out loud on the fourth morning of last week that you are the best instrument in four counties.*”

“*And that is why nobody is going to let you do it.*”

“*Six: and a woman of twenty-eight has told you twice this fortnight that you keep handing about nine people something to hold.*”

“*Seven: a woman of forty-four took you off in six sentences on Sunday morning for asking to be somebody’s. She did not raise her voice, and about nine of us heard it.*”

“*Eight: I am not saying no because you would do it badly. I have watched you for about a week and you would do it well.*”

“*Nine: I am saying no because on the Wednesday of next week I am going to stop. I do not want the last thing my two hands do to have a man of thirty-one in it that nobody can be sent for about.*”

Nine sentences and the ninth one was about a day and about two hands, and nobody in that passage said a word.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of that ninth sentence in his own mouth, and gave the working. He said out loud that nobody was going to be thanked for him not putting his hands on it.

“*He is right about all nine of them and I am not going to take one of them back.*”

“*A man of forty-one has just told me what my two hands are worth, out loud, in the dark.*”

“*A hundred and sixty is seven hundred and fourteen less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and sixty days.*”

“*Nobody has asked me for anything in one of them, and I have just asked for two minutes of somebody else’s morning in the dark before the light.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for not putting my hands on that stone, and I am not going to be told I was right.*”

“*If any of you are going to be relieved about it in about four days, I would rather it was nothing at all.*”

Nine sentences went into that passage. A man of thirty-one in it had been told what his two hands were worth, and about nine of them had heard the ninth one.

Then two people put their hands on a stone about as big as a door and pushed it nine feet along a groove and pushed it back. It took about a minute, and then the light went out.

And a man of fifty-four put his own hand into the gap under the ninth door-stone before the light went out, and said what was in it. He cannot read, and he did not round it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of anything any of them have found. He is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about four things, and nobody has asked him to be a fifth.

“*An inch and about the thickness of my own finger. Same as it was on the first day of this week, and same as it was on the Thursday before it. That is three mornings and none of them is a different figure.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked about it again this week, and I am not going to be thanked for putting my own hand in a gap in the dark for about nine days running.*”

“*And I have been the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about that water for about six weeks, and nobody has sent me, and nobody has asked me whether I would move it.*”

“*And I have not moved it.*”

“*And I want that said on that bank this morning by a man of fifty-four who cannot read, before the light, in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*Because somebody is going to say a thing about it in about four days, and I am not going to let the first person to say it be a man with a hand and a book.*”

The inch was the only figure in that passage that had not moved. About nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had said he would wait to be asked about a thing.

Then they went up, and the man of thirty-eight carried the lamp up last and did not say a word about the light on the way past it. About nine of them stood at the head of that flight in the wind.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks and four days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty days.

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night for the sixth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week. About nine people went down at the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light, and nothing fell down.

And a man of thirty-eight said the light under the eighth door-stone is the width of his own hand at the knuckles and has been that figure for eight days. It is the first time in seven weeks he has given one figure twice, and he would not say what that means.

And a man of forty-one said out loud in the dark that the Wednesday of next week is the end of his fortnight. He was telling about nine people about it and not asking them anything, and he has a Wednesday and no day, because he said a week ago that he would not do it on a Wednesday.

And a man of thirty-one offered to be one of the two hands and was refused in nine sentences, the ninth of them being that a man of forty-one did not want the last thing his two hands did to have a man of thirty-one in it that nobody can be sent for about.
