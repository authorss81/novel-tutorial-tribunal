# Chapter 712: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Third Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That He Stopped A Count Of Days Himself On The Wednesday Of The Hundred And Fifteenth Week And Is Not Going To Give The Figure Of It This Morning Or Any Other, And A Woman Of Forty Reads Eleven Names Against Nine Days And Gives Five That Nobody In Four Counties Can Be Sent For About And Refuses To Say Which Three Of Those Five Have A Day Against Them, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Somebody Ought To Write The Five Down

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-third week. His four hundred and sixty-second morning. Three hundred and twelve days after the settlement. The fever sixty-one weeks and two days. Two hundred and sixty-six days since the division. Two hundred and twelve days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour and the man of forty-three gave the reading and said the day. Then he said the one thing he had been keeping back since Saturday, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and he stopped saying he had nothing to say about a night five times. Then he gave two figures for a night instead, one on the Saturday of last week and one on the Saturday before it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Six days, and a full bank holds, and there is no more working in it than that and there has not been for three days.*”

“*The next time about ninety steps can be walked is on the Wednesday of next week. That is two days from this morning, and I am giving you the day and the figure and they are not the same size today.*”

“*And that is four days after a full bank, and a full bank is the Saturday of last week.*”

Six days on that landing, and every one of the nine people on it had a Wednesday of next week.

Then a man of sixty-one gave three counts and refused a fourth, and the fourth was one he had stopped himself.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, has kept heads and carts in this county eleven years, and has written one number in that book in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head of it, and a number is the last of the five.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing for eighty-two days. Eighty-two is seven hundred and twelve less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for eighty-three days. Eighty-three is seven hundred and twelve less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*And I have not added them and I am not going to.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week and has been in this county seventy days. Seventy is seven hundred and twelve less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*And there is a fourth count. I stopped it myself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week.*”

“*I am not going to give you the figure of it this morning or any other morning.*”

“*It is the count of days a man was in a place he is not in. It does not go on. It is not going to start on a Monday because a man of thirty-eight has started reading a piece of paper out loud on a Sunday. It is not going to start on a Wednesday either, and nobody is going to get it out of me in about nine weeks.*”

Every one of them had the eighty-two and the eighty-three. Four of them had the count of times a man of sixty-one has stopped.

Then the man of thirty-eight read it out loud for the third morning running, and it took about nine minutes, and about four of them could read it and about nine of them could not.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and put the sheet back on the counter afterwards, and nobody carried it anywhere. He did not say a word about the third morning being the same as the second.

Then a woman of forty read eleven names against nine days out loud on that bank in the open air. She gave the count with the working, and then she stopped, and would not give the rest of it.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name in every room in this county since before any of them came up that plank, and she has never once asked for a reason instead. She is the only person in four counties who can tell you from memory which names are in a book in this county.

“*Eleven names. Nine of them have a day against them and two of them do not. The two of them are on the eighth line and the ninth, and that is the same as it was on the Sunday of last week.*”

“*And five of the eleven are in no book in this county. A person in no book in this county cannot be sent for about from this county.*”

“*And that is not a rule anybody made. It is what has been true every time it has been needed for about nineteen years.*”

“*And two of those five have no day against them. Two of the five have nothing to point at, and that leaves three of the five with a day on them.*”

“*And a day is a place a man sat in a chair.*”

“*Three, out of five. I have given the working and I am going to stop there and I am not going to say which three.*”

The three was the figure that went round that bank. The woman of forty had stopped in the middle of her own arithmetic and about nine of them had watched her do it.

Then she said why she had stopped, and she said it flat, and about nine of them on that bank understood it at about the same time.

“*On the Sunday morning I gave this bank a count of eleven names and nine days and two blanks.*”

“*This morning I have given this bank the same three figures and a fourth one I did not ask for and did not want.*”

“*And I found the fourth one out of the three I already had. I did not go and look. Nobody made me look. I have been asking for a day against a name for about nineteen years and the fourth one was in there the whole time and it came out when I did the sum.*”

“*And I am not going to say the three, and it is not because I do not know. It is because a name is a person you can be sent for about. I have spent about nineteen years making sure that was true and I have just made it true about three people who were safe on Saturday morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for finding that out on the last morning of a week, and I am not going to be told it is a good thing.*”

“*And I would like somebody to say in about four days that I stopped in the middle of it on purpose.*”

Nobody on that bank got the three. A woman of forty had worked out that she could be sent for about three strangers, and about nine of them had not known that about her until that morning.

Then a man of thirty-one said somebody ought to write the five down, and he said it before he had thought about it. A man of sixty-one refused him in six sentences and gave the reason in the last one.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and fifty-eight days. A hundred and fifty-eight is seven hundred and twelve less five hundred and fifty-four, and there is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it.

“*Somebody should write the five down. That is all. Not in a book. On anything. So that about nine people could point at them.*”

“*No. Six sentences and the sixth one is the reason.*” He did not raise his voice. “*A list of five people nobody can be sent for about is five people somebody can be sent for about. That is the whole of it and it is not a finding, it is arithmetic done the other way round.*”

“*And a man of thirty-six carried a sheet of printed paper up that road with eleven names on it. A name with a day on it was a name somebody could be sent for about.*”

“*And a woman of forty has just worked out that there are three more of them, and you want them written down so that about nine people can point at them.*”

“*And I have refused nine spaces and two names and a first of the nine in about a fortnight. I am not going to refuse a fifth kind of thing on the last morning of a week.*”

“*And the fifth sentence is that you are the only man on this bank who has asked me for it. I have been keeping a book eleven years and there has not been a fifth kind of thing in it, and there is not going to be one.*”

“*And the sixth is that I am not going to ask you why, because I have got a figure for it, and it is a hundred and fifty-eight.*”

“*The figure is seven hundred and twelve less five hundred and fifty-four. If I am wrong about it you can correct me in about four days and I will not be thanked for that either.*”

Six sentences and the sixth one was a figure of a man of thirty-one, given out loud, and nobody on that bank improved on it.

Then a man of thirty-one gave the working on that sixth sentence in his own mouth, and said the true of it. Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong and nobody relieved him of it.

“*He is not wrong about the figure and I am not going to pretend he is.*”

“*I have been in this county a hundred and fifty-eight days. There is no column in the county of Kell with my name at the top of it and no form that renews me and no rate on me and no hundred.*”

“*And I have said that pair of sentences in about four counties for about twelve weeks.*”

“*And I have just asked a man of sixty-one to write five names down so that about nine people could point at them.*”

“*The reason I asked is that I have wanted for a hundred and fifty-eight days to be on a piece of paper somewhere. That is not a reason anybody can put on the page.*”

“*And I am not going to be forgiven for it, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and about four of you can tell in three days that I said it and did not put it back.*”

About nine of them had a man of thirty-one who had said a true thing about himself out loud. That was the fourth time in six weeks and four of them had been there for two of the others.

Then a boy of thirteen said the thing nobody had said, which was that one of the three with a day on her is lying down. He gave the working, and nobody asked him a question about it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. His mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about in this county, and he has read all eleven names four times, and has not been asked for anything this week.

“*The fifth line is one of the three. I am not going to say her name and I have said it on this bank twice already and a man of thirty-eight has read it out loud five times.*”

“*And she has not stood up since about the second hour on the Tuesday of the hundred and twelfth week.*”

“*Eighty-three days is seven hundred and twelve less six hundred and twenty-nine. I counted it myself on the road coming down and nobody asked me.*”

“*And a day is a place, and a man can be sent to a place, and about nine people in this county cannot be sent for about the fifth line. I am one of them.*”

“*And I still cannot say what a place is when the place has got a woman lying in it.*”

The eighty-three went into about four heads in that grass. About nine of them had a boy of thirteen standing at the edge of a thing he could have said.

Then a man of fifty-four took the whole of it off a man of thirty-one in about nine sentences, and he cannot read, and he said it before anybody had asked him anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain and cannot read a word of the paper lying in the open about four feet below that bank, and he is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about four things.

“*A man of thirty-one has just heard that a woman of twenty-nine in a reed has got a day against her, and has worked out that she is one of three people about nine of you can be sent for about.*”

“*Here is what that is, and it took about nine seconds and I do not read, so I have not had a book to do it out of.*”

“*To look at a place you have to go to it. And to go to that place you have to go along the edge of that drain about a hundred yards. Then you stand about twenty feet off a reed and look into it at a woman lying on her side.*”

“*And that is what being sent for about somebody is. Not the asking. The going.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four told about nine people on the Thursday of last week that it took about nine weeks and about nine doors to come to her, and nobody was in a coat on any of them.*”

“*And that is the same thing happening the other way round.*”

“*So a man of thirty-one has just worked out that about nine people can be sent to stand over a woman in a reed. He has worked it out out loud, on the last morning of a week, in front of about nine of us.*”

“*And I am the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about four things, and I am telling you that a fifth is not better than the four.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for standing here and saying that.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four who cannot read has about nine days of saying things nobody asked him about left in him if he keeps going and he is not going to keep going.*”

About four people on that bank had the four things and about nine of them had a man of fifty-four who had said he was not going to keep going.

Then the sheet went back on the counter, and about nine people walked up the water from that landing. About four of them had a count of five and a count of three, and neither of them was written down anywhere.

---

The fever was sixty-one weeks and two days old. He had been in that county a hundred and fifty-eight days.

A man of sixty-one stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and refused the figure of it out loud on the last morning of a week. He gave three other counts in the order he gives them, and added none of them to anything.

And a woman of forty read eleven names against nine days and gave five of the eleven that nobody in four counties can be sent for about. She worked out that three of those five have a day against them, refused to say which three, and said why.

Somebody ought to write the five down, and a man of thirty-one said it out loud before he had thought about it. A man of fifty-four who cannot read said that to look at a place is to go and stand over a woman in it, and about nine of them walked up that water afterwards with two counts and no place to put either of them.
