# Chapter 703: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Second Week, And A Saturday, And On Which About Nine People Walk Four Miles Up A Cart Road To A Bakehouse And A Woman Of Forty-Four Gives The Question In Her Own Words And Says She Answered It And Is Not Going To Be Told She Was Made To, And Then Says The Second Asking Was About A Door Four Miles On That She Does Not Know Was The Same Question, And A Man Of Thirty-One Is Taken Off In One Sentence By The Woman He Said It To

Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-second week. His four hundred and fifty-third morning. Three hundred and three days after the settlement. The fever sixty weeks old. Two hundred and fifty-seven days since the division. Two hundred and three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full. The man of forty-three gave the reading at about the seventh hour and said the working and said it was the fifth day, and then he went up the cart road with the rest of them and did not go in the boat.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept a landing nineteen years. A bank that is full stays full until it comes off, and he has given that figure to the bank and not to himself.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour. Five days, and it was about sixteen at this hour yesterday, and that is the whole of the working because a full bank stays full.*”

“*And I am going up that road this morning with about nine people because I have not been up it in about four weeks and there is nothing about a reading that needs me here.*”

About four people on that landing had the sixteen inches and about nine of them had gone up four miles.

About nine people walked four miles up that cart road and arrived at a bakehouse at about the ninth hour, and nobody had sent for anybody, and a woman of forty-four came out on the step and did not ask them whether they had come.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps a bakehouse four miles up that cart road from the landing. About nine houses in that village take her bread, about four of the nine cannot do a day without it, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a tradesman four miles off may be sent for about a morning of his work.

“*I have got the loaves out and the fire banked and I have not put the door bar on, and I put the door bar off at about half past seven, and I want that said first and I would like it said before anybody says anything else.*”

“*Sit down or stand up, I do not mind, and there is water in the trough and it is our water.*”

Then she gave the question in her own words, exactly as it was put to her, and she gave it in the first person because she has given three other things in this county in the first person.

“*It was nine words. He asked me whether I would come at first light, on the days it is written on, and put my two hands on the stone in the ninth room and push it to the far end of the room and push it back.*”

“*And I said yes.*”

Nobody on that step said anything. A question of nine words and an answer of one word had been said out loud on that step in front of nine or ten of them, and nobody improved on it.

“*I am going to say why and then I am not going to say it again, and I am not going to be told that I was made to say it, because I said it.*”

“*It is a small thing. It gets light about a quarter of an hour before a man of thirty-eight can read two copies nine miles apart, and it gets light the same in that room, and it has got light like that on my own floor for nineteen years and nobody has ever once asked me for it.*”

“*And there are about nine houses a day late on my bread and about four of the nine cannot do a day without it, and one of those four is a man of eighty-one whose Thursday was three days after a Sunday.*”

“*And if it is me that is up at half past four in the dark nine days out of nine, then nobody is a day late, and that is the whole of what I thought about at about eight o’clock on the Wednesday morning while a man I did not know stood on my step with a piece of paper.*”

Then she said the other half, and she said it flat, and it took her about four seconds to say it and it was the whole chapter.

“*He asked me on the Wednesday. That is the part I want in front of about nine people and I want it said before anybody asks me a question.*”

“*And on the Friday of last week a man of thirty-six came to this door in a different coat and asked me whether I would come at first light and put my two hands on a stone and push it to the far end of a room and push it back.*”

“*And I said yes to that one as well and I did not notice that I had.*”

Nobody on that step said anything at all and about four of them had the different coat.

“*I have not been able to work out whether it was the same question and I am not going to work it out on this step with nine of you looking at me.*”

“*It was a door four miles on, and that is a village I do not trade into, and it was the same two hands and the same first light, and I have been baking for nineteen years and the road has been there every one of them and I have not been up it to that village once in about nine years.*”

“*And a person can be asked the same question twice by two men in two coats and answer it twice and only be asked once, and there is nobody in four counties who could tell you that it was one question and not two, because nobody was in the room but me.*”

About four people on that step had one question and about nine of them had two coats.

Then a man of thirty-one said the thing he had come up four miles to say, and it was the wrong thing, and she took it off him in one sentence before he got to the end of it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county a hundred and forty-nine days. A hundred and forty-nine is seven hundred and three less five hundred and fifty-four.

“*Nobody is going to be let stand on this step and say they were made to say yes to a thing they did not understand, and if a sheet of paper has come up this road and written down a yes that somebody did not—*”

“*You have been in this county a hundred and forty-nine days and nobody has asked you for anything in one of them.*”

She did not raise her voice and nine or ten of them were on that step.

“*And I have been asked three times and I have answered twice and I have told you what I answered and I have told you what the questions were, and one of them was nine words and both of them were about a door.*”

“*You are the one who has not been asked. I am not saying that to be cruel and I am not saying it to be clever. I am saying it because you are about to go back down that road and tell about nine people at a landing that a woman of forty-four was got at, and nobody got at me, and I have said yes to a stone twice in my own mouth.*”

She had his whole morning and his hundred and forty-nine days in one sentence, and about nine of them on that step had come up that road for one reason only.

“*And I am not going to be thanked for any of this and I am not going to be sorry for any of it, and if anybody up that road says it was worth the walk I will stand on this step and let them say it and then it will have been said and I will not have said it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four said what had happened on that step in a fortnight, and he cannot read, and he said it before he had been asked anything and he said what it was a figure of.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain. He has said no four times in nine days and has been the only person anybody can be sent for about four things, and has not been asked to be a fifth.

“*A woman of forty-four has stood on her own step this morning and told about nine people three askings and two answers and nine words and one word and two coats, and nobody asked her one question about any of it.*”

“*That is about six weeks of what the rest of us have been doing and I have got it in one morning because she was not asked anything and there was nothing to stop her.*”

“*I cannot read a word of the sheet and I am not going to be able to read a word of it, and I have not been sent for about asking this question and neither has she, and I am not going to be thanked for noticing the difference.*”

Nobody on that step said sorry and about nine of them had the six weeks.

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count of what had happened in nine days, and he gave it in the order it happened, and he refused to write any of it in about nine sentences.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of them.

“*Nine days. Nine is seven hundred and three less six hundred and ninety-four. And I am not going to call that a week.*”

“*One man of thirty-six came up that road twice and carried a sheet of printed paper four miles each way. One woman was asked three times. One of the three was not about a door nine miles under a river and was about a door four miles on.*”

“*Two answers, out loud, and one of them was given twice without the woman knowing she had given it a first time.*”

“*And I am not going to write any of that in this book and here are three reasons and I have given them before.*”

“*A thing said out loud in front of about nine people is a figure of about nine people and not a record. Two of those three times I was not in the room and I do not have a figure of them at all. And if I write it I become the only man in this county who knows it, and a man who knows a thing alone is a man who will say it in about four years when somebody asks him whether it was true.*”

“*And I am not going to give a fourth reason and I am not going to be asked for one on this step.*”

He said nothing about the inside of a coat and about four of them had heard him refuse it anyway, and nobody on that step wrote one word of any of it down.

Then a man of thirty-eight said what the whole of it was in about four sentences, and he said he was going to be the man who says it and not the man who says it first, and nobody improved on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board that is not his in his own coat.

“*A sheet of printed paper asked a woman of forty-four the same question twice through two men in two coats and she answered it twice and cannot tell anybody whether that is one asking or two.*”

“*That is not a thing a person did to her and I want it said that way and not the other way round, because it is going to come out the other way round in about four days and somebody is going to be pleased about it.*”

“*And it is not a trick. I have been in this county long enough to know what a trick looks like and that is not one, and that is worse, and I am not going to say out loud what it is worse than.*”

“*Somebody is doing the whole of it one person at a time in the plainest words anybody has ever used, and there are about nine of us on a step and every one of us could be the next one it comes to, and I have been on this road about four times this fortnight.*”

About four people on that step had the one person at a time and about nine of them had the plainest words.

---

The fever was sixty weeks old. He had been in that county a hundred and forty-nine days.

About nine people walked four miles up a cart road to a bakehouse and a woman of forty-four gave a question of nine words and said she answered it, and said she answered it twice because a man of thirty-six in a different coat asked it on a Friday about a door four miles on.

And she said she was not going to be told that she was made to say it, and took a man of thirty-one off in one sentence by saying that he is the one who has not been asked.

And three askings and two answers were given out loud on that step, and none of them was written down.
