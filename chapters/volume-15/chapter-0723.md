# Chapter 723: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Week, And A Friday, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Asks For The Ninth Line To Be Read To Him In The Open And Gets It, And A Man Of Sixty-One Gives Two Counts And Refuses To Make One Figure Of Them, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Shape About A Woman In A Reed

Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. His four hundred and seventy-third morning. Three hundred and twenty-three days after the settlement. The fever sixty-two weeks and six days. Two hundred and seventy-seven days since the division. Two hundred and twenty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working, and it was the shortest thing he has given anybody in about a fortnight.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gave two figures for a night a fortnight ago and has not given one since, and he says neither of them is his.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour. One day.*”

“*It was off all ninety yesterday at the sixth hour, and that is the working.*”

“*And I am not going to say it four times this morning.*”

“*And a bank that is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth.*”

“*I gave you that yesterday and I am not going to give it twice.*”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open, before anything else happened. He cannot read and he said it flat.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel at the head of that drain. He cannot read a word of the paper on that counter. He asked for the ninth line to be read to him yesterday in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under a bank, and said he would ask every morning while there is a reading on that counter.

“*That is the second morning.*”

“*I asked for it yesterday in the dark. About four of you heard me and the rest of you did not.*”

“*That is the correct way round and it has not changed since the Sunday of last week.*”

“*I am asking for it again in the open, and I am going to keep asking for it.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for reading it out to me.*”

“*And I am not going to tell you what I do with it.*”

“*There is a woman of forty in this county who has been asking for a day against a name for about nineteen years, and she is not going to get one out of what I do with it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line out loud in the open on that bank, in the wind, and put the sheet back where anybody could put a hand on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet twelve times in this county and has been refused a figure for how long three times.

“*Nine words, and about four of them are about two hands and about nine feet along a groove.*”

“*And there is still no day against either name on that line, and there never has been.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has been asked about that line, and there is nothing on it to come on.*”

“*Thirteen mornings. Thirteen is seven hundred and twenty-three less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And there was not one of those thirteen in the dark.*”

“*I asked a man of thirty-one for a figure for how long on the Tuesday of this week and he did not give me one.*”

“*I am going to keep asking until somebody gives me one or until I stop asking, and I am not going to be thanked for either.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave two counts with the working, and refused to make one figure of them, and gave a reason he had not given before.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county eleven years. He has written one number in it in eleven years, and there are five lines on the leaf after the last head. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has not given the figure of it since.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing for ninety-three days.*”

“*Ninety-three is seven hundred and twenty-three less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for ninety-four days.*”

“*Ninety-four is seven hundred and twenty-three less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*And the last time I gave those two I said I was not going to add them, and I am not going to add them, and this time I am going to give you a different reason for it.*”

“*Last time the reason was that I did not want a total.*”

“*This time the reason is that if either of those two asks me for the figure, then I am going to say it to them and not to you.*”

“*One of them has not asked me for anything in more than ninety days, and the other has said one word in about eighty.*”

“*And I have got no figure for how many of you have worked out that a total of ninety-three and ninety-four is a figure of two people and not a figure of anything.*”

“*And I am not going to help you.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the other half of it. It was three days old, and she said she had not worked out how she knew.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years. She got up out of the book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and nobody has been able to find her by writing her name down in any one of them since.

“*On the Tuesday of this week I said, in nine people’s hearing, that somebody was going to write another name on that thing.*”

“*And that nobody was going to be sent for about it.*”

“*And on that Tuesday I told the nine of you standing there that I had not got a figure for when, and I am not going to pretend to you this morning that I gave you one.*”

“*And I have got one now and I am giving the working first, because that is how it is done on this bank.*”

“*Four is seven hundred and twenty-four less seven hundred and twenty, and it is tomorrow morning, and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

“*And I have not worked out how I knew, and I am not going to pretend to a woman of forty that I did.*”

“*She has been on that bank for about nineteen years and she has never once pretended to anything.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it a week early on purpose.*”

“*And nobody on this bank is going to be stood up and asked whether I am frightened of tomorrow.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight said what those two things are together, in four sentences, and gave the count she is not keeping.

Sera Quill is twenty-eight and is not one of the nine and cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She has said the same sentence nine times without saying it. She said on the Tuesday of this week that she was not going to begin again, and has not begun.

“*A woman of sixty-four has told nine of you that something is going to happen and cannot say how she knows.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has asked for a line to be read to him every morning and cannot say what he is going to do with it.*”

“*Those are two people with the same thing, and neither of them has got a name for it, and I am not going to give them one.*”

“*I gave somebody one once and it did not hold.*”

“*And I did not check it this morning. There is no figure for how long I have not checked it and I am not going to make one up on a Friday.*”

“*And the count of the times I have said that sentence and not said it does not go up.*”

“*It carries no ordinal and it is not restarted, and it is not a figure of anything.*”

“*It is the only thing in this county I have got that nobody can be sent for about.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said a shape out loud about a woman in a reed, in front of nine people, before anybody had asked him anything.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and sixty-nine days. A hundred and sixty-nine is seven hundred and twenty-three less five hundred and fifty-four, and nobody has asked him for anything in one of them.

“*The fifth line is the only line on that sheet anybody in this county could send somebody to.*”

“*Nine of you have known that for a fortnight and not one of you has said it out loud on a bank.*”

“*A day on that line is a place. A place is a hundred yards along the edge of a drain and twenty feet off a reed.*”

“*And that is what being sent for about somebody is, and a man of fifty-four said it on the Monday of a fortnight in nine words.*”

“*They were in his mouth and not mine.*”

Then a woman of forty took it off him in seven sentences. She said it flat, and she had said on the Monday of last week that she would not say which three.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She is the only person in four counties who can tell you from memory which names are in a book in this county. She read eleven names against nine days on the Monday of a fortnight and stopped in the middle of her own arithmetic.

“*I said on the Monday that I was not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and I have not started.*”

“*And you have just made it a shape about a person.*”

“*A shape about a person is the thing you do. I have spent about nineteen years making sure that a name is a person you can be sent for about.*”

“*And you have just given nine people a sentence that does the same work in four sentences.*”

“*And about four of them are going to use it this week, and not one of them is going to be standing at the edge of that drain.*”

“*And you are not going to be either, and that is going to happen whatever you said on this bank.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for stopping it, and I am not going to tell you which three.*”

“*I would like somebody to say in four days that I said no to that before anybody had asked me.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it and gave the working, and said out loud that he was not going to be told which half of it was his.

“*She is right about all seven of them and I am not going to take one of them back.*”

“*A hundred and sixty-nine is seven hundred and twenty-three less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and sixty-nine days.*”

“*I said on the Monday of last week that I have wanted for a hundred and sixty-nine days to be somewhere a person could send somebody to.*”

“*And you heard that and did not answer it, and I have not asked you again and I am not going to.*”

“*And this morning I said a shape out loud about a woman in a reed, and I am not going to say one word more about her.*”

“*And I am not going to be told I was half right, and I am not going to be told I was wrong.*”

Nine of them on that bank had a man of thirty-one who had said a sentence that four of them were going to use. Four of them had a woman of forty who had said no before anybody had asked her.

Then the sheet went back on that counter and nobody carried it anywhere, and nine people stood in the wind on a bank with four inches of water under the top step.


---

The fever was sixty-two weeks and six days old. He had been in that county a hundred and sixty-nine days.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for the ninth line of a printed sheet to be read to him in the open before anything else happened, and said he would ask every morning while there is a reading on that counter, and would not say what he does with it.

And a man of sixty-one gave two counts with the working. A man has been in an ash for ninety-three days and a woman has been in a reed for ninety-four days, and he refused to add them for a different reason than last time. If either of those two asks him, he will say it to them and not to the bank.

And a man of thirty-one said a shape out loud about a woman in a reed and was refused by a woman of forty, and she is not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and has not started.
