# Chapter 883: The Barrow, And No Argument About The World

Third day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-third morning. Four hundred and eighty-three days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and five days old. Four hundred and thirty-seven days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Four inches came up on the top step at about the seventh hour and about ninety of those ninety steps were still under. That was seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and said that the whole landing would be under by the middle of next week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and does not hold the gravel, which is held by a man of fifty-four who cannot read.

“Four at the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps under, and it came up four inches since the sixth hour yesterday and all ninety of them were dry at the seventh hour.”

“Ninety. Not sixty. I said about sixty on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-seventh week and it came off all ninety this morning at the sixth hour, which is why it is ninety and not sixty, and I would rather be out by thirty than in by thirty.”

Then a man of thirty-eight came up with a case, and it took one morning, and nobody on that bank used the word world.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. There is a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, which is a figure of what is on a board.

“I have a case and it is small and I want it settled before the light goes off the top of that step.”

“My boy is nine. He has been carrying gravel up that cut for eleven mornings after school because there is nobody to carry it and I am on the stone at the seventh hour. Eleven mornings. I counted them myself and I am not asking anybody to check me.”

“I want those eleven mornings entered on a run. Not on mine. I do not care whose. I want them entered so that they happened.”

“And I have said the whole of my case and there is no argument in it about anything except a barrow.”

Then a man of thirty-one offered to enter the case on his own run, and was told what a hearing is.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twenty-nine days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ninety-eight days old.

“Then I will enter them. I have a run and there are nine positions on a page and there is no harm in eleven mornings of a boy with a barrow going into a run where somebody can see them.”

“Twenty-three wrong things in twenty-three mornings, and here is the twenty-third. Three hundred and twenty-nine days in this county and the cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-eight days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-three days old, and I have not asked it.”

“That is not what you have just offered. A hearing is not a day somebody did the work. You have taken a morning in which two men stood on a bank and said words at each other, and you have offered to enter it as though it were gravel up a cut.”

Corvin Tace said what a case is on that landing, and gave the working, and it took him nine seconds.

“A case is two people on a step arguing about a thing that has already happened, and it is not a day anybody worked, and if you enter those then I have entered every morning I have ever sat on this landing, and that is every morning for nineteen years.”

“Here is the working and it is short. The evidence that a day was done is the line somebody entered that day. If the boy carried the barrow then the line is his and it goes against his name and not against his father’s, and a father does not get to put his son’s morning in his own book.”

“And that is the whole of it. Nobody has said a word about the ninth line, or about a page four hundred miles off, or about three things that are lost, and nobody needed to, and that is what a case is for.”

Then the man of thirty-eight was told it in his own hearing, and what it cost him was on the inside of his coat.

“I am not going to argue with that. I have been on that bank asking what three of the nine positions are for eleven days and I have never once had an argument that took a morning and no more.”

“So the eleven mornings are my boy’s. That is the ruling and I will take it. And they are not going on my board, and I am not going to cut a notch for them, and I am not going to tell anybody I have cut one.”

“I have thirty-two notches on that board and about four of you have never once asked what they are. They are not the nine and they are not the seven. They are mornings I could not check while I was doing something else.”

“And this morning one of the reasons that board is thin is sitting on that step with his knees in the gravel, and he does not know that yet, and nobody is going to be thanked for telling him.”

He then gave the whole of the thing, because nobody had asked him and a case that does not say what the thing was is not a case.

“That is the second half of my case and nobody asked me for it. Eleven mornings with a barrow up that cut after school, and it is not a case about a barrow. It is a case about whether anybody was standing there.”

“On two of the eleven I took an end of it myself. Two mornings, one barrow, two men. So on those two mornings neither of us did that work alone, and I do not know whose morning either of them is, and neither does he.”

“So it is not eleven. It is nine of his and two of nothing, and a man of thirty-one was about to write eleven on something, and I have stopped him by saying it out loud before he got a pen.”

Corvin Tace said the same thing twice, once for each half of it, and it took him longer than the ruling had.

“Two mornings of a barrow carried by two men are two men’s work in one morning. A run wants one line and one person, and on those two mornings neither man has a line, and that is not a hole in a record. That is a morning where two people did a thing together and it does not go on a leaf.”

“And I am not going to say what it goes on instead, because it does not go on anything yet. Nine mornings against the boy. Two mornings against nobody. That is the whole of it and it is worse than eleven and it took a morning to get to it.”

“And nobody is going to be thanked for that either, and I did not do it for the thanks, and I would have done it anyway because I would rather be wrong on a Thursday than right on a Friday with a number nobody checked.”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-second morning, and asked a different question about a barrow.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and sixty-two mornings, which is eight hundred and eighty-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and sixty-two. And I have been on that cut for nineteen years with the gravel and I have never once had to rule on it. A man of forty-three has just ruled on it in nine seconds and he did not read anything.”

“A man of fifty-four who cannot read can hear a man of forty-three rule on a barrow. So here is my question and it is not for the man of thirty-eight.”

“If a boy carries a barrow for eleven mornings and it goes against his name and not his father’s name, then a man of fifty-four who cannot read has asked for one line for eleven years and it has not gone against his name. It has not gone against anybody. That is what I have been saying and I did not have the words for it until a boy of nine needed them.”

“I am not going to say the man of forty-three has given me anything. He has not. He has said a thing about a barrow.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty is eight hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up, and there were about ninety steps under that cut and every one of them was dry at the top and wet at the bottom.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-two mornings. A man of thirty-eight has just had a morning given to a boy of nine instead of to him, and nobody asked me whether that was right, and I am not saying that it was.”

“A barrow is a barrow and a line is a line and a case is a case, and that is three kinds of thing in one morning, and there was a moment a few lines back when a man of thirty-one was going to make them one kind of thing and put them in a column.”

“And a man of thirty-eight has just told the whole bank that his own figure was wrong and he worked out how it was wrong himself, with nobody in the room able to tell him. Nobody asked him to. Nobody gave him a wrong figure. He got there on his own and he took it back before any of you could use it, and I would like that noticed, because about four of you were waiting for me to say it and I am saying it.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself, and a man of fifty-four watched her do it.

“That is the twenty-sixth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and it is on a Thursday, and I entered it, and today is not a gap.”

The barrow went up the cut twice more before the light went off the top of the step, and about nine of them were still on the bank, and nothing had been argued about the world, and one of the men on that bank had one fewer reason to keep a board he never explained.
