# Chapter 886: A Complete Account Is Not A Complete Account

Sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-sixth morning. Four hundred and eighty-six days after the settlement. The fever eighty-six weeks and one day old. Four hundred and forty days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank, with four days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave both words and named the day he had named on the morning before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gave four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour of a Sunday. He named this morning on the fifth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, with the day and the week on it.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on a Sunday.”

“And I said yesterday that it would be a full bank on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, and it is the sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and it is a full bank, and I said it a day early. I would rather be out by a day than in by one.”

Then a man of thirty-one got as far as the name of a thing, and stopped one word late, and the woman who owns the question told him to finish it, and he stopped in his own mouth instead.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-two days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and one days old.

“Three hundred and thirty-two days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-six days old, and I have not asked it.”

“Twenty-six wrong things in twenty-six mornings, and here is the twenty-sixth. I have had a thing in my mouth for three days and I am going to say it and I am going to stop where I stop.”

“If somebody gave the three lost beginnings back — if somebody gave the front of those three runs back, whole, in the hand they were written in — then there would not be an incomplete account anywhere on that landing, and there would not be a question on it, and about nine of us could stop.”

“A complete account. That is the thing. And if it were —”

“Finish it.”

“No, and I am going to leave it there for a minute.”

Orla Fennimore said the three sentences that everybody on that bank has already heard, out loud, and said she was not discovering anything.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and has put her question down five times and is not going to put it down a sixth.

“I am going to say it, and I want the whole bank to notice that I am saying it, and I am not saying it because I have found it. It has been said on this landing for eleven days in four or five mouths and every one of you has heard it.”

“A complete account is not the right of refusal. It has never been that, and there is a name on that landing that may not be put on it in a mouth, and a person cannot buy an answer by asking somebody for the pages.”

“A run with a hole at the front of it is not it either. That is not a discovery either. A man who keeps a boat said that on the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and it is true and it is not what we are looking for.”

“And an incomplete account is not it. And I am the one saying that and not a woman of forty-nine and not a man of about fifty-two, because it is my question and somebody in this bank who does not own a question is not the mouth for it.”

Then the woman who rings the bell at the seventh of the nine had three sentences of her own, and she had had them since she took the seventh, and she said them out loud so that about four of you would have heard the shape out loud three times in one morning.

Hessa Vinch is thirty-six and holds the seventh of the nine positions and has a hand-bell on a bracket with nothing behind it, and she rings it on the mornings the work is done, and she enters the day on a leaf like anybody else, and the front of that run was burnt before she took it.

“A bell is not it. A bell rung for a thing that did not happen is not a day somebody did the work, and a bell rung for a thing that did happen is not a day either. It is a bell.”

“A run with a hole at the front of it is not it. I have heard it said on this landing by four or five people in a fortnight and I am saying it now and it is the same sentence.”

“An incomplete account is not it. And I hold the seventh of the nine and there is no stone behind it, and the front of that run was burnt before I took it and what is left of it is a wedge, and that is the one position on that page nobody can go and look at, and it is still not it, and if that does not stop nine of you then nothing will.”

“Now here is what I am not doing this morning. I am not putting it down a sixth time. One word of it has never moved and it is not going to move, and I would rather carry it unanswered for nineteen more years than hand it to a phrase.”

“Because a phrase would answer it. That is the whole trouble with a phrase. It closes the question, and a closed question is a thing nobody looks at again, and I have spent nineteen years on twenty-nine chairs and I have never once wanted that.”

“And I am going to say what a working day is, since I asked for one on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and nobody has given me one and I have not stopped asking.”

“A working day is a day somebody does a thing and somebody else puts down that they did it. That is the whole of it. It is not a favour and it is not a settlement and it is not anybody being let off. It is a morning with a line in it that somebody else entered.”

“I have not asked for a day off. I have asked to be on somebody else’s page for a morning, once, for a thing I actually did, and to have it entered by a person who is not me and who is allowed to say I got it wrong.”

“Twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years and every one of them entered by me. That is not a working day. That is a woman writing her own account in a room nobody else goes into, and I have known that since about the twelfth year and I have gone on doing it.”

Then the woman who keeps the ninth of the nine said where the arrangement is, and who is in the room with it.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. She entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, in front of anybody.

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It answered inside this week and it will answer again inside a week, and I have put three of its answers in front of this bank and refused two of them.”

“I want that said plainly on a Sunday when about four of you are tired. There is not one person on this landing in the room where it answers. There is not one person on this landing who has been in that room in nineteen years.”

“And a thing four hundred miles off that answers on a schedule is the reason a figure of leaves is not a figure of days and the reason a figure of houses is not a figure of people, and I am not going to develop that this morning because it would take the whole day and it would not change a thing.”

“Twenty-nine mornings in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered this one on a Sunday while nine of you were standing on a bank arguing about a word.”

“And I want to say one thing about a woman of forty-four’s working day, because she has just given the working out loud and half of you heard it and about four of you wrote it down as her asking for a gift.”

“She is not asking for a gift. She is asking to stop being the only person in the account. Every morning for nineteen years the line has gone in her own hand, and a line nobody else entered is a line a person cannot be shown.”

“That is the same sentence as the one about a page and it is the same sentence as the one about a barrow, and three people said three versions of it this week without any of them knowing, and I am not going to pretend I worked that out before today.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-fifth morning, and said what a word is when nobody has agreed what it means.

“A hundred and sixty-five. And a woman of forty-four has just said the word out loud and told nine of us it is not the thing, and I have been asking for a day for eleven years without ever once saying what a day is.”

“You all know what a day is. Nobody has ever told me. And that is not a complaint, because I cannot read and I have been on the step with a number in my mouth since before you were all standing here.”

“If somebody finished that word this morning, then it would be a thing with a meaning in it, and then I would know whether I have been asking for it. And I would rather not know.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-three days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-three is eight hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-three days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-five mornings. A man of thirty-one stopped one word from the end of a sentence this morning and a woman of forty-four made him stop, and about four of you were waiting to hear the word.”

“You did not hear the word. I am not going to say it either. If he had finished it then it would have been on that step in front of nine of us, and now it is not, and a boy of thirteen is not going to be the one who puts it back.”

Sundays are the mornings when about half of that bank go down early, and they went down early, and a man of thirty-one did not go with them, and a woman of forty-four watched him not go and did not say anything about it, and the word he had come up that bank to say stayed where he had left it.
