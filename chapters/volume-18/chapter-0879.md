# Chapter 879: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Seventh Week, And A Sunday, And Sixteen Inches Holding With Three Days Of The Coming Back And About Sixty Of Those Steps Under, And A Woman Of Forty-Nine Says The Whole Of What A Run Of Days Is For When The Thing It Is About Is Not One Of The Nine, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Somebody Else Could Keep One Of Them And Is Told That Would Be A Different Day And Not On That Run

Sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. His six hundred and twenty-ninth morning. Four hundred and seventy-nine days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and one day old. Four hundred and thirty-three days since the division. Three hundred and seventy-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was three days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and said that a Sunday was the day the fourth thing got asked for the hardest.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour, and has been asked about a fifth thing on five mornings running without giving one.

“*Sixteen at the seventh hour, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, and it holds.*”

“*It is holding because a full bank holds until it comes off. I said that on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I am saying it again and about four of you have begun to write the word holds when I say the number and not the other way round, which is the wrong order and I am too old to keep catching it.*”

“*And it is a Sunday and about four of you want a fifth thing out of me because Sunday is a long day and there is nothing else to do on that bank. There is nothing else to do on that bank on a Wednesday either. I have had four figures on a Wednesday for nineteen years and I have never had a fifth.*”

Then a woman of forty-nine said the whole of what a run of days is for, when the thing it is about is not one of the nine, and gave the whole of it in about nine of your ears.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. On the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week she said, in about nine of your ears, that the three blanks were never blanks.

“*Four of you have been saying pieces of this all week and a man of about fifty-two has said it four times and a man of forty-seven has said it and a woman of thirty-three has said it and a woman of twenty-eight has said it four miles down a cart road. I am going to say the whole of it once and then I am finished with it.*”

“*A run is not the position. That is what I said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week and it is about a page and not about the people on the bank.*”

“*A position that is not a stone is not a thing on a list. It is a day somebody did the work. The only evidence that the day was done is the line somebody entered that day. The run is where the day is written down.*”

“*And a position may be checked from the day its record starts. It may not be checked from before that day, and the day its record starts is a figure of a thing and is owed to nobody.*”

“*Now. What is that for, on a landing where the man of forty-three has four figures every morning and six of the nine positions have a stone you can stand in front of and three of them do not have one?*”

“*It is for a thing that happens every day whether or not anybody is watching. The boat crosses or it does not. The drain runs or it does not. The school opens or it does not. The gate is shut or it is not.*”

“*None of those four is one of the nine. Nothing will ever check them. There is no stone in front of them to stand at and no index that carries them and no page four hundred miles off that has any of them on it. And they are the only four on that bank where a person does the same work on a morning and nobody is going to look at it.*”

“*And that is not the right of refusal and it may not be called that in a mouth. A run with a hole in it is not it either. An incomplete account is not it either. A day entered on a piece of paper binds nobody and protects nobody and I have been saying that for twenty-three years and about four of you have stopped hearing it as a small thing.*”

“*And what three of the nine positions are is still open. I said that on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week and I am saying it on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, and a man of thirty-one has spent a week making four ordinary things into a sentence about the nine, and he got it wrong twice, and it has not moved that question by one morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that if a keeper were ill then about four of them could keep her run for a week, and was refused by the woman who keeps a drain.

“*A week. That is all I am asking. About four of you on that run for a week and then she comes back and nothing is different.*”

Nessa Toomy is thirty-three, keeps a run of days for a drain, and her run is a hundred and fifty-eight days long with the anchor being a stone step that went in flat.

“*No. And this is the ninth time somebody has wanted about four of you on somebody else’s run this week and it is the first time it has come with a kindness attached to it.*”

“*Here is the working. The only evidence that a day was done is the line somebody entered that day. If the line is entered by another person then it is evidence that that other person did it, on that day, and not that I did.*”

“*So on the Monday a man of fifty-four would have had a line about the drain in his hand, and on the Tuesday another, and on the Wednesday a man of forty-seven would have, and by the Friday about nine of you would have got into a habit of writing at that step in the morning, and the seventh day would have had four names on it and none of them mine.*”

“*I have had three mornings off out of the last ten. I did not write three lines. I did not ask anybody to write three lines for me, and nobody offered, and nobody was waiting to offer, because they did not know I was going to be off until I was off.*”

“*That is the whole of what a run is. It is a place where it says who was there. Take the who out of it and you have got a piece of paper with wet on it.*”

Ilyan Vester said that the work got done either way and that he was offering to be useful.

“*And the man of forty-seven has said since the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that he may come and walk it with me any morning. I am going to take that and I am not going to give the other thing up for it, and about four of you are going to say I have had it both ways and I have not.*”

“*Three hundred and twenty-five days in this county, and that is eight hundred and seventy-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-four days old, and that is eight hundred and seventy-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and seventy-nine days old, and a hundred and seventy-nine is eight hundred and seventy-nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and it is a hundred and eighty tomorrow, and I am not going to ask it tomorrow and I am going to say it out loud tomorrow and those are two different things and about four of you have never had those apart.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight asked his first half for the third time and was given the same sentence in the same mouth with the day it was said on.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. The board with thirty-two notches cut in its edge is what he carries and he has never once told about nine of you what is written on the face of it.

“*First half. What the three are. Third time. And I am not going to stand here and do the thing where a man of thirty-eight builds up to a question over four days.*”

“*I went and looked at four ordinary things with my own feet this week and I am not putting that anywhere. A boat, a drain, a gate, and a school I did not get to see because about four of you would have gone with me and that would have made it a fifth thing, and a woman of twenty-eight said on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week that nobody is to come up that road to look at what she does, and she said it about her own work and not about mine, and I am going to obey it about her work and I am going to think about what else it means.*”

“*And I am not going to be told this is progress. I have been told that twice this week by people who were not talking about my question.*”

Sena Dorr gave him the same sentence, with the day it was said on, and said it was the third time.

“*On the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, which was a Monday, I said an index is a list of what a person can be shown. A list has got one thing it can say about a position, and the one thing is whether there is a thing to go and stand in front of.*”

“*I said that on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week. I said it on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. That is three times, and this is the fourth, and I am going to keep saying it on the same day of the week for as long as you ask, and that is not a punishment and it is not a favour.*”

“*What the three are, is a different question. And a page cannot answer it and I am not going to invent a way round my own working because you have got a Sunday and nothing else to do.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty-six days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty-six is eight hundred and seventy-nine less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and twenty-six days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*Nobody has asked me for the count I am keeping. That is eighteen mornings and I am nearly at a figure that sounds like something and it is not, and about four of you have noticed that it is nearly at a figure and about nine of you have not.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine has said a run is for a thing that happens whether or not anybody is watching. That is the count. It is not that I am keeping a secret. It is that I have been counting mornings where nobody asked about what was on them, and about nine of you have been on that bank every morning this week and about four of you asked.*”

“*I am still not going to say it and I am not going to stop.*”

Orla Fennimore said one thing about a woman of twenty-eight and about a Saturday four miles up a cart road, and said what she was not going to do with it.

“*She went back down that road on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and entered today’s line about her school and she has not come up it since, and nobody is going to go up it, and I am not going to say what is on it.*”

“*Her own count is two hundred and thirty-seven days, and that is eight hundred and seventy-nine less six hundred and forty-two, and that is the last time I will say it this week. About four of you have been keeping a figure of a woman who cannot be sent for and that is not keeping, that is looking.*”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fifty-eighth morning and said what a woman of forty-nine had said that morning that he was taking away with him.

“*A hundred and fifty-eight. And a woman of forty-nine has said that a run of days is for a thing that happens whether or not anybody is watching, and I am going to be honest about what I did with that.*”

“*I have been on that bank for eleven years asking whether anybody watched what I did. And the answer, from a woman of forty-nine, this morning, is that that is not what a run is for. It is for putting down that it happened.*”

“*I am not going to say that settles it. I am going to say I heard it and I am going to keep asking in the morning, because I have not got the day and I would rather ask badly for eleven more years than stop on a Sunday because a woman of forty-nine said a true thing.*”

“*Nobody is to be thanked. Not her and not me and not the woman of thirty-three and not the man of forty-seven and not the boy of thirteen.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of the Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week with sixteen inches on the top step, and a woman of forty-nine who had said the whole of it once and said she was finished with it.
