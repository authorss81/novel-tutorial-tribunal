# Chapter 898: The Twenty-Ninth Time And Nothing To Be About

Fourth day of the hundred and fiftieth week. His six hundred and forty-eighth morning. Four hundred and ninety-eight days after the settlement. The fever eighty-seven weeks and six days old. Four hundred and fifty-two days since the division. Three hundred and ninety-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh. That was the twenty-ninth time. The man of forty-three gave the hour, the count and the number of the time, and gave nothing else, and said out loud why there was nothing else.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has said on three of the six off-mornings in nineteen years that there is no fifth thing.

“It came off at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps are walkable now, and that is the twenty-ninth time.”

“There is no figure of inches this morning, and no figure of steps under, and no figure of days of the coming back. That is not a gap in my morning.”

“Nineteen years of getting up at the fifth hour on every one of these has not once given me a fourth thing to say, and I am not going to invent one this morning, and about four of you would not know the difference if I did.”

“I said the whole of what I have to say about that on the third day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, in about nine of your ears, and it was the best thing I have ever said on a step and I am not going to say it again. It is said. That is what said means.”

Then a man of thirty-one offered to carry the figure four hundred miles to the woman who washes lime, and was refused in a mouth that gave the working again, out loud, on an off-morning.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and forty-four days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and thirteen days old.

“Three hundred and forty-four days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and thirteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and ninety-eight days old, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Thirty-eight wrong things in thirty-eight mornings, and here is the thirty-eighth. The leaves those stitches stand for were cut out of a book and used. She is the only person in this county who goes into other people’s rooms for a living and does not knock.”

“I will carry it to her. I will carry it four hundred miles and I will not say the number in about nine of her neighbours’ ears, I will say it in a kitchen at a table, and then if there is a wall in this county with paper laid in the plaster, she knows where to start and she has a figure to start with.”

“No, and I am going to give you the working again because it is a Friday and three of my four things have nothing to be about, and that is what a morning like this one is for.”

“You cannot carry it to me. There is no version of carrying a figure of stitches to a lime-washer, and the reason there is no version is that it is not a thing anybody can carry. It is the place where something was taken out and used.”

“And the looking is not yours to hand me, and it is not mine to hand you, and I am not going to say how many walls there are, and I am not going to say how many days that would take, because I said that on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, and handing you a figure of miles now that it is easier is not a thing I am going to do.”

“It is eleven days of my working and that is what it is, and it may not be done alone, and there has to be a person with me who is allowed to say stop and who goes home at night to a different house. You offered to be that on the fifth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and I refused you and I am refusing you now in a different shape and it is the same no.”

“You are not going to be the second person and you are not going to be the first person, and a figure of stitches does not make you either of them, and I would rather you had not come up that road this week at all and I have not said that to anybody before you and I am saying it now.”

Hannah Voss is forty-one and washes lime, and has said five pieces of a working across three mornings, and has been asked for a person to stand with her on those eleven days and has not named one.

Then the woman of forty-four said out loud, on an off-morning, in about nine ears, what three of the nine positions are, and did not answer them, and said she was not going to.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. Nobody on this landing has ever stood in the room where the arrangement answers.

“No. I am not going to answer it and I am not going to help anybody answer it, and I am going to say so on a morning when three of the four figures a man of forty-three gives have nothing to be about, because that is the morning nobody is in a hurry.”

“What three of the nine positions are is still open. It has been open since the Saturday of the hundred and forty-second week and it is open this morning, and everything said on this landing this week has left it exactly where it was, and a man of thirty-eight is going to ask me for the first half of it for the fifth time before the light goes off and he will get the same sentence with the day on it.”

“Knowing which of the nine a person can be shown is not knowing what the other three are. That is all I have. It has not moved and it is not going to move because I am on an off-morning and I have got time to think about it.”

“And I am the ninth. I am a building where paper is kept and there is no stone at the end of my walk. A man of about fifty-two can put a hand on six of them and twice on one thing. He has said he would rather say so than have about four of you assume the rest, and he was right to and he is not going to be thanked for it.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and seventy-seventh morning, and said why a man of fifty-four stays longer on an off-morning than on any other.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and seventy-seven mornings, which is eight hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and seventy-seven. And all ninety of those steps are dry and there is nothing to do on any of them, and that is the morning I stay longest.”

“Every other morning I stay because I have said a number and about four of them have stood here while I said it. On this morning there is nothing to say, and about four of them stayed anyway, and that is not for me and I am not going to take it.”

“And I have been holding sixty-three since yesterday afternoon in a room with the door shut and I have not said it and I am not going to, and there is a difference between holding a figure and being given a figure and this is the first one that has ever been the second.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and forty-five days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and forty-five is eight hundred and ninety-eight less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and forty-five days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-seven mornings, and I said a figure out loud yesterday in a room and it is still the same figure today, and I have not said it again and I am not going to.”

“And a man of thirty-one has just offered to carry it four hundred miles and a woman of forty-one has said no, and about four of you looked at me while he was saying it. I am not going to be the one who carries it anywhere and I never was.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the twenty-ninth off-morning, and did not put a day on anything else.

“That is the forty-first morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering, and it does not answer, and nobody on this landing is in the room where it answers, and nobody was in it this morning and nobody was asked to go and I did not ask anybody to go.”

All ninety of those steps were dry and there was nothing on any of them to be about, and there was a number in about five ears on that landing that was in no book and on no paper and had not left the county, and the man of thirty-one was still on the step where he had been since the fifth hour.
