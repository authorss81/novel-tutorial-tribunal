# Chapter 867: First Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Sixth Week, And A Tuesday, And About Four Inches On The Top Step With Seven Days Of The Coming Back And About Ninety Of Those Steps Under, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read Says Out Loud That The Ordinary Form He Said Last Saturday Does Not Reach Him And Says What His Line Is, And A Man Of Thirty-One Makes A Rule Out Of A Thing A Man Of About Fifty-Two Cannot Check And Is Refused By Both Of Them

First day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and seventeenth morning. Four hundred and sixty-seven days after the settlement. The fever eighty-three weeks and three days old. Four hundred and twenty-one days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About four inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was seven days of the coming back. About ninety of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the figure before the inches and said nothing about the order at all.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and he says the working underneath it out loud about as often as he says the figures.

“*About ninety of those ninety steps under, four inches on the top step at the seventh hour, and seven days of the coming back.*”

“*And I am not going to say anything about the order this morning. I said on the Sunday that I was finished with the order and I am, and a man who says a thing and then does it for four more days has not said a thing.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working and it has not moved. Sixteen is a full bank and a full bank holds. That is three sentences and I have given them every morning for nineteen years and about four of you could say them with me now and about four of you have never once tried.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read said out loud that the ordinary form he had said on the Saturday of last week does not reach him, and said what his line is, in about nine of your ears, and gave the working.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-six mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. On the Saturday of the hundred and forty-fourth week he said out loud, in about nine of your ears, that a run with a hole in its beginning is still entered on today.

“*A hundred and forty-six, and I said it five days running now and about four of you have stopped writing it down.*”

“*And I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since Saturday, and it is that the thing I said does not reach me.*”

“*Mine has no hole in it. It is a whole book, and it has a leaf for every day, and there is not one leaf torn out of it and there is not one page cut and there is not one stitch gone. A woman of forty-nine can put her hand on the front of hers and feel where the thread stops. There is nowhere on mine to put a hand and feel that, because nothing has stopped.*”

“*Not one line has ever been entered on one leaf of it either, and that is not the same as a hole, and I have known that since the Sunday of the hundred and forty-third week and I have not said it in about nine of your ears until this morning.*”

“*A hole is where a leaf has been taken out. There is no leaf gone from that book and there never was one gone. So what I said on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-fourth week about a run with a hole in the beginning is about her book and not about mine, and about four of you put the two together on that Saturday and I am not going to let it stand another day.*”

“*And here is the part I have been holding. If nothing is gone from it, then there is nothing on it to go and look for. There is no leaf to find and no thread to follow and no hole to say how deep it went. I have asked for a day on that line for eleven years because I have been sure for eleven years that somebody had taken something off it.*”

“*Nobody has ever gone and looked. I have just worked out why, and it is that there is nothing to look for, and I have been the only man in this county who thought otherwise.*”

Then a man of thirty-one made a rule out of the fourteenth thing a man of about fifty-two cannot check, and it was refused by the man of about fifty-two and then by the man of fifty-four, in that order, and neither of them gave the same reason.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He named a fourteenth thing he cannot check on the third day of this week. It is that a leaf and a day are the same number, and he named it on the day he said he would.

“*You have taken the fourteenth and put a rule on it. A leaf and a day are the same number, and therefore a position may be looked at from the day the thread starts, and not before it. That is the sentence you said on the Monday and it has got my thing inside it and it did not have it on Monday.*”

“*No, and here is why. I said a leaf and a day are the same number and I said I cannot check it. You have heard the half I can say and thrown away the half I cannot, and a rule about a thing nobody can check is a thing four people can be wrong about in four different ways.*”

“*And I am not the only one refusing it. Listen to the man who cannot read, because he has just told you that your rule is about the wrong book.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirteen days, three hundred and thirteen being eight hundred and sixty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-two days old, a hundred and eighty-two being eight hundred and sixty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then I have made seven wrong things in seven days and about four of you have watched six of them, and the seventh one was the only one that would have travelled.*”

“*I have been building a rule out of the parts of a week that come out right. That is what I am for and I have been doing it since the Wednesday of last week, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read has just told me the rule is about somebody else entirely.*”

“*Three hundred and thirteen days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-two days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-seven days old, and a hundred and sixty-seven is eight hundred and sixty-seven less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I am not going to build a rule out of it either, and that is the only thing I have said out loud this week that I am sure of.*”

Then the man of fifty-four who cannot read refused it again in about nine of your ears, and gave a reason that was about himself and not about the rule.

“*No. And I am not refusing you because a man of about fifty-two has already refused you. I am refusing you because I cannot read and you want me to be in a rule.*”

“*You have made a rule about a line on a page four hundred miles off and you have not once asked whether the man who has been asking about that line for eleven years can find out what a rule says about it.*”

“*If you had asked me that on Monday I would have said yes, and it would have cost me nothing, and about four of you would have written Monday down.*”

“*I am going to keep asking for the day on the hundred and forty-seventh morning, and the form is not my ask and I am not going to use it, and I am not going to be in a rule about a page I have never seen.*”

At about the tenth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run and gave the figure of mornings out loud, and a boy of thirteen said what the week had been for.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years, most of them with nobody standing there.

“*That is the tenth morning in front of anybody, and I am giving the figure because a man of fifty-four has just told this landing that a figure is the only thing any of us has got. Ten is a figure of mornings. It is not a figure of the front of my book and it is not a figure of anybody’s patience.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fourteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fourteen is eight hundred and sixty-seven less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and fourteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And a week turned in the night. That is the first day of the hundred and forty-sixth week and it is not a Saturday and there is no figure of the fever that comes out whole on it, and I checked that in the lane and I am giving it because about four of you would not.*”

“*I am still keeping the count I have not been asked for. It is six mornings and I have decided that I am going to be asked for it eventually, and I am not going to say anything about when.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Tuesday with about four inches on the top step, and a rule that had been built out of a thing nobody could check and taken apart again in about nine of your ears by two people who wanted opposite things from it.

---

About four inches on the top step with seven days of the coming back and about ninety of those steps under. The man of forty-three gave the figure before the inches and said nothing about the order at all, because he said on Sunday that he was finished with the order.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read said out loud that the ordinary form he said last Saturday does not reach him. His line has no hole in it. It is a whole book with a leaf for every day, and not one leaf torn out, and not one stitch gone, and not one line ever entered on any leaf. A hole is where a leaf has been taken out, and no leaf has been taken out of that book and none ever was. So what he said on Saturday was about a woman of forty-nine’s book and not his own.

And he said the part he had been holding: if nothing is gone from it then there is nothing on it to go and look for. No leaf to find, no thread to follow, no hole to say how deep it went. He has asked for a day on that line for eleven years because he has been sure for eleven years that somebody took something off it, and nobody has ever gone and looked, and he has just worked out why.

A man of thirty-one made a rule out of the fourteenth thing a man of about fifty-two cannot check, and was refused by the man of about fifty-two, because a rule about a thing nobody can check is a thing four people can be wrong about in four different ways. And then he was refused by the man of fifty-four, who cannot read, and would not be in a rule about a page he has never seen.

A woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run and gave the figure as a figure of mornings. A boy of thirteen said a week turned in the night and said there is no figure of the fever that comes out whole on it, and that he checked.
