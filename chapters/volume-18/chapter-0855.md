# Chapter 855: Third Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fourth Week, And A Thursday, And Sixteen Inches Holding With Three Days Of The Coming Back, And A Woman Of Forty-Nine Comes Down From Four Hundred Miles Off With A Bound Run Of Day-Leaves That Has A Gap In The Stitching At The Front Of It, And Two People On That Landing Have Counted The Stitches And Neither Of Them Will Say The Figure, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Destroyed And Is Asked To Take It Back

Third day of the hundred and forty-fourth week. His six hundred and fifth morning. Four hundred and fifty-five days after the settlement. The fever eighty-one weeks and five days old. Four hundred and nine days since the division. Three hundred and fifty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank held at sixteen over the top step and it was three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave both figures and said the word holds out loud, because about four of them had been waiting three days to see whether he would.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and about sixty of them are under, and it is the second morning of a full bank, and he has given the figure four mornings running.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back, and it holds. It is the third morning it has held at that figure and I have said the word three times and I am not going to apologise for the number.*”

“*And about sixty of those ninety steps are under, the same figure as yesterday, and the water has not moved a leaf’s width since the seventh hour yesterday. A full bank is the only thing in this county that is honest about being still.*”

At about the eighth hour a woman of forty-nine got down off a public cart with a bundle of about four inches on her. She set it on the counter herself, and she named herself before anybody asked her anything.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine. She has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and she keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. She came down on the ordinary cart because she would not have it carried.

“*Sena Dorr. Forty-nine. And I am the ninth, and the ninth is a building where paper is kept, and there is no stone at the end of my walk.*”

“*That is the run. It is a bound sequence of day-leaves, a leaf for every day, and the person holding the seat writes that day’s work on that day’s leaf in one line and nothing else. It is not the ninth line. I will come back to that, because about four of you have already got the two of them mixed up and one of you has mixed them up out loud.*”

“*The gap is at the front. Somebody drew the threads out and took the leaves away and used them. I have the stubs and I have the thread and I have the holes, and the holes are the stitch-holes and the leaves are not on them.*”

“*I am not going to say how many, in about nine of your ears, this morning or any morning this week, and that is not a mystery. It is a count, and I have made it, and a person who has read for a living for twenty-three years does not need a crowd to do arithmetic.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said what he could and could not put a line under about that bundle, and named it as the thirteenth thing he cannot check. He said the woman had named herself before he got there, that he had not named her, and that nobody was to say he had.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a palm and a forearm, and named a thirteenth thing he cannot check on the Thursday of last week. A fortnight from that Thursday is the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and he has seven days of it left.

“*She walked down off that cart and said her own name and her own age at about nine of you before I said anything. That is the thirteenth thing, and I did not name her, and nobody is to say that I did.*”

“*What I can put a line under. There is a gap at the front of it. The stitching at the front is drawn and the thread is in the bundle. There are stitch-holes on that edge with nothing on them, and that is a thing anybody could check with a hand and about four of you have.*”

“*What I cannot put a line under. Whether the leaves that are not there are the leaves this run is missing, or whether somebody has been in the middle of it. There is no way on this landing to tell one from the other, and I have looked.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said he had counted the stitches in that gap and was not going to give the count, and gave the reason in about nine ears. A man of fifty-four asked for it and was given the same refusal from a woman of forty-nine who has not given it once yet.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and two days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and one is eight hundred and fifty-five less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And here is the thing I have to say before anybody asks me, so that nobody spends a morning trying to get it out of me. I have counted the stitches in that gap. I counted them on Tuesday in the lane, twice, and got the same both times.*”

“*And I am not going to give the count this morning, and I have been asked for it about four times, and I would like about nine of you to have heard the reason and not the number.*”

“*Because a number that a boy of thirteen says out loud on a Thursday in about nine ears has got to a room four hundred miles off by the Saturday. And there is a woman standing next to that bundle who has been keeping it for twenty-three years and not one person has ever asked her how she counts it.*”

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel there, cannot read, and has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and thirty-four mornings, which is eight hundred and fifty-five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*Then I am going to ask you for it anyway, and I am going to say why, and it is the first time in about nine days I have wanted a thing on this bank that I could have had.*”

“*I have been asking for a day on a book four hundred miles off for a hundred and thirty-three mornings, and a figure of stitches is the first thing anybody has put in front of me this week that somebody counted themselves.*”

“*And I am not asking you to tell me. I am asking you to say that you counted it. You have said that. I am going on Monday and I am going to ask for the count, and about four of you can go with me or not.*”

Sena Dorr, forty-nine, said the boy’s reason was better than the man of fifty-four’s, and did not give the count. She said she was not going to break a refusal a person of thirteen had got to first.

“*The boy is right and I am not going to overrule him with a thing of my own. Two people on this landing have now counted the same gap and neither of them is going to say the figure, and that is a figure of two refusals and not a figure of anybody.*”

“*If I say the number, then the number is the thing and the gap is a thing that somebody knows the size of. I have kept that gap for twenty-three years as a thing I know the size of, and it has not been the size of anything I have said out loud in about nine ears.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the word destroyed, and a woman of forty-nine told him it was the wrong word and gave him the working of why. He took it back, and the cost of it was that about four of them now knew he has a word he reaches for when he has not got a thing.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and one days, three hundred and one being eight hundred and fifty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seventy days old, a hundred and seventy being eight hundred and fifty-five less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*It was destroyed. I am not going to unsay that I said it, and I would rather it were the wrong word in about nine of your ears than the right one about four hundred miles off.*”

Sena Dorr, forty-nine, said it once, said she was not going to say it again, and said that a man who has only one word for a thing will use it on the wrong thing by the end of a week.

“*Destroyed is a word for a thing that had a whole and does not have it now. Mine had a whole. Somebody took the front of it and used it, and taking is not the same as destroying, and there is a difference in the two words and one of them is a fact and the other one is a feeling.*”

“*And I would like the right one in about nine of your ears. It is a figure of holes in a stitch-edge. Take the other one back and do not use it again this week.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, said he was going to have to be more careful with the words he owns, and said the want he had out loud in about nine ears before anybody could be angry about the word.

“*Then it is gone, and I am not going to use it again this week. I have been in this county three hundred and one days and I have been nobody’s for all of them, and eight hundred and fifty-five less five hundred and fifty-four is three hundred and one.*”

“*The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy days old, and eight hundred and fifty-five less six hundred and eighty-five is a hundred and seventy, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And here is the want, and I would rather say it with a bad word still drying than wait a day to say it well. I want you to stay nine days.*”

Sena Dorr, forty-nine, said no in one sentence, and used a sentence that a man of about fifty-two had given to about nine of them on Wednesday morning, and said she was using his because it was his and not hers.

“*No. Nine days is how long it takes a description to outgrow a thing, and a man of about fifty-two told that to about nine of you on Wednesday. You have been carrying his sentence around since then as if it were yours.*”

“*I have a room with nine clerks in it four hundred miles off and about nine of the nine of us have worked there for years. You are asking me to give that up on a Thursday because you want a run on a bank in about nine ears. I am not going to do it, and I am not going to be thanked for the no.*”

“*And I am not going to be told I was right to say it. Both of those are the same sentence and I have heard the first one too many times this year.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with sixteen inches holding, a bundle of about four inches on the counter with a gap in the stitching at the front of it, and two people who had counted it and were not saying.
