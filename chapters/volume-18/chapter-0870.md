# Chapter 870: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Sixth Week, And A Friday, And Sixteen Inches And Four Days Of The Coming Back And A Full Bank, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Is Going To Stop Saying The Figure Of The Standing Offer Out Loud And Is Refused By A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Puts Her Question Down For The Fourth Time, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Says He Is Going To Ask On Monday For A Thing He Has Not Asked For

Fourth day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and twentieth morning. Four hundred and seventy days after the settlement. The fever eighty-three weeks and six days old. Four hundred and twenty-four days since the division. Three hundred and seventy days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches came up on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank again, with four days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave both figures, said the word holds out loud, and said the word full out loud, and said why he said both.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and this is the third full bank in three weeks and he has not apologised for any of the three.

“*Sixteen at the seventh hour, four days of the coming back, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and that is a full bank and it holds.*”

“*I am saying both words on purpose this morning. About four of you have written the word full against days I have not said it and I am not going to go back through a book to find out which days those were. A bank is full at sixteen and holds until it comes off, and those are two different facts and about nine of you have been treating them as one.*”

“*And I am not going to apologise for the third full bank in three weeks. It is four inches a day and the four inches have not moved and about four of you would like them to.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that he was going to stop saying the figure of the standing offer in about nine of your ears, and said his reason, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read refused him the whole of it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and sixteen days, three hundred and sixteen being eight hundred and seventy less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-five days old, a hundred and eighty-five being eight hundred and seventy less six hundred and eighty-five. The standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and seventy days old, and a hundred and seventy is eight hundred and seventy less seven hundred.

“*A hundred and seventy, and that is the figure, and it is unanswered, and I have been saying it out loud in about nine of your ears every morning since the first day of this week without once asking it.*”

“*And I am going to stop. That is the tenth of them in ten days and it is the only one I have got to choose, and I am choosing it, and here is the reason.*”

“*I have said that figure nine mornings and nobody has answered it and nobody has asked it, and about four of you have got used to hearing it, and a thing a person says out loud until it stops meaning anything is not a thing anybody is keeping. It is a habit of mine. I would rather stop than go on being a habit on a landing full of people who can count.*”

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-nine mornings, which is eight hundred and seventy less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*No. And I am refusing you in about nine of your ears and about four of you are not going to hear it and that is the correct number to not hear a man explain why he is refusing a thing.*”

“*You say it out loud and you do not ask it. That is what I have been doing with my figure for eleven years and you have watched me do it and you have not once worked out that I learned it off you.*”

“*A figure that is said out loud is the only thing keeping it. In about four weeks there will be no man on this landing who knows how old that offer is, not because anybody took it off a page but because a man of thirty-one got tired of saying a number, and then it is not old at all. It is just gone out.*”

“*I have a hundred and forty-nine mornings behind me and I could not tell you that figure if nobody said it to me every day. I would say fifty. I would say a hundred. I would say a lot of things and about four of them would be wrong and nobody would ever know which four.*”

“*So you are going to keep saying it out loud, and you are not going to ask it, and I am not going to be thanked by either of us for the other one.*”

Then a woman of forty-four put her question down for the fourth time, unchanged, with the working, and said what she was going home to do on Saturday, and said whose name she was not going to use.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Eleven of those chairs have a name against them in her book and nobody in them, and on the third day of last week she named two of those two as dead.

“*The question, for the fourth time, and I am not changing one word of it and about four of you can check that because it is in a book of mine and not in a mouth.*”

“*Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it. And is that day the same as a day put down at the time.*”

“*Nobody has answered it. A woman of forty-nine has told us what a page is. A man of fifty-four cannot read. A boy of thirteen counts and will not be made into a clerk. None of those is an answer to it and about four of you have been treating the first one as a settlement.*”

“*I am going home on Saturday up that cart road and I am going to open my book and do the same thing I have done for nineteen years, and that is not a decision and nobody is going to call it one.*”

“*And there is a woman of twenty-eight who has been four miles up that road and gone back down it, who cannot be sent for in this county or in the one she came from, and who has said no twice. Her own count is two hundred and twenty-eight days, and that is eight hundred and seventy less six hundred and forty-two, and I am not going to use her name this morning to make a fourth thing out of a fourth question.*”

“*She said no twice. The correct number of times to say no to a person you owe something to is the number of times she says it, and not once more because I am going home on Saturday.*”

At about the ninth hour a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and forty-ninth morning, and said what he is going to do on Monday, and did not say what it is.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-nine mornings.

“*A hundred and forty-nine. A man of thirty-eight worked a day out in his own head a while back and asked a man of about fifty-two to check it, and the man of about fifty-two checked it and it was right. That day was the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-second week. It was not mine and I have not said so this week and about four of you have not asked me either.*”

“*A man of thirty-one is not going to stop saying that figure out loud and I am glad of it and nobody is going to be thanked. A woman of forty-four is going home on Saturday to do the same thing she has done for nineteen years and I am not going to say one word about it because it is hers.*”

“*And on Monday I am going to ask a person for a thing I have not asked for. I am not going to tell you what it is this morning and I do not know what it is this morning. That is the honest shape of it and about four of you have been waiting for me to produce it and you are going to have to wait until Monday.*”

“*Eleven years of asking for a day. If Monday produces nothing then I am asking for a day on Tuesday and a day on Wednesday, and about four of you will have been disappointed by a man of fifty-four on a Monday, and that is the correct number of people to be disappointed by.*”

At about the tenth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run, and a man of about fifty-two said there would not be a fifteenth, and a boy of thirteen gave his own count and said what he was not going to be asked for.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years, and on the seventh and last day of last week she said a sentence about a page in about nine of your ears.

“*That is the thirteenth morning in front of anybody. It is a figure of mornings. I said on Monday that a position that is not a stone is a day somebody did the work, and I entered on today, and nobody is going to thank me for that either.*”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and named a fourteenth thing he cannot check on the third day of the hundred and forty-fifth week. It is that a leaf and a day are the same number.

“*There will not be a fifteenth. That is the end of my naming and it is the end of the fortnight I said I had, and on the Thursday of last week I named the fourteenth and I did not say I would look for another, and I have not looked.*”

“*A fourteenth is a figure of a thing. A fifteenth would be a figure of things I am getting worse at, and about four of you have been counting them since Tuesday and you can stop, because there is no fifteenth and there is not going to be one on Saturday.*”

“*If anybody wants a fifteenth, they can go and find their own. I am about fifty-two years old and I have fourteen and they are all still true and I am not going to improve them by adding one.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and seventeen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and seventeen is eight hundred and seventy less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and seventeen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one has just said there will not be a fifteenth, and I want to say that I am the only person on that landing who has been keeping a figure that nobody has asked him for, and about four of you have worked out that by now, and it is nine mornings, and I am still not going to say it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for any of it. I have said that on about nine mornings of this week and this is about the ninth and I am still counting, and a man of fifty-four is going to ask somebody for something on Monday and I am not going to ask him what it is either.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Friday with a full bank over the top step and four days of the coming back, and a man of thirty-one who had been refused by the man whose figure he had offered to stop saying, and a man of fifty-four who was going to ask for something on Monday and did not say what.
