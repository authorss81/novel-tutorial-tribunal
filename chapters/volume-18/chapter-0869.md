# Chapter 869: Third Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Sixth Week, And A Thursday, And About Twelve Inches On The Top Step With Five Days Of The Coming Back And About Seventy Of Those Steps Under, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asks The First Half Of His Question And Is Given The Same Sentence In The Same Mouth With The Day It Was Said On, And A Boy Of Thirteen Counts A Thing Again In A Lane On A Thursday And Does Not Say It

Third day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and nineteenth morning. Four hundred and sixty-nine days after the settlement. The fever eighty-three weeks and five days old. Four hundred and twenty-three days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About twelve inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was five days of the coming back. About seventy of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave both figures and gave the working, and said the working was the thing and the two figures were a morning.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and the working underneath all four inches a day has not moved in his lifetime.

“*About seventy of those ninety steps under, about twelve inches on the top step, five days of the coming back, and it is not yet a full bank and I am going to stop saying that every morning until it is one.*”

“*Four inches a day. That is the working and it has not moved since the first day of this century, and it is the reason a figure of inches is the only figure on this landing that has never once been wrong.*”

“*And the next one is in five days, and that is the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-seventh week. I have said the day out loud twice now, once on an off-morning and once on a Thursday, and about four of you have stopped writing it down, which I notice and am not going to correct.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight asked the first half of his question, in about nine of your ears, and was given the same sentence in the same mouth, with the day it was said on, and was told it was not a discovery.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and there is a board with thirty-two notches on the edge of it in the inside of his coat, which is a figure of what is on a board. He asked what three of the nine positions are and who put them there on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-third week, and was not answered.

“*Then I am going to ask the first half. What the three are. I have had the second half since the Sunday of the hundred and forty-fourth week and I have been sitting on the first half for every morning since. Eight hundred and sixty-nine less eight hundred and fifty-eight is eleven. Eleven is a figure of days and not a figure of weeks and I am not going to call it three of these weeks because it is not.*”

“*I have been sitting on it because I wanted to be asked something I could answer. That is the whole of it. I did not want the thing. I wanted to be the man who was told.*”

“*So that is me done with that, and here is the first half, and about nine of you have heard it.*”

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years. On the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week she said, in about nine of your ears, that the three blanks were never blanks, and gave the working in four pieces.

“*You are owed the answer to that and I am not going to give it to you this morning. I am going to give you the answer to the other thing you asked and the day I said it on, so that nobody can tell you it is new.*”

“*On the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, which is a Monday, I said an index is a list of what a person can be shown, and that a list has got one thing it can say about a position, and that the one thing is whether there is a thing to go and stand in front of.*”

“*That is the working for your second half and it is exact and it is about a page. I said it on Monday in about nine of your ears. I have said it to you twice since, on Wednesday, and I am saying it again this morning, and it is the same sentence and it is not progress and nobody is going home to tell about four people that a man of thirty-eight got further today.*”

“*What the three are, is a different question. And a page cannot answer it and I have told you why and I am not going to invent a way round my own working.*”

Garrin Tolley took the first half back. He said what he was going to do with it, and said he was not going to be thanked for asking it again.

“*Then I have asked it twice and been given the same thing twice and that is what a record does, and I have been on a cart road and in a room for eleven days with a thing in my head and I did not know that is what a record is.*”

“*I am going to go on asking it. Not because I think about nine of you will change, and not because I have got a better way of asking it. Because a man of thirty-eight who stops asking after two goes home and tells himself there was never a question, and there was.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked for me asking it a third time, and I am not going to be told it is progress, and about four of you are not going to write down that a man of thirty-eight said the word record out loud on that landing.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that a man of sixty-one leaving three positions blank on purpose meant nobody had filled them, and a woman of forty-four asked him for the working.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fifteen days, three hundred and fifteen being eight hundred and sixty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-four days old, a hundred and eighty-four being eight hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then it is answered. A man of sixty-one read the index out on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-third week and left three of the nine blank on purpose, and nobody has filled them since, and so the three have never been filled by anybody.*”

“*That is a sentence with no working in it and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Leave a line blank on purpose and the line stays blank for a day, and a line is a day, and a day is one day.*”

“*What has been left blank for a fortnight is a different thing from what has been left blank for a day, and I have put the two together because I wanted the sentence to come out. That is the ninth wrong thing in these nine days and it is the first one I have got from somebody else’s mouth rather than from my own arithmetic.*”

“*Three hundred and fifteen days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-four days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-nine days old, and a hundred and sixty-nine is eight hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and it will be a hundred and seventy in the morning and a hundred and seventy-one the day after, and I am not going to ask it on either of those mornings.*”

Orla Fennimore said he could not go home and put it back the way it was. She gave the figure of a man in an ash out loud, and said what she is owed and is not getting.

“*You could go home and put it back the way it was. About four of you have watched a man of thirty-one pick things up again for a fortnight and every single one of them was a sentence. A figure of a line left blank for a fortnight is not the same thing and you cannot go home and put a fortnight back.*”

“*There is a man of thirty-four in an ash down the low side of that landing and he has been in it two hundred and thirty-nine days, which is eight hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and thirty. He has a day against his name in a book nobody four hundred miles off can read, and he made himself computable with a pencil on the Friday of the hundred and forty-third week.*”

“*And he is not standing on this stone this morning and nobody has asked him anything and I am not going to ask him anything on his behalf, because a man of thirty-four was asked about a bridge four hundred yards off and said yes out loud, and that was his, and this is not.*”

“*What I am owed is a piece of paper and a practice and I am not getting either of them this week. A day entered on a piece of paper in my own hand binds nobody and protects nobody. I have said that three times and I am going to say it once more in about nine of your ears and then I am going home on Saturday to do the same thing I have done for nineteen years.*”

A man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and forty-eighth morning, and said the thing he has been saying for a week about what he will not do on Saturday.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-eight mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and forty-eight, and I have said that figure six days running and about four of you have stopped asking me to slow down, which I have noticed.*”

“*And about four of you want to know what I am going to do on Saturday. I am going to hold the gravel and I am going to say that figure, and that is all of it, and I am telling you now so that on Saturday you cannot say a man of fifty-four kept something back.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine has a gap in the front of hers and I have none in mine, and that is the difference, and I have worked it out in about nine of your ears this week and I am not going to work it out again.*”

At about the tenth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run, and a boy of thirteen said he had counted a thing again in a lane on a Thursday and was not going to say it, and gave the same reason he gave on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fourth week.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions and has entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years.

“*That is the twelfth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings and I have not said anything else about it all week and I am not going to start on a Thursday.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and sixteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and sixteen is eight hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and sixteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And I counted a thing again this morning. I counted it in the lane before the light came up and I got the same as I got on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fourth week, and I am not going to say what it is, and the reason is the reason I gave you on that Thursday and it has not stopped being the reason.*”

“*A figure said out loud on a Thursday has got four hundred miles by the Saturday. Nobody has ever asked a woman of forty-nine how she counts it. That is not a new reason because I have had a week to think of a new one and I have not found one and about four of you can stop waiting for me to find one.*”

“*And nobody asked me for the count I have been keeping. It is eight mornings. I am going to go on keeping it and I am going to go on not being asked.*”
“*That is the only thing on this landing this week that has not been made into a sentence by somebody, and about four of you would find that worse than a figure.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with about twelve inches on the top step.
A man of thirty-eight had asked the first half twice and been told the same thing twice on purpose. A boy had counted a thing again and would not say it, and did not give a new reason.

---

About twelve inches on the top step with five days of the coming back and about seventy of those steps under, and the man of forty-three gave the working, which is four inches a day, and said it is the reason a figure of inches has never once been wrong.

A man of thirty-eight asked the first half of his question, after saying out loud that he had been sitting on it for eleven days because he wanted to be asked something he could answer.
He was given the same sentence in the same mouth, with the day it was said on. An index is a list of what a person can be shown, and it can tell you only whether there is a thing to go and stand in front of.
It was said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week and twice since, and it is not progress. What the three are is a different question, and a page cannot answer it.

A man of thirty-one said it was answered, because a man of sixty-one left three positions blank on purpose and nobody has filled them. A woman of forty-four said that was a sentence with no working in it, that he could pick a sentence up again and put it back and he could not put a fortnight back, and gave the figure of a man of thirty-four in an ash out loud, two hundred and thirty-nine days, with the subtraction.

A boy of thirteen counted a thing again in a lane on a Thursday, got the same as last Thursday, and gave the same reason, and said nobody has ever asked a woman of forty-nine how she counts it. He has been keeping a count for eight mornings that nobody has asked him for.

A man of fifty-four asked for a day for the hundred and forty-eighth morning and said he is going to hold the gravel and say his figure on Saturday, and told about four of them now so that they cannot say he kept something back.
