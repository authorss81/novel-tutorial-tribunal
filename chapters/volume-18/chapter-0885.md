# Chapter 885: Eleven Days, And A Refusal With The Working Given

Fifth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Four hundred and eighty-five days after the settlement. The fever eighty-six weeks old. Four hundred and thirty-nine days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Twelve inches came up on the top step at about the seventh hour and about seventy of those ninety steps were under. That was five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and said that it would be a full bank on the last day of the week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gave four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour of a Saturday on which the fever comes out at a whole number of weeks, and he has given that figure on every seventh Saturday for nineteen years and has never once added to it.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, five days of the coming back, and about seventy of those ninety steps under, and it is four inches higher than it stood at the seventh hour on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week.”

“It will be a full bank on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. Sixteen, a full bank, four days of the coming back, about sixty of those ninety steps under. I am naming that morning now, on a Saturday, with the day and the week on it.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked to carry the looking four hundred miles, and offered his own working days for it, and was refused in a mouth with the working given, and was not told he was right.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-one days. The cut across that palm is two hundred days old.

“Twenty-five wrong things in twenty-five mornings. Three hundred and thirty-one days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-five days old, and I have not asked it.”

“I am going to ask for the thing and I am going to ask in front of about nine of you, because that is the only way it counts.”

“Four hundred miles. I will go. I will walk it and I will go from house to house and I will take down what is in the walls and I will bring it here, and I will not come back until it is finished or until there is nothing left to take down.”

“And here is what I am offering, and I want it entered. I have no work on this landing. I am nobody’s. Barnaby holds the gravel and a woman of forty-four holds the chairs and the man of forty-three holds the step. There is not one morning of the week that anybody has given me to do anything.”

“So take eleven mornings of mine. That is not a favour, that is the trade. I have got them and you have not, and a man of thirty-one walking four hundred miles is cheaper than a woman of forty-one on a ladder, and everybody on that bank knows it.”

Hannah Voss put the brush across the top of the bucket and gave the working in four pieces, and he was not told he was right.

“I am not going to say whether that was a good offer. I am going to tell you what you have just done, and then you can go away and decide about yourself for as long as you like.”

“No, and here is the working, in four pieces, and I am going to say all four because about four of you will take the first and stop.”

“One. It may not be done alone, and you are the only man in this county who has offered, so you are the last man here who can be asked. That is not a compliment. It is the shape of it.”

“Two. There are two jobs in it and they are not the same job. One of them is asking a stranger to let you take their wall down. The other one is reading what comes off it. A man who does the asking in the morning cannot do the reading in the afternoon, and a man who does both will do both badly and will not know which one he did badly.”

“Three. If you go alone and they refuse you at the third house, you come down that road with one no and no working, and I cannot use a no. A no is not a thing. And if they let you in and you take it down and you are alone with the bucket, then what was in that wall is in my fire by the Thursday and there is no record of it anywhere, because the only person who held it was holding it alone.”

“Four. So it is two people, and the two of them cannot be two halves of one person, and a man who offers his mornings is offering to be two people, and he does not get to be two people. That is not me refusing your offer. That is the offer not being the shape of the job.”

“And the cost, since somebody was going to ask for it and I would rather hand it over than have it taken out of me. It is eleven days of my working. Eleven. That is the whole figure and it is mine and it belongs to nobody on that bank.”

“It is a figure of working days and it is not a figure of miles, and there is no figure of miles on this landing, and I am not going to have one put beside it.”

Then a man of about fifty-two said, out loud, that about four of them had already done the arithmetic, and that the arithmetic was the mistake.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, and is the only man on that landing who can put a line under six of the nine positions and not under the other three.

“About four of you have already worked out how many days that is on foot. You have done it in your heads since she said four hundred miles and you did it before she said eleven.”

“Write it down. Go on. Put it on that step. Because the moment that journey has a figure of days in it, the first thing anybody did on it was turn a distance into a number of days, and that is the same shape as the column of leaves.”

“I have watched this happen on that bank for eleven days. A figure of leaves becomes a figure of days. A figure of houses becomes a figure of people. And now a figure of working days becomes a figure of days on a road.”

“A figure of days is a figure of days when a person knows what the day was worth. Nobody here knows what a day of a lime-washer’s working is worth, and that includes the woman standing in front of you.”

“And I am not going to say this is what I said on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week. It is not. It is a different sentence and I am not improving anything.”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read said what he had heard, and would not tell anybody whether the offer had been good.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and sixty-four mornings, which is eight hundred and eighty-five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and sixty-four. And I am going to say the one thing on that bank that nobody else is going to say, and then I am going to stop.”

“He has offered me things nine times since the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week. Nine. And I have said no nine times and about four of you know the shape of the nine and one of them was a man who cannot read and could not even work out what was being asked of him.”

“This morning he offered something I have not heard anybody offer. He put eleven mornings of his own on a bucket and gave them away, and it sounded to me like the best thing anybody has said on this landing in a fortnight.”

“I am not going to tell him it was a good offer. I do not know whether it was a good offer. She does not know whether it was a good offer, and she has just told him what it was instead of telling him that, and I am not going to be the mouth that says the other thing.”

“And I am not going to stop asking in the morning. A man of fifty-four cannot read has been at this for eleven years and I have never once been able to tell whether anybody has heard me, and I am not going to start guessing about it on a Saturday.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-two days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-two is eight hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-two days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-four mornings, and I am not saying what it is and I said on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week what kind of figure it is.”

“And nobody asked me to count anything this morning either. There was a figure about to be counted on that step and about four of you were waiting for me to be the one who did it, and a man of about fifty-two said no out loud before any of you could ask, and he was not looking at me when he said it.”

That Saturday the light went off the top of the step at the same hour it always did, and the man of thirty-one was still standing on the bank with an offer in his mouth that nobody had taken and nobody had called good, and eleven days of a woman who washes lime were now a figure everybody on that landing knew and none of them owned.
