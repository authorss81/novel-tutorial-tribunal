# Chapter 880: One Hundred And Eighty Days Old, And Unanswered

Seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. His six hundred and thirtieth morning. Four hundred and eighty days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and two days old. Four hundred and thirty-four days since the division. Three hundred and eighty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was two days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures, named the day on both sides of the boundary, and named the next off-morning with the week on it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off that one stone at about the seventh hour, and has not given a fifth thing on any morning of the last six days.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, three days of the coming back — no. Two days. I said three on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week and it is two today and I would rather catch it out loud than let anybody here carry Saturday’s figure into Monday.”

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, and it holds.”

“Today is the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. Tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and it is a Tuesday.”

“And the next off-morning is in two days, and that is the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, which is a Wednesday, and that is the twenty-seventh time. I have said that day four mornings running with the week on it. You have written it down four times, and some of you have written it down as the second day of the wrong week, which is exactly what a man of fifty-four said would happen on the third day of the hundred and forty-seventh week.”

Then a man of thirty-one tried to say the whole of the four ordinary runs as one sentence about the nine, and was given his own week back in order.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twenty-six days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ninety-five days old.

“Then here is the whole of it, and I have been carrying it since the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-fourth week and I am going to put it down in front of the bank on a Monday, because a Monday is the day you stop listening.”

“A run is a run. A ferry, a drain, a school, a gate. Those are the same kind of thing as the seventh and the eighth and the ninth, and they have no stone behind them and a hand-bell and a works and an archive have no stone behind them either. So the three that are on the page and not on the ground are the same kind of thing as four things on this bank that nobody has ever heard of.”

“And a record with a hole at the front of it can be relied on for the years it does cover, and that is what a man who keeps a boat said on the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. So the seven that can be checked and the three that cannot are the same and the only difference is that the rest of us have heard of seven of them.”

“Three hundred and twenty-six days in this county. The cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-five days old. Nobody has said whether it will keep.”

“And that is the twentieth wrong thing in twenty mornings, and I have been holding it for six days, and I am putting it down in front of the bank so that somebody can stop me.”

Sena Dorr said no, and gave him his own week back in order, in front of the bank.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine. For twenty-three years she has read for a living in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and she holds the ninth of the nine positions, and there is no stone at it.

“No. And I am going to do the thing I have done four times this fortnight and I am going to do it out loud, because half of you have noticed and half of you have not.”

“Wednesday of the hundred and forty-fourth week. You put four figures of leaves in a column and called the column six hundred and sixteen days. Thursday of the hundred and forty-sixth week. You told a man of thirty-one what to ask for. Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week. You said the ordinary form was the answer to a question of his. Saturday of the hundred and forty-seventh week. You said a run with nothing wrong with it was not a record, and then you said out loud that you wanted it to be true. Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week. You said other people could keep somebody else’s run.”

“That is six mornings and it is one shape. You keep putting a person in a list because a person in a list can be counted, and it is the same mistake five ways, and half of you have been correcting the shape instead of the thing.”

“Now the working, and I am going to give it as four pieces, and half of you will take the first one.”

“One. A ferry is not a stone. A gate is not a stone. A school is not a stone. A drain is not a stone. Not one of those four is on a list, and you cannot make them the same kind of thing as the three by noticing that they share a property, because a hand-bell and a works and an archive are things the arrangement put there on purpose and those four were never anybody’s to put anywhere.”

“Two. What is on a list is nine things. Three of them are things a list does not carry lines for. That is where this started, on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and it has not moved since and it is not moving this morning.”

“Three. What three of the nine positions are is still open. I am not answering it. It is not to be answered on a Monday by a man who has had a good week with four gates.”

“Four. A record with a hole at the front of it may be relied on for the years it does cover. That is true and a man who keeps a boat said it on the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-seventh week and it is the best thing anybody has said on this landing in a fortnight. And it is true of a ferry, and it is true of the ninth run for the years it does cover, and saying it about one of them is not saying it about the other, and you have just put them in one sentence, and half of you have written it down as a thing that covers everything.”

“You want that sentence because it lets you stop asking. That is what the sentence is for. I have been in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it for twenty-three years and I know what a convenient sentence looks like and you are holding one in both hands.”

Then the standing offer was a hundred and eighty days old, and four people tried to answer it, and it was not answered.

“A hundred and eighty. That is the figure, and it is unanswered. I have not asked it on any morning of this week and I am not going to ask it this morning, and every one of you has heard me give it on every one of those mornings.”

Garrin Tolley, thirty-eight, said that a figure which old ought to be let go, and was refused by the woman who keeps a day-book four hundred miles off.

“It is not yours to let go. You said that on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-sixth week, in a mouth, and you said you were going to go on asking it. Letting go is not asking. It is the other thing and it is quieter and it gets the same result.”

“And a figure that old is exactly the one that has to keep being said. That is the working and it is one sentence and I am not going to improve it on a Monday.”

Wat Marshe said that he could count it forward instead of counting it back, and was refused by the man the figure belongs to.

“Then you have taken it. That is what that is. You have taken the one count on that landing that the rest of us have all been using, and you are thirteen, and you would have had to carry it, and you would have carried it wrong inside a fortnight and not known.”

“And I know what that is, because I offered to stop saying my own out loud on the Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and you refused me, and nobody here has worked out that a refusal does not put the figure back anywhere.”

Rod Veal, forty-seven, said that nobody had ever asked to see it and so it could go, and was refused by a man of thirty-one.

“It is not going anywhere because nobody asked about it. That is a thing I said about a gate on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and half of you have turned it round and used it on a number. A thing nobody asked about is not a thing nobody is keeping. It is a thing nobody has checked.”

“And a man of fifty-four has been asking a woman of forty-nine for a day for eleven years and nobody has ever asked to see that either, and it is the oldest figure on that bank and it is not going quiet because of a gate.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fifty-ninth morning and said what he was going to do on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, and nobody offered him anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and fifty-nine mornings, which is eight hundred and eighty less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and fifty-nine. And a man of thirty-one has put his whole week on that stone in front of the bank and been given it back in order, and that was the correct thing for a woman of forty-nine to do, and half of you enjoyed it and I did not.”

“On Tuesday I am going to ask a woman of thirty-three whether she has found anything for me. Not whether she will write a line. Whether she has found anything. I am changing the question and half of you will say I have changed it to something easier, and I have, and I know that, and I am going to do it anyway and take what comes.”

“And I am going to keep asking for a day against the ninth line in the morning for as long as I am on this bank. Nobody is going to be thanked. Not her, not the woman of forty-four, not the man of forty-two — ”

He stopped. He had not said forty-two. He said the number he had in his mouth was not a number about anybody.

“Not the man of forty-three. And that is the ninth one since the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week, and every one of the nine is a number I had in my mouth that was not about the thing I was talking about, and not one of you heard this one, and I am the one who said it.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty-seven days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty-seven is eight hundred and eighty less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and twenty-seven days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is nineteen mornings, and you have all been leaving me alone about it for a week and I have noticed, and nobody is to be thanked for it, because being left alone is not the same as being asked and I have wanted to be asked since the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week.

“That is the twenty-third morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and it is on a Monday and I entered it, and today is not a gap.”

Orla Fennimore said what four people had said this week and what it had not settled, and gave her question for the fifth holding.

“A man who keeps a boat said a record with a hole at the front of it may be relied on for the years it does cover. A man who keeps a gate said a run of days is the only place that a thing happening every day lands. A woman of thirty-three said a leaf is a day on her run and is not a day on his. A woman of twenty-eight said no, twice, and then said the rest of why, and did not say a yes.”

“And her own count is two hundred and thirty-eight days, and she is four miles down that cart road and she entered a line this morning about a school and not about this bank, and half of you would have liked me to make a sentence out of that and I am not going to.”

“And my question is still my question and it is the fifth holding of it and not the fifth wording of it. Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it. And is that day the same as a day put down at the time.”

“Nobody has answered it. And half of you have spent this week waiting for somebody to walk up four hundred miles and tell me, and one of them said out loud on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week that nobody is to come up that road to look at what she does.”

“And I am not going to send anybody. If it is going to be answered it will be answered by somebody who goes on their own two feet and not by me sending a man of thirty-one four hundred miles with a look in his hands.”

That was the seventh and last morning of that week, and they were still on the step when the light went off the top of it, and nothing had been settled, and nobody had gone anywhere.
