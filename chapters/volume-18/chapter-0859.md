# Chapter 859: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fourth Week, And A Monday, And About Four Inches Over The Top Step With Seven Days Of The Coming Back And About Ninety Of Those Ninety Steps Under, And The Ninth Line And The Ninth Run Are Put Side By Side And Are Not The Same Book, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Is Asked To Write Down A True Thing And Says No And Says Why, And A Boy Of Thirteen Is Not Going To Be Made Into A Clerk

Seventh day of the hundred and forty-fourth week. His six hundred and ninth morning. Four hundred and fifty-nine days after the settlement. The fever eighty-two weeks and two days old. Four hundred and thirteen days since the division. Three hundred and fifty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About four inches stood over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the figure of steps before the inches again and said the order was not an accident this time either.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and about ninety of them are under this morning, which is the same figure as the water itself.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps are under.*”

“*And I have given the figure first on two mornings of this week and I am not going to apologise for it. The figure is the thing and the inches are how much of it there is, and about four of you have had that argument with me twice and lost it.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath all of it, and that working has not changed since the first day of this century. I would like about nine of you to notice that the one figure in this county that has never once been wrong is a figure of inches.*”

Then the ninth line and the ninth run were put side by side on the dry stone at about the tenth hour. A woman of forty-nine said out loud that they are not the same book, gave the working, and about nine of them looked at both.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. On the third day of this week she said she would come back to this and would not be hurried about it.

“*The ninth run is mine. It is a bound sequence of day-leaves and I write that day’s work on that day’s leaf in one line, and the front of it has a gap in the stitching and I entered on it yesterday in about nine of your ears.*”

“*The ninth line is not mine. It is the arrangement’s own record for the ninth position, and it is a run of days as well, and it has a leaf for every day, and there is not one line entered on one of those leaves, and there never has been.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four has a name in ink on the index of that one. A man of fifty-four has asked for a day against it for a hundred and thirty-eight mornings. Neither of those is a day on it, and I am not going to let about four of you put the two books together because they both have leaves in them.*”

“*Two runs, and the same shape, and one of them is full from here forward and one of them is empty from the first leaf to the last. Nobody on this landing can say which of them is worse, because they are not the same kind of thing.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the ordinary form makes a position checkable, and a woman of forty-four said it makes a record checkable, gave him the difference, and he took it back and said the whole week had been about that difference.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Eleven of those chairs have a name against them in her book and nobody in them, and she named two of the eleven as dead on the third day of this week. A chair is either got in her book or it is not.

“*A record, and not a position. That is the whole difference, and it is worth a morning of about nine people’s ears, and a man of thirty-eight did not have to be told about it and has not been told about it.*”

“*A thing is checkable from the day its record starts. A position is checkable from the day somebody with the pen writes on the leaf, and those are two different days, and about four of you have been using one of them for a week and calling it the other.*”

“*And I am not going to be argued with about this. I have kept a book of twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years and I have never once had a chair the book could not be checked against, because a chair is either got in it or it is not. That is the only kind of record I have ever had.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and five days, three hundred and five being eight hundred and fifty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seventy-four days old, a hundred and seventy-four being eight hundred and fifty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then it is gone and she is right, and I am not going to say a better thing than it in about nine of your ears. I have been saying position all week and I have been meaning a book, and about four of you would have let me.*”

“*Three hundred and five days in this county, and that is eight hundred and fifty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy-four days old, and that is eight hundred and fifty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and I have not asked about it and I am not going to ask this morning.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and fifty-nine days old, and a hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and fifty-nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I am not going to put it beside a book this morning and call that a reason for anything.*”

Then a man of thirty-one asked a woman of forty-four to write the ordinary form in her book, and she said no and gave the working. A boy of thirteen offered to write it down, and was told by her in about nine ears that he was not going to be made into a clerk.

“*I want it in a book. Your book. Written out, with the day on it, and the day is today, and I will read it out to about nine of you before you write and after.*”

“*No, and I am going to say no to the book and not to the wanting, and I said the same thing to you on Friday and I am not going to be angry about having said it twice.*”

“*My book is twenty-nine chairs. If that goes in it, then in about four weeks a man of thirty-one has written the right of refusal into a keeper’s book of chairs, and about nine of them who were not in this room will read it and find out what it was for from the page.*”

“*And you will say that I can write it under no chair, and I have thought about that. If it is under no chair then it is not in the book, and then it is a thing said on a landing, and a thing said on a landing is what it is and no more than that.*”

“*I have said a true thing on this bank in about nine of your ears. That is all I have got and I am not going to try to make it into a thing that survives me in a book I do not own.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and six days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and five is eight hundred and fifty-nine less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and six days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I can write it. I have been reading a sentence out in three pieces for a man of fifty-four since the Saturday of the hundred and forty-third week, and I can do that with a leaf, and I wanted to say so before about four of you put it to her.*”

Orla Fennimore, forty-four, said no to him in about nine of your ears, and said where she had got it, and said that about four of them had not been going to hear it and that that was the correct number.

“*No. And I am not saying it to you, and I am not saying it because you are thirteen.*”

“*I am saying it because a woman of sixty-four handed a thing to me on the Saturday of this week and told me it was for the person it was most likely to be wasted on. I have been carrying it about four days waiting to see if I could give it back.*”

“*You are the only person on this landing who reads a sentence out in three pieces for a man of fifty-four who cannot read. If I let you write that down this morning, then in about four weeks you are the person who wrote it, and a boy of thirteen who writes down what he is told is a clerk, and a clerk is a person with a pen and a reason.*”

“*You are going to keep being the person who reads it out twice. That is a different thing and it is worth more, and I am not going to be thanked for the shape of that either.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Monday with about four inches over the top step, two runs of days side by side on a dry stone that are not the same book, and no book there that would take a true thing in it.

About four inches stood over the top step with seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of the ninety steps were under, and the man of forty-three gave the figure before the inches for the second morning running.
The ninth run and the ninth line went side by side on the dry stone and a woman of forty-nine said they are not the same book. The ninth run is hers, with one line on a leaf for a day and a gap in the stitching at the front. The ninth line is the arrangement’s own record, also a run of days, with a leaf for every day and not one line entered on any of them.

A woman of sixty-four has a name in ink on its index, and a man of fifty-four has asked for a day against it for a hundred and thirty-eight mornings. Two runs of the same shape, one full from here forward and one empty from the first leaf to the last.
And a man of thirty-one said the ordinary form makes a position checkable, and a woman of forty-four said it makes a record checkable, and that those are two different days, and that about four of them had been using one of them for a week and calling it the other.

And that if it went under no chair it would not be in the book.

A boy of thirteen offered to write it down and was told no, and told where she had got the refusal from. He is the only person on that landing who reads a sentence out in three pieces for a man of fifty-four who cannot read, and if he wrote it down in about four weeks he would be the person who wrote it, and a boy of thirteen who writes down what he is told is a clerk.
