# Chapter 861: Second Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fifth Week, And A Wednesday, And About Seventy Of Those Ninety Steps Under With Twelve Inches On The Top One And Five Days Of The Coming Back, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Says Out Loud The Sentence He Said On The Wednesday Of The Hundred And Forty-Second Week And Says He Has One Day Of His Own Fortnight Left And Is Not Going To Name A Fourteenth On A Wednesday, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Read It Out For Him And Is Refused In About Nine Of Your Ears

Second day of the hundred and forty-fifth week. His six hundred and eleventh morning. Four hundred and sixty-one days after the settlement. The fever eighty-two weeks and four days old. Four hundred and fifteen days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About seventy of those ninety steps were under at about the seventh hour, and there were twelve inches on the top one, and it was five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the steps before the inches, and then said he was not going to go back and forth again.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and he has given it every morning of nineteen years.

“*About seventy of those ninety steps under, and twelve inches on the top step, and five days of the coming back.*”

“*And I have given that order twice this week and the day before, and about four of you have had an argument with me about it both times, and I am finished with the order. I am going to say them the way they come and nobody is to write down which way they came.*”

“*What I do not change is the working. Four inches a day, and sixteen is a full bank, and a full bank holds until it comes off. That has not moved once since the first day of this century and I am not going to start now because a man of fifty-four has started something else.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said a sentence out loud on that landing. He had said it before. He gave the day he had said it on, and he said he had not said anything better since.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He has a palm and a forearm. He named a thirteenth thing he cannot check on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-third week, and it is a figure of a woman. On the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week he read nine positions out loud in about nine of your ears. He said what he could check and what he could not.

“*The six being checkable does not make the three checkable, and I said that on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week in about nine of your ears and I have not said anything better in three of these weeks.*”

“*I have watched about four of you do that arithmetic in your heads in the last minute, and that was the next sentence I said that morning, and I would rather say it out loud than have it be done quietly.*”

“*I said that on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week, and that is three of these weeks back, and I can work that out without a boy because it is the same day of the week and it is three of the numbers off. Eight hundred and sixty-one less eight hundred and forty is twenty-one, and twenty-one days is three of these weeks.*”

“*And I have not said anything better since. Not one morning in three of these weeks. About four of you have been waiting for me to say something better and I am not going to, because a sentence that is said out loud twice in a fortnight is a sentence, and a sentence that is said out loud and got better is a thing being worked on by about four people who are not me.*”

“*And I have one day of my own fortnight left. Tomorrow is the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week and that is the day I said I would name a fourteenth thing I cannot check on, and I am not going to name it on a Wednesday, because about four of you would write Wednesday down and go home and say a man of about fifty-two said it on a Wednesday.*”

Then a man of thirty-one offered to read that sentence out for him in about nine of your ears. He was refused in about nine of your ears. The reason given was a sentence a woman of forty-nine had used on the Sunday.

“*I will read it for you. Both halves, and I will do the second day as well, and about nine of you will have it and I will have said it, and that is a better morning for you than a man who cannot see well saying it twice.*”

“*No. And I am not saying no because you read it badly, because you read the terms of two declining seats on Friday and about four of them did not have to ask you twice.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine said a thing on the Sunday of last week and I am going to give it to you in her words because it is hers. She entered a line on a run in about nine of your ears and would not let you read it for her. And she said a line she has read out is a line about nine of you have heard, and a line a man of thirty-one read out is a line a man of thirty-one read out.*”

“*So that is the answer and it is about four of you have been asking me to let somebody else carry my sentences since the Sunday. I am fifty-two years old and I have carried every one of them myself and I am not going to be a thing that a man of thirty-one is good at.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and seven days, three hundred and seven being eight hundred and sixty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seventy-six days old, a hundred and seventy-six being eight hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then I have it wrong and I am glad I have it in the wrong place on a Wednesday morning with about nine of you listening, because on a Friday I would have carried it and been thanked for it and that would have been worse.*”

“*Three hundred and seven days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy-six days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-one days old, and a hundred and sixty-one is eight hundred and sixty-one less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I am going to go on saying that figure out loud and not going to ask it, and a man of about fifty-two has just shown me the shape of how to say a thing without handing it to somebody else.*”

At about the eighth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run, on the dry stone, and read it out herself. She gave the figure of how many mornings she had done it on, and said what the figure was a figure of.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and has read for a living for twenty-three years. She keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and the room with the nine clerks in it is four hundred miles off.

“*Today, on the leaf for today, in about nine of your ears, and I have read it out myself. That is the fourth morning I have done this on in front of anybody.*”

“*Four is a figure of mornings. It is not a figure of how many of you were standing here, and it is not a figure of the front of that book, and I am giving it because a figure of mornings is the only kind of figure I have got this morning.*”

“*I do four things a day as well. You get three and I get four because you count people and I count leaves, and that is the whole difference between this landing and that room and it is not a better thing.*”

At about the ninth hour a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for a day against the ninth line. It was the hundred and fortieth morning of the asking. He said he was not going to make anything else of the morning.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing. He cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and forty, and I asked it on a Wednesday this morning and I am not going to make anything of the day of the week.*”

“*And I am not asking for anything smaller. A man of fifty-four asked for something smaller on the Tuesday and got it and I am not going to have about four of you say I have gone back on it. The index is read out and it is good and it is not what I am asking for, and both of those are true at once and about four of you find that hard.*”

“*One more morning is not a small thing to me and I am not going to say it is so that the morning goes easier. I have asked it on a hundred and forty mornings and I am going to ask it on the hundred and forty-first.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own count, with the subtraction in it, and said what a man of fifty-four who cannot read was doing on that landing.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and eight days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and eight is eight hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and eight days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two said a thing on this landing and I know that sentence and I know he knows I know it, and I read a sentence out in three pieces for a man of fifty-four who cannot read, and this morning I read nothing at all.*”

“*Not because he could not read it out. Because a woman of forty-nine said on the Sunday that a line a man of thirty-one read out is a line a man of thirty-one read out, and if I read it out it is a line a boy of thirteen read out, and then about four of you have it twice and neither of them is him.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for that either. I have said that on about nine mornings and this is about the ninth and I am still counting.*”

About nine of them stood on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Wednesday. There were twelve inches on the top step. A man of about fifty-two had one day left in his own fortnight, and had said a sentence three of these weeks old without improving it. A man of thirty-one had offered to carry that sentence, and had been refused by the person it belonged to.

---

About seventy of those ninety steps were under with twelve inches on the top one. The man of forty-three said he was finished with the order the two figures came out in. He said nobody was to write down which way they came.

A man of about fifty-two said the same sentence again in about nine of your ears, the one about the six and the three.
He gave the day he said it on. It was the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week. That is twenty-one days back, and twenty-one days is three of these weeks. He has not said anything better since and is not going to try.
He has one day of his own fortnight left. Tomorrow is the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week. He is not going to name a fourteenth thing he cannot check on a Wednesday, because about four of them would write Wednesday down.

And a man of thirty-one offered to read it out for him in about nine of your ears, and was refused. The reason was a sentence of a woman of forty-nine, from the Sunday, given in her own words.
A line she has read out is a line about nine of them have heard. A line a man of thirty-one read out is a line a man of thirty-one read out.
He said he was glad to have it wrong on a Wednesday with nine of them listening, rather than right and thanked for it on a Friday.

A woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run in front of about nine of them and read it out herself, and gave the figure of how many mornings she had done that on, and said the figure was a figure of mornings and not a figure of how many of them were standing there. She said she gets four a day and the man of forty-three gets three, and that the difference is that she counts leaves and he counts people, and that this is not a better thing.

A man of fifty-four asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fortieth morning, and said he was not asking for anything smaller this morning.
A boy of thirteen read nothing at all out loud this morning. He said why, and gave his own count with the subtraction in it.
