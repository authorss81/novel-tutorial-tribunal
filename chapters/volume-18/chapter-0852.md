# Chapter 852: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Third Week, And A Monday, And The Fourth Thing Comes Back At Last And Is Given, And A Man Of Forty-Three Names The Last Morning He Gave It And Is Wrong By One Day And Says So, And A Woman Of Thirty-Six Comes Down Four Miles With A Wedge Of Leaves In A Satchel, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Burn Took The Front Of A Run In One Night And Is Told Nobody Knows When, And A Boy Of Thirteen Counts Nineteen Marks Off A Post

Seventh day of the hundred and forty-third week. His six hundred and second morning. Four hundred and fifty-two days after the settlement. The fever eighty-one weeks and two days old. Four hundred and six days since the division. Three hundred and fifty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The man of forty-three gave the fourth thing at the seventh hour on Monday, out of the same place he always gives it. He gave the count of mornings it had been nothing before he gave the figure. Then he corrected a thing he has had wrong in his own head for about four of these weeks.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives three things every morning. The fourth thing is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under. On Saturday he said out loud that he would start again on Monday, and this is that Monday, and about eighty of those ninety steps are under this morning.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps are under this morning.*”

“*And that is the fourth thing, given on the morning I said I would give it on, and I did not give it a day early, and about four of you have been waiting two days to be sure.*”

“*Now I am going to say how long it was nothing, and then I am going to say where I have been wrong, and the second is the reason I am saying the first.*”

“*It was nothing for nineteen mornings. From the second day of the hundred and forty-first week to yesterday. That is nineteen, and a boy of thirteen counted them off the post at the top of that cut and got nineteen, and I would rather have the boy’s figure than mine.*”

“*And the last morning I gave that figure out loud was the first day of the hundred and forty-first week, which is a Tuesday. On the Wednesday of that week I told about four of you the last time was the Monday, and the Monday was the day before, and I did not give it on the Monday.*”

“*I have had that wrong in my own head since the Wednesday of that week. Nobody has used it against me, and a man of thirty-one did not raise it, and that is not why I am saying it now. A figure I am wrong about is a figure somebody else has to carry, and I have been carrying it wrong for four of these weeks.*”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for his day against the ninth line for the hundred and thirty-first morning, and a boy of thirteen gave his own figure, and neither of them asked the man of forty-three anything, which was noticed.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and thirty-one mornings, and the figure is one more than the morning before, and it is eight hundred and fifty-two less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and thirty-one, and it is one more than yesterday, and I asked on a Monday this time, and that is not a different thing and it is not a smaller thing.*”

“*And I am not going to ask the man of forty-three for anything else this morning. He has just told about nine of us that he had a thing wrong for four of these weeks, and that is a bigger thing to be given on a Monday than a day on a book four hundred miles off.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county a hundred and ninety-nine days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. A hundred and ninety-nine is eight hundred and fifty-two less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*A hundred and ninety-nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I counted the nineteen for him. I counted on the post at the top of the cut where he keeps the figure, and the post has a nick on it for every ten, and there are one nick and nine marks, and that is nineteen, and it took me a moment and he watched me do it.*”

“*And here is the thing I want about nine of you to have. He was wrong by one day for about four of these weeks, and he said so at the seventh hour on a Monday, in about nine ears, and he did not say it to anybody else first.*”

“*That is a count and not an argument. A man being one out and saying so is a figure of a man being one out, and I am not going to let about four of you make it a figure of a week.*”

At about the ninth hour a woman of thirty-six walked the last four miles of that cart road from the bakehouse, carrying a satchel. In the satchel there was a wedge of burnt leaves. About nine of them on that landing looked at it, and nobody touched it.

Hessa Vinch is thirty-six, has rung a hand-bell at the top of a valley in the Briar March for nineteen years, and holds the seventh of the nine positions. She declined the common archive with a duty attached. She said, in a room four hundred miles off on the Saturday of this week, that a bell tells you something is happening and does not know what.

“*I came down on the Saturday and slept at the bakehouse, and I walked the last four miles this morning because I would not put this down in a cart and let a cart be the last thing that happened to it.*”

“*That is the run. It is a bound sequence of day-leaves, a leaf for a day, and the person in the seat writes that day’s work on that day’s leaf, in one line, and nothing else. Nineteen years of that. A leaf for every day and one line on it, and the line is what I did that day and it is not a sentence.*”

“*And the front of it went. What came down that road is what is left, and what is left is a wedge, and a wedge is a shape and not a figure.*”

“*Here is the anchor, and a woman of sixty-four taught me to say it that way. There is a hearthstone at the head of my valley and there is a scorch on it the length of my forearm, and it goes down into the stone, and it is the only thing left about the burning that is not burnt.*”

“*How deep the burn went into the leaves, I cannot tell you. The leaves that would have told me are the leaves it took. That is the whole of what I know and I have said it out loud in about nine of your ears, and about four of you were going to work it out from the shape of it and I would rather you did not.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said a want, and a woman of thirty-six gave the directions from her own valley in her own mouth, because a place nobody has been to has to be given a distance by somebody who has.

“*You are going to ask me how far. I am going to answer it before you ask, and I am going to say it the way a person says it who has walked it, and not the way a man says it who has a map of it.*”

“*The bakehouse is four miles up that cart road from this landing. The landing is nine miles off the plank in Hask. The hearthstone is two days up from the bakehouse going in, and one day down if the water is out, and there is no road to it and there is not going to be one.*”

“*That is the whole of the directions and nobody has ever asked me for them. I have had them in my head for nineteen years and I have never had to say any of it to another person, and it has just occurred to me that I have been keeping a place in my head instead of on paper.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said when the burning happened. A woman of thirty-six told him that nobody knows, and he took it back in front of about nine of them, and the cost of saying it went on the ground where it could be picked up.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and ninety-eight days, two hundred and ninety-eight being eight hundred and fifty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and sixty-seven days old, a hundred and sixty-seven being eight hundred and fifty-two less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*One night. I am going to say that the front of that run went in one night, because that is how a run of things gets into a person, and I want to know what it means for the shape of it.*”

Hessa Vinch, thirty-six, did not answer him at once, and then answered him, and said that she was not going to be gentle about it because gentle would be worse.

“*No. You do not know that, and I have not said it, and the reason I have not said it is that nobody in that valley knows it, including me, and including the person who was standing in the room when it started.*”

“*A bound run does not burn in a night. It burns the way a thatch goes, from the inside outward, and by the time the outside of it is black the inside of it has been gone a while. And the front of a run is the oldest part, so the oldest part went first, and there is a morning somewhere in the middle of it and nobody alive was awake for it.*”

“*You have just put a day on a fire in about nine of your ears. A day is a figure of a thing. Take it back.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, took it back in the same breath, said he did not know where he had got the night from, and said the next thing out loud before anybody could ask him for it.

“*Then it is gone, and I do not know where I got it, and I am not going to guess again in front of about four of you.*”

“*And here is the thing I actually want, and I am saying it now rather than on Saturday, because I have learned one thing in about nine days and it is that a want said late is a want that has been growing.*”

“*I want to be the one who carries that wedge four hundred miles. Not to the room. To whoever would look at it. I want to be the pair of hands that takes a thing nobody has seen down a road and puts it in front of people.*”

“*And a woman of thirty-six has just told me that a person who carries a thing arrives with a story about the thing, and that after about nine days the story is the thing. I have not got an answer to that and I am not going to pretend I have.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the tenth hour of a Monday with eight inches over the top step, and a wedge of burnt leaves in a satchel on the dry stone that nobody had touched, and a man of thirty-one who had just said out loud in about nine ears that he wanted to be the one who carried it.

---

The fever was eighty-one weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and ninety-eight days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and sixty-seven days old, and a hundred and fifty-two days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

The man of forty-three gave the fourth thing at the seventh hour on Monday, out of the same place, and gave the count before he gave the figure. It was nothing for nineteen mornings, from the second day of the hundred and forty-first week to yesterday. A boy of thirteen counted nineteen off the post at the top of the cut and got the same. He named the last morning he gave that figure out loud, which was the first day of the hundred and forty-first week, a Tuesday. On the Wednesday of that week he had told about four of them it was the Monday, and he was wrong by one day for about four of these weeks.

A woman of thirty-six walked the last four miles of the cart road with a satchel, and in the satchel was a wedge of burnt leaves. It is the seventh of the nine positions, and the run is a bound sequence of day-leaves, one line on a leaf, and the front of it is gone.
The anchor is a scorch on a hearthstone at the head of her valley, the length of her forearm, going down into the stone. How deep the burn went cannot be told, because the leaves that would have told it are the leaves it took. She gave the directions out loud for the first time in nineteen years.

And a man of thirty-one said the burning happened in one night, and a woman of thirty-six told him that nobody knows. A bound run does not burn in a night, and there is a morning somewhere in the middle of it that nobody alive was awake for. He took it back in the same breath.
And then he said he wanted to be the one who carries that wedge four hundred miles, in about nine ears, and that he had said it now rather than on Saturday because a want said late is a want that has been growing.
