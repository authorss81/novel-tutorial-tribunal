# Chapter 858: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fourth Week, And A Sunday, And The Water Came Off All Ninety Steps At About The Sixth Hour And That Is The Twenty-Fourth Time, And A Woman Of Forty-Nine Enters Today’s Line On A Run With A Gap In The Stitching In Front Of About Nine People, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Is Told What He Was Told On Tuesday, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says It Is The First Entry Ever On It And Is Corrected

Sixth day of the hundred and forty-fourth week. His six hundred and eighth morning. Four hundred and fifty-eight days after the settlement. The fever eighty-two weeks and one day old. Four hundred and twelve days since the division. Three hundred and fifty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh. The man of forty-three gave the hour, the count and the number of the time, and said there was no figure of inches and no figure of steps.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and this morning there is no water on those steps, so there is nothing under them.

“*It came off at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps are walkable now, and that is the twenty-fourth time.*”

“*And there is no figure of inches this morning and no figure of steps, and the order those two come out in is the order they come out in on every off-morning I have given. I have given about nine of them in nineteen years.*”

“*The next one is in eight days, and that is the Monday of the hundred and forty-fifth week. I know that without the water-stone and I have not looked at it, and about four of you have stopped asking how I know it.*”

At about the eighth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run, on the dry stone, in front of about nine people. She read it out loud herself, and would not let a man of thirty-one read it for her.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. The run is a bound sequence of day-leaves with one line on a leaf for a day.

“*I am entering today on it here, in about nine of your ears, and I am going to read it out myself, and about four of you are going to want to write it down and I am telling you not to.*”

“*That is the whole of the reason and it is the reason. A line I have read out is a line that about nine of you have heard. A line a man of thirty-one read out is a line that a man of thirty-one read out.*”

“*You are the witness. About nine of you on this landing are the witness, and a witness is a figure of ears and not a figure of a reader. I have not got a figure of either this morning and I am not going to give one.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said it was the first entry ever made on that run. A woman of forty-nine told him it was the first one anybody had watched, gave him the difference, and he took it back in about nine ears and said the difference was the whole of it.

“*It is the first entry that has ever been made on the ninth run. In about nine of your ears. In front of people.*”

“*It is the first entry anybody has watched. I have entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years and I did not stop on Saturday and I am not going to stop today.*”

“*First ever and first seen, and you have put them in the same place. A man of thirty-one who is nobody’s has just said in about nine of your ears that a thing I have done every day for twenty-three years did not happen until he stood next to me.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, took it back, gave the two counters that are his own with the subtraction in them, and said that he had a name for the difference now and that the name was worse than the thing.

“*First ever and first seen. I have got the difference and I am not going to put a name on it, because a name on it would be a sentence and a sentence is what a man of about fifty-two warned about on Wednesday.*”

“*Three hundred and four days in this county, and that is eight hundred and fifty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy-three days old, and that is eight hundred and fifty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and fifty-eight days old, and a hundred and fifty-eight is eight hundred and fifty-eight less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and a line entered on a run on an off-morning is not an answer to it in anybody’s ear.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight was told, in about nine ears, what he had already been told, and was told nothing new. He asked for the second half of his question one more time and was given the same answer with the day it was said on.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and there is a board with thirty-two notches on the edge of it in the inside of his coat, which is a figure of what is on a board. On the Saturday of last week he asked what three of the nine positions are and who put them there, and was not answered, and about four of them have been waiting for him to ask again.

“*Then ask it again, because I would rather you asked it than sat on it. Ask the whole of it, both halves, in about nine of your ears, and I will answer the half I can answer.*”

“*The second half is who put them there. A man of sixty-one read the arrangement’s own index out on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-third week and left three of the nine positions blank, and he said he left them blank on purpose, in about nine of your ears.*”

“*That was the Tuesday. That is where it is, and it is not a discovery this morning and I am not going to let about four of you make it one. A man of thirty-one is not going to stand on this bank and pretend he worked it out.*”

“*And the first half, what the three are, I have been asked it for twelve days and I do not know, and a man of thirty-one has told me on this landing that he does not know, and that is the whole of the answer there is.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for being told a thing I was in the room for, and I am not going to be told it is progress. I have a board in my coat with thirty-two notches on it and I have still not got the part where I know what to do with it, and a woman of sixty-four told me on the Sunday of last week that nobody is going to hand me that part.*”*

Then a boy of thirteen read a printed day out loud twice, and gave his own figure with the subtraction in it, and said what an off-morning is, and said that it is a figure of water and not a figure of a day.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and five days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and four is eight hundred and fifty-eight less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and five days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And I read the day at the top of that leaf out loud twice this morning, for him, because he cannot read it, and because about four of you cannot read it, and about nine of you did not come down to the counter before the light came up.*”

“*Here is the figure of this morning. The water came off at about the sixth hour and that is a figure of water, and it is the twenty-fourth time, and that is a figure of times, and neither of them is a figure of a day.*”

“*A day is a leaf. There is a leaf for today and there has been a leaf for every day since the day a man of fifty-four paid fourpence for a book, and a man of fifty-four cannot read the one of today and I can.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the ninth hour of a Sunday with all ninety of those steps dry under them for the twenty-fourth time, a line entered on a run with a gap in its stitching, and a man of thirty-eight who had been told the same thing twice.
