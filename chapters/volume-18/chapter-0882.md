# Chapter 882: The Twenty-Seventh Time, And The Same Sentences

Second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-second morning. Four hundred and eighty-two days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and four days old. Four hundred and thirty-six days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh. That was the twenty-seventh time. The man of forty-three gave the hour, the count and the number of the time, and said that three figures had nothing this morning to be about.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four things off one stone every morning, and says that on an off-morning three of the four have nothing to be about.

“It came off at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps are walkable now, and that is the twenty-seventh time.”

“There is no figure of inches this morning and there is no figure of steps under, and there is no figure of days of the coming back either. That is not a gap in my morning. It is a morning where three of my four things have nothing to be about.”

“And the next one is in eight days. That is the third day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, which is a Thursday, and that is the twenty-eighth time. I am saying it on the twenty-seventh, and about four of you have been writing my days down on the forty-seventh.”

Then the man of forty-three said, in his own mouth, with the working given, why nothing on that landing may be done by one person.

“There is a thing you have all been circling for nine days and nobody has put down flat, so I am going to put it down flat. There is a thing on this landing that may not be done alone, and I am the man to say it, because I have held these ninety steps for nineteen years.”

“Here it is. You do not go down them alone. Not in the dark and not in the rain and not because you have done it a hundred times. And I am not saying that because I am careful. I am saying it because of the shape of it.”

“If I went down and did not come up, then a thing that happened to me would be a thing that happened to one man. And nobody would know what was at the bottom of it, or how long it had been there, or whether it had been coming for a week. One man, and then no record at all.”

“And the other half, and this is the half I want written down. If two people go down, then one of them comes back up and says what happened. That is the whole of it. That is why there are always two on that cut when there is water on it. It is not company. It is that one person cannot make a record by himself.”

“It is the same reason a man of forty-four wants a working day and not a settlement. And it is the same reason a woman of forty-nine will not take a number off a person in a room. It is one shape and it has got three names this week and none of them is the shape.”

Then the woman who keeps the ninth of the nine said the same sentence again, out loud, in front of about nine of them, and said it was about a page.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. She said the sentence on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and she said it on the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, and she said it on the sixth day of that week.

“Those three were never blanks.”

“And I am saying it a fourth time, and I am saying it out loud, and I am saying it on a Wednesday on a morning when there is no water on the steps and about four of you had already decided you had heard it.”

“It is about a page. It is not about the three of them and it is not about the three of you. And it is the same sentence and it is not progress, and if any of you go off that bank this morning with it in your pocket as something you have got, then you have got the wrong thing and I will have spent it.”

“An index is a list of what a person can be shown. A list has got one thing it can say about a position, and the one thing is whether there is a thing to go and stand in front of.”

“A position that is not a stone is a day somebody did the work. The run is where the day is written down, and the run is not the position.”

“A run with a hole at the front of it is entered on today, and today’s line is not a gap, and a position may be checked from the day its record starts and not from before it.”

“That is the twenty-fifth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and today is not a gap.”

Then a man of thirty-one took the sentence about a page and put it into a question it has nothing to do with, and was told so by the woman the question belongs to.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twenty-eight days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ninety-seven days old.

“Three hundred and twenty-eight days in this county, and the cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-seven days old, and nobody has said whether it will keep. And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-two days old and I have not asked it.”

“Twenty-two wrong things in twenty-two mornings, and here is the twenty-second. A woman of forty-four has asked whether a day can be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it. That is her question and it is five times asked and not answered.”

“And a woman of forty-nine has just said four sentences out loud on a bank. And the second one of them answers it. A run with a hole at the front of it is entered on today, and today’s line is not a gap.”

“So that is settled, and it was settled on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and I have been carrying it round since that Monday like a man carrying a door he could have put down anywhere, and I did not put it down on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and I did not put it down on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week.”

Orla Fennimore said it was not her question, and said which of the four sentences had anything to do with her question, and none of them.

“No. And I am not going to be angry with you, because you have done this three times in front of me now and about four of you watched it happen three times.”

“A hole at the front of a run is a hole at the front of a run. That is what she said, and it is true, and it is about a page. My question is a different shape and you have just handed it to me as though the two were the same size.”

“Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it. That is not about a hole. A hole is where somebody knew there was a day and did not enter it. Mine is a day nobody was in a room for.”

“You are the only man in this county who keeps doing that and I would like it stopped in a plain way. Take the four sentences back off that question. They are the best sentences said on this landing in a fortnight and they are about a page, and they are not mine.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty-nine days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty-nine is eight hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and twenty-nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-one mornings, and I am not going to say what it is, and I said on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week what kind of figure it is.”

“And a woman of forty-nine has just said four sentences on that bank and about four of you wrote them down. I do not know what any of them has to do with me and I am not going to pretend it does.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-first morning, and said what a man of forty-three had said that he had not expected a man of forty-three to say.

“A hundred and sixty-one. And a man who has held a landing for nineteen years stood on it this morning and said that two people go down a cut because one person cannot make a record by himself.”

“I have been standing in front of nine of you every morning for eleven years asking one woman for one day, and I did not know that was the same thing. It is not the same thing. Do not tell me it is.”

“Because I am not going down a cut in the dark and I am not going to be one of two. I am asking for a line on a page, and a line is not a ladder, and nobody is to be thanked for the ladder.”

Nobody went down the cut that morning, and about nine of them were still standing on the bank when the light came off the top of it, and the sentence about the three of them was said once and went out into the ordinary air of a Wednesday like any other thing said out loud on a landing, and a boy of thirteen heard it go and did not write it down, which is the whole of what he has ever done about it.
