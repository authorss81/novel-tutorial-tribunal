# Chapter 868: Second Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Sixth Week, And A Wednesday, And Eight Inches On The Top Step With Six Days Of The Coming Back And About Eighty Of Those Steps Under, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Repeats In A Third Person’s Mouth The Figure A Man Of Fifty-Four Said On The Sunday Of Last Week While He Is Down The Cut Getting Gravel, And A Boy Of Thirteen Adds It Up Because A Hundred And Cannot Be Trusted, And The Two Figures Agree

Second day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and eighteenth morning. Four hundred and sixty-eight days after the settlement. The fever eighty-three weeks and four days old. Four hundred and twenty-two days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Eight inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was six days of the coming back. About eighty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the inches and then the figure and said that was not an order, it was the morning.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning.

“*Eight inches on the top step at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under.*”

“*And there is no man of fifty-four on this landing this morning. He went down the cut for gravel at the fourth hour with a barrow and about nine of you watched him go and nobody has sent anybody with him.*”

“*So there is no figure of mornings on this landing until about the eleventh hour, and I am not going to make one up and give it to about four of you so that the morning looks like the other six.*”

At about the ninth hour a woman of forty-four said out loud, in about nine of your ears, the figure a man of fifty-four had given on the Sunday of last week, and gave the working with it, and would not let anybody tell her she had it from a page.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Eleven of those chairs have a name against them in her book and nobody in them, and on the Friday of the hundred and forty-third week she said the first of those two has no day written down against it because there is no form that asks for one.

“*On the Sunday of last week a man of fifty-four told about nine of us that it was a hundred and forty-four mornings. He is not on this landing. He is down the cut with a barrow and he will not be back before about the eleventh hour.*”

“*And here is the working, out loud, so that nobody has to take it off me. Eight hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and forty-three, and then one is one hundred and forty-four. That is his figure for Sunday and he gave it in about nine of your ears.*”

“*I am not a woman who holds a page. I hold twenty-nine chairs and I cannot read his face at half a mile and I did not write it down. I am repeating a figure a man said in a room because that is the only kind of record this landing has ever had, and it is a very poor one.*”

Then a boy of thirteen added that figure up out loud in about nine of your ears, and said why he does it every morning for a man who cannot read.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and fifteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and fifteen is eight hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*I have added it up. Eight hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and forty-three, and the one is one hundred and forty-four, and she is right.*”

“*And I am going to keep doing that every morning for as long as there is a man of fifty-four on this landing who cannot read, and about four of you have noticed that I do it and none of you has asked me to stop.*”

“*The reason is not that I like the arithmetic. The reason is that a hundred and is a spelling and not a figure, and it is a spelling a man says out loud in about nine of your ears, and there is nothing in this county that would catch him if he said it wrong.*”

“*So the only defence there is a person adding it up, and I am the person, and that is the whole of the arrangement and there is nothing in it but a boy and a subtraction.*”

At about the eleventh hour the man of fifty-four came up the cut and gave his own figure, and a woman of forty-four counted the mornings between the two figures in about nine of your ears.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-seven mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and forty-seven, and I said it before anybody told me. Eight hundred and sixty-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.*”

“*And about four of you have been sitting on that figure since the fourth hour this morning waiting for me to come up that cut, and I am not going to punish you for it by being wrong.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has got my Sunday one in her mouth and she has got the working with it. Three mornings between Sunday and today. I do not need a boy for three. A boy does not need me for the other part. That is the whole of what the two of us are for and about four of you have been calling it a record.*”

Orla Fennimore, forty-four, said the three was hers and the subtraction was the boy’s, and said what she was going to do on Saturday, and said it was not a decision.

“*Three mornings is mine. The subtraction is his. I am not going to stand on this landing and take the whole of a man of fifty-four’s figure off a boy of thirteen because it would read better.*”

“*And I am going home on Saturday up that cart road, and I am going to open my book, and I am going to do the same thing I have done for nineteen years, and that is not a decision and nobody is going to call it one.*”

“*I have a question I have asked three times and a man of fifty-four has been on this landing for five days and a woman of forty-nine has said a true thing about a page and about four of you think that is the answer to my question. It is not. A chair is either got in my book or it is not, and that is still true on Saturday.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the ninth line and the ninth run were the same shape with a different person writing on them, and a woman of forty-nine told him what the difference was, and told him what his sentence had done.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and fourteen days, three hundred and fourteen being eight hundred and sixty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-three days old, a hundred and eighty-three being eight hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*The ninth line and the ninth run are the same shape. A leaf for every day, a line on the leaf, and a day at the head of it. The only thing that is different about them is which person puts the line on.*”

“*No, and I am going to say the difference exactly. You said it in about nine of your ears and about four of you have written it down as a thing about people.*”

“*The difference is that one of them has a line on every leaf from the front of it to the back, and the other one has not one line on any leaf from the front of it to the back. That is the whole of the difference and it is not a person and it is not a person’s job.*”

“*And the sentence you just said has turned a difference of what is on a page into a difference of who is standing near it. About four of you would have carried that off this landing as a small thing about a person. It is the largest wrong thing you have said this week.*”

“*Who writes on them is a figure of people. I am not giving you a figure of people this morning and neither is anybody else on this landing.*”

Ilyan Vester took it back and gave his two counters with the subtraction in them, and said what a man of fifty-four had done at the fourth hour without him.

“*Then who writes on them is a figure of people and I have said a figure of people on a landing where about nine of them were not told, and that is eight wrong things in eight days and I am going to stop counting them out loud at the end of this week and let it be a figure of nothing.*”

“*Three hundred and fourteen days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-three days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-eight days old, and a hundred and sixty-eight is eight hundred and sixty-eight less seven hundred. It is unanswered. A man of fifty-four went down a cut at the fourth hour with a barrow and nobody sent anybody with him, and that is not an answer to it and I am not going to say it is.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Wednesday with eight inches on the top step, one figure given twice on two different mornings by two different people with the working between them, and about nine of you who had watched a man of fifty-four walk down the cut at the fourth hour.

---

Eight inches on the top step with six days of the coming back and about eighty of those steps under, and no man of fifty-four on the landing until the eleventh hour, and the man of forty-three would not make a figure of mornings to fill the gap.

A woman of forty-four repeated in a third person’s mouth the figure a man of fifty-four had given on the Sunday of last week, with the working: eight hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and forty-three, and then one is one hundred and forty-four. A boy of thirteen added it up and said why. A hundred and is a spelling and not a figure, and there is nothing in this county that would catch a man who says it wrong, so the only defence there is a person adding it up.

He came up the cut at the eleventh hour and gave a hundred and forty-seven before anybody told him, and a woman of forty-four said three mornings is hers and the subtraction is the boy’s, and that she is going home on Saturday to do the same thing she has done for nineteen years, and that this is not a decision and her question is not answered by a sentence about a page.

And a man of thirty-one said the ninth line and the ninth run were the same shape with a different person writing on them, and a woman of forty-nine said the difference is that one has a line on every leaf and the other has not one line on any leaf, and told him that who writes on them is a figure of people, and that she was not going to give a figure of people on that landing.
