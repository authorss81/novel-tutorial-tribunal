# Chapter 900: The Account Stays Incomplete And Stays In Use

Sixth day of the hundred and fiftieth week. His six hundred and fiftieth morning. Five hundred days after the settlement. The fever eighty-eight weeks and one day old. Four hundred and fifty-four days since the division. Four hundred days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was at eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, about eighty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and said that this was a Sunday and that about half of that bank would go down early and that he would be here anyway.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and does not give the figure of the settlement or the figure of the division or any figure of a thing that is not standing in front of him on a stone. He gives four figures and has never once given a fifth.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and fiftieth week, and it is a Sunday.”

“There is a figure about the settlement in the air on this landing this morning that somebody has done a sum on, and I am not going to say it out loud and I am not going to agree with it either.”

“If I agree with it then it is a fifth thing and I do not give a fifth thing. Nineteen years and I have never once said that figure out loud on a step and I am not starting on the morning it happens to fall on a whole figure.”

“And it is a Sunday, and half of this bank goes down early on a Sunday, and I have got up at the fifth hour on every morning for nineteen years and I am not going to start making an exception for a figure of days that somebody else did the sum on.”

Then the woman of forty-four said what has happened to the account, out loud, in about nine ears, on the last morning of it, and said it out loud because it is the ordinary work and not a finding.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, has kept twenty-nine chairs for nineteen years, has put her question down five times, and is not going to put it down a sixth. She has one line in another person’s hand and nothing has been settled.

“An incomplete account stays incomplete. That is the resolution and I am saying it on a Sunday morning in about nine of your ears and I am not saying it because I have got a better one.”

“And it stays in use. That is the other half and it is the half that matters and nobody has ever said it to me in that shape and I have been waiting nineteen years to have to say it.”

“Here is what the account is. It is twenty-nine chairs and a line in my own hand for nineteen years. It is one line in a hand that is not mine, about a chair in a passage, on a sheet with nothing at the head of it. It is a figure of stitches said out loud in a room with a door shut and it is in no book. It is a man of fifty-four with a hundred and seventy-nine mornings in his mouth and no day against any of them.”

“That is the account. It is exactly as incomplete as it was on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and not one day of it has been settled, and it is being worked at, and it will be worked at next week, and a thing that is worked at is not a thing that has failed.”

“A position may be checked from the day its record starts and may not be checked before it. That binds nobody and it protects nobody and it is not a seal, and I stopped that word on this step and I am not letting it back in on a Sunday.”

“I am not settling my question. It is still open, it is the same question, and one line on a sheet has not answered it and was not meant to and I was told so before I got it.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked to be the one who kept the sheet, and was refused in the mouth of the woman it is for, and was not told he was right.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and forty-six days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and fifteen days old.

“Three hundred and forty-six days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and fifteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is two hundred days old, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has answered it, and nobody has been asked about it.”

“Forty wrong things in forty mornings, and here is the fortieth. Let me keep the sheet.”

“Not the chair. The chair is hers and the line is hers and I am not asking to be anywhere near the chair. The sheet. Let it be me. It is a sheet of paper with nothing at the head of it and one line in a hand that is not hers, and about nine weeks from now there is going to be somebody in that room who wants to know how a thing gets entered, and I could be the one who says, and I would be somebody’s.”

“No. And I am not going to be unkind about it, because you have been trying to be used for eleven weeks and you have been refused eleven weeks and I would rather say the shape of this than let you keep the same one for another morning.”

“The whole of what I asked for on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week was to be on somebody else’s page for one morning, for a thing I actually did, entered by somebody who could say I got it wrong. That is all of it. It is not a seat and it is not a keeper’s place and it is not anybody’s paper.”

“If you keep it, then the thing I asked for is that there is one person who could be shown, and you are nobody’s, and a nobody cannot be shown. And I am not saying that to put you down. I am saying it because it is the only sentence in this conversation that is true and it is the one that costs you something, and you have not been told one of those all week.”

“I am not asking anybody for anything about that offer in his mouth. He gives it every morning and nobody has ever answered it and I have not been asked about it and I am not going to go and find out what it was for. That is not mine to go and find out. It has been two hundred days old this morning and there is nobody on this landing who has ever said a word about it and that is going to be how it ends this week.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and seventy-ninth morning, and said he was not going to say anything else.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and seventy-nine mornings, which is eight hundred and ninety less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and seventy-nine. And that is the whole of what I am going to say and I am not going to add anything to it, because I have said something every morning for eleven years and I have run out of new things to say and I would rather stop than say one of the old ones again.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and forty-seven days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and forty-seven is nine hundred less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and forty-seven days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-nine mornings, and it is the last one of this week, and I am not giving it, and I am not going to start giving it because it is the end of something. The end of a week is not a reason.”

“And I said sixty-three in a room on the third day of the hundred and fiftieth week and it is a figure of stitches and it is in about five ears and it is on no page, and about four of you have wanted it on a page since, and it is going to stay off the page, and I am not the person who is going to change that.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He has been keeping a count that nobody has asked him for for thirty-nine mornings, and he named the kind of figure it is on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, and he has not given it and is not going to.

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the sixth day of the hundred and fiftieth week, and said where the arrangement was, and did not put a day on it.

“That is the forty-third morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing, and today is not a gap and today was not a gap.”

“The arrangement is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It does not answer. It has not answered anybody in this county this week and nobody in this county is in the room where it answers, and I am not going to put a day on it because no day belongs to it and about four of you have been waiting since the sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week for me to.”

“And nothing was settled. I want that said by somebody who has been here the whole week. A bundle came down off a cart on the third day of the hundred and forty-fourth week, which was a Thursday, and a boy counted a gap in it in a lane before the light came up, and a woman of forty-four moved a chair out of her room on the second day of the hundred and fiftieth week, and a woman of forty-four has been owed a working day for nineteen years and has got one line.”

“That is the week. None of it is a settlement and all of it is use.”

About nine of them were still on that landing at about the eleventh hour of the sixth day of the hundred and fiftieth week, and the account was still incomplete and still in use, and a man of thirty-one was standing on the step with two hundred days in his mouth that nobody had answered and nobody was going to.
