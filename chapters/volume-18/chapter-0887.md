# Chapter 887: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Eighth Week

Seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-seventh morning. Four hundred and eighty-seven days after the settlement. The fever eighty-six weeks and two days old. Four hundred and forty-one days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was three days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the four figures and named the day on both sides of the boundary.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has said the same three sentences about a Monday for eleven years.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, and it holds on the seventh and last day of this week, which is not the same thing as holding on any other day.”

“Today is the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. Tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week and it is a Tuesday. That is the boundary, and today is the Monday that comes before it, and it happens every seven days and nobody has ever been sentimental about it.”

“And the next off-morning is in three days, and that is the third day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, which is a Thursday, and that is the twenty-eighth time. I said that day on the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and about four of you have it on a Tuesday.”

“I have said a Monday out loud on that step for eleven years and nobody has ever written it down, so I am going to say it once with the week on it and let it exist somewhere. Today is the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. Tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week.”

“And that is the whole of what I have to say about Mondays. There is nothing in it. It is the day of the week and it is not a position and it is not on a page and it does not make anybody’s account better or worse. Half of you have been hoping for eleven years that a Monday would turn into a thing.”

Then the man of about fifty-two said that what three of the nine positions are is still open, and said what would have to happen to him, and said it was not an answer.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, named a fourteenth thing he cannot check on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and said on the Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that there would not be a fifteenth.

“I am going to say a thing I have said before and I am going to say it in the same words, and I am going to say it on a Monday because on a Monday about nine of you are still standing here at the eleventh hour.”

“What three of the nine positions are is still open. It is still open this morning. Nobody has answered it and nothing said on that bank this fortnight has answered it and I am not going to answer it and I am not going to help anybody answer it.”

“I have been asked it by about four of you, and asked it again the day before, and I am going to answer it once properly. And here is what would stop me, and I am giving it because about four of you would otherwise go on building me a nicer answer out of my own sentences.”

“An answer I could check. That is all. Not a stone. Not a page. Something a man with bad sight could put his hand on twice and get the same thing both times.”

“And I am not going to describe what that would look like, because about four of you would go and make it, and then you would come back here next week with a thing you built out of a sentence I said to be polite to you.”

Then a man of thirty-one said the three lost beginnings were the only thing on that landing worth a morning, and put it out as a plan for this week, and was told it was not a plan.

“Three lost beginnings. One burnt, one drowned, one cut out and used. That is the only thing on that landing worth a morning. Everything else we have done for a fortnight has been standing about a bank waiting for those three to turn up.”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-three days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and two days old.

“Three hundred and thirty-three days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-seven days old, and I have not asked it.”

“Twenty-seven wrong things in twenty-seven mornings, and here is the twenty-seventh. So here is the week. Monday we take the burnt one. Tuesday the drowned one. Wednesday the one that was cut out. And by Friday there is a figure for all three and it goes in the same column.”

“That is a week that finishes. That is the first thing anybody on this landing has said in a fortnight that finishes.”

Orla Fennimore said it was not a plan and not a week, and said what it actually was, and it took her four sentences.

“No. And I am not going to be sharp with you, because on a Monday you are all still here and you have had a long fortnight.”

“That is not a plan. A plan is four things done in an order. That is three things you would like to be finished. Those are different and only one of them has anything to do with a Tuesday.”

“And you have just said the word column out loud on a Monday, and about four of you heard it and about four of you did not, and the man of thirty-one did not hear it at all because he was busy finishing.”

“You put four figures of leaves in a column on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-seventh week and a boy of thirteen told you the addition was right and the sentence underneath it was wrong. Now you have put three more things in a column and the column is Monday to Friday, and a column is not a column if the things in it are not the same thing.”

“And that is three times on this landing in eleven days, and I am not counting them out loud for anybody, and if you want a figure of times then a man of about fifty-two has one and it is not for you.”

Then a man of thirty-eight asked the first half of his question for the fourth time in front of about nine of them, and was given the same sentence with the day it was said on.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, and has a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, which is a figure of what is on a board.

“Then the first half again. What three of the nine positions are, and who put them there, and why they were left off a page that has got room on it.”

“That is the fourth time you have asked the first half and I am going to give you the same sentence, and I am going to give you the day I said it on, and neither of those two things is progress.”

“You have been given it on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week and on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. Same words every time. You are going to keep asking and nobody is going to change, and I am not going to improve it to be kind to you.”

“And I will tell you the whole of what I have, since you have asked me twice this week. Knowing which of the nine a person can be shown is not knowing what the other three are. That is all of it. You have had that since the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week and it will not move.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-sixth morning, and said what a Monday is for.

“A hundred and sixty-six. And I am going to say the one thing I have learned this fortnight and I am not going to learn anything from it.”

“On a Monday they stay. I have stood at the top of this step on a Monday for eleven years and about nine of them stay until the light goes off, and on every other morning half of them are gone by the ninth hour.”

“And it has not once occurred to me until this morning that I have been standing here in front of fewer people than I thought. Eleven years of asking, and I have been asking the front half of a bank.”

“That is not a discovery. I cannot read and I cannot count a bank. It is just the shape of a Monday.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-four is eight hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-six mornings. A man of thirty-one has just given Monday away to a burnt run and a boy of thirteen has not been asked for Monday and is not going to be.”

“And the man of fifty-four says the bank is smaller on a Monday than he thought. It is not smaller. It is the same bank and about half of them went down early. Those are two different mistakes and he has made one and I have made the other.”

That week ended on the step at about the eleventh hour with about nine of them still on the bank, and the light went off the top of it, and there was no column on that landing anywhere, and on the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, a Tuesday, it began.
