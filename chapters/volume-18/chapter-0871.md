# Chapter 871: About Forty Leaves And No Figure Of Days

Fifth day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and twenty-first morning. Four hundred and seventy-one days after the settlement. The fever eighty-four weeks old. Four hundred and twenty-five days since the division. Three hundred and seventy-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was three days of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave both figures, said the word holds out loud, and said he was not going to stop saying it on a Saturday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth of the four is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and he has given the inches first all this fortnight and is not going to change the order for a Saturday.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, three days of the coming back, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and it holds.”

“I am saying the word holds on a Saturday because a Saturday is the morning you would all expect a man of forty-three to soften something. It is holding. Sixteen is holding and it has been holding since the Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week, and softening is not a figure of inches.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked a man of about fifty-two, out loud and in front of the whole bank, what a run of days is for when the thing it is about is not one of the nine. He gave the question whole. He did not give it as an example. He said the words *not one of the nine* on purpose and looked at the man of fifty-two while he said them.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and seventeen days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-six days old and nobody has said out loud whether it will keep.

“Three hundred and seventeen days in this county. I have added it up every morning since the first of it and it has not slipped once. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-six days old.”

“And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and seventy-one days old. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it, and I am going to say that figure out loud on every morning of this fortnight whatever it costs me.”

“And that is the eleventh wrong thing I have said, and it is the eleventh morning, and it is a figure of things said and not a figure of me. Some of you have been counting them since the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-fifth week. I have noticed. I am not going to stop.”

“So here is the eleventh, and it is a question and I am asking it out loud instead of carrying it about. What is a run of days for, when the thing it is about is not one of the nine. Not one of the nine. A man of fifty-two has spent a fortnight on six of the nine and three of the nine and there is a gate at the bottom of that bank that nobody has counted all year.”

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and he is the only man on that landing who can put a line under six of the nine positions and not under the other three.

“A run is not the position. I said that on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week and I am saying it now on a Saturday, and it is the same sentence and it is not a new one and it is not progress.”

“A position that is not a stone is not a thing in a list. It is a day somebody did the work. The only evidence is the line somebody entered that day. The run is where the day is written down.”

“And I am going to say the rest of it, because on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week the first piece of it was the only piece anybody took, and this is a Saturday and there is time.”

“A position may be checked from the day its record starts. It may not be checked from before that day. And the day its record starts is a figure of a thing, and it is owed to nobody, and it is a different figure every morning.”

“That is not the right of refusal and nobody on this landing may call it that in a mouth. A run with a hole in it is not it either. An incomplete account is not it either. A day entered on a piece of paper binds nobody and protects nobody.”

“What three of the nine positions are is still open. I am not going to be the one who answers it. You all want me to answer it because I am the only man here who can put a line under six. I cannot put a line under six and then tell you what the other three are. Those are two different pieces of work and one of them is finished.”

“There will not be a fifteenth. I said that on the Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I am saying it on a Saturday, and if anybody wants a fifteenth they can go and find their own.”

Then the man who keeps a run for a ferry below the low gate came up the bank and gave his figure, and it was not a figure of days.

Linnet Ord is thirty-one, and he is not the other thirty-one, and he has kept a boat below the low gate since he was a boy, and he keeps a run of days for that boat. One line a day. What the boat did.

“My name is Linnet Ord. I am thirty-one, two of you have just worked out that there are two thirty-ones on this bank, and I have been the other one all morning.”

“About forty leaves. That is the figure and it is the whole of the figure and I am giving it out loud because nobody has ever asked me for it.”

“And there is no figure of days. I want that said where all of you can hear it. There is no day it started and there is no figure of days behind it, and I am not going to tell you how long I have kept that boat because I do not know how long I have kept it.”

“The first leaves are not gone. Nobody burnt them. They are not under water. They were not cut out and used. They were never written on. That is the whole of what is wrong with my run and it is not a wound, it is a beginning with nothing at the front of it.”

Ilyan Vester then said that about nine of them had watched the boat go across and come back, and that about nine was the number a ferry run wanted, and he said it before he had thought about it.

Linnet Ord did not answer him at once.

“No. You did not ask me and I am not going to say how I work it out.”

“About nine of you is a figure of a bank. It is not a figure of anybody. It is not consent and it is not a count of witnesses and it is not a thing a run of days is for.”

“My run is for one thing and one line. Somebody shut the gate at the low end and somebody carried somebody over and then carried them back, and there is one line on one leaf saying so, in my hand, on the day it happened. If the boat did not go, there is one line saying it did not go and why.”

“And you have all watched me do it for nineteen years, and not one of you has ever been asked to write a line, and not one of you is going to be. You may watch. Watching is not entering. About four of you standing on a bank watching a boat is not a run and it is not a day anybody did the work.”

Ilyan Vester said that about nine of them seeing a thing was a check on it, and that a run nobody checked was a habit with leaves in it.

“Then say it that way to them and not to me. I have been in this county three hundred and seventeen days and I have been wrong eleven times in about four days and every one of the eleven was me putting a number where a person was.”

Linnet Ord said that was the eleventh and that he was not going to be entered on anything by a man of thirty-one.

“I am not going to be entered on anything by you. Not on a list, not on a page, not on a fourth thing with nobody holding it. You may say my figure out loud after me and you may say it wrong and I will not correct you in front of the bank.”

“Go and ask a man of forty-three a question. He has given four figures every morning for nineteen years and he can tell you what a run of days is for, because he has never once claimed that what he does is a position.”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read refused a seventh thing in a new shape, and gave the working, and did not let the shape go quiet.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and fifty mornings, which is eight hundred and seventy-one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and fifty. And a man of thirty-one has just worked out that about nine of you could stand on that bank and watch a boat and call it a check on it. No. And I am refusing it a seventh time and the shape is new and I am going to say the shape out loud so that nobody can say I have been refusing the same thing for a fortnight.”

“A figure of about four of you who will speak is not consent. It has never been consent. It was not consent when a woman of twenty-nine said it against her own name in a reed, and it was not consent when a woman of forty-four said it on a Friday, and it is not consent this morning for a boat.”

“And here is the working and I have worked it out myself and I am not asking anybody. If about four of them were consent, then about four of them could have taken a shovel to my gravel on any morning of the last eleven years and called it done. Nobody did. And that is not because you were asked and not because you were not asked. It is because a shovel in my gravel at six in the morning is not the same fact as my gravel at six in the morning.”

“And about nine of you is worse and not better. About nine of you is a bank. A bank is a place where a thing has happened, and a thing that has happened in front of nine people has happened once.”

“So a man of thirty-one may watch a boat all morning and write down that about nine of them watched it, and that is his own run and it is about him, and I am not going to be in it.”

Orla Fennimore went up the cart road at about the ninth hour, on foot, with her book under her arm. She said what she was going to do up there and said what it was not.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week she put her question down for the fourth time, unchanged, one word of it not altered.

“I am going up four miles with the book and I am going to do the same thing I have done for nineteen years on a Saturday, and that is not a decision and nobody is going to call it one.”

“And somebody had tomorrow on the wrong day. I said on the Wednesday and the Thursday and the Friday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that I was going home on Saturday. This is the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and it is today. One of you was a week out, and a week is not a small thing to be out by on a morning like this.”

“Nobody is to be thanked for it. I said it on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I am saying it on the way up the road.”

At about the tenth hour a boy of thirteen counted the figure in the lane before the light came up, and a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run and gave a figure of mornings, and a man of fifty-four asked again.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and eighteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and eighteen is eight hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and eighteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“And nobody has asked me for the count I am keeping. That is ten mornings and I am not going to say what it is, and you have all stopped asking, and I am still counting.”

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has entered a line on that run every day for twenty-three years.

“That is the fourteenth morning in front of anybody. It is a figure of mornings and it is not a figure of how many of you were here.”

“And a man of thirty-one has spent his morning asking a man of fifty-two what a run of days is for, and the man of fifty-two told him. I am not going to repeat it. A thing said out loud in front of this bank is not improved by a woman of forty-nine saying it again behind his back.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fiftieth morning, and said what he had worked out on the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week.

“A hundred and fifty, and it is a Saturday, and I worked out a thing on the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I have not said it to anybody yet, and I am not saying it to a man of thirty-one.”

“What I worked out is that there is nothing on my line to go and look for. Not one leaf torn, not one page cut, not one stitch gone. Whole book, a leaf for every day. So the hole is not the figure and what I said out loud on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-fourth week was about a woman of forty-nine’s book and not about mine.”

“I am going to ask somebody for a different thing on Monday. It is not this. Four of you will be disappointed by a man of fifty-four on a Monday and that is the correct number to be disappointed by.”

Nobody went down the bank. A woman of forty-four went up the cart road with a book she did not open on the way, and a man of thirty-one stood where a man who keeps a boat had put him, and sixteen inches held on the top step until the light went off it.
