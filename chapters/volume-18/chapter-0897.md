# Chapter 897: Sixty-Three Stitches, Said Out Loud In A Room With The Door Shut

Third day of the hundred and fiftieth week. His six hundred and forty-seventh morning. Four hundred and ninety-seven days after the settlement. The fever eighty-seven weeks and five days old. Four hundred and fifty-one days since the division. Three hundred and ninety-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches held on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure, with one day of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and named the next one for the last time, with the week on it, and said it was a Friday and not a Sunday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has carried the sixth off-morning in his own mouth since he named it on the third day of the hundred and forty-ninth week.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the third day of the hundred and fiftieth week, which is a Thursday.”

“Tomorrow it comes off. It comes off at about the sixth hour tomorrow morning and all ninety of those steps are walkable by the seventh. That is the fourth day of the hundred and fiftieth week, which is a Friday, and that is the twenty-ninth time.”

“I have said that day twice and I am going to say it a third time today and then I am going to stop saying it, because a day that gets said out loud three times in about nine ears stops being a thing somebody might get wrong and starts being a thing somebody might repeat.”

Then the man of forty-three said the two halves of a thing he said on the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, in the same words, out loud, about a room.

“Counting it is cheap. Counting a gap is the cheapest thing anybody on this landing could do this week, and it is cheap in the way a thing is cheap when it takes no time and costs nobody anything and cannot be undone. And that is the half that about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for, and it has been sitting on that counter since the third day of the hundred and forty-fourth week.”

“Acting on the count is not cheap. It is not the same job. It is not even the same kind of job. And it is done by a person, in a room, on a morning, with the figure in front of them and another person in the room who is allowed to say no. And that half is the one nobody on this landing has done, and I am not going to count how many of you have done it, because that would be a figure of people.”

“I said that on the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week with nothing in it and I am saying it again now that there is going to be a room, and I am saying it about the room and not about the boy, and there is still no figure in it and I am not putting one there. I am not going to say it a fourth time this week.”

---

The room had twenty-eight chairs in it and the twenty-ninth was against a wall in the passage, and the door was shut at about the second hour. The four who had gone up the road were in it, and the woman of forty-four, and the woman of forty-nine with the bundle of about four inches under her arm, and the boy of thirteen.

Orla Fennimore said the working out loud first, before anything else, because she had said it twice this week on a step and she wanted it said in a room as well.

“A day somebody does a thing and somebody else puts down that they did it. That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it since I said it on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, and I am not going to say it a better way than I said it then.”

“A line in somebody else’s hand. Not mine. That is the whole of what I have wanted for nineteen years and it is four inches long and it is about a chair.”

“And I want to say it here because there are four of you in this room who can write and there is one man in here who cannot, and the one who cannot is going to be given a figure in about nine seconds, and I am not going to have one single person in this room come out of it with a page.”

Sena Dorr put the bundle on the table with its front edge toward the boy, and then stepped back from it, and said she was going to stand where he could see her the whole time.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and forty-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and forty-four is eight hundred and ninety-seven less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-six mornings and I am not giving it and I am not going to give it after this either.”

“I counted the stitches in the gap at the front of that this morning before I came up the road. I counted them twice. I got sixty-three both times and I am going to say sixty-three and I am not going to say it twice because saying it twice is what a person does when they have not got it right the first time.”

“Sixty-three.”

“That is a figure of stitches. It is not a figure of days. Nor is it a figure of leaves. And it is not a figure of anything that anybody in this room has done.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked for a second count, in a room with the door shut, in about five ears, and was refused in a voice that did not rise.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and forty-three days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and twelve days old.

“Three hundred and forty-three days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and twelve days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and ninety-seven days old, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it on any morning of this week.”

“Thirty-seven wrong things in thirty-seven mornings. Count it again. Not by him. By one of you.”

“No, and I am not going to be angry about it, because you are in a room with a door shut and you have been in rooms with shut doors before.”

“A second count is not a check. It is a second figure. If he counts it again and gets sixty-three again then there are two figures of sixty-three and about four of you will write both of them down and then there will be a count of counts, and nobody will ever be able to tell which one of the two is the one that was in the stitching.”

“If you want it checked, then somebody has to put their hand on the edge of that thing and look at the same holes at the same time and say yes or no out loud while he is counting. That is not a second count. That is a person in the room who is allowed to say no.”

“And nobody in this room has done that and I am not asking anybody to do it now. I counted it twice on my own and the twice is the whole of the check and it is not a very good check and it is the one there is.”

“And I am not going to be the second person on that. I have been refused for offering to be a second person all week and I am not going to become one by being the only person who agreed to look.”

Sena Dorr said what she was going to do with the figure, out loud, in the room, with it in front of her and the boy standing where she could be answered.

“Then I am going to say what I do with it, and he can stop me if I am wrong and he has been asked to and he has not, so I am going ahead.”

“I am not taking it four hundred miles. I am not putting nine people’s counting in front of nine clerks. I am not entering it on that run, because a run of days takes a line about a day and this is not a day, and I said so on the second day of the hundred and fiftieth week to a man who had come very close to being right and I am not going to un-say it to be kind to a room.”

“It came into this bank this morning with a hand on it, and it is going out of this bank at about the ninth hour in the same bundle, and it is standing on that counter at the eleventh hour, and that is the whole of its journey and it is not a disappointing journey. It is a small one and it is the correct size.”

“And here is the reason, out loud, in a room, because a man of thirty-one has been refused six mornings running for putting things next to each other and he is going to do it again in about nine seconds if I do not.”

“Those sixty-three are stitches and not days and they are never going to be days. The leaves they stand for were cut out of that book and used, and nobody alive saw them cut, and there is no morning behind them that a person could put a hand on. A figure of stitches is a figure of stitches. It is not a smaller figure of days and it is not a bigger one.”

“I have said that on this landing before. I am not saying it now because I found it. I am saying it now because there is a figure in the room and about four of you have not heard the reason yet.”

“And nobody is going to be thanked for it. Not the boy, who is thirteen, and not the woman who owns the room, and not me. Nobody on this landing is going to be thanked for anything this week and I want that written nowhere at all.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He has been keeping a count that nobody has asked him for for thirty-six mornings, and he named the kind of figure it is on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, and he has not given it and is not going to.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and has put her question down five times and is not going to put it down a sixth, and she said that on the way out of the room with the door open again.

“And I am not putting sixty-three on that sheet and neither is anybody else.”

“It is not mine and it is not hers and it is not a day, and if it goes on that paper then in about nine weeks there will be a figure of stitches and a line about a chair on one page, and about four of you will read the pair of them as the same kind of thing, and they are not the same kind of thing, and they are the only two things that are going on it.”

“That sheet is going to have one line on it and it is going to be about a chair and it is going to be in a hand that is not mine. If anybody puts a second thing on it then I will take the sheet back and I will not be asked why.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and forty-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up.

“Two hundred and forty-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-six mornings, and I have said one figure out loud today and it is sixty-three and it is stitches, and the figure I have been keeping is thirty-six, and about four of you have been carrying one of them all week and they are not the same figure and they are not the same kind of figure.”

“That is the last time I am saying anything about either of them. I said I would not be thanked for what I do and I am not, and being left alone is not the same as being asked, and today somebody asked me and I did it and neither of those two things is the other one.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and seventy-sixth morning, in a passage, with a chair standing in it, after the door had been opened again.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and seventy-six mornings, which is eight hundred and ninety-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and seventy-six. And I was in the room. I want that said and I want it said first, before anything else anybody says about it.”

“I cannot read and I was in a room with a door shut and about four of us heard a number and I have got it and nobody had to write it down for me, and that is exactly what I asked for on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week.”

“And I am not going to say the number. It is not mine and it was not given to me and I am not going to be the man on this step who says a figure out of a room.”

“And the chair went back in at about the third hour while I was standing in that passage and I saw it go and I am the only person in this county who has seen it go.”

The bundle was on the counter at about the eleventh hour of the third day of the hundred and fiftieth week where a woman of forty-nine had put it herself, and about nine of them were on the landing, and not one of them had written sixty-three on anything.
