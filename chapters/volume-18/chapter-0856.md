# Chapter 856: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fourth Week, And A Friday, And Sixteen Inches Holding With Two Days Of The Coming Back And Tomorrow It Comes Off At The Sixth Hour For The Twenty-Fourth Time, And Three Figures Are Put Down Side By Side On The Dry Stone, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Day Is A Day And Is Standing Next To The Man Who Said It First, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Puts Her Question Down For The Third Time And Is Not Answered

Fourth day of the hundred and forty-fourth week. His six hundred and sixth morning. Four hundred and fifty-six days after the settlement. The fever eighty-one weeks and six days old. Four hundred and ten days since the division. Three hundred and fifty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank held at sixteen with two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three said the word for the coming back out loud, gave the hour it is going to happen at, gave the number of the time, and said the count was his and not anybody’s.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and about sixty of them are under this morning, and he has given it five mornings running.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is two days of the coming back, and it holds, and about sixty of those ninety steps are under.*”

“*And tomorrow at about the sixth hour the water comes off this bank again, and all ninety of those steps will be walkable by about the seventh, and that is the twenty-fourth time.*”

“*I have given that number for twenty-four mornings now, and nobody has ever asked me to stop giving it, and about four of you have started saying it after me, and I am not going to ask which of you.*”

At about the ninth hour the three figures went down side by side on the dry stone. About nine of them stood round them, and a man of about fifty-two put his own hand flat on the stone and said there was no sum of them.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a palm and a forearm, and named a thirteenth thing he cannot check on the Thursday of last week, and has six days of his own fortnight left. He put down a wedge of burnt leaves, a figure of inches, and a gap with a stitch-edge on it.

“*Here are the three, in about nine of your ears, and here is what each one is a figure of. That is a figure of leaves. That is a figure of inches of water over a stone step. That is a figure of holes in a stitch-edge.*”

“*And I said on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week that a figure of six, a figure of three and a figure of four people are three figures of three different things. I have not said anything better than that since, and I am not going to try to say it better this morning.*”

“*Three figures of three different things. There is no sum of them, and there was no sum of them before, and the three of them are the whole of what has been put in front of about nine of you this week.*”

“*And I have six days of my fortnight left and I am not going to name a fourteenth thing I cannot check. I want that said in the same breath as the three, because about four of you have been waiting for a fourteenth to make the three into one.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave a count and not an argument. He had put the three figures down himself on a piece of board, he was not going to make a figure of the three of them, and he gave his own figure with the subtraction in it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and three days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and two is eight hundred and fifty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and two days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*I wrote the three figures on a board this morning and I am not going to add them up, and I want about nine of you to hear that I decided that before I knew what the answer would be.*”

“*A figure of leaves and a figure of inches and a figure of holes are three figures, and three is a figure of figures and not a figure of anything on that stone. If I add them I have made a fourth thing, and the fourth thing is a figure of me.*”

“*That is the whole of my arithmetic for the day. I have not got a figure of how many of the nine of us are standing here, and I am not going to give one. A figure of people is the only figure this week that nobody asked me for and that I would not give.*”

Then a woman of forty-four came up that cart road for the second time in about nine days. She put her question down for the third time and gave the working of it out loud, so that about nine of them could see the shape of it. It was not answered.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Eleven of those chairs have a name against them in a book and nobody in them. On the third day of this week she named two of the eleven as dead and said the first has no day written down because there is no form that asks for one.

“*I am going to say my question for the third time in about nine of your ears, and I am not going to change a word of it. About four of you have heard the other two and can check that I have not.*”

“*The question is this. Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it down, and is that day the same as a day that was put down at the time?*”

“*I have asked that three times and nobody has answered it, and I am not going to ask it a fourth time in about nine days. I am asking it now in front of about nine of you and then I am going to stop asking, and stopping is not the same as being answered, and I want that said in about nine of your ears.*”

“*Here is the working of the question, because a question without its working is a complaint. I am the only person who knows when the first of the eleven died, and I have had it in my own head for nineteen years, and I can put it on a piece of paper this afternoon.*”

“*And a piece of paper with a day on it that I wrote this afternoon is not the same as a piece of paper with a day on it that a person wrote down on the day. And I do not know whether that is because of the paper or because of the nineteen years, and nobody has ever told me which.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said a sentence a man of fifty-four has been saying on that landing for eleven years. A man of fifty-four said it was his and said what it does, and the man of thirty-one found that a day is a day is not an answer.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and two days, three hundred and two being eight hundred and fifty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seventy-one days old, a hundred and seventy-one being eight hundred and fifty-six less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*It is the same. A day is a day, and if it is written down then it is written down, and that is the answer to your question and I am giving it to you in about nine of your ears.*”

Barnaby Crove, fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, cannot read, and has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and thirty-five mornings, which is eight hundred and fifty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*That is my sentence.*”

“*I have said that on this bank for eleven years, a hundred and thirty-five mornings about a day on a book four hundred miles off, and it has never once been an answer to that woman’s question. I would like about nine of you to notice that a sentence can be right and be no use at all.*”

“*A day is a day is what I say so that the asking does not turn into a fight. It is not an answer about a day that gets written down late, and about four of you have just heard a man of thirty-one take my sentence and put it where it does not work.*”

“*And I am not angry with him, because he has never once pretended to be me, and I am not going to start. I am saying the sentence is mine and that it is being used today, and about four of you heard it used before I said it.*”*

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, took it back in about nine ears. A woman of sixty-four had told him that a rule and an instruction look the same, and he had just done that with a sentence. He gave his own two counters with the subtraction in them.

“*Then it is gone, and she is right that I took it, and I am not going to pretend I said it because I have not read it.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four told me on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-second week that for nineteen years she could not tell a rule from an instruction, because both of them are in ink and both of them are said in a room with the door shut.*”

“*And I have just done that with a sentence of a man of fifty-four’s, out loud, on a bank, in about nine of your ears. Ink and a public mouth. Same shape. I am not going to name it anything better than that.*”

“*Three hundred and two days in this county, and that is eight hundred and fifty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy-one days old, and that is eight hundred and fifty-six less six hundred and eighty-five.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the want he had out loud, and it was a book. A woman of forty-four said the book would not take it and gave the working, and about nine of them stood round three figures that did not add up.

“*I want you to have a day-book. The same one a man of fifty-four has, with a leaf for every day, and I will pay for it out of what I get for carrying gravel in a week, and about nine of you saw me pay for one on Sunday.*”

Orla Fennimore, forty-four, said no to the book and not to the wanting, and said the thing about a leaf out loud, and said that nobody was to thank her for the shape of her refusal.

“*No, and I am not saying no to you wanting it. I am saying no to a day-book, and there is a difference and I would like it kept.*”

“*A leaf for a day, and one line on that leaf. My question is not a day’s work. It is a question. If I put it on a leaf it is on that leaf and on no other, about four of you would find it on the Saturday, and about nine of you would never find it at all.*”

“*And I have had this counter for nineteen years. Thirteen shillings and fourpence. I know exactly what a book that is entered in costs and what it is worth, and this is a day-book, and I know the price of this because I have been charging it.*”

“*So I am not going to take one. And I am not going to be thanked for the shape of that, and if a man of thirty-one walks off this landing thinking a woman of forty-four refused him, he has got it the wrong way round and I would rather he had it the wrong way round than the other.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Friday, with two days of the coming back and tomorrow the twenty-fourth time, and three figures on a dry stone that a man of about fifty-two and a boy of thirteen had both refused to add together.

The fever was eighty-one weeks and six days old. He had been in that county three hundred and two days, and the cut across that palm was a hundred and seventy-one days old, and a hundred and fifty-five days after an offer that was asked at the seven-hundredth and has not been answered.

The bank held at sixteen with two days of the coming back, and about sixty of the ninety steps were under. Tomorrow the water comes off again at about the sixth hour, and that is the twenty-fourth time. The man of forty-three has given that number for twenty-four mornings.
The three figures went down side by side on the dry stone. A figure of leaves, a figure of inches of water over a stone step, and a figure of holes in a stitch-edge. A man of about fifty-two said there was no sum of them, and that he had said so on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week.

A boy of thirteen wrote the three figures on a board and did not add them. Three is a figure of figures and not a figure of anything on that stone, and adding them would make a fourth thing and the fourth thing is a figure of him. He gave no figure of how many were standing there, and said that was the only figure he would not give.

A woman of forty-four put her question down for the third time, unchanged. Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it, and is that day the same as a day put down at the time. She said she is the only person who knows when the first of the eleven died.

And a man of thirty-one said it is the same, that a day is a day, and that it is the answer to her question. A man of fifty-four said that is his sentence, that he has said it for eleven years, and that it has never once been an answer to that woman’s question, and that a sentence can be right and be no use at all.
He took it back, and said a woman of sixty-four had told him he could not tell a rule from an instruction, and that he had just done the same with a sentence of a man of fifty-four’s, out loud, on a bank.

And he asked a woman of forty-four to have a day-book and said he would pay for it. She said no to the book and not to the wanting, and said her question is not a day’s work, and that a leaf for a day would hold it on that leaf and on no other, and that she knows the price of a book that is entered in.
