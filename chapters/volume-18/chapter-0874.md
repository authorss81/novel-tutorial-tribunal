# Chapter 874: First Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Seventh Week, And A Tuesday, And The Water Came Off All Ninety Of Those Steps And That Is The Twenty-Sixth Time And There Is No Figure Of Inches And No Figure Of Steps Under, And A Man Who Keeps A Run Of Days For A Low Gate Says The Ordinary Form Out Loud And Says What It Does Not Do, And A Man Who Keeps A Ferry Says A Record With A Hole At The Front Of It May Be Relied On For The Years It Does Cover

First day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. His six hundred and twenty-fourth morning. Four hundred and seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever eighty-four weeks and three days old. Four hundred and twenty-eight days since the division. Three hundred and seventy-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh. That was the twenty-sixth time. The man of forty-three gave the hour, the count and the number of the time, and said there was no figure of inches and no figure of steps under and no figure of days of the coming back, and gave the day of the week on both sides of the boundary.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gives four figures every morning off that stone. On an off-morning the fourth of the four has nothing to be about, and he has said so for nineteen years and has never once made it a fifth thing.

“*It came off at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps are walkable now, and that is the twenty-sixth time.*”

“*There is no figure of inches this morning. Nothing stands on that step. Nor is there a figure of steps under, because there is nothing over them, and there is no figure of days of the coming back. That is not a gap in my morning. It is a morning where the fourth thing has nothing to be about.*”

“*Today is the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week. Yesterday was the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. I am going to say both of those out loud and about four of you are going to write one of them down and forget the other, and that is what I am doing this for.*”

“*And the next one is in eight days, which is the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, and that is a Wednesday, and that is the twenty-seventh time. A man of forty-three has named that day four mornings running and has never been asked to check it, and I would like it noted that I have named it four times and not once with the day of the week left off.*”

Then a man of forty-seven stood up off the flat stone above the cut and said the ordinary form out loud, in about nine of your ears, and then said what it does not do, and said it was a thing about a gate.

Rod Veal is forty-seven and keeps a run of days for the low gate, and his run is two hundred and six days long and the anchor is the first evening he shut that gate himself and wrote the line on the same night in his own hand.

“*A run with a hole in its beginning is still entered on today. I am saying that out loud in about nine of your ears and it is about my gate and it is not about the nine and it is not about anybody in this county.*”

“*A position may be checked from the day its record starts. It may not be checked from before that day. And the day its record starts is a figure of a thing and it is owed to nobody, and it is a different figure every morning and it is not a figure of anybody.*”

“*And here is the part about four of you will leave out when you repeat it, so I am saying it before you can. That is not the right of refusal. Nobody on this landing may call it that in a mouth, and I am not calling it that.*”

“*A run with a hole in it is not it either. An incomplete account is not it either. A day entered on a piece of paper binds nobody and protects nobody and I have heard a woman of forty-four say that on this bank twice and she is right.*”

“*And what three of the nine positions are is still open. I am not going to touch it. I keep a gate. A gate is a gate.*”

“*You may write that down and you may put it in my mouth and it will be a wrong sentence by about Wednesday, because the ordinary form is not a gate and a gate is not the ordinary form. What I am saying this morning is that a run of days is the only place that a gate got shut lands, and that has nothing to do with what three of the nine positions are and everything to do with my own two hundred and six days.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for it. It is a Tuesday. I do not care what day of the week it is.*”

Then the man who keeps a run for a ferry below the low gate said out loud what a hole at the front of a run is, and said what it is worth.

Linnet Ord is thirty-one, and he is not the other thirty-one, and his figure is about forty leaves and he has no figure of days at all.

“*Yesterday a man of thirty-one told you my figure and said it was about forty leaves and no days. Today I am going to say the rest of it, because on an off-morning about four of you think nothing happened and something happened.*”

“*There is no day at the front of my run. Not because the leaves are gone. Nobody burnt them and nobody drowned them and nobody cut them out and used them. They were never written on. My father had the boat and my father did not keep a run, and the day I started was a day nobody wrote down.*”

“*So there is no figure of days on me and there never will be one and I am not going to be asked to guess one by anybody this morning.*”

“*Now here is what a hole at the front of a record is, and I have thought about it for a week and I have had it wrong twice.*”

“*It costs nothing. That is the whole of it. You cannot check a position before the day its record starts, and you cannot check my boat before the day my run starts, and there is no day my run starts, so there was never anything there for anybody to lose.*”

“*A record with a hole at the front of it may be relied on for the years it does cover. That is not a thing I have read. That is a thing I have been doing on that boat every morning for nineteen years, and about forty leaves is what I have got, and every one of the forty is a day I was there and carried somebody both ways.*”

“*I cannot tell you how long. That is a different thing from it not being a run. I have been mixing those two up since the Thursday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I have stopped, and that is five days and I have counted them.*”

Then a man of thirty-one offered to give a man of thirty-one the day his run started, as a figure of days, and was refused by him.

“*You do not have one. Do not give me one. There is nothing to give.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twenty days, three hundred and twenty being eight hundred and seventy-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-nine days old, a hundred and eighty-nine being eight hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*I have three figures this morning and the fourth of them is empty and I am going to fill it. That is the fourteenth wrong thing and it is the fourteenth morning, and it is a figure of things said and not a figure of me.*”

“*Three hundred and twenty days in this county, and that is eight hundred and seventy-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-nine days old, and that is eight hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and seventy-four days old, and a hundred and seventy-four is eight hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred. It is unanswered and I am not going to ask it and I am not going to give up saying it.*”

“*So here it is. You have a run with nothing at the front of it, and I am going to put a day at the front of it for you, and I will take it off a run of my own, because a run of mine has a day at the front of it that I know, and if I put mine at the front of yours then you have a figure of days and about nine of us can go and stand in front of your two hundred and six.*”

Linnet Ord said no three times and then said the rest of it once.

“*No. No. And no.*”

“*That is not yours to give away either. You have said the number yourself this morning, three hundred and twenty days in this county, and if you take a day off the front of that and put it on my run then you have made a hole in a run that has none, and you have done it to help me, and about four of us would never let you forget it.*”

“*And a hole at the front of a run costs nothing. I worked that out on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and said it on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I said it again this morning. What I want is not a day at the front of it. What I want is for about nine of you to know that a run with no front is still a run, and to go and stop looking for the front of mine.*”

“*I am not going to take a figure off you for the front of my own run and that is the end of what I will take off you this week.*”

Then a boy of thirteen counted the figure in the lane before the light came up, and a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line, and a man of fifty-four asked for the hundred and fifty-third morning and said what he was not going to do on the off-morning.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty-one is eight hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and twenty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*Nobody has asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirteen mornings. On an off-morning nobody can say a morning was wasted and I want that written down as a fact about the count and not about the morning.*”

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has kept the ninth of the nine positions for twenty-three years, and that is a public archive and not a stone.

“*That is the seventeenth morning in front of anybody and it is a figure of mornings and it is entered in front of about nine of them on an off-morning as well, because it is a Tuesday and I enter it on Tuesdays.*”

“*And a man of forty-seven has just said a thing on that bank that I said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and he said it about a gate, and I am not going to claim it and I am not going to improve it, and about four of you are already deciding which of us said it first.*”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fifty-third morning, and said what an off-morning is for a man who asks.

“*A hundred and fifty-three, and it is an off-morning, and on an off-morning I have less to hold on to and that is when about four of you would like me to say something different.*”

“*And I am not. I am going to ask a woman of thirty-three for a day and I do not have the day and I have told about nine of you that I do not have the day, and that is the first time in eleven years I have said out loud that I do not know what I am going to ask for.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine told you on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week that the three blanks were never blanks. A man of forty-seven told you this morning that a run with a hole at the front of it is entered on today. And a man of fifty-four who cannot read has still got nothing, and nobody is going to be thanked and nobody has been.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Tuesday, the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, with all ninety of those steps dry, and no figure of inches anywhere on the bank.

---

The water came off all ninety of those steps at about the sixth hour, and that was the twenty-sixth time, and there was no figure of inches and no figure of steps under and no figure of days of the coming back. The man of forty-three said today is the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and yesterday was the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-sixth, and named the next one as the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and the twenty-seventh time.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for the hundred and fifty-third morning and said out loud, for the first time in eleven years, that he does not know what he is going to ask for. Nobody on that bank gave him anything, and nobody is going to be thanked.

A man who keeps a run of days for a low gate said the ordinary form out loud and said what it does not do. A run with a hole in its beginning is still entered on today. A position may be checked from the day its record starts and not from before it. It is not the right of refusal and may not be called it in a mouth. A run with a hole in it is not it either. An incomplete account is not it either. And what three of the nine positions are is still open, and he is not going to touch it.

A man of thirty-one tried to fill an empty off-morning by putting a day from his own run at the front of another man’s, and was refused three times, and told that the hole in his costs nothing and does not need filling.

And a man who keeps a run of days for a ferry said that nobody burnt his first leaves and nobody drowned them and nobody cut them out, and that they were never written on, and that a hole at the front of a record costs nothing, because there is no day to check from, and that a record with a hole at the front of it may be relied on for the years it does cover. He cannot tell you how long. That is a different thing from it not being a run.
