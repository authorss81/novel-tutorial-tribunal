# Chapter 881: A Working Day And Not A Settlement

First day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-first morning. Four hundred and eighty-one days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and three days old. Four hundred and thirty-five days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was one day of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the four figures and named the day he had named on the day before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gives four figures off one stone. He named this morning on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, with the day and the week on it, and it came out right.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, and it is holding there this morning.”

“Yesterday I said that today is the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and it is a Tuesday. It is the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and it is a Tuesday.”

“And the next off-morning is in one day, and that is the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, which is a Wednesday, and that is the twenty-seventh time. I have said that day three mornings running with the week on it and about four of you have written it down as the second day of the forty-seventh.”

Then a woman of forty-four said she was not going to put her question down a sixth time, and asked for a working day instead of a settlement.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She has put her question down five times. What she is owed is a working day and not a settlement.

“I have put it down five times. Can a day be put down for a thing that happened before anybody thought to write it. And is that day the same as a day put down at the time. One word of that has never moved and it is not going to move this morning.”

“And I am not going to put it down a sixth. I am going to ask you for something else and it is smaller and it is not what I want.”

“I am asking for a working day. Not a settlement. A working day is a thing a person can be given. There are people on this bank who could be given one and none of you has offered one and I have been waiting nineteen years for somebody to think of it.”

Then she named the woman at the top of that bank, and gave her name and her age and her trade out loud, and said she was not going to ask her anything.

“There is a woman at the top of this bank washing the stone. She is the only person in this county who goes into other people’s rooms for a living and does not knock, because a room is empty while she is in it.”

“Her name is Hannah Voss and she is forty-one and she washes lime. I am saying her name and her age and her trade out loud before anybody asks her anything, because about four of you have been saying her trade for nine days without her name on it. I am not asking her anything this morning.”

Hannah Voss is forty-one and washes lime, and said one thing nobody had asked her for.

“I am not going to be asked anything. I am going to say one thing anyway, because you have all been talking on that bank for nine days about a thing I do every morning and never decided to do.”

“Here is what I do. There is paper in walls. Not writing on it. Pasted, or folded in, or laid in the plaster to hold the damp out. Every room in this county that has been papered twice has some in it. I take it down when I wash. It goes in a bucket and the bucket goes in my fire, because lime and paper do not go together.”

“That is a fact about walls. It is not a fact about nine of anything and I am not going to be the person who makes it one.”

“And here is the piece I have. You cannot do that in another person’s house on your own. Not because of the law. Because of what a person brings back down the road.”

“If I go in alone and I ask, and they say no, then I come down that road with a no. And a no is not a thing. It is not a figure and it is not a day and it cannot be carried anywhere by anybody.”

“And if I go in alone and they let me in, then what is in the wall is in my bucket and my bucket is in my fire by the Thursday of that week. And nobody on this bank will ever know whether I looked properly or whether I got tired on a Thursday and stopped halfway up a staircase.”

“So it may not be done alone. I have not worked out whether it can be done at all and I am not going to work it out standing on a ladder with a brush in my hand.”

Then a man of thirty-one divided four hundred miles into weeks of houses, and was given the cost of it by the woman he had divided it for.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twenty-seven days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ninety-six days old.

“Three hundred and twenty-seven days in this county, and that is eight hundred and eighty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-six days old, and that is eight hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.”

“The standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-one days old. It is unanswered and I have not asked it.”

“Twenty-one wrong things in twenty-one mornings, and here is the twenty-first. Four hundred miles is not a distance. It is a number of houses. And I have it in weeks. Six houses a week, and eight the week after, and it comes out even inside a year.”

Hannah Voss put her brush down in the bucket and said what a week of houses is.

“No. And that is the first mistake anybody has made on that bank that I know the cost of, and I know it because I have watched two lime-washers do it.”

“A house where they let you in is a day. Most of that day is standing on a ladder in a cold room waiting for somebody to make up their mind about the skirting. A house where they do not let you in takes nothing at all, and the nothing is the whole of what it takes.”

“So you have put a figure of houses on a piece of paper and called the piece of paper a week. And about four of you are going to copy it down, and then in a year somebody is going to be four hundred miles from here in a place where there is no wall left to look at.”

“And I am not going to be the one holding the piece of paper. Nobody is to give me a piece of paper.”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixtieth morning, and said the thing he could say about a figure he cannot read.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and sixty mornings, which is eight hundred and eighty-one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and sixty. And I cannot read, and there is a woman at the top of that bank washing the stone, and I am going to say the one thing I can say about what she said.”

“A man of fifty-four who cannot read can still tell which kind of figure a number is. She said six houses a week and she meant nothing. I have had a number in my mouth for eleven years that means nothing. And it is a different nothing. And I can hear it.”

“And she has not been asked anything, and I am not going to ask her anything either, and I would like that written down because about four of you are already walking towards her.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty-eight days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty-eight is eight hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and twenty-eight days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty mornings, and I am not going to say what it is, because I said on the Sunday of the hundred and forty-seventh week what kind of figure it is and I am not going to say it again in different words.”

“And a man of thirty-one has just put a number of houses on a piece of paper. I counted the houses on this bank from the top step before the light came. It is a different number and it is a number of houses and not a number of anything else. I am not saying mine and I am not saying his.”

The woman washed the last of that step and went down the cut with her bucket, and about four of them stayed on the bank after the light had come off the top of it, and nothing had been given to anybody, and a man of thirty-one was still standing where she had told him not to put a piece of paper, holding nothing at all.
