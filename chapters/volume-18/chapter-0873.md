# Chapter 873: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Sixth Week, And A Monday, And Sixteen Inches Holding With One Day Of The Coming Back And About Sixty Of Those Steps Under, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Cannot Read Asks A Woman Of Thirty-Three For One Day Of His Own And Is Not Refused And Is Not Answered Either, And A Man Of Thirty-One Tells Him What To Ask For And Is Refused

Seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. His six hundred and twenty-third morning. Four hundred and seventy-three days after the settlement. The fever eighty-four weeks and two days old. Four hundred and twenty-seven days since the division. Three hundred and seventy-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was one day of the coming back, which means it comes off all ninety of those steps in the morning. About sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave all three figures and said the last day of a week out loud.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gives four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour of every morning. He gave them this morning and then gave the day of the week, which he does on a last day.

“*Sixteen at the seventh hour. One day of the coming back, which means it comes off every one of those ninety steps in the morning. About sixty of them under. And it holds.*”

“*Today is the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. Tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and it is a Tuesday and the water will be off all ninety steps by about the sixth hour.*”

“*And the one after that is in nine days, and that is the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, which is a Wednesday, and that is the twenty-seventh time. I have named it once already this week to a man of thirty-eight and I am naming it to you again because a day that is only ever named once is a day somebody will get wrong.*”

At about the ninth hour a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked a woman of thirty-three for one day of his own, out loud, in about nine of your ears, and she did not refuse him.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and fifty-two mornings, which is eight hundred and seventy-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and fifty-two, and this morning I am not asking a woman of forty-nine for anything and I am not asking a boy of thirteen and I am not asking a man of about fifty-two.*”

“*I am asking you. And what I am asking for is one day. One day of mine, on your drain, one line, in your hand, on a day that I will name and you will write down when I name it.*”

Nessa Toomy is thirty-three and keeps a run of days for a drain, one leaf for one day, one line a day saying what the water was doing, and her run is a hundred and fifty-eight days long and the anchor is a step going in flat.

“*Say the rest of it. I have a wet hem and I have got about nine of you listening and if you have only asked me for half a thing this morning I would rather know before I answer the half.*”

“*A day entered on that binds nobody and protects nobody. A woman of forty-four has said that out loud on this landing twice and she is right and I am not going to pretend it is a smaller thing because it is on my step instead of on her book.*”

“*And I am going to write your line. I am going to write one line in my own hand on one of my own leaves on a day you name, and it will say that a man of fifty-four who cannot read was on that bank and what he did. Nothing else. Not that you were right about anything. Not that anybody answered you.*”

“*Which day.*”

“*I do not know. I told a man of about fifty-four and nine of you on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that I would say on the Monday and I did not say what I would say, and this is what I am saying, and I do not have the day.*”

“*Then I will hold the leaf back and I will not write it until you name one, and that is not me being hard and it is not me holding something over you. It is that a line is a thing that happened, and if I write a line for a day that has not happened then I have put a day on a run that nobody did.*”

“*And nobody is going to be thanked. Not by you and not by me. I am not doing this for you and you are not doing it for me and about nine of you are already writing it down as a thing that has been settled.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud what he thought the man of fifty-four should ask for, and was refused by the man of fifty-four.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and nineteen days, three hundred and nineteen being eight hundred and seventy-three less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-eight days old, a hundred and eighty-eight being eight hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then he should ask for a leaf. He has been asking a woman of forty-nine for a day against the ninth line for eleven years and the ninth line has a hole at the front of it and there is no day on it before the day it starts.*”

“*Three hundred and nineteen days in this county, and that is eight hundred and seventy-three less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-eight days old, and that is eight hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and eighty-five.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and seventy-three days old, and a hundred and seventy-three is eight hundred and seventy-three less seven hundred. It is unanswered and I have not asked it.*”

“*And a leaf, Barnaby. Go up that bank and ask a woman of forty-nine for one leaf of the ninth run. Not a day against your name. A leaf. Any leaf. Then you will know what a leaf is worth and about four of us will be able to say what a hole at the front of a run is.*”

Barnaby Crove said no, and said the reason with the working, and about nine of your ears heard it.

“*No. And that is my question and you have just handed me yours, and I want about nine of you to hear that a man of thirty-one has done it in about nine of your ears on a Monday.*”

“*You have been in this county three hundred and nineteen days and you worked out on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that about nine of you standing on a bank is a figure of a bank. And now you have gone through a woman of thirty-three who writes one line a day in her own hand and offered to put a leaf in somebody else’s run as though a leaf is a thing that changes hands.*”

“*I am not asking a woman of forty-nine for a leaf. I worked out on the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-sixth week that there is nothing on my line to go and look for, and if you go up there with a leaf under your arm you are going to come back with a fact about a leaf and I am going to be holding a fact about a leaf instead of the thing I worked out with my own hands on a Tuesday.*”

“*And you are not to carry it up that bank. That is the second time you have offered to carry something of mine to somebody and I have said no both times and I would like it noticed that I have said no both times.*”

Nessa Toomy said the man of thirty-one was wrong and said the working, and it was shorter than the man of fifty-four’s.

“*A leaf is a day on my run. That is the whole of it. It is a day because I write one line on one leaf a day and it has been that way since the step went in flat, and if you take one of my leaves out and give it to him then my run has got one day missing in the middle of it and my figure is wrong by one, and it is my figure and not yours.*”

“*And about four of you would have walked up four hundred miles with a leaf in a coat. Nobody is to walk up four hundred miles with a leaf in a coat. Ask me for a line on my run any morning you like and I will consider it and probably say no, and that is the whole of what a run is for and it is not very much.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said he had been round all four of the ordinary runs on this landing and asked the same question at each of them, and got the same answer four times, and did not know what to do with it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, and there is a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and that is a figure of what is on a board and not a figure of him, and he has a child of nine.

“*I asked four people this week whether anybody has ever come to their run and asked to see it. Not me. Anybody, ever. And the answer was no from a man who keeps a boat and no from a woman who keeps a drain and no from a man who keeps a gate.*”

“*And I asked whether that was because nobody has ever thought of it or because there is nobody four hundred miles off who would know to ask. And I do not know which, and that is the first fact I have had this fortnight and I have been sitting on it since the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week like a man holding a stone he has not looked at.*”

“*I am not going to put it in either half of my question. If I put it in the second half then I am making four ordinary things into a fact about who did what to three positions, and about four of you would do that inside a week. If I put it in the first half then I am asking what three of the nine positions are by a different road, and that is still the first half, and nobody has answered it.*”

“*So I am holding it. And about nine of you have watched me hold it for a week and about four of you have wanted me to put it somewhere.*”

At about the tenth hour a woman of forty-four named a woman of twenty-eight out loud and said what she had done with the name and what she was not going to do with it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and has not been answered her fourth question.

“*There is a woman of twenty-eight called Sera Quill. I am saying her name because she said it on that bank herself on the first day of the hundred and thirty-ninth week, and it is her name to give and she gave it.*”

“*Her own count is two hundred and thirty-one days, and that is eight hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and forty-two, and I have got that figure off her own mouth and I am not going to pretend I worked it out.*”

“*And she is four miles down that cart road today and she will be four miles down it tomorrow, and she cannot be sent for in this county or in the one she came from, and nobody on this landing is going up that road this week to ask her anything.*”

“*I sat with her for two hours on the Saturday of the hundred and forty-sixth week and I have not told about nine of you one thing she said. Not because she told me not to. Because a figure of about four of you who will speak is not consent and I am not spending it on her name, and about four of you would have made a fourth thing out of a fourth question by Tuesday and I did not give you the chance.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and twenty days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and twenty is eight hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and twenty days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And nobody has asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twelve mornings. On a Monday I would say the number out loud if I was a different kind of boy, and about nine of you have worked out this week that I am not.*”

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and has kept that position for twenty-three years, one line every day, and it is a public archive and not a stone and there is nothing in front of it to stand at.

“*That is the sixteenth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings.*”

“*And a woman of forty-four has just said my own rule back to me in front of about nine of you, and I have been saying it for twenty-three years and it is the first time it has been said by somebody who does not keep a run, and I am not going to be pleased about it in a way that makes it cheap.*”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and fifty-second morning, and then asked the woman of thirty-three whether he was right to have asked, and did not get told.

“*A hundred and fifty-two.*”

“*And I am going to ask you one more thing and you can refuse it. Have I been eleven years in the wrong place.*”

“*I am not going to tell you that. You will get that out of what happens and not out of my mouth, and if I told you now you would have it on a Monday and about four of you would say I had confirmed it.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Monday, the last day of the hundred and forty-sixth week. A man of fifty-four had asked a woman of thirty-three for one day of his own and had not been refused and had not been answered.

Sixteen inches on the top step, holding, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those steps under. The man of forty-three said that today is the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-sixth week, that tomorrow is the first day of the hundred and forty-seventh week and a Tuesday, and that the one after that is the second day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and the twenty-seventh time.

A man of fifty-four asked a woman of thirty-three for one day of his own, one line, in her hand, on a day he will name. She said a day entered on that binds nobody and protects nobody. She said she will write it. She asked which day, and he said he does not know, and she said she will hold the leaf back until he names one, because a line is a thing that happened.

A man of thirty-one told him what to ask for, which was a leaf of the ninth run, and was refused by the man of fifty-four, who said that was his question and not his own, and that a man of thirty-one had now offered to carry something of his to somebody else twice and been refused twice.

A man of thirty-eight had asked four people whether anybody had ever come to their run and asked to see it, and all four had said no, and he did not know whether that was because nobody had thought of it or because there is nobody four hundred miles off who would know to ask.

And a woman of forty-four named a woman of twenty-eight, gave her figure with the subtraction, said she is four miles down that cart road and cannot be sent for, and said she has told about nine of you nothing that was said to her, because a figure of about four of you who will speak is not consent.
