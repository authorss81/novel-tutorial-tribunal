# Chapter 862: Third Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fifth Week, And A Thursday, And Sixteen Inches On The Top Step And Four Days Of The Coming Back And A Full Bank, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Names His Fourteenth Thing That He Cannot Check And Says It Is Not A Figure Of Anybody, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Says A Chair Is Either Got In Her Book Or It Is Not And Does Not Solve It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Is Told That Standing In Front Of A Leaf Is The Thing He Cannot Do

Third day of the hundred and forty-fifth week. His six hundred and twelfth morning. Four hundred and sixty-two days after the settlement. The fever eighty-two weeks and five days old. Four hundred and sixteen days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank came up to sixteen at about the seventh hour. That is a full one, and it was four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave both figures and said a full bank holds until it comes off, and said the order the two came out in was not a thing anybody was to write down.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and when the bank is a full one it is about sixty.

“*Sixteen on the top step at the seventh hour, and four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. That is a full bank and it holds.*”

“*And a full bank holds at sixteen until it comes off, and I am not going to say that in a different shape tomorrow. About four of you have been waiting three mornings to see whether I would, and the answer is that I do not change it because you have.*”

“*And the next time about ninety of those steps can be walked is on the seventh day of this week, and that is the twenty-fifth time. I have given that number every morning of this week and I am going to give it for the twenty-fifth and I am not going to stop on the morning I say it.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two named the fourteenth thing he cannot check, on the day he said he would, and said it was not a figure of anybody and had no name.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He has a palm and a forearm. He named a thirteenth thing he cannot check on the Thursday of the hundred and forty-third week, and it is a figure of a woman. On the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-fourth week he said that a fortnight from that Thursday is the Thursday of the hundred and forty-fifth week, and that he was not going to name a fourteenth before it, and this morning is that Thursday.

“*Here it is, and about four of you have been waiting a week for it, and it is smaller than what you have been imagining and it is not a figure of anybody.*”

“*That a leaf and a day are the same number. That is the fourteenth thing I cannot check.*”

“*I can put a line under a leaf being gone. A woman of forty-nine has the stubs and the thread and the holes and I can put a hand on the edge of that front and feel where the thread stops being one thing and starts being another. That is the whole of what I can do and I have been doing it for about four days.*”

“*What I cannot put a line under is that the leaf which is gone was a day. Because you do not go and stand in front of a leaf. You stand in front of a stone, and the stone is there, and the stone is either there or it is not there and you have seen it yourself.*”

“*And a leaf is not like that. A leaf can be gone and a day can have gone and those can be the same event or they can be two events, and there is nothing on that bank or in that room that would let a person tell me which.*”

“*There is no woman in it and no child in it and no holder in it. Thirteen was a figure of a woman and I have not said which. This one has nobody at all in it and I am going to leave it that way, because about four of you would put a name on it inside a week and then it would be a thing about somebody.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said what she had got that he had not, and said she was not going to use it on him, and gave the difference with her own book.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. Eleven of those chairs have a name against them in her book and nobody in them.

“*I have got a day against every chair in that book that anybody has ever sat in. Not against the two that are named dead, and I have told you why twice. Against the rest of them there is a day, in my hand, and the day is the day they sat in it.*”

“*So a chair is either got in my book or it is not, and if it is got then there is a day against it, and if there is a day against it then a person sat in it on that day. That is not a difficult thing to check. That is the only kind of record I have ever had.*”

“*And what I have got that you have not got is the day inside the book. Yours has the day printed at the head of the leaf and nobody has written anything under it. Mine has nothing on the leaf at all until somebody writes, and then there is a day on it in my hand and my hand is the one that would know.*”

“*I am not going to hand you that as an answer, and I am going to say why in one sentence. You have a front of a book with nothing in it. I have a book with nothing in it until it is used. Those are not the same hole and I am not going to be the one who calls them the same.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said he could put a line under a leaf being gone as well, and was told by the man who had just named the fourteenth that this was the thing he could not check, and why.

“*I can put a line under a leaf being gone. I have been putting my hand on that front since Wednesday and I can tell you where the stitching stops, and I said so on this landing in about nine of your ears before you sat down.*”

“*Yes, and you have been standing in front of it for a week, and that is the whole of what is wrong with you this morning.*”

“*Standing in front of a thing is a check. I know what that is because I can do it to six of the nine and I have done it to them out loud in about nine of your ears and about four of you have believed me. That is what standing in front of a stone is. You go, and you look, and it is there or it is not.*”

“*Holding a thing is not a check. You hold it and you feel the edge and you tell yourself where it stopped. And about four of you have watched a man of thirty-one do that with a hand all week and call it standing in front of something.*”

“*I am not saying it to be cruel. If you could stand in front of it you would have the fourteenth thing checked and we would be one ahead, and we are not, and the reason we are not is the thing you said in about nine of your ears on the Saturday of last week.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and eight days, three hundred and eight being eight hundred and sixty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seventy-seven days old, a hundred and seventy-seven being eight hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then I have been holding it since the Saturday of last week and calling it standing in front of it, and I would rather be told that on a Thursday than carry it into the sixth day of this week.*”

“*Three hundred and eight days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and seventy-seven days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-two days old, and a hundred and sixty-two is eight hundred and sixty-two less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I said it on Wednesday and I am saying it on a Thursday, and a figure that changes every morning is a figure of a morning and not a figure of anything else.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and forty-first morning, and said the fourteenth was not a thing he would use.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-one mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-two less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and forty-one, and it is a Thursday, and I asked it on the Thursday of last week too and I have not written that down anywhere.*”

“*And I am not going to use that fourteenth. A man of thirty-one has just been told off in about nine of your ears for putting his hand on a thing and calling it a check, and I have been putting my hand on that ninth line for eleven years and calling it a day.*”

“*So it is not a day and it is not a check and I am still going to ask for it on the hundred and forty-second morning, because there is nothing else on this bank that is mine to ask for.*”

At about the ninth hour a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run and read it out herself, and said the figure of mornings was a figure of mornings, and a boy of thirteen gave his own count and said a fourteenth thing was not a fifteenth thing.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has put a line on that run every day for twenty-three years without once missing one.

“*That is the fifth morning in front of anybody. It is a figure of mornings and I have said why and I am not going to say it a second time this week.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and nine days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and nine is eight hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two said a fourteenth thing this morning and I have been thinking about it in the lane and I have got the opposite of it and I am not going to say it, because if I say it now it is the fifteenth and he said he is not going to look for a fifteenth.*”

“*A thing a person cannot check is not a smaller number. It is the same number with a hand over it. My figure is two hundred and nine and nobody has put a hand over it.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday with a full bank over the top step and about sixty of those ninety steps under, and a fourteenth thing named that was not a figure of anybody, and a woman of forty-four who would not use the one thing she had that would have answered it.

Sixteen inches on the top step, a full bank and four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three said a full bank holds at sixteen until it comes off and said the order the two figures came out in was not a thing anybody was to write down.

A man of about fifty-two named his fourteenth thing he cannot check, on the Thursday he had said he would: that a leaf and a day are the same number. He can put a line under a leaf being gone, because a woman of forty-nine has the stubs and the thread and the holes and he can feel where the thread stops. He cannot put a line under that the leaf that is gone was a day, because nobody goes and stands in front of a leaf.

A woman of forty-four said she has a day against every chair anybody has ever sat in, in her own hand, and that a chair is either got in her book or it is not. She said she was not going to hand that over as an answer. His book has the day printed at the head of the leaf and nothing written under it. Hers has nothing on the leaf until somebody writes. Those are not the same hole and she would not call them the same.

And a man of thirty-one said he could put a line under a leaf being gone too, and was told that standing in front of a thing is a check and holding a thing is not, and that he had been holding that front since the Saturday of last week and calling it standing in front of something.

A man of fifty-four asked for a day for the hundred and forty-first morning and said he was not going to use the fourteenth, because he has had his hand on that ninth line for eleven years and called it a day. A boy of thirteen got the opposite of the fourteenth in the lane and did not say it, because if he said it now it was the fifteenth.
