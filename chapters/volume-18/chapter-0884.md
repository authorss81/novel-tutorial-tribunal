# Chapter 884: A Figure Of Houses Is Not A Figure Of People

Fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. His six hundred and thirty-fourth morning. Four hundred and eighty-four days after the settlement. The fever eighty-five weeks and six days old. Four hundred and thirty-eight days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Eight inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and about eighty of those ninety steps were under. That was six days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and named the day he had named on the morning before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gave four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour of a Friday. He named this morning on the third day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, with the day and the week on it.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under.”

“And I named this morning yesterday. I said the fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and it is the fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week. A figure said out loud a day early is a figure said out loud a day early.”

Then the woman who washes lime gave the only figure of houses anybody gave all week, and gave what it is.

Hannah Voss is forty-one and washes lime, and goes into other people’s rooms and does not knock because a room is empty while she is in it.

“There is one figure of houses on that bank this week and I am going to give it and then I am going to be finished with it, because I can tell what it is going to be used for.”

“I washed eleven houses along one side of one lane between the bridge and the mill. That was the year the bridge was shut for six weeks and nobody could get a cart over it. One side. Eleven. I did the top floor of each and the front room and the stair and out again, and that is eleven houses and it took me eleven days and there is no figure of anything else on it.”

“Eleven is eleven houses. It is not eleven people. I never once knew how many of them were in any of the eleven and I would not have asked, and I have not been back down that lane since.”

“Now here is the part I want said out loud on a Friday while about nine of you are listening, because you are all going to do the arithmetic and I want to be standing here when you do it.”

“A figure of houses is a figure of houses. The moment a person multiplies it by anything at all, it stops being a figure of houses and it becomes a figure of people, and a figure of people is a figure they have no business having.”

“And that is true even when every house has somebody in it. That is the trap. Eleven houses along one lane and every one of them full, and you have still got eleven houses.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked her how many people were in them, and was refused, and did not hear the working until the second time.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ninety-nine days old.

“Twenty-four wrong things in twenty-four mornings, and here is the twenty-fourth. I asked her for a figure of people and she gave me the working twice and I heard it the second time and not the first.”

“Three hundred and thirty days in this county and the cut across this palm is a hundred and ninety-nine days old and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-four days old, and I have not asked it.”


“That is not what I asked. How many people live in those eleven houses. If I am going to walk into a house and ask a stranger to let me take their wall down, I want to know who I am standing in front of.”

“No. That is the figure of people and you have asked me for it in a different set of words, and if I had answered you in the first set of words you would have taken it, and you would not have known what had happened to you.”

“And you are a man who keeps figures in his mouth every morning of his life and you cannot tell the two kinds apart, which is a thing I would not have believed on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and would have believed on the fourth.”
“And I am going to tell you what the cost of that is, because nobody else on that bank is going to. You have a figure of people in your mouth now. You did not have one at the seventh hour.”

“It is in there and you cannot get it out, and if you ever do go and stand in front of somebody’s door you are going to be the man who has already decided how many are in it.”


Then a woman of forty-one who holds a seat with no stone behind it asked for the figure of houses so that the bank could plan something, and was refused in a mouth with the working.

Ivet Quillon is forty-one and holds a seat that has no stone behind it, and has never once pretended there was going to be one, and has said the ordinary form in about nine ears more than once and has never once given it a name. There are two of us of forty-one on that step this morning, and she washes lime and I hold a seat with nothing behind it, and about four of you have had us the wrong way round since the sixth hour.

“Then give me the number. Not for writing down. For planning. I will go and be the one who asks at the doors and you will not have to go at all, and we will do it in an order instead of at random.”

“No. And I want the whole bank to hear why, because you are the second person this week to offer me a kindness with a number inside it.”

“A number in a plan is a number somebody decided about. Eleven houses is a number I found out by being let in. The moment it is in a plan it is a number about what may happen, and what may happen does not have a wall in it.”

“And I am not refusing you because you would make a bad second person. I am refusing you because a plan is one person deciding, and that is the thing I have been trying to get somebody else to stop doing since the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week.”

Then a woman of twenty-nine in a reed said the sentence about houses and people in her own mouth, and gave no figure at all.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine, and there is a run of days for her that is in a reed, and nobody has spoken a figure of it on that bank, and nobody has put her near a stone.

“I have heard that said out loud and I am going to say it once from where I stand, and I am not going to give a figure with it.”

“A figure of houses is not a figure of people. That is the whole of what she said and she said it first, and I am not going to say it a second time.”

“And I am going to say the thing that makes it true for me, which is that I keep a figure. One of them. My own. Two hundred and fifty-two mornings of it and I write it down and nobody has ever asked me for it and nobody ever will.”

“That is the only figure I have and I have never once used it on anything else. Not on how many houses I have lived in. Not on how many people are in any of them. A figure you have kept about one thing does not get to go out and stand for other things, and about four of you have been letting it out all week without asking me.”

“I have got on with nothing from anybody for a long time and I have got on with it perfectly well, and I am not standing here to be a lesson. I am standing here because about four of you have been saying her sentence wrong all week and I would like it said right once, and I am the only person on this bank who can tell you what a figure you have kept is worth.”

“And I am going to say the other half of it, which is that I would still rather be a figure of nobody than a figure of one of something.”

“You do not know that about me. You have known it since the third day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, when a man of thirty-one used my name as an example and got a thing out of it, and about four of you have been trying to put me on that bank since then, and I have said no and you have written it down and here we are on the fourth day of another week.”

“You want a woman in a reed to be the fifth ordinary thing on a landing with a ferry and a drain and a school and a gate. I would be the fifth and there would be a figure of five, and I would rather be nobody and have it be four.”

“And I am going to say one more thing and then I am finished. There is not one person on this bank who has ever asked me how many houses I have lived in, and there is not one person on this bank who would know what to do with the answer.”

“You have all spent a week learning it from two people who cannot tell two kinds of number apart standing next to each other. I can tell them apart. It is the only thing I have ever been asked for and I have never once said yes to it.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-third morning, and asked the woman in the reed for one, and got what she gave him.

“A hundred and sixty-three. And I asked her. I am going to say that I asked her in front of all of you because about four of you would have said I did not.”

“I asked her for a day and she said no. She said it in one word and she did not give me a figure and she did not give me a reason, and she was not unkind about it and she was not troubled about it.”

“I am going to hold that no. I am not going to use it for anything. It is not a notch and it is not an entry and it is not a thing I am going to bring up on a Friday next week and say she said no again.”

“Nobody is going to be thanked for my asking and nobody is going to be thanked for her saying it.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-one is eight hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-three mornings. A woman of twenty-nine has just said she keeps a figure of her own and has never once used it on anything else, and about four of you have been letting hers out all week without asking her.”

“I am thirteen and that is the only reason I get to say it. I am not a woman in a reed and I have not got a figure of my own that anybody would listen to. I am saying it because she is right and nobody has said so for her.”

The woman in the reed went down the bank before the light came off the top of it, and the two of them of forty-one were still at each other about who had washed which wall, and a man of thirty-one was standing on the step with a figure of people in his mouth that nobody had given him.
