# Chapter 866: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Forty-Fifth Week, And A Monday, And The Water Came Off All Ninety Steps At About The Sixth Hour And That Is The Twenty-Fifth Time, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Counts About Ninety Dry Steps And A Man Of Forty-Three Agrees With Him, And A Woman Of Forty-Nine Says In About Nine Of Your Ears That The Three Blanks Were Never Blanks And Says It About The Page And Not About The People On The Bank

Seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week. His six hundred and sixteenth morning. Four hundred and sixty-six days after the settlement. The fever eighty-three weeks and two days old. Four hundred and twenty days since the division. Three hundred and sixty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh. That was the twenty-fifth time. The man of forty-three gave the hour, the count and the number of the time, and said there was no figure of inches and no figure of steps, and gave a figure of dry steps that was not his.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and gives four things every morning. The fourth is a figure of how many of those ninety steps are under, and on an off-morning there is no water on those steps, so there is nothing under them.

“*It came off at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps are walkable now, and that is the twenty-fifth time.*”

“*And there is no figure of inches this morning and there is no figure of steps under, and that is not a gap in my morning. It is a morning where the fourth thing has nothing to be about.*”

“*The next one is in eight days, and that is the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-seventh week. I know that without the water-stone and about four of you stopped asking how I know it a fortnight ago and I have noticed.*”

At about the seventh hour a man of thirty-eight counted the steps that were dry at the top of that cut and gave the figure out loud, and the man of forty-three gave his after it, and the two figures were the same.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and there is a board with thirty-two notches on the edge of it in the inside of his coat, which is a figure of what is on a board. On the Sunday of this week he was given the job of counting the dry steps and was told he did not want to do it right.

“*About ninety of those ninety steps are dry. I counted them twice because I did not believe the first time, and it is about ninety both times, and I am saying about ninety and not ninety because I could not tell you where the last one stops being a step.*”

Corvin Tace, forty-three, said his figure after it, and said the two of them agreed, and said what two men agreeing about ninety is and is not.

“*About ninety of those ninety steps are dry.*”

“*And that is mine and it is the same figure and I said it after his and not before, and I want that in about nine of your ears in that order.*”

“*Two men have agreed about ninety. About four of you are about to write that down as though it were two figures that happened to be the same. It is one figure said twice by two people, and one of those people has been saying it nineteen years and the other said it once.*”

“*That is not a better morning. It is a morning where about four of you can go and look at about ninety steps with your own feet instead of taking a figure off a man of forty-three, and I am not going to be thanked for that either.*”

Then a woman of forty-nine said a sentence out loud on that landing that about nine of them heard, and said it was about a page and not about the three positions, and gave the working in four pieces.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and keeps the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone. On the second day of this week she entered today’s line on that run in front of about nine of them, and on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-fifth week she said the sentence that is in this chapter.

“*Here is the sentence, and I have had it since Saturday and about four of you have been getting bits of it all week, and I am going to say the whole of it once.*”

“*Those three were never blanks.*”

“*And that is about the page. It is not about the three of them and it is not about the three of us, and if about four of you take it away from me this morning and carry it round this landing as a thing about the ninth position then I have said it wrong and I will say it again in a better shape.*”

“*The working, in four pieces, and I am going to say all four in about nine of your ears because about four of you will only take the first.*”

“*One. An index is a list of what a person can be shown. That is what an index is for. A man opens it and he is told where to go and stand, and that is the whole of what he has been told.*”

“*Two. A blank in a list is not a hole in a record. A hole in a record is where a line used to be and is not now. There is no line on any of those three, and there never was one there to take away.*”

“*Three. So what is on that page is not three positions that are empty. It is three positions a list does not carry lines for. And one of the three is mine. I have entered a line on a leaf every day for twenty-three years and not one of them went on that page, because that page is not where the lines go.*”

“*Four. A position that is not a stone is not a thing on a list. It is a day somebody did the work. The run is where the day is written down. The run is not the position.*”

“*And that fourth one is the whole of what has happened on this landing this week, and about four of you have been carrying the run round in their hands as though it were the thing itself. It is not the thing. It is the only place the thing is written down. Those are two sentences and they are not the same one, and I have watched about four of you use the first to do the work of the second.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said that this was the answer to his question, and was told which half of his question it was the answer to, and was told that a list has got one thing it can say about a position.

“*That is my question. That is what the three are. They are not blanks, they are positions a list does not carry lines for, and I have been asking for four days —”

“*No. That is the second half of your question, and you had it on the Sunday of last week in about nine of your ears and I am not giving it to you again this morning because you are pleased about the other half.*”

“*What the three are, is a different question and I have not answered it this morning and I am not going to. What I have answered is what a page is, and it is the working for the other half and it is exact: an index can tell you whether there is a thing to go and stand in front of. It can tell you nothing else about a position at all.*”

“*You asked a list to describe three things. A list has got one thing it can say. That is not me refusing you. That is what a page is, and I have held the ninth of the nine for four days and about four of you have been asking me what it is instead of asking me what a page is.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the sentence about the page was the answer to the first half as well, and was told what he had done, in one sentence, with the working beside it.

“*Then it answers the first half as well. A position that is not a stone is a day somebody did the work. That is what the three are. They are days. That is the answer and about four of you have been sitting on it since the fourth hour.*”

“*You have just asked a list to describe three things, and a list has got one thing it can say about a position, and that is whether you can be shown it.*”

“*That is the working and it is the same sentence twice on purpose, because about four of you are going to hear the first one and stop listening, and it is not me refusing you. It is a page. I have been telling you what this is since Wednesday and about four of you have been asking me what it means instead.*”

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, and is nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and twelve days, three hundred and twelve being eight hundred and sixty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and eighty-one days old, a hundred and eighty-one being eight hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Then I said it four days running this morning and I said it wrong four days running, and about four of you let three of them stand and stopped the fourth at the front of nine of you.*”

“*Three hundred and twelve days in this county, and that is eight hundred and sixty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across this palm is a hundred and eighty-one days old, and that is eight hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.*”

“*And the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and sixty-six days old, and a hundred and sixty-six is eight hundred and sixty-six less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and a woman of forty-nine has said a true thing about a page this morning and that is not it and I am not going to put the two side by side.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the same sentence he said on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week, in about nine of your ears, and said it was not progress, and said what about four of them were about to do with the morning.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He named a fourteenth thing he cannot check on the third day of this week, and he is the only man on that landing who can put a line under six of the nine positions and not under the other three.

“*The six being checkable does not make the three checkable, and I said that on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week, and I have watched about four of you do that arithmetic in your heads in the last minute.*”

“*And I would rather say it out loud than have it be done quietly. I said that on the same morning, in the same nine ears, and I am not going to improve it now, because a man of thirty-eight has been told something true on this landing and about four of you want to hang it on my sentence.*”

“*I said that on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week and I am saying it on the twenty-fifth off-morning, and it is the same sentence and it is not progress, and nobody is to go home and tell about four people that we have got somewhere.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine has said something this morning that about four of you want to be a reason. It is not a reason. It is a thing about a page. It does not put a stone anywhere, and it does not make a bell rung on a Thursday checkable on a Friday, and it does not put a day on a book four hundred miles off.*”

“*I have got fourteen things I cannot check and that sentence is not one of them and I am not adding it. And about four of you have been waiting five days for me to say something better than the sentence I said on Wednesday, and there is nothing better, and I told you on Wednesday that there was nothing better, and you waited five days anyway.*”

At about the ninth hour a man of fifty-four who cannot read asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and forty-fifth morning, and a woman of forty-nine entered today’s line on the ninth run and gave the figure of mornings, and a boy of thirteen gave his own.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and forty-five mornings, which is eight hundred and sixty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“*A hundred and forty-five, and it is a Monday, and I have said that figure in about nine of your ears on every morning of this week but one, and this week has run seven mornings, and I have not once said anything about Mondays. About four of you have been waiting for me to say something about the day and I am not going to.*”

“*A woman of forty-nine has said a sentence about a page this morning that about four of you have been carrying off that bank like bread. It is about a page. I cannot read a page. I have been asking for a day on one for eleven years and a page is not a day and I have known that since the Saturday of last week.*”

“*I am not going to thank her for it and nobody is going to be thanked, and I am going to keep asking for the day on the hundred and forty-sixth morning, and about four of you can go and work out for yourselves what a page is.*”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself. She said it was the ninth morning she had done that in front of anybody, and gave the working of the figure.

“*That is the ninth morning, in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings. It is not a figure of how many of you were here and it is not a figure of the front of my book and I am not going to say those two things again this week.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirteen is eight hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“*Two hundred and thirteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.*”

“*And nobody asked me for the count I have been keeping. That is five mornings and I am not going to say what it is, and about four of you have stopped asking, and a man of thirty-eight has just agreed with a man of forty-three about about ninety steps and neither of them thanked anybody.*”

“*That is the same shape as me and I am not comparing myself to a man of thirty-eight in about nine of your ears. I am saying that about ninety of us on this landing have found a way of doing something this week without being thanked and I have counted that and it is not my count to give.*”

About nine of them were on that landing at about the eleventh hour of a Monday with all ninety of those steps dry, and a sentence about a page said once in about nine of your ears, and two figures of about ninety steps said twice by two people and agreed.

The water came off all ninety steps at about the sixth hour, and that was the twenty-fifth time, and there was no figure of inches and no figure of steps under. The next one is in eight days, which is the Tuesday of the hundred and forty-seventh week.

A man of thirty-eight counted about ninety dry steps and a man of forty-three gave his figure after him, and the two figures were the same. Two men have agreed about ninety. That is one figure said twice, and one of the two has been saying it nineteen years and the other said it once, and it is not a better morning.

And a woman of forty-nine said a sentence out loud on that landing that about nine of them heard. She said it was about a page and not about the three positions, and gave the working in four pieces. Nobody wrote it down. She said it once and did not say it twice, and about four of you are already carrying it off that bank.

A man of thirty-eight said that was his question. He was told it was the second half of it, and that a list has one thing it can say about a position. A man of thirty-one said it answered the first half too, and was given the same sentence twice on purpose.

And a man of about fifty-two said the six being checkable does not make the three checkable, that he would rather say it out loud than have it done quietly, that it is the sentence he said on the Wednesday of the hundred and forty-second week, and that it is not progress. A man of fifty-four, who cannot read, said a page is not a day and that he has known that since the Saturday of the hundred and forty-fourth week, and asked for the hundred and forty-fifth morning.
