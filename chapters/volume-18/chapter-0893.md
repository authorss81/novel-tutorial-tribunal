# Chapter 893: A Word That Belongs To A Thing You Can Hold

Sixth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week. His six hundred and forty-third morning. Four hundred and ninety-three days after the settlement. The fever eighty-seven weeks and one day old. Four hundred and forty-seven days since the division. Three hundred and ninety-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Twelve inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour, with five days of the coming back and about seventy of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and said that the bank would be full on the seventh and last day of that week and that he was naming it a day early and would rather be out by a day than in by one.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has been out by a day in one direction or the other more often than he has been exact.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, five days of the coming back, and about seventy of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week.”

“I am naming tomorrow in advance and I am naming it a day early, on purpose, in about nine of your ears. Sixteen, a full bank, four days of the coming back and about sixty under, on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, which is a Monday. That is three figures and a day, and the day is not one of them, and I am not going to fold it in.”

Then the woman who holds the ninth of the nine said where the arrangement is, and did not put a day on it, and said why not.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years in a room four hundred miles off with nine clerks in it, and holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has not put a day on the arrangement since she got off that cart.

“It is four hundred miles off and it is still answering. It is not slow and it is not tired and it does not need anybody in this county to be told anything.”

“And I am not going to put a day on it and neither is anybody else on this step, and about four of you have been waiting for me to do it since I got off that cart.”

“Because the last morning in this county when somebody put a day on that thing, that day was the day a person chose for it, and not the day it did anything. A thing that answers on a schedule does not get to have one of our mornings stuck on it because that would be the easiest way to make it ours.”

“It will answer again inside a week. I have put three of its answers in front of this bank and refused two of them. There is still not one person on this landing who has stood in the room where it answers, and nobody is going to be put there this week, and a woman of forty-four has just offered about four people a room with the door shut and the two rooms are not the same room.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked a man who cannot read not to carry the figure out of the landing, and was refused by the man who cannot read, in his own mouth.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-nine days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and eight days old.

“Three hundred and thirty-nine days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and eight days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and ninety-three days old, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Thirty-three wrong things in thirty-three mornings, and here is the thirty-third. Barnaby. When the count is said in that room it is going to leave the room in about nine ears, and you are the only man on this bank who cannot write a figure down, so everything that leaves the room is going to leave it through your mouth.”

“Do not repeat it. Not the figure and not the story around it. Just do not say it out loud on this step afterwards, and nobody will be able to say a man of fifty-four carried it off a landing.”

“You have just done the ninth thing of that shape and I do not think you have noticed any of the eight.”

“I have noticed this one. And that is the difference, and it is not a good enough difference, and I am not going to pretend it is.”

“You are the only man on this bank who has to hold a figure in his head instead of on a page, and every one of us who can write has been letting you do that since you got here. I have not asked you to carry anything. You have been carrying it because nobody gave you anywhere to put it.”

“A hundred and seventy-two, and I am not going to do what you have just asked me to do, and I am not going to do the other thing either, and about four of you are waiting to hear which.”

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and seventy-two mornings, which is eight hundred and ninety-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and seventy-two. And the man has just asked me not to say a thing out loud, in front of about nine of them, on a Sunday, and he has told me it is so that nothing gets carried off a landing.”

“No. And here is why, and it is not because I want it.”

“I cannot write it down, so the only place a figure I have said goes is somebody else’s head. That is how it has worked for eleven years and it is the reason I have a number in my mouth and a crumb of bread in my apron and nothing in a book. If I start choosing which figures I let out, then from this morning the ones I let out are the ones I have decided about, and that is not holding a figure, that is handing people a filtered one and letting them think it came off a shelf.”

“So I will say what I am given and what I am not given and I will not say a figure I have not got. That is the arrangement and it is eleven years old and it is the only thing I have.”

“And you can ask me that every morning for the rest of the time you are here and the answer will be the same and it will not be softer on the morning you ask it in front of more people.”

Then the woman of forty-four said three sentences that were already on that landing, out loud, and said that nobody was to write them down as new, and refused a word that had started being used about her question.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, has kept twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, every one of them entered by her own hand, and has put her question down five times. One word of it has never moved.

“I am going to say it, and I want the whole bank to notice me saying it, and I am not saying it because I have found it. Every one of you has heard all three of these and two of them have been said by me in a different mouth than this one.”

“A complete account is not the right of refusal. There is a name on this landing that may not be put on this in a mouth, and a person cannot buy an answer by asking somebody for the pages.”

“A run with a hole at the front of it is not it either. And an incomplete account is not it. Those are my three and they have been said on this step twice and neither time by anybody new.”

“And now the other thing, and it is a small thing and it has been going on for two days. Somebody on this landing has started using the word seal about my question, and I want it stopped, and I am not going to be the woman who says it twice.”

“A seal is a thing that closes and fastens and is made to be looked at and not opened. There is nothing here that fastens. There is a run of days with a leaf for every day and a woman writing in her own hand, and if a run like that were a seal then a seal would be something you could do up in an afternoon and I would not have asked for anything for nineteen years.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and forty days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and forty is eight hundred and ninety-three less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and forty days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-two mornings, and a man of thirty-one has just told a man of fifty-four not to say things out loud, and about four of you looked at me while he was saying it.”

“You looked at me because you have all been waiting five days for me to be the one who carries it, and I am not, and I would like that said on this step before it goes on being true.”

Sena Dorr entered today’s line on the ninth run at about the tenth hour and read it out herself on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, and about half of that bank had gone down the cut early.

“That is the thirty-sixth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it while about four of you were arguing about a word that belongs to a thing you can hold.”

“And nobody took the word and used it after I said it. About four of you heard it. That is the whole of what I did and I am not going to be thanked for stopping a word, because stopping a word is not a thing anybody should have to be thanked for.”
