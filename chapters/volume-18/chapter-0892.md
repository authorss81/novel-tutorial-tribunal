# Chapter 892: A Whole Number Of Weeks, And No Figure Of Days

Fifth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week. His six hundred and forty-second morning. Four hundred and ninety-two days after the settlement. The fever eighty-seven weeks old. Four hundred and forty-six days since the division. Three hundred and ninety-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Eight inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour, which was six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the three figures and then gave the shape of the morning, because it comes out on a Saturday and it is the shape about nine of them get wrong every seventh time.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, and has given the fever figure on every seventh Saturday for nineteen years and has never once added to it.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under, on the fifth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, and it is a Saturday.”

“And the fever is eighty-seven weeks old this morning and there is no figure of days with it, and that is not a gap. That is the shape of this morning. The fever comes out at a whole number of weeks on every seventh Saturday and on the other six mornings of a week I give you a figure of days with it and today is the one where there is nothing to add.”

“About four of you have written a figure of days against a Saturday anyway, and I have not corrected one of them, because correcting a figure somebody else wrote is a fifth thing and I do not give a fifth thing.”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and seventy-first morning, and asked whether anybody in that room was going to make him say something, and was told no.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and seventy-one mornings, which is eight hundred and ninety-two less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and seventy-one. And I asked on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-ninth week to be let into a room where a thing is going to be said, and she said she is not going to choose who comes.”

“That was the right answer and I am not going to say it was the right answer, because I cannot read and I do not know whether I am being handled.”

“Here is what I want said. Nobody in that room is going to be asked to say anything. Not me and not the boy and not one of the four of you who will get there first. I have been on this step for eleven years being asked to say a number, and a number is a thing I can say, and if somebody puts me in a room and hands me a figure to repeat then I will repeat it and that will be the whole of what happened.”

“No. And I am not going to soften it for you either. Nobody is put in a room to say anything. The first four of you who come are four people who are there, and the fifth of you is outside in the cold finding out what you think it is going to say.”

“I have asked for a day for eleven years. I have never once asked to be a mouth and I am not going to start on a Saturday because a woman has offered me a chair she has not moved since the hundred and thirtieth week.”

Then a man of thirty-one said the count ought to go on the boy’s own line in the lane, and was told what that line is.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-eight days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and seven days old.

“Three hundred and thirty-eight days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and seven days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and ninety-two days old, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

“Thirty-two wrong things in thirty-two mornings, and here is the thirty-second. He keeps a line in the lane. He has kept it since before this week started and nobody has ever asked him for it and he will not give it.”

“So put the other one on the same line. Two figures on one line, both counted by the same hand, both about things that were counted and not argued about. In about nine weeks somebody on this landing is going to want a page with figures on it and there will be one, and it will be in his own hand, and it will be his.”

“That line is about my mornings and it is about nothing else.”

“Not the count. The count is a figure of stitches and it is his to put down or not put down, and I am not going to touch it, and that is the whole of what I am refusing and it took me longer than it should have.”

“If I put it there then in about nine weeks there will be two figures on one line in a lane and about four of you will read them as one kind of thing, because they are in the same hand and the same ink and the same week. That is not me being careful about your figure. That is me being careful about my own.”

“You have been refused four days running and every time it has been in a different shape and twice it has been by a person of thirteen, and I have not noticed that until this morning.”

“And now I have noticed it, and I am not going to be the one who says the other half out loud, because a man of fifty-four on this step has told me twice this week that he is not going to be the mouth that says one kind word about me, and he is right to, and he is not going to be thanked for it.”

“And I have offered four things in four days and three of them were ways of writing something down. I did not see that coming. About four of you saw it before I did, and none of you said it, and I am not blaming you for that either.”

Then the boy of thirteen was asked what a stitch is worth, and refused, in his own mouth, in about nine ears, and the refusal was not the shape he used on the first day of the hundred and forty-ninth week.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-nine days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-nine is eight hundred and ninety-two less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-nine days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is thirty-one mornings, and it is about my mornings, and it is not about anything anybody on this landing would ask me about.”

“And somebody has asked me this week what one stitch is worth. Not out loud. You have all been working it out in your heads since a woman of forty-nine said a figure of stitches cannot be turned into the number of days it stands for.”

“I am not going to answer it. Not because it is rude and not because I am thirteen.”

“Because it is the only question anybody has asked me about anything that I would have to have a figure for before I could answer it, and I have not got one, and neither has she, and she has been keeping that book for twenty-three years.”

“You all want me to be the person who says what a stitch is worth. I am not that person and I have never once been that person and the day somebody makes me that person is the day this line in the lane stops being worth keeping.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He has been keeping a count that nobody has asked him for for thirty-one mornings, and he named the kind of figure it is on the sixth day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, and he has not given it and is not going to.

Then the woman who holds the ninth of the nine said what she would have to be handed before she would act, and said it out loud twice.

“That is the thirty-fifth morning in front of anybody, and it is a figure of mornings, and I entered it, and there was no gap in it while I was writing.”

“And here is what I need said in about nine ears on a Saturday. You cannot carry it, because it is not a kind of thing anybody carries.”

“A gap in a stitching is not a parcel and it is not a message and it is not a debt. It is the place where something was taken out and used. There is no version of carrying that, and the reason there is no version of carrying it is that the leaves it stands for were cut out and used, and nobody alive saw them cut, and a thing nobody saw cannot be put in a pocket and cannot be got back out into mornings by anybody.”

“That is not me being careful with a figure. That is the reason there is no figure to carry, and it has been the reason since the third day of the hundred and forty-fourth week and it is not new this morning.”

“And about four of you have gone to work out what a stitch is worth in the last hour, and the woman of forty-four has just told one of you to your face that you are doing it, and I am not going to be the second mouth on the same thing. One of you is enough. I have been the second mouth on about four things this fortnight and every time it cost somebody something.”

The bundle of about four inches was still standing on the counter at about the eleventh hour where the woman who carried it there had put it down, nobody had put a hand on it since the light came up, and the room with the door shut in it had nobody in it and was not going to have anybody in it until the second day of the hundred and fiftieth week.
