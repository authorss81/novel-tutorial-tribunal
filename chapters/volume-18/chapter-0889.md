# Chapter 889: The Second Person, And Who It Has To Be

Second day of the hundred and forty-ninth week. His six hundred and thirty-ninth morning. Four hundred and eighty-nine days after the settlement. The fever eighty-six weeks and four days old. Four hundred and forty-three days since the division. Three hundred and eighty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Sixteen inches stood on the top step at about the seventh hour and it was holding at that figure. It was one day of the coming back and about sixty of those ninety steps were under. The man of forty-three gave the four figures and named the morning the water comes off.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, has held that landing nineteen years, and gave four figures off one stone at about the seventh hour of a Wednesday, and has never once given a fifth.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. It will be off all ninety before the seventh hour tomorrow.”

“It comes off at about the sixth hour tomorrow morning and all ninety of those steps are walkable by the seventh. That is the twenty-eighth time, and that is the third day of the hundred and forty-ninth week, and it is a Thursday, and I am saying it now on a Wednesday so that about four of you have it before it happens.”

“And there is no fifth thing. There has never been a fifth thing and about four of you keep waiting for a Wednesday to make one.”

Then the woman who washes lime said the last piece of the working, which is about the second person, and would not say who it is.

Hannah Voss is forty-one and washes lime, and has said four pieces of a working on the fifth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and has not been asked for any of them.

“There is a fifth piece and I have been sitting on it since Saturday and I am giving it this morning because nobody asked and I would rather have it out of me than have it sitting in a bucket.”

“The second person is not a set of hands. That is what everybody thinks. A man of thirty-one offered me eleven of his own mornings, and a woman of forty-one who holds a seat with nothing behind it offered to go and be the one who asked at the doors so that I would not have to go at all, and both of them were offering to be a second pair of hands, and a second pair of hands on a ladder is worth nothing.”

“The second person is the one who says no. On every morning of those eleven days there has to be a person with me who is allowed to say stop, and who is not paid by the person doing the asking, and who will go home at night and be somewhere else in the morning.”

“That is the whole of it. Not strength. Not a second pair of hands. Somebody who can stop me and who does not sleep in my house.”

“And I am not going to name one. Not because I have not thought about it. Because I have thought about it and I have not got anybody, and if I name a person on this landing in front of nine of you then that person is doing it because nine of you saw them named.”

“I have thought about it three times and I am going to give you all three in about nine seconds and then I am going to leave you with them. A woman of forty-four was the first one I thought of and I would not, because she has asked for a working day of her own for nineteen years and she has not had one and she is not going to get one out of my errand.”

“I thought of a man of fifty-four and I would not, because he cannot read, and there is not one thing I could ask him to do in a cold room all day that would not be a favour with a shape on it.”

“And the third one I thought of was the man who asked. So that is three, and I am not going to say who, and about four of you have been writing the names down since the word gone went out of my mouth.”

“You have all just made a list of the three people I did not name. That is what I was talking about on Friday. You did it in about nine seconds and you did not decide to and one of you has a pencil out.”

Then a man of thirty-one asked for the cost of the looking in miles, and was refused by the woman who owns the question, with the working.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county three hundred and thirty-five days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and four days old.

“Three hundred and thirty-five days in this county, and the cut across this palm is two hundred and four days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundredth is a hundred and eighty-nine days old, and I have not asked it.”

“Twenty-nine wrong things in twenty-nine mornings, and here is the twenty-ninth. Then give it to me in miles. I have walked a long way in my life and I am not frightened of the figure. Eleven days of her working is a thing I cannot use. Miles are a thing I can use.”

Orla Fennimore said no, and said why, and said it was the same no she had given on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week.

“No. And it is the same no and it is not a new one and I am not going to pretend it is.”

“I said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and forty-seventh week, which is a Monday, that I was not going to send a man of thirty-one four hundred miles with a look in his hands. He has been at it for four days since and he has not given up and I have not made it easier.”

“And here is the working, and it is about four sentences. Miles are a figure of a body. Eleven days is a figure of a working. A body is worth what it can carry and a working is worth what it produces, and those two figures have never once been the same figure and they are not going to be made the same figure on my bank.”

“You do not get to be answered in the unit you already carry in your own mouth. If you want a figure of miles then go and be four hundred miles off and find out what a mile is on a road with nobody else on it.”

“And I will tell you what you would have got if I had answered you, because about four of you are working it out in your heads and one of you is putting a pencil out.”

“You would have got a number. You would have carried it up that road, and at the fourth house a woman would have said no, and you would have come down with a number and one no and no working at all.”

“And the number would have been in your mouth for a fortnight, and about four of us would have believed it, and none of us could have checked it, and that is a figure of days made up out of a figure of legs.”

“And a figure of working days is not going to be added to a figure of houses either, because about four of you have been holding those two figures next to each other since the fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week and they are not the same kind of thing and one of them is not a number at all.”

Barnaby Crove asked for a day against the ninth line for the hundred and sixty-eighth morning, and said what a fifth piece sounded like.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for a day against the ninth line for a hundred and sixty-eight mornings, which is eight hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“A hundred and sixty-eight. And a woman of forty-one has just told nine of us that she has not got anybody, and she would not say who, and she would not say why she would not say who.”

“I asked a woman of twenty-nine on the fourth day of the hundred and forty-eighth week, in front of all of you, for a day. And she said no in one word and gave me no figure and no reason and was not unkind about it and was not troubled about it.”

“I have been holding that no for three days and I am not going to use it for anything. It is not a notch and it is not an entry and it is not a thing I am going to bring up on a Friday and say she said no again.”

“And I have not asked the woman who washes lime. I told this bank on the first day of the hundred and forty-eighth week that I was not going to ask her anything and I have kept to it, and I want that kept in the record too, because about four of you have been waiting three days for me to break it.”

“And here is why I am not going to ask her either. If she said a name on that step then that person would be doing it because nine of us were here. I have been doing this eleven years with nobody watching and I am not going to be the reason somebody gets watched.”

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county two hundred and thirty-six days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Two hundred and thirty-six is eight hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Two hundred and thirty-six days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.”

“Nobody asked me for the count I am keeping. That is twenty-eight mornings. A woman of forty-one has just said she has not got a second person, and about four of you looked at me when she said it, and I am not the second person.”

“I am thirteen and I am on this bank and I read things out in three pieces. That is not the same as standing in somebody’s cold room for eleven days and being allowed to say stop. Nobody has asked me and nobody is going to and I would like it said on this step that nobody asked me.”

Four days in a row, about four of them had looked at the boy of thirteen every time a thing was proposed on that step, and on the fourth day a woman of forty-one said out loud that a figure she keeps about one thing does not get to go out and stand for other things, and nobody has repeated it since and about four of them had written it down.
