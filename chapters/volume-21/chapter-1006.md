# Chapter 1006: The Last Morning Of The Hundred And Sixty-Fifth Week And A Man Of Thirty-One Stood At The Top Of Those Steps With His Face To The Wall And Used A Shape Out Loud In Front Of The Wood And Would Not Say The Rest Of It Out Loud, And A Man With A Broom Did His Whole Morning Without Anybody Having To Say Why It Mattered, And Nobody Went Near Anything On A Morning When Every One Of Those Ninety Steps Was Walkable

Sixteen inches came up over that top step in the night and stood there, and by the seventh hour it had stopped rising and had begun, very slightly, to come off, and that is the only kind of morning there is on that landing where the water is high and still standing and everybody knows which way it is going.

Seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fifty-sixth morning. Six hundred and six days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and two days old. Five hundred and sixty days since the division. Five hundred and six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-two days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, and that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and gave no fourth and did not say why not, and worked.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he had got through a morning like this one before without anybody having to say why it mattered, and that he was going to do it again, and that the reason was that there was nothing to say.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Monday. Three figures.” He did not lean the broom against the wall. He kept it in his hands. “I am not going to say one word after that. Not because there is nothing to say. Because everything anybody is going to want to say today is about something other than water, and this stone has been used all week as a place to stand and say things, and I am not going to let it be used like that again today.”

Nobody on that landing took that as an invitation and nobody took it as a rule.

He swept all ninety of those steps that morning. He swept the thirty that were out and he went along the top of the water on the sixty that were under, and he went round the fortieth one, and he went round the chair, and he did not sit on the chair, and he did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step, and he swept round both pieces of wood on the dry strip and did not put the broom near either of them. It took him until about the tenth hour and nobody on that landing spoke to him once while he was doing it.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that a high morning and a dry morning had both been asked on and that his question had been the same shape on both and he wanted that noticed.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-fifth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-fifth. One thousand and six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and I am not going to stand here in sixteen inches and tell you that water is the same as nothing.”

“Water is water. Whether it is up or off it is water.” He set the barrow down and did not start down the bank. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say the thing about the name and then I am not going to say anything about the name again.”

Nobody asked him to go on with the name.

“There is a morning in this week when that water came off that bank and every one of those ninety steps was walkable from the bottom to the top.” He said it to the middle of the landing and not to anybody. “I know when that morning was. I am not going to tell anybody what it was, because a figure of a morning is a figure of me being on that landing and I have not got to the business of being on that landing for nothing.”

Nobody worked out the morning and nobody asked.

“And I am telling you that I did not walk up that bank on that morning with anything in my hands except a barrow, and that is all I am going to say about it, and it is not a promise and it is not a virtue and it is not a refusal of anybody. It is where I was.”

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him, found the wall, and stood against it, and said that nobody had asked him for anything and that he wanted that said before the morning got past the middle.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that nobody on that landing had asked him to put his hand on that wood this week and that he wanted it said out loud in his own mouth before anybody else claimed it for them.

“Nobody has asked me this morning and nobody has asked me this week. I have stood at this wall with my hand at the end of that strip and nobody has come up this bank and said would you put your hand on it, and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to offer.” He kept his hand on the stone. “And I have got that thing on my list where it has been since I found it and I have not crossed it off and I am not going to cross it off this week, and crossing it off is the only thing on that list that would have taken me a minute.”

Nobody crossed it off and nobody offered to do it for him.

“And nobody is to thank me for not being asked. Nobody asked me, so there was nothing done by me, and a man who is thanked for a thing that did not happen is a man who has been thanked for stopping.”

Nobody thanked him.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures, and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and came all the way to the top of those steps, and turned his back on the dry strip and faced the wall, and used a shape out loud in front of it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-two days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-sixth is three hundred and six days old, which is one thousand and six less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. I am not going to be relieved about it and I am not going to be unrelieved about it and I am not going to ask anybody whether I ought to be.”

He put his face to the cold stone and stayed there long enough that his breath came back off it.

“One hundred and forty-sixth wrong things in one hundred and forty-sixth mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-sixth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything to him about either of those.

“And here is the sentence. I have been standing on this landing all week waiting for somebody to hand me a sentence and I have worked out that nobody is going to, and I am going to use one that has been going round this county in other mouths, and I am going to stand here with my back to that wood and use it, and nobody is to stop me and nobody is to tell me I am the first one to say it.”

Nobody stopped him. Nobody said he was the first one to say it and nobody said he was not the first one to say it.

“A thing that is named for a person is not that person’s thing.” He said it into the wall. “That is the shape. And I have used it, and I am not going to turn round and say the rest of it, because the rest of it is about me and I am not going to say a thing about me in front of that wood.”

Nobody turned him round.

“I am going to say why not, once, and then I am going to stop. A man who puts his face to a wall and says a shape about a piece of wood behind him has made himself a figure. Not a person. A figure. And this landing has spent a week keeping figures of people off it, and a man of forty-three has said out loud that a big number is not a good week and a man with a broom has said out loud that he does not want to be made a matter, and I am not going to be the one who does that.”

Nobody told him what kind of figure it would have been.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-two mornings, and both halves of that are going to be said out loud on this landing every morning until somebody stops me, and nobody is going to stop me.”

Nobody stopped him.

“And there was a morning this week when every one of those ninety steps was walkable and I have not said a word about it and I am not going to say a word about it now either. A man of fifty-four has said where he was that morning and he has said what he had in his hands and I am not going to stand next to a man and match it.”

Nobody matched it and nobody thanked Barnaby Crove for saying where he was.

Then everybody went down that bank at about the tenth hour and the landing was left with a broom and a woman who reads for a living who had not gone yet and who sat down on the bottom step, which she has done twice in this county.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it, and did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step. He did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Sixteen inches over that top step and it holds, that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A man said a shape out loud with his face to the wall and would not say the rest of it, and nobody said he was the first. A man who cannot read said where he was on the one morning of that week when ninety steps were walkable and would not say which morning it was, and nobody worked it out, and nothing on that landing was touched.
