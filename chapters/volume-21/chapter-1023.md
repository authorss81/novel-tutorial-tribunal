# Chapter 1023: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Eighth Week And Sixteen Inches That Hold On The Top Step At About The Seventh Hour And About Sixty Of Those Ninety Steps Under And Three Days Of The Coming Back, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Came Up That Cart Road Having Decided On The Way Up It To Stop Saying A Name Out Loud For The Rest Of His Life And Asked A Man With A Broom For The Figure Of How That Name Got Onto That Strip And Was Refused The Figure And Was Not Refused Anything Else, And Was Not Told He Was Right And Was Not Told He Was Wrong

The line of grit on that top step had gone from a line to a few stones, and every one of the stones was sitting in a saucer of its own making, which is what happens when water goes over grit and then goes off again without troubling to move it.

Third day of the hundred and sixty-eighth week. His seven hundred and seventy-third morning. Six hundred and twenty-three days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and five weeks and five days old. Five hundred and seventy-seven days since the division. Five hundred and twenty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and sixty-nine days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that three days was the figure he had given on this stone the last time it held this high, and that he had said on the morning before that a line of grit on that step would not stop the water, and that this morning was the morning that proved it and that he had not said he was pleased about it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-eighth week that a thing on his stone that a man might put a number to was a thing he had been asked to sweep round for nineteen years without being asked why, and that he was not going to start asking it now.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the third day of the hundred and sixty-eighth week, and it is a Thursday. Three figures.”

He worked along the top step and went round the two stones with the saucers round them and did not push them off, because pushing them off would be a thing he had decided about them.

“And that is the finding of this morning and it is not a figure. That grit came down off that stone in a week of wind and it has had three days of standing water on it and it is still sitting there in the same place in a hole of its own making. The water went over it and made it comfortable and did not take it. I said it would not stop for it and it did not, and I am not going to make anything of that, and if anybody wants to know whether I am glad then they can come up here at the seventh hour for the next fortnight and watch a man sweep.” Nobody watched a man sweep.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour, and set the barrow down on the dry part, and put his hands flat on the wall, which is what he does.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and second mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and twenty-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and second. One thousand and twenty-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“The man with the broom said three things off that stone at the seventh hour and I understood none of them and I am not going to ask.” He set the barrow down and put his hands flat on the wall again. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am not going to use the fact that a man talked at the seventh hour. A man talks at the seventh hour every morning of nineteen years and it has never once been about my question, and the morning I begin taking what he says as being about my question is the morning my question is finished, because from then on it will be whatever he happened to say last.” Nobody told him whether his question was finished.

“And I am going to say the last of it and it is about me and not about that stone. Nineteen years, at the sixth hour, in the same words, and it has never once been answered and it has never once been the worse for it. I would like that written down by somebody who can write, and nobody on this landing is going to write it down, and I am not going to stop, and I would rather somebody said out loud that I had said it than that they had said nothing.” Nobody wrote anything down for him and he did not stop.

Then the man of thirty-eight came up that cart road at about the ninth hour with his hand flat against the board in his coat and without the length he had carried on other mornings, and he came the whole way without speaking to the woman at the wall, and got to the top step before he spoke.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, with a child of nine, thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board in the inside of his coat, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-eighth week that he had decided at the bottom of his own road that morning not to say that name out loud ever again, and had come up with the intention of finding out whether he was entitled to stop, and that the only person on that landing with any standing to say was a man with a broom.

He did not look at the strip. He put his hand on the wall and left it there.

“I have come to ask you for a figure.”

Corvin Tace turned the broom over and did not stop working.

“You are going to be told three figures at the seventh hour and that was this morning.”

“Not the water. The other thing.” He took his hand off his coat and held it up, flat, the way a man holds up a thing he has not decided to put down. “I want the figure of how that name got onto that strip. Not who put it there. The number of times. I have come up that road with a sentence in my mouth and I could not put it down, and I have worked out that the reason I could not put it down is that I do not know what it is that I am carrying. Give me the figure and I will know whether there is anything there to carry.”

Corvin Tace stopped working.

“No.”

“That is a very fast no for a man who gives figures for a living.”

“I know it is.” He put the broom head down on the top step. “Listen to the reason and then decide whether you want to argue with it, because you cannot argue with the reason and you can argue with the no all morning. Every figure that has ever come off this stone is about this stone. Depth on the top step. How much of the flight is standing in it. How long before it comes back. There is not one number on this landing that is about a person and I have kept it that way for nineteen years by not giving one, and I am not going to open that on the third day of the hundred and sixty-eighth week because a man with a board in his coat has come up a road and asked nicely.”

“Ask it differently then. Does it change anything.”

“Nothing on that stone changes, and nothing on that strip changes, and I am going to tell you the working of that in a moment.”

“Does it change what you sweep.”

“No. And there is a working attached to it, so you can have the working and stop having the no.” He turned the broom over again. “I swept round that strip before there was a name in anybody’s mouth and I sweep round it now. Same order. Same line down the front of it, same round the back, same off the end. I have not changed my order once in nineteen years and I have not had to change it for a word, and if I had to change it for a word then that word would be a figure about this stone and this stone does not hand out figures about words.” Nobody said anything about what a word was or was not.

“But that is not the half I am refusing.” He came down off the step and stood in front of him and did not raise his voice, which was the thing that made it land. “You came up here wanting to know whether you are in that or not. You have been in that since the second morning of the last week and you have not been able to put it down, and you have come up a road to find out whether you are allowed to stop. And I am not going to tell you that you are and I am not going to tell you that you are not, and that is not a kindness, it is arithmetic.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the only answer there is and you know it is.” He put his hands on the broom head. “If I tell you that you are in that, then you are in that because I said so, and you will be a man in it by a man with a broom. If I tell you that you are not, then you will be out of it by me, and you will spend the rest of your life knowing which side of it you got out on. Neither of those is a fact about that strip. Both of them are a fact about this stone and about me, and I do not hand those out either.” Nobody handed those out either.

“So you can have the working or you can have nothing, and the working is that a thing lying on a strip of stone does not get changed by people saying things near it, and that is a thing you can stand on top of and believe or not believe and it does not need a number under it. If you want to stop saying that name, stop. Nobody on this landing is going to stop you and nobody on this landing is going to hand you a paper saying that you were right to.”

He did not say the man of thirty-eight was right to stop. He did not say the man of thirty-eight was wrong to have said it. He turned the broom over and went back to the top step and worked the stones round the saucers.

“Has anybody else on this landing ever put that name on anything.”

That question got nothing from that stone, and none of the shapes an answer can come in came back off it, and the man with the broom was the last person on that landing who would have handed it over and he did not.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, asked for nothing, and stood at the top step and heard the whole of it, because he had been on the landing before the man of thirty-eight got to the step and had not said anything.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and sixty-nine days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-eight days old.

“Four hundred and sixty-nine days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-eight days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-seventy-third is three hundred and twenty-three days old, which is one thousand and twenty-three less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to ask for one on the morning after a man has told me what he is owed a paper for.”

“One hundred and sixty-third wrong things in one hundred and sixty-third mornings, and this is the hundred and sixty-third, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Neither half of it was answered and nobody asked him which he had meant.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and sixty-nine mornings, and I am going to keep saying both halves of that out loud on this landing until somebody stops me.” Nobody said either half for him. He did not say them again, which is a thing he has not managed on any other morning of this county, and he noticed himself doing it and did not say so.

“Now the part I want in the air and I am putting it there because I did not put it there at the eighth hour and that was cowardice.” He came down one step. “I wanted a man with a broom to tell a man of thirty-eight to stop saying that name. That is what I wanted and I did not ask for it and I did not say it and I stood on that landing at the eighth hour working out how it would look if somebody else said it, and I worked it out, and I let it happen, and he did it without me, and he was never asked.” Nobody said anything about how it looked.

“And I am not going to be thanked for the letting, and nobody is to thank him either, because he did that before I could get to it and he did it in his own mouth and if I say one word about it I am claiming a piece of it.” He stood still on the step. “A man who wants somebody else to do his refusing has already done the refusing. That is what I have learned this morning and I have got no use for it.” Nobody told him he had got no use for it either.

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and the man of thirty-eight went down it first and then down the cart road, and nobody went up the road after him.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went along the top of the water on them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side. At the end of that morning the two stones with the saucers round them were still in the same places they had been at the seventh hour, and he had walked round them nine times, and he had put his foot on neither of them, and he could not have said why he was minding so much.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it, then or since, and nobody laid a hand on it after those sentences either. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said one out loud either, and nobody said what a piece of wood was, and nobody was given a figure of how many people had put themselves into anything on that landing.

A man of thirty-eight came up a road with the intention of finding out whether he was entitled to stop saying a name, and a man with a broom refused him the figure and gave him the working instead, and did not tell him he was right to stop and did not tell him he was wrong to have started, and would not say whether that name had put him into that strip or had not, on the grounds that either answer would have done it by arithmetic. And at the foot of that bank the man of thirty-eight took his hand out from under his coat, where a board with thirty-two notches in it had been sitting against his ribs all morning, and put both hands in his pockets, and went down the cart road without saying it.
