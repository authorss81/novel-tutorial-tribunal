# Chapter 1010: The Same Gaps Again

The water came off that bank in the night and the man with the broom was the first one up it, which he had not been since the bank filled, because there was nothing to sweep on the dry stone and there was nothing to sweep on the wet stone either and he came up anyway.

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week. His seven hundred and sixtieth morning. Six hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and six days old. Five hundred and sixty-four days since the division. Five hundred and ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-six days in this county of Kell.

The man of forty-three came up at about the seventh hour and stood on the top step with the broom in his two hands and said there were three gaps in the morning, and said them in words he had not used before, and then did not use again.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that he had said the same three absences before in different words and was not going to pretend the second time was a different kind of thing.

“There is no figure of water on this stone this morning. There is no figure of how much of that flight is standing in it. There is no figure of how long before it comes back.” He turned the broom head down on the top step. “Three gaps in a morning, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and it is a Friday. I have said three gaps before this week and I said them in other words and I am not going to pretend that saying them a second time makes them a different kind of thing, and I am not going to say them a third time in these words.”

Nobody asked him to say them again.

“The water came off this bank in the night and it will be back, and in between there is a morning with nothing in it, and I have stopped being able to improve on that by wanting a figure. Nineteen years I gave whatever the stone gave me. There are mornings the stone gives me nothing and I am still here at the seventh hour with a broom, and that is not a complaint and it is not a virtue. It is what a landing is.”

Nobody said it was a complaint and nobody said it was a virtue.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that he had come to the end of what he had to say about it and that he was going to keep saying the same words, and that the landing could do what it liked with that.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-ninth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and ten less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-ninth. One thousand and ten less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and it is the same words and it is the same hour and it is the same question, and the only thing that has changed on this landing in a fortnight is what people say when they pass each other.”

“Everything has changed and nobody has noticed because they were all too busy not saying it.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And that is it, and it has been it, and it is going to be it on the first morning of the next week whatever is standing on that strip by then.”

Nobody told him he was owed an answer and nobody told him what was going to be standing on the strip.

Then the man of thirty-eight came up that cart road again with the board inside his coat and stood at the foot of the steps and asked one question out loud, and it was about the name, and nobody answered him.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, with a child of nine, thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board in the inside of his coat, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that he had come up a road he had said he would not come up to ask a question and that he knew before he asked it what the answer was going to be and that he had asked it anyway.

He put his hand flat over the notches through the cloth.

“Has anybody on this landing got a figure of how long that name has stood on that piece of wood.” He said it to the landing and not to anybody. “That is the question. That is all of it and I am not asking it because I want the figure. I am asking it because a shape has been going round this county all week about a name and a name with nothing at all behind it is not a thing anybody on a landing should be holding.”

Nobody answered him.

Not the man with the broom, and not the woman who saws lengths who was not there, and not the man of thirty-one who was at the bottom of the steps with his back to the landing. Nobody said yes and nobody said no and nobody asked him a question back and nobody gave him a figure of mornings.

Nobody answered that question.

“And that is the answer.” He did not raise his voice. “Not one of you said a word and I want it recorded that not one of you said a word, because in about three weeks there will be somebody come up this road who asks it and gets a figure off somebody, and it will not be me and it will not be from this landing. And I am not going to be thanked for asking it and I am not going to be told I was right to ask it.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had been right.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and there was a want in him that he did not give, and everybody on that landing could see there was one.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-six days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-five days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-six days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-five days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixtieth is three hundred and ten days old, which is one thousand and ten less seven hundred.” He said it without stopping on any part of it. “It is unanswered. I have not asked it on any morning this week. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one. I am not relieved and I am not unrelieved and I am not going to be either of those things quietly.”

Nobody said it had been worth the waiting and nobody said it had been a waste of it.

“One hundred and fiftieth wrong things in one hundred and fiftieth mornings, and this is the hundred and fiftieth, and it is a whole figure, and nobody has told me what kind of figure it is and nobody has told me that I found anything, and I have noticed that a man on this landing said a whole figure out loud on a Saturday with nothing beside it and nobody asked him what it was for, and I am not going to ask him either.”

Nobody asked him.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed and it is not going to be changed on this landing by anything that happens to that wood.” He stood on the top step. “And here is what I am not saying this morning, and I am telling you that I am not saying it, because a man who says out loud that he is holding something back on a landing turns his back into a thing that is being held back.”

Nobody asked him what he was holding back.

“It is not about the wood. I want that understood. What I am not saying this morning is about a thing I want that I have not wanted before this week, and I am not going to give it out on a morning when every one of those ninety steps is walkable from the bottom to the top, because I have watched what this landing does with a thing that is said on a morning like that.”

Nobody said anything about it and nobody worked out what it was.

“My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-six mornings. That is the same as it was at the start of the week and it is the same as it will be at the end of it and I am going to keep saying it on this landing because it is the only figure I have got that nobody else can say.”

Nobody said it for him.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him, found the wall, and stood against it with his hand above the level of the strip, and said one thing about the fortnight and then went down.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that his list was the same as it had been at the start of that fortnight and that he was not going to be thanked for that.

“My list is where it was. The thing at the end of that strip is on it and it has not come off and it is not going to come off, and nobody is to ask me whether I have thought any more about it because I have thought about it every morning and the answer has not changed and it is not going to change while I am on this landing.” He took his hand off the wall. “Nobody is to thank me for that. There is nothing in it. I stood against a wall and I did not put my hand out.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him.

Then the woman of forty-four stood at the wall at the bottom of that cart road and said one sentence and then went back down it, and nobody went up that road.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, keeps twenty-nine chairs at a house that is not in this county, was asked once what she did and has not been asked since, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that there was a shape going round about a name and that she was still not in it and that nobody was to thank her.

Nobody thanked her.

And everybody went down that bank, and Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps with the water off them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it, and did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step. He did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip, and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Both pieces of it were where they had been on every morning of that fortnight, and the grit was swept up off the stone around them and off the stone between them, and the dry strip at the top of those steps came out of that morning as the only clean bit of stone on the whole landing.

There was no figure of water on that landing that morning and no figure of how much of that flight was standing in it and no figure of how long before it came back, and an offer stood at three hundred and ten days and was not answered, and nobody was relieved.
