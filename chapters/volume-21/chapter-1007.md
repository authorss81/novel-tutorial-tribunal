# Chapter 1007: The First Morning Of The Hundred And Sixty-Sixth Week And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Came Up A Road He Said He Would Not Come Up And Carried A Board Inside His Coat And Said Out Loud That A Shape Was His And Nobody Was To Tell Him Where He Got It, And A Boy Gave A Figure Of His Own And Would Not Turn It Into A Figure Of Anything Else, And A Woman Stood At A Wall At The Bottom Of That Cart Road And Nobody Went Up To Her

First day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week. His seven hundred and fifty-seventh morning. Six hundred and seven days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and three days old. Five hundred and sixty-one days since the division. Five hundred and seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-three days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said the week had turned and that he was not going to make anything out of a week turning, and that everything anybody wanted to say about last week could be said on a Tuesday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that a week turning is a thing that happens to a landing whether or not anything on it has turned.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the first day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and it is a Tuesday. Three figures.” He set the broom against the wall, clear of the strip. “The week has turned. Nothing else on this stone has turned. I would like it noticed that those are not the same statement and that I have made both of them out loud.”

Nobody noticed and nobody said the second one was false.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said that a new week was not a new question and that he was not going to be pleased or disappointed by a Tuesday.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-sixth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-sixth. One thousand and seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and you have been on this bank at the sixth hour through a change of week without once moving a word of it, and I have stopped noticing, and I want you to know that I have stopped noticing.”

“You have not stopped noticing. You have stopped saying so.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And that is the whole of what I bring up this bank and it does not get a new coat on a Tuesday.”

Then the boy came up out of the lane before the light had properly come off the wall and gave his own figure and said in the same breath what it was not.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and fifty-four days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and fifty-four is one thousand less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and fifty-four days, and it is one more than the morning before, and it is four more than the morning this time last week, and I have not worked out why it should be four more and I have not been asked.” He put his bucket down on the stone. “And somebody on this landing gave a figure of days out loud yesterday and somebody said out loud that a big number is not a good week. I did not hear either of those. I am not going to ask. I have got a number of my own and it is a number of times and it is going to stay a number of times, and I am not going to turn it into a number of water or a number of steps or a number of things lying on a strip, and if anybody on this landing wants me to do that they can want it in front of me.”

Nobody wanted him to do it and nobody told him he was right.

Then the man of thirty-eight came up that cart road with a board under his arm and then put the board inside his coat when he got to the steps, and said out loud why he had done that.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, with a child of nine, thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board in the inside of his coat, and who came back up a road he said he would not come up, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that a shape that had been in other mouths was his and that he was going to keep saying it.

He put his hand flat on the inside of his coat over the notches and left it there while he spoke, and he spoke to the middle of the landing and not to anybody in it.

“You have all been saying a shape this week. A thing named for a person is not that person’s thing. I have had it off more than one of you this week and I have had it off a woman at the bottom of a cart road and off a man with a broom who does not know where he got it either.”

Nobody said where he got it.

“I am going to keep saying it. And I am saying now, out loud, that it is mine. Not because I made it up. I did not make it up and I could not make it up, I have never had a thought in my life that anybody would want after it.” He moved his hand a little and the notches went under the cloth. “It is mine the way a haft is the man’s once the man has stood in front of the sawyer and named what he wants off it. I have come up this road to say a thing I have been saying in my own head for years without knowing it had words in it.”

Nobody told him it was his and nobody told him it was not.

“And nobody on this landing is to tell me I have taken it. I have not taken it off anybody. And nobody is to tell me I have come into it. A thing can be in a man before it is in his mouth and a man who says a thing he has been carrying is not a thief and is not an heir and is not a disciple.” He took his hand out of his coat and let it hang. “And I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and I am not going to be told I have a right to it, and I am not going to be asked where I got it, because I will answer that I do not know and I will mean it.”

Nobody asked him and nobody thanked him and nobody told him about any right of anything.

“And I am going to say one more thing about the board in my coat and then I am going down that bank. There are thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it and every one of them is a notch and not a word, and I cut them and I have not cut a word into it, and there is a notch in it for every kind of thing I have done that I would not do again and there is going to be a notch cut in it for anything I do from here.” He put his hand back over them. “And it is nobody’s board. It is a board with notches in it that a man of thirty-eight is carrying home.”

Nobody asked whose board it was and nobody asked him to cut another notch.

Then the woman of forty-four stood at the wall at the bottom of that cart road before the seventh hour and said what she was not part of out loud before any of the morning had finished itself, and nobody went up that road.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, keeps twenty-nine chairs at a house that is not in this county, was asked once what she did and has not been asked since, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that there was a shape going round about a name and she was not in it and was not going to be asked whether she was in it.

She said it from the wall and did not come past it.

“Nobody is to come up this road.” She had her hands in her sleeves. “Not about the water. Not about a name. Not about the shape that has been going round this county since Tuesday, and I am not going to tell anybody whether it is a good shape or a bad one, because that is a thing a person gets to have an opinion about and I have not got one and I am not going to perform one at a wall.”

Nobody had an opinion about it at that wall and nobody came up the road.

“I keep twenty-nine chairs at a house that is not in this county. I have been asked about them once in my life and I have not been asked since, and that is the whole of what anybody knows about what I do, and it is enough of a life that I am not going to stand at the bottom of a road and let a week like this one put me in it.”

Nobody put her in it.

“I have said before that nobody thanks me and nobody is going to thank me and I am saying it again before anybody can. Nobody thanks me for standing at a wall. Nobody thanks me for not coming past it. Nobody is to thank me for saying that I am not in this.”

Nobody thanked her.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and did not ask for anything, and stood at the top of the steps and looked down the cart road at the wall without saying one word about what was at it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-three days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-one days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-seventh is three hundred and seven days old, which is one thousand and seven less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one.”

He looked down the cart road for a while and then stopped looking at it.

“One hundred and forty-seventh wrong things in one hundred and forty-seventh mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-seventh, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything to him about either of those.

“My want is to be somebody and a man of thirty-eight has come up that road this morning to say a thing is his, and I am not going to be jealous of him in front of you. I am going to be jealous of him down that bank where you cannot see it, and I am saying it out loud here so that nobody can say I hid it, and I am not going to do anything about it.”

Nobody did anything about it.

“My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-three mornings. And I am going to say the last part and I want it said in my own mouth before a week of this finishes and somebody else says it better.”

Nobody said anything.

“A man of thirty-eight has decided that a shape is his and he came up a road he said he would not come up to say so. I have decided nothing this week except that I am not going to stand next to a man and match him, and I have not got a shape and I am not going to take one off anybody, and there is a difference between those two things that I do not know how to put into words this morning, and I would rather say that than pretend to a word I have not got.”

He put his hands behind his back and stayed there a while.

Then everybody went down that bank, and nobody went up the cart road.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Sixteen inches on the top step and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A shape that was in more mouths than one on that landing was declared by one of them to be his, and nobody told him he had taken it or come into it. A boy gave a figure of his own and would not turn it into a figure of anything else. A woman stood at a wall and said she was not in it before anybody could have thanked her, and nobody thanked her, and nobody went up the road.
