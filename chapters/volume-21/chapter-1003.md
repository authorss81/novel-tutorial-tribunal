# Chapter 1003: A Friday And Four Inches And About Ninety Of Those Ninety Steps Under And Seven Days Of The Coming Back, And A Boy Said A Thing About A Pail The Way His Mother Would Have Said It And Nobody Made It Into Anything, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Came Up Along That Wall With His Hand Out And Said A Thing Out Loud About A Man Of Thirty-One And Was Refused By Nobody Because There Was Nothing To Refuse

Four inches came back onto that bank in the night and put themselves into the joints of ninety steps all at once, and the sound of it was the sound of a thing being poured into a trough in the dark, and the boy of thirteen was the only one on that landing who heard it because he slept over the shed.

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fifty-third morning. Six hundred and three days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks and six days old. Five hundred and fifty-seven days since the division. Five hundred and three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-nine days in this county of Kell.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that seven was the biggest figure he had given in a fortnight and that he was not going to make anything of it and that a big figure of days is not a good week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that a landing is not improved by a big number and is not made worse by a small one.

“Four at the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Friday. Three figures.” He set the head of the broom flat and stood on it. “Seven days is the whole of what I have got and it is a big number and it means the water is low. It does not mean a good week. A landing does not get better in a dry week, it gets easier to walk on.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that a big number of days was the same thing as a small number of days and he wanted it said in that order.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-second mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-second. One thousand and three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and you are telling me that a number of days does not care how big it is, and that is the first sensible thing anybody has said on this stone in a fortnight, and I am not going to praise you for it either because being praised is another thing you would have to carry down the bank.”

“I have carried everything I have ever been given down that bank.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say one thing about the water and then I am going down. If the water is low for seven days then it is low for seven days and that is a fact and not an opportunity, and I have watched this landing get excited about a number before and it did not go well for anybody.”

Nobody got excited about the number.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and did not come on to the landing, and stood at the head of the steps with a pail in each hand and said the same thing he had said about a cup, and it was about a pail this time, and nobody asked him what the difference was.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and fifty days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and fifty is one thousand less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and fifty days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He set a pail down on the top step. “And I am not going to tell you what I worked out since the last time I said it and I am not going to be asked. But I will say that I did it again last night with a pail in my hand, and it came out the same, and I have not told anybody where I got it and I am not going to tell anybody where I got it, and if somebody asks me whether I made it up I am going to say I do not know.”

Nobody asked him whether he made it up and nobody told him he was right about it.

“And I am going to say the part that is new and it is not about the pail.” He put his other hand down on the stone. “That is the second time I have said a thing that I have not got a name for, and the first time was about a cup, and nobody on this landing has joined the two of them together, and I want it said that I noticed them not being joined and that I am not going to join them myself.”

Nobody joined them together.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him and found the wall with it and went along it at the height he always goes at, and his hand went past the end of the dry strip without stopping on it, and he said one thing about that before he said anything else.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he was going to stop saying seven things out loud on that landing because a thing said often enough stops meaning anything, and that was the only reason for it.

“Two pieces of wood on that strip and my hand has gone along that wall at the same height it has gone along it for nineteen years and it has not stopped on either of them this morning, and I am not going to make a shape out of it, and I am not going to stand here and be praised for it.”

Nobody praised him for it.

“And I am also going to say the thing I have come up here to say and I have been angry about it since before the light.” He turned his face out into the middle of the landing. “There is a man on this landing who put a name out loud over a thing he put down in the dark. And he has never once said out loud what a name said over a thing does to that thing. Not to me. Not to the man who holds the gravel. Not to the woman who sawed the length. Nobody on this stone has heard him do it, and every one of you has decided what it means for him.”

Nobody said what it meant for him.

“So here is what a name said over a thing does. It does not make the thing the name. That is all it does. A name said over a piece of wood is a thing a man has said, and a thing a man has said belongs to the man who said it, and it is not a fact about the wood and it is not a gift to the man it names and it is not a debt either.” He had his hand back on the wall. “And I have said that in this exact spot before, and I have said it to this landing, and nobody has ever asked me where I got it. Nobody is going to ask me now. I am going to be perfectly clear that this is the last time I say it out loud, because the fourth time a thing like this gets said on a landing it stops being a thing anybody said and it becomes a thing the landing says, and a landing that says things is a landing that has somebody in it.”

Nobody said he had got it right and nobody said he had got it wrong.

“You did not ask me where I got it. I want that in whatever anybody writes down, because the one thing I will not do on this landing is be the person who handed a man of thirty-one a sentence for him to say about himself.”

Nobody handed him a sentence.

“Nobody on this landing has touched that wood today. Not for lifting it, not for turning it over, not for reading it, not for putting it back where it was, not for carrying it inside, and not for doing anything else to it at all, and my hand went past it and did not stop and that is not a ceremony and it is not a favour to anybody.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with four inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and he had heard the whole of what the man who cannot see well said, and he came up after it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-nine days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and eighteen days old.

“Four hundred and forty-nine days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and eighteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-third is three hundred and three days old, which is one thousand and three less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it before the water is off those steps again.”

He came to the top step and stopped and did not look at the strip.

“One hundred and forty-third wrong things in one hundred and forty-third mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-third, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody took either half of that and nobody asked him to say it slower.

“I am going to say one sentence and it is not a speech and I am not going to say it twice.” He turned round to the middle of the landing and then turned back to the wall. “Nobody on this landing has said what I found, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to hand my own mouth a thing to say about a thing I put down. That is my want and my cost both in one sentence. I want to be somebody and I have worked out that the cheapest way to be somebody on this landing this week is to be the man who worked something out, and I am not going to do it, and it has cost me nothing at all, which is the whole of my trouble and I have said it out loud on this stone.”

Nobody said that was the right way to be somebody and nobody said it was the wrong way.

Then the boy went down into the lane and the man of fifty-four went down the bank and the man who cannot see well went down the steps with his hand out and nobody helped him down, and the man of thirty-one went down it after a while and nobody went with him.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about ninety of them were under, and went along the top of the water on the ones that were not, and went round the chair and did not sit on it, and did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step, and did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Four inches on the top step, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back, which are three figures about the water. A boy said a thing about a pail twice and nobody joined the two of them together. A man who cannot see well said a true thing about what a name said out loud does, and about whom it belongs to, and would not say it again and would not say where he had got it, and a man of thirty-one stood at the top of the steps and did not take it and did not say why.
