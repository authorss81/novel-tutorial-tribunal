# Chapter 1008: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Sixth Week And A Woman Who Saws Lengths Came Up The Cart Road And Used A Shape In A Mouth That Owed Something And Would Not Say What It Owed And A Man Of Thirty-One Saw What It Cost Her And Did Not Say So, And Nobody On That Landing Was Told What To Say By A Person Who Wanted It Said

The two days of the coming back came out of that stone at about the seventh hour in a voice that had been giving figures off it for nineteen years, and it was the shortest figure he gave and it told nobody anything at all, and he said so.

Second day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week. His seven hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Six hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and four days old. Five hundred and sixty-two days since the division. Five hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-four days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that two days was the shortest figure he gave and that it was the one he knew least about and that he was not going to improve on it by saying it twice.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that a short figure is not a small morning and that nobody on his stone was to carry it anywhere.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the second day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and it is a Wednesday. Three figures.” He put the head of the broom down on the top step. “Two days. That is the shortest thing I give and it is not a small morning and it is not a warning. I have said that before on this landing and I am not going to say it a fourth time this week. Take it or leave it. It is the water.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said that two days was the number he had been waiting for without knowing that he had been waiting for it, and that he was not going to say what he would do in two days.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-seventh mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-seventh. One thousand and eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and a short figure of days is not a short week, and there are sixty of those ninety steps under this morning and you have been up a bank with sixty steps of water in front of you for longer than most men have been alive.”

“I have. And I have noticed that in two days there will be about thirty of them out, and I am not going to say that out loud, and you are not going to repeat it back to me.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say one more thing about that name and it is the last one.”

Nobody asked him for another.

“There is a name on that strip and I have had a week to get used to the idea of it, and I have not got used to it, and I have decided that I am not going to. Not because anybody has told me to. Because there is no version of being used to it that does not have me in it, and I have been in a great many things this year without being asked and I am not going to volunteer.”

Then the woman who saws lengths came up the cart road at about the eighth hour with nothing in her hands, which is the first time in this county that anybody has come up that road with nothing in her hands, and she came up the steps and stood on the top one.

Wenna Pyle is thirty-eight, who saws lengths and sets studs at a bench in an open-sided shed behind a wheelwright’s about three hundred yards down that cart road, and who carries her own cup out to that bench because there is no table in the shed, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that she was going to use a shape she had said once and had somebody else’s mouth on it, and that she was going to do it because of something she had done and was not going to name.

Nobody told her to say anything.

She stood on the top step and did not look at the strip.

“A man said a true thing on that step earlier this week and would not say it again. I have not said it and I am not going to say it again either.” She put her hands behind her back. “I am going to use it once and I am going to use it properly, and I am going to use it about my own trade and not about that strip, because if I use it about that strip this morning then it is being used by the person who put a name on that strip, and that is a thing a person can do and I would rather not be that person at the top of these steps at the eighth hour.”

Nobody asked her why she was not that person.

“A thing cut for a person’s use is not that person’s until somebody says the cut was for them. And nobody in this county is going to say the cut was for them. That is the shape and it is mine to use today because I said a thing once in my own mouth and I have watched what it did.”

Nobody said what it did and nobody disagreed with her about it.

“And here is the part that is why I came up with empty hands and it is not a speech and it is two sentences.” Her voice went slightly and she let it. “I have been waiting all week for somebody to come up that road and tell me that I should not have said it. And nobody has come. And I have found out that I can wait for a thing for a week and it does not come, and the not-coming does not make the saying smaller. That is all. I am not relieved and nobody is to thank me and I am not going to stand here and be told that I have got anything right.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody told her she had got anything right and nobody told her she ought not to have said it.

“And I am not going to say what that wood is and nobody is going to get it out of me this week. I said a thing about a piece of wood once. Whatever comes out of that is what comes out of it.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour, and had been standing at the bottom of the steps for the last part of it, and came up, and did not say what he had seen, and everybody on that landing waited for him to.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-four days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-three days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-three days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-eighth is three hundred and eight days old, which is one thousand and eight less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to.”

He stood on the top step on the other side of her from the strip.

“One hundred and forty-eighth wrong things in one hundred and forty-eighth mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-eighth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody picked the figure up and nobody put it down again, and both of those are the same silence.

“A woman of thirty-eight stood on this step and used a shape and said out loud that she was waiting for somebody to tell her she ought not to have said a thing she said a week ago, and that nobody came, and that the not-coming did not make the saying smaller. I am not going to say anything about that.” He put his hands behind his back, which is what she had done. “I want it noticed that I could say something about that and I have decided not to, and I am not going to be thanked for not saying it, and I am not going to be asked afterwards what I would have said.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him what he would have said.

“But I am going to say the other half of that, because it is mine and it is old and it is not new this morning. I worked out on a step on this landing that saying a thing out loud makes it mine and not the landing’s. That was the whole of what I worked out and it has not got any bigger this week and nobody is to treat this week as though I found it this week. I found it once. It has been standing on this landing since then being the size it was.”

Nobody said he had found it this week.

“My want is to be somebody. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-four mornings. And I am going to say the last thing and it is the only thing I have been thinking about since the eighth hour.”

He turned his face towards the water and not towards the strip.

“Nobody on this landing was told what to say this morning. I want that said. A woman of thirty-eight came up a road with empty hands and used a shape that was in other mouths before hers and she picked it up because of a thing she had done, and not one person on this landing opened their mouth to tell her to say it, and not one person has opened their mouth to tell her she said it right. That is the whole of what happened at the eighth hour. I have been watching that landing all week wanting somebody to hand me a sentence and this morning I watched a mouth take one and nobody hand it over, and I would like it recorded that I noticed and I am not going to make a figure of how many mornings I have wanted it.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the tenth hour and the man of thirty-one went last and stopped at the top step and looked back along the strip for a moment before he went down.

He did the whole flight before the others were off it, and he did the thirty that were out first and the sixty that were under after, and he went round the fortieth one both times, and he went round the chair, and he did not sit on it. The iron in the seam of the fortieth step was under his hand twice and he took his hand off it twice. Neither piece of wood on the dry strip was under his hand at all. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Sixteen inches on the top step and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A woman used a shape out loud about her own trade and would not say what the wood was a second time, and a man of thirty-one did not say what it had cost her, and nobody on that landing was told what to say by anybody who wanted it said.
