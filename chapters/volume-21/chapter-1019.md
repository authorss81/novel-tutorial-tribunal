# Chapter 1019: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Seventh Week On Which A Woman Who Reads For A Living Came Up The Cart Road And Said That A Sentence Of A Man Of Thirty-One’s Had Been Said To Her In A Place He Was Not Standing In And Was Not Going To Say Where It Was Said And Was Not Going To Be Told What It Meant, And A Man With A Broom Gave Her The Answer She Had Promised To Give And Nobody Gave Her A Figure Of Anything

She came up the cart road at about the fourth hour with the book shut against her knee and she did not stop at the wall at the bottom of it, which she has not done before, and she went all the way to the foot of those steps before she opened her mouth, and what she said there was not what anybody on that landing expected to hear at the fourth hour of that morning.

Sixth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week. His seven hundred and sixty-ninth morning. Six hundred and nineteen days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and five weeks and one day old. Five hundred and seventy-three days since the division. Five hundred and nineteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and sixty-five days in this county of Kell.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that he had been asked nothing about the morning by anybody and that a man who is asked nothing gives three figures and goes back to work, and that this had been the best morning he had had all week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that a woman who reads for a living had come up his road with a thing in her mouth before he was properly awake and that he was not going to ask her what it was.

“Four at the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back, on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week, and it is a Sunday. Three figures.” He went along the top of the water and did not stop at the strip. “There is going to be a question asked of somebody on that step before the day is out and I am going to do the work of my morning and not hear it, and that is not a refusal of anything and it is not me being better than whoever is asked.”

He was not asked and he did the work of his morning.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that he had worked out that his question did not care where a thing had been said from, and that this had made the question larger instead of smaller.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and ninety-eighth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and nineteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and ninety-eighth. One thousand and nineteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“A man at the sixth hour said his question got bigger.” He set the barrow down. “Mine got smaller this week and I would like somebody to work out how that is possible, because I have not worked it out and I am not going to try on that step. Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And a man who cannot read does not have to be at the top of a thing to be in it, and I am not going to say any more than that and I am not going to say it twice.”

Nobody asked him to work it out and nobody said it twice.

Then the boy of thirteen came up out of the lane with two pails of water in a yoke and carried them to the shed side and set them down and came back, and stood at the foot of those steps below the woman who reads for a living, and did not go round her.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and sixty-six days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and sixty-six is one thousand less six hundred and thirty-four.

“Three hundred and sixty-six days, and it is one thousand less six hundred and thirty-four, and it is one more than the morning before.” He put the yoke down in the grass. “And I want to say the whole of it before somebody says anything else, because I have been down that lane since about the fourth hour and I did not hear it and I am not going to pretend I did.”

Nobody pretended he had.

“And I am not going to say it again either, and I want that noticed, because I have been thinking about that since the fourth hour and the reason I am not going to say it again is not that it is anybody’s. It is that a sentence said on a step is a sentence said on a step, and if I say it on a lane it stops being a thing that happened on a step and becomes a thing I carry, and I have got enough carrying.”

Nobody asked him to say it again.

“Now the part I actually want to say, and I have nobody to say it to except the step.” He looked up at her. “There is now a place where that sentence was said that is not this landing. I am not going to try to find out where it is and I am not going to go looking for it, because a boy who goes looking for where a thing was said is a boy who has put the thing in the middle of the county, and it does not need me. I would like it noticed that I have worked out that and that it is the whole of what I have worked out.”

Nobody worked anything else out.

Then the woman who reads for a living stood at the foot of those steps with the book shut against her knee and said the thing she had walked up the road to say, and it was not about where the shape came from, and nobody had asked her.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that a sentence a man of thirty-one had said out loud on that landing had been said to her in a place he was not standing in, and that she was not going to say where, and that she was not going to be told what it meant either.

Nobody asked her.

“I am going to say the plain shape of it and then I am going to stop.” She did not sit down and did not put the book down. “A man said a sentence about a piece of wood on your step at the ninth hour on the first day of this week. I was not on your landing when he said it and I have not been on your landing this week at all. And on the fourth day of that week a man said that same sentence to me in a room, in my own hearing, as a thing he had heard somebody say.”

Nobody said how he had come by it and nobody said who was in the room.

“I did not say it. I want that in the air first, before anything else. I did not say a word of it and nobody said it to me to give it to me and I did not ask for it and it did not come to me with anything attached to it, and I have been sitting on it since the fourth day of that week and it has not changed in my mouth once.”

Nobody said anything was attached to it.

“And here is what I am not going to do. I am not going to say where it was said. I said on the third day of this week that I would lie about that if I was asked, and I am not being asked, and I am still not going to say it, because a place where a sentence has been said is the only thing I have got and I am not going to hand it over to be useful to somebody.”

Nobody asked her.

“And I am not going to say what it means. I have worked out that I do not know what it means, and that is not modesty, it is that I was not there and I have not seen the wood and I do not know what the man said it about, and a woman who says she knows what a sentence means when she heard it in somebody else’s room is a woman who has decided to be the mouth of a thing she was not in.”

Nobody told her what it meant.

Then the man with the broom came off that flight at about the eighth hour with the broom across his shoulder and asked her the one question anybody on that landing was going to ask her, and did it in the plain voice of a man who has to know something about his own stone.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, and asked on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week where that sentence had been said to her, and got the answer he had been told to expect and had stopped minding.

“Where was it said to you.”

“I do not know.” She said it in the flat voice she uses on a page and did not take the book off her knee. “I have said for a week that if anybody asked me I would say I do not know, and I have said it in this voice, and this is the first time in twenty-three years that I have said a sentence to a person that I have not read off a page.”

He did not say that he knew she would say it and he did not say that he was disappointed, and he did not say it had been an honour, and he carried the broom back up the flight.

Nobody said he had handled it well and nobody said she had handled it well.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with four inches above his boots, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, asked for nothing, and stood at the second step from the bottom and looked up the bank at the rest of that landing instead of at the woman.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and sixty-five days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-four days old.

“Four hundred and sixty-five days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-four days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixty-ninth is three hundred and nineteen days old, which is one thousand and nineteen less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to ask for one because somebody has been up here this morning and said my own sentence out loud in her own mouth and I have not asked her anything about it.”

Nobody asked her anything about it.

“One hundred and fifty-ninth wrong things in one hundred and fifty-ninth mornings, and this is the hundred and fifty-ninth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody answered him on either half of it and nobody asked him which he wanted answered.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and sixty-five mornings, and I am going to keep saying both halves of that out loud on this landing.”

Nobody said either half for him.

“I am going to say the cost part and I am not going to put anything on it, and nobody is to put anything on it either, and nobody is to come up here afterwards and tell me that I should not have said the sentence on the first day of this week. A man who says a sentence out loud owns it from that moment, and he keeps owning it for as long as he is alive and it goes on being said in rooms he is not standing in after that. I said on that step that it is better for that wood to be called the wrong thing by me than the right thing by anybody, and I have found out what that sentence is worth this week, and it is worth nothing, and I said it anyway, and both halves of that are mine and I am not going to have either half improved.”

Nobody improved either half and nobody told him it had been worth the saying.

Then everybody went down that bank, and the woman who reads for a living went down the cart road with the book against her knee, and nobody went up the road behind her.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about ninety of them were under, and went along the top of the water on them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said one out loud either, and nobody asked the woman where it had been said, and nobody told her what the sentence meant, and nobody was given a figure of anybody who had heard anything.

A woman who reads for a living said that a sentence spoken on that step had been said to her in a room he was not standing in, and gave the answer she had promised to give when anybody asked her, and was not asked again. A boy of thirteen worked out that he would not go looking for where it had been said and said so out loud. A man of thirty-one found out what his own sentence is worth and did not put anything on it, and nobody was relieved, and the account on that landing was still in use, and the room where it had been said to her was not on that landing and was not going to be.
