# Chapter 1001: A Stone He Could Sweep

The broom went along the stone and stopped short, and then went on again, and the stopping was the whole of his morning.

The landing was forty feet across and there was a strip of it at the top end, dry, where the wall had taken the weather for three hundred years and the stone under it had gone the colour of a duck’s egg. Two lengths of wood lay on that strip. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end, and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, and one of the two run out of the side because the saw went off at the corner.

Corvin Tace swept up to the strip and stopped short of it and went along the front of the strip and came off the end of it and went round the back of it and came up to where he had started from, and the grit went with him and the wood stayed where it was. He did the same thing the whole length of the landing. He did not go across the strip and he did not put the broom near either piece of wood, and when the wind came off the water and moved the grit he waited for the wind to finish rather than shorten the distance.

Second day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fifty-first morning. Six hundred and one days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks and four days old. Five hundred and fifty-five days since the division. Five hundred and one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-seven days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said the thing he wanted, which he had not said before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he wanted a stone he could sweep, and had wanted one every morning of nineteen years, and had not said it out loud in nineteen years.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the second day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Wednesday. Three figures.”

He turned the broom over and beat the flat of it against the stone twice, which is how he keeps the bristles from going down in a wet week.

“And here is the other thing and I have never said it to anybody. In nineteen years I have wanted one thing off this landing and it is a stone with nothing on it. Not a good stone. An empty one. A stone I could walk a line down the middle of with the broom touching both edges, and never once have to think about where the broom is going.”

He looked along the strip.

“That is all it is. It is not about the wood and I am not asking anybody to move the wood and if the wood was not there this landing would be exactly as hard to sweep as it is now, and I know that, and I have never said it, and I am not going to pretend it is a large thing to say out loud.”

Nobody said it was a large thing and nobody said it was a small one.

Then the man of fifty-four came up the bank at about the sixth hour with the barrow and set it down and asked in the same words at the same hour, and nobody answered him, and nobody thanked him.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eightieth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eightieth. One thousand and one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and it has not stopped. I want that said on the stone because somebody is going to think this week that a thing started. It did not start. It has not stopped. There is a man on this bank at the sixth hour on the second morning of the hundred and sixty-fifth week doing what he did at the sixth hour on the second morning of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and he is going to do it at the sixth hour on the first morning of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and that is not an event, and I would like it entered nowhere.”

Nobody entered it anywhere.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.” He put both hands flat on the wall, which is what he does. “And I am going to say the rest of it out loud because I have worked something out and nobody has asked me. There is a name on that strip. I cannot read it and I am never going to read it, and it has not made my question one single bit easier or harder, and if it had, I would have had to give the question up, because a question that is easy when a name is on something is not my question.”

He picked up the barrow handles and did not start down the bank yet.

“I am not going to tell you how long a name has stood on a piece of wood. I am not going to tell you how many times a name has been said in this county. I am not going to tell you who has heard a name being said. Nobody has asked me for one of those three and if somebody does, they will get nothing off me, and I want it noticed that I have put all three of them in the air this morning without being asked for one of them, and that I am not being thanked for that either.”

Nobody noticed and nobody thanked him.

Nobody gave him a figure about anything, and nobody asked him again.

Then the boy of thirteen came up out of the lane with two pails of water in a yoke and carried them to the shed side and set them down and came back, and stopped at the strip, and said a thing that was about a cup.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and forty-eight days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and forty-eight is one thousand less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and forty-eight days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He put the yoke down in the grass. “And I want to say a thing while the two of you are on that stone and then I am going to go and sweep the joints, and I do not know where I got it and nobody is to tell me.”

Nobody said anything.

“That cup in the shed is not her cup. She carries it out and she washes it and she puts it down on the bench and it is not her cup, and it is not anybody’s cup either, and if you cut her name into the side of it in your own mouth it would still not be her cup.” He picked the yoke up again. “That is the whole of it. I have been thinking about it since about the fourth hour and it is not about this landing and I am not going to stand here and make it about this landing, because the moment somebody makes it about this landing it is a thing said about this landing and I would rather it were a thing I said about a cup.”

Nobody made it about the landing. Nobody told him he had it right and nobody told him he had it wrong.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures, and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and stood at the bottom of the steps and did not come up them.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-seven days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and sixteen days old.

“Four hundred and forty-seven days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and sixteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-first is three hundred and one days old, which is one thousand and one less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it this morning and I am not going to ask it on the morning after.”

He said all of that standing on the second step from the bottom with the water over his boot tops and did not look at the strip once.

“One hundred and forty-first wrong things in one hundred and forty-first mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-first, and it is a whole figure, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody answered him on either half of it, and nobody asked him which half he meant.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed since I said it out loud and I am not going to say it in a new shape because a new shape would sound as though the old one had gone stale.” He turned the hand over and shut it. “My cost is that asking costs me nothing. Four hundred and forty-seven mornings in this county and I have not asked anybody for one thing, and I want both halves of that said out loud, and I want neither half of it improved.”

Nobody comforted him and nobody told him to stop, and those are the two things that would have been the same thing.

“And I am going to say the last part and then I am going to go down that bank, and it is about a morning I am not going to have again this week, so nobody is to go up and look.” He looked at the water and not at the strip. “There was a morning four mornings back when that water came off that bank and every one of those ninety steps was walkable from the bottom to the top with nothing in the way. That was the morning a man could have walked up there and put a hand on that wood. I know that. I have known it since the water went off. And I was not one of the people who went up, and I am not going to be thanked for that, because not going up is not doing anything and anybody who stays behind can do it.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody went up.

“I am not going to say what a name on a thing is for this morning. I have had a whole night to work out whether I have got one and I have not got one. And I am not going to hold the not having one in front of the rest of you either, because a man who says out loud that he has nothing yet is asking a landing to feel something about it, and this landing has not done that for me once and I am not going to start asking it now.”

Nothing on that landing felt anything about it.

Then the boy swept the joints of about forty of those ninety steps with a bit of wood that was not a broom, and the man of fifty-four went down the bank with his barrow, and the man of thirty-one went down it after a while without speaking to anybody, and nobody went after him.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on the chair. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step. He did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not say who had put the second one there, and he said nothing about the first one either, and nobody on that landing asked him to.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Sixteen inches over that top step and it holds, and one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. The man with the broom wanted a stone he could sweep and did not get one and did not stop sweeping. A boy said a true thing about a cup and nobody connected it to anything. An offer stood at three hundred and one days, unanswered, and nobody was relieved, and the wood was on the strip where it had been, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour, and it was still in use.
