# Chapter 1011: Gaps That Were Not Water

The water came back in the night and stopped four inches deep over the stone, which is the shallowest it has ever come to, and it came so gently that nobody on that landing heard it arrive. What it did instead was find the grit that the broom had been driving to the low edge all week and hold it there, so that in the morning the whole top of that bank was a pale line and nothing in it moved.

Fifth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week. His seven hundred and sixty-first morning. Six hundred and eleven days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and four weeks old. Five hundred and sixty-five days since the division. Five hundred and eleven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-seven days in this county of Kell.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then gave three more absences, which were about nothing at all, and said that was the only reason he was going to give them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that the stone had handed him three figures and three gaps this morning and that he was not going to let the gaps pass for figures.

“Four at the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back, on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and it is a Saturday. Three figures.” He stood with the head of the broom off the strip. “Now the three gaps, and they are not about water, and I am not going to say them in the same words I said the last water gaps in because a man who says a gap twice in the same words has made it into a figure and there are enough of those on this landing.”

Nobody made anything into a figure.

“First gap. There is a man standing at that wall with a whole number of weeks in his mouth and no number of days beside it, and there will be no number of days beside it today, and I am not going to supply one, and I am not going to ask him for one.” He turned the broom over. “A whole figure is the most eager thing on this landing. It stands there with nothing beside it and it waits. You put a small thing next to it and people read the pair. And I have watched people read a pair all my life and I have never once been able to see the pair they were reading.”

Nobody read a pair.

“Second gap. There is no figure on this stone this morning of how many of us are standing on it, and I have given figures off this stone for nineteen years and that one has never been one of mine, and it is not going to become one this week because somebody wants it to.”

Nobody gave one.

“Third gap, and this is the one I would ask you to keep. There is no figure of how long anything has been lying on that strip, and I am not going to work one out, and I would like it noticed that I have had nineteen years to work one out and there is a whole morning in every week where I could have done it standing on this stone with nothing else to do.”

Nobody worked one out and nobody noticed.

Then the man of fifty-four came up the bank at about the sixth hour with the barrow and said a whole figure out loud with nothing beside it, and put his hands flat on the wall while he said it, and did not put a day to it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and ninetieth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and eleven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and ninetieth. One thousand and eleven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“One hundred and four weeks.” He did not take his hands off the wall. “Whole, and no days with it, and a man with a broom has just told this landing not to put a small thing next to it and I am going to say out loud that I have heard him, and I am going to say that hearing him is not the same as him owing me anything.”

Nobody said he did.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.” He set the barrow down. “And I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to do my work. A whole figure of weeks is not a gift and nobody is to thank me for it and nobody is to work out what it is doing there. I have got a wall under my hands and the wall is the whole of what I have got, and if a number comes out of a wall once without a day beside it then it comes out of that wall twice without a day beside it, and after a while a landing starts reading them as a calendar, and then it has got a calendar with one hand and no face, and I have seen a county go wrong on that and I am not going to be the man who starts it.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody said what the figure was doing there.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him, found the wall, stood against it, and said that he could hear which sort of morning this landing was having without being able to see any of it.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that he could tell a morning with something on it from a morning with nothing on it by the sound of the stone and that he was not going to tell anybody which sort this one was.

He kept his hand on the stone above the level of the strip and did not put it lower.

“I am not going to say which sort of morning this is. I have got a way of knowing and I have had it since I could not see a step, and there are mornings when I would give a good deal to not have it.” He took his hand off the wall. “And I want it understood that I have said nothing about what is on those mornings, and that what is on them is a thing I cannot check and cannot help with, and it is not going on my list, because the list is for things I have got to do about and there is nothing on this morning I have got to do about.”

Nobody asked him and nothing went on his list.

“And I am going to ask one question on this stone and then I am going down that bank and I want it asked in a plain voice, because I have got nobody to ask and I have thought about it all morning.” He turned his head towards the middle of the landing. “Has anybody laid a hand on that strip.”

Nobody answered him.

“Nobody. And that is what I expected and it is why I asked it at the tenth hour instead of asking it at the second hour of the morning on a day when nobody had any reason to be careful.” He took his hand off the wall and found it again further along. “I am not going to ask it again and I am not going to be told, and I would like the not-being-told noticed, because a man who gets told is a man who has been put in the position of the one who knows.”

Nobody told him.

“Nobody is to thank me for keeping my hand off that strip. Nobody asked me to put it on there and that is the whole of what I did, and a man who is thanked for a thing that did not happen has been thanked for stopping, and I have been thanked for stopping once already on this landing and I am not going to be thanked for it again this week.”

Nobody thanked him.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with four inches above his boots, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, asked for nothing, and then told the landing he was not going to finish a thing he had started.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-seven days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-six days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-seven days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-six days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixty-first is three hundred and eleven days old, which is one thousand and eleven less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it on the day after either.”

He came up to the second step from the bottom and stood there with the water over his boot tops and looked at the water and not at the strip.

“One hundred and fifty-first wrong things in one hundred and fifty-first mornings, and this is the hundred and fifty-first, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody answered him on the figure and nobody answered him on the want.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed since I said it out loud. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-seven mornings, and I am going to keep saying both halves of that until somebody stops me, and nobody is going to stop me.”

Nobody stopped him.

“And now the thing I came up here to say, and I have said it out loud on purpose, because a man who holds something back on a landing turns his own back into a thing that is being held back.” He put his hands behind him. “I have got a sentence half built. It has been half built in my mouth since before the light and I have not said a word of it out loud on this stone and I am not going to. Not on this morning. I have decided that I am going to let it arrive, and if it does not arrive then it was not a sentence, it was a thing I had wanted to say.”

Nobody asked him what the sentence was.

“I want that noticed that a man of thirty-one is standing on a step at the ninth hour with a mouth full of something he will not say, and that I am not asking anybody to help me with it and I am not asking anybody to guess it and I am not going to be relieved if nobody ever hears it. A man who says a thing out loud twice has said it twice, and the second one is the one that counts, and I am not going to be a man who stands on this landing and says the same sentence in two mouths. If I say it, I say it once, and I do not know what it is yet.”

Nobody guessed it and nobody helped him and nobody was relieved.

“A man who cannot see well asked this landing at the tenth hour whether anybody had laid a hand on that strip, and not one person on this stone opened their mouth, and I am not going to be the one who fills it, and I am not going to say whether it is a good thing or a bad thing that nobody answered him. I have worked out that a person who answers that question is a person who has been made into the record, and I am not going to be the record on a landing, and I have just spent a morning telling this landing I would not even build a sentence, so I am the last person here who should be handing anybody an answer.”

Nobody answered it.

Then everybody went down that bank, and the man of thirty-one went last and stood at the second step from the bottom with his hands behind him and did not go further up.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about ninety of them were under, and went along the top of the water on them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said one out loud either, and nobody answered the one question that was asked about that strip, and nobody answered it with a figure of how many mornings, or with a yes, or with a no, or with a question back.

Four inches over that top step and about ninety of those ninety steps under and seven days of the coming back, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A whole figure of weeks came out of a wall with no days beside it and was not thanked for it. A man who cannot see well said that he knows which sort of morning this landing was having and would not say which sort it was. A man of thirty-one told a landing he had a sentence in his mouth and was not going to say it, and nobody asked him for a word of it, and nobody was relieved, and the offer stood at three hundred and eleven days and it was not answered, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour, and it was still in use.
