# Chapter 1038: Which Of Them

The water stood at the top of that bank at its highest that Friday, sixteen inches over the top step, and it had stopped coming over the edge anywhere at all, so that the whole landing was one flat sheet with the fortieth step and the chair standing out of it and the dry strip at the top end standing higher than anything else, which is the shape that landing has a few times in every eight or so days and which everybody who works there knows and nobody can measure.

Fourth day of the hundred and seventieth week, and it is the Friday of that week. His seven hundred and eighty-eighth morning. Six hundred and thirty-eight days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and seven weeks and six days old. Five hundred and ninety-two days since the division. Five hundred and thirty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and eighty-four days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and four days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and gave them in the way he has given them every morning of that week from the bottom of the flight going up, and then did not give anything else for some time, and then gave the other thing.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and seventieth week that the stone had put sixteen inches on that top step and held it, and that this was the morning he was going to tell that landing something he had known for a week and had not put in the air.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and seventieth week, and it is a Friday. Three figures.”

He stood at the top of that flight with the water up to the middle of his thigh and the broom in his hand and not working.

“There is a man on this landing that name on that strip is for. I have not asked. I am going to say the sentence out loud and then I am going to say I am not going to ask, and I have thought about the order of those two all week and that is the order they go in.” He did not look down the bank. “I do not know which of you it is. I have stood on this stone every morning of nineteen years with my hand on a broom and I do not know, and that is not a figure of anybody and I am not going to give it as one. I do not know how old the naming is. I do not know how many mouths have had it. I do not know how many of you have heard it. Those are not things I have and they are not things I am going to get.”

“Now the working, and this is the whole of why I am not going to find out, and I have thought it through on that stone about a dozen times this week and I would like it written down that I have thought it through and not just decided it in a temper.” He put the broom head down in the water and left it there. “If I stand up here at the seventh hour and I ask that landing who that name is for, then every person on this stone who opens their mouth is in it. Not the ones who open their mouths. Everybody. The one who is for it, and the ones who were not. And the one who is for it cannot tell me, and cannot read me, and cannot check me, and if they say a word they are in it and if they do not say a word they are in it, and there is nothing a man can do with that. So no.”

“I have had a figure in my mouth all week and it is a figure of me and this landing and it is a true one, and I am not going to put it out this morning on top of this. Because the day I ask who that name is for and the day I stand up here and say out loud how long I have been holding this stone, those are two questions and a person is going to put them together and do the working out for me, and the working out will be about a person I have never spoken to. So the figure stays in my mouth and the question stays in my mouth and they stay in there separately.”

He took the broom up again and went back to work.

“And nobody is to thank me for saying any of that, and nobody is to tell me I was right to say it, and I would rather the whole of this morning went down as a man with a broom telling three figures and one fact and going back to work.” He worked the top step and it did not make a sound. “That is all of it. There is a person on this landing and I have not asked and I am not going to.”

Then the woman who reads for a living came up that cart road at about the fourth hour and stood at the foot of the steps with the book in her hands instead of shut against her knee, and she did not sit down, and she did not open it.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and seventieth week that she had come up that road to do nothing at all and to say so in advance, and that the reason she could do it was that a man with a broom had three figures off that stone and no more, and that a morning with three figures in it was the safest morning in the county for a woman with a voice like hers.

“I have brought the book up the road and I am going to take it down again and I am not going to open it, and I would like that written down as the whole of what I came for.” She held it out in front of her and did not open it. “I did this once before and I stopped myself at the wall, and I have stopped myself twice since, and this is the third time I have come up here with the intention of putting one sentence into a quiet morning and it is not a quiet morning, there is a man on that stone with his voice about to go, and I am not going to do it.”

“And I am not going to be thanked for coming up a road to do nothing. I am aware that not doing a thing and being thanked for it is a way of being thanked for things. I am not going to have it. I am going to stand here, and I am going to hear what that landing says this morning, and I am going to go down that road again, and if what it says is worth writing down then it is worth somebody standing here with a pencil, which is not me.” Nobody thanked her and nobody wrote anything down.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and came up sixty steps of standing water with the barrow high, and put his hands flat on the wall and left them there through everything that was said after that.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and seventeenth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and thirty-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and seventeenth. One thousand and thirty-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He did not take his hands off the wall while he said it and he did not take them off afterwards, and everybody on that landing understood what he was about before he had got to it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.”

And then he said the other thing, and he said it to the wall, because he has been looking at that wall every morning of this week.

“I am not going to ask. Not this morning and not in a week and not when I am on my back and not at the end of it if it goes on as long as I do. I have been wanting to ask since the middle of this week and I have wanted it every morning since, and this morning I am saying out loud that I have worked out I am not going to do it, and I am not doing it because I cannot read and I cannot check anything anybody would tell me.”

“Here is the whole of it and it is not very deep. If I ask this landing who that name on that strip is for, then I will be a man who cannot read, asking to be told what a thing is for. And there is not one of those answers I can do anything with. Either they say a name and it is a name and I cannot hold it and I cannot check it and I cannot ever find out whether it was true. Or they say a sentence about it and it is longer than a name and it goes into me and stays in me and I will be carrying it for the rest of my life with no way of laying it down. Both of those are a thing being handed to a man who cannot check what he has been handed.”

He took one hand off the wall and used it to count on the fingers of the other. “I have been handed things before. Everybody on this landing hands things to each other all week. And every one of them is a thing I have to hold and cannot lay down. I am not asking for another one and I would like it noticed that the not asking is not because I do not want it.”

“And I am going to say a thing out loud while I have my hands on this wall and it is about my own trade and it is what I have been turning over since Tuesday.” He put his hands flat again. “A thing a man asks a day against is not that man’s day. I have been asking a day against a line for a long time and the day is not mine and the line is not mine and the asking is not mine either. It is a thing I have been doing, and it does not belong to me, and I would still be doing it if nobody ever gave me the day. And I am not naming this. I am not saying it is a rule and I am not saying it is right. I am saying it once, out loud, at the wall, because a man with a broom has stood up here this morning and said out loud that he does not know which of us that name is for, and he is right not to ask, and so am I, and neither of us is to be thanked and neither of us is to be told we have got it right.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had it right and nobody told him he had it wrong.

Then the man who cannot see well came up that flight at about the tenth hour and went along the top of the water on it with his hand out in front of him on the wall and got to the top and asked his question, and nothing came of it, and he said so out loud, and then said the other thing.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, and has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint. On the fourth day of the hundred and seventieth week a man with a broom said out loud a thing about a person on that landing, and Rook understood every word of it, and he was not going to be the man who helped with it.

His palm stayed on the wall and did not travel.

“Has anybody on this landing put that name into anybody else’s ear.” Nothing came back, and it came back fast, and he did not add it to the list. “I am not going to say a second time what I am not going to do about it. I have got a list and nothing on the list this week and there is nothing going on it this morning. I am not going to put a man’s voice on that list and I am not going to put a man’s silence on it either, because a silence is not a thing I have checked.”

“And I am going to say one thing about a man with a broom and then I am done.” He took his hand off the wall and put it in his coat. “He has told us there is a person on this landing and that he does not know which. That is the first true thing said on that stone this week that was not said by me, and I have got nothing to put against it, and I would rather be a man with an empty list than a man with one line on it that says a man with a broom admitted he did not know.”

Then Ilyan came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches of water standing round his boots and the middle of his shins, and went round the two pieces of ash, and gave his figures, and gave the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and said nothing at all about the fact that a man with a broom had said a person was on that landing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and eighty-four days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and fifty-three days old.

“Four hundred and eighty-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and fifty-three days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-eighty-eighth is three hundred and thirty-eight days old, which is one thousand and thirty-eight less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one, and I will not ask for one on the morning a man with a broom has told us all that there is a person on this stone and he will not say which, and a man who cannot read has told us all that he is never going to ask.”

He stood on the top step with the water up to his knee and did not step over onto the dry strip.

“One hundred and seventy-eighth wrong things in one hundred and seventy-eighth mornings, and this is the hundred and seventy-eighth, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to do arithmetic at me about it. My want is to be somebody and it has not changed and it will not be said in a new shape, because that landing has had true things said about names on it this morning.”

“And here is where I am this morning and I am going to say it plainly and I am not going to improve it.” He held his hand up out of the water and looked at the cut in it. “A man with a broom has said there is a person on this landing and does not know which, and that man has a figure in his mouth and will not put it out, and a man who cannot read has a whole figure in his mouth and has just said he is never going to ask. And I am a man of thirty-one with four figures in his mouth who has been standing on this step every morning of this week saying the same four and asking for nothing.” He shut his hand.

“I have worked out what I want out of that. I want both of them to keep their figures, and I am not going to say whose I want it more for. Not out of kindness. Because the day a figure comes out of a mouth on this stone and somebody else does the working out, that is the day a name on a piece of ash on this landing gets somebody after it, and everybody on this stone will have been in the doing of it, including me, and I have got no version of myself that I want to be in that.”

He put his hand down and looked at the standing water and at the strip and did not touch anything.

“That is the whole of me this morning. I am not going to say anything else and nobody is to answer any part of it.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour and nobody went up that cart road and Ilyan went last and stood on the second step from the bottom for a moment and went on down.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and he went at the whole flight from the bottom upward with the water at the top of his thigh, which is as deep as he works it. The fortieth one went round rather than under his boot, as it has gone round every morning of nineteen years, and the chair went round as well and nobody has ever seen him sit on it. He swept the iron in that seam with the head of the broom and did not once put a finger on it, and he kept well off the dry strip, and both pieces of ash on it stayed lying where they had lain through two days of water coming over that landing. Neither piece of ash had been touched, and under six inches of moving water they were still a forearm long each, both split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to and both with the thick end left as it came apart. The second had its saw mark on the square end and its two shallow notches in the flat, and one of the two run out of the side.

At sixteen inches there is nothing to sweep on a flight, only the wet stone under about half a foot of moving water, and he worked every one of them anyway, and at the top of it he stopped with the water at his thigh and the top step out, and he said nothing to the strip and he did not look at it, and he stood there with the broom across his shoulder with his back to it for a while and then went down.

Nobody turned anything over on that landing, or weighed it in the hand, or read it out loud, or carried it inside, or put it back where it had been, or used it, then or afterwards, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it, including after those sentences. Nothing there was named that morning and no name was said out loud on it, and nothing was said out loud about what that wood is, and nothing standing on that landing was counted.

That morning came and went, and the standing offer stood at three hundred and thirty-eight days and was not asked on and was not answered on and was not withdrawn on, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour and it was still in use, and the name on that second piece of ash was still wrong.

And the man of fifty-four took his hands off that wall one at a time when the others had gone, the left one first and then the right one a good deal slower, holding on to the stone with his fingertips until the last of it came off, and then he turned the barrow round in the wet with both hands on the shafts and walked it down about sixty steps of standing water and up out onto the flat of the landing and down that bank, and neither hand went near the strip at any point of it, and he did not look back at it, and that was the first morning this week he had not said his own question twice.
