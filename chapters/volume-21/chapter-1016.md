# Chapter 1016: Another Mouth

The name went out over that landing at about the eighth hour in a man’s voice that had not used it before, and it took the landing a moment to notice that the mouth it came out of was not the mouth it had come out of before, and by the time it had noticed, the sentence around the name had already been said.

Third day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week. His seven hundred and sixty-sixth morning. Six hundred and sixteen days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and four weeks and five days old. Five hundred and seventy days since the division. Five hundred and sixteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and sixty-two days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that he had heard a thing he had not been expecting at the eighth hour and was going to say nothing about it until the morning after, and that a man with a broom was not the man anybody should wait on for an opinion about anything.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that he was not going to be the man who came up that bank the morning after somebody had used a name out loud on it, because everybody would be waiting on him and he had nothing.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the third day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week, and it is a Thursday. Three figures.” He went along the top of the water and did not stop at the strip. “I heard a name said on that landing this morning and I am not going to have an opinion about it and I am not going to work out what it means, and if anybody on that bank comes up here tomorrow morning and asks me what I thought of a thing I heard, I am going to tell them that I was sweeping.”

Nobody asked him and nobody waited on him.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that a name is a sound and that a sound said twice is not the same thing said once, and that he could hear the difference and could not read either of them.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and ninety-fifth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and sixteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and ninety-fifth. One thousand and sixteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“I heard a name go this morning and it was not the voice it was in before.” He put his hands flat on the wall. “A name is a sound. A sound is the easiest thing in this world for two mouths to make and the hardest thing for a man to be certain about, and I cannot read, so what I have got is that I heard it twice and I have no way of setting the one against the other and finding out whether they are the same word. That is not a complaint. That is what not being able to read is, on a morning like this.”

Nobody said it was the same word and nobody said it was a different word.

“And I am not going to be the man who says it a third time. If it comes up that bank again I am going to stand where I am with my hands on this wall and I am going to let it go past me, because the third time a name goes out of a mouth on a landing it stops being a thing somebody said and becomes a thing the landing says.”

He picked the barrow handles up.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And that question has got nothing to do with any of that and I want it noticed that it has not, and that nobody is to go looking round that stone for a connection to my question, because there is not one and if there is then it is not mine to find.”

Nobody looked for one.

Then the woman who saws lengths came up that cart road at about the eighth hour, and the man of thirty-eight was at the foot of the steps, and the first voice on that road was hers and not his.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, with a child of nine, thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board in the inside of his coat, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that a name said out loud on a landing is a thing anybody can stand next to, and that he had come up the road to be the man standing next to it.

He had his hand flat over the notches through the cloth and he was not looking at the strip.

“A barrow haft.” He said it in the plain voice of a man reading out a thing he had heard and knew. “That is what is on that strip and I have heard it said and I am saying it, and I am going to keep saying it, because a name on this landing has been in one mouth since the morning it was put there and a name in one mouth is a thing that belongs to that mouth, and I have come up this road to put myself in the same sentence as it.”

Nobody said he was wrong and nobody said he was right.

“Not because I put it there. I have not been anywhere near that strip and I am not going to be.” He took his hand off the board. “Because a man who says a name out loud is a man who has taken something up. That is what saying it out loud is. And nobody on this landing has taken anything up, and I have watched a week of this landing not taking things up, and I am the only man up here who has spent a fortnight doing nothing else.”

Nobody took anything up.

“And nobody is to ask me whether that is a good reason, because I have asked myself that on the road all the way here and I have not got an answer and I am not putting it down without one. I am not going to hand a thing to a landing that I cannot stand behind myself, and I am not going to be thanked for standing on a step with a name in my mouth, because it is not mine and I have never once pretended it was.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody told him to go and stand somewhere else.

“You do not know what that wood is.”

It was the woman, at the foot of the steps, with a length under her arm, and she said it to him and not to the landing.

Wenna Pyle is thirty-eight, who saws lengths and sets studs at a bench in an open-sided shed behind a wheelwright’s about three hundred yards down that cart road, and who carries her own cup out to that bench because there is no table in the shed, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that she was not going to be used by anybody that morning, and gave the reason out of her own trade, and did not say what that wood was.

“I have said one thing about a piece of wood in my own mouth and I have watched what it did for a week and I am not going to be the second mouth on anything.” She did not put the length down. “You have come up a road in order to stand next to a name, and you want a woman who cuts wood to tell you the wood is not the thing you want it to be, and if I said one word about what that is, then whatever you went up that road to do would be half done. I am not going to be half of a thing. That is the whole refusal and there is nothing else in it.”

He did not say she was wrong and he did not say she was right.

“You want the working out of my trade and I will give it to you because it is mine and it is about a hole and not about a strip. I set a stud in a hole. That is what I am for.” She looked at him. “A man brings me a thing and tells me the hole it was made for is not the hole. I do not make him another thing. I do not tell him the hole is the hole he had in mind. I make the thing to the hole that is in front of me, because that is the only hole I can get to, and if the hole is wrong then the thing is wrong and that is between him and the hole and not between him and me.”

Nobody asked her a question about the hole.

“And I am not going to say whether what is on that strip is the thing I would have made for that hole or not, and nobody is going to get it out of me this morning by asking me straight, and nobody is going to get it out of me by being clever about it either, and I have said out loud that I am not saying and I am going to hold to it in the same way I hold to a length.”

Nobody got anything out of her.

“And here is the last of it and it is about me and not about you. You have come up here to put yourself in the same sentence as a name, and I have told you no, and I am not going to tell you that no was the right no. You are not the man who put that wood down. I am the woman who cuts lengths and I have not put that wood down, and between the two of us there is nothing on this landing that either of us can be the man who said a word about.”

He did not say she was wrong and he did not say she was right and nobody told either of them.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, asked for nothing, and was standing at the second step from the bottom before the man of thirty-eight had finished coming down the bank.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and sixty-two days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-one days old.

“Four hundred and sixty-two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixty-sixth is three hundred and sixteen days old, which is one thousand and sixteen less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to ask for one this week.”

He did not go up any further than the second step.

“One hundred and fifty-sixth wrong things in one hundred and fifty-sixth mornings, and this is the hundred and fifty-sixth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody answered him on either half of that either.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and sixty-two mornings, and I am going to keep saying both halves of that out loud on this landing.”

Nobody said either half for him.

“And I am going to say one thing about a name that was said on that landing this morning in a voice that is not mine, and then I am going to stop and I am not going to say anything else about it for the rest of this week.” He looked at the man of thirty-eight. “A name said out loud by the man who put a thing down is a thing that man said. A name said out loud by anybody else is not that, and it is not more than that, and it does not go back into the first mouth by being said in a second one, and I am not going to stand here and explain which of those two it is, because I have already explained it once this week and I am not going to be the second mouth to it.”

Nobody asked him which of those two it was.

He did not answer, and he did not answer the man of thirty-eight when that man turned round at the bottom of the bank and put a question to him about it, and he did not answer it after that either, and nobody on that landing filled the silence in his place.

Nobody answered him and nobody asked him a second time and nobody told him he had said it well.

Then everybody went down that bank, and the man of thirty-eight went down it first and did not stop at the wall at the bottom of the road, and nobody went up the road behind anybody.

He swept the whole of that flight, all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went along the top of the water on them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Nobody put a new name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said a new name out loud either, and nobody answered a man of thirty-eight when he asked a question at the bottom of that bank, and nobody told a woman who saws lengths what that wood was.

A man of thirty-eight said a name out loud and put himself in the same sentence as it, and a woman who saws lengths told him she would not be used and gave the working out of a stud in a hole, and he was not told he was wrong and he was not told he was right, and she was not told she was wrong and she was not told she was right. A man who cannot read heard a name go out of a second mouth and said he was not going to be the third. By the end of that morning the name on that strip had been in two mouths and would have been in more, and nobody put anything on it and nobody took anything off it, and the offer stood at three hundred and sixteen days unanswered and nobody was relieved, and the account on that landing was still in use.
