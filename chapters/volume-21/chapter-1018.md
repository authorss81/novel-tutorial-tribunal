# Chapter 1018: A Saturday On Which The Water Came Off That Bank In The Night And There Was No Figure Of Inches And No Figure Of How Much Of That Flight Was Standing In It And No Figure Of How Long Before It Came Back, And A Man With A Broom Said Three Gaps In A Third Set Of Words And Worked Without Anything To Work Towards, And A Whole Figure Of Weeks Came Out Of A Wall With Nothing Beside It And A Man Who Cannot See Well Came Up And Did Not Go Past The Fortieth Step

The water left that bank in the night and took the sound of the landing with it, so that by first light the whole of that flight stood dry and empty from the bottom to the top, and the landing had never looked so much like a landing, and not one person came up it for hours.

Fifth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week. His seven hundred and sixty-eighth morning. Six hundred and eighteen days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and five weeks old. Five hundred and seventy-two days since the division. Five hundred and eighteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and sixty-four days in this county of Kell.

There was no figure of inches on that stone that morning, and no figure of how much of that flight was standing in it, and no figure of how long before it came back. The man of forty-three said so at the seventh hour, out loud, in a third set of words, and then swept a landing that gave him nothing to work towards for the first time in nineteen years.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that there were three gaps in the morning and that he was saying them a third time in words he had not used, because the second time is not the same thing as the first time and the third time is not the same thing as the second.

“The stone has told me nothing this morning about itself.” He turned the broom head down on the top step. “There is no depth in my mouth at all, and there is no figure of how much of that flight is standing in it, and there is no morning coming back on this landing that I am working towards. That is on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week, and it is a Saturday.”

Nobody worked out a figure for him.

“I have said three gaps before and I said them in other words both times and I am not going to pretend that saying them a third time makes them a smaller thing. They are the same size. And I want it noticed that the third one is the one I mind, and that a man with a broom can sweep for nineteen years because there is always a morning coming that he is sweeping towards, and this morning there is not one, and he has got to stand here at the seventh hour with a broom in his hands and no morning to put it in.”

Nobody supplied him with a morning.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said a whole figure out loud with nothing beside it, and put it down the same way he put the barrow down.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and ninety-seventh mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and eighteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and ninety-seventh. One thousand and eighteen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“One hundred and five weeks.” He did not take his hands off the wall. “Whole, and no days with it, and there is no figure of anything else in this morning for me to put beside it either, and I am going to leave it standing there on its own for a whole day and see what the landing does with a whole figure and nothing at all, because for a fortnight now every morning has had something on it and I have watched the whole thing fill up like a ditch.”

Nobody put anything beside it and nobody said what the landing did with it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And that is it, and I am going down that bank now and putting gravel where gravel goes, and I am not going to come back up it today unless the gravel wants coming back up.”

Nobody thanked him.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto that flight at about the tenth hour, walked up all ninety of the steps because all ninety of them were out and none of them were wet, and stopped at the fortieth one, and did not go past it.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that he had kept a notice he gave the day before and that keeping it had turned out to be the dullest thing a man can do, and that he had come up a bank with nothing in the way and stopped at the fortieth step anyway.

He stood on the fortieth step with his hand on the wall.

“I said yesterday, out loud, that there was one morning this week where I could get to the end of that strip and nobody would know, and that I was not going to, and I have kept it, and I have walked up this bank this morning on ninety dry steps past a place where I could have put my hand on that wood without anybody seeing me do it, and I stopped here.” He took his hand off the wall and put it back on it. “And I am not going to make anything of that. I have not got a speech about it. I have kept a notice and here I am standing on a step in the middle of a flight and there is nothing whatever to report.”

Nobody reported it and nobody thanked him.

“The thing at the end of that strip is still on my list and it has not moved on it and I have not crossed it off, and this morning it was further away from me than it has been at any hour of that week, and that made no difference at all, which is the whole of what my list has been telling me since before I could see well and this is the first morning it has said it out loud where I could hear it.”

Nobody told him what his list was for.

He had his hand out along the stone and he did not put it on the wood and he said nothing at all, and the man at the sixth hour asked him the question out loud in front of everybody who was standing there.

“Has anything on that strip been moved.”

Nobody answered him, and the man standing on the fortieth step was the one most able to and said nothing, and nobody thanked him for saying nothing and nobody told him it was the right thing.

“That is the answer, and it is the answer I get at the sixth hour every morning, and I have got used to it.” He did not take his hands off the wall. “And I am going to say the other half and then I am going down that bank. A man walked up ninety dry steps this morning and stopped on the fortieth one and did not come past it, and I do not know what to do with that, and I am a man who cannot read and I am not going to work it out on this landing, and nobody is to work it out for me either.”

Nobody worked it out.

Then the man of thirty-one came up the cart road to the bottom of that bank at about the ninth hour, and stopped at the water side of it, and did not go up a single one of the ninety steps, and gave his figures from down there to nobody.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and sixty-four days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-three days old.

“Four hundred and sixty-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-three days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixty-eighth is three hundred and eighteen days old, which is one thousand and eighteen less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to.”

He said all of that standing on the flat at the bottom of the bank with the whole flight in front of him and his back to the water.

“I am saying this at the bottom and not at the top because I told this landing yesterday at the ninth hour that I was not coming up it, and I am not coming up it, and a man who keeps a notice does not come half way up a bank to be seen keeping it. Nobody up there has heard a word of this and that is exactly how much of this morning is going to be worth.” He turned the hand over and shut it. “One hundred and fifty-eighth wrong things in one hundred and fifty-eighth mornings, and this is the hundred and fifty-eighth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody told him and nobody up there heard him.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and sixty-four mornings, and I am saying both halves of that from the bottom of a bank because that is where I am, and I am not going to come up and say them again.”

Nobody said either half for him.

“And I am going to say one thing and then I am going to go down the cart road, and it is about this morning and it is not about anything that was said on that landing earlier in the week.” He looked up at ninety dry steps with a broom on them. “That water is off that bank and every one of those steps is out and there is nothing in the way of that strip this morning except the men who are on it and what they have decided not to do. I gave a notice yesterday and I am keeping it. Nobody is to come to the bottom of this bank and ask me whether I meant it.”

Nobody came to the bottom of the bank and nobody asked him whether he had meant it.

Then everybody who was on that landing went down it, and the man who cannot see well went down it on the same ninety dry steps he had come up on, and the man of thirty-one went down the cart road and did not go up the bank at all, and the man of fifty-four worked his barrow, and the man with the broom swept a landing with no morning in it.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps and none of them were under, and he did the whole flight in a different order, from the bottom up instead of the top down, and he went round the fortieth one on the way and did not stop on it, and he went round the chair, and he did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said one out loud either, and nobody on that landing said a sentence out loud, and nobody asked anybody anything about the wood or about the name or about anything that had been said on that landing earlier in that week, and the man who had put a name on it did not come up that bank.

There was no figure of water on that landing that morning and no figure of how much of that flight was standing in it and no figure of how long before it came back, and a whole figure of weeks stood in a man’s mouth with nothing beside it, and a man with a broom swept with nothing to work towards, and the morning came out of that bank with nothing put on it. The offer stood at three hundred and eighteen days, unanswered, and nobody was relieved, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour, and it was still in use.
