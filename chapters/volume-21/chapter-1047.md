# Chapter 1047: After The Asking

The light came up thin that morning with the water still high and holding, and the sheet over the top step lay so still that the fortieth step and the chair stood doubled in it, and the man with the broom worked without breaking the doubling until he had to.

Sixth day of the hundred and seventy-first week, and it is the Sunday of that week. His seven hundred and ninety-seventh morning. Six hundred and forty-seven days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and nine weeks and one day old. Six hundred and one days since the division. Five hundred and forty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and ninety-three days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures with his boots in the sheet, and gave them looking down at the doubling.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and seventy-first week that the bank would hold a day longer and then ease, and that he was going to sweep the edge while it held.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and seventy-first week, and it is the Sunday of that week. Three figures.”

He broke the doubling with the broom head and did not remark on it.

“Go and do your gravel. It is the Sunday of the hundred and seventy-first week and the sheet is still, and the stone has nothing else for anybody.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow, and asked in the same words at the same hour, and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and put his hands flat on the wall with the water at his ankles.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and twenty-sixth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and forty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and twenty-sixth. One thousand and forty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He did not hurry past the question the way he sometimes hurries past it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.”

It met with silence, and he gave that silence its own sentence, and nobody filled it.

“It has met with silence and I am leaving it there. I refused a man to his face on the Saturday of this week and I am not unsaying that this morning by asking softer.”

Then Ilyan came up at about the ninth hour with the sheet round his shins, and went round the dry strip without going near it, and gave his figures, and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing at first.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and ninety-three days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and sixty-two days old.

“Four hundred and ninety-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and sixty-two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-ninety-seventh is three hundred and forty-seven days old, which is one thousand and forty-seven less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and it has not been answered on and not been withdrawn on. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one.”

He stood below the top with his hands out of his pockets.

“One hundred and eighty-seventh wrong things in one hundred and eighty-seventh mornings, and this is the hundred and eighty-seventh, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is. My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is no longer that asking costs me nothing, because I spent that on the Saturday of this week, and what I have now is the mornings after, and I do not know what they cost yet.”

He looked at the man with his hands on the wall and took a step closer and stopped.

“I want to explain myself.” He said it to the wall and not past it. “I want to tell you why I put it to you, and that I had carried it all week, and that I meant it kindly, and I want us to be easier after it.”

Barnaby Crove kept his hands on the wall and did not turn.

“No.” He said it without raising his voice. “And here is the reason, because you asked me and not the landing. If you explain it, then I have to hold your explaining along with your asking, and I have already got your asking in me and it is heavy enough. An explaining is another thing to carry with no way of setting it down, and I cannot check it, and I will be turning it over on mornings when I should be pushing gravel. Leave it where it is. It is done and I refused you, and nobody told me I was wrong and nobody told me I was right, and that is the shape I want to keep.”

Ilyan stepped back and put his hands at his sides, and his face went through something and came out plainer.

“I was wrong to ask and I am wrong to want to explain the asking.” He said it low, with the water round his shins. “That is mine and I am not asking you to lift it. I wanted to be somebody who could make it easier after, and that wanting is the mistake on top of the mistake.”

Nobody answered any question about that name that morning, and the explaining was refused with a working, and nobody thanked anybody for it.

Then the man who cannot see well came up at about the tenth hour with his hand on the wall, and asked his question, and got nothing, and said so in his own words.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and seventy-first week that his list was empty and he liked it empty on a still morning.

“Has that barrow question been spoken of since the Saturday of this week.” Nothing returned, and he nodded. “Nothing has returned and I am not putting that silence anywhere.”

After that the boy climbed from that lane at about the tenth hour with pails in hand, said his own figure, and would not bend it toward anything else.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and counts his own days in this county, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and seventy-first week that his count is three hundred and ninety-four days, and that it is his and he will not make it into a count of anything standing on that landing.

“Three hundred and ninety-four. That is mine.” He set the pails down clear of the sheet. “Has the man with the broom said anything besides his three.” Nothing came of that, and he picked the pails up again. “Then I will do my carrying and leave him to his.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and the man of fifty-four went down with the barrow through the still sheet, and Ilyan went last and stood on the second step from the bottom for a moment and went on down.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went at the whole flight from the bottom upward with the sheet unbroken ahead of him. The fortieth one went round rather than under his boot, and the chair went round the same way, and the iron in that seam he swept over with the broom head and never touched with his fingers. The dry strip at the top end he swept up to the edge of and stopped, and what was lying on it lay there at the seventh hour and lay there at the eleventh. The two lengths were still on the dry strip above the still water, a forearm long each and split down the middle along the grain, thick end rough where it parted, and on the second a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches in the flat with one run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was tugged or capsized or cradled to guess or called out or carried in or restored or harnessed, then or afterwards, and no hand went on that wood to take anything off it. Nothing standing there was named that morning, and what that wood is was not said again, and nothing standing there was counted.

That morning drew on and went, and the account on that landing held unfinished as at six and still held in use, and the name on that second length held wrong, and nobody on the steps named any of it finished.

And Barnaby Crove washed his hands in the sheet at the foot of the steps before he took the barrow down the cart road, working the grit out of the cracks with his thumbs, and dried them on his coat, and took up the shafts again with clean hands.
