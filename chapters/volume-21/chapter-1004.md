# Chapter 1004: The Shape He Would Not Name

The fever came out whole on that morning and the man who cannot read had it in his mouth before the light was properly on the wall, and there was no figure of days with it.

Fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fifty-fourth morning. Six hundred and four days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks old. Five hundred and fifty-eight days since the division. Five hundred and four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty days in this county of Kell.

Eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and then gave the rest of it, and what he gave was not a figure.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he had been given a shape of words that week which was more use to him than anything anybody had said on that landing in nineteen years, and that he was not going to say where he had got it and did not much care.

“Eight at the seventh hour, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back, on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Saturday. Three figures.” He leaned the broom against the wall, clear of the strip. “And now the other thing, and I want it understood that this is a compliment to nobody, because I do not know who to pay. There is a shape going round this county this week in more mouths than one and nobody on this landing has written it down and nobody has given it a name, and it has made my morning easier, and I am going to say how it has made my morning easier, and then I am going to stop.”

He put his thumb on the wall.

“It is this. A thing that is named for a person is not that person’s thing. It sounds like nothing. It has got no figures in it and it has not got a use in it either, and it is the reason I have been able to sweep round that strip every morning this week without once standing here working out in my own head whose anything it is.”

Nobody worked anything out in his own head.

“Nineteen years I have known whose wood is on that strip and whose iron is in the fortieth step and I have never needed to be told about either of them. The moment a thing on a landing gets a person in it, it stops being stone and becomes a matter, and a matter on a landing is a thing you have to have an opinion about, and I have never once in nineteen years had an opinion about a piece of wood and I am not starting this week.”

Nobody had an opinion about it.

“And I have been asked since Tuesday where it came from by nobody, and I am not going to tell them, and I do not know. If a shape turns up in more than one mouth in one week without anybody saying where it came from then it was not invented on this landing, and I have got a landing that has been here nineteen years and I have never once seen anybody invent a thing on it.”

Nobody said they had invented it and nobody said they had not.

“And I am going to say the other half of it out loud now so that it is done. Whatever that shape is, it is not a gift, and it is not offered, and it is not given, and it is not left, and it is not provided, and it is not kept, and it is not meant. None of those words are going to be used on that strip by me, and if any of them goes round this county about that wood I would like somebody to come and tell me, because I have not got the strength left to say it again.”

Nobody came and told him and nobody used any of those words about it.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and said a whole figure out loud before anybody on that landing had finished its morning, and nobody worked it out for him, and nobody asked him what it was for.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-third mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-third. One thousand and four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“One hundred and three weeks.” He put both hands flat on the wall, which is what he does. “There is no figure of days with it this morning and I am not going to supply one and nobody is to supply one for me, and if you ask me what a whole figure of weeks is for I am going to tell you that I do not know, and that will be the true answer, and it will also be the answer that stops the next person asking.”

Nobody worked it out for him. Nobody named a figure for it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say the part I have not said before and I have had a whole week to find it.” He did not take his hands off the wall. “There is a name on that strip. I do not know what it is because I cannot read it, and I have stopped asking, and I want it understood that I have stopped on my own and not because anybody in this county told me to stop. And I am not going to say anything about that name, not one word, good or bad, and I am not going to be asked, and if I am asked I am going to put my hands on this wall and say nothing at all.”

Nobody asked him and nobody said anything at all on the landing when he said that, and nobody thanked him for not saying anything.

He went down the bank with the barrow and put gravel where it was going and did not say a figure of what he had left or what he had done it in.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with eight inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stood on the top step on a Saturday with a whole figure of weeks standing in the air behind him that nobody was going to put a meaning to.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and nineteen days old.

“Four hundred and fifty days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and nineteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-fourth is three hundred and four days old, which is one thousand and four less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it this morning and I am not going to ask it this week.”

He said it without stopping on it and then he looked round the landing, at nothing in particular, for a while.

“One hundred and forty-fourth wrong things in one hundred and forty-fourth mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-fourth. It is a whole figure and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is, and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything, and I have noticed that a man at the sixth hour has just said a whole figure out loud with nothing beside it and that nobody asked him what it was for.”

Nobody asked him what the hundred and forty-fourth was for either.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty mornings, and I am neither relieved nor unrelieved about it and I would like that written down in the hand of somebody who is not me.”

Nobody wrote it down in any hand.

“And the last thing. A man with a broom has said out loud that he has found a shape of words that makes his morning easier and that he does not know where it came from, and he has said that it is not a gift and it is not offered and it is not given and it is not left and it is not provided and it is not kept and it is not meant. I am going to agree with him about all seven of those and I am going to say so out loud, and I am going to say why, and the why is not about him.”

Nobody stopped him and nobody agreed with him out loud.

“It is because a man who can say those seven words about a thing on a landing is a man who has decided what the thing is, and I have not decided what that wood is, and if I say yes to him I am agreeing that I have decided, and I have not.”

Then the woman who saws lengths went up the cart road to the shed and carried her cup out to the bench and said nothing to anybody about anything, and the man of fifty-four worked his barrow, and the man of thirty-one went down the bank at about the tenth hour and nobody went with him.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about eighty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip, and nobody put a name on anything anywhere on that landing that morning and nobody said one out loud either.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Eight inches on the top step, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A whole figure of weeks stood in the air on that landing with nothing beside it and nobody asked what it was for. A man with a broom said he had found a shape of words that made his morning easier and said where it had come from was nothing to do with anybody, and it was not given a name, and it was not called anything, and a man of thirty-one agreed with seven words and gave his reason for agreeing with them.
