# Chapter 1005: A Sunday And Twelve Inches And About Seventy Of Those Ninety Steps Under And Five Days Of The Coming Back, And A Woman Who Reads For A Living Used A Shape That Has Been Going Round This County Against The People Who Would Be Cheered By It And A Man With A Broom Said Out Loud What That Cost Her And It Was Not A Figure And Nobody Was Told They Had The Right Of Anything

The woman who reads for a living came up the cart road with the book shut against her knee and a bad colour in her face, and she had walked two hours before she started talking, which everybody on that landing could see because she came in tired.

Sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fifty-fifth morning. Six hundred and five days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and one day old. Five hundred and fifty-nine days since the division. Five hundred and five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-one days in this county of Kell.

Twelve inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said he was going to be short of things to say that morning because there was going to be a great deal said on that landing and it was not his to fill in the gaps of.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that when a thing is said on his stone that he did not say, the least he can do is say what it did to the person who said it.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back, on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Sunday. Three figures.” He set the broom down and stood with it. “I am going to stand here a while this morning. I have held this landing nineteen years and I have never once stood on it with nothing to do while somebody else was using it, and I am going to do that today and I am not going to say a word while it is happening.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said that a Sunday was a day and not an occasion, and that he was not going to bring a new question for it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-fourth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-fourth. One thousand and five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and I am not going to be standing here saying what a Sunday is.”

“I said what a Sunday is. I am not asking you for anything and you are not giving me anything.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I will say the one thing I have learned standing on this landing and it is not a big thing. Somebody is going to get that name right one day. I do not know how and I do not know who. And when it happens my question will not change, and I have stopped minding.”

Nobody said he had got that right and nobody said he had got it wrong.

Then the woman who reads for a living came up the steps and did not sit down and put the book down at her feet with her boot on it, which is a thing she does.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that she was going to use a shape out loud against people, and that she knew what that would cost her before she started and was going to do it anyway.

She did not look at the strip once while she said it.

“There are people in this county who want that name on that wood to be right.” She said it flat and fast. “Not because they know anything about the wood. Because a right name is a finished thing. A right name is a thing you can be done with. And there is a shape going round this county this week about a thing named for a person not being that person’s, and it has got in very well, and I am going to use it now, and I am going to use it against those people, and I am aware of exactly what I am doing.”

Nobody stopped her and nobody asked her which people.

“The shape says a name does not make a thing yours. Good. True. I have read it in other people’s words for twenty-three years without one page of it ever once doing anything on the ground. And I am telling you now, in front of this landing, that it is going to do nothing on the ground for them either.” She turned round to the middle of the steps. “When that name comes out right, or when somebody puts a different name on, the people who wanted it finished are not going to be different people. They are going to be the same people with a nicer thing to hold.”

Nobody made a face about it and nobody said she was right.

“And I am going to say the part that is mine and not the shape’s. I have read for twenty-three years and every page that has ever made a reader feel clean has done it by putting the work on the ground and the feeling in the room, and it has never once done it the other way round. And I have never before stood on a landing and told a landing that a shape cannot give them what they want, and I have never before had to, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be told that I have any right to it.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody told her she had a right to anything.

“And nobody is to tell me what I have done. Not this morning and not next week. A person who says a shape out loud on a landing gets to walk down the bank on her own and that is the whole of what she gets.”

Then the man with the broom picked the broom up again and said what it had cost her, out loud, in his own mouth, and it was not a figure.

Corvin Tace stood where he had been standing and did not move until he had finished.

“I have held this landing nineteen years and I have stood at the top of it while a great many people went up and down that bank, and I tell you what happened just now in about a minute and a half by that wall.” He put the head of the broom on the stone. “That woman read for a living for twenty-three years. She has had the one thing in this world that makes people stop and listen, and she has never once used it to keep anybody from feeling better about themselves, because that is not a thing a person does with reading, it is a thing you keep for yourself.”

Nobody said a word.

“And this morning she used it to take a hope off a room. And I am not saying she was wrong, because I am not going to be the man on this stone who tells a person of forty-nine that she was wrong at the seventh hour on a Sunday. I am saying what it cost. It cost her the right to be a comfort to anybody, for as long as she stands on this landing, and she did not get that back when she got down the bank.”

Nobody said that she had been comforted and nobody said she had been relieved.

“And it is not a number and I am not going to give it as one. A number of what it cost her would be a figure of her and I have been at pains all week to keep figures of people off this stone.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with twelve inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-one days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-one days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-fifth is three hundred and five days old, which is one thousand and five less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it and I am not going to be offered a way out of it.”

He came up and stood with his back to the strip, which is a thing he has not done before.

“One hundred and forty-fifth wrong things in one hundred and forty-fifth mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-fifth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said a word against the figure and nobody said a word for it either.

“A woman of forty-nine has just used a shape against a room and a man of forty-three has said out loud what it cost her, and neither of them said one word about me, and I want that said, because I have been standing on this landing all week waiting for somebody to use something at me and they have not and I do not know what to do with that.”

Nobody told him what to do with that.

“My want is to be somebody. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-one mornings. And I am going to say the third thing and I am going to say it with my back to that strip, because if I turn round I will be able to say it in a way that is about the strip and I do not want it to be about the strip.” He kept facing the wall. “A shape has been used on this landing this week to take a hope off a room, and to keep a name off a piece of wood, and neither of those is a fight and neither of those is a rule. A man of thirty-one would like it noted that he has been counting the mouths it has been used in and is not going to tell the figure out loud, and that he is not going to count it tomorrow either.”

Nobody said what the figure was and nobody asked him for it.

Then everybody went down that bank by the end of it, in ones and twos, the way a landing empties when nobody has agreed to be late, and nobody said one word to anybody on the way down.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about seventy of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it, and did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step, and did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Twelve inches on the top step, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back, which are three figures about the water and not about a morning. A shape that has been going round this county without an author was used against the people who would have been comforted by it, and a man with a broom said out loud in his own mouth what it cost the woman who used it, and it was not a figure, and nobody was thanked, and nobody was told they had the right of anything.
