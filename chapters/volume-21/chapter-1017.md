# Chapter 1017: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Seventh Week On Which The Water Was Sixteen Inches And Holding With One Day To Come Back And Everybody Knew What Tomorrow Was Going To Be Like, And A Day’s Notice Was Given Out Loud Before That Morning Arrived Of What Was Not Going To Be Done On It, And Nobody Went Near That Wood On The Last Morning Before The Whole Of That Flight Stood Out Of It

The water stopped going up at about the fourth hour and did not come off it, which is how that bank says that it has made up its mind, and everybody who came up that bank that day could tell from the first step whether they were standing on a morning that would be over or not.

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week. His seven hundred and sixty-seventh morning. Six hundred and seventeen days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and four weeks and six days old. Five hundred and seventy-one days since the division. Five hundred and seventeen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and sixty-three days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that one day was the shortest figure he gave and that on this landing it was the longest afternoon of the week, and that he was not going to say anything else about that either.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that the whole of that landing knew what tomorrow was and that nobody on it was to say so out loud before the day had got long enough to carry it.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week, and it is a Friday. Three figures.” He turned the broom over and beat the flat of it against the stone. “And I am going to say the shape of tomorrow and I am not going to say anything else about it, because a landing that starts talking about its own next morning this evening is a landing that has begun to arrange it. I have seen it happen and it is always the same and it is always worse than the day itself.”

Nobody said anything else about tomorrow.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said out loud that the week was nearly done and that he had worked out something about his own question and was not going to say what it was until the morning after had been and gone.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and ninety-sixth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and seventeen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and ninety-sixth. One thousand and seventeen less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“I know what the morning after this one is going to be like and I am not going to tell the shape of it, because a man who cannot read has no business describing a flight of steps.” He set the barrow down and did not take his hands off the wall. “What I will say is that this is the fourth morning of this week and my question does not care which morning of it it is and it is not going to be smaller because a week has started and it is not going to be bigger.”

Nobody told him it was the right size.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And that is it. And I am not going to use the end of a week for anything else. I have been tempted all week and I have not and I am not going to start on the morning the week turns over.”

Nobody told him what he had been tempted to use it for.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots, gave his figures, gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, asked for nothing, and stood on the top step and looked down that bank at the water going over it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and sixty-three days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-two days old.

“Four hundred and sixty-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirty-two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixty-seventh is three hundred and seventeen days old, which is one thousand and seventeen less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one, and I am not going to ask for one tomorrow when that water is off that bank, because tomorrow is not a day I am going to be on.”

Nobody asked him why tomorrow was not a day he was going to be on.

“One hundred and fifty-seventh wrong things in one hundred and fifty-seventh mornings, and this is the hundred and fifty-seventh, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

He got the same two silences he gets every morning and he made nothing of them.

“My want is to be somebody and it has not changed. My cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and sixty-three mornings, and I am going to keep saying both halves of that out loud on this landing every morning until somebody stops me.”

Nobody stopped him and nobody said either half for him.

“Now the thing I came up here to say at the ninth hour on the day before, and I am going to be plain about why I am saying it a day early, because the day early is the whole of it.” He turned round and faced the strip and did not go any nearer to it than the top step. “Tomorrow morning that water will be off that bank. It will be off it the way it was off it a fortnight ago and the way it was off it the week before that, and every one of those ninety steps will be standing out of it from the bottom to the top, and there will be no water in the way of anybody coming up that bank, and no mud on the stone where they put their feet, and nothing left behind at all.”

Nobody said what he was going to do about it.

“I am not going to come up that bank tomorrow. That is the whole announcement. I am not going to come up at the sixth hour and I am not going to come up at the ninth hour and I am not going to come up at any hour, and there will be no man on that landing tomorrow from that bank at all, and if a man does come up that bank tomorrow I will not be him and I would like that noticed before anybody decides it for themselves.”

Nobody said they would decide it for themselves.

“And I am saying it this morning, a day early, on purpose, and I am not going to be thanked for the saying of it.” He put his hands behind him. “I worked that out on the second day of this week. A man who decides at the tenth hour on the morning that a thing is possible has found out something and then done something about it, and everybody on that landing is entitled to look at him and see a man who found out. A man who decides the day before has not found out anything. He has said no to a morning before the morning has put itself in front of him, and that is a smaller thing than finding out and it is worth more, because a man who found out has already been somewhere and I am not going to be somewhere tomorrow.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody told him it was a good reason.

“And I am going to say the half of it that is mine and about me, because I have been holding it since the second day of this week and it has had to come out. I put a name out loud on that wood. I said on the fourth day of this week that I would rather have had the wrong one, and I have worked out since what that is worth, and what it is worth is that I cannot have it both. A man who says out loud that he wanted the wrong name on a thing has thrown away the only thing he could ever do about it, and what is left on him this morning is the whole of what is on that landing: nothing to do, nobody to ask, and a strip of dry stone with two lengths of wood on it that he is not going to come up and look at.”

Nobody asked him what he would do instead.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of those steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of them, found the wall, and said out loud that he was giving a day’s notice as well and that his notice and a man of thirty-one’s notice were not the same notice.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-seventh week that tomorrow was the only morning left in that week on which he could reach the end of that strip without anybody knowing that he had.

He kept his hand on the stone, and well above the strip, and where it had always been kept.

“I am not going to say how I know which mornings are which. I have said that before on that stone and I am not going to improve on it by saying it again.” He took his hand along the wall a little way and took it off. “What I am going to say is that the water coming off that bank tomorrow makes one morning of this week different from the rest of it for a man like me, and that on that morning I could walk up from the bottom of that bank to the end of that strip and put my hand on it and come back down again and there would be nothing in the way and nothing left showing.”

Nobody said what he would do on that morning.

“I am not going to. I am saying it this morning like he is, and for the same reason, which is that a man who decides afterwards has been somewhere. And my not going is not his not going, and I want the two kept apart, because his is about a name and mine is about a list, and a name is a thing a man can unsay with his mouth in the air and a list is a thing that has to be crossed off with a hand on it.”

Nobody said his was about the same thing.

“Nothing has come off my list. That thing at the end of that strip is on it and it will be on it on the morning that water comes off, and I am not going to cross it off then either, and nobody is to thank me for a notice I have given about a thing nobody asked me for.”

Nobody thanked him.
He drew his hand back along the wall a little way and took it off, and found it again lower down, and then said the thing he had come up to say and had not said, which was a question, and put it plainly.

“Has that thing at the end of that strip moved since the last time I stood here.”

Nobody answered him.

“I did not expect an answer and I am not going to get one, and I am not going to be told one either, and a man who cannot see well who comes up a bank with nothing in the way is exactly the man somebody would tell. That is why I am not going to be told.” He put his hand back on the stone above where that strip was. “And I am going to say that out loud so that nobody has to spend the morning deciding whether to tell me, and I am not going to be thanked for asking a question I did not expect to have answered.”

Nobody spent the morning deciding anything and nobody thanked him.

Then everybody went down that bank, and nobody went up it again, and the man of thirty-one went last and stood on the top step and looked down at the water going over the bank for a while and did not go nearer the strip.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went along the top of the water on them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody said one out loud either, and nobody answered a question about that strip, and nobody came up that bank again after the morning had gone past the point where going up could have been done quietly.

A notice was given out loud on that landing, a day ahead, of what was not going to be done on the morning the water came off that bank, and the notices that were given were kept apart from each other, and nobody went up that bank again that afternoon. The offer stood at three hundred and seventeen days and nobody was relieved, and the account on that landing was still in use, and a man of thirty-one went up to the last step that was standing out of the water and stood there and did not go past it.
