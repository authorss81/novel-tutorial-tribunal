# Chapter 1031: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Ninth Week On Which Sixteen Inches Stood On That Top Step At About The Seventh Hour And It Holds And About Sixty Of Those Ninety Steps Under And Three Days Of The Coming Back, And A Woman Who Saws Lengths Came Up That Cart Road At About The Eighth Hour With Nothing In Her Hands To Say That A Voice Had Carried A Man’s Name Down That Road To Her Bench Without A Soul Carrying It, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Asked Whether Anybody Had Said It Since The Week Turned And Was Given Nothing At All, And A Man With A Broom Began That Flight At The Bottom Of It Going Up Because The Sound Comes Up

The wind came down that cart road with the cold still in it and it went through the open side of the shed behind the wheelwright’s and moved the sawdust on the bench, and the bench is where the man with the saw works with both hands and cannot hear a thing over his own saw.

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week, and it is the Friday of that week. His seven hundred and eighty-first morning. Six hundred and thirty-one days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and six weeks and six days old. Five hundred and eighty-five days since the division. Five hundred and thirty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and seventy-seven days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and gave nothing else off that stone, and he said before he gave them that he had stood at the top of the flight and tried to hear whether his own landing had a sound in it that it had not had before, and that he was not going to put what he heard into a figure, because what he heard was either a voice or the wind and he cannot tell them apart at that distance and he is not going to pretend otherwise on a landing that has three figures in it already.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that he had been listening to his own stone since the light came up and had decided that a man who cannot tell a voice from the wind at the top of ninety cut steps is not a man who gets to say a thing has gone further than it has.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week, and it is a Friday. Three figures, and that is what the stone has.”

He worked the top step and stopped with the broom head against the wet stone.

“And I am going to do something on that flight this morning that I have not done since I came to it, and I am going to say what I am doing before I do it so that nobody has to work out later whether I thought about it.” He turned the broom in his hands. “I have swept that flight off the top step downward every morning of nineteen years. From this morning I am going to sweep it from the bottom upward. That is the whole of my reason and I have got no better one. If a voice can get off this landing and down that road then it is coming up from somewhere, and I would rather be standing at the low end of that flight with my back to it than at the top with my face to it when it does. I am not saying anything is coming. I am saying where I will be standing.” Nobody asked him to change where he stood.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and set the barrow down, and put his hands flat on the wall, which is what he does.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and tenth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and thirty-one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and tenth. One thousand and thirty-one less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He did not take his hands off the wall.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And a man with a broom has just said out loud that he has been listening to his own stone since the light came up, and I have been on this landing since before the light and I have not heard a thing out of it, and I am not going to say that settles anything with him, because he is up the top of it and I am up the bottom of it and the wind goes along a bank the way it likes.” He turned his head up the flight. “And there is a thing I have been carrying round in my mouth since the middle of the week and I am going to put it out and then I am going to do a day’s gravel. A man came up my road. Not up the cart road. Up the road that goes past the lime. He said a thing in the ordinary way a man says a thing he has been saying for a while, and I did not understand the half of it, and he did not stop to explain it to me and he was right not to. That is all. I am not going to say it again and I would be obliged if nobody asked me what it was about.”

Then the woman who saws lengths came up that cart road with nothing under her arm at all and stopped at the foot of those steps, and did not come up one of them, and said the thing she had walked up there for before she had said anything else.

Wenna Pyle is thirty-eight, who saws lengths and sets studs at a bench in an open-sided shed behind a wheelwright’s about three hundred yards down that cart road, and who carries her own cup out to that bench because there is no table in the shed, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that a voice had come down that road in the wind with a man’s name in it and that she had been at her own bench with her own saw going at the time, and that she had not been on that landing at any part of it.

“I want to say this to the landing and not to a person, so I am going to say it standing at the bottom of your steps where anybody who happens to be here can hear it and nobody can say I said it to them.” She did not put her hands behind her back and she did not fold them. “There was a man’s name in that voice. It came down the road with the wind off the top of that bank and it went into an open shed and I heard it with a saw going. I have been up and down that road every working day of my life and I know what every sound on it is, and that was not one of mine, and it was not the wind, because the wind does not make a word. And I want it understood that I did not carry it. Nobody carried it to me. There was no man on that road with a message and there was no woman with a message and I did not go and ask anybody for it. A voice came off that landing and it went down a road and there was me at the end of the road with my saw going, and that is the whole of how it happened.”

“Which brings me to the thing I am actually here for, and I have thought about it all the way up and I am not going to soften it.” She looked up the bank and did not look at any one person on it. “I know what that wood is. I named it out of my own trade before I had said it to anybody else, and I said the same morning that I was not giving it to anybody. I have not changed that and I am not going to change it this morning because a word of mine has gone out of a door I cannot shut. I know what it is and I am not going to say it, and if you have all been standing here waiting for the woman who saws lengths to say what that piece of wood is then you have been standing here for nothing this morning, and I would rather you had it now than in another week.”

Nobody said what the wood was, and nobody asked her to say it, and she stood at the foot of the steps while nobody said anything for a while.

“And the last of it and it is about me. There is a name in my head now that was not in my head on Tuesday and I am not going to be able to put it down again, and I have not got a single thing I can do about that, and I have looked at it from every side I can reach from a bench.” She turned her head up the flight. “I am not going to say the name back to the man who said it, and I am not going to say it back to the man it is for, and I am not going to say it in a kitchen or a cart road or a doorway. If a name gets said back to you, you have got a second copy of it, and I have not got a use for a second copy and neither has that strip. So it stays where it landed, which is in me, and nobody is to thank me for it and nobody is to tell me I did well, and anybody who thanks me for coming up this road is going to find that they have wasted a breath.” No thanks were given on that road that morning.

Then the man of thirty-one came up that bank at about the ninth hour, went round the two pieces of ash on the dry strip without going near them, and gave his figures, and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and seventy-seven days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and forty-six days old.

“Four hundred and seventy-seven days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and forty-six days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-eighty-first is three hundred and thirty-one days old, which is one thousand and thirty-one less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one, and I am not going to ask for one on the morning a woman has walked up a road to tell us that something of mine has left this landing without me.”

He stood on the second step from the top with the wall behind him and did not go towards the strip.

“One hundred and seventy-first wrong things in one hundred and seventy-first mornings, and this is the hundred and seventy-first, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is to tell me that I found anything. My want is to be somebody and it is the same want and I am not going to say it in a new shape because a morning like this morning is exactly the morning a man says his want in a new shape to look as though the morning did something to him.”

“And I am going to say the last of it and nobody is to answer any part of it.” He shut his hand and opened it again. “A woman who saws lengths has just told that landing that a voice of mine got to a bench three hundred yards down that road, and that nobody carried it, and that the woman it got to is the woman who knows what that piece of wood is and is not going to say. That is the shape of the whole of this and I have been putting the wrong pieces round it for days. It is not that a name has gone anywhere. It is that a mouth of mine goes out of a door I cannot shut and lands in a person, and there is no road back on it, and that is not a figure of anybody and I am not going to give it one.” Nobody gave it one.

Then the man who cannot see well came up that flight at about the tenth hour, about sixty of those ninety steps being under, and stopped where the wall was not wet any more, and put his hand on it at the height it always goes at.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that a man with a broom had changed the way he swept that flight and that he had heard the changing from the bottom of it, and that he was not going to say what he thought of the change because a man with a broom does not need a man who cannot see well telling him what his own stone sounds like.

He did not take his hand off the wall.

“Has anybody said it since the week turned.” That got nothing at all out of the air, and none of the shapes an answer can arrive in arrived in it, and the man who keeps a list did not put it on the list, and he did not take his hand off the stone while it did not come, and he did not say a second time that it had not come, because a man who says a silence twice has started to want it.

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and the woman who saws lengths went down the road at the back of the others with nothing in her hands, and the man of thirty-one went last and stood on the second step from the bottom for a moment and went on down.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and this time he began at the bottom of the flight and worked upward with the water still coming over the top of it, so that on every step he had already wet the stone under the step he was on, and he went up and round the fortieth one on the way and did not stand on it, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side. He said nothing to anybody about what he was listening for at the top of that flight, and nobody asked him, and by the time the light was off the water there was no grit left standing on the wet edge of that top step from the wind of the morning before.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it, then or since, and nobody laid a hand on it after those sentences either. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody on that landing said out loud what that name was, and nobody put a name on anything else either, and nobody said out loud what that piece of wood is.

That morning came and went, and the standing offer stood at three hundred and thirty-one days and was not asked on and was not answered on and was not withdrawn on, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour and it was still in use, and the name on that second piece of ash was still wrong.

A woman who saws lengths went back down that cart road to an open-sided shed with no sawdust on her clothes and a name in her head that had not been there on Tuesday, and she went in and set her saw down on the bench with its teeth turned away from her, which is not the way she leaves it, and stood looking at them for a while and then went to open the side of the shed and started on a length that had nothing to do with that landing.
