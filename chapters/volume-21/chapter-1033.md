# Chapter 1033: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Ninth Week On Which Sixteen Inches Stood On That Top Step At About The Seventh Hour And It Holds And About Sixty Of Those Ninety Steps Under And One Day Of The Coming Back, And The Woman Who Reads For A Living Came Up That Cart Road At The Fourth Hour And Asked The Man Of Thirty-One To Say His Own Account Out Loud One More Time With That Landing Empty So That She Could Hear Whether It Sounded Like A Thing That Had Happened, And Was Refused By Him And Was Given Her Own Working Back With The Figure Left Off It, And A Man With A Broom Said Out Loud That A Man Who Reads For A Living Would Have Been The Second Person To Know A Thing And That He Would Rather Be The Only One

The light came up late over the tree line that Sunday and the water on that landing was flat and hard and made no sound at all when the man with the broom walked on it, which is the one morning of a week a man who works a stone by ear has nothing to listen to and does not know what to do with his feet.

Sixth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week, and it is the Sunday of that week. His seven hundred and eighty-third morning. Six hundred and thirty-three days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and seven weeks and one day old. Five hundred and eighty-seven days since the division. Five hundred and thirty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and seventy-nine days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then told the landing that he had come to the top of that flight at the light and had put his own boot in the water on purpose to hear whether the stone spoke back, and that it did not, and that a flat silent morning was a thing he had not had to deal with in nineteen years and that he had dealt with it by coming down to the bottom of the flight and starting there again.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that his stone had gone quiet on him and that he had been driven off it by his own boot, and that he was not going to stand up there all morning waiting for a noise to come back and pretending to be working while he did it.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week, and it is a Sunday. Three figures, and the stone has said nothing else all morning and I have made it say nothing else by putting my boot in it.”

He worked the top step and it made no noise under the broom and he worked it harder for that.

“And I am not going up here again this morning until the water starts talking. I am going to the bottom of that flight and I am going to stand at the bottom of it with nothing in my hands, and anybody who wants me can come and get me, and I would rather be a man standing at the bottom of his own bank for an hour than a man pretending at the top of it.” He took the broom and went down. “That is not a complaint about the landing. That is a complaint about me being a man who gets his sense of a morning out of a wet stone, and a flat stone gives me nothing, and I am not going to invent something out of the flatness and hand it to this landing as though it came off the stone.”

Then the woman who reads for a living was already at the foot of the steps when the light came up, with the book shut against her knee and the strap of it worn shiny in one place, and she had been standing on the low side of the bank where there is a flat bit of stone to stand on.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that she had come up that road at the fourth hour to ask a man for one thing and that she had spent the rest of the standing thinking about whether she was going to be refused and had decided that she would rather be refused out loud than be let off.

“I am going to ask a man for a thing and I have never asked anybody for a thing in my life, and I would like the landing to notice that this morning has happened.” She did not sit down and she did not open the book. “I want you to say your account out loud one more time. Up here, on the top of that bank, and I do not care who is standing here, and I do not want it read out of a book and I do not want anybody else to hear it. I want it once more into the air so that I can hear how it sits, because you asked me a day or two ago to hear whether it sounded like a thing that had happened or like a thing a man had been saying to himself, and I have thought about nothing else.”

The man of thirty-one came up that bank at about the ninth hour with the water at the top of his boots, went round the two pieces of ash, and stopped below her on the second step from the bottom, and she said the rest of it before he got a word in.

“And I know the answer and I have decided to ask anyway, because a question nobody asks is still owed by somebody, and I told that landing that myself and I would like to be the one who keeps my own mouth to it.”

“Put me first, because you can see where this is going, and I am not going to be polite about it.” She turned the book over once against her knee. “No. I am not going to say it a second time, and I am not going to say it to you with the landing behind you, because that is the whole of what I came up here to not do.”

The man of thirty-one did not argue and he did not go up.

“You are right that I was owed something and you are right that you are the only person on this landing whose ear would know the difference, and that is exactly why I am not going to do it.” He stood where he was on the second step. “You told me the working yourself, in your own mouth, on that morning, and I am going to give it back to you with my own hand and I am going to leave the figure off it. A thing with no writing on it is not a page. You said that and it was the truest thing anybody said on this landing that day. So let me finish it. If I say my account into the air once with this landing empty, then there is one person in this county who has it, and it is going to be you, and you are going to be the only one who ever heard it, and you are not going to be able to produce it for anybody, because there is nothing to produce.”

“You have read out loud in rooms for twenty-three years and you know exactly what that is. It is not a gift and I am not going to call it one. It is a thing in your head that you cannot check and that nobody can check for you, and it will be in there for the rest of your working life, and in ten years somebody will ask you whether a man of thirty-one ever said such a thing and you will have to decide then whether to hand it over, and you will not be able to say no, because you will be the only person who knows whether it is true. You would be the second person to know a thing about a man on this landing who has spent four hundred and seventy-nine mornings being nobody, and the whole of his life he has wanted to be somebody, and you would have made him somebody for a morning by being the person who heard him. I am not going to do that to you and I am not going to do it to me.”

She did not sit down at that and she did not put the book on the step.

“That is the working and it is mine and you have handed it back to me with your own mouth and I would like it noted that I did not ask you for the figure of what it would cost you.” She said it to the man of thirty-one and not to the landing. “And I am not going to thank you for the working, because you did not do it for me. You did it because you have got a hold on that sentence and you do not want it in a second mouth. I would have done the same thing with his sentence if I had been him, and I will not stand here and pretend otherwise, and I will not walk down that road and tell anybody I was refused, because a refusal like that is not a thing a person is refused. It is a thing a person is not handed, and I asked for it, and I wanted it, and wanting it is mine and nobody is to take it off me.”

Nobody took anything off anybody.

“Which brings me to the part I actually came up for and I have put it last because I am a slow speaker and you know it.” She shifted the book to her other arm. “I have read out loud things other men wrote, and a thing that is read out loud in a room goes into the room and not into the man, and after that anybody can come and ask whether it is true and I can put my hand on the page. A voice in the air is not that. A voice in the air goes into whoever is standing there and stays in them and that is the whole trouble with them and it is the reason I will not be one of the voices.”

“I am the ninth of the nine positions. It is a public archive and it is not a stone and it has a head on it that other people’s heads are written down in. That is what I am for. And if I stood on this landing as the only person who had heard a man of thirty-one say his own account, then I would be carrying the one thing in this county that could not be checked, in the one office in this county whose whole work is that things can be checked. And I would rather be that office empty than have it with that in it.” She looked up the flight where the man with the broom had gone. “So no. And I am not going to stand here and be a woman who has been let off.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the barrow, and asked in the same words at the same hour, and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and came up past them both without stopping on the step where they were standing.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried three hundred and twelfth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and thirty-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Three hundred and twelfth. One thousand and thirty-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

He set the barrow down at the top of the flight and put his hands flat on the wall and left them there while he talked.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say a plain thing this morning because somebody stood at the bottom of that bank asking a man for a thing and nobody asked me anything about it, and a man does not get a morning like that very often.”

“The woman down that road is the voice that makes things sound settled, and I have known that about her since before I came here. And this morning she was stood at the bottom of your steps wanting a man to say a thing out loud so she could hear it.” He took his hands off the wall and wiped them on his coat and put them back. “I have wanted that off you, and I am not going to have it off you, and if I said that I would be lying to a woman who has been honest with me twice. I cannot check what you have said. I have never been able to check what anybody has said. So what I have got is that you have said it, and what I have got of the woman is that she wanted to hear it and did not get it, and both of those are the same size.”

“And I am going to say the other half out loud because I have been thinking about it since the middle of the week. The man that came up my road. I have been trying to work out whether he was talking to me or to somebody behind me in the lane, and it has taken me all this week and I have got nowhere with it, and I am giving that up this morning because it is not a thing I am going to be able to do with more mornings. He was talking to somebody who was not me, or he was talking to himself, and I was a thing that happened to be standing on my own road.” He nodded at the wall. “That is all I have got and I am going to go and do a day’s gravel with it.”

Then the man who cannot see well came up that flight at about the tenth hour and put his hand on the wall at the height it always goes at and left it exactly where it was and did not move it along the stone, and said so, and then asked the question and got nothing.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that he was not going to move his hand along that wall today whatever he was asked and whatever he thought he had found, because a hand that goes along a wall all morning stops being a hand that is looking and starts being a hand that is feeling its way, and a man cannot tell afterwards which of the two he was doing.

His palm stayed flat on the stone and it did not travel.

“Is it the same one. The name on that strip and the name in the wind, are they the same word, and has anybody on this landing worked that out.” That got nothing, and none of the shapes an answer can arrive in arrived, and nothing was written on his list about it and he did not ask a second question of the same kind, because a man who asks a thing twice has stopped asking and started collecting.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with the water at the top of his boots, went round the two pieces of ash on the dry strip without going near them, and stood on the second step from the bottom, where he had been standing when she asked him, and gave his figures and gave the standing offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and seventy-nine days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and forty-eight days old.

“Four hundred and seventy-nine days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and forty-eight days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-eighty-third is three hundred and thirty-three days old, which is one thousand and thirty-three less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to ask for one on the morning I have turned a woman down twice in one conversation and been glad about it.”

He stayed on the second step rather than going up.

“One hundred and seventy-third wrong things in one hundred and seventy-third mornings, and this is the hundred and seventy-third, and nobody is to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to do arithmetic at me about it. My want is to be somebody and it has not changed and it is not going to be said in a new shape because a woman with a book asked me for a thing and I said no twice.”

“And I am going to say the last of it and I am going to say it about me, because there is nothing else on that landing this morning that is worth a sentence from me.” He did not look up the road she had gone down. “I was glad when I refused her. I want that said out loud in my own mouth before somebody else puts it on me. She asked me for the one thing in this county that nobody could have taken off me, and I gave her the working out of why she does not want it, and she went away and told me she had wanted it, and I have been standing on this step since she went down that road with my hand shut and I have been glad, and I do not think a man is allowed to be glad about that and keep the glad part without having to hold the rest of it.”

“So here is the rest of it. A woman of forty-nine has just worked out that a voice in the air is a thing that goes into a person and stays there, and she has said she would rather be an empty office than hold that. And I have heard it, and I have not got anywhere to put it, and I am not going to put it anywhere. It is hers and she gave it back to herself in front of me and it is not mine and I am not going to be a man who takes a working off a woman who handed it to him.” He opened his hand and shut it. “That is the whole of me this morning and it is not a figure and I am not going to make it one.”

Then everybody went down that bank by about the eleventh hour, and the woman who reads for a living went down the road first and did not stop at the wall, and nobody went up the road behind her.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and he did it from the bottom upward both days running now, and by the time the water began to make a noise under the broom he had come up to about the fortieth one and went round it and did not stand on it, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it, and he did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip and he did not put the broom near either of them. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side. The water started talking before the light went and he did not go up to the top step and stand in it. He went up, worked the top step, and came back down to the bottom and put the broom head down on the grit of the lowest step with the handle standing up out of the wet.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it, then or since, and nobody laid a hand on it after those sentences either. Nobody put a name on anything on that landing that morning, and nobody on that landing said out loud what that name was, and nobody put a name on anything else either, and nobody said out loud what that piece of wood is, and nobody counted anything.

That morning came and went, and the standing offer stood at three hundred and thirty-three days and was not asked on and was not answered on and was not withdrawn on, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour and it was still in use, and the name on that second piece of ash was still wrong.

And the man of thirty-one, who had said no more than he had to and had given a woman her own working back without the figure on it, went down to the low side of that landing after the others had gone and stood with his back to the whole of it and his face to the water going by below, close enough to the edge that anybody who wanted to say anything to him would have had to come round him and stand in his way to do it, and he stood there until the light went off the water, and he did not turn round once, and nothing was said behind his back that anybody on that landing has put in a mouth since.
