# Chapter 1009: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Sixth Week And A Woman Who Reads For A Living Was Asked By Nobody Where A Shape Came From And Said Out Loud That She Knew And That She Was Not Going To Say It And Was Not Going To Be Told She Was The One Who Started It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Said Out Loud That He Was Not Going To Go Down A Lane To Stand Beside A Thing Nobody Had Asked Him To Stand Beside

Third day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week. His seven hundred and fifty-ninth morning. Six hundred and nine days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and three weeks and five days old. Five hundred and sixty-three days since the division. Five hundred and nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifty-five days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that one day was the shortest figure he gave and that he had said that on another morning this week and was not going to say it again, and that anybody who wanted to hear it could come up at the seventh hour any morning this county.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that he had spent everything he had to say about numbers and that from that point on the landing could do as it liked.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the third day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week, and it is a Thursday. Three figures.”

He set the broom against the wall, clear of the strip, and stood on the top step and listened to the water for a while.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and said that he had begun to notice that nobody was looking at him while he said it, and that he had decided that was a thing to be glad of rather than a thing to be sad of, and that he did not intend to work out which.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and eighty-eighth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand and nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and eighty-eighth. One thousand and nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and you have been looking at the same wall for a fortnight and I would like you to know that I have watched you do it and I have not once worked out whether you are reading it.”

“I am not reading it. There is nothing on it.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am not going to talk about the name this morning. I have said what I am going to say about the name and I said it with my hands on that wall and I am not going to take it back and I am not going to say it again.”

Nobody said it back to him and nobody said it was the right thing to have said.

Then the woman who reads for a living came up the cart road at about the fourth hour with the book shut against her knee, as she does, and stood at the foot of the steps and said the thing she had walked up the road to say, and it was about where a shape came from, and nobody had asked her.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that she knew where that shape came from and was not going to say it and was going to lie about it if she was asked.

Nobody asked her.

“You have all been carrying a shape about this landing since Tuesday and there is nobody on this stone who made it.” She did not sit down and did not put the book down. “And you have all been very careful this week not to ask each other where it came from, and I want you to know that I have noticed the carefulness, and that the carefulness is the most interesting thing that has happened on this landing in nineteen years.”

Nobody said anything about the carefulness.

“I know where it came from. I am not going to tell you. I have had it in my mouth since before the light and it has been a comfortable thing to carry up a road and it is going to die up here, and I have made my choice and I made it before I got to the bottom of your steps.”

Nobody told her she was the one who had started it. Nobody said she was not the one who had started it. Nobody asked her.

“And I am going to use it once before I go up, because I have worked out where it came from and I am not going to say, and a thing a person carries up a hill for nothing is a thing they pay for on the way down.” She did not look at the strip. “A name said out loud over a thing is not the thing and it is not the man it names either, and that is the whole of it, and I have read that in other people’s words for twenty-three years without once standing on a stone and saying it. I am not saying it because it came to me this morning. I am saying it because it does not cost the person who says it anything, and I have spent the morning watching what it costs.”

Nobody asked her where she got it, and nobody gave that sentence a name, and she stood at the foot of those steps with the book shut against her knee until somebody else began.

“If anybody asks me where it came from I am going to say I do not know, and I am going to say it in the flat voice I use on a page, and it will be the only time in twenty-three years that I have said a sentence to a person that I have not read.” She turned the book over against her knee. “And I am not going to be thanked for not saying it. A person who knows where a thing came from and does not say it has not done anything and there is nothing to thank.”

Nobody thanked her.

“And here is the other half of it and I am going to say it before somebody puts it in for me. There is no age in that shape. It has no age in it. It is not new this week and it is not old and nobody on this landing is going to stand up and say how many weeks it has been going round, and if one of you does, I will read the page that says it and I will know it is a page.”

Nobody said how many weeks anything had been going round.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and then said a thing about a lane that nobody on that landing had asked him to say anything about.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifty-five days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-four days old.

“Four hundred and fifty-five days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twenty-four days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifty-ninth is three hundred and nine days old, which is one thousand and nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one and I am not going to ask for one before the water is off those steps again.”

He came up the steps that were out and stood on the top one.

“One hundred and forty-ninth wrong things in one hundred and forty-ninth mornings, and this is the hundred and forty-ninth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything to him about either of those.

“My want is to be somebody and my cost is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for one thing in four hundred and fifty-five mornings, and I am going to be neither relieved nor unrelieved about that and I would like that said by somebody who is not me.”

Nobody said it for him.

“And now the thing I have not said all week and I am saying it at the ninth hour because the water is about to come off that bank and after that I will not be able to say it in the same voice.” He turned round to the whole landing. “There is work at the low end of that drain that a person cannot do by themselves. I am not going to say whose work it is because it is not mine to say and because a woman has asked out loud for a person to stand with her and she has not named anybody, and I have been walking past that end of that drain for four hundred and fifty-five days.”

Nobody on that landing said whose work it was.

“And I am not going to go down there.” He said it flat and he did not put any weight on it. “Not because I do not want to. I want to. I have wanted to every morning of this county and the reason I am not going to is that nobody has asked me. A man who goes down a lane and stands beside a thing because it would be nice of him has turned a woman’s asking into an audience, and she asked for a person and not for a man who thinks well of himself.”

Nobody went down the lane.

“And I am not going to be thanked for not going, and I am not going to be told that was the right of it either, and if anybody on this landing has a word for what I have just declined to do I would like them to keep it, because I have not got a word for it and I am the one doing it.”

Nobody had a word for it and nobody gave him one.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him and found the wall and stood against it, and said that he could tell from the sound of it how many people had gone down that bank that morning, and that he was not going to give the figure, and that this was the first time in nineteen years he had had a figure in his hand and thrown it away.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-sixth week that he was not going to put a figure of people on his list either.

“I can hear that landing empty out. I have been doing it since before I could see well and I could tell you in a moment how many boots have gone down that bank this morning and I am not going to tell you.” He kept his hand on the stone. “I have got a list of things I cannot check and I have never once written a person on it, and I am not going to start at the end of a week when everybody else is tired.”

Nobody asked him for it and nobody wrote a person on anything.

“And a woman stood at the foot of those steps and said she knows where a shape came from and is not going to say it, and I want it said that I did not know that, and that I am glad, and that nobody is to thank me for being glad because I have not done anything except stand against a wall.”

Nobody thanked him.

Then everybody went down that bank and the man of thirty-one went last and stood at the top of those steps and looked at the strip for a while without going near it.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety of those steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on it. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of wood on the dry strip. The first was a forearm long and had been split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, with the thick end left as it came apart. The second was the same and had a saw mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the two run out of the side.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and nobody laid a hand on that wood in order to take anything off it.

Sixteen inches on the top step and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. A woman who reads for a living carried a thing she knew up a road and would not put it down and was not asked and was not told she had started anything. A man of thirty-one said out loud that he was not going to go down a lane and stand beside a thing nobody had asked him to stand beside, and nobody went down the lane, and nobody told him he had the right of anything.
