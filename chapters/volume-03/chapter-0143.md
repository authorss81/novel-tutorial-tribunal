# Chapter 143: The Refusal

The second of the ninth tide is a good day and the flat takes six, and the sea at the north end was down and going, and the middle eleven yards of a wall nineteen years and eight months old was under nine foot of green water and would be until the fourth of that tide.

At about the third bell, in the lane at the head of the flat, with the chain up and a cart of deals waiting at the near stones and about nine people within twenty feet of him and no more than that because it is a second and a good day and everybody on a good day is somewhere else, Hal Bevan said the thing out loud.

He is fifty-four and he has been on that chain nineteen years and he keeps a slate in his pocket, and the slate has three marks on the face of it and no rule behind any of them anywhere on four hundred miles of this coast, and he has never written it down and does not intend to.

And he had never before in nineteen years stood in that lane and asked the flat anything, and everybody within earshot knew that was what was happening about four seconds before anybody could have told you.

“I am going to say a thing and I want it in the open and I am not going to write it on anything,” he said. “There is a wall at the north end with eleven yards of the foot of it gone, and there is a man with a number in a book in a shed that says so, and a boil in the middle of that wall on the sixth evening with nobody in it. And that is three things and none of them is a claim.

“A claim has got somebody standing behind it. That is the whole of what a claim is. You cannot have a claim with nobody’s name at the end of it — that is not me being careful, that is what a claim *is*, and I have been on this chain nineteen years and I have watched about four men make a claim out of a thing that ought to have had a person on it and I have watched what happened to all four.

“And the people who can stand behind a claim about that flat are the people who go over it. Not the guild. The guild will not, and the guild is right, and I have read their four lines twice and every word of them is the truest thing anybody has sent up this coast in a year. Not a man in a good coat from a city four hundred miles behind us — he has no standing on this road and he is right about that and he is right about it more than anybody here. Not the man who keeps the wall, because he keeps it and he is owed by it and he has been waiting nineteen years for somebody to ask him for a day and nobody did.

“The people who go over it. Nine hundred yards of shingle, six times today, and a flat takes nine three days a week, and I have been the one who decides who gets it when there is more flat than there is time, and I do that with a slate in my pocket and no rule, and I have done it nineteen years and it has never once been written down and I have never once been asked whether I would rather it was something else.

“So I am asking the flat. Not asking whether anybody objects. I am asking the flat, and I want an answer in the open, and I am going to be told the difference in a minute, and I have never done this in nineteen years and it is not going to feel like anything.”

Nobody objected.

That is the whole of what happened in the first four seconds and it is worth being exact about, because everything else that morning turned on it, and because a blank is not a no. It is not a yes either. There is a clerk two hundred miles up this coast who reads a line carrying a wage with nobody’s name on it, and when he gets to a line like that he may either put a name in or leave it, and there is no third thing to do, and neither of the two is any use to anybody, and this quarter he left it.

Nobody objected. A carter with a load of deals said nothing. A man sorting bait in a drying yard forty yards off said nothing. A boy of about ten who was not asked anything said nothing. Nine people stood in a lane on a good day and not one of them said the word no.

Corrie Tallow was forty yards off under her awning at the stall with her board turned to the weather, because a sentence like the one that had just been said carries about four times further than a man thinks it does and a woman of forty-eight on this coast has learned that in four years of writing down a ninth crossing nobody pays her for. She had heard all of it.

“No,” she said.

Nine people heard it and about six of them turned round, which told her exactly how far it had carried, which is the only measurement she has ever wanted.

“I am going to say it properly and out loud and I am not going to be asked twice and I am not going to say it twice myself. That is a no, and it is the flat’s, and it is mine to say because I have been on this shingle longer than anybody here and I am the one that writes the ninth down and nobody pays me for it, and if this is going to be somebody’s business then it is going to be mine before it is anybody else’s.

“And it is not a no because anybody here thinks the wall does not matter. I have heard six of you not say anything and four of you have already worked out that a wall with eleven yards of the foot of it gone is not going to wait for anybody, and I am not one of the four and I am not one of the six either, and I will tell you what it is.

“It is that you cannot be a party by not objecting. That is the whole of it and it has taken me eleven minutes to get to and I have been at this stall four years and I did not have it before this morning.

“A party is not a silence. A party is a person who has said something out loud that they are then standing behind, and the standing behind is the whole of what the word means, and if you have not said it then you have not got it, and anybody who tells you that nine people not objecting is nine people agreeing is a man who has never been on a road with anybody on it.

“Nowhere on four hundred miles of this coast does one person answer for another person about a crossing. That is about four hundred years old and it is in nobody’s book, and everybody in this lane has it filed as a rule about who signs what, and it is not a rule about signing. I have had it since I was seventeen and started this stall and I have never had the words for it until this morning.

“It is the shape of a person not being used by somebody else’s pen. And it is the shape of a person saying *this is what I said and I am the one who said it* and being the only one who can say it, and it is why a crossing on this coast counts a person and not a cart. And you cannot get that out of a silence, and you cannot get it out of nine people, and you cannot get it out of a body, and if the flat stands behind a claim about that wall then what stands behind it is a body, and there is not one thing on this coast a body can stand behind.”

She turned her board about a quarter of a turn into the weather and then turned it back again, which is a thing she does with her hands when she is not going to be interrupted.

“Now the part that is about the wall and not about me, and I have thought about it all morning at that stall and this is the true part and I want it in the open.

“If the flat is not a party, then the claim about that wall has to be carried by people who were not on the flat. And there are three of those, and I know who they are because I have been listening to a man with a bad leg for a fortnight and he has not asked any of them for anything either, and I will not say more than that because it is not my thing to say.

“And to carry it, a claim about that wall has to go into a thing. And the only things on this coast that a claim can go into are glass and paper, and the glass is a year and a fire and eleven months of a woman of twenty-four who is paid nothing, and nobody has asked her, and the paper is a room a mile up this hill with a lock on it where two panes have hung in oak frames for nineteen years and have not been out of that room once in nineteen years.

“So the answer is no, and the answer being no does not stop the water, and the eleven yards is still going, and the number is still in a book in a shed, and I want that said in the same breath as the no, because I have watched a thing get refused on this coast once and I am not going to be the second thing that gets refused and then gets pointed at as though the refusing had done something.”

Nobody said anything.

“It has not stopped anything,” she said. “It has cost something, and I will tell you what it has cost, because a refusal that costs nobody anything is a performance and mine is not a performance.

“It has cost a day, and it has not cost it yet. That is the whole of it. A day on this coast is not money, because the money is not there and the guild has told us the money is not there in four lines and they are right, and a day is a man’s own day and a cart and a pair of beasts and an afternoon at a landing that somebody else was going to lose.

“The only way anybody finds out anything about that wall is a lead line, a boat, a man who can sound and a day. Three of those four are in a shed and in a boat and in a man of fifty-eight. The fourth one is not for sale. The fourth one is somebody’s, and it goes to whoever gives it, and the person it goes to is the person who gives it, and nobody buys one, and nobody can promise it will be there next week.

“Cleve Gannock gave one on the fifth of the eighth tide. That was four days ago and it cost him a cart, a pair of beasts and his own afternoon at a landing and he was two hours late and told the man there it was the beasts, and nobody thanked him, and he said it in about nine words in a lane and he said *you are not going to ask me* before anybody had asked him anything at all.”

Nobody at the head of that lane said anything at all, and a man of fifty-four on a chain of nineteen years had a piece of information in his own hands and could not think what to do with it.

“He gave it once,” said Corrie Tallow. “He is not going to give it twice, and he is right, and I know he is right because I would not give it twice either and I am the one writing this down.

“And nobody has asked him for a second one. That is the cost, and I want it in the open, and it is not anybody being unkind and it is not the guild being unkind and it is not that man with the bad leg. Nobody asked him and nobody is going to. A man gives a day once and after that a day is a day he has already spent, and that is not a stingy thing, that is a man who has worked out what his own day is worth to him, which is more than anybody on this coast has done about anything.

“And so that wall is not going to be sounded again by anybody this season, and that is the whole of what my no has bought, and it has bought it for free, and I would like somebody to write down that it was free, because in about six weeks one of you is going to be standing in this lane telling somebody it was the worst thing anybody ever did here.”

“So the wall has got a number in a shed and it has not got a claim, and it is going to keep on not having a claim, and the eleven yards is going to go on going, and the only thing that has changed since the fifth is that we know why, out loud, in the open, with about nine people hearing it, and nobody is going to write it down.”

A boy of twenty-two with a board under his arm was standing about six feet off and had a brush in his cuff, and he is Jory Vance, and he has been on that counter four years and writes down what happened while it is happening, and he is the only person in that building who does not know what Bettany Rook wants either.

He made the entry standing up at the chain in the ordinary way with the sea coming off the flat round him, and it went on the board and the board went into the counter drawer, and there is no name at the end of any line of it and there is not going to be one, because that is the custom and the custom is four hundred years old and it is right.

> IN THE LANE AT THE HEAD OF THE FLAT ON THE SECOND DAY OF THE NINTH TIDE, AT THE THIRD BELL, THE MAN ON THE CHAIN ASKED WHETHER THE PEOPLE WHO GO OVER THE FLAT WOULD STAND BEHIND A CLAIM ABOUT ELEVEN YARDS OF THE NORTH WALL. HE ASKED IT IN THE OPEN AND HE ASKED IT ONCE. NOBODY OBJECTED. THE ANSWER GIVEN WAS NO, AND THE REASON GIVEN WAS THAT A PARTY IS NOT A SILENCE, AND THAT A BODY CANNOT STAND BEHIND A CLAIM ABOUT A ROAD, AND THAT NO PERSON ON THIS COAST ANSWERS FOR ANOTHER PERSON ABOUT A CROSSING. A DAY WAS NAMED AS THE COST OF THE ANSWER, AND A DAY WAS NAMED AS HAVING BEEN GIVEN ON THE FIFTH OF THE EIGHTH TIDE BY A MAN WITH A CART, AND NOBODY HAS THANKED HIM FOR IT. THE WALL IS NOT CHANGED BY THE ANSWER.

That is a sheet of paper in a drawer a mile and a flight of stairs up a hill and it says about a hundred and fifty words and the name of no person, and it is the only record of the most important thing anybody has said on that flat in six weeks, and there is no name at the end of any line of it, and there never will be, and that is not an oversight and it is not caution.

Because a name at the end of that line would be a person standing behind it, and a person who has said nothing out loud is not standing behind anything, and a clerk in a building two hundred miles up this coast would read a name there and enter it and go on down the page, and in about a year somebody would go looking for the minute that made this and would not be able to find the person, and would put the minute back.

A man of thirty-one was out of the wind behind a stack of shingle baskets about thirty feet off, on the dry side of the lane, with his weight on his good side, and had heard the whole of it and had worked out, standing there in the cold in about ninety seconds, that the thing that had just been refused was the only door on this coast that had ever been built for a thing that was not the water, and that it had been built by nobody and shut by a woman of forty-eight who sells shingle and gets four shillings and twopence a day and is owed nothing, and that it was correct.

He had five pages in a penny exercise book in his coat and there was no sixth. He had known there was no sixth since a fortnight back and had not found one, and on the sixth of the seventh tide a woman had told him out loud in front of seventy people that he did not spend it on the water, and he had not spent it on the water then either.

There was a sheet from a woman four hundred miles behind him folded into the back of that book, and it had two questions on it in her own hand, which was a hand he had asked her not to write in, and one of the two questions was about consent, and it had reached him in a city four hundred miles from any coast, and it was the reason he had understood what a refusal was, standing at a counter, in about four minutes, on a day four hundred miles behind him that he could not have got back to in his life.

It had not helped. It had handed him the name of the thing a long way before the thing itself came up this coast, and a name is not the thing, and he had spent a fortnight on this coast finding that out the slow way, from a woman with a slate on her knee, at half the volume she used to say things at.

At the ninth bell the water went off the flat and left the shingle a shade darker than it had been, and the chain came up and went into its sockets, and the sixth crossing went over, and a boy of eleven went over at a penny with a hand-lead in his fist, and a woman of forty-eight wrote the sixth down and nothing else.

And a claim about eleven yards of the middle of a wall went forward up this coast with nobody’s name at the end of any line of it, and it is going to be carried by three people who were not on that flat, and not one of them has been asked.
