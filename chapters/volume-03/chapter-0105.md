# Chapter 105: Thirty-One Seats and a Day Off the Water

The third of the tide, the second bell, in a net loft with the doors open on both ends, because a body of thirty-one that shuts its doors in the first week of a tide is a body that stops turning up, and Slackwater shuts them for four days of every seven in the middle of the season, and the people who make the meeting happen are the people who have to be outside in the weather while it happens.

Pether Voke had lost a tide before he started and said so first, which nobody in the loft expected and which changed the temperature of the room more than anything that was said afterwards.

“My lighter is over on the sixth with two men on her,” he said, “and I am in here, and so is the sixth tide, and that is eleven pounds of freight and two men’s day, and I have said it out loud in front of thirty-one people so that if anybody in this room decides I did it for the story of it later on, they will find I did it in the open, before I had said a word of it, before I knew which way I was going.” He put his hand on the trestle. “Carry on.”

Then he said, “And one thing before the man says his piece, because he has not asked and I am going to answer it so he does not have to stand there and be told later.

“Who sent you? Nobody. I asked him yesterday on the water and he told me the truth, which is that a woman with a stall walked eleven miles to find out whether there was a man in a city who could look at a document and find where it was bolted to what. And that a counsel of nineteen years said her the name of that man for no reason he gave her, in a lane, and that neither of them is going to be paid and neither of them is a party to anything.

“And there is a court eleven miles up that road. I know about it. Every body on this coast knows about it the way you know about weather. It has a tray and a clerk and it can do one thing, which is put a mark on a sheet of paper, and it has no power over a strait, and there is no instrument in this Reach or in yours or in the city that can make anybody at this counter do anything they have not decided to do. If you have come here to say that a court is going to make them hear you, then you have come nine miles by water on a bad leg for nothing, and I would rather you spent the rest of the morning on the flat with a stick and a board, because that is where the good days are.”

“Yes,” said Ilyan Vester. “All of that is right. None of it is why I am here.”

“Then say the real thing.”

He said the real thing. He had said it twice already in a loft with nine people in it and he said it a third time to thirty-one, and he said it in the same words, which he had decided on the boat and which he was now sorry of, and he got to the end of it and stopped, and the tariff and the thirty-nine tides and the thirteen times were in the room inside about four minutes.

Then Pether Voke stood up and said no.

“I am going to say it once, in the open, and then I am going to sit down, and then anybody in this room may say anything they like about it, and I will hear all of it and I will not take a word of it back, and at the end I will ask whether I am to take the answer to the next sitting or whether it dies here, and that is the whole of the procedure and it took me four years to write it down and I am not going to be improved out of it in this room.”

“Slackwater is not in this matter.”

He let that stand for a moment, on purpose, the way you let a nail go into a board before you hit it again.

“I have said the word *we* four times in eleven years and three of them were about a roof,” said Pether Voke, “and I have never said *we* about anything that is not a roof because the last time I did, we were served, and there is a book in this loft with four dates in it and four sums, and the third one is a woman called Merrow Vaile whose hand was on a bollard for nine days and whose papers are in the creek at the bottom of the water stairs and were sworn out again out of her own memory because we were allowed to do that.

“You have come here to ask me whether Slackwater will consent to being a party to a matter about a strait and about a flat, and I will tell you the truth about consent, which I have had eleven years to think about and which nobody in a city ever tells you.

“Consent is not a yes or a no. Consent is a door with a person in it, and a person in a door can be moved, and everybody on this coast knows exactly how our doors get moved, because the way you get moved is that somebody comes and stands in the doorway and it is awkward for you and bad for them and worse for everybody, and so you say the thing that ends the standing. That is not cowardice. I am fifty-four and I have three brothers' worth of water to keep and I have watched men agree to things in doorways for thirty-one years and it never once went well for the man who agreed.

“And I will give you the real reason, and it is not the day.

“If the Reach and the flat and Slackwater are in one matter, then one matter ends in one answer. That is what a matter is. And in one answer there is one way the water behaves and one price for a tide and one set of days, and the guild's price for a tide is a penny and ours is a day. You cannot have both in one room. Somebody in that room is going to be wrong in public about the water, and it is not going to be the guild, because the guild has nineteen years in it and a pane on a wall, and it is not going to be you, because you are a man off a road with a leg and everything anybody knows about you here happened in two days.

“So it is us. It is Slackwater, in front of thirty-one people, holding the wrong end of the only water any of us can use, because we are the ones who can be moved.” He was still standing. “And after it is over — and it will be over, and the answer will be written down and it will be read out at this table in four years by a person we have not met — Slackwater will be a body that was in a matter about the water and was wrong. That is what we would have bought with a day. Not a door. That.”

He sat down.

---

And then he let three people at him and Ilyan Vester was one of them, and he was the worst of the three and he knew it inside the first sentence.

He stood up and said that consent was the whole of it, and that the guild had not asked this assembly either, and that the same door was being stood in, and Pether Voke said, “The guild has a pane on a wall and a man in it who has been right about the water for nineteen years, and you have a man in a doorway, and I am not being clever. I am saying that the man in the doorway has nothing behind him and the man with the pane has everything behind him, and only one of us is going to be moved and it is not going to be the one with the wall.”

He said that if the serving could be done by somebody else, the day would not be Pether Voke's, and Pether Voke said, “It cannot. It has to be a person of this Reach, and it has to be a person the assembly will take a paper off, and there is me and there is a man of sixty-three who cannot read at sea, and the man of sixty-three was served in the second year and it took him two days and he has never sat in this loft again and he is not angry, he is finished. So it is me. That is the whole of your offer and I have had it before from a man with a seal.”

And then he said the true thing, which was that a tariff which did not name the days was not safe, and the flat was nine hundred yards of shingle with a channel down the middle of it, and the Hask boy was on it nine times a day on the three days the water was worst, at a penny, and that was the right tariff to bring to this room and it ought to be brought, and Slackwater would stand behind it and say so in any room that would have him, and there is a man off a road four hundred miles away who cannot go round it and cannot be sent for it, and the guild has known for eleven years that nobody local can be, and will not staff it.

Pether Voke listened to all of that. He listened to the end of it.

“Aye,” he said. “All of that is true and I have said most of it myself inside this building. And you have found the one true thing in a two-day visit that nine years of us have not, and I am not going to pretend otherwise, and it changes nothing at all.

“Because you are asking us to be a party so that you can use us. And when this is over there will be something in a book with Slackwater written at the bottom of it, and the thing at the bottom of it will be whatever you had to have in order to get the flat in, and I have no way on this coast to look inside a book before I sign it, because the only book in this Reach that anybody can read in is the one on the wall of that guild house and you cannot read it either, and I have known that about you since the second day.”

The room did not make a sound. Thirty-one people, and a man of fifty-four sitting at the front of a trestle table with his hands flat on it.

“So no,” said Pether Voke. “And I want it recorded that I was asked and I understood and I said no, and not that I did not understand.”

---

The System put the refusal in front of Ilyan Vester on the third of a tide, at the second bell, in a net loft with the doors open at both ends, and it was a record of a thing that was not entered anywhere in particular — a thing said out loud in a room, on the back of a fishmonger's notice, and read twice to the room before it was given away.

**A MATTER IN TWO PLACES WAS ASKED FOR IN A LOFT AT THE THIRD OF A TIDE. THE SECOND PLACE WAS REFUSED IN THE OPEN BY A PERSON OF THAT PLACE, WHO WAS TOLD HE WAS BEING ASKED AND WHAT IT COST BEFORE HE ANSWERED, AND WHO SAID THE COST OUT LOUD BEFORE HE BEGAN.**
**ENTERED: THAT IT COST A TIDE, ELEVEN POUNDS OF FREIGHT AND TWO MEN'S DAY, AND THAT HE WAS NOT TALKED OUT OF IT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHAT A MATTER PUT IN ONE PLACE COSTS THE OTHER PLACE, AND ON WHOSE BACK IT NOW SITS.**

The assembly's own practice is that a refusal is written on the back of the notice and read out at the next sitting, and Pether Voke asked for it to be written and then asked who would write it, and Efa Lund, thirty-four, who keeps the shore's count of what crosses the flat and has kept it eleven years in a book of her own, was the only person in the loft who could do it, and she said so, and he asked her, and she said yes.

She wrote it on the back of a notice that was a fishmonger's, and she read it out twice to the room as the assembly's practice requires, and then she turned it round and gave it to him, and he was the man it was refusing.

“It is on a fishmonger’s,” she said. “It is not in your book and it is not in mine and it is not in any clerk’s hands in a city four hundred miles off, because there is no clerk in this Reach and there is no book in this Reach and I have been standing in this room for thirty-five minutes watching a piece of cardboard get the only record it is ever going to get. Read it and take it with you. It is no use to anybody. That is not a complaint. That is what it is for.”

---

He read it twice on the steps outside with the doors open behind him, and the number of things it cost was four, and he had three of them on his own sheet already.

Eleven pounds of freight. Two men’s day. Four years of a woman who wanted a thing written down, which is the whole of what she walked eleven miles for and it is not one year. And a boy’s thirty-nine tides, which is still there, which has not gone anywhere, which is outside the matter from the moment the door closed, because a tariff on a road is not in a matter about a strait and never was, and he had known that on the first day and had come nine miles by water to see whether a body would let him put it in anyway.

Corrie Tallow had said it. *A matter in one place about a strait is not enough.*

She had not said *and that is the only road there is.*

Pether Voke came out and stood on the step and put his hand on the wall the way you do to a building you have to row back to.

“Two things and then I have to go and be wrong in front of two men about a tarpaulin,” he said. “The first is that I have not said no to you. I have said no to the matter. Those are different and in a year you will be glad of the difference, and if you are not glad of the difference you were not listening, and I have given you the whole of my reasons in front of thirty-one people, which is the only way I know how to do anything.”

“And the second?”

“The second is that you will not be able to fix the flat,” said Pether Voke. “You were never going to be able to fix the flat. It is not in anything, and it has not been in anything for four hundred years, and it does not belong to the guild because the guild did not make it and it does not belong to a road because there is no road on the flat, and there is not one body on this coast that owns the nine hundred yards of shingle that your boy is on nine times a day. That is not a complaint and I have not got a remedy. I have got a lighter with a tarpaulin on her and eleven pounds gone.”
