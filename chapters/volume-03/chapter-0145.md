# Chapter 145: On the Wall

The fourth of the ninth tide is a bar day and the flat takes nine, and the sea outside had come up over the crown of the bar some time in the night and stayed there, and by the second bell the whole two hundred yards of gravel at the north end was gone under and there were seven carts on that flat and about seventy people on nine hundred yards of shingle, because everybody on this coast knows where everybody stands on a fourth.

At about the fourth bell Bettany Rook came down off that hill, a mile and a flight of stairs above the crossing, with a board under her arm and her hood up. It is what she does. She looks at the near end, she walks round to the far stones, and she goes back up, and it has been her ordinary morning for six years and nobody on that shingle has ever once made a thing of it.

The stone at the head of the flat was not bare. It is bare five days in seven and this was one of the two, and the harbourmaster does not know the seventh and does not know that she has not been on it either, and about fourteen people within earshot of that lane worked out inside a minute that it was the second day in a week the stone was there and nobody was standing on it, and not one of them said a word about it then or afterwards, because this coast does not discuss a door it has decided to walk past.

She did not go round the far stones. She went up the lane four hundred yards to the north end instead, and about nine people saw her do it and about four of them worked out why inside about a minute, and the reason was the ordinary reason and it is the whole of her job: the wall is out of the water on a fourth, a sixth and a seventh, and those are three days in seven, and a harbourmaster who has not looked at a wall on one of the three days in seven in nineteen years has not been looking at a wall.

Halse Tarrant was at the inner end with a bar in his hand, walking the top course stone by stone and putting the flat of it on every stone he went over. There were five people on the top course at the outer end and one of them had a basket.

The leg went before she got to the wall. He had been on wet shingle at the north end for about nine minutes, looking at eleven yards of the middle of a face from the shore, and it went the way it goes, from the hip down, all of it, inside about four seconds, and it stayed gone for the best part of two hundred yards of standing still on wet stone and came back a minute later worse, and he did not go down, and there were about nine people on that shingle who could see him from where they were and not one of them said a word to him about it then or afterwards.

He had not cleared since the fourth of the third tide, at a fire two miles along this coast, where it had been his own for about four hours. It had not been his own in a minute of the fifty-four days he had been here, and nobody on this coast was going to be told that and nobody was going to be asked.

---

She stood at the foot of the wall on the shingle at about the fourth bell and looked up it, and she did not go out on it, and he understood about nine seconds later why and it took him a further nine seconds to understand that he was not going to be able to use it.

“I have not been on that wall,” she said. “In nineteen years. I have stood on the flat and looked at it about nine feet off and I have read about it in two panes in oak frames in a room a mile up this hill, and I have never had my boots on it, and I am fifty-one years old and I have never once in my life been on a wall that was built by a guild to hold a gravel bank against an ebb, and I am not going to be the first woman on it on a fourth with the sea standing over the crown, and you may write that down as cowardice and it is not cowardice, it is nineteen years of being the person people come to and not the person who goes out.”

“That is not what I came to ask you.”

“No,” she said. “It is not. You have come to ask me the thing you asked a woman on a shingle eight days ago, and you have walked a mile and a flight of stairs and come down a hill on a fourth, and I have watched you not say it for a whole minute, and I am going to make you say it here, at the foot of it, out loud, because you asked it out loud last time and that is the only way anything on this coast gets said.”

“Would you like to be the one standing there.”

She did not shift the board. She looked at the middle eleven yards of that face for about four seconds.

“No,” she said. “And I am going to tell you why I said no so fast, and it is not because I do not want it, and I am not going to be kind about it, and I would rather you had the true thing than the thing you came up the hill for.

“Eight days ago I stood on the shingle at the head of that flat with the stone bare at my back and said no to you in front of about forty people, and then I said the rest of it, and I said it out loud, and I have not said it to anybody since and I have not been asked it since, and you have come back down that hill to ask me the same question in a different place and I would like you to know what you have done, which is that you have made my answer of eight days ago the more expensive.

“Because I said: I would rather be a coast than a woman. And I meant it, and it is the true thing, and it is the only thing I have got, and I have never said it to anybody before you and I did not enjoy saying it and I have not regretted it and I have not gone back and looked at it in the last eight days and wondered whether I should have said it differently.

“And you have come and asked me again. Which means one of two things. Either you did not hear me, which nobody is going to believe and I am not going to pretend, or you heard me and you have come back anyway, and if it is the second thing then I would like to know what you have come back with, and I would like to know now, and I would rather have it in the open at the foot of this wall than in a room with a door shut, and I will tell you why.”

She put the board down on the shingle.

“Because a room is where this coast puts things that are about people, and a room is a place where a person is made to be a person in front of other people, and in nineteen years nobody in that building has ever asked me to stand in a particular place, and the one time I have been asked has been on a shingle and out loud, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what I have been trying to tell you for eight days.”

---

He had come a mile and a flight of stairs to be careful and he had about nine seconds to work out what to do with being handed that, and he did what he had done on a shingle eight days ago and got wrong the first time: he said the wrong thing out loud, on a bar day, at the foot of a wall, in front of about nine people.

“I am not quoting a record,” he said. “There is no record in this and I am not going to bring one down to you and I have not asked you for one twice now and I am not going to ask you a third time. I am quoting a woman, on a shingle, eight days ago, at four in the afternoon, out loud, and you said it and I heard it and there is nobody else on four hundred miles of this coast who could have said it.

“You said: I would rather be a coast than a woman. And then you said: do not write it that I stood on that stone for six years out of duty. And then you said the rest of it, and I have got the rest of it as well, and the rest of it was that standing in somebody’s habit is not the same thing as agreeing to a duty, and that if you had agreed to it you would have had to have been asked first, and nobody asked you.

“That is the whole of what I have got and I have had it in my mouth for eight days and there is nothing else in it. There is no argument about the eleven yards in it and there is no argument about a record in it and there is not a number in it, and I have not come up that hill to tell you a wall is going. The wall is going. A man with a lead line wrote that down the day before it happened and a boil went out through the middle of it on the sixth evening and nobody was in it, and you heard both of those from a man at my counter on that same evening and you asked me how many feet and I could not answer you, and I have not been up that lane since and I am not going to, because that is what this coast does to people.”

He put his hand on the back of his own neck because it was the only thing he had.

“I am here about the other half. You said nobody has ever asked you, and you said duty is a thing you agree to, and you said if you had agreed to it you would have had to be asked. And I have not come to tell you that you agreed to it. I have come to ask you.

“That is all the difference there is and it is the whole of what I have got, and I have got nothing else, and I have been on this coast fifty-four days and nobody on it has asked me for one thing and you are the first person I have asked for one and you said no in four seconds and you have told me the true reason for the no in about ninety seconds, which is more than anybody gets out of anybody anywhere and I am not going to pretend the two are the same.”

She picked the board up off the shingle.

“I do not like this,” she said. “I want that said before anything else, because I have worked out what you have done and I do not like it, and you are going to go away from this wall believing that a woman of fifty-one on a bar day has been persuaded.

“I have not been persuaded of anything. I have been asked a question I have never been asked, and I have said no to it, and I have given you the reason for the no that I have never given anybody, and that is not being persuaded. Persuaded is when somebody has talked you round. Nobody has talked me round. You have quoted me to myself in the open and it has not moved me one inch about the stone, and it is not going to move me one inch about the stone, and if you go up that hill tonight and think I have come round on that, you have come a long way for nothing and I will not be responsible for it.”

“No,” he said. “I have not thought that.”

“Good. Then hear the other half, because you have not asked me the question yet, and you have been circling it for four minutes and you are as bad at this as the last three men who have tried it on me, and one of them was a man with a very good coat.

“You have not asked me whether I would be in a thing. You have asked me whether I would like to be the one standing there, and that is a different question, and the answer to that one is the one I gave you eight days ago, and it is no, and it will be no next week, and if you ask me it at the turn on the sixth I will say it again before you get the words out.

“What you actually want is for me to put my name to a claim about a wall. And the answer to that is a different question and I have been asked it four times in nineteen years by people in good coats and I have said no four times, and three of those men were wrong about the money and one of them was wrong about me, and I am going to say no again, and I am going to tell you what I have never said out loud to anybody about why, and then you can go and be wrong about it somewhere else.”

She looked at the eleven yards in the middle of the face.

“Not because of the money, which you have not mentioned once and I notice. Not because the guild would be difficult, because the guild will be entirely correct and there is nothing in this I could win against them in a room and we both know it.

“Because the moment there is a claim about that wall with a person’s name at the end of it, that person is the person for it. And not for a season. For ever. And on this coast a person who is the person for a thing cannot stop being the person for it, because there is no second door and no other counter and about nine hundred people come to a person and not to a bench and not to a court and not to a guild. And in four months the guild will have got a perfectly good reason to put me at a counter for the remainder of my sight, and they will be right to, and I will be the woman who handed them the reason, and I am going to say a sentence here that I have said once in my life, out loud, on a shingle on the third of the eighth tide, and I am saying it again because it has not stopped being true and because you are standing here and there is a wall at the end of this lane.

“And I am not going to be argued out of it by a leg and a mile and a half of stairs and a man quoting me at myself, because that is not being argued out of anything. That is being worn down, and I am the harbourmaster of Kestrel Reach and I have been the person that nine hundred people come to for six years and I have never once been worn down, and the day I am is the day this coast finds out what it has actually been getting, and it will not be a woman of fifty-one on a wall. It will be a queue.”

Nobody at the foot of that wall said anything, and the ebb was about an hour and a quarter long and had a quarter of it left.

“So that is the no, and I have given it to you properly, in the open, on a day when you cannot get anything out of me, and I would like it recorded that I said no in the easiest possible circumstances for you and it did not change anything.

“Now. Sit down, because you have been on your feet for nine minutes and I am not going to do this standing up, and there is a stone about four hundred yards back that has been standing there empty since the first of the ninth and neither of us has said a word about it, and I am going to say the rest of this in about four minutes and then I am going to walk round that flat and go back up that hill and do four hours’ work and get it out inside the hour, and nobody in that room is going to ask me a question, and tomorrow it will be a fifth and the flat will take six.”

She sat down on the shingle at the foot of a wall that is nineteen years and eight months old with a board under her arm.

“Yes,” she said.

“You said no.”

“I said no to the stone eight days ago in front of forty people and I meant it and I am not taking it back. I am saying yes to the other thing, and you did not ask me the other thing, and I answered it anyway, and I would like it noted that I answered a question you did not ask, which is the first time in nineteen years and is not going to be a habit.”

“If I put my name to it, what would it have to say.”

“It would have to say me,” said Bettany Rook. “That is the whole of it and it is the only part I have thought about for eight days and it is why I said no for about ninety seconds just now.

“It must not say the harbourmaster of Kestrel Reach. It must not say the office at Kestrel Reach. It must not say the guild, because the guild will be right about this wall whether or not it is on a piece of paper and that is not the point either. If it says a body, then in four months somebody will write to that body, and the body will be answered by a clerk of twenty-two with a brush in his cuff, or it will not be answered, and either way a carter at the near stones on a bad morning has gone to a name and not to a person, and he will do it again in a fortnight and then he will not do it at all.

“About nine hundred people on this water come to a person, and not to anything with more than one door in it, and that is the whole of what this coast is, and it is not going to be improved by a piece of paper that has got a building on the end of it.

“So it says Bettany Rook, and it says what I said, in my own words, and it does not say what I think about the wall, because I have not been on the wall and I have not sounded it and I have not got a number and I am not going to be given somebody else’s number and put my name to it. And the burden on it has to be a person and it has to be me, and it does not go to an office and it does not go to a harbour, and when I cannot do it then it stops, and I have thought about that for eight days and I know exactly what it costs and I have decided that I would rather it stopped than that it went to a building, and I would rather be a woman about that than a coast, and you may write that down and take it away and it will not frighten me.”

She got up off the shingle and squared the board against her hip.

“That is the whole of it. There is no version of this with a building on the end of it, and I will not look at one, and you may tell whoever is writing it that the woman has been asked and has said yes to a duty and has said no to an office, and that the duty is mine and the office is nobody’s, and if that does not close the matter then the matter does not close.”

---

She went back up the lane and across the flat and round the far stones and up that hill, and got into her own building at about the seventh bell, and did four hours of figures with a clerk of twenty-two and finished inside the hour, and not one person in that room put a single question to her, and they did not afterwards either.

A man of thirty-one was at the foot of a wall at the north end at about the fifth bell with a leg that had come back worse and a sheet of a woman’s words in his head that he was not going to write down anywhere, because there was nowhere on this coast to put a thing that had happened to a woman, and about nine people had seen him stand still on wet shingle for two hundred yards and not one of them had said a word.

And at about the ninth bell the water came back over the north end and made nine foot of green water over the middle eleven yards of that wall, and the flat took its ninth crossing about four hundred yards off with the chain going up and coming down all evening, and a boy of eleven went over at a penny with a hand-lead in his fist, and a woman of forty-eight wrote the ninth down on a slate on her knee at the far stones and was paid nothing and had never once said so, and the stone at the head of the flat was standing there with nobody on it, which is the fourth time in ten days that it has stood there with nobody on it, and not one of the four was a day anybody asked for.
