# Chapter 141: The Day After

The seventh of the eighth tide is a bar day and the flat takes nine, and the westerly that had been getting up for two days came in the night with the rain behind it, and by the first bell the sea was over the crown of the bar at the north end and the whole two hundred yards of gravel was gone under, and in the middle eleven yards of a wall that is nineteen years and eight months old there was a place where the water was not going down the face.

Nobody was hurt. That is the first thing about it and it is the reason a coast of about nine hundred people got through a night about it. No bell was rung and no boat was lost and no man was in the water at any point in it, and at the fourth bell a carter said to a second carter that it was a good seventh, and the second carter said it was a good seventh, and that was the whole of what was said about it on nine hundred yards of shingle for the better part of two days.

At about the turn Halse Tarrant went out along the top of the wall with a bar in his hand. He went out because his hour and a quarter of ebb was gone and the sand had come up into that deep and filled it level, and on a fourth, a sixth and a seventh the whole of that wall stands up out of the sand, and he had about an hour before the flood came back in behind it and made nine foot of green water over the whole of it again and put every boat on this coast out of reach of it until the fourth of the next tide, which is three days off.

He went out to look at the eleven yards, and he was on it for about half an hour, and what he did on it was walk it.

“I have been over this wall every day for thirty-one years,” he said afterwards, to two men in a lane, and neither of them had asked him and one of them was about forty feet off with his weight on his good side. “And I have never once in thirty-one years had a reason to put my hand on the ninth course in the middle and find out what was under it. And I have not got one now either. I have got a bar and I have got my own eyes and I have got the fact that a boil the size of a doorway was going in that wall for about an hour last night in front of six of us, and a boil is not a thing water does at a wall that is sound.”

He put the bar down on the top course and left it there.

“So I will tell you what it is and I will tell you the whole of it now while it is fresh, because in about a week there will be a version of this in three yards and none of them will be this one.

“The gravel behind that wall is two feet. Two feet is the whole of that point. That wall is not holding the sea up. It is holding two feet of gravel sideways against an ebb that comes out of a hole the ebb cut itself, and where the bed has gone out from under the bottom course the water comes through the wall now instead of past the end of it, and the first thing it does on the far side of that is take gravel. That is what a breach is for. That is the entire trade of it. A breach is not a hole in a wall, it is a hole in a gravel bank with a wall in front of it, and the wall was never going to stop the water, the wall was only ever going to keep the gravel where the water wanted to take it, and there is about eleven yards of that face where there is nothing to keep it with.

“Which is a shore going, and I have said so and I will say so again, and the guild was right, and I want that said by me and in the open and not once in the next year by anybody else. They put two hundred and sixty yards of stone on that shoulder in a season of about seven weeks with eight men because the water had already taken a hut and a boat slip, and the danger was real and it is there this morning and it will be there in ten years. Take that wall out and you have got no north end in three winters. I would say so to anybody who asked me today including a man in a building two hundred miles up this coast.”

Nobody said anything for a bit, and the sea went on doing what it does three days a week.

“And I said last night and I will say it now and I have not slept, so you may take it or leave it. A breach in the foot of a wall is not a morning’s work with a cart. That is the part I want said and it is the part nobody is going to want. I have been on this wall thirty-one years and I have never had to say it and nobody has ever asked me: I cannot get to it.

“Not because I am old. I am fifty-eight and I can sound to a foot inside of forty yards and I have a boat of my own that has not been out in about a year and I have a lead line in that shed that the guild put there when they laid the stone and has hung there ever since and nobody has ever asked for it. I have got three of the four things and I have had three of them since about the sixth year.

“The fourth thing is a day, and a day is not mine, and that is not a complaint, that is arithmetic. But that is not even the thing that is stopping me and I want to be exact because I have watched about four of you not understand it and one of you is going to go and try it.

“A hole in the foot of a wall in nine foot of water is not a morning with a cart and a barrow of stone. It is nine foot of water on a good day and there is nothing on this coast with wheels on that comes near it. And on a bad day — on a fourth, a sixth or a seventh, which are three days in seven — the sand comes out of that deep and the wall stands up out of it and you can walk a flat boat over the top of the whole of it. Which sounds like the answer. It is not the answer, and here is why, and it is the whole of why I am stood in a lane telling you this instead of in a boat.

“The ebb on those three days runs about an hour and a quarter, and it runs hard, and it comes out of that deep sideways, because the sea outside is on the seaward shoulder and the two of them are close together, and the deep is a hole the ebb has cut. If you are on the seaward face of that wall when the ebb starts, you are on the wrong side of the wall from the only water there is to get off on, and in about an hour and a quarter the water is going out through the middle eleven yards of the wall at about a doorway wide and it is not going out past you. It is going out where you are standing. And that hole holds about eleven times in twenty, and the nine is a man in nine foot of water, and it would be his fault for being on the wrong side of a wall on a day when the water is standing over the crown.”

He picked the bar up off the top course.

“So it wants somebody who has done it, and there is exactly one person on this coast who has done it, and she is not in this lane and she was not in this yard last night, and nobody has been to ask her, because a man who keeps a wall does not go and ask a person for a boat.”

A woman of thirty-six was at the north end with a boat on the hard and a towrope over her shoulder, and that is Sorrel Gannock, and of the three brothers on this coast there is exactly one who will take a flat boat across that shallow with the sea standing up over the crown, and it is her, and she has been doing it since she was twenty-five because her father did it before her and he is dead, and nobody has ever paid her for an hour of it.

She had been out in that deep twice the evening before and once in the morning, and she came in past the yard at about the seventh bell with the warp over her shoulder and the boat laid up on the shingle above the slip, and a man of thirty-one was standing at the head of the slip with his weight on his good side.

He said it out loud, in the open, at the north end, in front of about nine people who could hear all of it, and it took about four seconds and it was not a speech and there was nothing in front of him.

“Would you like to be the one standing there.”

She put the warp down on the shingle.

“Yes,” she said. “I have been waiting about two days for somebody to ask me that and I would like you to know that I am not going to pretend I have not.”

Nobody said anything. She went on sorting a coil that did not need sorting.

“Now I am going to tell you what that costs and I am telling you because you are stood there and I have not got anybody else to tell, and I am not being kind about it and I am not unkind either.

“A man with a lead line and a boat can sound it. A man with a boat can go under it on a seventh if the seventh behaves, and a seventh behaves about twice in five, and the rest of them is a man in nine foot with the man being whoever is holding the rope. That is one day. One day of a man with a boat, on a day the sea owns the bar, and the day is not mine because I have nine of them and the nine are not for this.

“And that is the whole of it and the whole of it is not the boat. It is that if I put my name to this then there is a thing on this coast with my name at the bottom of it, and I have had eleven years of a thing nobody has asked me the name of, and a name on a wall is not a wage. It is not a wage and it does not pay for oil. What it does is make a person able to point at me and say, that one, she was the one, and there is a day coming when that will be worth something to somebody and a day coming when it will cost me something, and I would rather have it on the day I chose it than have it put there by a man who needed a boat.”

She picked the warp up again.

“Yes,” she said. “To the asking. Not to being sent for. You are going to have to come back and ask me the next thing as well, and I am telling you now that the next thing is the thing I will want to be asked and not the thing I will be given, and I do not know what it is yet and I will tell you when I do.”

A man of fifty was standing at the mouth of the slip with a slip of his own a long way inside the gravel and had heard the whole of it, and that is Pell Cawley, who has made and mended flat-bottomed boats on that hard since before Ilyan Vester was born, and he put his plane down on the hull he was working on when she started talking and picked it up again when she stopped, and he did not say one word, because he is a man who has been at that hard for thirty-one years and it is not his wall and it is not his boat.

At about the ninth bell the flood came back over the north end and began to turn that sand out of the deep again, and by morning there would be nine foot of green water over the middle eleven yards of that wall and no way at all to see the bottom of it, and there would be no cart, and no boat, and no person on this coast with a number in a book about it that anybody in a room would have to take.

On the flat there were nine crossings. The chain went up at the second bell and came up and went down all morning, and about seventy people were on that shingle because it is a seventh and everybody on this coast knows where everybody stands on a seventh. A cart of deals went over and came back for a second load. A woman with a handcart of shingle went at the third bell and was owed a penny for it and took it. A man of thirty stood at the head of the lane with his back on the door of a lamp-oil shed and looked at the water for a while and said nothing, and shut the door at the ninth bell, and a boy of eleven went over for the ninth time at a penny with the lead in his fist, and a woman of forty-eight was where she is on any day the flat takes nine, at the far stones, with a slate on her knee, being paid nothing and not having said so.

A man of thirty-one stood at the near end in the dark at about the ninth bell and had asked somebody a question out loud for the first time in fifty days on this coast, and the answer had been yes, and he had two more to ask and no idea what any of them would be.
