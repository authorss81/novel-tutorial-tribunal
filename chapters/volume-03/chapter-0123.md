# Chapter 123: Three Accounts and One Sentence

The net loft is up over the drying yard at the north end with a ladder out of it and a hatch, and it belongs to the flat boat and to nobody else on that coast, and it is where the towrope lives and where a woman of thirty-six keeps what she owns, and on the third of the sixth tide at the third bell the three of them were in it and the tide was coming in under the boards and could be heard about four minutes before anybody in there could see it.

Cleve had called it. That was the thing none of them expected. He had called it for the second bell and had said *I want it said in this yard where it can be heard* and had meant the yard, and instead he had put a fire in the grate and put the kettle on and sat down at the sorting table with Halloran’s letter in front of him and said that if it was going to be written down then it was going to be written down with the three of them in the room, and not one word of it was to go up the coast road until it was.

Dell had come because Sorrel was there. Sorrel had come because she had been there.

And the man with the bad leg was on the ladder at the third bell because Cleve Gannock had gone down the hard to fetch him, and Cleve Gannock had not asked him anything and had not explained anything and had said *come up*, and had gone back down.

---

He did not start well and he started well by accident, which is how most of his useful work has gone.

He asked Sorrel first, because she was nearest, and he asked her to say the whole of it in order from the first thing to the last thing and not to stop for questions, and she did it in about ninety seconds, and it was a good account.

Then he asked Dell the same thing, and Dell did it in about two minutes, and it was a good account, and it did not agree with hers on one point that both of them were certain of.

Then he asked Cleve the same thing, and Cleve did it in about four minutes because he kept qualifying it, and it was a good account, and it was the account of a man who was not there.

Then he asked all three of them the second question, which was the one that mattered, and it was the same question and he asked it in the same words to each of them, and it was: was the water over the road that morning, or was it not.

Over the flat, said Cleve. I could not tell you. From the bank it looked like an ordinary day and I have not been in that water in my life and I am not going to now.

Over it, said Dell. Every yard of the nine hundred. To the axle of the cart and over the boards of it twice.

Not at the north end, said Sorrel. Under six inches at the lowest and the lowest was about the eighth bell. And the road is four hundred yards south of where I was.

And he asked the third question, the same words again, and it was: where was the channel that morning.

I have no idea and it is not my trade, said Cleve.

Out of the low, said Dell. It had come out about three weeks before and it was running the whole width of the flat and you could have named the banks of it.

Across the shallow and not along it, said Sorrel. Under six inches. And not making anything new at the north end at all, because it has not made anything new there since before I was in this boat.

---

He had it inside about four minutes and he did not put it down straight away, and the reason he did not put it down straight away is that it was the best-shaped thing he had found in a month, and it was this.

Three accounts. One thing they were all sure of. And a test.

That is the shape of a thing that can be closed. He had spent six years in a city being paid to find that shape and he had never once found it on this coast, because everything on this coast was a song or a post or a pane or a man with a hammer, and none of them could be closed. And here, on the third day of the sixth tide, in a net loft over a drying yard, with a fire in the grate and the tide coming in under the floor, three people who did not agree about anything had put three things on the table and all three of them were about one piece of water and one morning, and if you asked the one question that narrowed it, the answer would either be the same from three directions or it would not, and either way the thing would be a finding.

The question was: was the shallow at the north end crossable that morning, and by what.

The exchange went on for another twenty minutes and it was the only twenty minutes of the morning in which anybody on that coast sounded like a person arguing with another person, and Ilyan Vester did not take a word of it in, because there was nothing in it but the three of them doing the thing they had come to do.

Dell wanted it settled that a cart cannot cross a shingle flat with the channel out of the low. He could not say how he knew it and he did not want to say anything else, because the moment he said anything else it became a thing men argued with, and he was forty-two and he had been in that water since he was nine.

Sorrel wanted it settled that a boat is a boat. She was extremely clear about the difference and she was entirely right and she made it four times in four different ways, and on the fourth Dell said *you are not taking a cart over it and you know you are not*, and she said *I have never once said I was*, and that was the end of that and they both sat down.

Cleve wanted one thing and he wanted it the whole time and it sat in the middle of the loft like a fourth person: he wanted the thing his father did put in a place where it would be still there. He said, twice, in nearly the same words, that it did not matter what happened that morning, because a man two days up the coast road was going to write something on a piece of paper in a book and everything a man writes is decided before he sits down, and if the three of them went on for another hour the thing that got written would be the thing he had wanted since he put the letter in his coat.

Nobody told him to shut up, because he was right about that too, and being right about that is the most expensive thing a man can be in a room where three accounts are being taken down.

He put the fire in with the poker and asked Cleve whether there was any paper.

“There is half a school exercise book,” said Cleve. “I have had it since I was eleven and it is the back of it and there is nothing in the back of it.”

---

He wrote the three accounts down in the order he had taken them, in full, in his own hand, on the last leaf of a pad of writing paper that had come out of the back of his coat, with each of them under its own name, and he read each one back to the person it belonged to, which is a thing he has done all his life at counters and which nobody in that loft remarked on.

Then he wrote the one sentence at the top of the page.

> ON THE MORNING IN QUESTION THE CHANNEL WAS NOT IN THE LOW. A CART WENT OVER THE WATER AT THE NORTH END AND CAME OFF DRY. THE WATER WAS UNDER SIX INCHES.

He looked at it for a while. It was a good sentence. It was short, it was plain, it was the kind of sentence a man writes in a letter when he wants a thing to be believed at a distance, and it was made out of three true sentences by three people in the room, and there was no join in it anywhere that a person could put a finger into, and he was quite sure of that, and he had looked.

Sorrel Gannock had got up off the bench without making any noise and had come round behind him and read it over his shoulder, and she read it twice.

“No,” she said.

“It is all in the room.”

“No,” said Sorrel. “It is all in the room and it is not one of the three things anybody said, and I want to tell you exactly what you have done and then I want you to do what I am going to ask you and I am not asking for the whole of the loft to hear it.

“You have taken three people’s water and you have put it all in one place. My six inches was over a gravel bar two hundred yards wide with a boat on it, and it was in a boat, and it was in the channel, and it was at the lowest of the ebb on a good day with no sea standing. Dell’s not-in-the-low was about a shingle flat nine hundred yards long with a cart in it up to the axle, and there was four feet of water across the middle of it and the water was not six inches, and nobody in this loft has said the words *four feet*. And Cleve’s cart came off the flat in the ordinary way at the crossing four hundred yards south of where either of us was, and it came off a flat that was under an inch of water, and it came off with the water about ankle deep on a good day, and not one inch of that is the same water as my six inches.” She put her hand flat on the back of the bench. “So put my six inches where I said it, or do not put it in at all.”

“I am putting it where you said it.”

“Then it is not the sentence you have written at the top,” said Sorrel Gannock, “and you and I both know it is not, and I would like you to be careful, because I have not given you a hard time and I am not going to.”

And she sat down on the bench again and let it go, and the letting go was the worst part of the morning, and he noticed it and did not understand it for about a day and a half.

---

He finished the page in nine minutes.

Under the sentence he put the three accounts in full, each one under its own name, and under those he put the date, which was the third of the sixth tide, and the two questions, and the fact that the three of them had all been asked the same words. And there was no reason on the page, and there had been no reason on the page for three pages now, and the pad it was on was in the inside pocket of his coat with a sheet of writing paper from a city four hundred miles behind him that had two questions on it in a hand he had asked her not to write in.

Then he tore the sheet out along the perforation and gave it to Cleve Gannock, who read it standing up, in the ordinary way, and who read it twice.

“That is what I wanted,” said Cleve.

“That is a true account of three things that happened,” said Ilyan Vester, “with your brother’s water in it and your sister’s water in it, and the sentence at the top is made out of the three of them and nothing else. It is not wrong. I want to say that. Every part of it is in that room and none of it came from me.”

“I do not need it to be wrong,” said Cleve Gannock. “I need it to be short.”

And he went down the ladder with the page in his hand and put it inside his coat, and the ladder went up and down about four times after that and then stopped, and the three of them stayed in the loft and did not say anything about it, and the tide came in under the boards and the water in the loft’s bilge tin went up about an inch and nobody mentioned that either.

---

He sat down on the bottom step of the ladder with his back against the post and worked out that he was pleased.

It had taken nine minutes. It had cost nothing: a page out of a book he had paid for, and nine minutes, and a leg that was no worse for standing still on a step, and nobody’s name anywhere that could be used against anybody. He had three accounts that did not agree, and one question that narrowed them, and a finding that could be carried two hundred miles up a coast road in a coat pocket and be believed, and the woman who had asked him not to had asked him to leave out one phrase and he had left out the one phrase and done the rest of it exactly as she had said.

He had not sold anybody. He had not asked anybody for anything. In thirty-two days on that coast he had not once been sent for, and he had gone into a net loft at the third bell because a man he had met for two days had said *come up*, and he had come out of it with the only thing he knew how to make, and it had taken nine minutes, and it had not cost a soul a thing.

Then he got up off the step, and went down the ladder, and the leg went on him at the second rung, and he stood at the bottom of it in the cold for a minute and a half while it came back worse and did not go.
