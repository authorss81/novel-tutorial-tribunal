# Chapter 137: Would You Like to Be the One Standing There

A good day on the third of the eighth tide and the flat takes six, and the stone at the head of the flat was bare, because the stone is bare five days in seven and this was one of the five.

Bettany Rook came down the hill at about the fourth bell with a board under her arm and a hood up, because the office is a mile and a flight of stairs above the crossing and she had come down to see the near end of a flat that takes six and then to walk round the far stones and go back up, which is what a harbourmaster does on a good day and has done for six years and is not a thing anybody on the flat remarks on.

Ilyan Vester was standing at the near end with his weight on his good side, and he had been standing at the near end since the first bell, and he had worked out during the second hour that if he was going to do it he was going to do it in daylight with people about, because doing it in an office would be a conversation and doing it on a seventh when the stone is bare would be a complaint, and there was one in a week of them when a woman of fifty-one came down a hill in daylight past about forty people, and he was not going to wait for the next one.

She was about forty yards off when he got in front of her, and she stopped, and she had a board under her arm and a hood up and six weeks of him on this coast behind her, and she knew what he was before he said it, which was unpleasant in a way he had not expected.

“No,” she said. “Before you say it. No.”

“You have not heard it.”

“You have had it in your mouth since about the sixth of a tide,” she said, “and I have watched you not say it to me for four days, and you are going to ask me whether I would like to be the one standing on that stone, and every person on this coast has known that was coming for about a year and not one of them has asked me either. You may ask it. I said no before you asked it and I will say it again after you have asked it, and I will say it out loud here where about forty people can hear me, because that is the only way anything on this coast gets said and you are not going to be the one who breaks that.”

Forty people were not hearing it. There were about fourteen within earshot and nine of those had the whole of it, and not one of the nine said a word to anybody about what had just been asked, then or afterwards, which is what a coast does about a thing it has decided to treat as a habit.

“I would like to ask you it properly,” he said, “because you have told me twice in a month that you are not going to invent a reason for me, and I am not going to ask you for one now.”

“Then ask it.”

“Would you like to be the one standing on it.”

She shifted the board under her arm.

“No,” she said. “And I am going to answer you properly, in the open, at four in the afternoon, and then I am going to walk round the far stones and go back up that hill, and you may write all of it down.”

---

“There are two halves to that and I have watched you get ready for one of them and it is not the one that is going to happen.

”The first half is the one you are expecting. You are expecting me to say that the stone is worth what the person on it is worth, and that the moment I am not standing on it, it is four feet of flat stone and a hundred years of people respecting a habit, and that a habit can go out of use the way a path goes out of use, and that I have been standing on the coast’s habit and not on anything else, and that you have had in your mouth since the sixth of a tide. You said the first half of it to me in a yard in the fifth and I told you to go and stand at the far end of something, and you have been working up to saying the rest of it ever since.

”That is all true. All of it. I have been the only person on four hundred miles of this coast that a person can be answered by for six years, and take me off that stone and it is four feet of flat stone and about a hundred years of people respecting a habit, and the guild has never interfered with it in a hundred years and never will, and that is the whole of what it is worth and I know it.

”And it is the half that does not decide anything, and I want to say so before you say the rest, because you are a man who has spent a month on this coast learning that the shape of a thing is not the thing, and you have learned it four times and got it wrong three.”

“Then the other half.”

“The other half is that I would not give it up, and that is not what you thought either, and I am not going to let you put it down in a place where it looks like a smallness.

”I have been in that office nineteen years. In nineteen years nobody in that building has asked me for one thing. Not one. I am the harbourmaster and I have been handed four panes’ worth of a coast and a counter and a girl of twenty-two who is good at sums, and I have never once been asked by anybody to stand in a particular place. The stone is the only thing on this coast that has ever happened to me, and what happened was that nobody made me do it. You can stand on a thing for six years and never once have anybody ask you about it, and the whole of what that gives you is that nobody can ever say you agreed to it.

”And if I stand down from it, then in about a month there will be a man on a bar road four miles along this coast who is going to find out what happens to a road with nobody on it, and he is not going to find it out from a book. He is going to find it out the way a carter finds it out, and it will be a person, and it will not be a rule, and I am not going to be the reason it happens because a man from a city asked me a question about my own life on a shingle at four in the afternoon.”

---

“And now the part you are actually going to write down, and I want it in your own words and not in mine, and if you put mine in I will know.

”I do not want to be a person doing it. I want it to keep being the coast.

”As long as it is the coast, it is a hundred years of everybody respecting a habit, and I am standing in the middle of that habit, and nobody has to be brave and nobody has to be asked and no day comes when a woman of fifty-one with one eye has to say to a room that she is going to stop. The day somebody asks is the day it stops being the coast and starts being a woman on a stone. And then it is one woman. And one woman goes blind in the eye she has got and then she is tired, and then she is a person in a chair in a building a mile and a flight of stairs above a flat, and the stone is bare for about a year while somebody works out what to do, and in that year a man on a bar road finds out.

”I would rather be a coast than a woman. That is not a noble thing to say. It is the true thing and it is the whole of what I have got, and I have never said it out loud to anybody and I am saying it to a man who has no standing on this coast at the near end of a flat at four in the afternoon, and I would like him to go and stand at the far end of something.”

She shifted the board again.

“And the second thing, and this is the one you will get wrong if you are careful and I am telling you now so that you are careful in the wrong direction instead.

”Do not write it that I stood on that stone for six years out of duty. That is the worst of the four ways it could be written and a person in a book two hundred miles off is going to write it that way in about a year because it is the one that makes sense to a person who has never been here. Standing in somebody’s habit is not duty. Duty is a thing you agree to. I never agreed to it, and if I had agreed to it I would have had to be asked, and nobody asked me, and that is the whole of what happened to me in six years in the chair I am in.”

---

He stood on the shingle and worked out that he had been wrong, and said so, out loud, on a good day, on the ground, in front of about nine people who could hear it, which is the fourth time in a fortnight he has been wrong in front of strangers and the first time he has said so before he was shown.

“I have had it in my mouth for a week that you were standing on a habit and that a habit is a thing that can stop, and that is why I wanted you to say yes, and I was wrong about the order of it and about the size of it.

”I want to say the wrong part out loud because you are not going to get a chance to correct it later, and because I have been wrong about the same shape of thing on this coast three times and every one of the three times I put it in a building and got told, and I would like it noticed that I have noticed.”

“Noticed by me,” she said, “and by about nine people on a shingle, and it makes no difference whatever, and I have told a glassmaker that and he said it to me first.”

“No,” he said. “You have not told a glassmaker that. I have. And you are right about the other half, and I am going to say the true half of the wrong thing and then we are going to walk round the far stones, because you came down that hill to look at a flat that takes six and I have taken your morning.

”You are not standing in a habit. A habit is what people do without anybody asking. You are the person in it, and the habit is the hundred years of everybody else, and I have had those the wrong way round in my mouth since the sixth of a tide, and the difference is the whole of the difference, and it is the difference between a woman doing a job and a coast doing a thing with a woman standing in it.

”And I was wrong about the other half too, and I was wrong about it in the other direction, which I did not think was possible. I had it that you were standing there by a hundred years of habit and that a hundred years of habit was the fragile part. The hundred years of habit is the strong part. It has outlasted every record anybody on this coast has ever made, including three glass ones, and it went on while a man of sixty-one sat on a bench two miles along this coast being right about photographs, and it is the only durable thing out here and it has never once been in a room.”

“That,” said Bettany Rook, “is the first thing anybody has said to me about it, and I am going to walk round the far stones now and I am not going to answer it, and you may have noticed that we have not agreed about a single thing in the last six minutes and we are still both going to be here tomorrow.”

“Yes,” he said. “I did notice.”

They walked round the far stones. Neither of them said another word about it. At about the fifth bell a cart came over the flat and the man with the bar took it, and a boy of eleven went over at a penny with a hand-lead in his fist, and the tide office got its woman back at about the sixth bell, a mile and a flight of stairs up the hill, and she went in and started on the second of the tide’s work with a clerk of twenty-two and did it for four hours and got it out inside the hour, and nobody in that room asked her one question.

There are two panes on the north wall of that room, in oak frames, and they have not been out of that building in nineteen years and were not out of it that afternoon, and nobody asked her to bring one down and he did not ask her to bring one down, because he has been wrong about that three times and he was not going to try it a fourth time with a woman who had just told him the truth about herself in the open at the near end of a flat.

He went down to the hard at about the eighth bell and got his leg to the top of the lane and put his weight on the good side, and it had been his own for about four hours once, on the fourth of the third tide, at a fire two miles along this coast, and it had not been his own since, and he had been on this coast forty-six days and not one person on it had asked him for one thing, and this afternoon one had told him the true thing about herself and had not asked him for anything either.
