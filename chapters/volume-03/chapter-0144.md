# Chapter 144: Eleven Months

The third of the ninth tide is a good day and the flat takes six, and Ilyan Vester walked two miles up the coast road at about the seventh bell with his weight on his good side, and it took him about an hour and a quarter, and nobody in that shed was told he had come and nobody met him on the road.

The shed is a working shed. There is a bench down one side with a pane on the wall above it in a frame of his own making, three years old, eleven columns for eleven places and every tide of the year, and the arch at the end wall is behind two feet of brick and has been cold for a season and will be cold for a year. Under the batch book on that bench there was a sheet of the guild’s own season paper, a date written on it in a clerk’s hand and nothing written under the date at all, and it had been lying under that book since a cart came up the road behind a load of oil, and it would be under that book at the turn.

The second fire had been let in at about the second bell, and the hole it comes out of is in the end wall with an iron plate hung in front of it, and a woman of twenty-four had been standing at that hole with a bar in her hand since the bell, because once a second fire is let in there is nobody who can walk away from it, and she was not going to be at it at the eighth bell either.

Hollis Thrale was at the bench with his hands on the frame.

“You have come up the road to ask me something,” he said, “and you have not said it and you are not going to, and I have been at a fire forty-one years and I know what a man looks like when he has walked a long way to say one sentence.

“I would like you to say it out loud in the shed anyway, because I am the only person on this coast who has ever been asked a question in this shed and I would like to know what it is like the second time.”

“It is the same question I asked a woman on a shingle four days ago,” said Ilyan Vester. “Would you like to be the one standing there.”

“No,” said Hollis Thrale, and then he stopped, and put his hand flat on the bench, and took about four seconds.

“That was the wrong answer and I heard it come out of my mouth before I had a thing behind it, and I am sixty-one and I have not said a wrong thing in this shed in about nine years, so you may take the four seconds as the real size of it.

“Yes. I would like to be the one standing there, and I have wanted to be asked for about two years and I have not told anybody that, including the woman at the fire, and I have said it out loud in a shed to a man with a bad leg who walked two miles up a coast road to say one sentence, and that is what I am going to be able to tell my apprentice at the end of this, if I ever tell her anything.

“And I am going to tell you now what I found out about two years ago, standing exactly where you are standing, and you may do what you like with it. A record is a thing a person has to stand behind. That is the whole of what a pane is. It is not the glass and it is not the guild and it is not the frame. It is the man who stood beside the arch on one morning with the weather he got, and the four hundred people who stand at that fire after him, and the guild has been borrowing my standing for three years on that wall and it does not know that it is borrowing it.

“Which means that the question you have just asked me is the only question anybody on this coast has ever asked about a record, and nobody has ever asked it of the man who makes them, and a man of sixty-one who has been the man at the fire for forty-one years is not a person anybody thinks to ask. You are four days late. You are not too late. Four days is nothing. Nineteen years would have been something.

“That is not an excuse. It is a description, and I have got very good at telling the difference since the woman at this fire put it in front of me in her own words and would not be talked out of it, and she was not asked for it either, and I have not paid her for that and she is not owed it for it and I have never mentioned it in this shed.”

He said the rest of it with his hands still on the frame and did not sit down and did not turn round.

“Now the part that stops it, and I want it in your own words and I am telling you what your words are before you use them, because you will use them and I have not got anybody else to use them on.

“The cost of a record on this coast is a person for eleven months. That is the whole of the price and there is no other price and there is no discount. A person, a fire, and a year, and the year has to be paid for before anybody knows whether there is going to be anything at the end of it, and the person is whoever is standing at the second fire, and the second fire cannot be left once it is let in, so she stands at it about six days in seven, and she is twenty-four, and she is paid nothing, and she is the third-year apprentice of a man who cannot hear on his right.

“Nobody has asked her. I have not asked her. You are not going to ask her, and I am telling you that now so that you cannot, because you have walked two miles to be careful and you will get here again and it will be easier the second time and that is exactly how it happens.

“Asking her is not a question. It is an instruction with a question on the end of it, and that is my sentence and it is about nine years old and I have never said it out loud in this shed before today. You would be putting a year on a woman of twenty-four who is standing at a fire six days in seven, in a bad season, for a thing with no money against it, in a shed she cannot walk out of. She would say yes. She would say yes because she is twenty-four and has not answered a letter and does not know yet that she is allowed to say no to a thing like that, and I would have her yes on my conscience and yours, and I have enough on mine and I am not putting it on a girl’s back to find out.

“So: no third pane. Not because a third pane is wrong. Because a third pane is another eleven months of the same person, and it would be fired with the same brass and the same hand and it would be another photograph of one morning, and you cannot settle a disagreement between two photographs by making a third one. I said that in this shed in the second of a tide and I have not moved and I am not going to.

“And the guild’s charter is nineteen years and eight months old and it is a pane, and a pane cannot be crossed out, and that one is never coming out of that room, and there is one key and the key is on a belt on a person. And the order for a fifth pane is in a drawer in that room with the date written on the outside of the drawer, and the season’s money is not in the arch, and that is a year, and a year ends on a letting day the tide chooses, and Danno Ray has been standing on shingle all season telling anybody who will stand still that a rule which cannot be changed for a year is not a rule but a season, and he is right, and I have not got a use for being right about it, and neither has he, and nobody on this coast has ever picked it up.”

Then he read the order sheet out loud, because it was on the bench in front of him and the fire wanted a bar put in and he was putting a bar in while he read it, which is how a working man reads.

> A PANE OF THE GUILD’S WATER AND PASSAGE TABLE, TO BE FIRED AND DELIVERED IN THE TURN. THERE IS A DATE ON IT. THERE IS NO FIGURE ON IT. IT CAME UP THE COAST ROAD IN A CART WITH A LOAD OF OIL.


---

It arrived at the cold end of that bench with a bar in a woman’s hand six feet off him and a man of sixty-one with his hand on a frame, and what it gave him was the sheet and one fact about the sheet and nothing else.

**A PANE OF THE GUILD’S WATER AND PASSAGE TABLE, TO BE FIRED AND DELIVERED IN THE TURN. THERE IS A DATE ON IT AND NO FIGURE ON IT. THE SEASON’S MONEY IS NOT IN THE ARCH.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER ANYBODY ON THIS COAST WILL BE TOLD ABOUT THE ARCH BEFORE THE MONEY IS IN IT, OR AFTER.**

Not one person in that shed asked what it was, and he did not say, and it is the second document he has been handed on this coast that exists because somebody wrote a thing down in the ordinary course of a day and did not enter it anywhere in particular.

Then the two columns, because he had come two miles for those as much as for the asking, and the man at the frame gave them again in about a minute and a half without being asked and without turning round.

“Third. The outer end of that wall, given as a place and not a figure. Two hundred and sixty-one yards from the post at the inner end, along the line of it. And after the figure it gives a height, and the height it gives is the top of the inner end, where the wall is nine courses and about six foot. It gives nothing at all for the outer end, because out there the wall is down to about two courses and the guild put it into the deep on purpose, so that the ebb runs under the end of it and has no corner to break.

“Eight. The toe of the seaward face, given as a place and not a figure. My wall says the gravel stands against that face to the second course for the whole length of it, and that at the inner end it stands to the fifth. The guild’s says the toe stands to the fourth course for the whole length and does not give the inner end at all.”

He turned round then, for the first time since the man came in.

“Now. What would those two have to be for that wall to be in the right place. You have not asked me that and I have been waiting two years for somebody to ask me it, and I am going to answer it and then I am going back to the fire.

“A wall does not move. That is the first thing and everybody gets it wrong. Two hundred and sixty yards of stone laid in courses in about seven weeks does not go anywhere. What moves is the hole, because the hole is water, and a wall is only ever in the right place if it is between the hole and the gravel, and the wall was put there because the ebb was cutting a hole on the inside of the shoulder and the gravel was on the outside of the hole and there was two feet of it.

“So the third column is the only thing on this coast that says where the edge of that hole is now, and it says the outer end of the wall is two hundred and sixty-one yards from the post instead of two hundred and sixty-four, and that is three yards, and three yards in nineteen years and eight months is the water moving along that wall and not a mistake. Something has taken the gravel off the outer end of it. I cannot tell you how much and I cannot tell you when, because a figure taken off a wall is a figure about a wall, and a figure about the water has to be had from a boat, and I have not been in a boat for this and I am not going to pretend a thing on a bench is one.

“And the eighth column is the other half. If the toe is standing to the fourth course then there is two feet eight of gravel packed in front of the whole of that face and the wall is doing exactly what it was put there to do and has been doing it for nineteen years. If the toe stands to the second course, then the gravel has dropped a foot and four for the whole of those two hundred and sixty yards and dropped further than that where the wall is highest, and what the ebb is then doing is coming round the bottom of a wall instead of running off the end of one, and there is a length of that wall with nothing under it.

“Those two things together are the answer. And the man on the coast who has an answer in a book is two miles down this road and has not been told that either of them exists, and I have not told him, and I have had the number for two years and have not walked down that road once, and that is on me and not on him, and I have not got a way to put it right that is not a coach.”

He went back to the fire and put the bar in where the bar goes, and a woman of twenty-four took it out of his hand about four seconds later without looking at him, because she had been taking the bar out of his hand for three years and it is not a thing either of them marks.

And a person in a room two hundred miles up this coast had a version of all of it in a book on a shelf, put there in a season of money about nineteen years and eight months ago, and it was a good version, and it was correct in every line, and it was not going to be told anything this week, and the man two hundred miles off who read it had a quarter in front of him with a line on it carrying a wage and no person on it, and he had two choices, and he was not thinking about a wall at the north end of a coast he had never walked, and he chose the one that writes nothing, the way he chose it last quarter, and will choose it next quarter.

At about the ninth bell the man who had walked two miles went back down the coast road on a leg that had not been his own since the fourth of the third tide, at a fire two miles from where he was now standing, and it had been his own for about four hours on that day and it had not been his own on any day since, and he told nobody then and he told nobody on the way down, and about nine people on that coast had now seen something happen to him on a road or in a boat and not one of them had said a word.
