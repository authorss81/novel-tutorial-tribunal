# Chapter 608: The First Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says Out Loud That He Was Standing In A Weigh-House Nine Miles Off On The Last Monday Of A Week And Heard A Thing Say Its Own Name, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Keeps The Book Says That The Only Person In That Town Anybody Can Be Sent For Is Not Going To Stand On A Deck With A Bank Behind Her Any More, And On Which She Is Right About That And About The Split And Nobody In That Room Has Said A Word That Could Move Her, And On Which It Is Said Out Loud That The Split Is Not Between Two Opinions And Is Between People Who Were On The Deck And People Who Were Not

First day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and fifty-eighth morning. Two hundred and eight days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and three days. One hundred and sixty-two days since the division. One hundred and eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the seventh and last day of the hundred and eighth week.

---

Orla Fennimore did not come up the plank on the Tuesday, and the boat-hook was not in his hands, and about four people on that landing noticed the first of those two things and about nine of the people in that town did not notice either.

He had been in that county fifty-four days.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the eight days.

---

Then a man of thirty-eight said the thing he had been keeping, and he said it before anybody had asked him for it, and about nine people were on that deck.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. He had not put the axe down. “*Because it is the first day of a week and about nine of us are on this deck, and there is going to be a thing said in the next four minutes that about four of you have not had and about nine of you have. I am the one who is going to say it, and I am six days late.*”

Nobody on that deck said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Nell Prince.

“*I was in a weigh-house nine miles up this water on the last Monday of a week at about the sixth hour, with a load of boards that never came. There were about nine people in that building. And a thing that was not a person said four words in the air, and the four words were its own name. About four of the people in that room heard it, and about nine heard nothing at all and thought for about nine seconds that it was the wind in a crane-beam.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And I have been in this town for eight days and I have not said it once,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*I am saying it now because a thing I heard in that room has been in this town since Monday night, and if I am not the one who says it then it is going to be said by somebody who was not there.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and say why you are saying it here,*” said a man of about sixty-one who had not been on that landing all week.

“*Because I am one of the four. And about nine people in this town have it in a wrong shape by now, and they have had it since Monday night. I am the only one of the four of us in that room who is not nine miles up this water, and I have not spoken to the other three since I got off that cart. That is the thing I want said in about nine people’s hearing, and I would like it said by me and not by a stranger.*”

---

Then the woman who keeps the book said why she was not coming up the plank, and she said it from the deck of a different boat, and about nine people heard her, and she was right about it and about everything else, and it was not settled.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Nell Prince, from the after end. “*Because the only person in this town anybody can be sent for about a chair is standing on a boat alongside and she has not come up our plank, and about four of us have noticed and about nine of us have not, and I am going to ask her in front of about nine people rather than carry it.*”

Nobody on that landing said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*I am not coming up this plank again this week and I am going to say why in about nine sentences and then I am going to go and rule a page.*”

“*Say it plain,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have kept a book for nineteen years, and a book is a list of people who can be found. The whole of what I have been able to give this town is that: if a person says a thing in front of nine people, then in about four days nine more people can be sent to that person and asked what they said and what they meant. That is nineteen years of me and thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, and about nine people in this town have used it and about nine have not.*”

Nobody on that landing moved.

“*On Friday I said that a man who can be asked about a river nine miles off is a thing that goes in a book. On Monday I sat on that deck with the book open in my hands and let a man be asked a question I could have stopped, and I did not stop him. About nine people on a bank heard a man’s voice in the dark about a stone and a river. In this town of about eleven hundred people there is now a thing about a man of thirty-one that I did not put in the book and cannot take out of the bank.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at the bank,*” said Nell Prince.

“*I am going to end it at the bank, because that is where it ends. If I come up this plank, then the one thing this town can be sent for is a woman who stands in a place with about nine people behind it. In about four years there will be a line in a book with my name at the top of it and a day against it. I have spent nineteen years being the other half of that line for other people, and I am not going to be on my side of it.*”

About four people on that landing had that. About nine did not.

---

Then she said the true thing about the split, and it was not what anybody in that room had been arguing about for two days, and nobody in that room could answer it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*A woman has said she is not coming up a plank and has said why, and it is not a temper. About four of us in this town have spent two days on whether we are for the eleven chairs being filled or against it, and about nine of us have not. I would like somebody to say what is actually being argued about.*”

Nobody on that landing said anything.

“*Then say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The split is not between two opinions,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*Everybody on that deck on Monday night and about everybody on the bank on Monday night and about four people in this town today think the same thing about the eleven chairs and about nine of them would have said the same word if you had asked them. The split is between people who were on that deck and people who were not. And every one of the first is right about their half and every one of the second is right about their half, and there is no way of putting them in one room again without one of the two being asked something they have already been asked once in the dark.*”

About four people on that landing said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say how that is settled,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*It is not settled,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*And I am not going to stand on a deck and say one word about how. The only way I know of settling it is by a person being the person who asks the other ones, and I have been that person in this town for nineteen years, and I am telling nine of you that it does not work from the middle of it.*”

---

Then a man of thirty-one said one sentence, and it was the first thing anybody said in his own defence, and it went to a bank of about nine people, and it was the wrong one.

He wanted it over. He had wanted it over since the fourth hour on the Sunday, and it was the first day of the ninth week and there was a woman standing on another boat who was not coming up his plank.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*A man of thirty-one is about to say something in his own defence for the first time in eight days. About nine of us are on this deck and about nine of us are on this bank, and I am telling all of them that I know what it is.*”

Nobody on that landing said anything.

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a bank, and I should have asked her on Monday and I did not, and there is nothing I can say this morning that is worth the fourth hour it would take.*”

About four seconds went past. Nobody said then say the rest of that, because there was nothing to add to it.

Then the Gull got under way at about the ninth hour with about nine people on the bank, and Orla Fennimore went to her own page with a pencil and a rule, and nobody on that landing said anything to anybody about it.

The fever was forty-six weeks and three days old. A woman of forty-four who has kept a book for nineteen years stopped coming up the plank of a lighter in a town of about eleven hundred people, and she was right about that and right about the split, and about four people on that landing understood why and about nine did not.

And the split was said out loud, in about nine people’s hearing, to be between people who were on a deck and people who were not. Every one of the first was right about their half and every one of the second was right about their half. There is no way of putting them in one room again without one of the two being asked something twice.

And a man of thirty-eight said in about nine people’s hearing that he was in a weigh-house nine miles off on the last Monday of a week and heard a thing say its own name. He is one of about four, and about nine people in that town have it in a shape that is not his, and he is the only one of the four in Hask.

And a lighter went off the landing at about the ninth hour with one chair turned over on her deck, and the book was not in the box, and a woman with a pencil and a rule was on the after thwart of a second boat, and nobody said a word to anybody about any of it.
