# Chapter 611: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Town Of About Eleven Hundred People Is Found To Be Carrying A Shape That Is Wrong In One Particular Way That A Woman Of Forty Can Name And That Naming Does Not Put Right, And On Which A Count Of Ten Is Said Out Loud Again Beside A Second Count Of Nine, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says A Sentence Out Loud On A Bank And About Nine People Take It, And He Is The One Who Said It

Fourth day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and sixty-first morning. Two hundred and eleven days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and six days. One hundred and sixty-five days since the division. One hundred and eleven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the third day of the hundred and ninth week.

---

Eleven days is not long. It is long enough for a thing to go round about eleven hundred people and about four miles of cart road in both directions, and it has gone round, and it has not gone as a sentence.

It has gone as a shape.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end, with the pole across his knee. “*Because it is the eleventh day you have been on that deck and the town has finished saying it, and I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people what it finished saying.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a bank with about four of them on it, and a town of about eleven hundred people that I cannot see the whole of and have not been in.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the eleven days.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Say what the town finished saying, because I have asked about nine people this week and about four of them have it one way and about nine of them have it another, and I am the one who cut the reed and I have not said a word to any of them.*”

“*I have not been in the town,*” said Ilyan. “*I have been on a plank and a bank. I cannot tell you what eleven hundred people have finished saying. I can tell you what is being said at the end of this plank, which is a smaller thing, and I would like it said out loud that it is smaller.*”

About four people on that plank had that. The other nine did not.

---

Then the shape was put into words by a man of thirty-one, and it was put into words badly, and that was the whole of the morning.

He had wanted to ask. He had wanted to go up the plank and ask about nine people one at a time, the way he had learned to do it in four counties, which is slowly, and with a name said out loud first so that anybody on the bank can hear what question is being asked of whom.

He did not do that. He got to the second hour with about nine people on that bank and he looked at the shape of them and it was a shape he had seen before, and it was a shape he had put there himself.

“*It is ten dead men,*” said Ilyan.

About four people on that bank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the deck.

“*I have been up and down that plank for ten days and I have been told that there are ten chairs with a name against them and nobody in them. About four of us have been careful to say the whole of it every time. About nine people in this town have not been careful, and they are saying it the way you say a thing you have heard in a market.*”

Nobody on that bank moved.

“*I have been up and down that plank for ten days and I have not once heard a person in this town say the word dead about those ten out loud, except the two who are dead. I have said it about four times on that deck with the number against it. And I am saying it again this morning on a bank, with a town of about eleven hundred people behind me, because I have learned in four counties that the sentence is easier to hand on than the shape.*”

About four people on that bank had that. About nine did not.

“*Then it is already late,*” said a woman of about sixty-eight from the gravel, in flat shoes, with her hands in an apron. “*Because you are the fourth person this week to say it and I have heard about nine people say it in this town who never went near that boat. It is not yours. Do not go taking it.*”

“*I am not taking it,*” said Ilyan. “*I am saying that I put it in. That is different and it is worse, because I put it in and I am still here and about four of you can be sent for and about nine of me cannot.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for that.

---

Then a woman of forty came up the plank, and she came up it the way people come up a plank when they have been up it their whole life, and she was not on the book and Orla Fennimore was not on the deck.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a woman has come up that plank and she is not one of the nine, and the woman who keeps the book is not here, and I would like the two of those said out loud in the same breath.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and I am the only person on this bank this morning who has not been in a chair and does not want to be,*” said the woman. “*My name is Wenna Callow and I am forty and my mother is Sefa Callow and she is the fourth chair from the after end and her name is against it and nobody is in it.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*She is not dead. She is at the fair at the crossroads. It is fair week and it comes twice a year and it is the only week in the year that road is not a road, and she went on the Wednesday morning with her own goods and she will be back on the Saturday or the Sunday, and she is sixty-eight and she will be back.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then how long has she been gone,*” said Ilyan.

“*Since the Wednesday. That is two days.*”

---

Then the count was said out loud again, and it was said by a man of sixty-one who had not come up that plank in ten days, and he had a reason of his own for being on it, and the reason was money and not a chair.

He was called Nevin Trask and he had been in the landing eleven years. He took the penny a head and half of the penny a cart, and Orla Fennimore took the penny a cart and the book. The two of them had between them three hundred and twenty pence a year, which is one pound six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence each, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence.

He had the landing book under his arm and it is not the book of chairs. It is a book of heads and carts and it is kept in the same town and by the same two people and it goes nowhere.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, “*because a man has come up that plank with a book under his arm on the eleventh day and there are about nine of us here for a count and he has not come for a count.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people with a book of heads and carts under my arm, and I have not come for a count, and the woman is right and I am not going to pretend she is not.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have come because a stranger has said ten dead men on a bank and about nine people in this town have it. The only two people in this county anybody can be sent for about a number are the woman who keeps the chairs and me. The woman who keeps the chairs is not on this plank, and so I have come up here with a book about money, and I would like it said out loud that I came up with the wrong book on purpose.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

“*Then say the count out loud,*” said Wenna Callow, “*and say it with the days in it, and do not be sorry for me.*”

“*Ten chairs with a name against them and nobody in them, as of yesterday. I have counted the names and not the chairs because the names are the thing. And one of the ten is a woman of sixty-eight who went to the crossroads on the Wednesday morning to sell what she has, and she has been in that number since the Wednesday, and before the Wednesday she was in that number too, and that is the whole of what I have.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then the one this town cannot ask is nine.*”

“*It is nine that this town has not been able to ask, and it is ten that are standing up on that boat. Both of those are true and they have both been true since Wednesday morning. I have been keeping the penny a head and the penny a cart for eleven years and I have never once had to know which of the two a number meant.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the count.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Nine, and two of them dead, and one of them gone up the water eleven years, and the other six not come. Say the subtraction in front of about nine people, because about four of us have been carrying a number for ten days and we have not all been carrying the same one.*”

“*Ten standing up. Two dead. One gone up the water in a lighter about eleven years and not heard of since. Six that have not come. One that is at the fair at the crossroads and will be back on the Saturday or the Sunday, and she went on the Wednesday, and that is two days.*”

About four people on that plank counted that on their fingers. The other nine did not.

---

Then the woman of forty said the thing that the whole of the eleven days had been standing behind, and she said it without being asked, and it took her four sentences.

“*You have all been saying the count, and the count is right, and it has been right since the Wednesday, and it is the reason the town says ten dead men.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Say the rest of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because a count of chairs with a name against them and nobody in them does not ask why. It never has. And in about four years nobody is going to be at a fair and come home to a chair that says she is one of the ten, because there is no column in that book for a woman who was at the crossroads. There is no column for a man who has been ill. There is no column for a person who is ashamed. Every one of those is a person who has not come, and every one of those is a person this town has not been able to ask.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

“*Then it is not ten dead men,*” said Ilyan.

“*It is two dead men and two dead women, and about nine people in this town cannot be asked anything, and one of those nine is my mother and she is at the crossroads selling withies in the rain.*”

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

“*And I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people,*” said Wenna Callow, “*that naming it does not put it right. My mother is going to come back on the Saturday or the Sunday and she is going to come up this plank and sit in the fourth chair, and the count will come down to nine, and nobody on this deck will know which nine.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*And if anybody in this town writes down the reason, then in about four years there will be a form in this county that says a person who gives a reason was not in the chair at all. My mother will have been at a fair and been struck off for it.*”

---

The fever was forty-six weeks and six days old. He had been in that county fifty-seven days. He could not leave it until the water went over a block of limestone nine miles up this river, he did not know when, and nobody could tell him, and he could tell them about the water at that cut to about a quarter of an inch without looking at anything.

And he had stood on a bank this morning and said ten dead men out loud, in front of about nine people, in a town of about eleven hundred people. The sentence had been said about four times on that deck in ten days and about nine times in the town behind him in the same ten days, and every one of the first four was his.

And about four people in that town can be sent for. About nine of him cannot.

Nobody in Hask was relieved and nobody in Hask was forgiven and nobody in Hask was thanked.

The count is nine and the ten on that boat is a different figure, and both of them are right. Eleven on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighth week, and ten from the Thursday of it, when a woman of twenty-nine sat down in the reed. Ten standing up on that deck this morning, and nine that this town has not been able to ask, because one of the ten went out of it on the Wednesday to sell withies at a crossroads four miles off on the first day of fair week. There is no column in that book for the reason and there is not going to be one.

And a man of sixty-one with a book of heads and carts under his arm walked back up the cart road toward the landing he has kept for eleven years, and the book is not the book of chairs, and about nine people in a town of about eleven hundred had the sentence ten dead men by the fourth hour, and about nine of them had two numbers instead, and the man with the book under his arm is the only one who knows both of them and he is not going to be asked.
