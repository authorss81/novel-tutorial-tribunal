# Chapter 622: The First Day Of The Hundred And Eleventh Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Cuts A Third Copy Of That Sheet Off A Thwart Of His Own Boat And Carries It Nine Miles Up The Water Because The Rule Does Not Say He May Not, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Keeps The Book Comes Up That Plank For The First Time In Nine Days And Is Asked A Question Out Loud In Front Of About Nine People And Answers Half Of It, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says No In The Open About A Chair Nobody Has Offered Him And Nothing Whatever Happens, And On Which A Woman Of Seventy-One Who Keeps A House That Is Not Hers Gets The Thirty-Second Line Of A Sheet In Two Places And A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That Under The Rule The Thing Is Not True

First day of the hundred and eleventh week. His three hundred and seventy-second morning. Two hundred and twenty-two days after the settlement. The fever forty-eight weeks and three days. One hundred and seventy-six days since the division. One hundred and twenty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The man of thirty-eight had a knife and he had a boat, and on the Tuesday morning at about the second hour he cut a sheet of deal out of the after thwart of the Gull, and the after thwart is about eleven feet by a foot and four inches, and he cut it across into two and had one of the two.

About four people on that bank saw him do it and about nine did not, and he did not ask anybody, and he did it in the open, and he said out loud what he was doing before he did it, which is the only reason anybody has it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, “*because a man of thirty-eight has a knife in his hand at about the second hour on a Tuesday and about nine people in this town cannot be sent for about a chair, and I would like the whole of this said in the open before any of it is done.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people with a knife and a third copy of that sheet, and I am not going to be asked whether I may do it, because nobody in this county can be asked anything about it. And because a man of sixty-eight gave a rule on Thursday and the rule does not say that a copy may not travel.*”

Nobody on that bank moved.

“*Say the rest of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*Say it in the open, and say it once.*”

“*I have read that sheet aloud in two yards and I have never once had it in a room with the other one, and the rule says the two are read against each other once a week. The only way that has ever happened is that a man of thirty-eight has poled nine miles up and nine miles down and stood about in two places and remembered it. I have a child of nine. I am not going to be a sheet.*”

About four people on that bank had that. The other nine were still carrying the count.

“*And I would like it said out loud that nobody asked me to do this and that I would have done it if nobody had asked me, and that a rule that stops a thing travelling is not a rule, it is a lid, and I have never in my life wanted a lid on anything.*”

Then he poled up the water with it under oilcloth and was gone by about the fourth hour, and about four people in a town of about eleven hundred people had it by the seventh hour and about nine had it a different way.

Nobody on that bank thanked him for a third copy.

---

She came up the plank at about the sixth hour, and it was nine days since she had been on it, and nobody sent for her and nobody had said one word about it in nine days, and about four people in that town had noticed the nine days and about nine had not.

The woman who keeps the book is forty-four and has kept it nineteen years and is paid a hundred and sixty pence a year, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and she is the only person in the county of Kell anybody can be sent for about a chair.

“*Then ask it,*” said Nevin Trask, from the bank, without the book, in the first minute she was on the deck. “*You said on Sunday you would be on this deck on the Tuesday and you have not said it in nine days and about nine of us have not noticed, and I have noticed, and there is no good reason why there should not be one.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a woman of forty-four who has kept a book for nineteen years,*” said Ilyan, “*and I am going to ask her a question that a woman of thirty-four from another county asked out loud on a Sunday nine days ago. I am going to ask it in the open because I said on that Sunday that I would, and I would like about four of you to stop me if I have got it wrong.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Whether the saying of no in the open has to be written down the way the saying of yes is written down, and if it is not, who decided that, and when, and on what day.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

---

She did not answer it for a long time, and when she did she answered one third of it and refused the other two thirds, and the refusal came before the third, and about four people on that deck had expected the third and about nine had expected the other two thirds.

“*No,*” said Orla Fennimore.

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am not going to say whether a no has to be written down, because you have not asked me a question. You have asked me a question somebody else asked, in a town nine miles off, and I have been asked it four times in nine days by people who were not in the room when it was asked and who are waiting for an answer from a woman who is.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked her for that.

“*And I will tell you why in four sentences and then I will answer one thing, because I have been the instrument in this county for nineteen years and I have never once been the person, and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. The rest of them heard a woman refuse something.

“*If I say no, a no does not have to be written down, then in about four years there will be a form in this county that says so, and about nine people will be sent for about a no. If I say yes, a no has to be written down, then in about four years there will be a column, and a woman of twenty-six who has been in a chair for nine years is right that a column for a reason is a column for a reason.*”

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them.

“*About nine of us will have taught that to nine more of us. I can see both of those and I have been the instrument of both of them for nineteen years and I was never asked which one I wanted.*”

About four seconds.

“*And the reason I will not say it is that a person in this county can be sent for about a chair and not about anything else, and an answer said by me in the open is not an answer, it is the position of the record, and this record has never had a position. It has had a woman in it. If I say a thing out loud on this deck then in about four years that thing is what a person in this county is sent for about, and about nine people will not have been in the room.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*I will answer one of the three. The other two are owed and I am not going to pretend they are not.*”

“*Then say the one,*” said Ilyan.

“*Nobody decided it. That is the true of the third of it and it is the only one of the three I will stand behind. The book has a column for a yes because a yes has to be written down or a chair is not a chair. A person holds a chair by saying yes out loud, and if the saying of it is not in a book, then in about four years nobody in this county can be checked against it and a chair is a thing somebody remembers. That is a reason and it is a good one and it is nineteen years old.*”

About four people on that deck counted that against each other. About nine of the rest had heads and carts in them and not a column.

“*And there is no column for a no, and I did not leave it out. A no was never needed by the book. A no is needed by the person, and the book was never for the person. That is not a decision. There was nobody in a room deciding it. I was nineteen and I had a book and the book had a column, and I did not go and ask, because you do not go and ask the person you keep a book for. That is the whole of it and I have not said it in nineteen years.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked her for nineteen years.

---

Then the man of thirty-one did the thing he had come to that deck to find out, and it was the smallest thing anybody has done on it and it was the only thing anybody had ever done there with a figure behind it.

“*I am going to say no,*” said Ilyan.

“*Out loud, in front of about nine people, about a chair, and I am going to do it about a chair that nobody has offered me. I would like it said before I say it that there is no chair, and that I know there is no chair, and that I am going to find out what happens to a person who says no in the open in this town. I have not found out in nineteen days because about nine of us have never done it.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*No,*” said Ilyan. “*I will not have a chair in this county. Not one of the twenty-nine, and not the plank, and not any ground a person offers me in four years.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nothing whatever happened.

The book was not written in, because it was under the after thwart and nobody had it. The count did not move, because the count is of chairs with a name against them and nobody in them, and he has no name against anything. Nobody said anything. The woman of forty-four looked at him for about as long as a person looks at a thing, and then she looked at the ground.

About four people on that deck had understood the whole of it. About nine had not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*There has never once been a day in that book for a thing a person said out loud and did not take,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*In nineteen years.*”

The man of thirty-one wrote it down in his own head and did not write it on anything.

“*Not one. And that is not because the book is unkind. It is because for nineteen years every no in this county was a person standing up out of a chair, and a person standing up out of a chair is in the book already, on the day they sat down, and they are not in it on the day they get up. The count is of chairs and not of people and you said that on a Wednesday nine days ago, and I have been keeping that book for nineteen years and I never once had it as a thing about a no.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

“*And you have said no about a chair nobody offered you, on a Tuesday, in front of nine people, and nothing has happened, and I would like it said out loud that that is the first time in nineteen years that something a person said out loud in this county did not go into that book.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

“*And the reason it did not go in is that there was no chair, and the reason there was no chair is that nobody in this county can be sent for about you, and the woman who keeps that book said so on the sixth day of the second week and about nine people heard it and about four of them are on this deck. *”

---

Then the thirty-second line went on, and it went on in the open, and it was asked for on the sixth day of the second week by a woman of seventy-one and the day went on it, and about nine people on that deck understood every word of it and about nine of them have a different shape of it by the seventh hour.

He said it out loud himself, in about nine people’s hearing, in the plainest words he had, because she had said *say it to the person who keeps the chairs, not to me*, and he had been asked not to put it in his coat and carry it about, and he had not.

“*A woman of seventy-one keeps a house on the low side of that landing and it is not hers and she has kept it eleven years. She has never once been asked whether she wanted it. She has never told anybody until the seventh day of the third week, when she told one person and asked him not to carry it about, and he has not.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

The woman of forty-four took the book out from under the after thwart and wrote it, and the day went on it, and the day was the first day of the hundred and eleventh week.

Then a man of sixty-one read the two sheets against each other and said out loud what the rule says about them, and it took him about nine sentences and he had the arithmetic and nobody had to give it to him.

“*Two copies. One is nailed inside the after thwart of this boat. One is kept at a landing nine miles up the water. They are read against each other once a week in the open, and if the two do not agree then the thing is not true.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then the two do not agree.*”

“*The line is on the copy in this boat and the copy is in this boat and the other copy is nine miles up the water, and it does not have the line, because a man of thirty-eight took a third copy up the water at about the second hour this morning with thirty-one lines on it. This line was written at about the seventh hour.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*So under the rule that a man of sixty-eight gave on Thursday, and which I have stood behind for five days, a woman of seventy-one who has kept a house that is not hers for eleven years and was never asked is not true this week. It is not that it is not true. It is that there are two copies and one of them does not have it, and the rule says the thing is not true until they agree.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody for the rule.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud, in front of about nine people, that he was not going to go and tell the woman of seventy-one, and why, and about four people on that deck understood the shape of it and about nine had been there before.

“*Because a man of thirty-one went up that plank nine days ago and did not go to a woman’s door, and about four people in this town have that against him.*”

“*And because if I go down to that house on the low side this evening and tell her that her eleven years are on a sheet in two places, and that a copy of that sheet went nine miles up the water at about the second hour this morning, then in about four years there will be a form in this county that says a person may not be put on a sheet and may not hear about it either.*”

About four people on that deck said nothing at all.

“*And I would like it said out loud that she asked for this. She said say it to the woman who keeps the chairs, in the open, on a Tuesday, and put the day on it. And I have done exactly what she asked and I am not going to tell her, and about four of you can see that those two things are the same shape as the thing I did nine days ago and about nine of you cannot, and I am not going to be forgiven for it in a room and I am not asking to be.*”

---

The fever was forty-eight weeks and three days old. He had been in that county sixty-eight days. A third copy of that sheet went nine miles up the water on a Tuesday morning on a boat, and about four people in a town of about eleven hundred people had it by the seventh hour, and nobody had asked for it. The rule a man of sixty-eight gave on Thursday does not say that a copy may not travel, and he said so out loud before he did it.

And a woman of forty-four came up that plank after nine days and answered one third of a question and refused the other two thirds, and the third of it was that nobody decided, because there was nobody in a room deciding it, and she was nineteen and she had a book and she did not go and ask.

And a man of thirty-one said no in the open about a chair nobody had offered him and nothing whatever happened, and a woman of forty-four said out loud that in nineteen years no has never been written in that book, and the reason is that a no was never needed by the book.

And under a rule five days old, a woman of seventy-one is not true this week, and it is a rule and not a lie, and there are two copies and one of them is nine miles up the water with thirty-one lines on it.
