# Chapter 614: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Man Of Fifty-Four Who Has Held A Chair For Nine Years Says Yes Out Loud In The Open To A Second Chair For A Ground He Has Never Walked, And On Which The Accepting Costs A Thing That Is Not His And A Man Of Sixty-Eight Who Keeps That Quarter Mile Cannot Say So From A Chair, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud That It Was Right And About Nine People Hear It

Seventh and last day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and sixty-fourth morning. Two hundred and fourteen days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and two days. One hundred and sixty-eight days since the division. One hundred and fourteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

There is a sitting in Hask when the town sits, and the town sits on a Monday and a Thursday, and there is no form that says they must and none that says they may, and the chairs are stood up along the starboard side of the Gull in two rows and a person holds one by being in it.

On the Monday the wind was off the flat and the Gull was knocking against the bank twice a minute, and about nine people were on her and about nine more were on the gravel.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the thirteen days.

---

Then a man of fifty-four who had held a chair for nine years said yes to a second one, and nobody asked him a question before he said it, and he noticed that afterwards.

He was called Barnaby Crove and he had said yes in the open on a Monday nine years ago in about nine people’s hearing and had not regretted it once and thought about it most weeks, and the ground he held was the gravel at the head of the drain.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because a man of fifty-four has just stood up out of a chair and said yes to a second one, and about four of us heard him and about nine did not, and the man with the pole would like the whole of it said twice.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people, and I have been in the gravel chair for nine years, and I have just said yes to the plank, and I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that I have not walked that quarter mile in about nine years. I do not know what is on it.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I said yes because a man of sixty-eight has been saying for two days that a plank is not a road, and about nine of us have agreed with him. If that is going to be said anywhere it is going to be said by somebody who can be sent for, and I am the only man on this deck who can be sent for about anything except a chair.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank moved.

---

Then he sat in it, and he did it in the open. About nine people watched a man hold a chair for a piece of ground he had told them on the deck he had never walked. A chair in that town is a person saying something for ground, and about nine people will say he has said it.

He did not say much. That was the thing about it afterwards.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a chair on this boat has just been used for about nine minutes by a man who is in it, and I would like every word of it said out loud before anybody in this town goes and does anything with it.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people, in the plank chair, and the plank is eleven feet long and it is the end of a cart road and I have never walked the quarter mile behind it. I am going to say one thing and I am going to say it about the ground I am the near party to, because that is the only ground I have ever been on this deck about.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Then say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*That the gravel at the head of the drain goes down at about four inches in a year in this country, and goes down at about nine where a man has cut a ditch on the top side of it. In about four years there will not be enough gravel in it to carry a loaded cart off the flat. That is the whole of what I know, and I have said it about the ground I am the near party to, and I have not said one word about the quarter mile.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

---

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that it was right, and about nine people heard it, and it was in front of about nine people, and it was the fourth time in fourteen days.

“*That is right,*” said Ilyan. “*A man who is the near party to the ground has said a true thing about the ground in a chair, and about nine people in this town have heard it, and it is the first time in four counties that I have watched the thing work.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for it.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said the man of sixty-eight, from the gravel, without getting up.

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and I have watched a man say a true thing about a piece of ground in a chair for a piece of ground, and it has been heard, and about nine of you heard it as being about the road.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

---

Then a man of sixty-eight said what it had cost, and it had not cost the man of fifty-four anything, and the man of fifty-four was not in a chair and could not be asked about it and did not know.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Pell Vearn, from the gravel, and he had kept the last quarter mile of that cart road and the plank at the end of it for twenty-two years. “*Because a man of thirty-one has just said the word right on a Monday morning, and I have been sitting on this gravel for about a quarter of an hour and I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that I am not the man of fifty-four and I am not in a chair.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I said before the sun was up that a plank is not a road. A plank is eleven feet and there is a good deal of the year that is not a road, and a quarter mile of it is under six inches of water by the second week of the wet and about nine carts a week do not come down here because of it. I have said that out loud in front of about nine people and it has cost me nothing, because a man of sixty-eight is not a person anybody is obliged to answer.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

“*And a man of fifty-four has now said in a chair about the ground he is the near party to that the gravel at the head of the drain is going down at about nine inches where a man has cut a ditch. He said it in the plank chair. About nine people in this town have it as being about the road, and by Saturday it will be in three places and none of them will be the chair it was said in.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And I cannot say so, because I am not in a chair and there is no form in this county that lets a man ask to be corrected in one, and there is nobody on this plank whose ground that gravel at the head of the drain is.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

---

Then the man of thirty-one tried to do the thing he does, and it was stopped, and the person who stopped him did it with arithmetic.

“*Then say it is not anybody’s fault and go on,*” said Ilyan.

“*No,*” said Nevin Trask, from the bank, with the landing book not under his arm this time.

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*You have said not anybody’s fault four times this week and about four of us have stopped listening to the rest of what you say after it. I am sixty-one and I take a penny a head and half a penny a cart at that landing and I am going to say the sum, because you do not know it and I do, and then we can all find out what it is about.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the sum.

“*A penny a head and a penny a cart. About eight hundred heads and about two hundred and forty carts in a year. That is a thousand and forty pence. Four pounds six shillings and eightpence. The book takes three pounds of that, and the book is ruling, binding, lamp oil and copying it out, and the woman who keeps it gets thirteen shillings and fourpence of what is left. What is left is three hundred and twenty pence, which is one pound six shillings and eightpence, between two people, which is a hundred and sixty pence each.*”

About four people on that bank counted that. About nine did not.

“*Now the gravel. A cart at a crossroads four miles off is four shillings there and four shillings back with a load, and that is eight shillings a load, and that is ninety-six pence a load. A quarter mile of a road nine feet wide is about nine loads and no man on this plank would say it is ten. Nine loads is eight hundred and sixty-four pence, which is three pounds and twelve shillings. I have said that in about nine people’s hearing and I would like it said that the gravel alone is more than the whole of that landing for a year, and the book has not been taken out of it and cannot be.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And I am not saying it is anybody’s fault. I am saying that a man of sixty-eight has said a true thing about a road on a Monday, and a man of fifty-four has said a true thing about gravel in a chair. Between them, the two of them have said that the money for this is not there. Neither of them knew that about the other. About nine people on this plank now know it and about nine do not.*”

---

The fever was forty-seven weeks and two days old. He had been in that county sixty days. A man of fifty-four who had held a chair for nine years said yes to a second one for a quarter mile he has not walked in nine years. He said one true thing in it about a piece of gravel that is not under it, and about nine people in a town of about eleven hundred have it as being about the road.

And the money for a causeway is three hundred and twenty pence a year after the book, and the gravel is eight hundred and sixty-four pence. A man of sixty-one said both of those out loud in front of about nine people, and said that the difference was not anybody’s fault. He said it as arithmetic and not as a comfort.

And the plank chair is stood up against the starboard side of that boat with a name against it in a book and nobody in it, and it is not one of the ten, and it was not one of the ten on the Friday before this, and there is no column in anything anybody in that town has ever written that can say which of the two that is.
