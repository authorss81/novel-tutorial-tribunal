# Chapter 617: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Tenth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which A Sheet Of Deal Is Written And Cut Into Two And One Of The Two Is Nailed Inside The After Thwart Of A Lighter And One Is Kept Nine Miles Up The Water, And On Which They Are Read Against Each Other Once A Week In The Open, And On Which It Is The First Thing In That Town That Anybody Can Be Sent For About, And On Which Twenty-Nine Lines Are Read And Ten Of Them Have A Name Against Them And Nobody In Them, And One Of The Ten Cannot Be Checked Against Anybody In That Town

Third day of the hundred and tenth week. His three hundred and sixty-seventh morning. Two hundred and seventeen days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and five days. One hundred and seventy-one days since the division. One hundred and seventeen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The rule came from a man of sixty-eight who was not in a chair, and he gave it in about nine sentences on a Thursday morning, and about four people on that deck understood it at once and about nine did not.

He was called Pell Vearn and he had kept the last quarter mile of the cart road for twenty-two years. He had said on the Monday that a man of fifty-four had said a true thing about gravel in a chair for a piece of ground, and about nine people had it as being about the road. He could not say so himself, because he was not in a chair.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because a man of sixty-eight who cannot correct himself in a chair has just told about nine people how to correct him, and I would like it said out loud that I did not know that was what he was doing until he was halfway through it.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people, and I said on Monday that about nine of us heard a sentence in a chair and took it for a sentence about a different piece of ground, and I cannot be asked about it because I am not in one. So I am not going to be the one who is right about a road.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Write it down. There is a book of chairs and there is a book of heads and carts and neither of them is wrong. I want a third thing with a chair and a day against it, in two places, where about nine people can read both and say whether the two agree. If the two agree then the thing is true and nobody has to be in a chair to say so. If they do not agree, then nobody is in the chair who is not in it, and that is the end of it and not a fight.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the rule.

---

Then it was written, and it was cut, and the man of thirty-one wrote both halves and a boy of eleven copied one of them, and the copying took most of the morning and was done on the bank with a board on a barrel because there was no other table in Hask.

**RECORDED. Two copies of one sheet. One is nailed inside the after thwart of the Gull. One is kept nine miles up the water. They are read against each other once a week in the open, and if they do not agree, the thing is not true. Twenty-nine lines. Ten have a name against them and nobody in them. One of the ten cannot be checked against anybody in that town.**

---

Then the twenty-nine lines were read out loud, one at a time, with the ground said before the name, because a chair is named for the ground and not for the person and everybody on that deck had learned that the hard way.

The woman of sixty-eight was read in the fourth chair from the after end, and she was in it, and about nine people on that deck looked at her and about nine looked away, and her line said a day.

The man of fifty-four was read in the gravel at the head of the drain and the plank, and he was in neither, and both lines said a day, and about four people on that deck said nothing about the second one and about nine said something.

The woman of twenty-six was read in the ash and she was in it.

About nine of the other twenty-eight were read with the ground said and nothing after it, and they are the substance of that town and they were read in about nine minutes, and the first of them was the gravel and withy at the top of the head above the drain, and the second was the low ground on the west side of the water where there is a foot of standing water in a bad week, and the third was the bank the plank is tied to, and the fourth was a yard at the bottom of the quarter mile that has had four names in nineteen years and is the near party to nobody living.

Then the woman of twenty-nine was read in the reed and nobody was in it, and the line said nothing else. About nine people on that deck did not say anything about that either, because there is no column in that sheet for a reason. The reason is nine seconds and thirteen days, and yesterday a woman of twenty-six said out loud why there is not going to be one.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the twenty-nine lines.

Then the man of sixty-eight asked for one thing to be added before the sheet was cut in two, and it went on, and it is the eleventh line of a second thing in this town and nobody has ever read it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a man has asked for a line and about four of us know what it is and about nine do not, and it is going on the sheet and it is not a chair.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a sheet of deal, and I would like a line on it for the last quarter mile of the cart road and the plank, because the plank has no chair and the plank is the reason nine of us came up here, and a thing that is not a chair can be a thing a person is sent for about.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

Nobody on that plank thanked him for asking.

---

Then the last line was read, and it is the last line because that chair is at the other end of the book. A man of thirty-eight had said on the sixth day of the hundred and ninth week that there is a chair at the other end of the same book, and he is the brother of the man who held the reed.

“*The gravel and withy at the top of the drain above the head. Wren Sayer. Gone up the water in a lighter about eleven years ago and not heard of since. He is the brother of Oriel Sayer, who held the reed.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*There is nobody in that chair, and I cannot tell you one thing about that line that is not in the book already,*” said Pell Vearn. “*I have been going along that ground for twenty-two years. I did not know, until a man of thirty-eight said it on a Monday, that the man it is for was the other one in that room.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end, where he had not moved all morning, and he said it more slowly than he had said anything that week.

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a sheet with twenty-nine lines on it, and I have not said a word in about an hour, and I would like it said out loud that the man in the last line was in a weigh-house nine miles up this water on a Monday morning about six weeks ago with four of us, and I have not said that out loud on this deck until this second, and I have said it once, on the sixth day, and I did not know then that the three other people in that room had a chair in this town.*”

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Three of us were in that room. About nine of us in this town think that a thing was in a room and I am the only one who was in it and it is the only thing in this town that is not on a sheet. And now it is on a sheet, because a man of sixty-eight wrote a rule this morning and I am the reason the last line of it cannot be checked, and I would like that said out loud and then I would like everybody in this town to leave me alone about it for a day.*”

About four seconds went past.

---

Then the two halves were carried apart, and one was nailed, and the other went up the water, and the man of thirty-one was the only person in the county who could not be checked against either of them, and he said so out loud on the bank before the nail went in.

“*I would like it said in front of about nine people that there is a sheet in this town with twenty-nine lines on it, and about nine of them are about people. I wrote both halves of it, and there is no form in this county that says a person can be sent for about a thing he has written with his own hand.*”

“*About nine people in a town of about eleven hundred have it now, and I have no column and no form and nobody nine miles off who can be sent for to ask me whether I wanted to make it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for it.

“*And I would like it said out loud in the same breath that this is the first thing I have made in four counties that anybody can be sent for about. I have been the person who carries things out of rooms in nine counties and about four of us have noticed. I do not know yet whether that is better or worse, and I am not going to find out this week.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

---

The fever was forty-seven weeks and five days old. He had been in that county sixty-three days. There is a sheet of deal in a town of about eleven hundred people with twenty-nine lines on it. One copy is nailed inside the after thwart of a lighter called the Gull and the other is kept at a landing nine miles up the water. They are read against each other once a week in the open, and if the two do not agree then the thing is not true, and about nine people in that town can be sent for about it.

Twenty-nine lines. Ten of them have a name against them and nobody in them, and it was ten on the Wednesday morning of this week, and one of the ten is a woman of twenty-nine who is standing nine feet off with a withy knife in her apron.

And the last line is a man of about thirty-four called Wren Sayer, who went up the water in a lighter about eleven years ago and has not been heard of since. He is the only one of the twenty-nine that nobody in that town can be checked against, and he was in a weigh-house nine miles up this water about six weeks ago.
