# Chapter 646: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Friday, And On Which One Man Poles Nine Miles Up And Nine Miles Down Again With A Board About Nine Inches Long

Fourth day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-sixth morning. Two hundred and forty-six days after the settlement. The fever fifty-one weeks and six days. Two hundred days since the division. One hundred and forty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

He went up the water in the Gull at about the fifth hour on the Friday morning with a sheet of deal in a coat and a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge in the same coat, and a woman of twenty-eight went with him because she had said she would give copies with her own hand and had never seen where a copy is kept.

Nobody on that plank thanked either of them for the going.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety-two days, and in Hask forty-five days, and the forty-five is since he came up that plank, and the ninety-two is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in.

He wanted to go with them, and he stayed nine feet off, and it was the third time in six days he had stayed where he was told to stay. About four people on that bank noticed it and about nine did not.

About nine people were in the yard nine miles up that water at about the ninth hour, and a man of forty-three who has kept that landing nineteen years was by the counter, and the box under the counter had one shilling and sixpence in it, which is about nine pence of heads and about nine pence of carts, and nine and nine is about eighteen pence.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, and he is the only person who may carry the board, and he read the whole sheet out loud in that yard to about nine people, and it took him about nine minutes, and he did not hurry the last four lines.

Nobody in that yard thanked him for nine minutes.

“*Thirty-two lines on both of them. The thirty-second is the thirty-second on both of them. The thirty-first is the thirty-first on both of them.*”

“*And it is the same day and the same Monday on both of them, and they agree, and yesterday was the fifth of the Thursdays, and this is not a Thursday, and the sixth is next Thursday.*”

“*And this is ordinary work, and it is not a hearing and it is not a Crown, and it is a boat and a pole and a board about nine inches long.*”

About four people in that yard had the ordinary. About nine had the lines and not the work.

Then he showed the woman of twenty-eight the box, and the book, and the column cut with a knife on the back of the last leaf, and the column is about four inches wide and about nine inches long, and it is a number of people and not of heads and not of carts.

“*A man of sixty-one cut that on the Thursday of last week, after asking four people whether a man of sixty-one may. About four of them did not answer him.*”

“*And there is no form in this county that says he may take that shilling and sixpence out, and he has tried to strike that line out twice.*”

“*And if you are going to write down what a Crown says and give it with your own hand, you are going to do it where a book cannot be sent for about you.*”

Nobody in that yard thanked him for the showing.

Then Sera Quill put her hand on the gunwale of that lighter and said out loud that she had understood one thing. About four people in that yard heard it at once and about nine did not.

“*I have understood that a sheet is only true when two people nine miles apart have said so in the open, and the two people are a man of thirty-eight and a man of forty-three.*”

“*And one of them has poled about nine miles twice this week to make it true, and the other has stood by a counter nineteen years.*”

“*And my nine sentences are not true that way, and that is why they are a burden and not a record.*”

About four people in that yard had the nine. Nobody said anything to her about it.

He came down and got in at about the second hour of the afternoon, and put the board on the barrel in front of about nine people, and the two copies had agreed twice in two days, and nobody had asked him to make them agree either time.

Then he said out loud that he is the one that pays it, and he said it a second time in six days, and he did the same work again to say it, because a saying without a doing is a word about nothing.

“*About nine days of one man’s work is what a Crown of about nine people costs, and a man of sixty-one did that sum out loud on a Friday, and a woman of twenty-nine did it again on a Thursday.*”

“*About eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and nine of them are on that board, and none of the thousand and forty pence reaches it, because there is no form that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel.*”

“*And I am the one that pays it, and I have not told anybody that I pay it until Saturday of the last week, and I am saying it again now, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to stop.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for nine days.

About four people in front of that barrel counted the thousand and forty against the nine. About nine heard a man say he pays and not a number.

Then he mended the plank while about nine people watched, and it is ordinary work, and it is load-bearing, and it is not a hearing and not a Crown. About four people in front of that barrel had never seen a plank mended.

The plank is eleven feet long, and thirty feet of withy hurdles are at the head of it, and the Gull is about thirty feet long with a pole under her fore thwart and a stone on it, and a man of thirty-eight knows every foot of it the way a man of sixty-one knows a penny.

“*Thirty feet of hurdles at the head of eleven feet of plank is what eleven people walk up in a working week, and nine of them were on that board.*”

“*And a stone on a pole under a fore thwart is how a lighter lies steady enough to read, and I set it myself at about the fifth hour.*”

“*And a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches is how I know thirty-two lines agree, and I cut the edge back to the same place every week since the Thursday of the tenth week.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for eleven feet.

Then a woman of forty-one with two carts brought two cart beds for the mending, and four sheets of deal had come out of her own cart beds. About nine people in front of that barrel saw wood given without a form.

“*Four sheets out of my own beds, and eleven sheets copied in about four hours of daylight on the Sunday of the hundred and eleventh week, and eleven and one and one and one is fourteen.*”

“*And fourteen is a figure of wood and not of people, and one of the fourteen has no arrangement and is in a county nobody is going to.*”

“*And the best-looking sheet in four counties is the one nobody is going to, and a man of thirty-one wrote it on the Friday evening in a hand nobody in that county can do.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the fourteen. About nine had the beds and not the number.

Then Ilyan said the true of it from nine feet off, and it was one half, and he did not come closer, and a woman of twenty-eight held up a hand at about the fourth sentence and he held it, and it was the eleventh time in forty-five days.

“*A man of thirty-eight has just done ordinary work twice in two days. About four people in front of that barrel have it as a boat and about nine have it as a Crown.*”

“*And it is not a hearing and it is not a Crown, and it is a boat and a pole and a board about nine inches long, and it will be load-bearing on Monday.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest is that I have been in Hask forty-five days, and I may read out and may stand nine feet off and may carry a sheet to the end of that bank, and I held all three today.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for nine feet.

Then night work was spoken of, because ordinary work does not end at the second hour of the afternoon. About nine people in front of that barrel heard what a lighter needs after dark.

“*She needs bailing. She needs a stone set right. She needs thirty feet of hurdles checked at the head of eleven feet of plank.*”

“*I do it without being asked. I did it on the Thursday. I did it again on the Friday.*”

“*A sheet true twice in two days is still true on the Saturday. That is why Friday work bears Monday.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for bailing.

Then Sera Quill said she had seen bailing, and she said it plain. She came up that cart road on the Monday and this is the fifth day of it, counting the day she came up.

“*I saw him bail with a cut-down pail. I saw water go back to water.*”

“*My nine sentences are bailing of another kind. Nobody sees it leave.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the pail. The rest had sentences.

Then eleven walked the plank again to show it holds. About nine went up. About four went down.

“*Eleven in a week. Nine on a board that was. That is the figure.*”

“*One week at one plank. Not a county. Not a year.*”

“*A year is water over stone. Nobody knows when.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked the eleven for walking.

---

The fever was fifty-one weeks and six days old. He had been in that county ninety-two days, and one man poled nine miles up and nine miles down again with a sheet in a coat and a board about nine inches long, and it is not a hearing and it is not a Crown.

And it took about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading, and the two agreed, and a woman of twenty-eight saw where a copy is kept and where a box is kept under a counter.

And the same man said out loud that he is the one that pays it. About nine days of one man’s work is what a Crown costs, and none of the thousand and forty pence reaches it.

And a man of thirty-one stayed nine feet off with a knife and held it, and ink was on the fingers of a woman of twenty-eight who had written nothing yet and would write with her own hand.
