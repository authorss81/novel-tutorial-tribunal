# Chapter 612: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Goes Down To The Low Side Of A Landing To Ask A Woman Of Seventy-One A Question That Is Not About A Chair And Is Refused The Instrument Anyway In Four Sentences, And On Which It Turns Out That She Has Been The Instrument For Eleven Years And That Nobody Wrote Down What She Gave For It, And On Which A Thing Is Said In That House That Was Not Said There, And On Which Nobody In Hask Was Relieved And Nobody Was Forgiven And Nobody Was Thanked

Fifth day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and sixty-second morning. Two hundred and twelve days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and no days. One hundred and sixty-six days since the division. One hundred and twelve days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

There is no reading in Hask on a Saturday. There is no lamp lit under a lamp on a Saturday in this town and there is no slate, and the only reason anybody stands on that plank on a Saturday is that there is a boat.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the twelve days.

A woman of sixty-eight came up that plank at about the fourth hour with what she had not sold in a basket on her arm and a length of oilcloth over her head. She walked eleven feet of it without stopping. She is the fourth chair from the after end, and she sat down in it in front of about nine people, and the count came down from ten to nine while she was doing it.

About four people on that deck knew exactly why the count had moved. About nine did not, and the woman who keeps the book was not on the plank and was not told until the Wednesday of the following week.

Nobody on that plank thanked her for the two days.

---

A woman of seventy-one kept a house on the low side of the landing that was not hers, and she had kept it eleven years. On the Tuesday of the second week she came up the plank with wet sleeves and said out loud that this is not the right of refusal. She said that a person who cannot be asked is a smaller thing and an uglier thing and is not anybody’s no. She has not come up the plank since. Nobody in that town has asked her a second thing and she has not offered one.

On the Friday he asked her for the first one himself.

He went down off the plank and along the hard ground to the low side, past the reed beds and the two lengths of withy heaped by the hedge, and he knocked on the door of a house with a low lintel and a slate nailed to the frame with no name on it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman of seventy-one, before she had the door open a hand’s width. “*Because it is a man of thirty-one at my door at about the ninth hour on a Saturday and I have not had anybody at this door since the Tuesday, and I would like it said out loud in front of my own door that I noticed it before I opened it.*”

“*I am standing in front of one woman of seventy-one and a door, and I am not asking you for a chair.*”

It was about four seconds and nobody filled them.

“*Then that is the second time this week a man has said that to me,*” said Eliza Lark, “*and the first one was me.*”

---

Then he asked her a question that was not about a chair, and the question was a real one, and the answer was the instrument, refused in four sentences, and it was not the same refusal.

“*I want to know how many people have been through this house since Monday,*” said Ilyan, “*and I am asking you because you are the only person in this town anybody can be sent for about anything. About four people have told me this week that things are being said in this house that are not being said by you, and I would like to know the number before I say the next thing.*”

“*No,*” said Eliza Lark.

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am not refusing you the chair, because you did not ask me for the chair and I have not said that you did.*”

Nobody on that step moved.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am refusing you because you have come down here to be refused. You said the words not asking at the door before you had the door open a hand’s width. A person who has not asked for a thing is the only kind of person who cannot be made to say yes to it. You have come down here to be the only kind of person in this county again, and I have watched eleven years of that from a window.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say what you have watched,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have watched about nine people in this town be sent for about a thing and take it. I have watched two of them be sent for twice. I have watched one of them be the thing that the other eight were sent for, and I know exactly what that costs, because I have been paying it. The price is eleven years of a house that is not mine. There is no column anywhere in this county for what I gave up and no person I could go to and say the name of.*”

---

He had not known that. He stood on that step and found out that the woman of seventy-one had been the instrument for eleven years and had not been asked once, and that the two facts had been sitting nine feet apart on opposite sides of the same landing since the Tuesday.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan, and he heard himself do it a second time inside four seconds.

Nobody on that step said anything to it.

“*I have held this house for a man who is not in it and has not been in it for nine years and is not dead. I have never once been asked whether I wanted it, and if I had said no I would have had nowhere in this county to go. I have never told anybody that. I am telling a man of thirty-one on a Saturday because he has been eleven days in this town, and he is the first person who has come to this door without wanting something out of the room.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Say it to the person who keeps the chairs. Not to me. Say it to Orla Fennimore, in front of about nine people, on a Tuesday, in the open, and put the day on it. Then somebody will have a line in that book that says a woman of seventy-one is holding a house she did not agree to hold, and that is a column that book has never had, and I am not asking for it. I am asking you not to put it in your coat and carry it about. There is a difference and it is the only difference I have left.*”

---

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it out loud, on a step, at the ninth hour of a Saturday, and it cost him the thing he had come down there with.

“*I have just been told the truest thing anybody has said to me in four counties, and it is that a person who cannot be sent for is not a clean thing to put in a book. I have heard that before this week from a woman of twenty-nine in exactly those words. About four of us now know the same thing about me and about nine of us have it a different way, and I would like it said out loud in front of my own door that I have now had it twice from two mouths in nine days, and that I did not know the second one had it too.*”

Nobody on that step moved.

“*And I am not going to carry it about, because I have been asked not to by the only person in this county who could ask me, and because I have been the other kind of person in four counties and I know what it is for.*”

---

Then the third thing was said, and it was said in that house, and it was not said there by anybody in that house, and the woman of seventy-one had not said it and did not know who had.

“*I am going to say a thing and I am going to say it because you have just told me a true thing and I would not be able to hold both of them otherwise. I would like it said out loud in this room that I am saying it to you and not to the man with the pole, because the man with the pole is the only other person in this town who has heard it. He has not asked me for it and he is not going to be told by me in a kitchen.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*On the Thursday, in this room, in the middle of the afternoon, a man said a name. Four words, and the four words were its own name. I have been in this house thirty-one years and I have never heard anybody say that name out loud in a room, and I said it back to ask whether I had heard it right, and the man looked at the door and said that I had.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*I did not say it. I want that said first. I have had about nine people through this house since Monday and I have not said it once, and there is no way on this earth of telling which of the nine of them said it, and I have looked at it for two days and I am not going to guess in front of a man of thirty-one.*”

“*Who came through on the Thursday,*” said Ilyan.

“*Nine. Two of them were children and I would not send a child back down that plank alone, so I kept them until their people came. One of the nine is a woman of about thirty-four who came down the water on the Friday with the man of forty-one, and she has not left my kitchen since, and she is asleep on the settle at this moment. I have not asked her a single thing about it and I am not going to.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*That is the part I would like written down, and it is not a part about the four words. It is that a thing that was said in a weigh-house nine miles up this water by something that was not a person has come down here as a thing a man says in a room at the middle of the afternoon, in the sun, in flat words. It is a different thing. The difference is that anybody who has been told it can say it again, and about nine of us in this house have been told it and I do not know which of the nine.*”

---

Nobody in that kitchen was relieved and nobody in that kitchen was forgiven and nobody in that kitchen was thanked.

The fever was forty-seven weeks old. He had been in that county fifty-eight days and could not leave it until the water went over a block of limestone nine miles up this river, and he could not tell anybody when.

A woman of seventy-one has kept a house that is not hers for eleven years and was never asked, and she has told that to one person and has asked him not to carry it about, and he is not going to.

And in that kitchen a woman of about thirty-four is asleep on a settle who came down the water on the Friday with a man of forty-one from a town two days off, and she has been in a chair in another county since she was nineteen, and nobody on this plank has met her.
