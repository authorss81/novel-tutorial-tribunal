# Chapter 626: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Eleventh Week, And A Saturday, And On Which About Nine People Walk Down A Cart Road Into A Front Room On The Low Side Of A Landing With The Door Open And They Are Neither Sent Nor Asked And They Go, And On Which The Person On One Side Of The Split Says The True Thing About The Other Side And About Nine People In That Room Are The Person On The Other Side, And On Which The Other Side Says The True Thing Back, And On Which Nothing Is Resolved And A Man Of Thirty-One Is In The Room And Is Wrong In It

Fifth day of the hundred and eleventh week. His three hundred and seventy-sixth morning. Two hundred and twenty-six days after the settlement. The fever forty-nine weeks and no days. One hundred and eighty days since the division. One hundred and twenty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About nine people went down that cart road on the Saturday afternoon and about four of them were on the side that wants the copies to stop and about four of them were on the side that wants them to go, and nobody sent any of them and nobody asked any of them, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a person may be asked to go to a room or may not.

The room had a low lintel and a slate nailed to the frame with no name on it and a fire in it that was not lit, and the door was open because it was the fourth hour of a Saturday, and the woman who keeps that house was seventy-one and she did not ask any of them why they had come and she said so out loud before any of them said anything.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody for the walking.

---

Then a woman of forty who wants a day written against every name in that book said the true thing about the other half of the split, in front of about nine people, and it took her about nine sentences, and about four of the nine had not thought it and about nine of them had known it and had not said it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow. “*Because I have been the loudest person in this town for three days about putting a day against a name, and I am going to say the true thing about the half of this room that is against those copies, and it is not mine, and I have had it since about a Tuesday and I have been waiting for a room.*”

About four people in that room said something at once. About nine did not.

“*I have said in front of about nine people that a day is not a judgement, and that if a man of thirty-one writes a day against a name then that man was in a chair on that day and about nine people can be sent for to ask him what he said. That is true and I stand behind it. And I have not once asked who it hurts.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*The half of this room that is against the copies says that a thing which can be sent for about is a thing that goes where it goes. That is true.*”

“*And the true thing about that half is that every person in it who is under sixty-five has been in that book nineteen years without being asked, and so have I, and so has the woman of forty-four who keeps it, and so has the woman of twenty-nine who cut withies off the land of the ninth and tenth lines. And so has a woman of seventy-one in this room.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*My mother is the fourth chair from the after end. She is sixty-eight. She went to a fair at a crossroads four miles off on the Wednesday morning of the ninth week and came back on the Saturday and sat down and did not know she had gone into a number, and there is no column in that book for a fair. I have been in that book nineteen years and nobody has ever asked me a question about it. About nine people in this room are the thing that half is protecting, and we have all come down here and sat down and said we are not it.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for nineteen years.

---

Then the other half said the true thing back, and it was said by a woman of thirty who has said it once and has not been asked for it twice, and about four people in that room had not expected it out of her and about nine had.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am going to say the true thing about your half and I am going to say it once and I have said it once already on a Wednesday and I have not been asked for it since.*”

About four people in that room understood that. About nine did not.

“*The half of this room that wants the copies to keep going says that a record nobody can be sent for about is a lid, and it is right, and every one of you said it as a thing about somebody else.*”

“*About four of you have said in the last three days that you want a day written against a name so that the man in a chair can be asked what he said, and about four of you are the man in a chair, and one of you keeps the book for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year and has never been asked what it is for.*”

About four people in that room said nothing at all.

“*And about nine people in this room are the thing your half is afraid of, and you have all come down here and said you are not that as well. I am not saying you are lying. I am saying that a thing you are frightened of is a thing you are in.*”

Nobody in that room said anything to that, and the fire was not lit, and about four people in that room looked at the woman of forty-four for about as long as a person looks at a thing and then looked somewhere else.

---

Then a woman of thirty-four who came nine miles down the water on a Friday a fortnight ago and has been in a chair since she was nineteen said one sentence, and it was the only thing said in that room in a fortnight that was about her. About four people in that room heard it and about nine heard a sentence about a book.

“*I have been in a chair for fifteen years,*” said Dorcas Slee, “*and the sentence about the lid is the only sentence anybody has said to me in fifteen years that was about me, and I have stood in about four rooms in nine days and watched about nine people say it about something else.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*I am not on either side of it and I am not going to be. I said that on a Sunday nine days ago and I have not changed and I am not going to be asked to. I came nine miles to be asked whether I wanted a chair and I have not been asked, and about four people in this room have been in a chair and about four of them have not been asked either, and the one who has been asked is the one who has been in it the longest and she is standing against a wall.*”

About four seconds went past.

Then the man of thirty-one said the true thing about one half of the split and nothing at all about the other half, and he did it in about nine sentences, and a woman of thirty-four caught it in about the fourth.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have been in this room since the fourth hour and I have been in it for about forty minutes and I have said one true thing. It is about the half that wants the copies to stop, and it is that they are not stopping anything, they are deciding where the harm of a record falls, and that is not the same thing, and about four of them have not been asked which it is.*”

About four people in that room said something at once. About nine did not.

“*You have done the thing,*” said Dorcas Slee.

Nobody in that room thanked her for it.

“*You have said the true thing about one half of a room and not one word about the other half, in about nine sentences, in front of about nine people, and about four of us have noticed and about nine have not. That is the fifth time in nineteen days. I have watched you do it twice on a deck in two days and I stopped it once and it is a different room and it is the same thing.*”

About four people in that room understood that. About nine did not.

“*And I would like it said out loud that a man of thirty-one who has been standing in a room for about forty minutes saying one true thing about one half of it is doing the thing he came to this town not to do, and that about four people in this room are glad of it and about nine of them cannot tell.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I heard it. I heard it from a woman of thirty-four and I have heard it four times this week in four counties’ worth of sentences and I heard it in this county first from a woman of twenty-six who told me on a Thursday that standing in a room and being wrong in it is the whole of it. I have been in a room for forty minutes and I have been careful in it, and I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that careful is what I do instead of wrong, and that about four of you can see the difference and about nine of you cannot.*”

Nobody in that room said anything to that.

---

Then a woman of seventy-one who is on neither side of it said the one thing that was left, and she said it in about nine sentences, and she said at the end of it that she would not say it a fourth time in that town.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Nothing in this room has been resolved, and I would like it said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that nothing in this room has been resolved. About four of you have come down that cart road expecting to be right by the seventh hour, and about nine of you have not, and about four of you have not noticed which one you are.*”

About four people in that room understood that. About nine did not.

“*I am not going to say which half of this room is right. I said on a Thursday that I was not going to be asked, and I have been asked twice since, by a man of thirty-one standing nine feet off and by a man of sixty-eight standing on my floor, and both of them did it in the open and both of them meant it and it makes no difference whatever.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for eleven years.

“*I have said the shape of this thing three times in this town in about eleven days and I will not say it a fourth. The next person who says it is going to have to be one of you, and I am going to sit on that settle and listen to it, and about four of you will find that unbearable and about nine of you will not notice.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*And I have not been told that there is a line in a book with my eleven years on it and a day against it, and I have not asked, and I am not going to ask. There is a man of thirty-one who knows and who has not come down that cart road today, and I have not sent for him and I have not asked anybody to.*”

About four seconds went past.

---

The fever was forty-nine weeks old. He had been in that county seventy-two days, and about nine people sat in a front room on the low side of a landing with the door open. About four of them were on one side of a split and about four were on the other and about one was on neither, and nothing was resolved. Both halves said the true thing about the other half, and about nine people in that room were the thing that the other half is protecting.

And a man of thirty-one said the true thing about one half of it and not one word about the other, and he did it in about nine sentences. A woman of thirty-four from another county, who has been in a chair since she was nineteen and came nine miles to be asked and has not been, caught it in about the fourth and told him so in about nine people’s hearing. She said it was the fifth time in nineteen days, and that about four of them were glad of it and about nine of them could not tell.

And a woman of seventy-one who is on neither side of it said that she has said the shape of it three times in eleven days and will not say it a fourth, and that she has not been told that there is a line in a book with her eleven years on it, and that she has not asked, and that she did not send for anybody.
