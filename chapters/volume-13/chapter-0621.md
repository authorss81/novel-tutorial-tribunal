# Chapter 621: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Tenth Week, And A Monday, And On Which Two Copies Of A Sheet Of Deal Are Nailed Side By Side And Read Against Each Other In The Open For The First Time In The Same Room, And On Which They Agree, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-Eight Who Gave The Rule On The Thursday Of The Tenth Week Asks For A Thirty-First Line And Has It Put On Both Of Them About A Man Of Thirty-One Who Was At That Moment Nine Feet Off With A Withy Knife In His Hand, And On Which About Nine People In A Town Of About Eleven Hundred People Find Out That A Thing Can Be True And Be About Somebody Else

Seventh and last day of the hundred and tenth week. His three hundred and seventy-first morning. Two hundred and twenty-one days after the settlement. The fever forty-eight weeks and two days. One hundred and seventy-five days since the division. One hundred and twenty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the sixth day of the hundred and tenth week.

---

The man of thirty-eight had gone up the water on the Sunday morning with the Gull and had come back down at about the seventh hour on the Sunday night, and the second copy of that sheet had been under oilcloth inside the boat for about eighteen hours.

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for the going.

It is nine miles up and it is nine miles down and he poled the whole of it because there is nobody in that town he would have asked to pole it. He stood in the yard of the landing at about the second hour on the Sunday and read that copy out loud, in the open, all of it, twice, the way a man reads a thing in a yard where about nine people are standing about and the rest of them can hear the words and not the sense.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, on the bank, on the Monday morning, with the two sheets lying on the deck boards under her hands. “*Because about nine people are going to be on that deck before the seventh hour and the rule says the two are read against each other once a week in the open, and I would like it said out loud that until this morning that has never once happened in a room.*”

“*They have agreed four times,*” said Ilyan.

“*A man of thirty-eight has agreed with a man of thirty-eight in two yards about nine miles apart,*” said Wenna Callow, “*and that is four agreements and not one, and I do not say that to make anything smaller. I say it because the rule is a week old and I would like to know how many weeks it is going to stand on.*”

About four people on that deck had that. The other nine did not.

---

Then the two were nailed up side by side on the inside of the gunwale above the after thwart, about four inches apart, so that a person standing on the deck could read down one and along the other without moving.

It took about nine minutes to read and it is in this record that it took about nine minutes, because thirty lines was read with the ground said before the name every single time, and nobody in that town has ever once read one of those lines in a hurry and the day before was a Sunday and the count was said on it.

The twenty-nine chairs were read. The woman of sixty-eight was in the fourth chair from the after end with a basket at her feet. The woman of twenty-six was in the ash and had not moved. The reed was read and nobody was in it. The man of fifty-four was read in two places, the gravel at the head of the drain and the plank, and both lines said a day.

And the thirtieth line was read, which is the line the man of sixty-eight asked for on the Thursday for the last quarter mile of the cart road and the plank, and it is the eleventh thing of its kind in that town and about four people in that town had forgotten it was on there.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I asked for it because the plank is why nine of us came up here,*” said Pell Vearn, from the gravel. “*I have not read it back. I would like it said out loud that I have not read it back in five days, and that a thing a man asks to be put on a sheet is not the same thing as a thing a man has read.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked him for the line.

The two sheets were read against each other. Line against line, name against name, day against day, and at the thirtieth line one of them had a day on it and the other had a day on it and the days were the same day.

They agreed in every line.

About four people on that deck said out loud that they had been wrong about something and about nine did not, and nobody asked them what.

---

Then a man of sixty-eight who is not in a chair and has never been in one and cannot be corrected in one said that a line had to go on both of them, and he said why, and it took him about nine sentences, and about four people on that deck understood it at once and about nine did not.

“*I gave that rule on Thursday morning and I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that I have been thinking about it for five days and that this is what it is for.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*A thing that is not a chair can be a thing a person is sent for about. That is what I said. I said it about a plank and about a quarter mile of a cart road, and I would like it said that I said it and that nobody made me say it and that I have not said one word about a man of thirty-one until this morning.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*And there is a sheet of deal in this county with thirty lines on it and about nine of them are about people, and every one of those people can be sent for to be asked what they said and on what day, because they are in chairs and a person in a chair is a person anybody can ask. And the thirty-first line is not a chair and it is not a piece of ground either.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And the woman who keeps the chairs said that on the sixth day of the second week, out loud, on that deck, in front of about nine people, and nobody wrote it down, and she has not come up that plank since the sixth day of the ninth. She said that a man of thirty-one is not his turn and not his ground, no household of his in this county, not a son of anybody on that list, and nobody nine miles off can be sent for to ask him whether he would like to be on it.*”

Nobody on that deck moved a hand, and the man of thirty-eight poled once and stopped.

“*That is her sentence. It is not mine. I am putting it on a sheet because a sheet is the only place in this county where a true thing is still a true thing on a Tuesday.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked him for it.

---

Then the line was written, and it was written twice, once on each sheet, and the two were written by two different people in two different places, and that is the whole of why they agree.

At Hask it was written on Monday at about the fourth hour by a boy of eleven on a board on a barrel, because there is no other table in Hask, and it took him about as long again as it took the man of forty-three and he was slower at it.

Nobody on that deck said anything while either of them was doing it.

Then the ground was said before the name, in the open, and the line was read out loud in a yard nine miles up the water and on a deck nine miles down it, and here it is.

“*The ground: a building with a bad coffee machine in it, in a country that is not this one, a hundred and nine miles off and across a sea, where a mark of two letters and a number is signed at the bottom of about nine hundred records. The name: one that nobody in this county makes. The day: the seventh and last day of the hundred and eighth week, a Monday, when a man of thirty-eight asked a man of thirty-one where he came from at about the fourth hour, in front of about nine people, and he answered in four things and none of the four of them was the answer.*”

---

And the man of thirty-one was nine feet off at the head of the plank, on his knees in the wet, cutting about nine feet of a hedge above the landing at about two inches at a time, with a withy knife, because Nell Prince had put the knife in his hand at about the eighth hour and had said nothing at all about it.

There is a knot in the middle of every length that was in the hedge before it was cut. He had been told that on the first day and had been putting the knots in a heap on the bank because he did not know what else to do with about nine hundred of them.

A boy of eleven came down that bank at about the eleventh hour and told him four lines out of a yard, in the wrong order, and ran back up.

Nobody said anything to him about it for about four seconds.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan, to about nine people, on a deck, nine feet off a bank.

“*There is a line about me,*” said Ilyan. “*I have been told that from the eleventh hour. I would like it said out loud in front of all of you that I have not asked for it and cannot stop it and did not hear it being written, and that I was holding a withy knife at the time and about nine hundred knots in a heap at my knee.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*And I would like the other half said in the same breath.*”

“*A man of sixty-eight who is not in a chair put it there under a rule he gave on Thursday, and the rule is his, and I gave that rule to him by asking him a question on a Thursday morning. About nine people in this town have that and about nine do not, and neither of those is going to be the thing I say next.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

---

The fever was forty-eight weeks and two days old. He had been in that county sixty-seven days. Two copies of a sheet of deal with thirty lines on it were nailed four inches apart on the inside of the gunwale of a lighter and were read against each other in the open and they agreed in every line, which is the first time in a fortnight that the rule has been worked and the first time that it has cost anybody anything.

And a thirty-first line went on both of them on a Monday at about the fourth hour, in a yard nine miles up the water and on a deck nine miles down it, written by a man of forty-three who has kept a landing nineteen years and by a boy of eleven on a board on a barrel. About nine people in a town of about eleven hundred people know that there is a thing they can be sent for about which is about a man who was nine feet off at the time with a withy knife in his hand.

And he is nobody’s. There is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it, no form that renews him, and no rate on him, and he is not one of about thirty men, because there is no hundred in that county either. The woman who keeps that book said on the sixth day of the second week that nobody nine miles off can be sent for to ask him whether he would like to be on it. On Monday a man of sixty-eight put it on a sheet in two places, and she is not on that bank and has not been told.
