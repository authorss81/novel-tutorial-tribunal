# Chapter 630: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Twelfth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-One Gives The Number Of The Copies Out Loud With The Working And Says What It Is A Figure Of And What It Is Not A Figure Of, And On Which A Boy Of Thirteen Is Named In His Mother’s Mouth And Asks A Question Out Loud In Front Of About Nine People That Nobody In That Town Answers, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Is Told By A Person In That Town That He May Not Answer It And Holds The No For The First Time In Nineteen Days, And On Which A Case Is Still Open And A Person Is Still Doing A Thing

Second day of the hundred and twelfth week. His three hundred and eightieth morning. Two hundred and thirty days after the settlement. The fever forty-nine weeks and four days. One hundred and eighty-four days since the division. One hundred and thirty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the nineteen days.

The case is open and it is not a case anybody can be sent for about, because there is no form in the county of Kell that makes a thing anybody can be sent for about unless it is a chair, and a chair is not what this is. A man of thirty-eight asked for a hearing about every record there is in a yard nine miles up that water on the Monday morning of the seventh and last day of the hundred and eleventh week.

Nobody granted it and nobody refused it and nobody has written it down, and it is sitting where a man said it out loud and about nine people heard it.



Two sheets of deal in that county are read against each other once a week in the open, and they agree, and the thirty-second line is in both of them. The woman of seventy-one whose eleven years it is has not come up that plank since the sixth day of the hundred and ninth week and has not sent for anybody and has not been asked.

---

Then a man of sixty-one gave the number out loud at about the tenth hour on the Wednesday morning, at the head of the plank, and about four people in that town counted it and about nine did not, and he gave the working.

“*Eleven on the Sunday. One at the landing on the Monday afternoon, written out by a man of about fifty-two who stood in that yard and read one and then asked for one of his own and was given it. He wrote it out himself in about four hours and would like it said that he is fifty-two and cannot see well, and that the fifth line is his and not the man’s.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

“*One in this town on the Tuesday morning, written out by a boy of thirteen. I would like it said out loud that it is the first sheet of deal a person under fourteen in this county has written out in the whole of nineteen years that I have been keeping a book at the other end of it, and his mother has told me to say it. I am saying it because she told me to and not because I thought of it.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And one at the landing on the Tuesday morning, written out by a man of forty-three who keeps it, out of a spare thwart he has had on the wall of that landing for nineteen years for a thing that has not happened.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Eleven and one and one and one is fourteen. That is fourteen pieces of wood in four counties with a copy of that sheet on them, and it is a figure of the wood. It is not a figure of how many people have read one, and about nine of us on that bank have read one and about four have read two and about nine in this town have read none and about four of them are standing here.*”

About four people on that bank counted that. About nine of the rest were looking at the water.

“*And I would like it said that five of the fourteen are a day and a half into a two-day road in a cart with a man of forty-one and a woman of thirty-four, and I do not know where they are and neither does anybody in this county, and that is the whole of what a man of sixty-one has got to say about a thing that can be sent for about.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for the number.

---

Then the boy of thirteen was named out loud in his mother’s mouth, and he was there, and he had been there all morning because his father is on that bank and there is a bench on that boat and nobody has ever told him he is not to.

“*Then say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow. “*Because a man of sixty-one has said out loud that a boy of thirteen wrote a sheet of deal on Tuesday morning, and about nine of us have not heard that, and the boy is standing about nine feet off with a pen in his hand, and I would like it said that he is thirteen and that I have known that all morning.*”

“*Say the boy,*” said Ibb Marshe, who was forty-one and had two carts and had not come up that plank in nine days and had come up it on the Saturday and had not said why, and who had not been thanked for either of those.

“*That is my Wat. He is thirteen. He is not in a chair and there is no column in that book for him, and he is not a head in the book of heads and carts. The penny a head is on everybody over sixteen who comes through that landing, and the reason for that is that a person over sixteen carries a basket and a person under sixteen does not. The man of sixty-one has kept it that way for eleven years and there is no form in this county that says he may.*”

About four people on that bank had that. About nine of the rest have never once been a head in a book of heads and carts.

“*He has never been asked for anything in his life. I would like that said out loud in front of about nine people and I would like it said by somebody who is not his mother and I am not going to say it any better than that.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything to that.

---

And then the boy of thirteen asked a question, out loud, in front of about nine people, and it was asked once, and about four people in that town understood all of it and about nine understood about half of it, and nobody in that town answered it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end, and he had not been up that water since Monday morning and he had poled down in the night and he had a child of nine at home and had not been sent for. “*Because a boy of thirteen has a question and about four of us have just heard the end of it and I would like the whole of it said out loud on a Wednesday morning in about nine people’s hearing, the way everything in this town is done, and I would like it said that I am not going to answer it either.*”

About four people on that bank said something at once. About nine of the others had come up a plank to find out what the boy of thirty-one was doing with his hand.

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a sheet of deal. I wrote one on Tuesday and I am going to write one on Friday, and I have read two of them out loud to about nine people each, which is about nine people twice. There is one of the fourteen that nobody is going to read out loud to anybody, because there is nobody in this county that is going to be in the room.*”

Nobody on that bank moved.

“*So who says how many people a thing has been read to. The man of sixty-one can say how many pieces of wood there are and he said so himself this morning and said it was not a figure of people. Nobody in this town can say how many people have read one of them, and there is no form in this county that would let anybody find out, and I am the only one who has read out one of them twice and I am thirteen and my father is on this bank.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

“*And I would like it said out loud that I asked it once, and that I am not going to ask it again this week. About four of you have got the whole of it and about nine of you have not. If the answer is that nobody ever says, then somebody has to write that down somewhere, and I do not know where, and I have been told there is a sheet of deal and I have been told there is a rule and I do not know of a third thing.*”

About four seconds went past.

---

Then a man of thirty-one opened his mouth, and a person in that town told him he might not answer it, out loud, in front of about nine people, and he held the no, and it was the first time in nineteen days that he had been told no by a person in that town and not said a thing anyway.

“*No,*” said Marda Vell, from the ash, where she had been for eleven days and had not stood up once and had not gone back to the reed. “*You may not answer it and I would like it said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that I am a person who has been in a chair for nine years and I have not stood up and I am saying no to a man of thirty-one on a Wednesday morning about a question a boy of thirteen asked.*”

About four people on that bank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Because you are the man that question is about in the only way it is about anybody. He has not asked how many people have read a sheet. He has asked who says. And there is no form in this county that would let a person be sent for about saying a number, and you are the only person in four counties that anybody cannot be sent for at all. If you stand up on that bank and give him a number it will be in about nine mouths by the seventh hour and in about four counties by the end of the week, and it will be the fifth time.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for saying no.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Hold it. That is all. I am not going to tell you what holding it is worth. I have been in a chair for nine years and I said yes in the open in about nine people’s hearing, and I have never been asked whether I wanted it, and I have not been able to do one single thing about it. The one thing I have learned in nine days is what a person sounds like when they are holding something and not putting it down.*”

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them and he said nothing.

---

Then the man of thirty-one said out loud what he was not going to do, and it was the smallest thing he had said in thirty days, and about four people on that bank had it and about nine did not.

“*I am going to be nine feet off at the head of that plank at about the fourth hour this afternoon with a withy knife, because a woman of twenty-nine gave it to me on the Wednesday of last week and did not ask for it back. I have not asked her for it, and about four of you have noticed that and about nine have not.*”

About four people on that bank said nothing at all.

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for anything at all, and nobody in Hask was relieved and nobody in Hask was forgiven and nobody in Hask was thanked.

---

The fever was forty-nine weeks and four days old. He had been in that county seventy-six days and he has a year of his own future against a block of limestone nine miles up that river, and he cannot leave the county until the water goes over the cut. He does not know when and nobody can tell him, and he can tell them about that water to about a quarter of an inch without looking at anything.

He is nobody’s. There is no column in the county of Kell with his name at the top of it, no form that renews him and no rate on him, and he is not one of about thirty men, because there is no hundred in that county either. The woman who keeps that book said on the sixth day of the second week that nobody nine miles off can be sent for to ask him whether he would like to be on it. It has been on a sheet in two places since Tuesday.

The count of the chairs is nine. Eleven on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighth week. Ten from the Thursday of it, nine from the Saturday of the hundred and ninth week, ten since the Wednesday of the hundred and tenth week, and nine since about the second hour of the first day of the hundred and twelfth week, when a woman of twenty-nine sat down in the reed in the open in front of about nine people and said out loud that she had not got a reason that will be believed in about four years.

And a case is open. A man of thirty-eight asked for a hearing about every record there is in a yard nine miles up the water and nobody granted it and nobody refused it and nobody has written it down.

And a man of sixty-one says the number is fourteen, and he says what it is a figure of, which is wood. A boy of thirteen who is not a head in the book of heads and carts and not a name in the book of chairs asked out loud, once, on a Wednesday morning, in about nine people’s hearing, who says how many people a thing has been read to.

Nobody in that town has an answer, and a boy of thirteen is still standing about nine feet off on a bank with a pen in his hand and about four hours of daylight left in the day.

A woman of twenty-six who has been in a chair for nine years told a man of thirty-one he might not answer it, and he said nothing, and about nine feet off at the head of that plank a woman of twenty-nine is cutting withies, and about four people in a town of about eleven hundred people will go home this evening carrying a question they have nowhere to put, and there is no form anywhere in that county that would let anybody send for them about it.
