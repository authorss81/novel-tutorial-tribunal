# Chapter 605: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Eighth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Offers In About Nine Sentences To Be The Instrument For Ten Chairs In A Town Of About Eleven Hundred People Where He Has No Household And No Ground And Nobody Nine Miles Off Who Can Be Sent For To Ask Him, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Who Is In One Of The Chairs Refuses Him In About Nine People’s Hearing With A Reason That Is About Him And Not About The Case, And On Which The Thing He Put Down Against A Block Of Limestone Nine Miles Up The Water Is Used Against Him By An Ordinary Person And Is True, And On Which He Is Given A Boat-Hook At About The Sixth Hour And Holds It

Fifth day of the hundred and eighth week. His three hundred and fifty-fifth morning. Two hundred and five days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and no days. One hundred and fifty-nine days since the division. One hundred and five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the fourth day of the hundred and eighth week.

---

There is no reading in Hask on a Saturday. There is no lamp lit under a lamp on a Saturday in this town and there is no slate and no boy who holds it, and the only reason anybody stands on that plank on a Saturday is that there is a boat.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the five days.

---

Then a man of thirty-one offered, and he offered in about nine sentences with the working said out loud, because that is the only way he has ever been able to put a thing down in a room in four counties.

He had been in that county fifty days. He had a year of his own future against a block of limestone with a line cut round it, nine miles up the water, and he was counting from a river every morning and could not stop, and about nine people in a hundred nine miles off had believed the price when he said it and about nine had not.

He wanted to be the thing that carried the ten, because there was nothing else on offer and because he had been a thing that carried four sentences out of a room in a town nine miles off and had never once been sorry of it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end. “*Because a man of thirty-one has been on this deck five days and has not said one word about himself until now, and about four of us have been waiting for it since Wednesday, and about nine of us have not.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people, ten chairs, and about eleven hundred people in this town. I am going to say a thing, and about four of you will understand it and about nine of you will not. I am saying it because I have nothing else to offer, and not because it is a good idea.*”

Nobody on that plank said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore.

“*There are ten chairs with a name on them and nobody in them. There is no son to ask, because they had none. There is a man of thirty-eight on this deck who cuts the reed and is not a son and has not said yes. There is a woman of twenty-nine who has said yes once, on Thursday, to a ground she is the near party to.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And there is a man of thirty-one standing in front of about nine people who has no household in this county, no ground, no seat, no roll, and nobody nine miles off who can be sent for to ask whether he would like to be asked,*” said Ilyan. “*I have offered in nine counties to be the thing that does the carrying, and about four people in each of them said yes. I am offering now.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*and say what you are offering exactly, because a name in a taking column is not a small thing to be handed to a man in his fifth day in a town.*”

“*I am offering to sit in the ten. All of them. Not one at a time. All of them, standing up on this deck, with a yes said out loud in front of about nine people, and a day against each one in the book, and no form in this county that could make any person in this town do anything about it. That is the same thing nine times over and not once.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody.

“*Then say why,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*and say it as a person of this town and not as a man of a record.*”

“*Because I am the only person in this room who cannot be sent for, and a thing that cannot be sent for is the only instrument there has ever been that nobody can be leaned on. And because I put a year of my own future down against a stone nine miles off on a Thursday in a room over a stable, and about nine people believed it and about nine did not. I have been in this town five days, and about nine of you have been kind to me and about nine of you have not, and I would like one of those two things to come out of me before the water goes over that stone again.*”

---

Then a woman of twenty-nine who was in one of the chairs refused him, and it was in about nine people’s hearing, and it was four sentences and nobody interrupted her, and it was about him and not about the case.

“*No,*” said Nell Prince.

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end, where he had a line across the boat he was not taking up.

“*I am not refusing you because you are a stranger and I am not refusing you because it is a Friday, and I would like that said before anything else is said about this. On Wednesday I took a chair off a man who is dead and I have not regretted it and I am not going to be one of about nine people in this town who say no to a stranger on a Saturday because the week has been long.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am refusing you because you cannot be asked. That is the whole of it and it is about you and not about the ten. Every one of those ten needs a person who can be sent for, and you are the only person on this deck who cannot be. In about four years somebody in this town is going to be stood up on that plank against your name, and there is not going to be anybody to stand up, because there has been nobody you can be sent for since the day you came off that cart. The standing up was going to be the whole of what that person did.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore.

“*And I would like it said on this deck that a man who cannot be asked is not a clean thing to put in a book. A book is a list of people who can be found. Orla Fennimore is a person who can be found, and she has been for nineteen years, and it is a poor wage and it is a house and it is findable. And in about four years somebody will stand on this plank and say that a man of thirty-one held ten chairs for ten days in one week in the year he was here, and they will be right, and there will be no way to put the chair back or to ask the man what he thought about it.*”

About four people on that plank said nothing at all. About nine said something at once.

---

Then the thing he had put down against a block of limestone nine miles up the water came out of his own mouth in about nine people’s hearing. It is the only figure about him that anybody in that town can be checked against, and a woman of twenty-nine was entitled to it, and nobody had thought to ask her whether she wanted it first.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*A woman has just said no to a man of thirty-one in front of about nine people. About four of us understood it and about nine of us did not, and the one of the nine who is going to understand it last is a man of thirty-one.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people in a town nine miles up the water, and I cannot leave it, and I do not know when the water goes over that stone, and nobody can tell me, and about nine people believed the price and about nine did not.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*And do not tell me what a year is in days, because about four of us in this town have a book and about nine of us have not, and a day-count is not a chair.*”

“*It is a year of my own future from the crossing of that stone to the crossing after it. It is not a wound and it is not a figure of weeks and there is no form in this county that makes it or that stops it. It is checked at that stone and nowhere else. And I have been counting from a river every morning since a Thursday morning in a room over a stable, and I did not choose it and I cannot stop it, and it is not a thing I can do anything with.*”

About four people on that plank had that. About nine did not.

“*Then it is the reason and not the fault,*” said Nell Prince, “*and I would like it minuted in the way this town minutes things, which is that somebody says it out loud and it goes nowhere. You offered because you cannot be sent for, and I said no because you cannot be sent for, and both of those are true. About four of us now know the same thing about you, and about nine of us will have it a different way by the fourth hour, and I have not said it to make you smaller. I have said it because you would have done it in about four seconds, and I am the one who would have had to sit in the eleventh chair afterwards and answer for it.*”

Nobody in that boat was sorry for anybody.

---

Then the sixth hour came and he was standing on a deck doing nothing with his hands, and a man of thirty-eight put a boat-hook into them and did not explain it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because it is about the sixth hour and there is a man of thirty-one on my deck holding his hands like a man at a market who has been told the price of a thing. About nine of us have watched him do that all week, and I am going to give him a hook, and he can do what he likes with it.*”

He gave him a boat-hook. It is about five feet of ash with a crook of iron at the end and it is older than anybody on that boat and the crook is polished on one side only, which is the side that has been used.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for it.

The fever was forty-six weeks and no days old. A man of thirty-one offered in nine sentences to be the instrument for ten chairs, and was refused in four sentences by a woman of twenty-nine. The reason she gave was about him and not about the case: a person who cannot be asked is not a thing a book can be built on. The year he put down against a block of limestone nine miles up the water turned out to be the truest thing anybody said about him in a town of about eleven hundred people.

And a man of thirty-eight gave him a boat-hook at about the sixth hour because he was standing on a deck with his hands doing nothing, and about nine people on that plank have the first shape of eleven and about nine have the second, and the two nines have not asked each other.

And he is standing at the after end of a lighter holding a five-foot ash stick with an iron crook polished on one side only, and he does not know what it is for, and he has not put it down.
