# Chapter 620: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Tenth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Woman Of Thirty-Four Asks Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing Whether The Saying Of No Has To Be Written Down The Way The Saying Of Yes Is Written Down, And On Which It Is Asked Once And Nobody In That Town Answers It, And On Which The Woman Who Could Answer It Best Is Forty Feet Off At A Landing And Nobody Goes And Gets Her, And On Which The Count Is Said Out Loud Again And It Is Ten, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-Eight Is Still Cutting About Nine Feet Off A Hedge At About Two Inches At A Time, And Nobody In Hask Was Relieved And Nobody Was Forgiven And Nobody Was Thanked

Sixth day of the hundred and tenth week. His three hundred and seventieth morning. Two hundred and twenty days after the settlement. The fever forty-eight weeks and one day. One hundred and seventy-four days since the division. One hundred and twenty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

There is no sitting in Hask on a Sunday. The chairs are stood up along the starboard side of the Gull in two rows and nobody is in any of them, and the only reason anybody is on that deck on a Sunday is that there is a boat and a man of thirty-eight going up the water with a load of boards that has not come.

The woman who keeps the book has not come up that plank since the sixth day of the hundred and ninth week, and that is a week, and about four people in that town have said out loud that a week is a week and about nine have not, and nobody has sent for her and she has not come.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the nineteen days.

---

Then a woman of thirty-four asked a question, out loud, in front of about nine people, and it was asked once.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Dorcas Slee, on the deck, in flat shoes she had not got wet in yet. “*Because a man of thirty-one has been on that deck for a fortnight saying true things out loud, and about nine of this town have never once been asked anything. I have been in a chair for fifteen years and I know exactly what is missing from it, and it is not a column and it is not a second copy.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*The book writes a yes down. When anybody in this town says yes to a chair in the open it goes in a column and a day goes against it and about nine people can be sent for to ask them what they said. A no does not. A no is a person standing up in the open and saying the word, and it is the only door out of a chair that anybody in this county has, and it does not go anywhere.*”

Nobody spoke for about four seconds.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have asked two women in this town in two days why there is no column for a reason, and I have been told it is because a column for a reason is a column for a reason. They are right, and they have not been asked the other one.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. The other nine did not.

“*If a no is not written down, then in about four years there will be a form in this county that says a person who says no is a person who is not in the chair and did not want to be in it. The two are not the same. About four of us can see that they are not the same, and about nine of us can see it, and only one of us can put it down.*”

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

“*So I am asking it. Out loud, once, in front of about nine people, on a Sunday, in a town of about eleven hundred people where I have been two days. Whether the saying of no has to be written down the way the saying of yes is written down, and if it is not, who decided that, and when, and on what day, and out loud in front of nine people the way everything in this town is done.*”

It was about four seconds.

Nobody on that plank said anything.

---

Then a man of thirty-one said out loud that somebody ought to go and get the woman, and about nine people heard it, and the woman who could answer it best was forty feet off at a landing, and nobody went.

“*Then somebody should go and get the woman who keeps the book,*” said Ilyan, “*because she is forty feet off and she has kept that book for nineteen years and she is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for about a chair, and this is a question about a chair.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Dorcas Slee.

“*She stopped coming up that plank on a Tuesday a week ago, because about nine of us had said a thing in front of about nine people, and she is the only person in this town that can be sent for. She was right. And you have not asked her for a second thing in seven days. You have asked four people for a column, a man of sixty-one about a sum, and me about a door.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And now you are going to go and get her, and put a question of a stranger from another county on a woman in this one, on a Sunday. If anybody in this town is going to carry that out of this room it is going to be you, and about nine of us are not going to be able to be sent for to say we agreed to it.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

“*And I am not saying no to her,*” said Dorcas Slee. “*I am saying not on a Sunday, and not out of a stranger’s mouth, and not on the day a boat is going up the water. I have been in a chair for fifteen years and I know that the worst thing that can be done to a person is to be asked in the middle of a thing by somebody who is leaving. Say it to her on a Tuesday, on the boat, in the open, with about nine people, and I will stand there and be asked it myself, and then there are two of us and one of us can be sent for.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

Nobody went and got her.

Then the man of thirty-one said out loud what he was going to do about it instead, and it was a small thing, and about four of the nine on that deck thought it was the right size.

“*Then say it out loud and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*I am going to be on that deck on the Tuesday, because the town sits on a Monday and a Thursday and I have been here nineteen days and have not once sat in one. On the Tuesday I am going to ask her, in the open, in front of about nine people, and if she says no, then I will have said no in front of about nine people about a chair.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And then I will find out what happens to a person who has said no in the open in this town, which is the thing a woman of thirty-four came nine miles for and did not get. I have not been asked a single question about any of it in nineteen days, and I am not going to go on not being asked one.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*And I would like it said out loud that I have just given a woman who came here to be asked a plan, and that that is the fifth thing I have done in nineteen days, and that about four of us have watched me do the other four.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Dorcas Slee.

“*You are learning it. It is not the same as knowing it, and I am not going to be the one who tells you which of the two you have.*”

---

Then the count was said out loud again, and it is ten, and the subtraction was said with it, and about four people on that deck have it and about nine have it a different way.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the fore end, where he had a line across the boat he was not taking up. “*Because a man of thirty-one has asked a question out loud on a Sunday and about nine of us have not answered it, and about nine of us have understood it. This town has been carrying a count for a fortnight, and about four of us have been carrying it wrong, and I would like it said one more time with the days in it.*”

“*Eleven on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighth week. Ten from the Thursday of that week, when a woman of twenty-nine sat down in the reed in about nine seconds. Nine from the Saturday of the hundred and ninth week, when a woman of sixty-eight came back from the fair at the crossroads and sat in the fourth chair from the after end, and did not know she was doing it.*”

About four people on that deck counted that against each other. About nine did not.

“*And ten since the Wednesday of this week, when a woman of twenty-nine stood up out of the reed in the open and took it back. It is ten now, on a Sunday, and one of the ten is standing nine feet off this boat with a knife in her apron.*”

About four people on that deck counted that against each other. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Wenna Callow, from the bank.

“*And the count follows the chair and not the person, and it has for nineteen years. My mother was in it for two days at a crossroads and out of it for nine years, and about nine people in this town have been reading it the other way round. A man of thirty-one has said a thing out loud in this town every day for nineteen days, and about four of the things he has said have been about which of those two numbers a person meant.*”

About four seconds went past.

---

Then the boards went up the water, and a man of thirty-eight poled, and a man of thirty-one did not go with them, and a man of about thirty-four who had put his own name against a chair in another county sat down on the bank and did not go either.

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for the nineteen days.

The fever was forty-eight weeks and one day old. He had been in that county sixty-six days and had a pound and fifteen shillings and eightpence of under two pounds left. He had a year of his own future against a block of limestone nine miles up this river, and he cannot leave the county until the water goes over, and there is no figure for it and nobody who can check it.

The count is ten. It was eleven, and it was ten, and it was nine for four days, and it is ten. One of the ten is a woman of twenty-nine who is cutting about nine feet off the hedge above the landing at about two inches at a time, and there is a knot in the middle of every length that was in the hedge before she cut it.

And a question was asked out loud on a Sunday in front of about nine people, once, and nobody in that town answered it. The woman who could answer it best is forty feet off at a landing, and nobody went and got her. A woman of thirty-four said it again in a kitchen on Monday and the week after that, and it has still not been answered and it is not going to be answered this week.
