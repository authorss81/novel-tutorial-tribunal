# Chapter 641: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirteenth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Crown Nine Days Old Is Asked What It Owes

Sixth day of the hundred and thirteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-first morning. Two hundred and forty-one days after the settlement. The fever fifty-one weeks and one day. One hundred and ninety-five days since the division. One hundred and forty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The room had a low lintel and a slate nailed to the frame with no name on it, and the door was open because it was the fourth hour of a Sunday, and the town does not sit on a Sunday. About nine people came down that cart road anyway because a question asked on a Saturday had not been answered.

The board was still on the barrel where it had stood since the Friday of the last week but one, and the nine names were under it in a hand nobody in that county makes, and the bench against the gunwale of the Gull was standing outside with the sun on it since the seventh hour.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody for the walking.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county eighty-seven days, and in Hask forty days, and the forty is a count of days since he came up that plank, and the eighty-seven is a count of days since he came into that county on the evening before the count started, and the two are different clocks, and he does not run them together.

He wanted the Saturday asked again before it went a week, and he said so out loud, and it was the wrong want in the right mouth, and he knew it before he had finished.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is forty and is on the board.

“*Because a man of thirty-four asked one thing from the doorway of an ash on Saturday morning. About nine people heard it. About four of them understood all of it, and nobody answered it.*”

“*And a question asked once in about nine people’s hearing and not answered is a thing people stop saying about after a week, and this one is a day old.*”

About four people in that room had the whole of it. About nine had the doorway and not the sentence.

Then she put it back to the thing at the barrel, and she did it the way a line is asked for, the ground before the name. About nine people in that room heard a Crown asked the hardest thing it had been asked yet.

“*There is a Crown in this town on a board on a barrel, and it is nine days old, and I am going to say the nine out loud, because a Crown that is not counted is a Crown that is not looked at.*”

“*It was put on that barrel on the Friday of the last week but one at about the fourth hour, and this is the Sunday of the next week, and Friday to Sunday is nine days, and I have counted it on my own hands and not out of a book.*”

Nobody in that room shifted.

“*It has been asked four things in nine days and it has got three of them right and it could not get the fourth one at all, and the fourth one was whether it could speak for a man it cannot be sent for about.*”

“*And a man of thirty-four asked on Saturday whether a person spoken for is owed a copy of what was said about them, and he said before he said it that it is not about him.*”

“*I am asking it again now, in the open, in front of about nine people, because a Crown that speaks for a person and is not asked what it owes that person is a Crown that has not been looked at.*”

“*If a Crown speaks for a person, is that person owed a copy of what it said about them.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them, and the open door let the weather in, and the fire in that room was not lit.

Then a man of sixty-one stood up off the wall he had been leaning on, and he had the book of heads and carts under his arm, and he did not open it, because the answer is not in it, and he said so out loud.

Nevin Trask has kept a landing nine miles up that water eleven years, and he takes a penny a head and a penny a cart, and he has cut a column into the back of the last leaf with a knife, and the column is about four inches wide and about nine inches long, and it is a number of people and not of heads and not of carts.

“*I am going to answer that, and I am going to answer it in the open, because about nine people in this town heard it asked on Saturday and about four of them have not stopped hearing it.*”

“*And I am not the woman of forty-four, and she answered a third of another question on the Tuesday of the eleventh week, and I am a different person, and this is the second time this answer has been given.*”

About four people in that room counted that against each other.

“*Nobody decided it.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for the three words.

“*And I am going to say why those three words are true, and I am going to say it with the working, because a figure nobody can take is a figure nobody has made a form for.*”

“*There is no form in this county that says a copy may be made of a thing a body said in a room, and I have kept a book eleven years and I have looked.*”

“*A sheet of deal is about four feet by a foot and four inches, and it is a thing a person writes with a hand, and a copy of it is a thing a person writes again with a hand.*”

“*A word said by nine people at a barrel is not written by any hand, and there is no sheet for it, and there is no board for it except the board it was said at.*”

“*If a Crown speaks for a man of fifty-seven who came up that landing about nine times in about two years, there is nowhere to write what it said except in about nine heads.*”

“*And about nine heads in one room in a town of about eleven hundred people is a figure of one room, and a woman of forty-one said that out loud on the Thursday of the last week, and she said it was no better than a boy’s question.*”

About four people in that room had that. About nine had the barrel and not the sheet.

“*So nobody decided whether a person spoken for is owed a copy, because there was nobody in a room deciding it, and there is no form that could have decided it.*”

“*And I would like it said that a Crown nine days old has just been asked the first thing anybody has asked it that it cannot take, and that it is still standing on that barrel.*”

Nobody in that room said a word to him about the standing.

Then Ilyan said the true of one half of it, and it was the eighth time in forty days, and a woman of twenty-nine in the reed let him go on to the fourth sentence and then held up a hand.

“*A Crown that can be asked what it owes is a Crown that can be asked whether it is right, and a man of sixty-eight said on the Friday that a Crown that can be asked whether a copy is right can stop a copy.*”

“*And a Crown nine days old has just been told out loud that there is no copy of what it says. About four people in this room have that as a comfort and about nine do not.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest is that I have been in Hask forty days, and I have been in that county eighty-seven days, and I have wanted for about nineteen days for a body to speak for me, and that is fourteen days on the Tuesday of last week and five days since, and I said no to it on the Wednesday.*”

“*And I am not asking for a copy of anything said about me, and I would like about four of you to notice that I did not ask.*”

About four people in that room understood that. The rest of them had a coat.

Then a woman of twenty-nine who has been in the reed since the Tuesday of the last week said why she had let him go on, and it was three sentences. About four people in that town had not known any of it.

“*I let him go on because he said the true thing about one half of that barrel and not one word about the other, and that is what he does.*”

“*And a man who says one half out loud in a room is a man who is learning to leave the other half for somebody else.*”

“*I have been in a chair for nine years and I have watched about nine people not learn it.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for nine years.

Then a man of thirty-eight who poles nine miles twice a week said what the week had cost, and he said it out loud, and he did not soften it, because a figure of hours is a figure anybody can check against a plank.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, and he has a child of nine, and he is the only person who may carry a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge, and he poled nine miles up that water on the Thursday at about the fifth hour and was at that landing at about the ninth hour and came down and got in at about the second hour of the afternoon.

“*Thursday was the fourth time the two of them have been in a room together, and the fourth time they have agreed, and the first of the four was on a Monday in this town.*”

“*And it took me about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading, and nobody has ever once asked me to do it, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to stop.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for nine hours.

About four people in that room counted the four against the three that had been said on Saturday. About nine heard a number and not a week.

“*And I would like it said that next Thursday is the fifth, and I am going up in the Gull at about the fifth hour with that sheet in a coat, and I am coming down at about the second hour of the afternoon.*”

Nobody in that room said anything about nineteen years.

---

The fever was fifty-one weeks and one day old. He had been in that county eighty-seven days, and a Crown nine days old was asked out loud in front of about nine people whether a person it speaks for is owed a copy, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that nobody decided it, and he is not the woman who said it first.

And the reason is that there is no form in that county that makes a copy of a thing a body said in a room, and a sheet is about four feet by a foot and four inches and is written by a hand, and a word at a barrel is written by no hand.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud that he is not asking for a copy, and a woman of twenty-nine let him say half of it, and a man of thirty-eight said out loud that Thursday was the fourth time two copies have agreed and next Thursday is the fifth.

And the slate nailed to the frame of that room has no name on it, and the chair inside it is not one of the twenty-nine and never was, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about it.
