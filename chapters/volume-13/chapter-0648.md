# Chapter 648: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Woman Of Seventy-One Is Asked A Fourth Time And Somebody Answers Her

Sixth day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-eighth morning. Two hundred and forty-eight days after the settlement. The fever fifty-two weeks and one day. Two hundred and two days since the division. One hundred and forty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town does not sit on a Sunday. About nine people came up that cart road anyway at about the fourth hour, because a Crown eight days old is a thing people come to look at whether it sits or not.

The board was still on the barrel, and the nine names were under it, and the bench against the gunwale of the Gull was empty, and a sheet about four feet by a foot and four inches was on the board, and there was ink on the sill of that room where a boy of thirteen had been mending a letter on Wednesday.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody for the coming.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety-four days, and in Hask forty-seven days, and the forty-seven is since he came up that plank, and the ninety-four is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in.

He wanted the woman of seventy-one not to be asked again, and he said so out loud before anybody asked, and it was the fourth time somebody had been about to ask her, and the first three were in about four days in the eleventh week. About four people in that room heard him in time and about nine did not.

“*She has said the shape of it three times and the last of the three was on the Monday of the hundred and eleventh week, and she will not say it a fourth, and I am saying that before anybody asks, because about nine people in this town have watched me not say it in time three times.*”

About four people in that room had the three. About nine had the shape and not the count.

Then a man of fifty-four who holds the gravel asked it anyway, and he is ordinary, and he cannot read, and he asked it because he had not been in the room any of the three times. About nine people in that room heard a fourth asking.

“*I was not in this town for any of the three, and I hold that gravel nine years, and I am asking what the split is.*”

“*And I am asking the woman of seventy-one, because she is the only person in this town who cannot be sent for about anything, and she said the shape three times.*”

Nobody in that room shifted.

Eliza Lark is seventy-one, and she keeps a house that is not hers and has kept it eleven years, and she came up that cart road on the Saturday of the last week but one to say she does not want to be spoken for, and the reason is about four years, and she said she would not say the shape a fourth time.

She looked at the door. About four people in that room thought she would go. About nine thought she would stay.

Then Sera Quill answered her, and the answer was not a comfort and it was not a form, and it was given to her and not to the room. About nine people in that room heard a thing said to one person that was not said to eight others.

Sera went to the doorway where the woman of seventy-one was standing, and stood a little off, and spoke low, and the room went on being a room, and the barrel went on being a barrel.

“*You do not have to say it a fourth time.*”

“*And I am not saying it for the room, and I am saying it for you. About nine people will hear that I am speaking and about four will hear what I say.*”

“*You said that in about four years there will be about nine people nobody can be sent for about, and a Crown will want to speak for every one of them.*”

“*And you said you would rather be one it does not speak for, and that is not a side, and the instrument is not a side.*”

“*And I have come four hundred miles with two letters nobody has read, and I am bearing nine sentences nobody can check, and I will carry your not speaking the fourth time as part of them.*”

“*And there is no form for that, and I am not going to write it down, and nobody nine miles up this water can check it.*”

About four people in that room heard the low speaking and not the words. About nine heard the doorway and not the sentence.

Then Eliza Lark put a hand on the frame of that door with no name on the slate, and she answered, and it was one sentence, and it was given back the same way, to one person and not to the room.

“*Then I am not the one who says it fourth, and you are the one who carries my not saying it, and I have kept that house eleven years and nobody has carried anything for me in eleven years.*”

Nobody in that room thanked either of them for the carrying.

Then Ilyan said the true of it from where he was standing nine feet off, and it was one half, and a woman of twenty-nine in the reed let him finish it, and it was the thirteenth time in forty-seven days.

“*A woman of seventy-one was asked a fourth time. About four people in this room wanted her to say the shape and about nine did not.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-eight answered her to herself and not to the room, and the answer is not a comfort and not a form.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest is that I have been in Hask forty-seven days, and the thing at that barrel was asked four things in its first nine days and it got three of them right, and I am not going to ask again about a thing that has been said once.*”

About four people in that room had that. The rest of them had a door.

Then night came on while about nine people were still in that room, and no lamp was lit, because a room with the door open in that county is lit by what comes through it. About four people in that room saw dusk on a slate with no name and about nine saw only dusk.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, low, because night makes people speak low whether they mean to or not.

“*Because a house that is not hers has been kept eleven years, and eleven is seventy-one less sixty, and I am not doing that sum out of a book.*”

“*And in eleven years nobody has come to that door to ask a question, and there is a difference between nobody coming and nobody being able to come, and she said that out loud on the Saturday.*”

About four people in that room had the eleven. About nine had night and not years.

Then a man of sixty-one told what eleven years are a figure of, and he did it with the working, because a figure without a unit is a decoration.

“*Eleven years is a figure of keeping, and it is not a figure of owning, and a landing I have kept eleven years takes a thousand and forty pence a year.*”

“*A thousand and forty pence is four pounds six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence of it is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and the rest is the landing.*”

“*And keeping without owning is what a woman of seventy-one does with a house, and what I do with a landing, and what a man of thirty-eight does with a board about nine inches long.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for keeping.

Then a woman of twenty-nine in the reed said what night does to a chair, and she has been in hers nine years, and she has not stood up since the Tuesday of the last week but one.

“*Night does not put anybody out of a chair, and taking back does, and a woman of forty-four said on Saturday that taking back puts a person out and does not put a line out.*”

“*And I took a yes back in the open, and I am still on that sheet, and night coming on does not change either half.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for nine years.

Then a man of sixty-eight said what answering to one person is, and the rule he gave is thirty-one days old, and thirty-one is six hundred and forty-eight less six hundred and seventeen, and he did the subtraction out loud.

“*An answer given to one person is not the position of the record, and a keeper of a book told this town that on the Tuesday of the eleventh week.*”

“*And a Crown of about nine people at a barrel can only say what is written down, and what is said low in a doorway is not written down.*”

“*And about four of you will want it written and about nine will not, and I am not going to take a rule back because of a doorway.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for thirty-one.

Then a boy of thirteen held a sheet up in the doorway to catch the last of the light.

“*The thirty-first line is about a man of thirty-one. The thirty-second is about a woman of seventy-one.*”

“*Thirty-two lines are on both. Thirty-two notches are on a board about nine inches long.*”

“*Dusk shows ink well. Night will hide it.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for dusk.

Then water was heard past that landing, though nobody went to look. About nine heard it. About four named it.

“*One river. One stone with a line cut round it. One count a man cannot stop.*”

“*Ninety-four days in that county. Forty-seven in Hask. Two clocks.*”

“*Night does not stop either. It comes on.*”

Nobody in that room thanked water for moving.

Then a slate with no name was wiped clean of dusk with a sleeve. About nine saw wiping. About four saw slate.

“*No name. Not one of the twenty-nine. Never was.*”

“*Wiping does not write. Night does not write. Only a hand writes.*”

Nobody in that room thanked the sleeve for wiping.

Then Sera Quill watched mending, and she spoke to one person again, low, not to the room. About nine heard speaking. About four heard words.

“*Your hands know ink. Mine are still clean.*”

“*Carrying your not saying fourth is ink of another kind. It does not show.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for watching.

---

The fever was fifty-two weeks and one day old. He had been in that county ninety-four days, and a woman of seventy-one was asked a fourth time to say the shape of a split, and she did not say it, and somebody answered her.

And the answer is not a comfort and not a form, and it was given to her in a doorway and not to the room. About four people heard the speaking and about nine heard only that speaking happened.

And a man of thirty-one said he would not ask a fifth, and a man of sixty-eight gave the age of his rule as thirty-one days with the subtraction said out loud.

And a house that is not hers has been kept eleven years by a woman of seventy-one who was carried once, and there is a slate nailed to the frame of that room with no name on it, and the chair inside is not one of the twenty-nine and never was.
