# Chapter 604: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Eighth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Puts A Second Version Of The Same Case In Front Of About Nine People In The Same Room In The Same Week And It Is Not The Same Shape, And On Which The Second Version Is Not Ten Things But One, And On Which It Is Proposed Out Loud In The Open Air To Strike All Eleven In One Morning And Count The Objections, And On Which About Nine People In That Room Have The First Shape And About Nine Have The Second And The Two Nines Have Not Asked Each Other, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Tries To Hold Both In One Frame And Is Told By A Woman Of Twenty-Nine That He Has Been Doing That Since Wednesday

Fourth day of the hundred and eighth week. His three hundred and fifty-fourth morning. Two hundred and four days after the settlement. The fever forty-five weeks and six days. One hundred and fifty-eight days since the division. One hundred and four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the third day of the hundred and eighth week.

---

There is no market in Hask. There is a landing, and there is a fair at a crossroads four miles off twice a year, and it is not fair week. About eleven hundred people live in that town and there is no day of the week on which more than about nine of them stand on a plank and listen to anything.

The lamp on the after end of the Gull is lit at the seventh hour in the dark and is out by the ninth. The book is kept in a deal box under the after thwart and comes out onto a board across the thwart when there is a thing to be written in it, and a thing written in it is a thing four feet off the ground with the river moving under it.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the fourth day.

---

Then a woman of twenty-nine put a second version of the same case in front of about nine people, and it was on the fourth day, and it was in the same room, and it was the same eleven, and it was not the same shape.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*because a woman put her name in this book on Thursday morning for a year and it is now Friday and she has come back with something else, and about four of us assume it is about Thursday morning and about nine of us do not.*”

“*It is not about Thursday morning,*” said Nell Prince. “*Thursday morning was right. I would say it again. I have been up in the night working out why it was right and it was right because a chair that has a person in it is a chair, and the only thing that makes a chair is a person in it, and the only thing that puts a person in one is somebody saying yes out loud. I am not here to take Thursday back.*”

“*Then say what you are here for,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*I am here because the ten is not ten things. It is one thing. And I found that out in the night and I have been wrong twice this week and I would rather say the third thing wrong in front of about nine people than go on being right about one chair.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

---

Then she said it, and it took her about four sentences and nobody interrupted her.

“*Two hundred and thirty years of this book and twenty-nine lines, and the whole of the trouble in it is on ten of them and not on the other nineteen. If a yes in the open air will put me in a chair, then a no in the open air will put a man out of one, and there is nothing whatever to stop it, and the man it happens to will be whoever is nearest the deck on the day.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*There is no form anywhere in this county that says a man may not be put out of a chair,*” said Nell Prince. “*I looked for one on Wednesday night and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that nobody has ever needed one. In two hundred and thirty years it has never once been done in the open.*”

“*Then say what you are proposing,*” said Ilyan.

“*Strike all ten. All of them. In one morning, in the open, on this deck. Every person in this town who objects to any one of the ten stands up on the plank at the same time and says the name out loud, and then we count the objections. The chairs stay where they are if there are objections, and go if there are not.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*I have cut that reed since I was nine, and I am one of the four people in this town who would be stood up against, and I would like about nine of us to think about what it is that I would be stood up against.*”

“*You would be stood up against a name going off a book in front of about nine people, in a town of about eleven hundred. If nobody stands up, then your name is not there on Monday, and you would still be cutting the reed on Tuesday. About four of the ten are in this town and have not come, and about two of them are old. One of them is a man of sixty-eight who has not been to a sitting since the flood came up, and he is not stopping coming because he does not care about the reed. He is stopping because he does not want to be counted in a room.*”

---

Then the two shapes were in the same room and they were not going into each other, and about nine people on that plank had the first one and about nine had the second one, and the two nines had not asked each other.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*There are about nine of us on this plank, and about nine of us have a version, and about nine of us have another version. I have kept this book for nineteen years, and in nineteen years that has happened twice, and both of the times it was about a name I did not expect, and I have the days on both of them.*”

Nobody on that plank said anything.

“*Then say the days,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*They are the two afternoons I have never written down,*” said Orla Fennimore.

About four seconds went past. About nine of the people on that plank had not known that and about four of the nine did not understand it.

---

Then a man of thirty-one tried to put both of them in one frame, and it was the thing he has done in four counties, and a woman of twenty-nine told him what he was doing while he was doing it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a man of thirty-one has had about four seconds of thinking and I can see it, and I have watched men do that in this room for twenty-nine years and it has never once been the right thing.*”

“*I am standing in front of two versions of one case, and they are the same case. The difference between them is only about how much is being decided at once. Take the eleven one at a time and you are doing the first thing on Wednesday slower, and take them together and you are doing the same thing faster. There is a figure of how much time there is which somebody in this room is going to want, and I have asked for it, and nobody has given it to me, and I have not asked for it out loud, which is my mistake and not anybody else’s.*”

“*No it is not,*” said Nell Prince. “*It is the same ten and the two shapes are not a difference of speed. On Wednesday a man who is dead is asked who speaks for him. Today eleven people are asked whether anybody at all does. Those are not the same question with more of it. And you have been carrying them in one hand since Wednesday because one hand is easier, and I have watched a man do that in about four counties and in every one of them about nine people ended up with the two nines and no answer, which is the same as what has happened on this plank this morning.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody.

“*Then say what the difference is in a shape that can be put in a book,*” said Ilyan, “*or do not say it at all.*”

“*I cannot put it in a book and I have stopped wanting to,*” said Nell Prince. “*One is a person and the other is a book. And I am not going to strike eleven chairs on Monday, and Orla is not going to let me. I have said it out loud in front of about nine people, and about nine of you have it, and about nine of you have it a different way by the fourth hour, and I would rather that than have it settled.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

---

Then somebody counted the ten again and it came out the same and neither of the two shapes went anywhere, and the lamp went out at the ninth hour, and a woman of twenty-nine put her hand along a row of chairs on her way to the plank.

She put it flat on the backs of them as she went, from the stem aft, not touching the seats. She took about four seconds over the whole row, and she stopped at the ninth one and went on to the eleventh. Then she came back and did it again from the after end, and she got the same nine and the same two.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*because a woman has just counted twenty-nine chairs twice in about nine seconds with her hand and about four of us have watched her do it and about nine of us have not, and about four of us have watched her stop at the ninth.*”

“*The ninth and the tenth are the ones I cut withies off the land of,*” said Nell Prince. “*I have not sat in either of them. I sat in the reed. And I would like it said out loud on this deck that on Wednesday I was offered a ground by proximity, and I took it. If I had been offered a ground I was the daughter of, I would not have thought twice about it. About nine of you in this town have a chair that is a daughter, and about nine of you have one that is not, and neither of those is a reason.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody and nobody on that plank was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-five weeks and six days old. A woman of twenty-nine put a second version of the same ten chairs in front of about nine people on the fourth day of a week, and proposed to strike all of them in one morning in the open and count the objections. About nine people in that room have the first shape and about nine have the second, and the two nines have not asked each other.

And the woman who keeps the book said out loud that there have been two such mornings in nineteen years and both of them are the two afternoons she has never written down, and about four people on that plank worked out what she had said and about nine did not.

And a man of thirty-one tried to put two questions in one hand, and was told by the woman of twenty-nine that the two are not one question with more of it. She counted twenty-nine chairs twice with her hand in about nine seconds and stopped at the ninth both times. The ninth and the tenth are the ones she cuts withies off the land of, and she is in neither of them and did not know that on Wednesday.
