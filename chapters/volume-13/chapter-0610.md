# Chapter 610: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which It Is Said Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing That It Was Eleven On Tuesday And It Is Ten Today And That Nobody In That Town Knows When The Water Goes Over A Block Of Limestone Nine Miles Up The Water, And On Which A Woman Of Seventy-One Who Keeps A House That Is Not Hers Says Nothing And Is Not Asked And Does Not Offer, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Asks Out Loud Whether A Person Who Has Said Yes To A Chair May Take The Yes Back When They Find Out What Is On The Chair, And On Which It Is Asked Once And Is Not Answered, And On Which Nobody In That Town Was Relieved And Nobody Was Forgiven And Nobody Was Thanked

Third day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and sixtieth morning. Two hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and five days. One hundred and sixty-four days since the division. One hundred and ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the second day of the hundred and ninth week.

---

A woman of seventy-one kept a house on the low side of the landing that was not hers, and she had kept it eleven years. She washed her hair in a bowl on the Wednesday, and she came up the plank that day to say one thing in about nine people’s hearing, and she has not come up the plank since. Nobody in that town has asked her a second thing and she has not offered one, and a man of thirty-eight said both of those out loud on the Thursday and both of them were true.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the ten days.

---

Then the number was said out loud, and the subtraction was said with it, and about nine people were on that deck and about nine of them were on the bank, and the man of thirty-one was nine feet off with a boat-hook he was still holding.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*It is the tenth day a man of thirty-one has been in this town, and about nine of us have been counting something since Tuesday. About four of us have been counting it out loud and about nine have not, and I would like the count said with the days in it.*”

“*Say what is being counted and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*The chairs with a name on them and nobody in them. It was eleven on Tuesday morning. It is ten today, and the one that went was the reed, and the reed went on the Thursday of last week with a woman of twenty-nine in it and her sleeves rolled up, and about four of us heard her and about nine of us did not. That was seven days.*”

About four people on that deck checked that against each other. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at ten,*” said Nell Prince.

“*Eleven on Tuesday. Ten today. Seven days for the one. And I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that I am the only person in this town who could tell you what that comes to, and that I am not going to say it. I would like about four of you to know why, and about nine of you to have it a different way by the fourth hour.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*Because seven days for one is a figure, and a figure wants a second figure, and the second figure is when the water goes over that block of limestone nine miles up this river. That is a year. Not one of the nine of us on this deck knows when it is, and the man standing nine feet off cannot leave the county until it does and does not know when it is either. He can tell you about the water at that cut to half an inch, and he cannot tell you when the water will be over it. I have watched about four of us work that out on Monday and about nine of us have not.*”

About four people on that deck turned round and looked at the man of thirty-one. About nine did not.

“*Then say what you have instead of the figure,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have a knife and about nine feet of withy off the hedge above the landing, and I have a daughter of nine at home who has asked me twice this week what the old woman at the low side is doing in that house. I have told her twice that it is not our business. I would like it said out loud on this deck that I have said that to my own child twice, and that I do not know whether it is a true thing to say or a coward’s thing to say, and I have not worked it out, and I am not going to work it out on a deck in front of about nine people.*”

Nobody on that deck said anything to that.

---

Then a man of thirty-one wanted to leave something that could be checked, and did the thing he has done in four counties, and it was the oldest one he has and it is the whole of what these ten days have been about.

He had been in that county fifty-six days. He had put a year down against a block of limestone nine miles up the water, and could not leave the county until it went over. He counted from that water every morning and could not stop. No person in the county of Kell was bound by a word of anything he had done, and a document of about nine hundred words in six hands with nothing at the top of it had been used once, on the Wednesday, by a man from another county who had read it.

He wanted to write ten lines and have them in a book where a person could put a thumb on them.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a man of thirty-one has had a boat-hook in his hands for five days and has just put it down and stood up straight, and about four of us know what that means and about nine do not.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and about nine hundred words in six hands that binds nobody. A man from another county has carried one page of it two days in a bag and used it to do a thing he has not taken back, and about four people in his town know where the page came from and about nine do not. I have been in this county fifty-six days, and I have never once been in a room where anything I did was still there afterwards.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Nell Prince.

“*And I have been about to say the same four sentences I have said in four counties in the last ten days, and I have said them in a weigh-house and on a rise and in a yard and on a deck. About four people in each of those rooms were the person who carried it out of the room. I would like it said out loud that I have worked out this morning that I am doing it again, and I would like about nine of you to hear me notice it.*”

About four people on that deck said nothing at all.

---

Then a woman of twenty-nine asked a question, and it was asked out loud, and it was in front of about nine people, and it was the first time it had been asked.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore, from a second boat alongside with a page in front of her and a rule in her hand. “*Because a man of thirty-one has just said out loud that he is about to do the thing he always does, and about four of us heard that, and about nine of us did not, and there is a question in this town that has been standing since Tuesday and nobody has asked it.*”

Nobody on either boat said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Nell Prince.

“*Whether a person who has said yes to a chair may take the yes back when they find out what is on the chair.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say it again,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*Whether a person who has said yes to a chair may take the yes back when they find out what is on the chair. That is the question. I am not asking it for Thursday.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*I am asking it because I said yes on the Thursday of last week, and in ten days I have found out that the reed has four feet of water on the low side of it in a bad spell, and that the ground the chair is for is not the ground I am on. I have not stopped cutting withies and I am not going to. I have not found out one single thing that makes me want the chair less, and that is the trouble, and I would like about nine people to know it in a plain way instead of in nine days.*”

Nobody on either boat said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan, and he began to answer it.

He got about four sentences out. They were the three ways a chair gets filled, and that in nineteen years about nine people had said yes and about two of them had not been sons, and that the third way is open to anybody and always has been. And then he stopped, out loud, in front of about nine people, and said why.

“*I have just said the fourth time in ten days that the third way is open to anybody. And you have not asked me a question about the third way. You have asked me a question about a person, and the only two answers I have got are the ones I have been giving about the third way. If I give you one of them now, then I have said the same four sentences in five rooms in ten days, and you will have the shape of a thing instead of an answer to your question. I would rather say nothing and have it be said in this book that I said nothing.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

Nobody on either boat said anything to it.

Nobody on that plank was relieved and nobody on that plank was forgiven and nobody on that plank was thanked.

The fever was forty-six weeks and five days old. It was eleven on Tuesday and it is ten today, and about nine people in a town of about eleven hundred people are carrying a count of seven days for the one. Nobody in that town can turn it into a figure, because the second figure is the day the water goes over a block of limestone nine miles up the water. About nine of them know that and about nine do not, and the two nines have still not asked each other.

And a woman of seventy-one is keeping a house that is not hers on the low side of a landing, and a man of thirty-eight has told a child of nine twice that it is not our business. A man of thirty-one has said out loud that he is the person who carries things out of rooms. A woman of twenty-nine has asked out loud whether a person may take a yes back when they find out what is on the chair. It was asked once, in front of about nine people, and nobody in that town answered it, and the man of thirty-one said in about nine people’s hearing that he was not going to answer it and why.

And at the top of that landing a woman of twenty-nine is cutting withies off a hedge in about nine feet lengths and about two inches at a time. There is a knot in the middle of every one of them that was in the hedge before she cut it. It cannot be untied. It can only be cut, and nobody in that town is going to cut one, and nobody in that town has said a word about that either.
