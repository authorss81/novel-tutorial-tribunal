# Chapter 638: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirteenth Week, And A Thursday, And The Town Sits And Is Over In About Forty Minutes, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-One Asks Four People Whether A Man Of Sixty-One May Write A Column On The Back Of The Last Leaf Of A Book Of Heads And Carts And Four People Do Not Answer Him, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Takes A Sheet Of Deal Nine Miles Up The Water In A Boat At The Fifth Hour And Reads It Against The Other One In A Yard And They Agree And He Poles Back Down And Gets In At The Ninth Hour, And On Which The Ordinary Work Of A Week In That County Is Done By One Man And Two Boats And Is Load-Bearing

Third day of the hundred and thirteenth week. His three hundred and eighty-eighth morning. Two hundred and thirty-eight days after the settlement. The fever fifty weeks and five days. One hundred and ninety-two days since the division. One hundred and thirty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town sat on the Thursday from the fourth hour to about the fortieth minute, and there was nothing to sit about.

About nine people got off that plank. About four of them went up the bank and about four went down the cart road.

A man of thirty-eight went up the water in the Gull at about the fifth hour with a sheet of deal in a coat, and did not say out loud what he was doing.

He has said it out loud every time for about ten days, and about nine people in that town have it by now.

Nobody on that plank thanked him for the going.

Then a man of sixty-one did a thing at about the sixth hour that nobody in that town had ever seen a person do, and he did it in the open in front of about nine people, and he asked permission first, and about four of them did not answer him.

“*I am going to write a column on the back of the last leaf of this book, and it is going to be about four inches wide and about nine inches long.*”

“*And it is going to have a number in it that is not a head and not a cart, and I am going to do it with a knife because I have not got a pen that works.*”

About four people on that bank had that. Nobody answered him.

“*I am asking four people whether a man of sixty-one may do it, and I have asked them, and I am going to wait about four minutes.*”

“*And if nobody answers me I am going to do it anyway and say out loud that nobody answered.*”

“*Because there is no form in this county that says he may not, and I have been treating a nothing as a reason for about two years, and a man of sixty-one said that out loud on the Monday of this week and about nine of us heard it.*”

About four minutes went past and nobody answered and nobody said no, and about nine people in that town watched a man of sixty-one cut a column into the back of a leaf.

“*That is about four. And about nine of you watched, and about four of you can read what is on it and about nine cannot.*”

“*And the man of fifty-four is one of the four who cannot, and I would like it said out loud that I know that, and that he has not said so this week.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for the four minutes.

---

Then a man of thirty-one offered to be the one who goes up the water, and the offer was made out loud in front of about nine people, and it was refused out loud in about four sentences by the man he had offered it to.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am not asking you to be the one that goes because you cannot. There is a better reason, and I am going to give it in about four sentences, and it is a man of sixty-eight’s reason.*”

“*And I am giving it because I have had it from him and I would rather it came from me than from a man who is not here.*”

About four people on that bank had that. About nine of them had not known it was anybody’s reason at all.

“*A board about nine inches long with a notch cut in it for every line is how a record is checked. That is the whole of it.*”

“*You wrote a rule that the two copies are read against each other once a week, and if they do not agree then the thing is not true, and there is a notch in the edge of this for every line of it, and I have cut the edge back to the same place every week since, and a notch is a thing I can hold in my hand and you cannot.*”

“*And a man of sixty-eight has been standing behind that rule since the Thursday of the tenth week.*”

“*If the man who carries the board is a man nobody can be sent for about, then in about four years the board and the man are the same thing.*”

“*And a record that is checked by a person it cannot reach is a record it cannot be wrong, and a record that cannot be wrong is a lid.*”

“*And that is a man of sixty-eight’s sentence, and he said it to you on the Friday of the eleventh week, and he has not taken it back.*”

About four people on that bank understood every word of that. About nine of them had a shape for it they liked better.

“*You may read them out. You may stand nine feet off with a knife. You may carry a sheet as far as the end of that bank.*”

“*You may not be the one who carries the board, and I am sorry about it and I am not going to soften it, and about four of you have seen me say that sentence to somebody before.*”

Nobody on that bank said a word that could have moved him.

---

Then he came back down, and got in at about the second hour of the afternoon, and put a board about nine inches long on the barrel in front of about nine people without saying a word about where it had been.

The two copies were read against each other on the deck of the Gull in the open, in about nine minutes, line against line, name against name, day against day.

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for nine miles.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is on the board.

“*Because the man of thirty-eight has been up and down since the fifth hour and about four of us watched him go and about nine did not.*”

“*And the two copies have been in that boat and a yard nine miles off, and they are on that barrel now.*”

“*And about nine people in a town of about eleven hundred have never once read a line of them against another line.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had done it in a room three times, on the three Mondays since the man of sixty-eight gave the rule, and about nine of them had never seen it done at all.

It was the first time in nineteen years that anybody had done it on a day that was not a Monday.

Then a man of thirty-one read the thirty-first line out loud, and a man of thirty-eight cut the thirty-first notch in the edge of the board, and about nine people in front of that barrel watched him do it.

About nine people in front of that barrel heard a line about him say the same thing twice in about nine minutes, and about four of them had heard it three times in four counties.

Nobody in front of that barrel said anything while it was being read.

Then a man of thirty-eight said the result, and he said it in four sentences, and it is the only finding in that week that is not about anybody.

“*Thirty-two lines on both of them. The thirty-second is the thirty-second on both of them. The thirty-first is the thirty-first on both of them.*”

“*And it is the same day and the same Monday on both of them, and a man of forty-three wrote it with a knife out of a whittling block in that yard on the Monday of the eleventh week at about the second hour, and I read the whole sheet out loud after him in about nine minutes.*”

“*And I read it in that yard this morning with about nine people standing about, and about four of them heard it and about nine did not.*”

About four people in front of that barrel counted that. Nobody else did.

“*They agree. And that is the fourth time the two of them have been in a room together, and the fourth time they have agreed, and the first of the four was on a Monday in this town and there was not one person standing on that deck who had ever seen it done.*”

“*And it took me about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading, and nobody has ever once asked me to do it, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to stop.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for the nine hours.

Then a woman of twenty-nine who was in the reed said the true of it in about nine sentences.

About four people in front of that barrel had not known any of it, and it was the finding the week had been going to need on a Thursday afternoon, and none of them had asked her for it.

“*About nine people at a barrel in this town are a Crown, and about four of them cannot describe what it is for, and a man of sixty-eight said on Monday that a Crown that cannot be asked about what is in it is a lid with a name on it.*”

About four people in front of that barrel went still.

“*And a Crown of about nine people at that barrel can only say what is written down, and what is written down is a sheet of thirty-two lines.*”

“*And that sheet is only true when two people nine miles apart have said so in the open, and the two people are a man of thirty-eight and a man of forty-three.*”

“*And one of them has poled about nine miles twice today to make it true.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the whole of it in about four seconds. Nobody said a word.

“*And I am going to say the rest of it out loud, because about four of you can see where it goes and about nine of you cannot.*”

“*I would like it said that I have been in this chair for nine years and I have watched about nine people be told a number and take it, and this is the first number in about nine days that somebody is going to have to do something about.*”

Nobody said anything for about the time it takes to pole a quarter mile.

“*About nine days of one man’s work is what a Crown of about nine people costs, and I said that out loud on a Friday at about the ninth hour in a room and a man of sixty-one did the sum.*”

“*And about four of you wrote the number down and about nine of you wrote the money down instead, and the money is nothing, because there is no form that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked her for the sum.

---

The fever was fifty weeks and five days old. He had been in that county eighty-four days, and a rule a man of sixty-eight gave on the Thursday of the tenth week, that two copies are read against each other once a week in the open and if the two do not agree then the thing is not true, is worked in that county by one man with a boat and a pole and a board about nine inches long in a coat.

It was worked twice on a Thursday, nine miles up the water in a yard and nine miles down it on a deck, in about nine minutes each, and the two agree.

And a man of sixty-one cut a column about four inches wide and about nine inches long into the back of the last leaf of a book of heads and carts, with a knife, after asking four people whether a man of sixty-one may, and about four of them did not answer him and about nine of them watched.

And the column has a number in it and it is a number of people and not of heads and not of carts.

And a man of thirty-one was refused the board in about four sentences on the ground that a record checked by a person it cannot reach is a record it cannot be wrong, and a record that cannot be wrong is a lid, and that reason is a man of sixty-eight’s reason and it has not been taken back.

And at about the second hour of the afternoon a board about nine inches long was lying on a barrel in the open with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and about four people in a town of about eleven hundred had held it in their hands that day, and about nine had not, and about nine miles up that water there was a yard where the other half of that comparison had been made at about the ninth hour by the same man.
