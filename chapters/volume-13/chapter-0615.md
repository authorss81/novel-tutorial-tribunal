# Chapter 615: The First Day Of The Hundred And Tenth Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which Nine People Finish Four Days Of Work At The Head Of A Plank That Nobody Is Going To Be Paid For And That No Form In The County Says They May Be, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Counts It Out Loud And Is Asked For The Count By The Woman Who Did It, And On Which She Says Out Loud What It Cost, And It Was Not The Money

First day of the hundred and tenth week. His three hundred and sixty-fifth morning. Two hundred and fifteen days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and three days. One hundred and sixty-nine days since the division. One hundred and fifteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

It was the last day of fair week at the crossroads four miles off, and about nine of the people who had been selling on somebody else’s ground came back down the cart road, and the road was a road again by the middle of the morning.

At the head of the plank there were about thirty feet of withy hurdles laid edge to edge, pegged at the four corners and at every third hurdle. A barrow and a half of gravel out of the head of the drain was spread over the top of them and trodden in. Sand out of a barrel went into the two low places where the water stands.

Nobody on that ground thanked anybody for the four days.

---

It had been done on four days. The Friday, the Saturday, the Monday, and the Tuesday. Nine people, about nine hours a day, and about four of the nine were there on all four days and about nine had been there for some of them.

The withy came off the hedge above the landing in about nine-foot lengths at about two inches at a time, cut by the woman of twenty-nine, who cuts all of it. There is a knot in the middle of every length that was in the hedge before she cut it. About nine hundred lengths were pegged into the ground across the four days, and the pegs were cut out of the same hedge and sharpened with a knife and not an axe.

The gravel was the near party’s. A barrow and a half out of the head of the drain, about a hundred yards, and the man of fifty-four gave it, and nobody asked him, and he gave it on the Saturday and said nothing about it until the Monday.

Nobody on that ground had said out loud that there would be money for it.

---

Then there was an argument about a barrel of sand, and it went on for about nine minutes, and it is in this record because it was the only thing said on that ground in four days that was about the work.

It was between the man of sixty-eight who kept the quarter mile and the man of fifty-four whose gravel it was, and it was about whether the sand went in the middle of the hurdles or on the top of them. The man of fifty-four said the middle, because the middle is where the cart’s wheel goes and the wheel is what is doing the damage. The man of sixty-eight said the top, because the middle is where the hurdle binds and the binding is what is going to fail first in the wet.

They agreed to put some in each, and about nine people watched two men who had said true things about gravel on a Monday decide a thing about sand on a Tuesday by splitting the difference, and neither of them was wrong and about nine of them noticed that too.

Nobody on that ground said anything clever about that.

---

Then a man of thirty-one counted it, out loud, in front of about nine people, and he had wanted to for three days.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the bank, where he had come up on the morning of it with a line across his own deck that he was not taking up. “*Because a man of thirty-one has been counting things out loud in four counties for eleven days and about nine of us have got used to it, and he has a piece of chalk and I would like it said that he is about to use it on four days of other people’s work.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and four days of work that nine people did, and I have counted it, and I am going to say the count out loud because about four of us have been waiting for a figure since Monday and I have one.*”

Nobody on that ground moved.

“*Then say it and do not be sorry for me,*” said the woman of forty-one, from the middle of it, with a barrow handle in her hand.

---

He said it out loud, and it was right, and about nine people heard it, and it took him eleven sentences and the woman asked him to stop after the ninth.

“*Nine people. Four days. About nine hours a day, which is the daylight and not a longer thing than that. That is thirty-six days of a person’s work, and about four of the nine were here for all four days, and the withies were cut at about two inches at a time which is the whole of the reason this took four days and not one, and the pegs were sharpened with a knife.*”

Nobody on that ground said anything to it.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Stop. You have said nine people and four days and thirty-six days and about nine hundred withies and you have not said one word about the sand, and I would like you to stop counting for about four seconds and let me tell you what you have counted.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

---

Then a woman of forty-one who kept two carts and a boy of thirteen who poled said what the four days cost, out loud, in front of about nine people, and it was in her own mouth, and about four of them had it and about nine did not.

“*There is no form in this county that says I may pay those nine people and there is no form in this county that says I may not, and that is the whole of what happened on that ground over four days. I could have had the sand carted from four miles off at a crossroads, which is four shillings there and four shillings back, and I did not. Four of us cut it out of a barrow and a half. About nine hundred withies came out of a hedge that a woman of twenty-nine has been cutting for nothing for nineteen years, and nobody has been paid for any of it in money.*”

Nobody on that ground thanked anybody for the four days.

“*And I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that I am one of the nine, and that about eight of the nine came because they had a reason that has nothing to do with the road. I came because I lost a load in the wet and I have not been able to say to anybody what it was that had gone. I am not asking to be paid. I am saying the count cannot do this job.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*A bed and a crust in this town is a penny and a penny, which is two pence a day, and that is what I have got and it is not a wage. It is what a person eats. Thirty-six days at two pence a day is seventy-two pence, which is six shillings.*”

About four people on that ground counted that. About nine did not.

“*The woman who keeps the chairs is paid a hundred and sixty pence a year, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and she keeps a book every day of it. Six shillings is a little under half a year of hers. That is what nine people gave on that ground over four days, and it is about half of what one person is given for keeping up one book.*”

About four people on that ground checked that against each other. About nine did not.

“*And now say the second half, and it is the half that has no name on it. A count of nine people and four days is a figure, and about four of you have heard the figure, and figures are what a book is built on. But the nine are not the same nine. Eight of us came for our own reasons and would have cut withies for nothing. I came for a load in the wet.*”

Nobody on that ground thanked her for the second half.

“*And the man of fifty-four gave away a barrow and a half of the ground he is the near party to on a Saturday, and did not mention it until the Monday. If you count that in as nine people and four days, then you have counted a gift as labour. There is no column anywhere in this county that can tell the two apart, and there never has been one.*”

Nobody on that ground moved.

“*That is what the four days cost. Not the six shillings. The six shillings is a thing I can say and I have said it. The cost is that if a person counts it, then in about four years there will be a form in this county that says a thing that was given is a thing that was done. About nine people who come up that plank for their own reasons will be on a list of people who owed the road something. I am one of them and I do not want to be.*”

---

Then a man of thirty-one had a pound and fifteen shillings and eightpence and said so out loud in front of about nine people, and it was the fourth time in fifteen days that he had said a number about himself in the open.

Nobody on that ground thanked him for it.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Nevin Trask, from the bank, without the book.

“*You have three hundred and thirty-eight pence. Six shillings is seventy-two pence, which is a little over a fifth of what you have said out loud on a Tuesday morning, and a person of thirty-one who cannot leave this county has just offered a fifth of himself to nine people on a piece of ground, and I am going to say why that is not a wage.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*If you put that on that ground, then four days of nine people is a thing a man of thirty-one paid for, and about nine people in this town will have it by the fourth hour. In about four years somebody is going to read about it and see that the man who put down a year of his own future against a stone nine miles up this water is the man who paid for the road. There will not be one person in this county who can be sent for to ask him whether he wanted to buy it.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

“*And I am not saying you may not. I am saying you have just been told no by a man of sixty-one about a sum, and you are going to have to hold the no the way the woman of twenty-nine held hers on the Tuesday of last week, in the open, in front of about nine people.*”

Nobody on that ground said anything to that.

---

The fever was forty-seven weeks and three days old. He had been in that county sixty-one days. Nine people worked four days at the head of a plank in a town of about eleven hundred people and were paid nothing. There is no form in the county of Kell that says they may be. A barrow and a half of another man’s ground went into it, and he gave it and did not say so for a day.

And a woman of forty-one said out loud that six shillings is a little under half a year of the woman who keeps the chairs. She said the cost of the four days is not the six shillings, and is the fact that a count of nine people and four days cannot tell a gift from a day’s work. There is no column in this county that can.

And a man of thirty-one said out loud how much money he had, and was told no about a sum, and did not answer.
