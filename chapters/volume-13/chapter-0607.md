# Chapter 607: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Eighth Week, And A Monday, And On Which At About The Fourth Hour With The Lamp Lit And About Nine People On The Deck Of A Lighter Putting Her Back Into The Reach A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asks A Man Of Thirty-One Where He Came From In About Nine People’s Hearing And Without Any Preparation At All, And On Which The Answer Is Four Checkable Things And Is Not A Refusal And Is Not A Confession, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Says Out Loud That Nobody In That Room Asked The Question He Was Asked And That Is What Made It Go Out Into The Bank, And On Which About Nine People On A Bank Nine Miles Off From A Weigh-House Are Going To Have It By The Fourth Hour

Seventh and last day of the hundred and eighth week. His three hundred and fifty-seventh morning. Two hundred and seven days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and two days. One hundred and sixty-one days since the division. One hundred and seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the sixth day of the hundred and eighth week. The morrow is the first day of the hundred and ninth week.

---

The lamp is lit at the seventh hour in the dark and is out by the ninth, and the seventh day of a week is the day the Gull is put back into the reach, and about nine people do that work and about nine of them are on the bank waiting for her to be got off.

He had been in that county fifty-three days. He had a year against a block of limestone nine miles up the water and a river he counted from every morning and could not stop, and a boat-hook he had been given on the Saturday and had carried about nine miles on the Sunday because there was nothing else to carry.

He wanted the week over.

---

Then a man of thirty-eight asked him, and it was without any preparation at all, and about nine people were on that deck, and it was the seventh day of a week and he had asked it once and been refused and was now working rope.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, hauling his own line. “*Because it is about the fourth hour, and about nine of us have been working her since the seventh, and about four of us are tired. I am about to ask you where you came from, and I have been not asking it since Saturday. About four of you know I have not been asking it and about nine of you do not.*”

Nobody on that deck said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore, who was holding the board across the thwart with the book open on it and nothing written on it. “*Because a man of thirty-one has been in this town seven days and about four of us have worked out that there is something in him that is not of this county, and I am the one who keeps the book, and I would like to know whether I am going to be the one who writes it down.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people, a river, and the fourth hour of the seventh day of a week. I came off a cart on Tuesday morning with a pound and fifteen shillings and eightpence, and I have been here seven days. You are asking me where I came from, and I am going to answer in four things, and I am not going to be able to answer it properly. I would like that said before I say them rather than after.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Garrin Tolley.

---

Then he said the four things, and they were checkable, and none of them was a no and none of them was a yes.

“*One. I came off a boat on the evening of a Wednesday fifty-four days ago into a town nine miles up this water, into a weigh-house sixty feet long, and I did not come out of it for two days.*”

“*Two. I put down one year of my own future against a block of limestone with a line cut round it, nine miles up this river. I cannot leave this county until the water goes over that cut, and I do not know when that is, and nobody can tell me. About nine people believed the price when I said it out loud and about nine did not.*”

“*Three. Since a Thursday morning in a room over a stable I have counted from that water every morning and I cannot stop. It is the one thing I have that nobody in this town can be checked against. I can tell you about a cut nine miles off in about four seconds, and I cannot tell you about the water at the top of this landing at all.*”

“*Four. There is a settlement of about nine hundred words on a wall in that weigh-house that I put four things into, and no person in this county is bound by one word of it, and about eleven hundred people in that town may read it. I am the only person alive who has been told that a thing was said in that room in about nine people’s hearing on a Monday morning, and that nobody in that room could answer what had been asked of it.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley, who had stopped hauling his line.

“*That is the whole of what I have got,*” said Ilyan. “*It is four things. Two of them are about a stone and two of them are about a thing I did nine days ago. None of them is the answer to your question, and I have known that since about the fourth hour yesterday, and I have not been able to do anything about it. I would rather you asked me again on a Tuesday than have you go away thinking I have given you something.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

---

Then a woman of twenty-nine said out loud what everybody in that room had been doing for seven days, and it was not about him, and it was the most useful thing anybody said on that landing in the whole of that week.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Nell Prince, who had a hoop of withies over her arm and had come up the plank at about the fourth hour because she was going to be here for the working. “*Because a man of thirty-one has just been asked where he came from and has answered in four things. About four of us heard four things, and about nine of us heard a refusal and a confession and did not know they were the same. I would like it said out loud what actually happened on that deck, and it is not what anybody in this room thinks happened.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore.

“*Nobody in this room asked him. A man of thirty-eight said he had been not asking since Saturday and then asked, and I was holding the book and did not stop him, and a man of forty-four did not stop him. There was no vote and there was no method and there was no third way of getting a thing said in the open. A man of thirty-eight wanted to know where a man had come from, and he wanted to know it before the fourth hour, and he asked.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*It went into a bank where about nine people were standing waiting for that boat to be got off,*” said Nell Prince. “*About nine of the people in this town will have it by the fourth hour today, and about nine of them will have it a different way.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at the bank,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*And I want it said that this is not a trick and I am not blaming him. On Wednesday I put my name in a book in about nine seconds because the room wanted it and he did not ask me, and I said so out loud and I was right. And on Saturday he offered to be the instrument for eleven chairs and I said no and I was right. And this week, when somebody was going to be asked a question that was going to be in about eleven hundred mouths in this town by the fourth hour, the two people on this deck who could have stopped it held the book open and did not.*”

---

Then the lamp went out at about the ninth hour, or near it, and the Gull was off the gravel and into the reach. About nine people got off her onto a plank. About nine of the nine on the bank had not been in the eleven on the deck, and every one of the nine had heard a man’s voice from about nine feet off, in the dark, with a lamp on it.

They did not have the words. They had a face, lit from below, talking to four people about a stone and a river nine miles up the water.

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody.

The fever was forty-six weeks and two days old. A man of thirty-eight asked a man of thirty-one where he came from at about the fourth hour on the seventh day of a week, in about nine people’s hearing and with no preparation at all. The answer was four checkable things. A woman of twenty-nine said out loud that none of them was an answer, and that nobody on that deck had asked the question.

And a woman of forty-four who had kept a book for nineteen years held it open on a board across a thwart and did not stop a question being asked in front of about nine people with a bank of about nine people behind them. She has not said out loud yet that that was the worst nine seconds of her nineteen years. About four people in this town have worked that out and about nine have not.

And a woman of seventy-one on the low side of that landing has not been asked anything this week and did not offer. The town has a stone, a river, a document, a boat-hook and about nine people, and there is not a figure in this town for the rest of them, and there is not going to be one.
