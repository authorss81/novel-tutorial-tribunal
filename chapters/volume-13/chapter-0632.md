# Chapter 632: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Twelfth Week, And A Friday, And On Which About Nine People In A Front Room Make A Standing Thing Out Of A Board And A Barrel And Nine Of Them Can Say What It Is For And About Four Cannot, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Keeps The Book Says No To It In Four Sentences, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Six Who Will Not Be In It Says The Word For It Out Loud Because She Would Rather It Had An Ugly Name, And On Which It Is Asked Two Things And Gets One Of Them Right

Fourth day of the hundred and twelfth week. His three hundred and eighty-second morning. Two hundred and thirty-two days after the settlement. The fever forty-nine weeks and six days. One hundred and eighty-six days since the division. One hundred and thirty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The room had a low lintel and a slate nailed to the frame with no name on it, and a fire in it that was not lit. The door was open because it was the fourth hour of a Friday, and about nine people came down that cart road.

About four of them had not been in that room since Saturday.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody for the walking.

There is no other table in Hask, so the board went on the barrel. It is the same board a boy of eleven has copied half a sheet of deal on, twice.

About four people in that room noticed that. About nine had not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow.

“*Because there is a woman of about thirty-eight in a town two days off who was read out loud on Wednesday evening in a room, and who stood up and said she had never been asked anything.*”

“*About nine people in this town have that by the twelfth hour on Thursday, and about nine of us have been standing in front of a barrel since the fourth hour of Friday working out what is to be done about it, and nobody sent for any of us.*”

---

Then a man of sixty-one read out the number and said what it was a figure of, and he did it in about nine sentences, and he had the working and nobody gave it to him.

“*About nine people in this town can write. That is the number this whole thing is built on.*”

“*It is a figure of the people who can write and not a figure of the people who can be asked. Those are two different numbers, and about four of you in this room have known that for nineteen years and about nine have not.*”

Nobody in that room shifted.

“*There are about nine people in this room who can describe what that thing on that barrel is for, and about four who cannot.*”

“*I am going to say both numbers out loud and say what each of them is a figure of, because the second one is the one that matters and it is the smaller.*”

About four people in that room counted that against each other.

“*About nine is a figure of what about nine people have said. It is not a figure of what anybody thinks, and I said on the Thursday of last week that that is the rule in this town and I have not said anything different since.*”

“*About four is a figure of about four people who have been in that room since the fourth hour this morning and have not been able to put the thing in a sentence.*”

“*About two of those four are thirteen, and one of them is twenty-nine, and about nine of the rest are not going to be asked about it because they are not in the room.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for the smaller number.

---

Then he read out the nine, and he read them the way a line is read on a sheet of deal, the ground said before the name.

About four people in that room had the whole of it before the last one.

“*Nevin Trask, sixty-one, and the book of heads and carts, and the penny a head and the penny a cart.*”

“*Garrin Tolley, thirty-eight, and the two copies and the plank and the quarter mile.*”

“*Wenna Callow, forty, and the asking for a day against a name.*”

“*Nell Prince, twenty-nine, and the reed, and the only person in this town who has taken a yes back in the open.*”

About four people in that room had the reed and about nine had the rest.

“*Barnaby Crove, fifty-four, and the gravel and a quarter mile he has not walked in nine years.*”

“*Pell Varn, sixty-eight, and the last quarter mile, and the rule he gave on the Thursday of the tenth week.*”

“*He is not in a chair and there is no form in this county that lets a man ask to be corrected in one.*”

“*Ibb Marshe, forty-one, and two carts, and four sheets of deal out of her own cart beds, and a boy of thirteen who is not a head in the book of heads and carts.*”

“*The penny a head is on everybody over sixteen, and there is no form in this county that says the man of sixty-one may keep it that way.*”

Nobody in that room said anything about the penny.

“*And Ada Renk, sixty-four, who was in that book when she was forty-five and got up out of it in the open nineteen years ago.*”

“*She is the only one of the nine who can say what a chair is from the outside of one.*”

About four people in that room did not know her. About nine of them had seen her at a fair at a crossroads four miles off.

---

Then they asked the woman of forty-four, and she was on the deck that morning and came down the road with the others, and they asked her in the open in front of about nine people.

She said no in four sentences.

“*No,*” said Orla Fennimore.

About four seconds and the fire in that room went on not being lit.

“*I keep the book. I have kept it nineteen years and I am paid a hundred and sixty pence a year for it, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and that is the only money in this county that anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*And about nine of you have been standing in front of a barrel for about four hours working out a thing that answers questions, and every one of the answers is going to come out of a book, and the book is me.*”

“*I said on the Tuesday of the eleventh week, in about nine people’s hearing, that an answer said by me in the open is not an answer. It is the position of the record.*”

About four people in that room had that, and had been there when she said it.

“*That was nine days ago and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to be asked to be in a thing that says what a record says in about nine people’s hearing.*”

Nobody in that room said a word about her nineteen years.

Then a man of sixty-eight said the consequence out loud, in four sentences, and about four of that room had it at once.

“*Then there is no Crown that anybody can be sent for about, because the only person anybody can be sent for about in this county has said no.*”

“*And about nine people in a room have just built a thing that no form in this county reaches.*”

“*I am not saying that is wrong. I gave a rule fourteen days ago that says a thing that is not a chair can be a thing a person is sent for about, and I meant it about a plank.*”

“*And about nine of you have taken it further than I meant it, and I am not going to take it back because of a woman of forty-four.*”

---

Then the cost, done by a man of sixty-one in about nine sentences. It is a figure of hours and not of money and he said so before he gave it.

“*A Crown is a person at a barrel answering what a sheet says. A day is about nine hours. About nine people.*”

“*That is about eighty-one hours, which is about nine days of one person’s working life.*”

“*About eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and about nine of them are on that board, so a Crown costs about nine days a week out of the working week of about eleven people.*”

About four people in that room counted that, and about nine of them had heads and carts in them and not a barrel.

“*And the money is nothing. A thousand and forty pence a year comes through that landing, which is four pounds six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence of it is the wage of the woman who just said no.*”

“*A Crown costs none of the thousand and forty, because there is no form in this county that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel.*”

“*I would like it said that that is not an advantage. It is a nothing, and I have been treating a nothing as a reason for a fortnight and I am not going to do it twice.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for the sum.

---

Then the name was settled, by a person who was not going to be in the thing and who did not want it settled.

About four people in that room had been waiting since the fourth hour for somebody to say the word that had been going round all morning.

Marda Vell was in the ash. She had been in the ash twenty-one days and had not stood up once and had not gone back to the reed.

She came to the door of that room at about the seventh hour and did not come further in.

Nobody in that room asked her to come in.

“*Say it and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*You have all been saying the standing and the speaking for and the thing at the barrel since the fourth hour this morning, and every one of you has done it on purpose.*”

“*About nine people in a room have worked out that a thing which stands above a town and answers for it has a name, and about four of you are hoping nobody will say it.*”

About four people in that room said nothing at all.

“*The Crown.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*I am not going to be in it and I am not going to be asked again, and I would like it said out loud that I said the word.*”

“*I said it because I would rather it had an ugly name than a kind one, and about four of you in this room are relieved and about nine of you are not.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-six has been in a chair for nine years and has watched about nine people make a thing out of a room, and has not been able to say one word about it until this moment.*”

Then a man of sixty-eight said what the word meant, in the plainest thing he had.

“*There is a thing in a county four days off that they put on a head in a room. It is a band and a piece of tin, and it stands above a room, and everybody in the room answers to it and nobody in the room can take it off.*”

“*That is a crown. That is what has been built on a barrel in this room in about four hours, and I would like it said that I put nine names on the board and I am one of the nine.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for the tin.

---

Then it was asked two things, because a thing with a name on it on a Friday morning and no answers behind it is a thing people ask.

About nine people in that room asked it two things, and about four of them were the same question.

The first was asked by the woman of forty, and it was about a fair.

“*What did my mother say on the Wednesday of the ninth week.*”

A man of sixty-one answered it off the board in the open. It was right, and it was no use.

“*The sheet says the fourth chair from the after end had the name of Sefa Callow against it on the Wednesday of the ninth week and the Saturday of the ninth week.*”

“*It does not say she was at a fair. It does not say where she was or what she said.*”

“*I have been taking the penny off her baskets for nineteen years, and there is no column in that book for a fair, and there is no column in that book for a market four miles off.*”

“*And I would like it said that the answer I have just given is true, and that it is worth nothing to you.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

The second was asked by a man of fifty-four who could not read and had not said so that week.

“*What did the man at the landing say on the Monday afternoon when he asked for a copy of his own and was given one.*”

“*He said he could not see well,*” said Nevin Trask.

“*And he is fifty-two, and the fifth line of the copy at that landing is his and not the man of forty-three’s.*”

“*And that line has been in the same shape in about nine mouths since Monday, and I can say that because it is on the board, and the board is in this room, and there are about nine people in it.*”

About four people in that room said something at once. Nobody said anything about the fair.

---

The fever was forty-nine weeks and six days old. He had been in that county seventy-eight days, and about nine people in a front room on the low side of a landing built a body that may speak for a person who cannot be asked, out of a board, a barrel and a rule fourteen days old.

And the woman who keeps the book said no to it in four sentences, and the man who gave the rule said the consequence out loud and did not take the rule back.

And about nine people in that room can say what it is for and about four cannot, and the smaller of those two numbers is the one a man of sixty-one wrote down in his own book in his own hand.

And a woman of twenty-six who is not going to be in it and will not be asked again said the word out loud from a doorway, and would rather it had an ugly name than a kind one, and a man of sixty-eight said what a crown is and said that he is one of the nine.

And at about the ninth hour a bench was carried up to the head of the plank and set against the gunwale of the Gull, for whoever is on the board to sit on.

There is no form in the county of Kell that says a bench may be carried up a plank for anybody, and nobody sat on it that evening, and it was still standing there at the tenth hour with the dew on it.
