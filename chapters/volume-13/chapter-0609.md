# Chapter 609: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Man Of Forty-One From A Town Two Days Off In Another County Comes Up The Water With Goods And Gets Off At A Landing And Asks To Sit In On A Deck For An Hour, And On Which He Says Out Loud That He Stood Under A Lamp In A Weigh-House Nine Miles Off On A Saturday Morning About Six Weeks Ago And Had About Nine Hundred Words Read At Him By A Boy Of Fourteen, And On Which He Quotes A Line Of It Back At The Man Who Put Four Things Into It And Uses It To Say That He Has Put His Own Name On A Chair In Another County And Will Not Come Back For It, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Is Quoted At Himself In About Nine People’s Hearing And Cannot Put The Sentence Right Without Saying More

Second day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and fifty-ninth morning. Two hundred and nine days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and four days. One hundred and sixty-three days since the division. One hundred and nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the first day of the hundred and ninth week.

---

A man of forty-one came up the water in the Gull on the Wednesday with eleven hundredweight of iron in her and got off at the landing and stood on the plank and did not go up into the town.

He said his name was Heston Brake and that he was of a town two days off in the county of Ashenfield, and that he was going to sit on the deck for an hour, and that he had asked the man with the pole who said yes, and that he would go back up the water on the Friday.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the nine days.

---

Then he said what he had come for, and he said it in about nine sentences, and he had not asked whether anybody was going to be on the deck and about nine were.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because there is a man of forty-one on my deck with a bag of nothing, and there are about nine of us, and about four of us have been here all week and about nine of us have not. He has come up the water with goods and got off, and that is not a thing a man does for the goods.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people on a lighter at a landing, and I want an hour of somebody who has been somewhere, and I have been to exactly one place in nine days and I did not go for the goods.*”

“*Then say where,*” said Nell Prince.

“*There is a weigh-house nine miles down this river, sixty feet long, and a wall on the north side of it with a lamp on a bracket over it. On a Saturday morning about six weeks ago I stood under that lamp with a load of boards that never came, and I waited, because a man on that landing told me a boy read that wall out every Saturday at the sixth hour to anybody who came.*”

About four people on that deck had that. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*I stood under the lamp. A boy of about fourteen held a slate. There were about nine people under the lamp and about four more in the street where they could hear. And he read two men long of parchment, and then he read a thing off the front of it that was about nine hundred words. I did not understand one word of it and I have been carrying it for six weeks. About four weeks ago I read it a second time because I went back on purpose. There is a line in it with your name on the other side of it, and I would like you to hear it said back.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

---

Then he said it, and he said it in about nine people’s hearing, and it was a line out of a document in six hands with nothing at the top of it, and it was about the man standing nine feet off him.

“*And a man of thirty-one has put down one year, from the crossing of that stone to the crossing after it.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say what you want done with it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have put my own name on a chair. In my town. Two days off, in another county, and the chair is for the ground at the head of a drain and the holder of it went out in a barge before the last flood and was not heard of after the first reach, and there is a son in another county who does not know the town.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*And I have not come here to be put in it. I have come here because about six weeks ago a boy read a wall at me and about four weeks ago I went back and had it read at me again, and it says in it, out loud, in about nine hundred words, that no person in the county of Kell is bound by one word of it. And I have said that sentence out loud in my own town in front of about nine people twice, and I have said that there is therefore nothing in that document which stops me putting my name against a chair in Ashenfield and never coming back to take it off.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at the sentence,*” said Nell Prince.

“*And I would like him to tell me, in front of about nine of you, whether that is a thing a person may do. I have read that document twice, and I have had a copy of one page of it, and I have been carrying it two days in a bag with nothing else in it. About four of us in my town know I have got it and about nine do not.*”

---

Then a man of thirty-one was quoted at himself in about nine people’s hearing, and the true of it was that he had not written the line, and that was not a correction, and everybody on that deck worked out in about four seconds that it was not a correction and nobody said so for about nine minutes.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because a man of forty-one has just put a line of that wall back in front of you in about nine people’s hearing, and about four of us know you did not write it, and about nine of us do not, and neither of those two things is what I am going to ask.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a line of a document that is in six hands, and about four of the six are standing on this landing or within nine miles of it, and one of the six is a woman who keeps a book who is not up this plank this week.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Nell Prince.

“*About four people wrote it and about four of those four are in this town. I wrote four things into it and a woman of twenty-eight wrote a number of feet into it that a man of fifty-eight wrote first and had said out loud he knew would be useless. And there is a line about a man of thirty-one in it, and there is not one word of that line in my hand. I have wanted it not to be in my hand for six weeks. About nine of you in a town nine miles off have read it, and about nine of them do not know whose it is.*”

“*Then I am going to say the thing I came up the water to say,*” said Heston Brake. “*Not whether I may put my name on a chair. I have put my name on a chair. Whether I may leave it there and go back to a town two days off, and have the leaving be a thing I did because a boy of fourteen read a wall at me under a lamp and I did not understand one word of it. About four people in that town know where the page of paper came from and about nine do not.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I do not know how to put the sentence right, and I have known that since the fourth hour yesterday. If I say a thing about it now, then about nine of you will have it by the fourth hour and about nine of those nine will have it a different way, and that is the whole of what happened here on Monday night, and I am not going to do it twice.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

**CITED. About nine hundred words. Six hands. No name at the top of it. Cited once, by a person who is not a party to it, in a town two days off, in another county. It binds nobody. It has now been used once. Nobody asked it to be used.**

---

Then a man of forty-one was asked what the boy looked like, and answered it, and a man of thirty-one stood nine feet off and did not ask a second question.

“*Then say what he looked like,*” said Ilyan, “*and do not be sorry for me.*”

“*Fourteen, and thin, and he held the slate in both hands, and he turned it over at the end of every run of them so he could see what was coming. There were about nine of us under the lamp and about four in the street.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And he told us afterwards that he had read about two hundred and thirty small figures going left out of the squares as a run of them, and had said so out loud before he started,*” said Heston Brake. “*I have never in my life been given anything by a stranger that I could check, and I checked that one. I counted about nine of the little figures myself while he read, and they were all the same size and none of them meant anything, and I have thought about it every day since.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Then say where that page of paper is now,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*In a bag with nothing else in it, at a house in a town two days off, and there are about four people in that house who have seen it and about nine of the people in that town know it exists.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody and nobody on that deck was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-six weeks and four days old. A settlement of about nine hundred words in six hands with nothing at the top of it, which binds nobody in the county of Kell, was read out under a lamp in a weigh-house nine miles down the water on a Saturday morning about six weeks ago. It was read to a man of forty-one who had come for a load of boards that never came. About four weeks ago he went back on purpose and had it read at him a second time.

On the Wednesday he came up the water with eleven hundredweight of iron in the boat, got off at a landing, and put a line of it back in front of the man who put four things into it.

And a man of forty-one from another county has put his own name on a chair in a town two days off and is going back up the water on the Friday without coming to take it off, and about four people in that town know where one page of it came from and about nine do not.

And about nine people in Hask have it and about nine of them have it a different way by the fourth hour, and a document that binds nobody has been used once by a person who has actually read it, and there is nobody in this town who can be sent for to say so.
