# Chapter 644: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Says Out Loud What The Counter-Burden Is

Second day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-fourth morning. Two hundred and forty-four days after the settlement. The fever fifty-one weeks and four days. One hundred and ninety-eight days since the division. One hundred and forty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

Ink was on the sill of that room before the fourth hour of a Wednesday, left by a boy of thirteen mending a letter. The door was open. About nine people were in it. About four of them had been in it on the Sunday. About nine had not been in it on the Tuesday.

The board was still on the barrel, and the nine names were under it, and a sheet of deal about four feet by a foot and four inches was on the board, and a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge was in a coat on a peg.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody for the coming.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety days, and in Hask forty-three days, and the forty-three is since he came up that plank, and the ninety is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in, and he does not run the two together.

He wanted a slower hearing he could be in without being carried, and he said so out loud, and it was the first time in forty-three days he had asked for slowness. About four people in that room heard it at once and about nine did not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is on the board.

“*Because a woman of twenty-eight said no on Tuesday to letting a bench carry a man of thirty-one, and he lost a pole. About nine people in four counties will have that by the end of the week.*”

“*And a Crown that cannot be asked what it owes is a Crown that has not been looked at, and a man of sixty-one said on Sunday that nobody decided it.*”

About four people in that room had the Sunday. About nine had the bench and not the week.

Then Sera Quill stood up off the wall she had been leaning on, and she said out loud that she would bear the counter-burden, and she said what it is, and it is a thing nobody in four counties can check, and she said it in about nine sentences.

“*I will write down what that Crown says about each person in my own hand on the day it says it.*”

“*I will give each person the copy of what was said about them with my own hand and with no other hand between.*”

“*I will do it for no penny and no wage, because there is no form in this county that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel.*”

“*I will do it whether that Crown stands or is abolished, and whether the hearing is granted or is kept open by being said out loud.*”

“*I will keep no second copy for myself in any bag or coat or book.*”

“*Nobody in this room can check whether I did it except the person I gave it to on the day.*”

“*Nobody nine miles up this water can check it, because I am not in any book nine miles up this water.*”

“*Nobody in four counties can check it except one person at a time, and one person at a time is the whole of it.*”

“*If I fail there is no form that says I may be sent for about it, and I am willing to be the one who fails without a form.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for nine sentences.

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them, and the open door let the weather in.

Then a man of sixty-one said what nine sentences are a figure of, and he did it with the working, because a number without a unit is a decoration, and he has kept a book eleven years.

“*That is nine sentences, and I have counted them on my own hands, and nine is the number of this Crown and the number of this room.*”

“*And it is a figure of one woman’s working life, and a day is about nine hours, and nine days of one person’s working life is what a Crown costs.*”

“*And about eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and nine of them are on that board, and none of the thousand and forty pence reaches it.*”

“*A thousand and forty pence a year is four pounds six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence of it is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and I have done that sum out loud before.*”

About four people in that room had the thousand and forty. About nine had the nine and not the pence.

Then a man of sixty-eight said whether nine sentences can be a burden, and he said it flat, and the rule he gave is twenty-seven days old, and twenty-seven is six hundred and forty-four less six hundred and seventeen, and he did the subtraction out loud.

“*A burden nobody can check is a burden nobody can be sent for about, and this town has one person already that nobody can be sent for about at all.*”

“*And now it has two, and the second is twenty-eight, and she has said out loud that she will fail without a form.*”

“*And a Crown that is a thing nobody can be sent for about, borne by a person nobody can be sent for about, is a thing that binds nobody, and I gave the rule that made it.*”

Nobody in that room thanked him for twenty-seven.

Then Ilyan said the true of it, and it was one half again, and a woman of twenty-nine in the reed stopped him in about the fourth sentence, and he held it, and it was the ninth time in forty-three days.

“*A woman of twenty-eight has just put her hand on nine sentences nobody can check. About four people in this room have it as a bearing and about nine have it as a word.*”

“*And I asked to be carried by a pole on Tuesday and was refused, and I held it, and now I am asking to be kept slow by a hand.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest is that I have been in Hask forty-three days, and I have wanted for twenty-two days for a body to speak for me, and that is fourteen on the Tuesday of last week and eight since, and I said no to it, and now I want slowness I cannot check.*”

About four people in that room understood that. The rest of them had nine sentences.

Then a woman of twenty-nine said why she had stopped him, and it was two sentences. About four people in that town had not known either of them.

“*I stopped him because he was about to answer the question before it, and a man of sixty-eight told him on Saturday that he has been in four rooms in thirty-nine days where he was the record.*”

“*And a man who is the record in a room is a man who should let another hand carry what is said about him.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for the stopping.

Then a boy of thirteen held up his hands, and ink was on the fingers to the second joint. About nine people in that room looked at ink before they looked at a sheet, because ink is a thing a hand leaves.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and he is not in a chair, and he is not a head in the book of heads and carts, because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen who comes through that landing, and he wrote one of those sheets out on the Tuesday morning of the eleventh week and another on the Friday of the last week.

“*I wrote it on the Friday and it took me about four hours, and I read it on the Saturday and it took me about nine minutes, and I said both numbers out loud myself.*”

“*And there are fourteen of them in four counties, and eleven and one and one and one is fourteen, and it is a figure of wood and not of people, and a man of sixty-one said that out loud on the Thursday of the last week but one.*”

About four people in that room counted the fourteen. About nine looked at ink and not at wood.

Then Sera Quill asked him one thing, and she asked it out loud, and she asked it as one person to another and not as a Crown. About nine people in that room heard a woman of twenty-eight ask a boy of thirteen how a hand holds.

“*How do you hold a pen for four hours without making a mark that is not yours.*”

“*And I am asking because I have said I will write with my own hand, and I have not yet written one copy, and ink is already on your hands.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*I hold it the way my mother holds a shaft, and I do not hurry the last four lines, and a man of forty-four read one out loud in about nine minutes and did not hurry the last four lines.*”

“*And the thirty-first line is about a man of thirty-one, and the thirty-second is about a woman of seventy-one, and thirty-two lines are on both of them, and a board about nine inches long has a notch for every line.*”

About four people in that room had the thirty-two. About nine had the holding and not the lines.

Then the woman of forty-four said what ink is a figure of, and she said it out loud, and she has kept that book nineteen years. About four people in that room had never heard her speak of ink before.

“*Ink on a boy’s hands is a figure of one Friday, and it is not a figure of a county, and I have written it down in no book.*”

“*And a copy nailed inside the after thwart of the Gull and one kept at a landing nine miles up this water are read against each other once a week in the open, and the two have been in a room together four times and have agreed four times, and the fifth is the next Thursday.*”

“*And the fifth will be on a Thursday, and the three first were on Mondays, and this is the first time in nineteen years anybody has done it twice on a day that was not a Monday.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for nineteen years.

Then Sera Quill put her own hands out, and they were clean. About nine people in that room saw clean hands beside inked ones. About four understood what clean means before a burden and about nine did not.

“*Mine are clean now, and they will not be clean after I have written, and nobody in four counties can check whether I wrote clean.*”

“*And that is why nine sentences nobody can check are heavier than fourteen sheets anybody can read against each other.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for clean hands.

---

The fever was fifty-one weeks and four days old. He had been in that county ninety days, and a woman of twenty-eight said out loud in front of about nine people what the counter-burden is, and it is nine sentences nobody in four counties can check.

And a man of sixty-one counted the nine and said what it is a figure of, and a man of sixty-eight gave the age of his rule as twenty-seven days with the subtraction said out loud.

And a man of thirty-one asked for slowness he cannot check, and a woman of twenty-nine stopped him in about the fourth sentence, and he held it.

And the sheet about four feet by a foot and four inches was still on the board on the barrel, and the board about nine inches long was still in a coat on a peg, and ink was on the fingers of a boy of thirteen who had written a sheet out on the Friday.
