# Chapter 649: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Monday, And On Which A Single Crown Is Abolished In The Open And Binds Nobody

Seventh and last day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-ninth morning. Two hundred and forty-nine days after the settlement. The fever fifty-two weeks and two days. Two hundred and three days since the division. One hundred and forty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town sat on the Monday from the fourth hour to about the fortieth minute, and there was a thing to sit about. About nine people got off that plank and went up the bank. About four of them went down the cart road.

About nine people were at the barrel at about the eighth hour on a working day, in the open, with no house and no door, and about thirteen people were in front of them with a view on it. About nine feet of an open boat was between the two groups.

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked anybody for the standing.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety-five days, and in Hask forty-eight days, and the forty-eight is since he came up that plank, and the ninety-five is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in.

He wanted the abolition said out loud by the people who made the thing, and not by him, and he stood nine feet off with a knife, and he did not put his hand on any copy. About four people in front of that barrel noticed it and about nine did not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is forty and is on the board.

“*Because a board on a barrel has had nine names on it for seventeen days. About nine people can describe what it is for and about four cannot.*”

“*And seventeen is six hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and thirty-two, and I am not doing that sum out of a book. About four of you can check me and about nine cannot.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the seventeen. About nine had the board and not the number.

Then a man of sixty-one said what seventeen days are a figure of, and he did it with the working, because a number without a unit is a decoration.

“*Seventeen days is a figure of a board standing, and it is not a figure of a county and not a figure of a year.*”

“*And a year in this county is the time between one crossing of a block of limestone nine miles up this river and the next one after it, and I do not know when that is and neither does anybody in this room.*”

“*And about eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and nine of them are on that board, and that is a figure of one week at one plank.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for one week.

Then the woman of forty-four who keeps the book answered the two thirds owed since the Tuesday of the eleventh week, and she said out loud that she was answering and not the Crown. About nine people in front of that barrel heard a keeper answer best.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and she has kept that book nineteen years, and she answered one third on that Tuesday, and the third was that nobody decided it, because there was nobody in a room deciding it.

“*A saying of no does not have to be written down the way a saying of yes is.*”

“*And nobody decided that, and there was nobody in a room deciding it, and in nineteen years there has never once been a day in that book for a thing a person said out loud and did not take.*”

“*And every no in this county for nineteen years was a person standing up out of a chair, and standing up is a thing anybody can see without writing.*”

“*And I have been asked three times since the Friday of the last week but one whether I will put a hearing in that book, and I have said no three times, and a hearing nobody granted and nobody refused is a thing with no person on either side of it.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the nineteen. About nine had the no and not the years.

Then Sera Quill answered who says how many people a thing has been read to, and it was asked by a boy of thirteen on the Wednesday of the last week but two, and a woman of forty-one said on the Thursday that nine heads in one room is a figure of one room and no better than his question.

“*The only person who can say how many is the person who was in the room, one at a time.*”

“*And nine heads in a room at the head of a drain is a figure of that room. About nine people in four counties have read a line in about four rooms, and nobody has counted a road.*”

“*And I will write down what is said about each person and give it with my own hand, and nobody in four counties can check it except one person at a time.*”

“*And a road is not a room, and that is the whole of what happened to that figure.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked her for one at a time.

Then they abolished it. About nine people at a barrel did it, and it binds nobody, and everybody in that room knows that, and it was abolished anyway.

Nevin Trask took the board off the barrel with his own hands, and Garrin Tolley lifted the barrel a little off, and Wenna Callow read the nine names out loud one under another a last time, and Pell Varn said the words, and Ibb Marshe and Barnaby Crove and Nell Prince and Ada Renk and Otho Pyle each said one word after him.

Pell Varn is sixty-eight, and he gave that town its first rule on the Thursday of the tenth week, and the rule is thirty-two days old, and thirty-two is six hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and seventeen, and he did the subtraction out loud.

“*A Crown of about nine people at a barrel is abolished from this hour, in the open, with no house and no door.*”

“*And it binds nobody, and no person in this county is bound by a word of it. About nine of you have that and about four of you will go home carrying it.*”

“*And I gave the rule that made it, and a Crown is a lid with a name on it, and I am worse off than I was on the Monday of the eleventh week, and nobody is to do anything about it.*”

“*And the rule is not going back, because a rule that goes back the second time a person turns out to be right about a brother is not a rule, it is a mood.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for being worse off.

Then a woman of sixty-four who got up out of a chair nineteen years ago said one thing, and she is the only one of the nine who can say what a chair is from the outside of one, because sixty-four less forty-five is nineteen, and she did the subtraction out loud.

Ada Renk stood a little off with about thirteen with a view on it. About nine feet of an open boat was between her and the barrel, and she had been in that book when she was forty-five.

“*I got up out of it in the open nineteen years ago, and there is no column for the getting out, and abolishing a board does not put me back in.*”

“*And a Crown that binds nobody being abolished by people who know it binds nobody is still worth doing, and I am saying that as a person outside.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked her for nineteen.

Then a boy of thirteen read the thirty-first line out loud a last time under no Crown, and it took him about nine minutes with the rest, and he did not hurry the last four lines. About nine people in front of that barrel heard a line about a man of thirty-one say the same thing without a board over it.

“*It is the same words. About four of you have heard it four times. About nine have heard it once, and that is what I have got.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for nine minutes.

Then the barrel itself was spoken of, because a barrel with no board is still a barrel. About nine people in front of it put hands on staves.

“*It held nothing but a board for seventeen days. Seventeen is six hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and thirty-two.*”

“*It will hold nothing now. It stands in the open with no house and no door.*”

“*About nine feet of an open boat is still between us and about thirteen with a view on it.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked it for standing.

Then gravel was spoken of, because one man holds it nine years without walking a quarter mile he holds. About nine looked at gravel. About four looked at feet.

“*Nine years holding. Nine years not walking. Both are mine.*”

“*Abolition does not walk it for me. It stays.*”

“*Nobody thanked me for holding. Nobody will.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked gravel for lying still.

Then hands were put on staves again, because a barrel with no board is still wood. About nine felt staves. About four felt hoops.

“*Wood about four feet by a foot and four inches once held. Now it leans.*”

“*Nine names under it. No Crown over it. That is abolition.*”

“*It binds nobody. Everybody knows. It stands anyway.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked wood for holding.

Then Ilyan said nothing. About four people in front of that barrel noticed that he said nothing. About nine did not know there was anything to say, and it was the eleventh time in forty-eight days.

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them. About nine feet of an open boat was still between about nine people and about thirteen with a view on it.

---

The fever was fifty-two weeks and two days old. He had been in that county ninety-five days, and a single Crown was abolished by about nine people at a barrel on a working day in the open with no house and no door and about nine feet of an open boat between them and about thirteen people in front of them, and it binds nobody, and everybody knows that, and it was abolished anyway.

And a woman of forty-four said out loud that a saying of no does not have to be written down the way a saying of yes is, and nobody decided that, and it was owed since the Tuesday of the eleventh week.

And a woman of twenty-eight said out loud that the only person who can say how many is the person who was in the room one at a time, and it was asked by a boy of thirteen and owed since.

And the board about four feet by a foot and four inches was leaning against a barrel with nine names under it and no Crown over it, and a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches was in a coat, and ink was on the fingers of a boy who had written a sheet out on the Friday.
