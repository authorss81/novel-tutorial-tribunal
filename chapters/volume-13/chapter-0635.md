# Chapter 635: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Twelfth Week, And A Monday, And The Town Sits And Is Over In About Forty Minutes, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-One Asks The Thing At The Barrel To Speak For A Man Whose Line Is In The Book Of Heads And Carts And In No Other Book In That County, And It Cannot, And On Which The Name Is Given Out Loud By A Man Of Sixty-One Against His Own Book And Not By A Man Of Thirty-One, And On Which A Line That Is A Penny A Head Has Been Standing In That Book For About Two Years And Cannot Be Struck Out

Seventh and last day of the hundred and twelfth week. His three hundred and eighty-fifth morning. Two hundred and thirty-five days after the settlement. The fever fifty weeks and two days. One hundred and eighty-nine days since the division. One hundred and thirty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town sat on the Monday from the fourth hour to about the fortieth minute, and there was nothing to sit about.

About nine people got off that plank and went up the bank, and about four of them went down the cart road.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the sitting.

Then the Crown sat, for the first time as a Crown, at about the eighth hour at the head of the plank, on a board on a barrel.

There were about nine people in it and about thirteen standing in front of it, and the bench that had been carried up on Friday was still standing against the gunwale of the Gull.

About four people had noticed that nobody had sat on it. About nine had not noticed the bench at all.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is on the board and wrote on it.

“*Because a thing that has a name on it is going to be asked about the first thing anybody thinks of, and I would like it said out loud that what I am going to ask is not a test and is not clever.*”

“*And it is a question I have wanted answered since Thursday.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel moved.

---

She asked it in the open, in the same shape a line is asked for, the ground before the name.

About four people standing in front of that barrel understood the whole of it before the name. About nine of them did not.

“*There is a line in the book of heads and carts that has been in that book about two years, and the man of sixty-one has been taking the penny off it every Friday for about two years.*”

“*There is no line against that man in the book of chairs, and there is no name of his on that sheet of deal, and there is no line in the book of heads and carts for anybody else who has ever come through that landing and not come back.*”

“*I keep two carts. I would like the thing at that barrel to speak for him.*”

Nothing was said for about the length of a hundred paces.

Then the Crown tried, out loud, in front of about thirteen people, and what it did was read.

“*There is no chair with his name on it,*” said Nevin Trask, from the board.

“*There is nothing against him on that sheet of deal, and there is nothing on that board.*”

“*And about nine of us have been in this town about nine years and have never once been told a name of his.*”

“*A Crown of about nine people can only say what is written down somewhere, and there is nowhere with him in it.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel said anything to that.

About four people in front of it had that. About nine of them heard a thing about a barrel.

Then a man of sixty-one took the book out from under his arm and said the name out loud, in the open, in about thirteen people’s hearing.

It was his own book, and it was against him.

About four people in that bank had known it. About nine had not.

“*Jonas Roel. Fifty-seven. Of a town four days off.*”

“*He came up this landing about nine times in about two years with a basket and a cart, and he went down again each time, and on the ninth time he went down and he did not come up.*”

“*And about two years ago the man of forty-four in that town came up the road with his coat buttoned up to the neck, and told me at that gate and not in a room.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for two years.

“*And I have been taking a penny off that line every Friday for about two years and I did not stop.*”

“*I am going to say the number out loud, because about four of you have it and about nine of you do not, and because I have not said it in nineteen years.*”

“*And because about two of you have been wondering about it since the Thursday of the tenth week.*”

About four people in that bank counted that against each other.

“*About nine heads at a penny a head is about nine pence, and about nine carts at a penny a cart is about nine pence.*”

“*And nine and nine is about eighteen pence, which is one shilling and sixpence.*”

About four people in that bank had the number. About nine of them had nothing else.

“*And that is a figure of about two years at one landing, and it is not a figure of what that man owed anybody, and it is not a figure of what he was worth.*”

“*I would like it said that one shilling and sixpence is about a fortnight of bread in a house of four.*”

“*And I have been taking it, and I have bought bread with it, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be forgiven for it, and both of those are mine to do and not yours.*”

---

Then he said why the line could not be struck out, and it took about nine sentences.

About four people in front of that barrel had known every word of it for about two years. About nine had not known any of it.

“*A line in that book is a penny. Take the line out and the penny goes with it.*”

“*About nine households in this town send a basket or a cart through that landing in a year, and about four of them send one on a Friday, and that is not my money. It is the landing’s.*”

“*And the landing is a thousand and forty pence a year, which is four pounds six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence of that is a woman’s wage and the rest is the landing.*”

About four people in front of that barrel counted the year out. About nine did not.

“*I have tried to strike that line out twice. Once in the first year and once in the second, and both times I found a reason before the ink was dry.*”

“*And both reasons were that there is no form in this county that says a man may.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel moved.

“*And I am sixty-one years old and I have kept that book eleven years, and I am going to say out loud in about thirteen people’s hearing that no form is not a reason.*”

“*It is a nothing. I have been treating a nothing as a reason for about two years and I have had it in my own hand in my own book the whole time.*”

“*And about four of you have known that since Friday and about nine of you have not.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for either of the two times.

---

Then the man of thirty-one tried, and was stopped, and the stopping was done by an ordinary person and not by the Crown.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*There is a woman in a town four days off with a coat buttoned up to the neck who can be sent for about, because she came to that gate and said it to the man of sixty-one.*”

“*And there is a man in that town who can be sent for about a gate. If a man of sixty-one can be sent for about a gate then he can be sent for about a line.*”

“*And a Crown of about nine people can stand in front of that gate in about four days and ask a question about a dead man, and then there is a thing in that town that answers for Jonas Roel.*”

“*No,*” said Wenna Callow.

She said it before he had got to the end of it, out loud, in about thirteen people’s hearing.

About four people in front of that barrel had expected her to say that. About nine had not.

“*You have not asked a man of sixty-one a question about that book in thirty-one days, and he has not offered it, and there is no form in this county that says either of you may.*”

“*And if you ask him now, in front of about thirteen people, on a Monday morning, about a dead man’s penny, then the first thing anybody in that town learns about a Crown is that it can be sent for about a man in a book.*”

“*And that is a Crown that anybody can use, and you would be the one who showed them the door.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the whole of it. Nobody said anything to him about it.

“*And a man of sixty-one has just said out loud that he has been taking a dead man’s money for about two years and that no form is not a reason, and about nine of us have heard that.*”

“*If you had asked him a question about it two minutes before he got there, we would all have had the number and none of the rest.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel said a word to him about that either.

---

Then the man of sixty-one said the finding, and it was the true shape of a thing four hours old.

He said it before anybody else could, and nobody thanked him for it.

“*A Crown that can answer for a person who is not in a chair and not in a book is a Crown that can answer for anybody.*”

“*And a Crown that can answer for anybody is not a Crown. It is a mouth.*”

About four people in front of that barrel understood every word of that. About nine of them needed it said twice.

“*It has taken four hours and about thirteen people to make and one Monday morning to find out what it is not.*”

“*And about four of you have that and about nine of you have not.*”

“*And I am going to write the second of those numbers down in this book tonight, because it is a number of people and I have not got a column for that, and I am going to make one and put it on the back of the last leaf.*”

“*And I would like about four of you to tell me if that is a thing a man of sixty-one may do.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel said yes and nobody said no.

---

The fever was fifty weeks and two days old. He had been in that county eighty-one days, and the first thing the Crown in that town was asked to speak for was a man of fifty-seven called Jonas Roel of a town four days off.

He came up that landing about nine times in about two years and went down the ninth time and did not come up, and his name is in no book in that county except one.

And the name was given out loud by the man of sixty-one who takes the money, against his own book, and the reason he gave was that he had been treating a nothing as a reason for about two years, and nobody was sent for about it and about thirteen people heard it in the open.

And the number is one shilling and sixpence, which is about nine heads at a penny and about nine carts at a penny in about two years at one landing, and it is a figure of two years at one landing and not a figure of what the man was worth.

And a line in a book of heads and carts is a penny a head, and the line has been there since a man of forty-four from a town four days off came up a road with his coat buttoned to the neck and told the man of sixty-one at a gate and not in a room.

And a man of sixty-one has been putting that penny in a box on Fridays for about two years, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says he may take it out, and the box is under the counter at a landing nine miles up that water, and about four people in that town have thought about the box in the last two days, and about nine have not.
