# Chapter 647: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Split Is Put To Both Sides In The Open One Under Another

Fifth day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-seventh morning. Two hundred and forty-seven days after the settlement. The fever fifty-two weeks old. Two hundred and one days since the division. One hundred and forty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About nine people were on that bank on the Saturday morning at about the ninth hour, and about thirteen were standing a little off with a view on it, and about eleven people come up that plank in a working week. About nine of them are on a board on a barrel in a front room.

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody for the coming.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety-three days, and in Hask forty-six days, and the forty-six is since he came up that plank, and the ninety-three is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in.

He wanted the split said out loud by the people who are in it, and not by him, and he stood nine feet off with a knife, and he did not say the true of either half first, and it was the first time in forty-six days he had not said one half before anybody else spoke.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Wenna Callow, who is forty and is on the board.

“*Because this town has been two halves since the Tuesday of the eleventh week. About nine people in the room it was described in are the person on the other side.*”

“*And both halves are still right. About four of you have it as opinions and about nine have it as people, and it is people.*”

About four people on that bank had the Tuesday. About nine had the halves and not the day.

Then she put it one under another in the open. About nine people were the person on the other side, and each said the other’s half out loud, and nobody thanked anybody for the saying.

“*The first half is the deck,*” said a man of sixty-one, standing on the bank, in about nine people’s hearing.

“*About nine people were on the deck of the Gull when a line about a man of thirty-one was read. About nine were not, and every one of the first is right about their half.*”

“*A thing read out in rooms is a thing people go looking with, and a woman of thirty went four days up this water because she wants to be the one who tells her brother.*”

“*And she is right. About four of you have it as a leaving and about nine do not.*”

Nobody on that bank took the other half off her.

“*The second half is the room,*” said Ibb Marshe, who is forty-one and keeps two carts, standing on the same bank, in the same hearing.

“*About nine people were in a room at the head of a drain in a town two days off when a man of forty-four read a sheet from the first line to the last in about nine minutes.*”

“*And a woman of about thirty-eight stood up off a wall and said she had never been asked anything, and she is right. About nine people in that room have said her name since.*”

“*And a record anybody can be sent for about goes where it goes. About four of you have it as a carrying and about nine do not.*”

About four people on that bank had both halves. About nine had one and not the other.

Then a woman of twenty-nine who sat down in the reed on the Tuesday of the last week but one said her half from the other side. About nine people on that bank heard a person say the half they are not in.

Nell Prince is twenty-nine, and she has not stood up since about the second hour of that Tuesday, and she stood up out of that chair on the Wednesday of the tenth week and sat down again on the Tuesday, and she is the only person in that town who has taken a yes back in the open.

“*I am of the room, and I am saying the deck, and the deck is right.*”

“*A woman of thirty who went four days up this water is right to go, because a line with her brother in it is a thing people go looking with.*”

“*And I am right to stay, because a chair I said yes to in about nine seconds is a chair I may take back when I find what is on it.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for staying.

Then a man of thirty-eight said the other half from the other side, and he has a child of nine, and he is on the board, and he poles nine miles twice a week.

“*I am of the deck, and I am saying the room, and the room is right.*”

“*A woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a goat is right to stand up off a wall and say she was never asked, because she was never asked.*”

“*And a man who poles nine miles with a sheet is right to pole it, because a sheet is only true when two people nine miles apart have said so.*”

About four people on that bank had the nine miles. About nine had the goat and not the pole.

Then the woman of forty-four who keeps the book answered the question asked once at the Thursday of the ninth week, and it was asked by a woman of twenty-nine, and it was whether a person may take a yes back when they find what is on the chair, and nobody answered it then.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, and she has kept that book nineteen years for thirteen shillings and fourpence a year, which is a hundred and sixty pence, and she is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about a chair, and she said out loud that she was answering and not the Crown.

“*A person may take a yes back.*”

Nobody on that bank moved.

“*And I am going to say the rest of it, because a yes taken back in the open is not a line taken off a sheet. About four of you have mixed the two since Thursday.*”

“*Nell Prince took a yes back in the open, and she is still on that sheet, and the sheet is what anybody can be sent for about, and the chair is what a person sits in.*”

“*And taking back puts a person out of a chair and does not put a line out of a book, and there is no form in this county that says it does.*”

“*And I have kept that book nineteen years and I have never once written a day for a thing a person said out loud and did not take, and that is why.*”

About four people on that bank had the difference. About nine had the taking and not the book.

Then a man of forty-five who keeps a smithy at the top of that drain said one word, and he has been in that room nine times since Saturday and has not said a word in any of them. About nine people on that bank heard a silent man speak.

Otho Pyle is forty-five, and he is on the board that was, and he said nothing for nine visits, and his one word was the other half.

“*Both.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for nine visits.

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them. About nine people on that bank heard one word carry two halves. About four understood that one word is a shape and about nine did not.

Then a girl of nine sitting on a settle four miles off that cart road was spoken of, and she has a slate on her knees with no name on it, and she has heard about nine words of what happened at that barrel. About four people on that bank knew she was in that house and about nine did not.

“*She will be told on a Saturday, and her father will tell her, and he has told her twice that this is not their business, and it is not true.*”

“*And a child of nine hearing both halves is a person hearing right twice, and there is no column for that in any book.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her father for the telling not yet done.

Then Ilyan said nothing about either half. About four people on that bank noticed that he said nothing. About nine did not know there was anything to say, and it was the twelfth time in forty-six days.

Sera Quill said why his silence is a shape and not a failure, and she said it out loud, and she has been in that county six days, and nobody can be sent for about her.

“*He has said one half out loud seven times in forty-six days. About four of you have been counting and about nine have not.*”

“*And today he said neither. About nine people said both, and both are still right.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for six days.

Then reeds were named, because one woman has not stood since Tuesday. About nine looked at reeds. About four looked at chairs.

“*Nine years in a chair. Eighteen days without standing. Both are true.*”

“*Taking back does not cut reeds. Staying does not plant them.*”

“*Both halves cut and mend. That is ordinary.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked reeds for standing.

Then hurdles were counted, because thirty feet stand at the head of eleven feet of plank. About nine counted. About four watched water.

“*Thirty feet of hurdles. Eleven feet of plank. Eleven people a week.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked hurdles for holding.

Then a cart was heard down that road, and it had one wheel making a noise, and about nine people on that bank knew the noise before they saw the cart. About four knew whose cart it is.

“*It is mine. It came up two days of a road with me driving it. It will go down again.*”

“*A road two days long does not shorten because both halves are right. It stays two days.*”

“*Fourteen sheets in four counties stay fourteen. Eleven and one and one and one is fourteen.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for the noise.

Then a man of thirty-one touched the sideboard of that cart, and he did not climb in. He stood nine feet off. He held it.

“*I have been in Hask forty-six days. I have not driven.*”

“*Both halves being right does not put me on a board. It puts me on a bank.*”

About four people on that bank heard a hand on wood. The rest heard days.

---

The fever was fifty-two weeks old. He had been in that county ninety-three days, and the split in that town was put to the people on both sides in the open one under another. About nine people were the person on the other side.

And both halves are still right, and the deck is right about going looking, and the room is right about going where it goes.

And a woman of forty-four said out loud that a person may take a yes back, and taking back puts a person out of a chair and does not put a line out of a book, and it was asked once and never answered until now.

And a man of thirty-one said neither half, and a woman of twenty-eight said why, and both halves of a town of about eleven hundred were still right on a Saturday morning, and a boat was lying with her head to a landing nine miles up this water.
