# Chapter 618: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Tenth Week, And A Friday, And On Which A Sheet With Twenty-Nine Lines On It Is Found To Have A Split In It That Is Not The One From The Sixth Day, And On Which A Man Of Sixty-One Is Proved Right In The Open By About Four People And It Costs Him, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-One Who Kept Two Carts Stops Coming Up That Plank, And On Which It Is Not A Betrayal And It Is Not A Misunderstanding, And On Which Two People Come Down The Water In A Lighter Who Have Not Been In This Town Before

Fourth day of the hundred and tenth week. His three hundred and sixty-eighth morning. Two hundred and eighteen days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and six days. One hundred and seventy-two days since the division. One hundred and eighteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The split in that town on the Thursday was not the split from the sixth day, and the man of thirty-eight said so out loud before anybody else could get it wrong, and about four people heard the difference and about nine did not.

The first split was between people who were on a deck and people who were not, and every one of the first was right about their half and every one of the second was right about their half. The two halves cannot be put in one room again without one of the two being asked something they have already been asked once in the dark.

This one was inside the nine. It had been on the sheet since Thursday morning, and it was about nine people against about four, and it was not about a deck.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a sheet with twenty-nine lines on it has been read three times since it was cut, and about four people on this deck want two more things on it and about nine do not. I would like the difference said out loud in the plainest words anybody in this town has used this fortnight.*”

“*The eleven,*” said Wenna Callow. “*The eleven lines with a name against them and nobody in them. I want the day on them. The day each name went in. Not a reason, just the day.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*And I am not asking for a reason,*” said Wenna Callow, “*I am asking for a day, and a day is not a judgement. If a man of thirty-one writes a day against a name, then that man was in a chair on that day, and about nine people can be sent for to ask him what he said. He cannot say he was not there.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine of them did not.

---

Then a woman of forty-one who kept two carts and a boy of thirteen who poled said what was wrong with a day, and it was four sentences, and she had been working on that ground four days the week before.

“*A day is a day. That is all it is and that is why about four of you want it, and it is exactly why we do not.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*The day my load went in the water is a day. I know the day. I have known it for the whole of the wet and I have not been able to say what the load was. If the day went on a sheet I would have to say what the load was on the same morning, in front of about nine people, because the day is only worth anything on that sheet if the person it belongs to can be asked about it.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*And the man of fifty-four gave a barrow and a half of his own ground on a Saturday and did not say so until the Monday, and if the day had gone on the sheet he would have had to say it on the Saturday. He did not want to say it on the Saturday. I did not want to say my load on a Wednesday. About eight of the eleven of you are the same and about four of you have not been here when the thing that was given was not a thing that was done.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the four days.

---

Then a man of sixty-one was proved right in the open, out loud, in front of about nine people, by about four of them, and it cost him, and he did not say one word about the cost.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I said on the Friday of the ninth week that I came up that plank with the wrong book on purpose, and I said it because there are two books in this town and a penny a head in one of them, and about nine people have gone eleven years without the two being confused.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And a man of sixty-eight wrote a rule on Thursday. There is a third thing now, with a chair and a day against it in two places. About four of us on this deck have spent Thursday and Friday putting more of this town into that sheet than is in it. The penny is the next thing, and a man of thirty-one is standing there with his mouth open, and I am going to say no before he asks.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*No,*” said Nevin Trask. “*The penny does not go on that sheet and it is not mine to put there. A penny a head and a penny a cart. About eight hundred heads and about two hundred and forty carts. A thousand and forty pence, four pounds six shillings and eightpence. Three pounds of that is the book. What is left is three hundred and twenty pence between two people who are not related and have never quarrelled in eleven years. That is a whole, and it is the only whole in this town, and it is a whole because nobody has ever written it down twice and compared it.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*Say this out loud and let it be said about me, because I would rather it was said by me than worked out by somebody else on a Sunday. It is not that I am afraid of a number.*”

About four people on that bank said something at once. About nine did not.

Nobody on that plank thanked the man of sixty-one for saying it out loud before anybody else could work it out, and about four people in a town of about eleven hundred people went home that evening knowing one more thing about him than they had known on the Thursday, and he did not know that and was not told.

Then the man of thirty-one said his own half out loud, and it was the shortest thing he had said in sixteen days, and nobody on that deck was sorry for him.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am the man with the open mouth. I am going to say that the penny should go on that sheet, and I am going to say why, and then I am going to be told no twice.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*A sheet that is read against another sheet and that cannot be quietly wrong is the first thing in this town that anybody can be sent for about. The penny is the next oldest thing in it. There are about eight hundred heads and about two hundred and forty carts in a year and every one of them comes to a landing, and every one of them is a person who can be checked against, and if the penny is on that sheet then the count of carts and the count of chairs are in the same room for the first time in nineteen years.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*I have said that out loud because I would rather be told no by the man who said no than have not said it. About four of us know by the fourth hour that I said it and about nine know that a man of sixty-one told me no before I opened my mouth, and both of those are in that town now and neither of them can be put back in the book.*”

About four people on that plank understood that. About nine did not.

“*It is that if the penny goes on that sheet, then the woman who keeps the chairs and I are in a room together once a week over a figure, and we have been eleven years without a room. There are two of us, and one of us has to be able to say no to the other about something that is not the book. I do not want that room. I have not wanted it for eleven years and I have never told anybody why.*”

About four seconds went past.

Nobody on that plank said anything to that.

“*And I would like it said out loud in front of about nine people that about four of you now know that the man who takes the money in this town would rather nobody counted it, and about nine of you do not, and I am not going to stand here and be a better man than that.*”

---

Then the woman of forty-one did not come up the plank on the Friday, and nobody sent for her, and about four people noticed and about nine did not, and it was not a quarrel and nobody in that town was on either side of it.

She had been on the ground four days the week before and she had been in a chair once, in the ash, about nine years ago, and both of those were on the sheet, and she had asked for neither.

The man of thirty-eight noticed at about the third hour and said so out loud, and about four people on that bank had not noticed either, and about nine had.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because a woman has not come up that plank who has come up it every working day since the Friday of the ninth week, and the only two people who can say why are a man of thirty-eight who does not know and a man of thirty-one who is not going to go and ask.*”

“*I am not going to go and ask her,*” said Ilyan.

“*Say why.*”

“*Because she was on that ground four days and nobody was paid for any of it, and there is no form in this county that says they may be. About four days ago she told me herself that the cost of those four days is that a count cannot tell a gift from a day’s work. And then on Thursday she is a line on a sheet, and I wrote the sheet.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*If I go up that cart road to a woman’s door and ask her why she is not here, then in about four years there is a form in this county that says a person does not come up a plank because a stranger came to the door about it. She is one of about nine people who would be on that form, and I put her there.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

Nobody on that bank thanked him for not going.

Nobody on that plank thanked her for the four days.

Then a woman of thirty-four came down the plank at about the second hour on the Friday afternoon, and she had come down the water in a lighter with a man of forty-one from a town two days off, and she was not asleep, and about nine people on that deck had never seen her before in their lives.

Nobody on that plank said anything about her at all.

---

The fever was forty-seven weeks and six days old. He had been in that county sixty-four days. A sheet with twenty-nine lines on it has a split in it, and it is not the split from the sixth day. The split is between about nine people who think a count is a fact and about four who think a count is a thing that happens to you, and every one of the first is right about their half.

And a man of sixty-one said no to the penny in the open and gave the true reason, which is that he does not want a room. About four people in a town of about eleven hundred now know that the man who takes the money would rather nobody counted it, and he said so himself before anybody else could.

And a woman of forty-one who kept two carts and cut four days of withy hurdles with the rest of them did not come up that plank on the Friday, and nobody sent for her.

And two carts are standing on the bank above that landing with a boy of thirteen asleep across the shafts of one of them, and about nine carts a week do not come down that plank in a bad spell, and the woman who keeps them has not come up it since the Thursday, and the boy of thirteen has come up it every day of his life because his father is on the bank, and about nine people in a town of about eleven hundred people have noticed that and about nine have not.
