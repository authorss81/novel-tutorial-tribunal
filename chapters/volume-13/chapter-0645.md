# Chapter 645: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Fourteenth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which Two Copies Are In A Room A Fifth Time And Agree, And On Which A Removed Witness Is Asked A Second Question Harder Than The First

Third day of the hundred and fourteenth week. His three hundred and ninety-fifth morning. Two hundred and forty-five days after the settlement. The fever fifty-one weeks and five days. One hundred and ninety-nine days since the division. One hundred and forty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town sat on the Thursday from the fourth hour to about the fortieth minute, and there was nothing to sit about. About nine people got off that plank. About four of them went up the bank. About four went down the cart road.

A man of thirty-eight went up the water in the Gull at about the fifth hour with a sheet of deal in a coat, and a board about nine inches long with thirty-two notches cut in its edge was in the same coat, and nobody on that plank thanked him for the going.

Ilyan Vester was thirty-one, and he had been in that county ninety-one days, and in Hask forty-four days, and the forty-four is since he came up that plank, and the ninety-one is since he came into that county, and the evening he came in is not a day he was in.

He wanted the fifth to be true without him touching it, and he stood nine feet off with a knife while it was read, and he did not put his hand on any copy, and it was the second time in four days he had held a no about his own hands and not been sorry for the wanting.

About nine people were in the yard nine miles up that water at about the ninth hour, and a man of forty-three who has kept that landing nineteen years stood by the counter, and a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well stood with a hand along the top of it, and a woman of forty-nine stood a little off who did not know what a copy was on the Friday of the last week but one.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the wall, with the board in his hand.

“*Because the two copies have been in a room together four times, and the four times they have agreed, and the three first were on Mondays and the fourth was on a Thursday.*”

“*And this is the fifth, and it is on a Thursday. About nine people in this yard have never once read a line of them against another line.*”

About four people in that yard had the four. About nine had the yard and not the week.

Then he read the whole thing out loud in that yard to about nine people, and it took him about nine minutes, and he did not hurry the last four lines, and a man of forty-three read the other one after him line against line, name against name, day against day.

Nobody in that yard thanked either of them for nine minutes.

“*Thirty-two lines on both of them. The thirty-second is the thirty-second on both of them. The thirty-first is the thirty-first on both of them.*”

“*And it is the same day and the same Monday on both of them, and a man of forty-three wrote it with a knife out of a whittling block in that yard on the Monday of the eleventh week at about the second hour.*”

“*They agree. And that is the fifth time the two of them have been in a room together, and the fifth time they have agreed.*”

“*And it took me about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading, and nobody has ever once asked me to do it, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to stop.*”

About four people in that yard counted the five. Nobody else did.

Then he cut the thirty-second notch? No, the board has thirty-two notches, and he ran a thumb along the edge to show it. About four people in that yard saw a board about nine inches long with a notch for every line. About nine did not know what a notch is a figure of.

“*A board about nine inches long with a notch cut in it for every line is how a record is checked, and a notch is a thing a man can hold in his hand.*”

Nobody in that yard thanked him for the holding.

---

He came down and got in at about the second hour of the afternoon, and put the board on the barrel in front of about nine people without saying a word about where it had been, and the two copies were read against each other on the deck of the Gull in the open, in about nine minutes, and they agreed a second time that day.

About nine people were in front of that barrel, and about thirteen were standing a little off with a view on it, and there were about nine feet of an open boat between the two groups, and there was no house and no door, because it was the head of a plank in the open.

Then an ordinary person asked the removed witness a second question, and it was harder than the first, and it was asked out loud in front of about nine people.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, and he holds the gravel at the head of that drain, and he cannot read, and he had not said so that week, and he said so now before he asked, because a question about a book is a question about reading.

“*I cannot read, and I have held that gravel nine years, and I have not walked a quarter mile I hold in nine years.*”

“*And I am asking a second thing about a man whose line is in the book of heads and carts and in no other book in that county, and the first thing was whether a Crown could speak for him, and it could not.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the first. About nine had the gravel and not the line.

“*Jonas Roel. Fifty-seven. Of a town four days off. He came up that landing about nine times in about two years and went down the ninth time and did not come up.*”

“*And a man of sixty-one has been taking a penny off that line every Friday for about two years, and the number is one shilling and sixpence, which is about nine pence of heads and about nine pence of carts, and nine and nine is about eighteen pence.*”

“*And it is a figure of about two years at one landing and not a figure of what that man was worth, and it is in a box under the counter at a landing nine miles up this water.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel moved.

“*Will somebody take that box out from under that counter, and strike that line out of that book, and carry that shilling and sixpence four days off to a house with a coat buttoned up to the neck, and say out loud that it is not owed.*”

About four seconds went past and nobody filled them.

Then a man of sixty-one answered, and he answered out loud, and the answer is that nobody is going to do it, and the reason is about four years.

“*Nobody is going to do it.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for the plainness.

“*And I am going to say why in the open, because about four of you have it as a penny and about nine have it as a man, and it is neither.*”

“*A line in that book is a penny, and take the line out and the penny goes with it. About nine households in this town send a basket or a cart through that landing in a year.*”

“*And the landing is a thousand and forty pence a year, which is four pounds six shillings and eightpence, and I have done that sum out loud, and there is no form that says a man may be paid for standing at a barrel.*”

“*If I strike that line now, then a Crown that can answer for anybody will have been taught by a man of sixty-one that a book can be made right by taking a penny out of it.*”

“*And in about four years a Crown of about nine people will want to speak for every person nobody can be sent for about, and it will point to that taking out and call it a speaking.*”

“*And I am not going to be the reason there is a form in that county that says a man may be asked why a line was struck, and I have tried to strike it out twice and found a reason before the ink was dry both times.*”

About four people in front of that barrel had the twice. About nine had the box and not the years.

Then Ilyan said the true of it, and it was one half, and a woman of twenty-eight let him say it to the fourth sentence and then held up a hand, and he held it.

“*A man of sixty-one has just said nobody is going to carry a shilling and sixpence four days off. About four people in front of that barrel have it as a hardness and about nine do not.*”

“*And the first question was whether a Crown could speak for a man it cannot be sent for about, and it could not, and this one is harder, because it is whether a book can be made right by undoing.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest is that I have been in Hask forty-four days, and I wanted a shortcut that would have worked in about four days, and I said no to it, and now I am watching a harder no.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel thanked him for the watching.

Then a woman of twenty-eight asked the man of sixty-one one thing about the box, and she asked it out loud. She has borne nine sentences nobody can check for three days.

“*Is the box locked.*”

“*It is under the counter at a landing nine miles up this water. It holds one shilling and sixpence. That is about nine pence of heads. That is about nine pence of carts.*”

“*If nobody is going to take it out, then my nine sentences must carry what the box cannot.*”

Nobody in front of that barrel moved.

“*The box is not locked. It is shut. There is no form that says I may open it. There is no form that says I may not.*”

“*I have kept it shut about two years. I will keep it shut.*”

About four people in front of that barrel heard shut and not locked. The rest heard a box.

---

The fever was fifty-one weeks and five days old. He had been in that county ninety-one days, and two copies were in a room a fifth time on a Thursday and agreed, and it took about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading.

And an ordinary man of fifty-four who cannot read asked a removed witness a second question harder than the first, whether somebody would take a box from under a counter and strike a line and carry one shilling and sixpence four days off.

And a man of sixty-one said out loud that nobody is going to do it, and the reason is about four years, and a Crown taught to take a penny out will call a taking out a speaking.

And a sheet about four feet by a foot and four inches was on a barrel in the open with about nine feet of an open boat between about nine people and about thirteen with a view on it, and ink was on the edge of a board about nine inches long where a thumb had run along thirty-two notches.
