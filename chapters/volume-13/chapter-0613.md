# Chapter 613: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Ninth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which The People Who Have Been Coming Up That Plank Are Counted Out Loud For The First Time And There Are Nine Of Them, And On Which Each Of The Nine Says Out Loud Why They Come, And On Which None Of The Nine Reasons Is About Any Of The Other Eight, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Gives The Nine A Word For It And A Man Of Sixty-One Says Out Loud That The Word Is His And Not Theirs, And On Which He Says So

Sixth day of the hundred and ninth week. His three hundred and sixty-third morning. Two hundred and thirteen days after the settlement. The fever forty-seven weeks and one day. One hundred and sixty-seven days since the division. One hundred and thirteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

It is fair week at the crossroads four miles off, and fair week comes twice a year, and in fair week the cart road is not a road and about nine of the people who would be on it are selling what they have on somebody else’s ground.

There were about nine people on the plank of the Gull on the Sunday, and that is more than there have been on any day of the eleven.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because a man of thirty-one has been asking one person at a time for two days. I have watched him do it in a way that nine people can hear. I would like it said out loud in front of all nine of them that this is happening, and that I have not stopped it because it seemed like a fair way to do.*”

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the thirteen days.

“*I am standing in front of nine people, and I have asked about nine people one at a time why they come up this plank. I am going to ask the ninth. Then I am going to do a thing with the answers that I am not going to enjoy, and I would like about four of you to stop me if I do it wrong.*”

About four people on that plank said something at once. About nine did not.

---

Then nine people said why they came, and nobody stopped anybody, and no two of them said the same thing.

The man of thirty-eight who cut the reed said the water and his child of nine. The water was because a chair for ground a person is not standing on is a strange thing to be asked to speak about. The child was because his child had asked twice and he had said twice that it was not their business.

The woman of forty said her mother was the fourth chair from the after end, and she wanted the number to be right before the Saturday, whichever way the Saturday went.

The man of sixty-one said the penny. He took a penny a head and half a penny a cart. The woman who kept the chairs took the penny a cart and the book. The two of them had three hundred and twenty pence between them in a year, which is one pound six shillings and eightpence, and a hundred and sixty pence each, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence.

He said he had come up the plank because there are two books in this town and a penny a head in one of them, and about nine people have gone eleven years without the two being confused. He would like it said that he came up on the Friday with the wrong book on purpose.

About four people on that plank had the whole of that. About nine had the penny and not the rest.

The woman of forty-one said two carts. She was called Ibb Marshe and she had two carts and a boy of thirteen who poled, and the road is the road the carts are on. The plank is eleven feet long and it is under water for six weeks of the year, and she had lost a load in the wet. She had not been able to say to anybody what it was that had gone.

The man of sixty-eight said the quarter mile. He was called Pell Vearn and he had kept the last quarter mile of that cart road and the plank at the end of it for twenty-two years. He said a plank is eleven feet and there is a good deal of the year that is not a road. He said about nine people in this town have been talking about a chair for a head of drain, and nobody has said the word bridge out loud, and there is no bridge.

The man of fifty-four said his own ground. He was called Barnaby Crove and he said the gravel at the head of the drain was his and had been his father’s. He said a chair for ground a person is the near party to is a chair nobody can ask him about. He had said yes to it in the open nine years ago in about nine people’s hearing, and had not once regretted it, and thought about it most weeks.

The woman of thirty said her brother. She was called Perdy Lound and one of the six who have not come is her brother. He is in a county four days off and she does not know whether he is ill. She had come up the plank on the Wednesday and had not been able to say that on the deck, and had said it to a man of thirty-one instead. She had said it to a stranger because a stranger cannot be the person who tells the rest of a family.

The boy of eleven said nowhere else. He was called Rennick Small and he came up because his father was on the bank and there was a bench on the boat. Nobody had ever told him he was not to, and he had worked out that if he stopped coming his father would stop coming.

And the woman of twenty-six said her chair. She was called Marda Vell and she was in the ash, and she had been in the ash for nine years. She came up the plank because if she stopped coming then her chair would be a chair with a name against it and nobody in it, and it would be one of the ten. She had got hers by saying yes, and she was not going to be one of the ten by not saying anything.

---

Nobody on that plank said anything at all for about four seconds, and it was the first time in thirteen days that nine people on that deck had all of them been quiet at once. The man of thirty-one was the only one of them who noticed it, and he noticed it because he had been the one who caused it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*because nine people on this deck have just said nine things out loud and about four of us have noticed that none of the nine was about any of the other eight. About nine of us have been standing here eleven days thinking we were something.*”

“*I am standing in front of nine people who have each said why they come up a plank. About four of the nine have said a true thing about money, and about nine of the nine have said a true thing about themselves. I am about to do a thing I have done in four counties, and I would like about four of you to watch me do it.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

---

Then he gave them a word for it, and he gave it to them out loud, and he believed it when he said it.

“*You are a coalition. Nine people, nine reasons, one plank, thirteen days, and nobody in this town can be sent for to ask you what you are, and that is what a coalition is. I have used the word four times in four counties and I have never once been in a room when it was used out loud by the people it was about.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said the man of sixty-one.

“*It is your word,*” said Nevin Trask, “*and it is a good one and it is the wrong one. I would like it said in front of all nine of us that it is the wrong one. I am the man who has been counting heads and carts in this town for eleven years, and I have the arithmetic for it.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the arithmetic,*” said Ilyan.

“*A coalition is nine people who have agreed on a thing. About four of the nine who were standing on this plank an hour ago have not spoken to each other since fair week last year. Two of those four did not know the other three were coming up here at all until the Sunday. The woman of thirty told a man of thirty-one her brother’s name on a Wednesday because she would not say it on a deck in front of nine people, and that is not a thing a coalition does. A coalition says its business in the room.*”

About four people on that deck understood that. About nine did not.

“*And I am not saying you are wrong about the word,*” said Nevin Trask, “*because a word is not a tax and I have spent eleven years taking a penny a head and I know exactly what a word costs. I am saying the nine of you have not agreed on a thing, and you have got up in front of them and given them a word for a thing that has not happened. A word that is in the wrong shape gets said back at you in about four years by somebody who has read it and has not been in the room.*”

---

Then the man of thirty-one said out loud, in front of nine people, that the word was his, and where it had gone, and what it had cost, and nobody in that town said anything to him about it afterwards.

“*I gave that word about four seconds ago and it was mine before it was yours. I would like it said out loud in front of all nine of you that I have said a thing in this county that was not true when I said it. I said it because nine people had said nine true things in about nine minutes and it looked like an answer.*”

Nobody on that plank moved.

“*And I would like the other half of it said out loud too, because I am not going to do this every morning. I have asked nine people one at a time on this deck, in front of the other eight, why they come up here, and every one of them has said a true thing. About nine of those true things are about a thing that two other people on this deck also want and cannot both have.*”

He stopped there and looked down the line of them, and named five of them out loud without stopping between the names.

“*The woman of forty wants the number to be right by Saturday. The man of sixty-one wants the two books kept apart. The woman of forty-one wants the road. The man of sixty-eight wants the quarter mile. The man of fifty-four has had his chair for nine years. I have put nine claims about a thing that binds nobody into the open in one afternoon, and I did not ask anybody whether they were in the same room for the other eight. About nine of them will have it by the fourth hour and about nine of you are standing here knowing it.*”

About four people on that plank said nothing at all.

“*That is the cost of asking nine people one at a time in front of the other eight, and I would like it said that I did it because it was the only way I have ever found of asking anybody anything, and I have now found out what it costs, and it is not the questions. It is the room.*”

---

Nobody in Hask was relieved and nobody in Hask was forgiven and nobody in Hask was thanked.

The fever was forty-seven weeks and one day old. He had been in that county fifty-nine days. Nine people came up a plank in a town of about eleven hundred people on the sixth day of the hundred and ninth week and said out loud why they came. About four of them have a reason about money and about nine of them have a reason about themselves, and not one of the nine reasons was about any of the other eight.

And a man of thirty-one gave them a word for it, and a man of sixty-one told him the word was his and not theirs. He said out loud in front of all nine of them that it was his, and about nine people in that county have a word for a thing that has not happened yet.
