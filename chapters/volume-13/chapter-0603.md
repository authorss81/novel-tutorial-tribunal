# Chapter 603: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Eighth Week, And A Thursday, And On Which Somebody Says Yes In Front Of About Nine People And The Day Goes In A Book And The Whole Of It Binds Nobody, And On Which It Is Said Out Loud That A Lighter Has No Door And No Wall And No Roof And That Anything Said On A Deck Goes To A Bank, And On Which Two Hundred And Ninety-One Is Refused Out Loud And Not Given A Substitute, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Does Not Ask A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Whether She Wants It And Is Told So In About Nine People’s Hearing

Third day of the hundred and eighth week. His three hundred and fifty-third morning. Two hundred and three days after the settlement. The fever forty-five weeks and five days. One hundred and fifty-seven days since the division. One hundred and three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the second day of the hundred and eighth week.

---

A woman of twenty-nine came up the plank at about the eighth hour on the Thursday with a hoop of withies over her arm and a bill in her hand, and about four people on that landing knew her and about nine of the people in that town knew her.

She cut withies above the landing and sold them at the lower end of it, and she had mended the split seat of one of the twenty-nine chairs with a piece of withy gone round and twisted twice, and had done it in her own time and had not been asked.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore, because she says it to everybody. “*Because there is a woman on this deck who mended a chair of mine on the Sunday and there are about nine of us here and about four of you know what a withy costs and about nine of you do not.*”

“*Two pence a hoop of nine, and the withy is mine and the hoop is theirs,*” said Nell Prince. “*And I would rather be asked than waited on.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

---

Then the method was said out loud on the deck in the open, and it was the third of the three ways and it was the only one anybody had, and everybody in that room knew it and nobody had heard it said in nineteen years.

“*Anybody who stands on this deck and says yes in front of about nine people has the chair until somebody says no,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*That is the third way and it is the only one of the three that has a day written against it, because it is the only one where a person says a word out loud that can be written down afterwards.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*I have been on this deck twenty-nine years and I have never once said yes to a chair and I have watched about nine people do it and about nine of those nine were sons, and the two that were not are the only two lines in that book anybody in this town can tell you were not a son.*”

“*Do you want the reed,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*or do you not.*”

“*I am going to say this and then I am going to go and cut it,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*I am thirty-eight and I have cut that reed since I was nine and I am not Oriel Sayer’s son and I have not said yes.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once. About nine did not.

Nobody on that deck found anything to say to that.

---

Then a woman of twenty-nine said yes, and nobody in that room had asked her, and the day went in against a name in a taking column with a pencil out from behind an ear.

“*I will say it and I will say it once,*” said Nell Prince. “*Not because I knew him. I did not know him. I knew the house on the low side and I knew the woman that keeps it and I have bought withies off that land since I was eleven.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*because a person is about to be written into a book for a year and about four of us would like to know why.*”

“*Because there is nobody holding the reed and the cutting of it is nobody’s business, and if nobody holds that chair then whether a man cuts a foot of it or four feet of it is a thing that has to be argued about at a table in about four years when there is a table. I cut withies. I am going to be here in four years. I am twenty-nine and I have not got anybody coming after me in a hurry and that is the only reason I have offered.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Then it goes in,*” said Orla Fennimore, and wrote it, and it took her about as long as it takes to say a name and a day, and there was nothing at the top of the page and there was a day against a name in a column called the taking and no other mark of any kind.

---

Then somebody asked the only question nobody had asked in nineteen years, and the man of thirty-one asked it, and about four people on that deck had already thought of it and about nine had not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*A stranger has been on this deck two days and has just watched a woman of twenty-nine put her name in a book for a year. About nine of us have been sitting here thinking about chairs, and about four of us have been sitting here thinking about who is in the room.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a book with twenty-nine chairs on it, and about eleven hundred people in this town, and I want to know who has not heard what was said on this deck in the last three days.*”

About four people on that deck said something at once.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*and I will tell you now, and I have told you the same thing twice already, which is that there are about nine of us on this deck and about eleven hundred people in this town. Those are the two figures I have. I will not give you a third one out of a number that belongs to another place.*”

“*Why will you not,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because you have had two hundred and ninety-one in your head since you came off that cart, and it is a figure off a market nine miles up the water where about three hundred people stood at a step. It is not a figure about this town and it does not travel. There are about nine of us on a boat. There are about eleven hundred of us in a town. Those are the two numbers I have, and I will not invent a third out of a number that belongs to another place.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*And if I did,*” said Orla Fennimore, “*in about four years there would be a thing in this book about how many people had not heard. A book with a figure like that in it is a book that is about counting instead of about people.*”

About four people on that deck checked that. About nine did not.

“*And say the other half of it,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*which is that a lighter has no door.*”

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore.

“*A weigh-house nine miles off has one door and a street behind it, and about nine people stand under that lamp every morning. Two hundred and seventy of them heard something at a step on a Friday and cannot be asked about it afterwards, and I have thought about that for a day and a half.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*This boat has no door, no wall and no roof,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Anything said on this deck goes to the bank, and the bank is where people wait for a boat. About four of us were on here this morning and about nine were on the bank, and every one of the nine heard a woman’s name said in the open air, and not one of the nine will be able to say afterwards whether they were meant to.*”

“*Then it binds nobody,*” said Ilyan.

“*It binds nobody because there is no form in this county that could make it bind anybody, and not because of where it was said, and those are two different answers and about four of us have been giving you the wrong one,*” said Orla Fennimore.

---

Then a man of thirty-one did the thing he has done in four counties, and it took him about nine seconds, and it was the oldest one he has and it is the one this whole ten days have been about.

He had been in that county forty-nine days. He had put a year of his own future down against a block of limestone nine miles up the water. He had counted from a river every morning since a Thursday in a room over a stable, and about nine people in that hundred had believed the price and about nine had not. About nine of those nine had told it to about nine more.

He wanted a thing that could be checked. He had wanted one for a long time, and this was the first room in four counties where the thing he was standing in front of was a person.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, from the after end, where he had a line across the boat he was not taking up. “*Because a man of thirty-one has been on this deck two days and has asked four questions out of nine and every one of them has been about who is in the room, and about four of us have noticed and about nine have not.*”

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and a book with twenty-nine chairs in it. A woman of twenty-nine has just put her name in it for a year, and I have been in nine counties, and I have never once been in a room where the only thing in it was whether a person had been asked.*”

Nobody on that deck said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore.

And he said: “*Somebody ought to ask her whether she wants it before the day goes in.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say that again and say it out loud,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*Somebody ought to have asked her whether she wanted it, before the day went in against her name.*”

“*You have not asked her,*” said Nell Prince.

Nobody on that deck moved.

“*Say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*You did not ask me, and I knew you would not, and I had my answer ready before you opened your mouth. That is not a thing to be proud of either, because I have been in a room before where a person decided something about me in about four seconds and told me afterwards. It was the best four seconds of that week for them and the worst of it for me. I have said yes, and I would have said yes if you had asked me.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*I would like it written down somewhere, not in your book, that you did not ask,*” said Nell Prince. “*And I would like about four of the people on this deck to have heard that, and about nine of them to have heard it a different way by the fourth hour.*”

Nobody on that deck thanked anybody.

Nobody in that boat was sorry for anybody.

---

The fever was forty-five weeks and five days old. A woman of twenty-nine said yes on a lighter in the open air in front of about nine people, and the day went into a taking column with a pencil and nothing at the top of the page, and no person in the county of Kell is bound by one word of it.

The whole of it binds nobody, and it is not because a lighter has no door, and that was said out loud in about nine people’s hearing on a Thursday morning by a woman of forty-four, and the two hundred and ninety-one was refused in her own mouth with a reason.

And about nine people on that bank heard a name said in the open air, and about nine of them were not asked. A man of thirty-one, who has been in nine counties and has never once been in a room where the only thing in it was whether a person had been asked, said the true of it about four seconds after a woman of twenty-nine had to say it for him. About four of the people on that deck had heard both halves and about nine had not.

And a boat came alongside at about the ninth hour and put her plank across, and about nine people got off her, and every one of the nine had a name in their ear that they had not been given.
