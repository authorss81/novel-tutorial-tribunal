# Chapter 624: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Eleventh Week, And A Thursday, And On Which The Split In A Town Of About Eleven Hundred People Is Described Out Loud In A Front Room On The Low Side Of A Landing By A Woman Of Seventy-One Who Is On Neither Side Of It And Who Says That She Is The Instrument And That The Instrument Is Not A Side, And On Which Both Halves Are Said To Be Right And The Split Is Said Not To Be A Betrayal And Not To Be A Misunderstanding, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Is Nine Feet Off With A Withy Knife For The Second Time In Three Days And Is Told About It Afterwards By Three People Who Each Tell It A Different Way

Third day of the hundred and eleventh week. His three hundred and seventy-fourth morning. Two hundred and twenty-four days after the settlement. The fever forty-eight weeks and five days. One hundred and seventy-eight days since the division. One hundred and twenty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The town sits on a Monday and a Thursday and there is no form that says they may and none that says they must, and the chairs are stood up along the starboard side of the Gull in two rows and nobody has ever been sent for about being in one.

About eleven people came up the plank on the Thursday. Two of them were in chairs and could not be asked whether they had come, and one of the two was the woman of twenty-six who had said no to a column for a reason in about nine seconds on the Wednesday of the tenth week, and she had not stood up since and had said on that Wednesday that she was not going to.

Nobody on that plank thanked anybody for the sitting.

Then the sitting was over in about forty minutes because there was nothing to sit about, and about nine people got off that plank and went up the bank, and about four of them went down the cart road to a house on the low side of the landing, and they were not asked to and they were not sent.

---

The room had a low lintel and a slate nailed to the frame with no name on it, and a fire in it that was not lit, and the door was open because it was the fourth hour of a Thursday and a door is open or it is shut and a house chooses.

About nine people were in that room. A man of thirty-one was not one of them, and he was about two hundred yards off at the head of the plank on his knees in the wet with a hoop of withies in front of him and a withy knife in his hand, because Nell Prince had put the knife back in his hand on the Wednesday morning at about the eighth hour and had again said nothing at all about it.

Nobody on that bank noticed he was not at the sitting. About four people in that room noticed he was not at the sitting, and about four of the nine in that room did not know there had been a sitting.

About four seconds went past in that room and nobody filled them.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Eliza Lark, from the settle, and she was seventy-one and the house was not hers and she had kept it eleven years. “*You have all come down that bank on a Thursday and about four of you have not said why and I have not asked. I am going to describe the thing you are all in and I am going to say it in about nine sentences and then I am going to refuse to do it twice.*”

About four people in that room had that. About nine did not.

“*There is a split in this town about a sheet of deal and about whether copies of it may go where copies of things go. I am going to say both halves and I am going to say that both of them are right, and I am going to say that I have listened to about nine people in three days and about four of them have told me a true thing and about four other people have told me another true thing and neither of the two has cost the other one anything and that is the whole of what a split is.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*The half that wants the copies to keep going says that a record nobody can be sent for about is a lid, and that a person who cannot be checked against is a smaller thing and an uglier thing, and that about nine people in this town have been under a lid for eleven years and know what one weighs. That is true. I have been under one and I know what it weighs and it weighs a house.*”

About four people in that room understood that. About nine did not.

“*The half that is against it says that a thing which can be sent for about is a thing that goes where it goes, and that where it goes it takes the people who were never asked with it. About nine people in four counties are going to be named in rooms they have never been to by people who have never met them. That is true. I have been taken four times in eleven years without a word of it and I know exactly what that is and it is this.*”

Nobody in that room thanked her for eleven years.

“*And the two halves are arguing about me. I would like that said out loud in front of about nine people, because it is the true shape of it and nobody in this room has said it and about four of you have known it since Tuesday. The half that wants the copies to keep going is saying that I should have been written down eleven years ago and that not writing me down was the wrong. The half that is against it is saying that I should never be written down at all and that writing me down is the same thing that was done to me.*”

About four seconds.

“*And I am standing in my own front room and neither of those two people has asked me which one I would rather be, and I am not going to be asked it today. I would like it said out loud that that is not a clever place for a woman of seventy-one to stand, and that I have been in it for eleven years.*”

About four people in that room said nothing at all.

“*And I would like the other half of that said out loud before anything else is said in this room, and it is mine and not anybody’s. I am the instrument. I have been the instrument in this county for eleven years and I have never once been the person, and the instrument is not a side. About four of you have known that since Tuesday and about nine of you have not, and neither of those is a thing I am going to be able to be asked about by anybody in this room.*”

“*It is not a betrayal. There is nobody in this town who has said one false thing to anybody in the last three days and about four people have said things that turned out to be true in a shape nobody wanted. It is not a misunderstanding either, and I would like that said, because about four people have it as a misunderstanding and about four of you have it as a quarrel, and it is neither and both of those are easier.*”

---

Then a man of sixty-one gave the count out loud, on the bank, at about the sixth hour, and he had the arithmetic and about nine people on that bank heard it and about four of them had it a different way by the seventh hour.

“*About nine people in this town want those copies to keep going. About four are against. That is about thirteen with a view on it, and about eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and the difference is that I have counted the ones who have spoken this week and not the ones who have not.*”

About four people on that bank counted that. About nine did not.

“*And I would like it said out loud that I am not telling anybody there are thirteen people. I am telling you there are about nine voices and about four voices, and about four of the nine have not said a word and about four of the four have not said a word, and neither of those two sets of four is a side. It is a figure of what has been said in a week and it is not a figure of what anybody thinks, and I have kept the penny a head for eleven years and I have never once had to know which of the two a number meant.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked him for the count.

---

Then three people told a man of thirty-one about it on the bank, in about eleven minutes, and each of them told it a different way, and about four people in that town heard two versions and about nine heard three.

The boy of eleven said there had been a sitting and that a woman had not been in it and that about nine people had been in a house.

The woman of forty said there were two halves and that the man of thirty-one had written both of them down in nineteen days and that was what had caused them.

And the woman of twenty-six said the true of it, and it was four sentences, and about four people on that bank had not known any of it.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Ilyan.

“*You have been nine feet off with a knife for two days out of three, in a town where about nine people have been describing you to your face in a room you were not in, and about four of us have noticed and about nine have not, and I have been in a chair for nine years and I have watched about nine of the people in that room do it to two other men.*”

About four people on that bank said nothing at all.

“*In five counties that is how a person becomes a thing that gets done in rooms he is not in, and I have said that to you before in a different county and you said you would be careful. Saying that you would be careful is not being careful. Being careful is standing in the room and being wrong in it in front of people.*”

About four people on that bank understood that. About nine did not.

“*And I am not standing up. I said on the Wednesday of the tenth week that I am not going to and I am not going to, and I have been in this chair for eleven days now and I would like it said that a woman of twenty-six who has not stood up is the one who told you this, and that about four people in that room have been on her side since Wednesday and about nine have not.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked her for eleven days.

---

Then a man of thirty-one went down the cart road to the low side at about the seventh hour and stood outside a gate with a low lintel and did not knock, and about four people in that town knew he was standing there and about nine did not. Nobody knocked the door for him either.

He stood about as long as it takes to cut nine feet of a hedge at about two inches at a time.

Then he went back up the bank and put the knife down in the withy heap and did not pick it up again that evening, and a woman of twenty-nine who had given it to him at about the eighth hour on two mornings said nothing about it and cut withies for about another hour.

---

The fever was forty-eight weeks and five days old. He had been in that county seventy days, and a split in a town of about eleven hundred people was described in about nine sentences in a front room on the low side of a landing by a woman of seventy-one who is on neither side of it. Both halves of it are right, and it is not a betrayal and it is not a misunderstanding.

And a man of sixty-one gave the count out loud, which is about nine voices and about four voices and about thirteen with a view and about eleven people who come up a plank in a working week, and he said what it is a figure of, which is what has been said in a week and not what anybody thinks.

And a man of thirty-one stood outside a gate for about the length of nine feet of a hedge and did not knock, and a woman of twenty-six who has been in a chair for nine years and has not stood up told him in four sentences that standing in a room and being wrong in it is the whole of the thing he has been doing for nineteen days, and about four people on that bank had not known any of it.
