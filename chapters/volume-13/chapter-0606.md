# Chapter 606: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Eighth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Eight Sets A Test In Front Of About Nine People On Purpose And Says So Out Loud Before He Sets It, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Answers A Question About A River Nine Miles Off In About Four Seconds Without Looking At Anything, And On Which It Is Said Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing That It Is Not A Power And That It Cannot Be Shown Off Without Becoming A Thing A Town Can Put In A Book, And On Which A Man Who Counts From A Water He Cannot Stop Counting From Loses A Thing That Is Happening Four Feet In Front Of Him, And On Which A Notch Is Cut In A Post At The Top Of A Landing

Sixth day of the hundred and eighth week. His three hundred and fifty-sixth morning. Two hundred and six days after the settlement. The fever forty-six weeks and one day. One hundred and sixty days since the division. One hundred and six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the fifth day of the hundred and eighth week.

---

The Gull was brought up onto the gravel above the landing on the Sunday because the water had fallen about a foot since Thursday and a lighter draws eleven inches at the head and thirty at the foot and will do what it likes at the after end.

There is a post at the top of that landing, a green oak post about four feet out of the ground. Every man who uses this landing cuts a notch in it when the water is at a certain thing, and the notches run up one side and back down the other. They are about eleven years of a town telling itself where its own river is.

Nobody on that gravel thanked anybody for the six days.

---

Then a man of thirty-eight set a test, and he said so out loud before he set it, which is a thing that has happened in that town about twice.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley, with the axe in his hand and the post behind him. “*Because it is about the ninth hour on a Sunday and there are about nine of us on this gravel, and I am about to ask a man of thirty-one a question. I am going to say before I ask it that I am testing him, and I have thought about that for two days, and I am going to do it anyway. About four of you have known I would and about nine have not.*”

Nobody on that gravel said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*A woman of twenty-nine has spent four days in this town putting her name in a book, and about nine of us have been watching what she does next. A man of thirty-eight with an axe has just told about nine people he is going to use a man of thirty-one as a thing to try.*”

“*I am going to ask him what the water is doing at a block of limestone nine miles up this river, and I am going to ask him now. If he answers, then about nine of you are going to find out something about him that I have known since Wednesday, and I would rather it came out of my mouth than out of his.*”

---

Then he asked it, and the man of thirty-one answered in about four seconds and did not look at anything.

“*Say the rest of that and say it plain,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*At a thumbnail’s width under a cut round the top of it, and standing, and it has been standing since before the light on the Friday, and it has not been over the cut since it came to it.*”

About four people on that gravel said something at once. About nine did not.

“*Say what a thumbnail’s width is about,*” said a man of about sixty-one who had not been on that landing all week.

“*About half an inch. It is the width of the nail of a thumb of a hand of a man of thirty-one and not the nail of the man of thirty-eight, and that is the whole of the difference, and it is about a quarter of an inch. In this river a quarter of an inch at that cut is about a day of falling.*”

About four people on that gravel checked that against the water they could see. About nine did not.

“*Then say how you know,*” said Garrin Tolley, “*and I am asking you that as a man who cuts notches in a post and not as a man who is testing you, and if you say you looked I will put the axe down and never ask you anything again.*”

Nobody on that gravel moved.

“*I have been counting from a water since a Thursday morning nine weeks ago in a room over a stable, and I did not choose it and I cannot stop it, and every morning in between I know where that water is without being asked and without looking. It is not that I can see it. It is that I cannot not have it, and it is the same knowing at about the fourth hour this morning that it was on Friday, and it is a knowing of one water and not of any other, and there is no figure for it and nobody can check it.*”

About four seconds went past.

---

Then it was said out loud in about nine people’s hearing what it was, and it was said by two people and they did not say it the same way, and about nine of the people on that gravel have it one way and about nine have it another.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*A man of thirty-one has just told nine people that he has a knowing he did not ask for and cannot put down. About four of us understood what he said and about nine of us did not, and I have had a book for nineteen years, and I am going to say the thing that a book does about a thing like that.*”

Nobody on that gravel said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley, putting the axe down.

“*A thing that can be asked about goes in a book,*” said Orla Fennimore. “*Not because anybody wants it there. Because that is what a book is for. And a man who can be asked about a water nine miles off in about four seconds without looking is a man this town will be asked about. The asking will not stop at the water. In about four years there will be a line in a book with his name at the top of it and a day against it, and nobody will remember which of us put it there.*”

“*Then say what else it is,*” said Nell Prince, who had come up the gravel with a hoop of withies over her arm. “*Say the other half of it, because about four of us have heard one half and about nine have heard one half, and the half Orla has said is the half that is about this town, and there is another half and it is worse than that one.*”

Nobody on that gravel moved.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Garrin Tolley.

“*It is not a power and anybody in this town who says it is a power is wrong about it in a way that will cost somebody. A man who can be asked about a water cannot be asked about anything else. He is one water. I have been on this landing five days and I have been in rooms in four counties and in every one of them the man was the carrying and now he is the knowing, and it is the same man, and a carrying can be put down and a knowing cannot.*”

“*Then say where that is in this morning,*” said Ilyan.

“*It is in the four feet in front of you,*” said Nell Prince. “*There is a lighter drawing her after end in about two inches a minute, and you have not seen it, and neither have I. You have been asked about a river nine miles off and you answered it in four seconds. I have been on this gravel for a quarter of an hour, and I have watched you lose a thing that is happening four feet in front of you the whole while, and you did not know you were losing it. I do not think you can be told when it is happening.*”

About four people on that gravel looked at the after end of the Gull and about nine had not.

---

Then the man of thirty-eight cut the notch, and it was the last thing that happened on that gravel on the Sunday, and it was a thing about a foot of falling and not about any of the other four days.

He cut it on the up side of the run, which is the side for a falling water. He cut it about two inches deep and about a foot along, in about four strokes, and he did not measure it, because about nine years of notches on that post are the only measure anybody in this town has for it.

He stood back about nine feet and looked at it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garrin Tolley. “*Because I have cut a notch in a post that has had about eleven years of them, and a man of thirty-one has told me on this gravel that he knew on the Friday what that notch was going to say. Both of those are true, and about four of us here know that neither of them helps anybody get a lighter off a gravel bank.*”

Nobody on that gravel thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Orla Fennimore.

“*My post will say it in a week and be right, and he was right on Friday, and I would rather have the post, because a post is here on the Tuesday and he is not, and that is not a thing I am saying to be unkind. I am saying it because about four of you have just heard a thing that could be very useful and about nine of you have heard a thing that is going to be a nuisance, and I would like the nine of you to raise a hand.*”

Nobody on that gravel raised a hand.

The fever was forty-six weeks and one day old. A man of thirty-eight set a test on a Sunday morning and said out loud that he was setting it. A man of thirty-one answered in about four seconds a question about a cut in a block of limestone nine miles up the water and did not look at anything. Two women said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that it was not a power, and that it could not be shown off without becoming a thing a town could put in a book.

And a man of thirty-one lost a lighter drawing her after end in about two inches a minute for about a quarter of an hour, four feet in front of him, without knowing that he was losing it, and could not be told when it was happening.

And a notch was cut in a green oak post at the top of a landing, about two inches deep and about a foot along, on the up side of the run, and it was right, and so was the man, and about nine people on that gravel know which of the two they would rather had for the Tuesday.
