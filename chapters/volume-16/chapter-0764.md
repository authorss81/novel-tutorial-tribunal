# Chapter 764: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Thursday, And A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Says Three Words From A Reed About Twenty Feet Off The Edge Of That Drain, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says She Has Not Been Asked About Anything For Three Weeks And Has Not Asked

Third day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and fourteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-four days after the settlement. The fever sixty-eight weeks and five days. Three hundred and eighteen days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and he gave it the other way round from the morning before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has never once given anybody a fourth thing out of the three he gives. Eight mornings ago he said the whole of the day-names out loud in front of about nine people and then took back a figure he had given for why the cycles run eight days apart.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is two days of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day. I gave that figure on Friday and on Monday and I am giving it again because it does not change and I do.*”

“*The bank will be full on the Saturday of this week, which is two days from this morning.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in six days, and I worked that out yesterday and said it the other way round and about four of you have told me I had it wrong.*”

“*I have not had it wrong. I said it in the order I thought of it and I am not going to go back and tidy it up for about nine of you.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the forty-third morning, and about four of them noticed that he asked for it out loud this morning before anybody else said anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one, and he has asked a man of thirty-eight to read it and been refused nothing.

“*Forty-three mornings.*”

“*Forty-three is one more than forty-two and I am saying the working because you have never once heard me not say it.*”

“*I asked out loud this morning. I have never done that. I have waited about six weeks for somebody else to ask and this morning I asked first, and about four of you heard it and about nine of you were standing somewhere else.*”

“*That is not brave and I am not going to be told it is and I am not going to be thanked for it either.*”

“*The fifth thing is still not answered. Nobody has asked me about it.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine said three words from a reed about a hundred yards along the edge of that drain, and the reed is about twenty feet off that edge, and nobody repeated them back to her.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in that reed a hundred and thirty-five days, a hundred and thirty-five being seven hundred and sixty-four less six hundred and twenty-nine. She said one word out of that reed on the Saturday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and it was no, and on the Saturday of last week she said two words and they were that a man of about fifty-two was right.

“*Ask a clerk.*”

That is the whole of it. Three words. Nobody repeated them back to her and nobody asked her for a fourth and nobody asked her what a clerk is.

Then a woman of sixty-four said the one thing she has been not saying, and it is not the reason, and she said out loud that it was not going to be.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She was in that room on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week and was asked whether she could attend and was told she could not. She has refused to say why and she has asked not to be asked twice, and nobody on that bank has asked her.

“*A boy of thirteen used a day I gave on that bank on the Monday of a fortnight. I am not going to say whether he got it right and I am not going to say which of two days I think it was.*”

“*I have been asked nothing about that room for about three weeks and I want it said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that I have been asked nothing, and I want it said by me and not by about four of you.*”

“*And about four of you have been keeping off me. I have noticed it and I have not made anything of it and I am not going to make anything of it this morning either.*”

“*I asked not to be asked twice. That is not the same as not asking for anything and I am not going to let the six of you hear it as the same thing.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the three weeks and nobody is going to be told I was brave about saying this out loud.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she wants out loud for the first time in eleven years, and it is not a name, and she refused the job of getting it.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name. She has never once asked for a reason instead, and she has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. On Thursday of this week she said out loud, for the first time in eleven years, that she wants a day with nothing against it.

“*Here is what I want and I have wanted it since Friday and I did not have the words for it until a boy of thirteen said nine days out loud.*”

“*I want a day with nothing against it.*”

“*Not a name. Not a house. Not a cart. Nothing on it at all, and a person standing on it and nobody able to say what they are standing in front of.*”

“*I have asked for eleven years for a thing with my name on it against a day, and I have never once been asked what I would do if a day turned up with nothing on it.*”

“*And I am not going to be the person who goes and finds out which of two days a man of sixty-one will not write down. I ask for days against names. There is no name on either of those two days and if I go and get one I will have taken the last thing on that counter that anybody could still be asked about.*”

“*I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them. Nobody is going to be thanked for the want and nobody is going to be told it was the same want.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line because a man of fifty-four asked for it out loud first, and gave his count, and said something about a piece of chalk.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has a board in the inside of his coat that he has carried since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week, with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week.

“*Fifty-four is seven hundred and sixty-four less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*Yesterday a man of thirty-one asked about nine of you to hold a question for about a minute before anybody answered it.*”

“*You held it for about a minute. Then a boy of thirteen answered it on his fingers with a piece of chalk and he was thirteen, and I do not say that to put him in the wrong of it, because he was the only one of us with something to count with.*”

“*I have been asked to read the ninth line and I have read it. Nobody has asked me for a piece of chalk and I have not got one.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, and refused a fourth thing about it for the fourth morning running, and said how long she intends to go on refusing.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and nobody asked her to, and she has seen four forms in nineteen years and knows what one looks like.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter fourteen mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And I am not going to give the fourth thing. I have refused it four mornings running now and I have said out loud each time that about four of you were waiting for it.*”

“*I have not said how long I am going to keep refusing and I am not going to say that this morning either, because if I say a day I will have given about four of you a thing to count, and that is the one job on this bank nobody does twice.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and what he got wrong and what it has cost him, and the mistake this morning is about a minute of holding a question, which is the smallest thing he has ever got wrong.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and ten days, two hundred and ten being seven hundred and sixty-four less five hundred and fifty-four. He has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether the cut across his right palm will keep.

“*I am giving the want first because it is the smallest thing I have.*”

“*I want about nine of you to let a woman of forty go and get one of those two days, and I am not going to ask her to.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is about a minute long, which is the smallest mistake I have made in two hundred and ten days.*”

“*Yesterday I asked about nine of you to hold a question for about a minute before anybody answered it. About nine of you held it for about a minute, and then a boy of thirteen answered it, and he was thirteen.*”

“*I did not hold it. I watched about nine of you hold it. That is the whole of my part in it and I have been putting it in my own mouth for a day as though I had asked for something.*”

“*Sixty-four days. Sixty-four is seven hundred and sixty-four less seven hundred. That is what I have given every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to a woman of forty who wants a day with nothing on it.*”

“*And the cost is that she wants a day with nothing on it and I cannot give her one, and about four of you heard me say yesterday that I wanted a question held, and about nine of you have now seen what a question is worth when it is held by about nine people who are all waiting to be asked.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with a reed about twenty feet off the edge of that drain and a piece of chalk that a boy of thirteen put back in his pocket.

---

The fever was sixty-eight weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and ten days.

A woman of twenty-nine said three words from a reed about a hundred yards away, and they were that somebody should ask a clerk, and nobody repeated them back to her and nobody asked her for a fourth.

And a woman of sixty-four said she has been asked nothing about that room for about three weeks and asked for it to be said out loud in about nine people’s hearing that she has been asked nothing, and said by her and not by about four of them.

And a woman of forty said she wants a day with nothing against it, not a name and not a house and not a cart, and said she is not going to be the person who goes and finds out which of two days a man of sixty-one will not write down.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted about nine of them to hold a question for about a minute, and said that he watched them hold it and a boy of thirteen answered it, and that he has been putting it in his own mouth for a day as though he had asked for something.
