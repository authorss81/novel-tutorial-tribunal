# Chapter 794: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fifth Week, And A Saturday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Sixteenth Time, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Says The One Thing He Has Not Said In Eleven Years, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Stands At The Up-Water End Of That Strip And Is Not Asked Why

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. His five hundred and forty-fourth morning. Three hundred and ninety-four days after the settlement. The fever seventy-three weeks old. Three hundred and forty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off all ninety of those steps in the night and the man of forty-three was on that landing before light and called the figure up the cut.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and a fifth this one, because it is the morning about nine of them have been waiting two weeks for.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour this morning.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working and I have given it on every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And I can give you the whole of it in one line this morning. I do not have a way of saying the day it comes off until I have said the day before it.*”

“*That is the sixteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it is a Saturday, and I said on Friday of last week that it would be a Saturday, and about four of you wrote Friday down on Tuesday.*”

“*And those ninety steps can be walked this morning, and they have not been walkable since the Friday of the week before last, and I am not going to be thanked for a thing I have said two hundred times.*”

Then a man of fifty-four was taken down those steps and had the ninth line read to him at the bottom of them, which nobody has done before, and he asked out loud at the top of the cut first.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He was taken down ninety cut steps to have it read to him at the bottom of them this morning.

“*I have been asked to be taken down to that landing to have that line read to me at the bottom of the steps, and I said yes, and about four of you said it was not necessary and I have heard all of you.*”

“*It is not necessary. That is not why I said yes. I said yes because for seventy-three mornings I have been asking for that line to be read to me in the open and open is a word and I have been taking the top of a cut for it.*”

“*The bottom of ninety cut steps is where that line has been read from since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and I have never once been at the same height as the paper.*”

“*Seventy-three mornings, and I went down to be read to on the seventy-fourth, and I want it said that nobody made me and I would have gone down in the water for it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read it at the bottom of the cut and gave the count from the bottom of the cut and said that the number of names is four and he is not going up there to say it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and there is a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He read at the bottom of that cut this morning because a man of fifty-four asked to be where the paper is.

“*Four names, and it is the sixteenth morning, and I said it yesterday from the top of that cut and I am saying it now from the bottom of it, and about four of you heard both and I am not going to give you the reason twice.*”

“*Eighty-four is seven hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred and ten, and I read the ninth, and I read it down here because a man of fifty-four asked to be where the paper is.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety-six days, and ninety-six is seven hundred and ninety-four less six hundred and ninety-eight, and a boy of thirteen knows that figure and has never said it to me.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the one thing in those four leaves that he has not said out loud in about eleven years, and he said it at the bottom of ninety cut steps with the water going off above him.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works, and four lines sit under him and any one of the four can be looked at to see whether he has read the fifth wrong. He has said out loud on sixteen mornings of the last seventeen that he has not said it.

“*I have said out loud that I have not said it every morning for about two weeks and I am going to stop saying that this morning, because saying it every day is a way of not saying it and about four of you worked that out on Tuesday.*”

“*The thing on that leaf that is mine is a mark. Not a word. A mark a pen makes when the person who wrote it did not know what else to put in that line.*”

“*Eleven years ago there were nine of us in that room and eight of them wrote words and I put a mark, and I have not said that out loud in eleven years, and the mark is on the fourth line of a leaf that has no room on it and no names on it.*”

“*And it is not evidence of anything. I have been waiting about two weeks to find out whether it is evidence of anything and it is not, and I would have found that out a fortnight ago if I had said it.*”

“*Here is the whole of what I have got, and it is one sentence, and I have been carrying it since a Wednesday eleven years ago.*”

“*I was in that room, and I put a mark, because I was the only one of the nine who was not asked what the line was for.”*

“*Nobody has been cruel to me about that in eleven years. I want that said, because I have heard what this week has been and I am not part of it and I am not going to be folded into it.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said she was going to stand at the up-water end of that strip of bank, and was not going to be asked why, and a woman of forty-four said she would not be the one who held it.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them, and she has a name in ink on the ninth line and has never had a day against it. She walked out of that strip on the Monday of last week and nobody followed her.

“*I am going to stand at the up-water end of that strip this morning while the water is off, and I am not going to be asked why, and I have said before that I would not say why I could not attend and I am not saying it now either.*”

“*A woman of forty-four stood at that end for an hour and a half on the Monday and I am not asking her to do it again and I am not asking anybody to do it.*”

“*I am sixty-four and I stood at the end of a strip of a bank for about a quarter of an hour this morning, with ninety steps of that cut dry in front of me.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be told I was brave about it.*”

“*A man of fifty-four said that exact sentence to a man of thirty-one on a Friday, and I heard it, and I agree with it.*”

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began her count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and ninety of those mornings were before anybody said a word to her about any of them.

“*I am not holding the other end of that strip this morning and I have said I would not be the one who holds things, and a woman of sixty-four is standing at that end on her own and that is her doing and not mine.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter fifty mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter forty-four mornings with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*And three: eighty-nine mornings of this bank, and eighty-nine is seven hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred and five.*”

Then a man of thirty-one went down those ninety cut steps with the water off and stood at the ninth opening again and did not open it, and came back up, and gave the want and the mistake and the cost.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and forty days, two hundred and forty being seven hundred and ninety-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and nine days old, a hundred and nine being seven hundred and ninety-four less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked three times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said.

“*I went down again this morning with ninety of those steps dry, and I stood at that ninth opening, and I did not open it.*”

“*I am not allowed to until the final hearing, and that hearing is four hundred miles off, and I have no day for it.*”

“*And I am not going to stand at the bottom of that cut this morning and be relieved of it. I have said that once and I am not going to say it again in a week.*”

“*The want has not changed. I want somebody to be asked who can say no to me and come back the next morning and be asked again.*”

“*The mistake is the tenth of the same mistake and it is one day old. I stood at a ninth opening this morning with my hand about four inches from it and about nine of you were on that bank, and I had not asked one of you to be there, and I have been not asking people for a fortnight now and I have noticed that it is the same thing as not asking them to be somewhere.*”

“*Ninety-four days. Ninety-four is seven hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*The cost is that a man of about fifty-two said a thing out loud this morning that he has carried for eleven years.*”

“*A man of fifty-four went down ninety cut steps to be read to at the bottom of them.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four stood at the end of a strip of bank on her own, and about four of you were on that bank, and all three of those things happened because I have been standing on it for ten days.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for any of it and none of those three people are going to be thanked for it and I have said that about all of them in front of about nine of you before I did it.*”

About nine of them were on that bank and ninety of those cut steps were dry at about the eighth hour of a Saturday, and four of them were still at the bottom of the cut at about the eleventh hour.

---

The fever was seventy-three weeks old, exactly, and he had been in that county two hundred and forty days.

A man of fifty-four went down ninety cut steps to have one line read to him at the bottom of them, and said nobody made him.

A man of about fifty-two said out loud, for the first time in eleven years, that he put a mark on that leaf because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for.

A woman of sixty-four stood at the up-water end of a strip of bank on her own, and would not say why, and asked to be thanked for nothing.
