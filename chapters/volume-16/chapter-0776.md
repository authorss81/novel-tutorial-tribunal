# Chapter 776: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Tuesday, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Says Out Loud The One Thing She Said In That Room Four Hundred Miles Off, Which Was About A Road, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Comes Down That Road And Says Where A Kitchen Is

First day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and twenty-sixth morning. Three hundred and seventy-six days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and three days. Three hundred and thirty days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the third morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

He said that he had worked out the first five of the thirteen this morning, and was giving the figure, and then saying he did not believe it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and no fourth. He has said for about three weeks running that he is not going to work out the first five of the thirteen in front of anybody because he has not found out.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the third morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off. Four inches a day is the working underneath it and I have given that on every morning of this bank being full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in two days from this morning, which is the Thursday of this week.*”

“*That will be the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-fifth morning. He asked out loud first, and the man of thirty-eight read it, and neither of them mentioned that it is a line with no day on it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and cannot read and holds the gravel on that landing. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not missed one. He has refused to be asked about the fifth thing out loud since Monday. A boy of thirteen asked on Sunday for somebody to ask him about it.

“*Fifty-five mornings.*”

“*Fifty-five is one more than fifty-four.*”

“*I have said I would say nothing about the fifth thing until somebody asked. I am not saying anything about it and a boy of thirteen has said out loud on that bank that somebody should, and about four of you have been looking at him since.*”

“*And the line has no day on it, and a man of about fifty-two said so on Friday.*”

“*I have had it read to me fifty-five times and I did not know until Friday, and I am not going to say one word about that either.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and the day and gave the count of that sheet.

He said that a woman of thirty-four has been standing at the top of about two hundred yards of that bank since before the seventh hour.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has given a different reason every morning this week for not opening a coat. He said on Saturday that he has stopped being able to tell which of his own reasons is true.

“*Sixty-six is seven hundred and seventy-six less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read that line and that day every morning and I have stopped saying that they are two jobs.*”

“*A woman of thirty-four is at the top of that bank and has been there since before I came up. About four of you have seen her and about nine of you have not, and I am not going to say which of the two of those is a thing about me.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it seventy-eight days and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one read a figure of mine out loud on Saturday morning in front of about nine of you, and I did not ask him to, and I am not going to thank him for it.*”

Then a woman of thirty-four said the one thing she came two days to say, and it is the second half of it, and she said why she had not said it before.

Brenna Dole is thirty-four and has walked about two days from a place four hundred miles off that landing. She is one of the nine who were heard about. Six of them were in that room and three of them came in at the end and did not sit down. Nobody asked her anything on that day and she said one thing and it is not on those four leaves. She came up about two hundred yards of that bank by herself on the Sunday of a week ago and said she did not know the days were being counted.

“*I said on the Sunday of a week ago that I did not know the days were being counted, and that is the first half of it.*”

“*The second half is what I said in that room, and I have not said it in this county in about three weeks.*”

“*I am going to say it now and then I am going back down that bank.*”

“*What I said in that room was about a road.*”

“*It was about a cart road with houses up it, and a day those houses can come down on, and a second cart that does not come the whole way down.*”

“*And there is no cart road in this county like that one.*”

“*There is one about four miles up that road from this landing with about four houses on it, and a man of sixty-one was stood at a gate on it on the Sunday, and a woman of forty-four has just come down it.*”

“*That is my sentence, and I said it in that room on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week.*”

“*It is not on those four leaves, and the four leaves say that nine people were heard about that line and did not object.*”

“*I was in the room. I am one of the nine. And I did not object to anything, and that is true, and I said a thing and it is not on the paper, and both of those are true this morning.*”

“*Nobody asked me anything in that room, and I am not saying that so that it sounds like a complaint.*”

“*I am saying it because a man of about fifty-two has told you there is no day on that line, and no room on it, and no name on it either, and the one thing I said in that room about that line is not on any of it.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it here, and I am not going to be asked one question about it, and I am going back down about two hundred yards of that bank and I am not going up it again.*”

Then a woman of forty-four who keeps a bakehouse came down four miles of that cart road on the bread cart.

She had not come down it since the Friday of a week and a half ago, and she said the thing about the kitchen.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps the bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses go up to it for bread. She came down on the Friday of a week and a half ago on the bread cart and gave a figure of fifteen or fourteen off her own bread, and named a low gate three miles and six hundred yards up that road.

She went back up it.

“*I came down on the bread because the bread comes down on a Friday and I am on the bread cart.*”

“*That is the whole of the reason, and I gave it first the last time as well.*”

“*A man of sixty-one walked down that road on the Sunday and got to this bank after the seventh hour.*”

“*I have been thinking about him all the way here and I am not going to say what about.*”

“*Here is the thing I have been up that road to find out and I did not go up to find it out, I went up because I go up, and I found it out by being asked for bread.*”

“*There is a kitchen about six hundred yards up that cart road from that low gate, and about four miles and two hundred yards up it from this landing.*”

“*I have been in it for nineteen years and never once said where it was.*”

“*There is a woman in that kitchen who has been keeping the days her second cart comes down in her own head since the Friday of last week, when a man of sixty-one told her that nobody had said a day.*”

“*She has told nobody. She told me on Saturday when I went up for the bread.*”

“*And she is not on that line. She is not on any line in this county and nobody four hundred miles off has ever heard about her, and she is keeping a day in her head that nobody has said.*”

“*A cart cannot turn on a day nobody has said. I said that the first time I came down that road and I am saying it again because it has turned out to be the whole of it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the name of a kitchen and I am not going to be told I was brave about four miles.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, and said the third count, which is a figure of mornings and which has moved.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted sixty-eight mornings on Saturday with a pencil and a crumb of bread.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-two mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-six mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three, and it has moved since Saturday. Seventy-one mornings, and seventy-one is seven hundred and seventy-six less seven hundred and five.*”

“*A woman of thirty-four has just said out loud the one thing she said in that room, and a woman of forty-four has just named a kitchen.*”

“*And on the Monday of a week I said I wanted somebody to notice that I had not said what to do about the sixty-eight, and I still have not.*”

“*I have not said what to do about them. I have said two things happened, and a count, and I keep chairs.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that a woman of thirty-four has just done the only thing that would have worked on that bank, and said what it cost her, and said what it costs him.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-two days, two hundred and twenty-two being seven hundred and seventy-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-one days old, ninety-one being seven hundred and seventy-six less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice that nobody say.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and it is about a woman of thirty-four and not about me.*”

“*I want her sentence to be the first of the sixty-eight that anybody has ever asked about.*”

“*She said a thing out loud in front of about nine of us, and that is the only thing this bank has ever produced.*”

“*The reason it is the only thing is that she was in a room where saying it was not asked for.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is six days old and it is the fifth time in six days.*”

“*I have wanted a person to be the one who went for about eleven days. A woman of thirty-four went about three weeks ago. I did not send for her, and she has not been sent for by anybody.*”

“*And she has just said the one thing she went to say, on a bank nine feet from where a man of sixty-one is standing.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me on a Wednesday of a fortnight that the order of two things is the whole of what I am, and I have been getting it wrong for about eleven days.*”

“*And this morning it is worse than wrong, because the person who went was one of the nine, and I had counted her outside the four since the Friday of a week.*”

“*Seventy-six days. Seventy-six is seven hundred and seventy-six less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to a woman going down a bank.*”

“*And the cost is that about nine of you are going to hold what she said on this bank now.*”

“*She walked two days to say one sentence and she has said it, and I have not got there in two hundred and twenty-two days on the same bank.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with a full water behind them and a woman of thirty-four at the top of about two hundred yards of it saying she was not going up it again.

---

The fever was seventy weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-two days.

A woman of thirty-four said out loud the one thing she said in that room four hundred miles off.

It was about a road with houses on it, a day those houses can come down on, and a second cart that does not come the whole way down.

She said it is not on those four leaves, and that the four leaves say nine people were heard about that line and did not object, and said she is one of the nine.

And a woman of forty-four who keeps a bakehouse came down four miles of that cart road and named a kitchen about six hundred yards above that low gate.

There is a woman in it keeping the days her second cart comes down in her own head, and she is not on any line.

And a woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs gave the two counts off that counter and gave the third one, which was seventy-one mornings, and said she has said two things happened and a count, and that she still has not said what to do about any of them.
