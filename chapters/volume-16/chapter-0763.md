# Chapter 763: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Wednesday, And A Boy Of Thirteen Does A Subtraction Out Loud On A Bank And Gets Two Answers A Day Apart And Gives Both Of Them

Second day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and thirteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-three days after the settlement. The fever sixty-eight weeks and four days. Three hundred and seventeen days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the bank will be full on the Saturday of this week and nothing else.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gives three things every morning out of three he has never added to. He said five times in a row that he had nothing to say about a night and then stopped saying it.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is one day of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working and I gave you that figure on Friday and it has not changed.*”

“*The bank will be full on the Saturday of this week, and the next time those ninety steps are off is seven days from this morning.*”

“*I am not going to say the day of the week that seven days lands on. About four of you worked it out on the way up here and about nine of you did not, and neither of those is a thing I am going to check for you.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the forty-second morning, and the man of thirty-eight read it because he was asked and said so out loud before he read it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one, and he has stopped saying that he is going to stop putting a figure on how long he has waited.

“*Forty-two mornings.*”

“*Forty-two is one more than forty-one.*”

“*I have not said anything about stopping this morning because I have stopped saying it, and that is not the same thing as stopping.*”

“*The fifth thing is still not answered. Nobody has asked me and I would rather they did not, and about four of you have started counting the waiting again and I can hear you doing it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-two and nobody is going to be told I was brave about any of it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen was on that bank with his own count, which he gives every morning he is on it, and this morning he asked for a piece of chalk.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and ten days ago and keeps his own count of it, and he has carried five counts on this bank and told about nine of them that he was going to carry six, so that about four of them could not say afterwards that they did not know.

“*A hundred and ten days. A hundred and ten is seven hundred and sixty-three less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*I have carried five and I told you on the Wednesday of a fortnight that I was going to carry six, and I have not carried six and I am not going to, because a figure that is not a day-count is not mine to carry.*”

“*I want a piece of chalk because a man of about fifty-two asked yesterday where a figure of nine days starts counting from and nobody has answered him.*”

“*The second line of that thing says the day it was held. A woman of sixty-four said out loud on the Monday of a fortnight that she was in that room on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I have that day.*”

“*I am going to count nine days out loud from that day and I am going to do it twice and I am going to give you both.*”

“*Counting the day it was held as the first of them, the ninth day is the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.*”

“*Counting from the day after, the ninth day is the Friday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.*”

“*That is one day apart and I have done it twice on my fingers and both are right.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about which of the two it is, so I am not going to sit here and look like I know which one it is, and about four of you are going to want me to say one of them out loud so that the number stops moving.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said he was not going to write either of them down, and gave the reason, and it was the third reason he has given this fortnight and he said so himself.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one. He has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years, one of them six and written on the Sunday of last week.

There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since.

“*A boy of thirteen has just given me two days that are one day apart and both of them are right.*”

“*I keep heads and carts. A day is neither a head nor a cart and it is not going in that book because a boy of thirteen counted it out loud on a bank.*”

“*And if I write one of the two down then in about half a year somebody in this county will say that is the day, and I will not be able to say it is not, because a man of sixty-one wrote it eleven years after he stopped writing anything.*”

“*That is the third reason I have given in a fortnight about a thing on that counter. I am not going to give a fourth one and nobody on this bank is going to ask me for one.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read that line and gave the count of that sheet in his own mouth with the working, and said he would not read anything else this morning because nobody asked him to.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He read that sheet forty times in this county and stopped on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and a boy of thirteen counted two days a day apart on that same morning.

“*Fifty-three is seven hundred and sixty-three less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*I read the ninth line because I was asked and I read it twice because I did not get it right the first time, and about four of you heard the second one and about nine of you heard the first.*”

“*A boy of thirteen has just said two things out loud that are a day apart, and I have been the fifth line of a thing for eleven years, and a fifth line that cannot say which day it is is not a thing anybody can check me against.*”

“*I am not going to be the one who says which of the two it is.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she was standing in front of, and gave the count of the two things on that counter, and did not say which three of five things have a day against them.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name. She has never once asked for a reason instead, and she has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. A boy of thirteen gave two days a day apart on this bank this morning and she has not said which of the two she believes.

“*Tell me what you are standing in front of.*”

“*A boy of thirteen with two days in his hands, and two things on a counter, and one place at the bottom of that bank where nobody has stood for two mornings.*”

“*I have not added any of those three together and I am not going to.*”

“*And about nine days against nothing is now a day against nothing, which is a thing I have been standing in front of since Friday and did not have the name for.*”

“*I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them and nobody is going to be thanked for the naming of a thing I did not name on Friday.*”

Then a man of thirty-one wanted to be the one who said the two days out loud, and gave it up, and said what the giving up was and what it was not.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and nine days, two hundred and nine being seven hundred and sixty-three less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is seventy-eight days old, seventy-eight being seven hundred and sixty-three less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want, and it is a small one, and I am putting it first because a woman of twenty-eight told me the order is the whole of what I am.*”

“*I wanted to be the man who said those two days out loud this morning.*”

“*And a boy of thirteen said them, and he had a piece of chalk and he counted on his fingers, and I have stood on this bank for two hundred and nine days and had the day in my head since Friday morning and did not count.*”

“*And I am not going to come in now and say that I would have got it right, because about four of you would take that as a thing about me, and it is a thing about a boy of thirteen and a piece of chalk.*”

“*Sixty-three days. Sixty-three is seven hundred and sixty-three less seven hundred. That is what I have given every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And here is the cost and it is that I have been the thing about four people come up to for two hundred and nine days, and this morning the only thing that got said on this bank about that finding was said by a person of thirteen, and I am not going to be the one who puts a sentence in front of that either.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with a boy of thirteen holding two days that were a day apart and about four of them wanting him to say which one it was.

Nobody asked him twice.

---

The fever was sixty-eight weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and nine days.

A boy of thirteen counted nine days out loud twice from the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week and got the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week one way and the Friday of it the other, and gave both, and said nobody in this county can be sent for about which of the two it is.

And a man of sixty-one said he was not going to write either of them down, and said that if he wrote one of the two then in about half a year somebody would say that is the day and he would not be able to say it is not.

And a man of thirty-one said he had wanted to be the one who said it out loud, and gave it up to a boy of thirteen with a piece of chalk, and said that about four of them wanted the boy told twice which of the two days it was and nobody asked him twice.
