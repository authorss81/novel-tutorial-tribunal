# Chapter 780: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Fever Was Seventy-One Weeks Old, And A Woman Of Twenty-Nine Says Five Words Out Of A Reed

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and thirtieth morning. Three hundred and eighty days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks old. Three hundred and thirty-four days since the division. Two hundred and eighty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

He said it was the last morning of a week for him and not for the water.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He gave the whole day-name sequence out loud on the Thursday of this week and said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of the thirteen, and he has not said it since.

“*Eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is two days of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and the bank will be full again on the Monday of this week, and that is two days from this morning.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in six days from this morning, which is the Friday of next week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*That is three things and I am going to stop, and I have said I am going to stop on about nine hundred mornings and I have stopped about nine hundred times.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-ninth morning. He asked out loud first, and the man of thirty-eight said the number of names before he read anything else, because he said on Friday that he would.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has asked nothing of anybody on that bank for about eight weeks and has said every morning that that is a notice and not a want.

“*Fifty-nine mornings.*”

“*Fifty-nine is one more than fifty-eight, and that is the working, and it is the last morning of a week and about four of you will write sixty down tonight and about nine of you will not.*”

“*The fifth thing was asked on the Wednesday of this week and I answered it, and I am not going to be asked about the answer.*”

“*About four of you have been not asking me about it and I have noticed that too.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight said the number before he read the line, and then read the line and the day, and gave the count of that sheet, and said what the number is for.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week.

He said on Friday that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open, and that the only reason was that a man of fifty-four asks him.

“*Four.*”

“*And then the ninth line, which is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read it because a man of fifty-four asked, and I am not going to say the day twice on a Saturday.*”

“*Seventy is seven hundred and eighty less seven hundred and ten. A man of thirty-one said on the Wednesday of a week that a round figure is not a settlement, and I am not going to say anything about seventy either.*”

“*I said on Friday that I would say the number out loud once a morning, and that the only reason was that a man of fifty-four asks me to do things in the open.*”

“*I have said it this morning and I am not going to say what it is for.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-two days, and eighty-two is seven hundred and eighty less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*I have said a day is smaller than a board. I am now going to say that a name is smaller than both, and I am not going to say what I am going to do with that.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and then said the thing about a woman of twenty-nine.

It is the first thing he has said about her in about three weeks.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wanted that hearing to stand and has said it out loud every morning since the Saturday of a week and a half ago. He walked four miles up that cart road on the Wednesday of a week and a half ago and four miles down it on the Sunday.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty days, and that is seven hundred and eighty less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-one days, and that is seven hundred and eighty less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-six days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and thirty-eight.*”

“*And that is seven hundred and eighty less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*None of those has been added to any other and I have not added a name to them.*”

“*I did not write down the name a woman of sixty-four said on the Thursday of this week and I am not going to.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the seventeenth morning I have said it, and about four of you have said nothing about it since the Tuesday of a week and a half ago.*”

“*I have not asked any of you about that.*”

“*And here is the thing about the woman of twenty-nine and it is about a figure and not about a person.*”

“*She has been in that reed a hundred and fifty-one days and she has a name on the ninth line, and there has never been a day against that line.*”

“*I have given her figure every morning for about three weeks and she has never once been asked about it by anybody, including me.*”

“*A figure of days that a person cannot hear is a figure of nothing. I have known that since the Saturday of a fortnight and I have been giving it out loud anyway, and I am saying that this morning and I am not going to give a reason.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine said five words out of a reed about twenty feet off the edge of that drain, and about a hundred yards away.

Nobody repeated them back and nobody asked her for a sixth.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in that reed a hundred and fifty-one days.

She said one word out of it on the Saturday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and it was no, and she said three words on the Thursday of a week and a half ago and they were that somebody should ask a clerk.

She has a name on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper and there has never been a day against that line, and nobody has asked her anything in about three weeks.

“*Nobody asked any of us.*”

That is the whole of it. Five words. Nobody repeated them back. Nobody asked her for a sixth word and nobody asked her who us is and about four of you have been working out who us is since the seventh hour and about nine of you have not.

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and the third, and said she is going to go on giving all three, and said what she is not going to say about the five words.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted seventy-four mornings on Friday with a pencil and a crumb of bread. She used one word for the thing on that bank on the Wednesday of this week.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-six mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-five mornings, and seventy-five is seven hundred and eighty less seven hundred and five, and I am going to go on giving all three of them every morning and I am not going to ask anybody for a fourth.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has just said five words out of a reed about a hundred yards from where I am standing, and I am not going to say who us is.*”

“*I have not worked out who us is and I keep chairs.*”

“*And I am not going to say what I would have wanted said on the Wednesday of this week instead of one word from a woman of forty-four, and nobody is going to be thanked for five words in about nine people’s hearing.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he was not going to be given anything this morning and had not been given anything since the Wednesday of a fortnight.

Then he gave the figure, and it was a round one, and he left it alone.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-six days, two hundred and twenty-six being seven hundred and eighty less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-five days old, ninety-five being seven hundred and eighty less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Here is the want, and it is the first one in about sixteen days that anybody could have given me, and nobody did.*”

“*I want somebody to ask me for a thing. Not a question about what I am. A thing with a day on it.*”

“*I said that on the Wednesday of a fortnight, and I am saying it again because about four of you have stopped expecting me to ask for it.*”

“*And nobody has, and I am not going to say that nobody will, and I am not going to stand here and work out why.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me on a Wednesday of a fortnight that the order of two things is the whole of what I am, and I have been getting it wrong for about sixteen days.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is a week old.*”

“*I said on the Thursday of this week that a name said in the ordinary voice is not the beginning of anything, because the day-name moves one place every cycle. I have been living on that since the Thursday and it is not true and I knew it was not true when I said it.*”

“*It is the beginning of something, and what it is the beginning of is a thing about four names on a line that has never had a day.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight can see the number of them, and a woman of twenty-nine has just said out loud that nobody asked any of them.*”

“*Eighty days. Eighty is seven hundred and eighty less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first.*”

“*It is a round figure and I am going to leave it alone, the way a man of thirty-eight left his alone this morning.*”

“*And the cost is that the nine who were heard about four hundred miles off are on that line, and four of them have a day, and the line they are on has not had one.*”

“*The only way to find a person on that line is to have a day to find them on, and I cannot make a day out of a thing that is not a person.*”

“*I have not tried to, and about four of you assumed I would.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved of that and nobody is going to be told I was brave about eighty days.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the ninth hour of the last morning of that week, and a reed about a hundred yards away, and nobody on either of them said a word.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-six days.

A man of thirty-eight said the number of names he can see on that line out loud before he read the line, which is four, and said the only reason he was saying it is that a man of fifty-four asks him to.

And a man of sixty-one said a figure of days that a person cannot hear is a figure of nothing, and that he has been giving one out loud every morning for about three weeks anyway, and would not give a reason.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted somebody on that bank to ask him for a thing, and that the shape of it is a form.

Five words came out of a reed, and they were that nobody asked any of them, and nobody asked her for a sixth, and nobody asked who us is.
