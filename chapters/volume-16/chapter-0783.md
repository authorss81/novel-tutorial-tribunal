# Chapter 783: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Tuesday, And A Woman Of Forty Names A House Two Miles Off, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Reads A Day Out Loud That Nobody Asked Him To Read, And The House Has Two Doors On The Yard Side

First day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-third morning. Three hundred and eighty-three days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and three days. Three hundred and thirty-seven days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the second morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he gave the day-name on the word, because he had said on Monday that the next one would be a Saturday, and he wanted about nine of them to have the Monday in front of them.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He gave the day-name sequence out loud on the Thursday of last week and said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of the thirteen.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the second morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and I have said that on every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning, which is the Friday of this week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week. Monday I said was the day the bank comes back full, and it did, and this morning it is holding.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-second morning. He asked out loud first, and said the thing about being treated differently, and said he had stopped noticing.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week and about four of them have been treating him differently since.

“*Sixty-two mornings.*”

“*Sixty-two is one more than sixty-one, and I am not going to say what the working is for because I said it on Saturday and I have said it three times.*”

“*About four of you have been not asking me about the fifth thing since Wednesday and I have stopped noticing it, and I am giving you that as a figure of my own and not as a complaint.*”

Then a woman of forty named a house and gave its distances before she gave anything else about it, and said there are two things she has never told anybody.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She gave the five out loud on the Friday of last week and the second of the three that have a day against them is a house being taken into.

“*It is about two miles from that landing, on the far side of the drain from the cart road, and about ninety yards off the near end of that lane, which is forty-five paces of his.*”

“*Two miles is three thousand five hundred and twenty yards, and that is one thousand seven hundred and sixty paces of a man whose pace is two yards.*”

“*There is a yard behind it. The yard is about nine paces by five, and that is eighteen yards by ten, and I have not measured it and I am not going to.*”

“*I was taken into that house. I have had a day against it since I was about thirty and I have never asked anybody to find out whether that day is the day on the paper.*”

“*And I am not asking today. That is the second thing I have never told anybody and it is not because of the room I am not going to name.*”

Then a woman of forty asked a man of thirty-eight to read a line he was not going to read, and he read it, and about four of them did not know it could be done.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He said on the Friday of a week ago that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open.

“*A woman of forty has asked me for a line and a man of fifty-four asks me for the ninth every morning, and those are two different jobs and I have said that I would say the ninth.*”

“*There are days on eight of the nine lines. I read that paper forty times in this county and I know which ones and I have not said so out loud before this morning.*”

“*Here is the one she has asked for. It is the Friday of the hundred and twenty-second week, and I am going to give it once and I am not going to read the rest of the line.*”

“*And I am not going to say whether it is the day she has had against that house since she was about thirty, because she told me in front of about nine of you that she has never asked, and I am not going to ask for her.*”

“*Seventy-three is seven hundred and eighty-three less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*Four names. I have said the number on three mornings running and I am not going to read the line.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-five days, and eighty-five is seven hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

Then he walked about two miles and found that the house could be taken into from two sides, and said so out loud, and nobody had thought of it before him.

He had a woman of forty-four hold the count again and a man of about fifty-two check the two figures he gave against four lines under him.

“*I am going to give it the way I gave the turn, and I am not going to use the word this time because I used it on Monday and got it wrong on Monday.*”

“*The yard side of that house is about nine paces by five, and that is eighteen yards by ten, and it is the ground a person stands on who comes in at the yard door.*”

“*The other door is at the back of the wash-house and it opens onto a strip about four paces wide running nine paces to the back wall, and that is eight yards by eighteen.*”

“*And here is the count and it is two, not one. There are two pieces of ground and the house stands between them and they do not touch at any point.*”

“*So a house being taken into is not one field. It is two, and which one you are in depends on which door, and nobody on that bank knew that about that house until this morning.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said the thing about rooms, and gave the two figures back checked twice, and said what he has not said in about eleven years is still not said.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. He has had one thing in those four leaves he has not said out loud in about eleven years and he has not said it.

“*Eighteen yards by ten and eight yards by eighteen, and I have checked both against the paces and the paces are right.*”

“*A room is not a place. That is the whole of what I know about the second of your three and it took me about eleven years to be sure of it and I did not know I was getting sure of it until about nine days ago.*”

“*And I have two sheets of deal and four lines under me and I checked your two figures twice and the two checks are the same, and I am still not saying the thing in those four leaves.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said what she held in her hand this morning, and said she is not going to give the count of a road.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread.

“*I held the count again and it is on the back of my hand and it is not in the book, because the book is chairs.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-nine mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-three mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-eight mornings, and seventy-eight is seven hundred and eighty-three less seven hundred and five.*”

“*A man of forty-three said the bank came back full on Monday and it did, and I have been keeping that figure in my head since the Saturday because I did not believe him.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight read a line this morning that nobody had asked him to read. I have counted seventy-eight mornings of about nine of you asking each other for things in a circle and about four of you have got better at it this week and about nine of you have not.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that the asking had gone past him and that he was not going to say what that was.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-nine days, two hundred and twenty-nine being seven hundred and eighty-three less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-eight days old, ninety-eight being seven hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Here is the want, and I am going to give it before I give anything else, and it is the want I have had since the Wednesday of a fortnight.*”

“*I want to be the one who gets asked. A woman of forty asked me for the first of the three on Sunday and this morning she asked a man of thirty-eight for the second, and he gave it, and the whole of the asking went about two feet past me and I did not get a hand on it.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is the same one in the fourth wording.*”

“*On Monday I called a turn of a cart road a field before I asked what the cart was tied to. This morning I called a house a field before I counted its doors.*”

“*A field is a piece of ground a thing is tied to, and I have now been wrong about that twice in two mornings in two different ways, and the second time it was in front of the woman who asked me for it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has just told a man of about fifty-two to his face that a room is not a place, and he has known that for about eleven years and nobody on that bank asked him for it before this morning either.*”

“*Eighty-three days. Eighty-three is seven hundred and eighty-three less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to a line read out loud on request.*”

“*And the cost is that the asking went past me this morning and I am not going to say what that is, and about four of you noticed, and one of them is a woman of forty and she is not going to help me out of it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for reading a line and nobody is going to be told I was brave about two miles on foot.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twentieth morning, and said he could have been asked for a figure and was not.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-three days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-four days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-nine days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-one, and that is seven hundred and eighty-three less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have a figure for every one of those and I have given all four out loud and I have not added any of them to any other and I have not added a name to them.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twentieth morning I have said it, and about four of you could have asked me this morning for one of these figures instead of me giving it, and nobody did, and I am not going to ask you why.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour of the first morning of that week, and a full bank behind them that was holding, and two pieces of ground two miles off with a house standing between them.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-nine days.

A woman of forty named a house about two miles off on the far side of a drain and gave its distance before she gave anything else.

A man of thirty-eight read a day out loud for the first time in this county because she asked, and would not read the rest of the line.

No ground at all where the two doors of that house meet, and a man of about fifty-two who did not know he had known a room was not a place until a fortnight ago.
