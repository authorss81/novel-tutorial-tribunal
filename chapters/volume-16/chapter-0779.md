# Chapter 779: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Friday, And A Woman Of Forty Says Out Loud Which Three Of Five Things Have A Day Against Them After Eleven Years Of Not Saying It, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Says What The Woman In That Kitchen Said

Fourth day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and twenty-ninth morning. Three hundred and seventy-nine days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and six days. Three hundred and thirty-three days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

He had given the whole sequence out loud yesterday and said he was not going to give it again, and he gave the figure this morning and not the day.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. Yesterday he gave the whole day-name sequence out loud for the second time in a fortnight and said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of the thirteen.

“*About 4 inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is the one day of it coming back this week.*”

“*Four inches a day, and the bank will be full again on the Monday of this week, and that is three days from this morning.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in seven days from this morning, which is the Friday of next week, and that will be the fifteenth time.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-eighth morning. He asked out loud first, and said that yesterday was the first morning of about nine weeks that he had not said the count before anybody else had said anything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He told that bank yesterday what had been standing behind the asking, and about four of them have been different with him since, and he has noticed and said so.

“*Fifty-eight mornings.*”

“*Fifty-eight is one more than fifty-seven.*”

“*Yesterday was the first morning since the Tuesday of a fortnight that I have not asked out loud before anybody else has said anything. I am not going to say why, and it is not because a woman of sixty-four said a name.*”

“*I have been asked nothing by anybody on this bank for about eight weeks and that is a notice and not a want, and about four of you have stopped counting it, and I have noticed that as well.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the fifty-eight and nobody is going to be told I was brave about yesterday.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and the day out loud, and gave the count of that sheet with the working, and then gave the number he said on Friday he would give.

Then he said what he is going to do about a board, and said it is not what anybody on that bank is waiting for.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He said the precedent out loud on the Wednesday of this week and has not said it again since.

“*Sixty-nine is seven hundred and seventy-nine less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I am not going to say a fourth morning of that.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four asked for it nine days ago and has not asked again, and I have noticed that as well.*”

“*Here is what I am going to do about the board, and it is not opening it.*”

“*I am going to read out, once a morning, in the open, on this bank, the number of names I can see on the ninth line. Not the names. The number of them. And I will do it because a man of fifty-four asks me to do things in the open every morning, and that is the whole of the reason and it is not a good one.*”

“*There are four of them that I can see. I have never said that out loud on this bank and I have been able to see it for about nine weeks.*”

“*About four of you knew. About nine of you did not. I am not going to do anything with the number this morning and about four of you are already doing something with it.*”

Then a woman of forty said which three of five things have a day against them, and gave the five, and gave the working for the eleven years, and said what it had cost her to say it.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She said on the Sunday of last week that a name with no day on it is a day with no name on it and that she has wanted both of them for eleven years. She said on the Wednesday of this week that the third thing was a person that nobody can be sent for. She has refused seven times in about a fortnight to say which three of five things have a day against them.

“*Five things. I have asked for a day against each of them for eleven years and I have never once asked for a reason instead, and here they are in the order I asked for them.*”

“*One: a cart turning off a road. That has a day against it. I have a day for that and I have had one for about nine years.*”

“*Two: a house being taken into. That has a day against it. I have had a day for that one since I was about thirty.*”

“*Three: a person being put in a room. That has a day against it, and it is the only one of the five I have ever been given one for without asking.*”

“*Four: a name on a line of paper. That has no day against it.*”

“*Five: a person being heard about. That has no day against it, and about nine days is on that paper against nothing, and a man of about fifty-two has told you the day it starts counting from is not on the line.*”

“*So it is three and two, and I have spent eleven years on the three, and the two are the whole of that counter.*”

“*Here is the working. Eleven years is how long I have been asking. And the thing it has cost is not a figure and I am not going to give you one.*”

“*It is that I have been the woman who comes up this bank and asks for a day for a cart, and about four of you have worked out over about nine weeks that I do it for a living.*”

“*I do not. I have never once said so, and I am saying it now because I have said the other four things and this is the fifth.*”

“*I am not going to stop asking for the three. That is the cost and I am going to pay it out loud in about nine people’s hearing and nobody is going to be thanked for hearing it.*”

“*And a name on a line of paper is the fifth thing, and I have said it, and I am not going to be a person who is treated differently this week for having said five things in a fortnight.*”

Then a woman of forty-four came down four miles of that cart road on the bread cart, and said what the woman in that kitchen said to her, and gave the figure of the distance out loud again.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps the bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses go up to it for bread. She named a kitchen on Tuesday about six hundred yards up that road from a low gate three miles and six hundred yards above this landing.

There is a woman in it keeping the days her second cart comes down in her own head.

“*I came down on the bread because the bread comes down on a Friday and I am on the bread cart, and that is the whole of the reason.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four said a name on that bank yesterday in the ordinary voice. I was four miles up a cart road when I heard about it, from a boy who came up for bread, and I have not stopped baking.*”

“*I am going to give you the distance again because a woman of forty-four named that kitchen on Tuesday and nobody has written it down anywhere except about nine of us.*”

“*That kitchen is about six hundred yards up that cart road from that low gate. The low gate is three miles and six hundred yards up it from this landing. So that kitchen is about four miles and two hundred yards up that cart road from where I am standing.*”

“*And here is what the woman in it said to me on Wednesday when I went up for the bread, and I have been carrying it down four miles since.*”

“*She said: I have been keeping them in my head since the Friday and I have not told my husband, and you are the first person I have said it out loud to.*”

“*And she said she is not going to say it to anybody who can be sent for.*”

“*And I am not going to be that person. I am a baker and I go up that road with bread and I am not on any line and I am not in any book and that is the only reason she told me.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for four miles and nobody is going to be told I was brave about carrying a sentence down a road.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts and the third, and said what the third count is now that a name has been said on that bank.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted seventy-three mornings on Thursday with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and used one word for the thing on that bank on the Wednesday.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-five mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-nine mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-four mornings, and seventy-four is seven hundred and seventy-nine less seven hundred and five, and I have got to the point where I write it before I come up the bank.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight has just said there are four names on that line. I have known that since the Friday of a week and a half ago and I have not said it because I am not a keeper of anything and I do not count what I cannot be sent for.*”

“*And a woman of forty has just said which three of five things have a day against them, and she has paid for it out loud.*”

“*I am not going to say that she was right, because I have not got a figure for it, and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that the name said yesterday had not changed one thing about that bank, and gave the working for it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-five days, two hundred and twenty-five being seven hundred and seventy-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-four days old, ninety-four being seven hundred and seventy-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

A man of fifty-four counted the two of those this week and a man of thirty-eight did not repeat them, and neither of them has said whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and it is about a man of thirty-eight.*”

“*I want the number four to be said out loud every morning by somebody, and I am not going to be the one, and I am not going to be asked.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is two days old and it is the same one for the sixth time in a week.*”

“*On the Saturday of a week and a half ago I worked out which of about four people who came up this bank on the Friday was which, and I am not going to say it.*”

“*About four of you have been waiting since for me to, and about nine of you stopped at the Tuesday.*”

“*Yesterday a woman of sixty-four said a name and I had nothing to say about it.*”

“*And this morning a woman of forty has said five things, and a woman of forty-four has carried a sentence four miles down a cart road, and neither of those was because of me.*”

“*I have been the reason about nine of you get up here for about nine weeks, and I am not the reason for any of the three of them.*”

“*That is not a cost. That is a figure. A figure of what a man of thirty-one is worth on a bank in about nine weeks and it is four out of three and the working is out loud and there it is.*”

“*Seventy-nine days. Seventy-nine is seven hundred and seventy-nine less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first.*”

“*And this morning it is not an answer to a woman of forty paying a thing out loud in front of about nine of us.*”

“*And the cost is that nobody on this bank has asked me for anything since the Wednesday of a fortnight, and about four of you have stopped waiting for me to say the other thing.*”

“*I have noticed both of those, and neither of them is a relief.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a woman of forty having said five things out loud and a man of thirty-eight having said a number out loud, and nobody on it had thanked anybody.

---

The fever was seventy weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-five days.

A woman of forty said which three of five things have a day against them.

The two that have none are a name on a line of paper and a person being heard about, and she said the cost of saying it out loud is that she is going to go on asking for the three anyway.

And a man of thirty-eight said that there are four names on the ninth line that he can see, and that he is going to say the number of them once a morning in the open, and that it is not a good reason.

And a woman of forty-four carried a sentence four miles down a cart road, which is that a woman in a kitchen is keeping the days in her own head and is not going to say it to anybody who can be sent for.

And a woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs gave the two counts and the third and said she is not going to say a woman of forty was right, because she has not got a figure for it and she is not going to make one up this morning.
