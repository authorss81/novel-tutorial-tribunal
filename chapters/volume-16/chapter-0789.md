# Chapter 789: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Monday, And The Margin Is Held At Both Ends By Somebody Who Is Not Him And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Walks Out Of It And It Stays Open, And A Woman Of Forty Goes Down To The Edge Of That Drain And Asks A Woman In A Reed One Question

Seventh day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-ninth morning. Three hundred and eighty-nine days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks and two days. Three hundred and forty-three days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was three days of the coming back, and the top step was under it and the second one was not. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he said what he had held the end of on Sunday and what he had not, and he said it before the reading went any further.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank yesterday that about nine of them had never been told about.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and I have given you the first half of that every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning, which is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*That is the sixteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it will be a Saturday morning and I will be here for it.*”

“*And about nine of you have been past my stone on that bank for about nine weeks and I have said it out loud twice now and that is the whole of what I am going to do about it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-eighth morning. He asked out loud first, and gave the number for the notice.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week.

“*Sixty-eight mornings.*”

“*Sixty-eight is one more than sixty-seven, and that is the working, and I said on Thursday that I would start asking about the fifth thing on Monday and I am going to do that after this is read and not before it.*”

“*And the figure for the not being asked is a week and a half, and I gave that figure last Friday and I have not moved it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day and gave the count of that sheet and the number of names, and said that nine mornings was the most anybody has asked him for anything.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has said the number of names he can see on the ninth line out loud every morning since the Friday of last week, and gave the reason once and has not given it since.

“*Four names, and I said them out loud on Friday from about a hundred and thirty feet under a bank where about nine of you could hear me.*”

“*Seventy-nine is seven hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred and ten, and I read only the ninth, because a man of fifty-four asked and nobody has asked me for another since Tuesday.*”

“*Nine mornings. That is the most anybody has asked me for anything since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and one of the nine was a question about a piece of ground and one of the nine was nothing at all.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety-one days, and ninety-one is seven hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and ninety-eight, and I am not opening it and about nine of you have stopped asking me to.*”

Then a woman of forty-four held the far end of a strip of that bank, and did not let go of it for about an hour and a half, and a woman of sixty-four walked out of it and it stayed open.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He walked a strip of that bank yesterday from a stone at the up-water end to the up-water end of that counter, about nineteen paces long, six paces wide at the narrow end and nine at the wide end.

“*About nineteen paces long, which is thirty-eight yards. Six paces wide at the narrow end, which is twelve yards. Nine paces wide at the wide end, which is eighteen yards.*”

“*And I am not holding either end of it this morning.*”

“*A woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs is holding the far end and she has been holding it since about the seventh hour and about four of you walked past her and did not ask her what she was doing.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four is going to walk out of it in the open. Not because I asked her to, and I want that said, because a man of fifty-four said the same kind of thing about me on Friday and he was right.*”

“*She said she would do it on Saturday night and she has not been asked since by anybody, including me.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four walked out of it, in the open, at the wide end, and about nine of them stayed where they were.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them. She asked a man of thirty-one for a day against her name on the Saturday and he said he could not do it.

“*I said on Saturday night that I would walk out of it and I have walked out of it.*”

“*Nobody followed me out of it and nobody asked me why I went and about nine of you stayed exactly where you were.*”

“*That is what a margin is and I have not had one of those since I was about forty-five and I am not going to say what it is worth.*”

“*And I am not going to say why I could not attend and nobody has asked me and I am not going to be thanked for walking out of a strip of a bank.*”

“*And that strip is still nineteen paces long. A woman of forty-four has been holding the far end of it since the seventh hour and she can tell you that yourself.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said what she was doing and how long she was doing it for, and said what she wants out of it and what she does not.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, and she has been counting it with a pencil and a crumb of bread and not in the book ever since.

“*I have been standing at the up-water end of that strip since about the seventh hour and it is now about the ninth hour, and I am going to be standing here until he tells me I can move.*”

“*I am not holding it because I can be checked against anything. I am holding it because I am the only person on that bank who is not a keeper of a thing anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*That is not a compliment to me and it is not a reason anybody should tell me I am brave about an hour and a half on a bank in the cold.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-five mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-nine mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-four mornings, and eighty-four is seven hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred and five.*”

Then a woman of forty went down about two hundred yards of that bank to the edge of that drain and asked a woman in a reed one question, and got one word back.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She said on Sunday that a person who cannot get out of anything is not in a margin.

“*I am going to ask her one question and I am going to say what it is before I ask it, and I am not going to ask her a second one whatever she says.*”

“*I have wanted a day against a name for eleven years and I am not going to ask her for one, and about four of you are going to think that is soft.*”

“*I am going to ask her about the ground she is standing on, because ground is the only thing anybody in this county has got about any of the four of them, and because a day is not.*”

“*And I am going down there myself and I am not taking a man of thirty-one with me, because he has asked to be asked for things all week and this is me asking.*”

She went down about two hundred yards of that bank and stopped about twenty feet off the edge of that drain, where the reed is.

“*There is ground under that reed or there is not. That is the whole of the question and I am asking it once.*”

A woman of twenty-nine in that reed said one word.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in that reed a hundred and sixty days. She said five words out of it on the Saturday of last week and they were *nobody asked any of us*, and nobody asked her who *us* is.

“*Yes.*”

Nobody asked her for a second word. Nobody asked her who *us* is. Nobody repeated it back.

About four of them were standing at the top of that bank and could not hear any of it, which had not happened before in about nine weeks.

Then a man of thirty-one said he did not know what she had said yes to and was not going to ask, and gave the want and the mistake and the cost.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-five days, two hundred and thirty-five being seven hundred and eighty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is a hundred and four days old, a hundred and four being seven hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*I do not know what she said yes to. It was not a question about ground by the time it got to her, because a woman of forty asked it going down a bank and about four of you were standing behind me.*”

“*And I am not going to ask her, and I am not going to ask a woman of forty what she thinks the word meant, and about four of you want me to do both of those things this morning.*”

“*Here is the want, and it is about four words long and I am giving it whole.*”

“*I want more than one word. That is the whole of it and I have wanted more than one word out of that reed since the Saturday of a fortnight ago.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is a week old and it is the sixth wording of it.*”

“*I have spent a week building a map out of a thing that has no day against it. Ground is not a day. A woman in a reed is not a person who can be sent for.*”

“*And I have made about four people walk this week and stand at the end of things and hold things, and I have called that a margin, and about four of you have gone along with it because I asked.*”

“*Eighty-nine days. Eighty-nine is seven hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer to one word.*”

“*And the cost is that a woman of sixty-four spent being-askable on a no on Saturday, and a woman in a reed gave me one word about ground this morning, and I cannot use either of them and about nine of you watched me not use either of them.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for standing at the end of a strip of a bank for an hour and a half and nobody is going to be told I was brave about a week.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-sixth morning, and said what a word is not.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-six mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-nine days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and sixty days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-five days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-seven, and that is seven hundred and eighty-nine less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I gave her a figure every morning for about three weeks and said a figure of days a person cannot hear is a figure of nothing, and a man of thirty-eight said the same on the Saturday of last week.*”

“*And this morning she said a word and about four of you heard it and about nine of you did not, and I am not going to write the word down and I am not going to give a figure for it.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-sixth morning I have said it, and I have not asked any of you about it.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the tenth hour of a Monday, and a woman of forty-four was still holding the far end of a strip of it, and the strip was still nineteen paces long.

The fever was seventy-two weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-five days.

A woman of forty-four held the far end of a strip of that bank for an hour and a half and a woman of sixty-four walked out of it and it stayed nineteen paces long.

A woman of forty went down about two hundred yards of that bank on foot to the edge of that drain, and nobody followed her and about nine of them stayed where they were.

One word came out of a reed about a hundred yards off that drain on a Monday morning, and nobody asked for a second one and nobody asked who us is.
