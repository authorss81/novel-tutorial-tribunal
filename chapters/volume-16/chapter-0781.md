# Chapter 781: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Sunday, And A Woman Of Forty Asks Him For A Thing With A Day On It, And He Says Yes

Sixth day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and thirty-first morning. Three hundred and eighty-one days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and one day. Three hundred and thirty-five days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he said the bank will be full in the morning, and that he has said that four mornings running and it has been true.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He gave the day-name sequence out loud on the Thursday of this week and said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of the thirteen, and he has not said it since.

“*Twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is three days of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and the bank will be full in the morning, which is the Monday of this week.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning, which is the Friday of this week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and the day-name has moved one place since Thursday, and the next one will be a Saturday.*”

“*That is four things and I said three. I have said four.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixtieth morning. He asked out loud first, and about nine of them stood there and did not make a sound while he did it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has asked nothing of anybody on that bank for about nine weeks, and he answered a thing at the Wednesday of this week and about four of them have been treating him differently since.

“*Sixty mornings.*”

“*Sixty is one more than fifty-nine, and that is the working, and it is the first morning of a week and I said the same thing last Sunday.*”

“*About four of you have not asked me about the fifth thing since Wednesday and I have noticed that and I am not going to say what I make of it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight said the number of names before he read anything else, and then read the line and the day, and gave the count of that sheet.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He said on Friday that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open, and the only reason he gave was that a man of fifty-four asks him to.

“*Four.*”

“*Seventy-one is seven hundred and eighty-one less seven hundred and ten, and I am going to leave that figure where it is and not say anything about it.*”

“*It is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read it because a man of fifty-four asked, and I have now said it out loud on two mornings running.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-three days, and eighty-three is seven hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

Then a woman of forty asked a man of thirty-one for a thing, and she said the thing out loud before she asked it, and about nine of them heard both halves.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She gave the five things out loud on the Friday of this week, and the three of them that have a day against them are a cart turning off a road, a house being taken into, and a person being put in a room.

“*I am going to ask him for a thing and I am going to say what it is before I ask, because I have watched about nine of you ask each other things in a circle for about nine weeks.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has said on this bank that he wants somebody to ask him for a thing with a day on it, and he said that on the Wednesday of this week and again on the Saturday of this week, and nobody has.*”

“*I am not asking him for a day. I have been asking for a day for eleven years and a day is not what is missing.*”

“*I am asking him to go and stand on the ground of the three things that have a day against them, and to count what is on the ground, and to come back up that bank and say the count out loud in front of about nine of you.*”

“*A cart turning off a road happens in a place, and a house being taken into happens in a place, and a person being put in a room happens in a place.*”

“*I have eleven years of asking and not one foot of it. That is what I am asking him for.*”

“*And it is provisional, and I am going to say that word tomorrow, and about four of you are going to tell me it is the wrong one.*”

Then he said yes, and it took him longer than one word.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-seven days, two hundred and twenty-seven being seven hundred and eighty-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-six days old, ninety-six being seven hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Yes. I am not going to make you ask it a second time and I am not going to make you be the one who asked me for it.*”

“*Here is the want and it is the one I said on the Saturday and it has just happened to me and I did not do anything about it.*”

“*I have said for six days that I want somebody to ask me for a thing with a day on it, and a woman of forty has asked me for one, and I have said yes, and about four of you are going to find out what that is worth to me in about nine weeks.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is four days old and I am not going to pretend it is a different one.*”

“*On the Wednesday of this week a woman of sixty-four made herself askable for the first time in nineteen years and she asked for nothing. And I have spent every morning since building something out of ground, and I did that because she asked for nothing, and that is the same mistake I made when I stood here and told you the shape of what I want is a form.*”

“*A woman of forty-four used one word about what about nine of us are on that bank and it was spoken once and I have not used it. I want her to have used it again.*”

“*Eighty-one days. Eighty-one is seven hundred and eighty-one less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to a woman of forty.*”

“*And the cost is that about nine of you are going to hold the fact that a man of thirty-one said yes to a woman of forty this morning, and I have not got one foot of that ground either and I have said yes to it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be told I was brave about it and nobody is going to be thanked for asking me.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and said the thing he has said every morning for a fortnight and has not offered a reason for.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud every morning since the Saturday of a week and a half ago.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-one days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-two days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-seven days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and thirty-nine.*”

“*And that is seven hundred and eighty-one less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*None of those has been added to any other and I have not added a name to them, and I am not going to write down what a woman of forty has just asked for.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the eighteenth morning I have said it, and about four of you have not said anything about it since the Tuesday of a week and a half ago, and I have not asked any of you about that.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts off that counter, and said she is going to go on giving all three, and said what she is not going to say about a woman of forty standing up on a Sunday morning.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted seventy-six mornings with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and she began that count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-seven mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-one mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-six mornings, and seventy-six is seven hundred and eighty-one less seven hundred and five.*”

“*A woman of forty stood up on this bank on a Sunday morning and asked a man for a thing, and that is not a third count and I am not going to make it one.*”

“*I am not going to say what to do about the seventy-six. I have been not saying what to do about them since the Saturday of a fortnight ago and I have not found a fourth thing to add to this and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for standing here while a woman of forty asked him, because I am not going to do anything about it.*”

A boy of thirteen gave a count of his own out loud and said that about four of them have stopped expecting him to keep it up.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county since a day he keeps himself. He has carried five counts for about nine weeks and has not carried six, and he has said out loud on this bank that somebody ought to ask a man of fifty-four a question and that he was not going to be the one.

“*A hundred and twenty-eight days, and I counted it on my fingers in the lane before I came up, and it is one more than last Sunday.*”

“*I am still carrying five and I said on Wednesday that I would put the sixth down this week.*”

“*And I have not, and about four of you have not asked me about it, and that is the fourth week running that nobody has asked me about it.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the ninth hour of the first morning of that week, and a full bank coming on in the morning, and a woman of forty standing on it who had been standing on it for eleven years without a foot of it.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-seven days.

A woman of forty asked him for a thing with a day on it, and she said the thing out loud before she asked it, and she said she was not asking him for a day.

He said yes, and gave the want and the mistake and the cost, and said nobody was going to be told he was brave about it.

Eighteen mornings of a man saying out loud that he wants that hearing to stand, and not one question asked him about any part of it in about nine weeks.
