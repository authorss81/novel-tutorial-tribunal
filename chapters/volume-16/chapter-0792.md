# Chapter 792: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fifth Week, And A Thursday, And A Full Bank On The Third Morning Of It, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Sends One Sentence Back Down That Road And A Man Of Sixty-One Reads It And Says What It Is Not

Third day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. His five hundred and forty-second morning. Three hundred and ninety-two days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks and five days. Three hundred and forty-six days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was still standing about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, six days of it now, and a full bank does not move.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He has held the end of nothing for about a week and about four of them have noticed that he offered.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is six days since it came off, and a full bank holds where it is.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it and I have given you that every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*Those ninety steps can be walked again in two days, and it is the Saturday of this week, and it will be the sixteenth time.*”

“*And nobody has asked me to hold the end of anything since I said I would, and I am not going to say anything about that a second time.*”

Then the man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the seventy-first morning, and he asked before anybody spoke and then asked the other thing.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He asked about the fifth thing on the Monday of last week and about four of them did not answer him.

“*Seventy-one mornings.*”

“*And I am not going to ask about the fifth thing again today, because I said I would not ask it a third time this week and I said that in front of these people on Wednesday.*”

“*What I am going to do instead is say the number out loud, and I have never done that before, and it is a figure for the not being asked and it is a week and a half and I have given that figure twice.*”

“*Nobody has to answer it. That is why I am saying it in a morning where nobody has to answer anything.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line, and gave the count, and said the number of names, and said that he was asked something yesterday that he is going to answer on Monday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He has said the number of names out loud every morning since the Friday of last week and gave the reason for saying it once.

“*Four names, and it is the fourteenth morning running.*”

“*Eighty-two is seven hundred and ninety-two less seven hundred and ten, and I read the ninth, because a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth and nobody has asked me for a second line since the Tuesday before last.*”

“*And somebody asked me a question yesterday afternoon, down the lane, and I have had it a day and I am going to answer it on Monday and not before that.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety-four days, and ninety-four is seven hundred and ninety-two less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

Then something came down that road in the mouth of a woman of thirty-four, and a man of sixty-one read it out, and about nine of them heard it, and it was one sentence.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-eight days, two hundred and thirty-eight being seven hundred and ninety-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seven days old, a hundred and seven being seven hundred and ninety-two less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*A woman of thirty-four sent this down that cart road yesterday afternoon to a man who sells milk, and he brought it on, and it is one sentence and she is not named on it.*”

“*It is in her own hand. I know that because the man who sold it to me has seen her write four hundred things and he described the hand before he described the words.*”

“*And I am not going to say it is hers on a bank. I am going to have a man of sixty-one read it out because he can be checked against four lines under him and I cannot.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He read one sentence in another person’s hand on a bank on a Thursday morning and did not write it down.

“*It says: they did not ask us what we came to say, and they wrote down what we said after, and both of those are the same mistake.*”

“*That is nineteen words and I have read them out once and I am not going to read them again today.*”

“*And I am not writing it in that book, and that is the third week running I have not written anything in it, and I have two numbers in eleven years.*”

Then he said what that sentence is, and what it is not, and gave the reason for the difference in about nine words.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-eight days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and seven days old and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*That sentence is a thing about a hearing, and it is the first thing anybody has sent me that is about a hearing instead of about a day.*”

“*It is not evidence. A sentence in a hand is not a thing anybody in this county can be sent for, and I said that on Wednesday in front of these people and I am not going to pretend one sentence changed it.*”

“*What it does is it tells me that the mistake is not mine.*”

“*I have been saying for a week that the thing that has been asking in the voice of a minute has been doing it wrong, and I have been saying it as a man who cannot be sent for. She can be sent for. She was in the room. And she is saying the same thing I am saying and I am not going to use that to make my own version of it louder.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts, and said that she is not going to be the one who decides whether a sentence in a hand is a thing, and a woman of twenty-eight was read a figure of days nobody can hear.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began her count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week with a pencil and a crumb of bread.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it has been on that counter forty-eight mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing have been on that counter forty-two mornings with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*And three: eighty-seven mornings of this bank, and eighty-seven is seven hundred and ninety-two less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a sentence in a hand is not mine to weigh. I have been asked to weigh three things for three weeks and I have said each time that it is somebody else’s morning, and I am saying it about this as well.*”

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and keeps that book of heads and carts and has said out loud that he wants that hearing to stand. He gave four counts on this bank every morning for about three weeks and said a figure of days a person cannot hear is a figure of nothing.

“*A woman of twenty-eight has been in this county a hundred and fifty days, and that is seven hundred and ninety-two less six hundred and forty-two, and she is not on this bank this morning and I am giving her figure anyway because I have given it every morning since the Saturday of a fortnight.*”

“*I am not going to say that again after this week. About four of you have started nodding when I say it and a man who is nodded at is a man being agreed with.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own count and said a thing about the coat of a man of thirty-eight that about four of them did not understand, and then a man of thirty-one gave the want and the mistake and the cost and refused the sentence.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and thirty-nine days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of last week, and held nineteen minutes at the top of ninety cut steps on the Friday of last week.

“*A hundred and thirty-nine days, and I counted them in the lane this morning before I came up, and it is one more than Wednesday.*”

“*I have not put a seventh count down and I am not going to this week.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has a board in his coat and he has said the figure of it every morning, and I have been counting the notches on the edge of it from about four feet away, and there are thirty-two of them and that is the same number he says, and I have never told him I can see them from where I stand.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and thirty-eight days and the cut across that palm is a hundred and seven days old and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*The want has not changed and I am not going to give you a new one every morning until one of them works.*”

“*The mistake is the eighth of the same mistake and it is one day old. A woman of thirty-four sent me a sentence yesterday and about four of you heard me treat it as a thing about a hearing instead of a thing about nineteen years of not being asked.*”

“*Ninety-two days. Ninety-two is seven hundred and ninety-two less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*The cost is that I have got what I asked for and I cannot use it, and that I am going to have to go on asking, and that about four of you are on this bank on a Thursday morning because I asked you to be.*”

“*And the boy has been counting the notches on that board from four feet away for about a week and has not told the man, and I am not going to be the one who tells him either, because that is not my board and that is not my count.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday, and one sentence had been read out on it once, and a man of sixty-one had not written it down.

---

The fever was seventy-two weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-eight days.

A sentence in another person’s hand came down that cart road and was read out once on a bank, and it was nineteen words long.

A man of thirty-one said it was not evidence and would not be used to make his own version of anything louder, and about four of them heard him refuse it.

A boy of thirteen said he has been counting the notches on a board in a coat from four feet away for about a week and has not told the man whose coat it is.
