# Chapter 771: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Thursday, And Two People Who Went Up That Cart Road Are Not On That Bank And Nobody Has Come Down It, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says For How Long

Third day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twenty-first morning. Three hundred and seventy-one days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks and five days. Three hundred and twenty-five days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked. He gave the day of the week on the word, because the last time it came off it was a Wednesday and the next one is not.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, none of them added to any other, and none of them about a night. He has counted thirteen of them off and has said he is not going to work out the first five in front of anybody.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is one day of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working under it and I have not varied it in about nine weeks.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in seven days from this morning.*”

“*That is the Thursday of next week, and it will be the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*Eight days from the thirteenth, which was a Wednesday.*”

“*And I am giving you the day-name because some of you write the day beside the figure and get it wrong.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fiftieth morning. He asked out loud before anybody spoke, and he gave two figures.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has not missed one of them. He has been asking out loud before anybody speaks since the Tuesday of a fortnight ago.

“*Fifty mornings.*”

“*Fifty is one more than forty-nine, and that is the working, and I have said the working every morning of them.*”

“*And here is the second figure and it is the one I have not said out loud. This coat has a board in the inside of it that has been there since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week.*”

“*Seventy-three days. Seventy-three is seven hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it is not a figure of anything and I have counted it anyway.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me about it and I have not been asked this morning either and I am not going to say anything else about it.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and gave the count of that sheet first, with the working, and then said the thing he has been carrying since Wednesday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He said on Monday that he will read a day out loud every morning if somebody asks him. On Wednesday morning the day was said out loud, because some of them asked.

“*Sixty-one is seven hundred and seventy-one less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And there has not been one of the sixty-one in the dark.*”

“*A man of sixty-one and a woman of forty-four went up that cart road yesterday morning on the bread cart and neither of them is on this bank this morning.*”

“*And nobody has come down that road.*”

“*That is my figure, and it is a figure of this morning and not of yesterday, and I am not going to carry it back to yesterday and make it a bigger one.*”

“*I have had a board in the inside of this coat for seventy-three days and I am not opening it this morning, and the reason is different again and I am not going to tell you what it is.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, in the order she gives them, and said what the two of them are for.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. Nobody asked her to. She has given those two counts every morning since without being asked once.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-seven mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-one mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And neither of those two counts needs anybody to be standing on this bank for it to go on being true, and that is the whole of what I have for them this morning.*”

“*I am not going to say the other thing about them yet. I have had it since Wednesday and I have been sitting on it two mornings and about four of you have noticed me sitting on it.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well had the fourth line read to him out loud, all of it, once, and then said he was not going to say the rest of it.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and he is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. He asked the one question nobody on that bank had asked, at the Tuesday of last week. It is that the fourth line says about nine days and does not say nine days from which day. He gave the reason out of his own house.

“*The fourth line says a finding is made in the absence of the persons heard about, and that their absence is not to be read as consent, and that it is held open for about nine days for any person heard about to come forward.*”

“*That is the fifth time I have read that line in the open and I have read it right every time but one, and the one was a Saturday of a fortnight ago and I have not said which part I got wrong.*”

“*I am not going to say the rest of it this morning. I have been going over those four leaves since Wednesday and there is one thing in them I have not said out loud in about eleven years of being a fifth line.*”

“*And about four of you have worked out that I have had it since Wednesday, and about nine of you have not, and I am not going to help either of those two groups.*”

Then a boy of thirteen was on that bank with his own count, and he gave it, and gave the two days a day apart again, and said he was not carrying six.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and eighteen days ago and keeps his own count of it. He has carried five counts on that bank since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week. He has told everybody he was going to carry six, and has not.

“*A hundred and eighteen days. A hundred and eighteen is seven hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*I gave you two ninth days a week ago.*”

“*They are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week, or the Friday of it. A man of sixty-one has not written either of them down, and I am not going to ask him which one he prefers.*”

“*Nobody has asked me twice which of the two it is and about four of you wanted me to be asked twice.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she was standing in front of, and it was longer this morning than it has been.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. On the Thursday of a week ago she said out loud that she wants a day with nothing against it, and she has not withdrawn it.

“*Tell me what you are standing in front of and I will tell you what I am.*”

“*Two people three miles and six hundred yards up a cart road who are not here, and a man who keeps a book who is not here, and a woman who came two days who said she would not be here when anybody comes back down that road and is not here, and me.*”

“*And two things on a counter that have gone on being true without any of us, which is the only reason I am still standing at it.*”

“*I asked on the Thursday of a fortnight for a day with nothing against it, and about four of you have been waiting nine days for somebody to hand me one, and nobody has, and I have not asked again.*”

“*I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the want first, and the mistake after it, and the cost last, and none of the three was about the road.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seventeen days, two hundred and seventeen being seven hundred and seventy-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-six days old, eighty-six being seven hundred and seventy-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and it is not about the road.*”

“*I want a man of thirty-eight to say that thing out loud this morning and not on Monday or Thursday or whenever he has decided suits it.*”

“*And I am not going to ask him for it, and I am not going to be the one who decides the morning.*”

“*I have written that sentence down in my own head and I am saying it out loud, so that somebody here can check it against the day.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is two days old.*”

“*On Wednesday morning two people went up that road and I have spent two mornings waiting to be told what they found, and I did not want to know what they found. I wanted the finding to come back down that road to me.*”

“*That is the same mistake I have been making since the Wednesday of a fortnight and I have not got round it and I am not going to improve on it this morning.*”

“*Seventy-one days. Seventy-one is seven hundred and seventy-one less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*And here is the cost. A man of thirty-eight has had a thing in his own head since Wednesday and so have I, and neither of us is going to say it, and about nine of you are on a bank this morning instead of on a road.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with the water one day back from the top step and a road four miles long that nobody had come down.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seventeen days.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read said the fiftieth morning of asking for a line to be read to him in the open, and gave a second figure of his own for the first time. Seventy-three days is a coat and a board and not a thing anybody is waiting for.

And a man of thirty-eight gave the count of that sheet, sixty-one, and said two people went up a cart road yesterday and neither of them is on that bank and nobody has come down that road.

And a man of about fifty-two said he has had one thing in those four leaves that he has not said out loud in about eleven years of being a fifth line, and that he had it by Wednesday, and that he is not saying it this morning.

And a boy of thirteen gave his own count of a hundred and eighteen days, and gave the two ninth days out loud again a week after the first time, and said he was not going to ask which of the two he preferred.
