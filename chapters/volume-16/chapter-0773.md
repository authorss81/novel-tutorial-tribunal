# Chapter 773: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Fever Was Seventy Weeks Old, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Says Out Loud What Has Been Going On Here For Sixty-Eight Mornings

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twenty-third morning. Three hundred and seventy-three days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks old. Three hundred and twenty-seven days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and said that the bank will be full tomorrow and that it is the last day of a week before anything else happens.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and no fourth. He has counted thirteen of them off and has said he is not going to work out the first five, and he has now said that on three mornings running and about four of them have stopped mentioning it to him.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is three days of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and the bank will be full tomorrow, which is the Sunday of this week.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in five days from this morning, and the bank being full tomorrow does not change that figure, and I am saying so because about four of you add them together.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-second morning, and he asked out loud first, and he said one thing about the ninth line that he had not said before.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not missed one of them. He asked out loud before anybody speaks, and the man of thirty-eight said yes out loud before he read any of it, and neither of them said anything about the other one doing it.

“*Fifty-two mornings.*”

“*Fifty-two is one more than fifty-one.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two told you yesterday that there is no day against that line, and I have had it read to me every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, and I did not know that until yesterday and I am not going to say what I did with the fifty-one of them.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me about it and I am not going to say one word about it until somebody does.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day out loud, and gave the count of that sheet first, and said that reading a line and reading a day are now one job and he is not going to separate them again.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and he has said a different reason every morning this week for not opening a coat, and he said on Wednesday that he would not say which of them he started with.

“*Sixty-three is seven hundred and seventy-three less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and a woman of sixty-four asked for that to be said out loud every morning nine days ago and was given one morning of it, and I am the one who gives it and that is a job now and it was not one on Monday.*”

“*A man of fifty-four asked me for a line that has no day on it fifty-one times before yesterday and I read it to him every time and I did not know what I was reading.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The reason is that I would have to say out loud this morning that I did not know what I had been reading, and about four of you would have heard it and about nine of you would not, and I have not decided which of those two things I can stand.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, in the order she gives them, and then gave a third thing, which is a count of mornings and has the working with it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to. She is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and neither is that book, and she has said so out loud on that bank more than once.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-nine mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-three mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three, and this is the one I have been sitting on since Wednesday, and I gave the count of it yesterday and I am giving the number of it this morning.*”

“*I have been counting since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, and that is sixty-eight mornings.*”

“*Sixty-eight is seven hundred and seventy-three less seven hundred and five, and I counted it on the counter with a pencil and a crumb of bread and not in that book.*”

“*Here is what I have counted. On each of those sixty-eight mornings, about nine people stood on this bank and every one of them said a thing out loud in front of the others.*”

“*And on each of those sixty-eight mornings, not one of the nine of them was asked one question by anybody on it.*”

“*That is the whole of the count. A man of fifty-four has said the second half out loud himself since the Saturday of last week. A woman of sixty-four has said it since the Thursday of a week ago. A woman of forty-four has said it six mornings running and has not been asked about it.*”

“*Here is what I am not going to say. I am not going to say what that is, because a woman of about fifty-two got to it yesterday and she is the fifth line of two sheets of deal and I keep chairs.*”

“*And I am not going to say what should be done about it, and I want somebody in about nine people’s hearing to notice that I have not said that.*”

Then a boy of thirteen was on that bank and gave his own count, and said the two ninth days one more time because nobody asked him, and then said he would not say them again.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and twenty days ago and keeps his own count of it, and he has carried five counts on that bank since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and has told everybody he was going to carry six, and has not carried six.

“*A hundred and twenty days. A hundred and twenty is seven hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has just counted sixty-eight mornings of people saying things out loud and nobody asking anybody anything.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two has told you the finding was held open for about nine days about a line that has no day on it.*”

“*That is nine and sixty-eight and one hundred and twenty and I have not added them and I am not going to.*”

“*The two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it, and nobody has asked me for them and I have said them out loud three times now, and I am not going to say them a fourth time without being asked.*”

“*Nobody has asked me twice. Nobody is going to be thanked for me carrying five and not six.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she is standing in front of, and it is a different list from the one she gave yesterday, and she gave the reason for the difference.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. She said on the Thursday of a fortnight ago that she wants a day with nothing against it, and she has not asked for it again.

“*Yesterday I gave you a list of about nine of us who have not been asked anything.*”

“*This morning I am standing in front of a woman of sixty-four and a man of fifty-four and a woman of about fifty-two, and none of the three of them is on that line, and the one of them who is on it has not been asked anything and has not asked for anything either.*”

“*That is a different list and I am giving it because the first one was about people standing on a bank and this one is about a name in ink four hundred miles off, and I have spent eleven years asking for days against names and a name is not a person on a bank.*”

“*And I am not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and about four of you have been waiting two mornings for me to say it now that I have said all of that, and you are going to keep waiting.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he has been on this bank sixty-eight mornings being the thing about four people come up to, and said the thing he wants, and it is the first want in about three days that is not about a sentence.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and nineteen days, two hundred and nineteen being seven hundred and seventy-three less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-eight days old, eighty-eight being seven hundred and seventy-three less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want. I want the sixty-eight to stop on Monday.*”

“*Not to stop for a week. I want the sixty-eight to stop on the Monday of this week, and I want a woman of forty-four to be the one who says it stops, and I am not going to be the one who says it and I am not going to ask her to.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is three days old and it is the same one for the fourth time in about nine days.*”

“*I have been the thing about four people come up to for two hundred and nineteen days, and about four of you came up on the Friday of the hundred and thirty-first week to say what I am, and what I have been doing every morning since is being the reason about nine of you are on this bank.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has just counted sixty-eight mornings of that. And I have not been asked one question in any of the sixty-eight, and neither has anybody else, and I have been the one telling it to be the way it is.*”

“*Seventy-three days. Seventy-three is seven hundred and seventy-three less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer and this morning it is not an answer to a woman of forty-four with a pencil and a crumb of bread.*”

“*And the cost is that I wanted it to stop on Monday, and a woman of forty-four is the one who would have to say it stops, and I have just told about nine of you which mouth I want that to come out of, and about four of you have heard me say it and about nine of you have not.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with the water three days back from the top step and sixty-eight mornings of it behind them.

---

The fever was seventy weeks old. He had been in that county two hundred and nineteen days.

A woman of forty-four counted sixty-eight mornings on that counter with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and said that on every one of them about nine people said a thing out loud in front of the others and not one of the nine was asked one question by anybody.

And she said what she was not going to say, which is what that is, because a man of about fifty-two got to it the day before and she keeps chairs.

And a boy of thirteen said the two ninth days out loud for the third time without being asked for them, and gave his own count of a hundred and twenty days, and said he is not going to say them a fourth time.
