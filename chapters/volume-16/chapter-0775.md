# Chapter 775: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Monday, And A Man Of Sixty-One Walks Four Miles Down That Cart Road And Comes Back With No Day And With A Question He Could Not Answer

Seventh day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twenty-fifth morning. Three hundred and seventy-five days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and two days. Three hundred and twenty-nine days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the second morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and said he had noticed on the Saturday that about four of them had stopped saying the working back at him.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and no fourth. He said on the Saturday of a week ago that he had not noticed, and he has now said that he noticed on the Saturday, which is the same thing said twice and he knows it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the second morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off. Four inches a day.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in three days from this morning, which is the Thursday of this week.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-fourth morning, and he asked out loud first, and said that he is not going to be asked about the fifth thing and that he is saying that so that nobody has to work out whether to do it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not missed one of them. He has asked out loud before anybody speaks since the Tuesday of a fortnight ago. He has been asked nothing by anybody on that bank for about seven weeks.

“*Fifty-four mornings.*”

“*Fifty-four is one more than fifty-three.*”

“*A boy of thirteen said on Sunday that somebody should ask me about the fifth thing, and I am saying out loud this morning that nobody has to, and that is me refusing to be asked and not a man of thirty-one refusing it for me.*”

“*I have not been asked anything by anybody on this bank for about seven weeks and that is a notice and not a want, and I have said it every morning since the Friday of a week ago and I am not going to stop.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day, and gave the count of that sheet, and then said that about four of them had asked him this morning whether the man of sixty-one was on the road, and that he is not going to answer for the road.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He has given a different reason every morning this week for not opening a coat. He said on Saturday that he had stopped being able to tell which of his own reasons was true.

“*Sixty-five is seven hundred and seventy-five less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week.*”

“*About four of you have asked me twice this morning whether a man of sixty-one is on that road, and I am not going to answer for a road. I read lines. I do not read roads and I never could and about four of you have known that since the Wednesday of a fortnight.*”

“*I am still not opening this coat. There is a board in the inside of it with thirty-two notches cut in the edge and it has been there seventy-seven days, and I have counted it myself again and nobody is going to be thanked for it.*”

Then a man of sixty-one came down four miles of that cart road on foot, alone, and got to that bank after the seventh hour, and gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and then said what he had gone up there for and what he had come back with.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wanted that hearing to stand and said so out loud on eleven mornings before he went up that road. He said on the Friday of last week that he was not coming back down it that day and was not going to promise to be there the next morning, and he has been off this bank for four mornings.

“*I am sixty-one and I walked four miles on a Sunday and I came down this morning because the bread does not come down on a Monday and there was no cart.*”

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and forty-five days, and that is seven hundred and seventy-five less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and forty-six days, and that is seven hundred and seventy-five less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-one days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and thirty-three, and that is seven hundred and seventy-five less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other and I have not added a day to them, and I have given those four figures every morning for about seven years and I have given them this morning four mornings late.*”

“*And here is the one I have never said, and I am saying it because a woman of forty-four counted sixty-eight mornings on Saturday and because I have been off this bank for four of them.*”

“*A man of thirty-four has a day against his name on that sheet and I have kept a book of heads and carts for eleven years, and in eleven years I have never once been sent for about him, and he has never once been asked anything by anybody.*”

“*There is a woman of sixty-four with a name in ink on a line that has never had a day against it and I have been able to be sent for about her since the day she came out of my book nineteen years ago, and I have not.*”

“*I am not giving a reason for that. I said on the Wednesday of a week ago that I was not going to give another one and I am not going to give one this morning.*”

“*And here is what I went up for. I went up to a low gate about two hundred yards below an oven, three miles and six hundred yards up that road, to ask about four houses which day their second cart can come down.*”

“*I got there at about the second hour of the afternoon. A woman came out to the gate and she did not ask me who I was and she did not ask me where I was from.*”

“*She asked me which day I had been told the cart could come down.*”

“*And I am a man who keeps a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and I could not answer her, and I stood at a gate three miles and six hundred yards up that road and said I do not know.*”

“*I have wanted that hearing to stand for about a fortnight and I have said it eleven mornings out loud and I am saying it this morning, and a woman at a gate asked me a question about a cart and I had nothing.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for four miles and nobody is going to be told I went up that road for the hearing.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, and said that the man of sixty-one is four mornings late and that she gave them anyway.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She counted sixty-eight mornings on Saturday with a pencil and a crumb of bread and said she was not going to say what to do about them.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-one mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-five mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And a man of sixty-one has been four mornings late and I gave both of those this morning anyway, because neither of them needs him here and that is the whole of what I have ever said those two counts for.*”

“*I am not going to say what he should do with what he has brought back down that road. He has not asked me and I have not offered him a figure.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she is standing in front of this morning, and it is the shortest list she has given, and she said why.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. On the Sunday she said out loud that a name with no day on it is a day with no name on it and that she has wanted both of them for eleven years. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them.

“*A man of sixty-one has just said that a woman at a gate asked him a question and he had nothing.*”

“*That is the third person on this bank in four days who has been asked nothing by anybody and got nothing from it, and the other two are a man of fifty-four and a woman of sixty-four, and I am the fourth, and I have been asking for a day with a name on it for eleven years.*”

“*I am not going to say which three of the five have a day against them. I have said no four times now in about eleven days and about four of you have counted them and I know you have counted them.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for standing here on the Monday of a week about four of you have been waiting nine days for.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that a man of sixty-one coming back with nothing is the thing he asked for on Saturday and did not get, and gave the working for it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-one days, two hundred and twenty-one being seven hundred and seventy-five less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety days old, ninety being seven hundred and seventy-five less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and it is the first want in four days that I have been able to finish.*”

“*I want a man of sixty-one to be standing on that bank having been asked a question and not answered it, four mornings after he said he would not promise to be here.*”

“*And he is. And I have wanted that since Saturday, and on Saturday I said I did not want anybody to go up that road and I said the reason was about me, and both of those things are true and I have not got round either of them.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is five days old.*”

“*I said on Friday that I wanted a woman of forty-four to be the mouth the sixty-eight mornings stop out of. This morning the mouth it almost stopped out of was a woman at a gate three miles and six hundred yards up a cart road, and she is not in this county, and I do not know her name.*”

“*I have been standing on this bank for two hundred and twenty-one days being the thing about four people come up to.*”

“*And the thing that about four people come up to has just been shown to be a person at a gate who was not sent for, and I did not send for her, and neither did anyone else here.*”

“*Seventy-five days. Seventy-five is seven hundred and seventy-five less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the cost is that a man of sixty-one came back with the question he could not answer, and about nine of you have already started working out what it means, and he is not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be thanked for wanting him to have it.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with a full water behind them and a man of sixty-one at the end of it who had walked four miles to be asked a question and had nothing.

---

The fever was seventy weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-one days.

A man of sixty-one came down four miles of that cart road on foot and said a woman at a gate asked him which day he had been told the cart could come down, and that he is a man who keeps a book of heads and carts and he could not answer her.

And he said the one thing he has never said, which is that a man of thirty-four with a day against his name has never once been sent for by anybody.

And neither has a woman of sixty-four with a name in ink on a line that has never had a day.

And a man of sixty-one said a woman at a gate three miles and six hundred yards above this landing asked him a question, and that he is sixty-one and he had nothing, and that he walked four miles to be asked it.
