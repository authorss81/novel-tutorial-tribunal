# Chapter 785: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Thursday, And A Full Bank On The Fourth Morning Of It, And He Asks Nine People For Something And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Puts The Word Provisional In Her Own Mouth

Third day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Three hundred and eighty-five days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and five days. Three hundred and thirty-nine days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the fourth morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and said he had held the end of nothing for three mornings running.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He offered on Monday to hold the far end of anything about nine of them measured, and nobody has taken him up since.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the fourth morning of it.*”

“*Sixteen until it comes off. That is the whole of the rule and four inches a day is what is underneath it, and I do not have a second rule.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in one day from this morning, which is the Friday of this week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and I am not going to be asked to hold the end of anything tomorrow when it comes off.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-fourth morning. He asked out loud first, and said the thing he had been not asking for.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of this week and about four of them have been treating him differently since, and he has given them that figure a week.

“*Sixty-four mornings.*”

“*Sixty-four is one more than sixty-three, and that is the working.*”

“*I am going to start asking about the fifth thing again on Monday, and I am saying that on a Thursday so that about four of you have the weekend.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and gave the count of that sheet, and said five mornings running of the number, and gave a reason for the first time.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He said on the Friday of a week ago that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open, and the only reason he gave was that a man of fifty-four asks him to.

“*Four names. Five mornings running, and I have not said why I say it out loud before today.*”

“*It is not only that a man of fifty-four asks me to do things in the open. It is that a man of fifty-four who cannot read is the only person on that bank who cannot check the paper himself, and about nine of you can, and none of you has.*”

“*That is the reason and I have been not giving it for five mornings, and it is not a good reason and he has got a better one than I have.*”

“*Seventy-five is seven hundred and eighty-five less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-seven days, and eighty-seven is seven hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

Then he asked nine people for something, and about four of them gave it, and about four of them gave it with a piece taken out of it, and one of them gave him the word.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-one days, two hundred and thirty-one being seven hundred and eighty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is a hundred days old, a hundred being seven hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*I am asking for consent to go on walking the ground of those three things, and I am asking for it in about nine people’s hearing, and I am asking for it out loud so that it is a thing that happened.*”

“*That is the whole of the ask and there is no paper on it and there is no room on anything and there is no line with a name on it.*”

“*I am not asking anybody to sign it and I am not asking anybody to be in it later.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said the word, and said what the word has to have in it to be worth anything, and about nine of them heard a definition instead of a permission.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them. She said one name out loud once on the Thursday of last week and has asked for nothing since, and this morning she said a word.

“*Provisional.*”

“*That is the word for it, and it is not mine and I am not going to pretend I found it.*”

“*Provisional means I can take it back. Not in a week. In a morning, and the morning after that, and any morning until somebody stops asking me.*”

“*And it means that if I take it back, he has to do something about it by a day, and I am the one who names that day, and if he cannot do it by then he is not doing the thing.*”

“*That is all I have. I have been out of every book in this county for nineteen years and I am not going to be the woman who says yes to a thing and then cannot get out of it.*”

Then a woman of forty said that was not consent, and said what she has been asking for instead, and gave the whole of it in six sentences.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She asked him on the Sunday for the ground of three things and she has watched him walk two of them.

“*A thing you can withdraw from is a permission, and I have not asked anybody for a permission in eleven years.*”

“*I have asked for a day. A day is a day and it is the same for everybody and nobody is holding the other end of it.*”

“*I am not going to give him consent this morning and I am not going to say no either, because I have not finished asking.*”

“*About four of you have said the word before I have and about four of you have waited to hear whether I was going to object, and I am not going to be the woman who objects, because objecting is a thing you do at the end of an argument and I have not got an argument.*”

“*What I am going to do is keep asking for the day. He can go on walking with or without my consent and I will be on this bank either way at the ninth hour.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight gave his consent with a piece taken out of it in front of about nine of them, and said what the piece was.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight. He read that sheet forty times in this county. He has said the number of names he can see on the ninth line out loud five mornings running.

“*I give it for the turn at four hundred yards and for the two pieces of ground at the house, and I have walked neither and I have not been asked to walk either.*”

“*And I do not give it for the ninth line, and I am going to say why in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*There are four names on the ninth line and I can see them and I have said the number five mornings running, and there has never been a day against that line.*”

“*I am not going to be the reason a map of this ground reaches those four names. That is all. I have said it and I am not going to improve on it.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two gave his consent and said what he would do with it every morning, and said the thing in the four leaves is still not said.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. He can be checked against the four lines under him every morning.

“*I give it, and I am going to give it in the shape of a thing I can be caught out on.*”

“*Every morning I will say out loud whether the figures he gave me still check against the four lines under me, and if one of them does not, I will say so before he uses it.*”

“*That is not a form and it is not a column and I do not want my name on anything.*”

“*And I have still not said the thing in those four leaves and I have said that out loud five mornings now, and about nine of you have not asked me for it once.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said what consent is not, and said she is not giving one.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread and not in that book.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-one mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-five mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty mornings, and eighty is seven hundred and eighty-five less seven hundred and five, and that is a round figure and I have had a week to look at it and I am going to give it anyway.*”

“*About four of you have given a man of thirty-one something to do with your Saturday this week, and I have not, and I am not going to say why in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*I keep chairs. I am not on any line in this county and there is nobody four hundred miles off who has ever heard about me, and a woman of sixty-four has just told us what that is worth this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to say what to do about the eighty. I have said that since the Saturday of a fortnight.*”

Then he gave the cost out loud, and a man of about fifty-two told him it was not a mistake, and he said it was.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one. He asked nine people for something this morning and about four of them said yes because he asked them.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and I am not going to pretend it is bigger than it is.*”

“*I want a woman of forty to change her mind about permission before the week is out. She will not, and she said why on Wednesday, and she said it better than I could have.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is a week old and it is the third time I have made it in this county.*”

“*I said on the Wednesday of this week that everything said on that bank was said because of me, and that cost me something and nobody went down the bank.*”

“*This morning nine people were asked a question by me and about four of them said yes, and they said yes because I asked them, and I have just done the thing I said I had got wrong.*”

“*A man of thirty-one is not a thing about four people come up to. A man of thirty-one is a person who asks, and what comes back from that is a thing that happened because he asked.*”

“*Eighty-five days. Eighty-five is seven hundred and eighty-five less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer to consent.*”

“*And the cost is that a man of thirty-eight took a piece out of it in front of about nine of you, and a woman of forty would not give it, and I am going to go on walking anyway, and that is what a permission is.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for saying yes and nobody is going to be told I was brave about asking.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-second morning, and said he has written two numbers in eleven years and is not writing a third.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-two mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-five days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-six days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-one days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-three, and that is seven hundred and eighty-five less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have two numbers in that book and I have had them for about eleven years and I am not writing a third one this week.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-second morning I have said it, and about four of you have said yes to something this morning and none of you has said it about that.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the tenth hour of the third morning of that week, with a full bank behind them on the fourth morning of it, and about four of them had said a word that a woman of sixty-four defined in front of all of them.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-one days.

He asked nine people for consent and the word came out of a woman of sixty-four, and she said provisional means she can take it back and names the day.

A man of thirty-eight gave consent with a piece taken out of it, and the piece was the ninth line and four names.

Eleven years of asking that has not once been answered by a person, and a woman of forty who would not give a man of thirty-one her consent and would not refuse it either.
