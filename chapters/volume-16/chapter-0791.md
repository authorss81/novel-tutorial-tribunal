# Chapter 791: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fifth Week, And A Wednesday, And A Full Bank On The Second Morning Of It, And He Opens A Case Against The Bench And Says Out Loud That The Record He Has Will Not Carry It

Second day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. His five hundred and forty-first morning. Three hundred and ninety-one days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks and four days. Three hundred and forty-five days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank stood about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, and it was the second morning of it sitting there, and the top step and the second one were both under.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning and a fourth when he has one. He gave the count of days back with the reading this morning, because about four of them have begun writing the figure down before he says it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is five days since it came off.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at that figure until it goes, and four inches a day is the working underneath the holding, and I have not got a fourth thing this morning.*”

“*Those ninety steps can be walked again in three days, and that is the Saturday of this week, and it will be the sixteenth time.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, seventy mornings running, and he asked out loud first and then asked the other thing.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He announced on the Thursday of last week that he would start asking about the fifth thing on the Monday, and he did, and about four of them did not answer him.

“*Seventy mornings.*”

“*Seventy is one more than sixty-nine, and I gave the last of them on the first morning of this week, and this is the second.*”

“*And I asked about the fifth thing yesterday in the ordinary voice the way I said I would, and about four of you did not answer me, and I am not going to ask it a third time this week.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and gave the count of the sheet and the number of names on it, and said what the coat is doing.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He has said the number of names out loud every morning since the Friday of last week, and gave the reason for it once.

“*Four names, and it is the eleventh morning, and I have not opened this coat in eleven of them.*”

“*Eighty-one is seven hundred and ninety-one less seven hundred and ten, and I read the ninth and not the sheet, because a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth and nobody has asked me for another since the Tuesday before last.*”

“*The board has been in here ninety-three days, and ninety-three is seven hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and ninety-eight, and about four of you have stopped asking me about it and I have noticed that too.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts off that counter and said what she is not going to do about them, and a man of thirty-one opened a case against a thing four hundred miles off and gave away what it cannot be built out of.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and it is in her own hand on the back of her hand and not in that book.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it has been on that counter forty-seven mornings, and there is nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing have been on that counter forty-one mornings, and there is a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-six mornings of that bank, and eighty-six is seven hundred and ninety-one less seven hundred and five, and that is a figure of this bank and not of anything that happened anywhere else.*”

“*And I said on the first morning of this week that what to do about those figures is somebody else’s morning, and I am not saying it again this week, and nobody is going to be thanked for my having said it once.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-seven days, two hundred and thirty-seven being seven hundred and ninety-one less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and six days old, a hundred and six being seven hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*I am opening a case this morning against the thing that hears cases, and I am doing it on a bank, and about nine of you are on it, and none of that is the case.*”

“*A case is a figure of an argument.*”

“*It is two people and a thing one of them wants and the other one will not give.*”

“*That is the whole of it and I am not going to dress it up this morning.*”

“*What I want is for the Bench that has been sitting on this county’s business for nineteen years to be made to answer for what it has done to the record.*”

“*I am saying that out loud on a bank because that is the only place I have got.*”

“*And here is what I have got to build it out of, and I am giving all of it before I use any of it.*”

“*One: a sheet with three columns and nothing in them. Two: four leaves with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*Three: nine days on the fourth line of a paper, for any person heard about, which run out whenever they run out.*”

“*Four: a ninth line that has never had a day against it, and four names on it.*”

“*Five: six on a leaf, which is the second number in eleven years, and a man of sixty-one wrote it because a stranger said six out loud, and he does not believe it.*”

“*Six: a woman of thirty-four who stood in that room, walked two days, stood at the end of a bank for six days being looked at, was asked nothing, and is not coming on the Monday.*”

“*Seven: a woman of sixty-four who was in that room and was told she could not attend, and who has asked not to be asked twice.*”

“*Eight: a road four miles up that cart road with about four houses on it that cannot come on a day.*”

“*And a man of sixty-one who wants that hearing to stand and cannot tell a woman at a gate which day it is.*”

“*Nine: a man of fifty-four who cannot read, who has asked for that line to be read to him in the open for seventy mornings.*”

“*And about four of you have started counting how long he has waited instead of reading it.*”

Then he said the part that is the reason the case is going to fail, and he said it before anybody asked him for it.

“*That is nine things and not one of them is a person, and I have been building on them for ten days and about four of you helped me.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for.*”

“*There is nobody here I can send anybody to about any of the nine.*”

“*And that is not a figure of this bank. It is the whole of the county.*”

“*I have been saying it since the Saturday of a fortnight and I am saying it again because today I am using it.*”

“*A man of thirty-four in an ash has a day against his name and I have never asked him one question.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine in that reed has no day, and nobody can be sent for about her.*”

“*A woman of thirty-four in a room four hundred miles off cannot be asked at all.*”

“*And a case that cannot be served is not a case that has been lost.*”

“*It is a case that has not been able to start.*”

“*And I am not going to stand on that bank and tell about nine of you that I have lost, because I have not had it.*”

Then he gave the want and the mistake and the cost, in that order, because he has given them in that order for a week and about four of them have started waiting for them.

“*The want is the same one and it is about six words long. I want somebody to be asked who can say no to me and come back the next morning and be asked again.*”

“*The mistake is the seventh of the same mistake and it is one day old.*”

“*I said on the first morning of this week that I do not know what any of it is.*”

“*Then I spent the whole of yesterday finding out what it was, and what it was, is a case I cannot serve.*”

“*The cost is that I have said nine things out loud in the order I got them, on a bank, to about nine people.*”

“*Any one of those nine can be taken back by the person it belongs to.*”

“*And about four of them can be taken back by somebody standing four hundred miles off who does not know that I said it.*”

“*Ninety-one days. Ninety-one is seven hundred and ninety-one less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave four counts and said the twenty-eighth morning, and said that he is going to stop saying what he wants, and said why in one line.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He has said out loud every morning for twenty-eight mornings running that he wants that hearing to stand.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash a hundred and sixty-one days, and that is seven hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed a hundred and sixty-two days, and that is seven hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-seven days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-nine, and that is seven hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*And I have said I want that hearing to stand twenty-eight mornings running, and I am not going to say it again this week, and that is not because I have stopped wanting it.*”

“*It is because about four of you have started answering me about it, and a man who wants a thing and is being argued into it is not a man who wants it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own count and said what he is not going to do, and a woman of forty asked him one question on the bank in front of everybody.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and thirty-eight days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of last week, and held nineteen minutes at the top of ninety cut steps on the Friday of last week. He told a man of thirty-eight on the first morning of this week that nobody had asked him for anything in ten days.

“*A hundred and thirty-eight days, and I counted them in the lane and not on his fingers, and it is one more than it was on Tuesday.*”

“*I have not put a seventh count down and I said on Tuesday that I was not going to start asking anybody for anything, and I have not, and I am not going to this week either.*”

“*And I am not going to be told that I was brave about not asking, because that is the other half of it and nobody says that half.*”

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She came up that cart road on the Monday to ask a woman in a reed a question about ground and got one word, and nobody followed her.

“*I am going to ask you the ninth one on your list, and I am going to ask it in front of these people, and I have thought about it for two days.*”

“*The woman of thirty-four. You said she stood in that room and was asked nothing and is not coming on the Monday. What is her name.*”

Ilyan Vester did not answer it for a while, and then he gave an answer that was not an answer, and said the reason out loud before he gave it.

“*I do not know her name. I know a figure and an age and that she walked two days, and I have not got a name and I have not asked anybody for one in about nine weeks.*”

“*And I am not going to get one this morning.*”

“*That is the seventh of the same mistake and it is the worst one I have made.*”

“*I opened a case this morning on nine things and the ninth of them has no name in it.*”

“*I am not going to be relieved of that and nobody is going to be thanked for asking me for it in front of about nine of them.”*

---

The fever was seventy-two weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-seven days.

A man of thirty-one opened a case against a bench four hundred miles off on a bank, and gave away nine things before he used any of them, and said that the record he has will not carry it.

He said a case that cannot be served is a case that has not started, and he did not stand on that bank and tell anybody he had lost one.

A woman of forty asked him for the name of the ninth thing on his own list, in front of about nine people, and he said he did not have it.
