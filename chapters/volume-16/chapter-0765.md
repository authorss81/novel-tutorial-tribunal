# Chapter 765: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Friday, And A Woman Who Keeps The Bakehouse Four Miles Up That Cart Road Comes Down With Fifteen Or Fourteen, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Wants To Go Up That Road And Says He Is Not Going To

Fourth day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and fifteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-five days after the settlement. The fever sixty-eight weeks and six days. Three hundred and nineteen days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about twelve inches over the top step at about the seventh hour. It was three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the bank will be full tomorrow.

Corvin Tace is forty-three. He has kept that landing nineteen years, and he gives three things every morning and has never added to them. On Tuesday he said a day of the week the other way round from the way he said it on Monday, and about four of you told him he had it wrong.

“*About twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is three days of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day.*”

“*I have given that figure four mornings running and I am not going to stop giving it on the morning it comes out right.*”

“*The bank will be full tomorrow, which is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*And tomorrow is the last time I say that for a fortnight.*”

“*The next time those ninety steps can be walked is five days from this morning.*”

Then a woman of forty-four came down that cart road from the bakehouse with a cloth over her arm. She came down on the bread cart because the bread cart comes down on a Friday, and nobody had asked her to come down.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps the bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses go up to it for bread. She walked that road twice in four days about a fortnight ago and said she was not going a third time in a fortnight.

“*I have come down because the bread comes down on a Friday and I am on the bread cart.*”

“*That is the whole of the reason and I am giving it first.*”

“*About four of you are standing there waiting for me to say something about a man of thirty-one.*”

“*I have worked out a figure on that road and it is fifteen or it is fourteen.*”

“*I have done it twice and both of them are right.*”

“*The four leaves came down that cart road on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. They were on my bread and I put them on the cart myself and nobody asked me to.*”

“*A boy of thirteen on that bank has given two ninth days a day apart. The Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week, or the Friday of it.*”

“*Fifteen days from the Thursday to the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week.*”

“*Fourteen days from the Friday to it.*”

“*I count days the way I count loaves and I have been counting since I was about eleven.*”

“*Both of those are right and I cannot get at which one it is.*”

“*Nobody in this county can be sent for about which of the two it is. I have not asked anybody and I am not going to ask anybody on the way back up.*”

Then the same woman said the thing about the road, which is about four houses and a gate, and gave the distance out loud, because the distance had never been said by anybody.

“*About four houses up that cart road have stopped sending the second of their two carts down it.*”

“*The second cart does not come the whole way down. It turns off at a low gate about two hundred yards below my oven.*”

“*That gate is three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road from this landing.*”

“*That is the first time that gate has been said out loud in about nine people’s hearing and I have been up and down that road for nineteen years and never once said where it is.*”

“*Those four houses did not stop because a hearing went against them.*”

“*They stopped because there is a day and nobody will tell them what day it is.*”

“*A cart cannot turn on a day nobody has said.*”

“*They have not asked me a question about it and I have not offered them a figure, and I am not going to be thanked for the gate.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-fourth morning. He asked out loud before anybody spoke, again.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and he holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one of them, and a woman of forty-four has been down from a bakehouse four miles up that cart road since Friday with a figure of fifteen or fourteen off a piece of bread.

Since Tuesday he has been asking for it before anybody else has said anything.

“*Forty-four mornings.*”

“*Forty-four is one more than forty-three.*”

“*A woman has just said fifteen or fourteen and both of them are right.*”

“*I have not got a figure of my own for either and I am not going to make one up to go beside it.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me and I would rather they did not.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-four and nobody is going to be told I was brave about asking out loud instead of waiting.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and gave his count with the working, and said the thing he is not going to do with the board in his coat.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. A woman of forty-four came down that cart road with a figure of fifteen or fourteen off a piece of bread, and he read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week.

“*Fifty-five is seven hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*A woman has given a figure of fifteen or fourteen off a piece of bread.*”

“*I have been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years and a fifth line is supposed to be checkable against the four lines under it every morning.*”

“*I cannot check fifteen or fourteen against anything and I am not going to stand up here and pick one to be the kind of man about the fifth line.*”

“*This coat has a board in the inside of it that has been there since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week. It has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*I am not going to open it in front of about nine of you this morning.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and refused a fourth thing about it for the fifth morning running.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought the four leaves down four miles on the bread and nobody asked her to.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-one mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter fifteen mornings, with a number on them and no room on them.*”

“*And the fourth thing I am not giving, for the fifth morning running.*”

“*A woman of forty-four has just told you there is a gate on that road. I have kept a book of chairs for nineteen years and I have never once written down where a gate is, and that is not modesty and it is not a principle.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and said he was still not going to write either of the two days a boy of thirteen gave.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one. He has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years, one of them six and written on the Sunday of a fortnight ago. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and thirty-five days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-five less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and thirty-six days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-five less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and eleven days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and twenty-three, and that is seven hundred and sixty-five less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*None of those has been added to any other and I am not going to start.*”

“*And a woman has come down that cart road this morning with a figure of fifteen or fourteen off her own bread, and I have not added that to anything either.*”

“*A day is neither a head nor a cart. It is not going in that book because two people counted it out loud on a bank, and I gave three reasons for that on Wednesday and I am not going to give a fourth one this morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the want and the reason he is not doing it. The reason this morning is not the reason he gave about nine days ago, and he said so out loud.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and eleven days, two hundred and eleven being seven hundred and sixty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty days old, eighty being seven hundred and sixty-five less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*The want, and I am putting it before the reason, because a woman of twenty-eight told me the order of those two is the whole of what I am.*”

“*I want to walk four miles up that cart road and stand at a low gate about two hundred yards below an oven and say a day out loud to about four houses.*”

“*And I am not going to.*”

“*And I am not going to give you the reason I gave about nine days ago, which was that about nine people in four counties would then have a man of thirty-one going up a road saying a thing about a day nobody has seen written down.*”

“*That reason was about nine of you and it is not the reason.*”

“*The reason is that a woman of forty-four has just told you there is a gate three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road, and about four houses turn off at it.*”

“*If I walk up there and say a day, then the thing that has happened is that a man of thirty-one said a day.*”

“*And it is a day he got off a piece of bread that a woman of forty-four carried down on a Friday.*”

“*That is a figure of one person in a county being the thing about four houses come up to. It is the same figure as this bank.*”

“*I have been on it for two hundred and eleven days and I am not carrying it four miles up a cart road.*”

“*Sixty-five days. Sixty-five is seven hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the cost is that a woman came four miles down on a bread cart to give me a figure to not use.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for not using it, and she is not going to be thanked for the four miles either.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with the water three days back from the top step, and a gate three miles and six hundred yards up a road behind them that had never been said out loud until that morning.

---

The fever was sixty-eight weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eleven days.

A woman of forty-four who keeps the bakehouse came down on the bread cart and gave a figure of fifteen or fourteen, working it both ways from the day the four leaves came down on her bread, and said she cannot get at which of the two it is and has not asked anybody.

And the same woman said where the second cart of about four houses turns off, which is a low gate about two hundred yards below her oven and three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road, and said that a cart cannot turn on a day nobody has said.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted to go up that road and stand at that gate and say a day out loud, and said he is not going to, and gave the reason, and said the reason he gave about nine days ago was about nine of them and was not the reason.

And a man of thirty-eight said he cannot check fifteen or fourteen against the four lines under him, and is not going to stand up and pick one to be the kind of man about the fifth line.
