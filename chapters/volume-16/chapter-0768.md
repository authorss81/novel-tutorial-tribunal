# Chapter 768: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Monday, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Who Was In That Room And Was Told She Could Not Attend Asks For One Thing, Which Is A Day To Be Said Out Loud Every Morning The Way The Water Is

Seventh day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and eighteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-eight days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks and two days. Three hundred and twenty-two days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the sixth morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and about four of them wrote two days down on Monday morning when he said Tuesday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gives three things every morning, and about four of you wrote Tuesday beside his figure on Monday morning, and he has said he is not going to say it a fourth time.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the sixth morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and four inches a day is the working underneath it.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow morning, which is the first day of a new week, and about four of you have written Tuesday beside it twice and I am not going to say it a fourth time.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-seventh morning, and he asked out loud first, and a man of thirty-eight read it, and neither of them said anything about it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read, and a woman of sixty-four asked for one thing on this bank this week and he has not stopped asking for a ninth line since. He has not missed one morning in about six weeks.

“*Forty-seven mornings.*”

“*Forty-seven is one more than forty-six.*”

“*A man of thirty-eight has not said anything about asking and he has not said anything about not asking, and that is two more mornings of him not saying anything, and I am not going to say thank you for either of them.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered and I have been asked nothing by anybody on this bank for about six weeks and I have said so out loud for four mornings and I am going to go on saying it.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said out loud that she was going to ask for something, and everybody on that bank stopped, and nobody had stopped for her in about three weeks.

Ada Renk is sixty-four and was in that room on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. She was asked whether she could attend and was told she could not. She has refused to say why. She asked not to be asked twice, and nobody on that bank has asked her.

“*I am going to ask for one thing and it is small, and I have been in this county nineteen years and I have watched about nine of you get up here every morning about one inch of water.*”

“*I want the day of that hearing said out loud on this bank every morning.*”

“*The Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. That is the day. A woman of sixty-four was in that room and six were in that room and nine were heard about and a woman of thirty-four walked two days and came after the ninth day.*”

“*And a day is worth an inch. That is the whole of what I am asking for. I am not asking for a column and I am not asking for a name and I am not asking anybody to write it down anywhere.*”

“*About nine of you have given a figure every morning since before I came down here and about nine of you have never once said a day out loud on that bank, and I have not asked anybody about it and I am not going to be asked twice.*”

“*And I am still not going to say why I could not attend and nobody is going to thank me for asking.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight said the count of that sheet with the working and said that he would read a day out loud every morning if somebody asked him to, and said the reason is different again from Tuesday and from Saturday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and on Monday a woman of sixty-four asked for one thing and he said yes out loud before he was asked.

“*Fifty-eight is seven hundred and sixty-eight less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*On Tuesday my reason was that a man of fifty-four should not be doing a job for me. On Saturday my reason was that a woman of thirty-four had said a thing about herself.*”

“*This morning my reason is that a woman of sixty-four has asked for one thing in about nine people’s hearing and about four of you are waiting to see whether anybody gives it to her.*”

“*I will read a day out loud every morning. I will read it because I am asked and I will say on the first morning that I have been asked, and if nobody asks me then I will not read it and I will not say why.*”

“*And I am still not opening this coat. There are thirty-two notches cut in the edge of that board and about four of you have been waiting for it for about nine weeks and a day is smaller than a board.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, and said the fourth thing about it again because about four of them had asked her to after Saturday.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to, and on Monday a man of sixty-one said he would not write down a day for a woman of sixty-four and she has not said whether she agrees with him.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-four mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter eighteen mornings, with a number on them and no room on them.*”

“*And the fourth thing, which I gave on Saturday and am giving again because about four of you asked me for it after Saturday.*”

“*The two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it. The day it was held is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. None of the three is in a book of chairs and all three are on the back of my own hand.*”

“*I am not going to give a fifth thing about that counter this morning. About four of you have been counting my refusals the way about four of you count a man of fifty-four’s mornings and I am not going to help you do it.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and then said he was not going to write down the day a woman of sixty-four has just asked for, and gave the reason, and it is not the reason he gave on Wednesday.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. A woman of sixty-four asked on Monday for a day to be said out loud and not written down, and he is the one man on that bank who could write it down and he is not going to.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and thirty-eight days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and thirty-nine days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and fourteen days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and twenty-six, and that is seven hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added a day to them either, and about four of you were waiting for me to.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four has asked for a day to be said out loud every morning, and she was careful to say not written down, and I am the one man on this bank who could write it down and I am not going to.*”

“*The reason on Wednesday was that a day is neither a head nor a cart. The reason this morning is different and it is that a day in a book is a day that stops being a day and becomes a line, and about four of you have been counting my lines for eleven years and have never once had to stand in front of anything.*”

“*I have not given a fourth reason about that counter and I am not going to give one this morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one gave the thing to one woman this morning and would not give it to tomorrow, and said the reason out loud, and it was the first time he had said a day out loud on that bank.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and fourteen days, two hundred and fourteen being seven hundred and sixty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-three days old, eighty-three being seven hundred and sixty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*Here is the want and it is about one person and it is the smallest one I have had since I came into this county.*”

“*I want a woman of sixty-four to have asked me for that and me to have been the one who gave it.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is that I nearly made it a promise, and I caught it, and about four of you heard me catch it.*”

“*It is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. That is the day the hearing was held, and six were in that room and nine were heard about.*”

“*And I am not going to say I will say it tomorrow, because I cannot be sent for about tomorrow and neither can about nine of you, and a thing one man promises on a Monday morning is a thing about one man and not about the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week.*”

“*Here is the cost. She asked for one thing in about nine people’s hearing and I gave her one morning of it, and she has been asked nothing for about three weeks and about four of you have been keeping off her, and I am one of the about four and I did not know I was until Saturday morning.*”

“*Sixty-eight days. Sixty-eight is seven hundred and sixty-eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank, and a man of thirty-one had said a day out loud for the first time on it, and a woman of sixty-four who had asked for one thing had been given one morning of it and neither of them was thanked.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and fourteen days.

A woman of sixty-four who was in that room and was told she could not attend asked for one thing out loud in the hearing of about nine of them, and it was that the day of that hearing be said out loud on that bank every morning, the way the water is.

And a man of thirty-one said the day, which is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and said he would not promise tomorrow because he cannot be sent for about tomorrow and neither can about nine of them.

And a man of thirty-eight said he would read a day out loud every morning if somebody asked him to, and would not say why if nobody did, and said that thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board is a bigger thing than a day.
