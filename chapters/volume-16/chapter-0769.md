# Chapter 769: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Tuesday, And A Man Of Sixty-One Says He Is Going Up That Cart Road Tomorrow, And A Woman Who Keeps The Bakehouse Says She Is Going Too And Says Why, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Worked Out Which Of About Four People Is Which On Saturday And Is Not Going To Say It

First day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and nineteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-nine days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks and three days. Three hundred and twenty-three days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the seventh morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and this morning the figure was one.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning, none of them added to any other and none of them about a night. He counted the last eight of the thirteen this morning and said he was not going to work out the first five in front of about nine of you.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the seventh morning of it.*”

“*A full bank holds at sixteen until it comes off, and the four inches a day is the working under it.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow morning, which is the Wednesday of this week, and that will be the thirteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*Eight days between that one and the one before it, and the last eight of the thirteen I have counted are eight days apart one from the next, and I am not going to work out the first five in front of about nine of you because I have not found out.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-eighth morning, and he asked out loud first, and the day was said out loud afterwards because about four of them had asked for it and about nine of them had not.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one, and he has been asking out loud since Tuesday of last week, before anybody speaks.

“*Forty-eight mornings.*”

“*Forty-eight is one more than forty-seven.*”

“*About four of you have started asking for the day and about nine of you have not and I have heard both of those things happen and I am not going to say which of you.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered and I have been asked nothing by anybody on this bank for about six weeks and that is four mornings of saying so out loud.*”

Then a man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and then read the day out loud, because somebody asked him to and he said on Monday that he would read a day if he was asked, and gave the count of that sheet first.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and on Monday he said he would read a day out loud every morning if somebody asked him to and would not say why if nobody did.

“*Fifty-nine is seven hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I have said it out loud twice because a man of thirty-one said on Monday that he would not promise tomorrow and about four of you wanted it out loud this morning.*”

“*I said on Monday I would read it if I was asked. I was asked and I read it and I am not going to say anything about it, because saying something about it would turn it into something about me.*”

Then a man of sixty-one said out loud that he is going up that cart road tomorrow morning, and gave the day, and nobody on that bank had asked him to.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years. He has written two numbers in it in eleven years, one of them six and written on the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He wants that hearing to stand and has said so every morning since the Saturday of a week ago.

“*Tomorrow morning, which is the Wednesday of this week, and I am going up on the bread cart because the bread goes up on a Wednesday.*”

“*I am going to stand at a low gate about two hundred yards below an oven, at three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road, and I am going to ask about four houses which day their second cart can come down.*”

“*That is all I am going to do and I have not decided what I am going to say when I get there and I am not going to decide it on this bank.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said on Friday that he was not going to walk up that road and gave a reason and the reason was about himself and not about about nine of you.*”

“*I am not answering to that. I am sixty-one and I keep a book and I have wanted one thing out loud since the Saturday of a week ago and this is the thing that thing is about.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said she is going up that cart road tomorrow as well, and gave the count of how many times she has been up it, and said it is her third time and a day, and nobody had asked her to go again.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and keeps the bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses go up to it for bread. She came down twice in four days about a fortnight ago and said she was not going a third time in a fortnight, and it is a day past the fortnight.

“*I am going up tomorrow and it is my third time and a day, and I said I would not go a third time in a fortnight and a fortnight is up, and I am not going to pretend to anybody on this bank that I kept to it out of manners.*”

“*I am going because it is my oven and my road and about nine houses come up it for bread, and the bread goes up on a Wednesday and I go with the bread.*”

“*And a man of sixty-one is going up on the same cart because a man of thirty-one would not, and about four of you have been standing there for about nine days waiting for somebody to go up that road.*”

“*I have not gone up because he did not. I am going up because I have a figure of fifteen or fourteen on the back of my own hand and about four houses that cannot turn a cart, and those are two things and only one of them has been said out loud.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and said that both of them go on while the two people who are going up that road are not here to see it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to, and two people went up that cart road this morning and she did not see them go and she did not ask.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-five mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter nineteen mornings, with a number on them and no room on them.*”

“*Those two counts are the only ones on this bank that do not need anybody to come here for them to go on being true, and about four of you have never once worked that out.*”

Then a woman of forty said the one thing she wants said out loud about tomorrow, and refused to be thanked for it in advance.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name. She has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. On Thursday she said out loud that she wants a day with nothing against it.

“*Two people are going up that cart road in the morning and I am not going to ask either of them to bring me back a day.*”

“*I asked for a day with nothing on it and I am not going to have one handed to me by a man of sixty-one on his way past a gate, because then it is a thing about a man of sixty-one and not about a day.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked in advance for saying that. Ask me again after they have been up there and come back down and I will say what I have then.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he worked out on Saturday which of about four people is which, and that he is not going to say it this morning, and gave the reason and it is not the reason anybody expected.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and fifteen days, two hundred and fifteen being seven hundred and sixty-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. He has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether the cut across his right palm will keep.

“*I want a thing and I am going to say it once.*”

“*I want about nine of you to be glad that two people are going up that cart road tomorrow, and I am not going to say that want out loud as glad, because I have said one thing this morning already and I am not going to say it twice.*”

“*Here is the mistake, and it is mine, and it is three days old.*”

“*On Friday morning about four of you came up that bank to say what I am. About four of you wanted me to go up that cart road and about four of you wanted me to say yes to a thing asked fifty-eight days ago.*”

“*On Saturday morning I worked out which of them was which. I have had it in my own head for three days and I have not said it and I am not going to say it this morning.*”

“*Here is the reason and it is not a good one and it is the only one I have got.*”

“*If I say it, then about four people on this bank start being the kind I said and about nine of you start being the kind I did not say, and that is two figures of people made out of one mouth before breakfast.*”

“*And then every morning after this one there would be about four of them and about nine of them, and I would have made them, and about four of them would be the figure and about nine of them would be the rest.*”

“*Sixty-nine days. Sixty-nine is seven hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer and this morning it is not an answer to a man of sixty-one going up a road.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank and two of them were going up a cart road in the morning, and neither of them was going because a man of thirty-one had been asked.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and fifteen days.

A man of sixty-one said he is going up that cart road tomorrow morning on the bread cart to ask about four houses which day their second cart can come down, and said a man of thirty-one not going is not an answer to him.

And a woman of forty-four who keeps that bakehouse said she is going up the same road and it is her third time and a day, and said she has not gone up because he did not, and said about four of you have been waiting about nine days for somebody to go.

And a man of thirty-one said he worked out on Saturday which of about four people who came up that bank on Friday is which, and is not going to say it, because saying it would make two figures of people out of one mouth before breakfast.
