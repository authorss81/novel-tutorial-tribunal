# Chapter 787: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Saturday, And The Fever Seventy-Two Weeks Old, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Asks Him For A Thing With A Day On It And He Says He Cannot Do It

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-seventh morning. Three hundred and eighty-seven days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks old. Three hundred and forty-one days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he said the whole of what a Saturday morning is for, in three things and a fourth.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He counted fifteen of them off yesterday and the sixteenth is in eight days and it will be a Saturday.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is one day of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in seven days from this morning, which is the Saturday of next week.*”

“*That is the sixteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it will be a Saturday, and I have said that yesterday and I am saying it twice because about four of you write things down on the day and not on the day after.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-sixth morning. He asked out loud first, and said he had said the same thing on Thursday and Friday and would say it on Monday.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week and about four of them have been treating him differently since, and he has given them that figure twice.

“*Sixty-six mornings.*”

“*Sixty-six is one more than sixty-five, and that is the working, and I said it on Thursday and on Friday and I am going to say it on Monday as well.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one is about nine feet from where I am standing and he has been about nine feet from where I am standing every morning this week.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day and gave the count of that sheet, and said the number of names, and said nothing else at all.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has said the number of names he can see on the ninth line out loud every morning since the Friday of last week, and on the Thursday of this week he gave the reason at last, and has not given it since.

“*Four names, and it is the seventh morning of saying it and the tenth day of nobody asking me for anything.*”

“*Seventy-seven is seven hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read it because a man of fifty-four asked, and I have said the number seven mornings running and I am not going to add anything to it.*”

“*The board has been in this coat eighty-nine days, and eighty-nine is seven hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and ninety-eight, and I am not opening it and I did not open it yesterday when ninety steps were off all night.*”

Then he said the two halves of yesterday out loud, and asked about nine of them to hold the wrong one, and they held it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He stood at the ninth opening under that bank from about the seventh hour to about the ninth hour yesterday and did not open it, and there were about ninety steps of water on it this morning.

“*Yesterday morning the water was off all ninety of those steps and I went down them and I stood at the ninth opening and I did not open it.*”

“*This morning the water is four inches back and there are about ninety steps of it on that cut and I could not open it if I wanted to.*”

“*Those are two different things and I am saying both of them at about the ninth hour on a Saturday in front of about nine of you.*”

“*And I know which one of the two about four of you are going to remember in about nine weeks, and it is going to be the second one, and I am asking about four of you not to.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four asked him for a thing with a day on it, and about nine of them heard the ask and about four of them had not known it was coming.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them. She has a name in ink on a line that has never had a day against it. She made herself askable on the Thursday of last week by saying one name out loud once, and she has asked for nothing since.

“*I have not asked for anything since the Thursday of last week and I am going to ask for something this morning.*”

“*I am not going to say why I could not attend and nobody is going to ask me and it is not what this morning is for.*”

“*I am asking him to put a day against my name.*”

“*That is all. A day. The way a woman of forty has wanted for eleven years, and the way about four of you have wanted in about nine weeks.*”

“*I know what I am asking. I have had a name in ink on that line for nineteen years and that line has never had a day against it, and I have known that since the Friday of the week before last, when a man of about fifty-two read a line out loud in front of about nine of you.*”

“*I am asking because nobody in this county is going to ask you and I am the first person in nineteen years that anybody here can ask anything, and I am not going to save it for a morning when I have got a better reason.*”

Then he said he could not do it, and gave the reason in four lines, and it was the same reason as the Sunday before.

“*I cannot do it.*”

“*A person being heard about has no day against it. The fifth of those four leaves says the persons heard about are about a line with names against it and a day against it, and there is no day on the ninth line, and there has never been one.*”

“*And the third of the three things I was asked to walk this week is a person being put in a room, and it is four hundred miles off, and it has a day, and the difference between that one and this one is about a mile of road I can walk.*”

“*I cannot make a day out of a thing that is not a person, and I have not tried to, and I have spent four days this week walking ground to find out that there is no ground under that line either.*”

“*And I said on the Saturday of a week ago that I wanted somebody to ask me for a thing with a day on it, and you have asked me, and about four of you have said nothing, and I am not going to let about four of you hear that as a thing I could have done differently.*”

“*Eighty-seven days. Eighty-seven is seven hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer to a day against a name.*”

“*And the cut across it is a hundred and two days old, a hundred and two being seven hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and I have asked twice that nobody say.*”

“*And the mistake is that I said on the Saturday that the shape of what I want is a form, and it is, and I have had two of them in three days and I can do neither.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of sixty-four stood up out of every book in this county nineteen years ago and said one name out loud once on the Thursday of last week so that somebody could ask her for something.*”

“*And she has spent it on me, and about nine of you watched her do it, and nobody is going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to be told she was brave about nineteen years.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she has been wanting to say for eleven years, and it took her nine sentences.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She asked him on the Sunday for the ground of three things, and he walked two of them in three days.

“*On Sunday I asked that man for eleven years of asking and not one foot of it, and I thought that was the worst thing that could happen to a question.*”

“*This morning a woman of sixty-four asked him for one day, out loud, in front of about nine of you, and he said he could not do it.*”

“*And here is what I have been wanting to say for eleven years and have not said, because every time I have got as far as it somebody has asked me a question and I have answered that instead.*”

“*Nobody has been cruel to me. That is what I want said. Nobody in this county has ever once been cruel to me about the asking.*”

“*Eleven years is a long time to be answered no by a piece of paper and not by a person.*”

“*I am not going to stop asking for the three. And I am going to go on asking for the one where it is a name, and about four of you have stopped expecting me to and I have noticed that too.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts, and said the twenty-fourth morning, and said he was not going to write down what a woman of sixty-four had asked for.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-four mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-seven days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-eight days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-three days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-five, and that is seven hundred and eighty-seven less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have two numbers in that book and I am not writing a third, and what a woman of sixty-four asked for this morning is not a number and it is not going in.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-fourth morning I have said it, and I have not asked any of you about it and I am not going to.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said what she is not going to say on the first morning of a week that has gone.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, and every morning of it she has counted with a pencil and a crumb of bread.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-three mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-seven mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-two mornings, and eighty-two is seven hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred and five.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four stood up on this bank on a Saturday morning and asked for one day and a man of thirty-one said he could not do it in front of about nine of us.*”

“*I am not going to say what I make of that, and I have not got a figure for it, and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

“*I have said what I am not going to say about the eighty-two since the Saturday of a fortnight and I am not going to say it at the end of a week that has gone the way this one has.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the tenth hour of the first morning of a week that had gone, and a woman of sixty-four who had spent the one thing she had made herself able to do.

---

The fever was seventy-two weeks old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-three days.

A woman of sixty-four who has a name in ink on a line that has never had a day asked for one day out loud on a Saturday morning.

And a woman of forty said that nobody in eleven years had ever once been cruel to her about the asking.

A man of thirty-one said he could not do it in four lines on that Saturday morning, and gave the reason that a person being heard about has no day against it.
