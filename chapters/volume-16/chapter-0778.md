# Chapter 778: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Thursday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Fourteenth Time, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says One Name Out Loud Once

Third day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and twenty-eighth morning. Three hundred and seventy-eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and five days. Three hundred and thirty-two days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night, for the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Thursday. The man of forty-three said the hour of it and the count of it and gave the day of the week on the word.

Then he said a thing he has never said.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He has counted fourteen of them off now. On the Tuesday of this week he said he had worked out the first five of the thirteen and did not believe his own figure, and he has not given another.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour this morning, and that is the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Thursday.*”

“*Eight days from the thirteenth, which was a Wednesday. The day-name moves one place every cycle, and I am giving you the sequence one more time.*”

“*About four of you have got two of the last three wrong.*”

“*Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday, and then it starts again on the Tuesday.*”

“*And I worked out the first five of the thirteen on the Tuesday of this week, and I gave you a figure, and I said I did not believe it.*”

“*I am not going to say what I believe now, and that is three things and then I stop.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-seventh morning. He asked out loud first, and said the one thing that had been standing behind the asking since the Friday of a week and a half ago.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. A woman of forty-four asked him the fifth thing yesterday and he answered it, and he has not been thanked and has not been told it was a good thing.

“*Fifty-seven mornings.*”

“*Fifty-seven is one more than fifty-six.*”

“*I was asked the fifth thing yesterday and I answered it, and about four of you have been treating me differently since.*”

“*I can see it. I am not going to thank any of you for it, and I am not going to be a person who is treated differently.*”

“*Here is what was standing behind the asking. I asked for that line every morning for about nine weeks because there was nothing on it to come on.*”

“*I did not have the words for that until Friday, and I have not told anybody until this morning.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be told I was brave about nine weeks.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day, and gave the count of that sheet in his own mouth.

Then he gave the figure he has counted in his own head for his own coat, and said he is leaving it alone.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He gave the precedent out loud yesterday morning, in about nine lines, and did not improve on it, and he has not said it again.

“*Sixty-eight is seven hundred and seventy-eight less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I have said it out loud on three mornings this week and I am not going to say it a fourth.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four has just said what was standing behind nine weeks of his own asking.*”

“*I have had a board in the inside of this coat since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week, and I counted it myself on Friday.*”

“*Eighty days. Eighty is seven hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

“*And eighty is the first round figure I have counted for anything, and I am going to leave it alone.*”

“*I am not opening this coat this morning. A woman of sixty-four is about nine feet from where I am standing, and she has not asked me to, and about four of you have.*”

“*I would rather she asked me.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said one name out loud, once, in the hearing of about nine of them, and did not say it twice.

Then she said what it had cost her to say it once.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago, and it cost her one thing a day of them.

She has been out of every book in this county since and she cannot be found by writing a name down.

She was in that room on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week and was asked whether she could attend and was told she could not.

She has refused to say why and has asked not to be asked twice, and nobody on that bank has asked her, and she has asked for nothing since the Thursday of a week and a half ago.

“*Nobody has asked me anything for about three weeks and I have said that out loud twice and neither time has anybody asked me about it.*”

“*I have a name in ink on the ninth line of a sheet of printed paper and that line has never had a day against it. A man of thirty-four has a day against his name. A woman of twenty-nine has none. I have known all three of those since the Friday of a week and a half ago.*”

“*I have been carrying it since the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and nobody in this county has told me one word about any of it until a man of about fifty-two read a line out loud on the Friday of last week.*”

“*I am going to say one name and then I am not going to say it again, and I am going to say it in the ordinary voice, and nobody is going to get up.*”

“*First Witness.*”

“*That is the name. It is the name of the thing that has been asking in the voice of a minute for about nineteen years.*”

“*It is not a person. It has never been in a room. It cannot be sent for, and it is not anybody in this county or in the four counties around it.*”

“*That is the name. I am not going to say it a second time this week, and I am not going to shout it.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for it, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about nineteen years of not saying it.*”

“*And here is what it cost and I am going to give it in the order I keep things in.*”

“*One: I have been out of every book in this county for nineteen years and the reason is that nobody could put me next to anything. That is what getting up out of a book in the open is.*”

“*Two: I have just said a name out loud in front of about nine of them, and about nine of them now know a thing about me.*”

“*That is the first time since I was forty-five that anybody in this county has been able to ask me anything at all.*”

“*Three: I did not refuse anything. Nothing has been put in front of me with a place to put no. A man of fifty-four has told us twice this week that a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything.*”

“*He is right, and I am not going to be the second person on that bank to say that sentence today.*”

“*And I am still not going to say why I could not attend. Nobody is going to ask me and I am not going to say it, and it is not what this morning was for.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave his four counts first, and then said the one thing he has wanted said every morning for a fortnight.

He also said he was not going to use the word a woman of forty-four used yesterday.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wanted that hearing to stand and said it out loud on eleven mornings before he went up that cart road. He walked four miles down it on the Sunday and got to that bank after the seventh hour.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and forty-eight days, and that is seven hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and forty-nine days, and that is seven hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-four days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and thirty-six.*”

“*And that is seven hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other, and I have not added a name to them.*”

“*I am a man who keeps a book of heads and carts. I have just watched a woman with a name in ink say one out loud on a bank, and I did not write it down and I am not going to.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the fifteenth morning I have said it, and about four of you have not said anything about it since the Tuesday of a week and a half ago.*”

“*I have noticed that, and I am not going to ask any of you about it.*”

“*And a woman of forty-four used one word on that bank yesterday for what the nine of us are, and I am not going to use it about what a woman of sixty-four has just said. I do not know yet what that is, and I keep heads and carts.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts and the third, and said what she is not going to do with the seventy-three mornings now that a name has been said.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted seventy-two mornings on Tuesday with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and used one word for the thing on that bank yesterday.

She said the count was a figure of a bank and not a figure of anybody.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-four mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-eight mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-three mornings, and seventy-three is seven hundred and seventy-eight less seven hundred and five, and I counted that one this morning before a woman of sixty-four stood up.*”

“*I said on the Saturday of a week and a half ago that I was not going to say what to do about the sixty-eight, and I have not said it.*”

“*A name has been said on that bank, and that is not a thing to do about the sixty-eight. It is the other side of them.*”

“*I am not going to say what a woman of sixty-four has said is true. I have kept chairs for nineteen years. I am going to say that a woman who has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years stood up on that bank and said one word.*”

“*And that about nine of you were on it, and that is a figure of a bank and not a figure of the world.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that the name had been said in the ordinary voice and that he was not going to do anything with it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-four days, two hundred and twenty-four being seven hundred and seventy-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-three days old, ninety-three being seven hundred and seventy-eight less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice that nobody say.

“*Here is the want and it is the first want in two days that is not a sentence.*”

“*I want to be able to say one true thing about that name and I am not going to be able to, and I want to have wanted it, and I want to have been honest about wanting it.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is the same one again.*”

“*Yesterday I said that everything said on that bank was said because of me, and I gave it to you in one sentence and I handed the morning to somebody else.*”

“*A woman of sixty-four has just said a name, and I have not got a single thing to say about it.*”

“*I have been standing on this bank for two hundred and twenty-four days being the thing about four people come up to, and the thing I was good at was finding the shape of a thing in about nine lines.*”

“*There is no shape in it. It is a name in the ordinary voice, said once, by a person who cannot be sent for, and about nine of you heard it and none of you got up.*”

“*And I am not going to say it is the beginning of anything.*”

“*A man of forty-three said the day-name sequence out loud this morning, and the day-name moves one place every cycle. That is what happens to a thing on that bank. It moves one place and it comes round again.*”

“*Seventy-eight days. Seventy-eight is seven hundred and seventy-eight less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to a name.*”

“*And the cost is that a woman of sixty-four who has not been asked anything for about three weeks has just made herself askable, and nobody thanked her.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for standing here while she did it, and about four of you looked at me while she said it, and I did not know what to do with my hands.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the seventh hour with all ninety steps off it for the fourteenth time and a name in the air that about nine of them had heard once.

The fever was seventy weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-four days.

The water came off all ninety steps for the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Thursday.

A man of forty-three gave the whole day-name sequence out loud, and then said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of them.

And a woman of sixty-four who has a name in ink on a line that has never had a day said one name out loud once, in the ordinary voice.

She gave the cost of it in three parts, the third of which is that she did not refuse anything.

And a man of fifty-four said out loud what had been standing behind nine weeks of his own asking, which is that there was nothing on that line to come on.

And a man of sixty-one gave his four counts, and said he did not write down the name a woman of sixty-four said on that bank, and that he is a man who keeps a book of heads and carts, and that he is not going to use the word a woman of forty-four used yesterday about what she said.
