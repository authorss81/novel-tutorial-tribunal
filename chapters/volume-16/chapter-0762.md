# Chapter 762: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Tuesday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Twelfth Time, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Asks The One Question Nobody On That Bank Has Asked, Which Is From Which Day

First day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and twelfth morning. Three hundred and sixty-two days after the settlement. The fever sixty-eight weeks and three days. Three hundred and sixteen days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night. The man of forty-three said the hour of it and the count of it, and gave the day of the week on the word.

Corvin Tace is forty-three. He has kept that landing nineteen years. He gives three things every morning and has never given a fourth out of them. He said five times in a row that he had nothing to say about a night and then stopped saying it.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour this morning.*”

“*That is the twelfth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And it was a Tuesday. I am giving you that because the day-name moves one place every cycle and about four of you would have had Tuesday wrong.*”

“*I said the whole of the sequence out loud eight mornings ago and I am not going to say it again.*”

“*I gave a figure for why the cycles run eight days apart and then I gave it back. I have not found out and I am not going to find out in front of about nine of you.*”

“*Tomorrow it will be about four inches over the top step. That is one day of the coming back.*”

“*And the bank will be full again in four days, which is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*The next time those ninety steps are off is eight days from this morning.*”

Then the ninety steps cut in that bank could be walked for the twelfth time since that Saturday. About four of the about nine people up there had walked them on some of the earlier ones, and nobody kept a figure of who had.

The morning was cold at the bottom of the steps and the water was still moving off the lowest of them. A man of sixty-one stood at the top with his hands behind him.

“*I have not walked them since the Thursday of last week and I am not going to today.*”

“*That is not about the water, and about four of you are going to ask me what it is about, and I am going to tell you I have not got it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-first morning. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight to read it, because he was going to be asked.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one.

“*Forty-one mornings.*”

“*Forty-one is one more than forty, and I worked that out before I said it.*”

“*I said on Friday that I was going to stop putting a figure on the waiting. I did not stop on Friday. I did not stop on Saturday. I did not stop yesterday.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me about it and I would rather they did not.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-one.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line because he was asked. He gave the count of that sheet first, in his own mouth, with the working.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight. He has a child of nine. He is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark.

He has read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. Fifty-one mornings of that count had been counted by yesterday morning.

“*Fifty-two is seven hundred and sixty-two less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*Nobody has asked me to open anything out of a coat this morning, and about four of you have looked at the front of this coat about nine times.*”

“*I have said since Saturday that a thing only about four of you are waiting for is a thing about four people and not about nine.*”

“*And there is one person about four of you were waiting for who was not on that bank yesterday morning. I am not going to be the one who says what that does to the four of you.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well had the fourth line read out loud again, all of it, slowly, twice, and then he asked the question.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well. He is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works.

He has asked in the open what a mark is worth as a figure four times in a fortnight, and a fifth time on Saturday. He was answered with a figure of nothing that was refused in the same breath. He has not asked it a sixth time.

“*I am going to read the fourth line twice and I am going to read it wrong both times.*”

“*A finding is made in the absence of the persons heard about.*”

“*That their absence is not to be read as consent.*”

“*And that it is held open for nine days for any person heard about to come forward.*”

“*Nine days. That is the third of the three things on that line. It is the only one of the three that is a figure of days.*”

“*Here is the question and I have never asked it out loud and I am about to.*”

“*Nine days from what.*”

“*That line says about nine days. It does not say nine days from which day.*”

“*It is printed on a piece of paper with no room on it and no name on it. The day it starts counting from is not on the line.*”

“*I have been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years. There is no fourth thing that comes before a fifth thing, and every morning I can be checked against the four lines under me.*”

“*A fifth thing that does not say where it starts from is not a fifth thing I know what to do with.*”

“*And I am not going to make this the sixth time I ask what a mark is worth. It is a different question, and about four of you are not going to be able to tell the difference, and I am not going to help them.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for reading a line twice, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about asking it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs gave the two counts off that counter in the order she gives them.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four. She has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter eighteen mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twelve mornings, with a number on them and no room on them.*”

“*And here is the third of the two. I am not going to give you a fourth thing about that counter, because about four of you have been waiting for it since Friday.*”

“*That is a figure of days and not a figure of anybody, and I keep a book of chairs and I do not keep days.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them. Then he said the one thing he wants out loud, and gave the count first, because that is how he gives everything.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one. He has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written two numbers in it in eleven years, one of them six and written on the Sunday of last week.

There are five lines on the leaf after the last head, and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and thirty-two days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and thirty-three days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and eight days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and twenty, and that is seven hundred and sixty-two less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other and I am not going to.*”

“*And here is what I want. It is the only want I have said on this bank and I am going to say it twice.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand.*”

“*It is about a road four miles up that cart road and about four houses that cannot come on a day.*”

“*It is not about the woman in the reed or the man in the ash or the woman of sixty-four.*”

“*And a man of about fifty-two has just asked where a figure of nine days starts from. That is a good question and it does not change what I want. A day I cannot get is worth the same whether I know where it started or not.*”

Then a man of thirty-one gave the want and the mistake and the cost. The mistake this morning is the oldest one on that bank and it is nine mornings old.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and eight days, two hundred and eight being seven hundred and sixty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. He has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether the cut across his right palm will keep.

“*The want is first.*”

“*I want about nine of you to hold the question a man of about fifty-two just asked for about a minute, before any of you answer it.*”

“*And here is the mistake, and it is mine, and it is nine mornings old and I have not said it out loud once in nine days.*”

“*The second line of that thing says the day the hearing was held.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two read it out loud on the Friday of last week, in the open, on this bank, with about nine of you standing here.*”

“*And I have had it in my own head since that morning as a day four hundred miles off. I have never once put it against a figure of nine days.*”

“*I have been standing in one place about two weeks and calling it work. I said that on the Friday of a fortnight and here it is again with a different sentence on it.*”

“*Sixty-two days. Sixty-two is seven hundred and sixty-two less seven hundred.*”

“*That is what I have given every morning since the fifty-first. None of it is an answer, and this morning it is not an answer to a man of about fifty-two either.*”

Then about nine of them stood on that bank with all ninety steps off it for the twelfth time, and a question sitting in the middle of about nine of them that nobody had asked before Saturday.

---

The fever was sixty-eight weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eight days.

A man of forty-three said the water came off all ninety steps for the twelfth time and gave the day of it out loud. Then he said he had given a figure for why the cycles run eight days apart and had given it back.

And a man of about fifty-two read the fourth line of that hearing out loud twice and asked the one question nobody on that bank had asked, which is that it says about nine days and does not say nine days from which day.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted about nine of them to hold that question for about a minute before anybody answered it, and said that the day of the hearing has been on that counter since Friday morning and he has never once put it against a figure of nine days.
