# Chapter 795: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fifth Week, And A Sunday, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Reads A Line Out Of A Book She Was In Nineteen Years Ago And Refuses To Read The Line Above It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says He Has Been Building A Case Out Of Things With No Name In Them

Sixth day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. His five hundred and forty-fifth morning. Three hundred and ninety-five days after the settlement. The fever seventy-three weeks and one day. Three hundred and forty-nine days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about four inches over the bottom of that cut at about the seventh hour, one day of the coming back, and the man of forty-three said the figure and the working and the day.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of last week and about four of them walk past it.

“*Four inches over the bottom of that cut at the seventh hour, and it is one day since it came off, and those ninety steps can be walked from the top down to about the fortieth one.*”

“*Four inches a day, and I gave you that on every morning of the full bank, and I am giving it again because about four of you have started asking me for it in the morning instead of at the hour.*”

“*And the next time all ninety of them can be walked is in seven days, and that is the Sunday of next week.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, seventy-fourth morning, and asked out loud first, and said that he is not going to say the number again this week.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He has asked for that line in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He went down ninety cut steps to have it read to him at the bottom of them yesterday.

“*Seventy-four mornings, and I am not going to give the figure for the not being asked this week, and about four of you wanted it and I have decided that a number I say about my own waiting is a number I can start trading on.*”

“*And I have been at the bottom of that cut and the top of it in two days, and I have worked out that the reason I asked to go down was not the reading.*”

“*It was that I had been standing above it for seventy-three mornings. That is the whole of it and I am not going to be thanked for having worked it out on a Sunday.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line, gave the count, and said the number of names, and said that he was asked a question on Friday evening and he is still deciding about Monday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine and a board in the inside of his coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it. He has said the number of names out loud every morning since the Friday of a fortnight ago and gave the reason once.

“*Four names, and it is the fifteenth morning, and it is the fifteenth and it is not going to stop being four because I say it every morning.*”

“*Eighty-five is seven hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred and ten, and I read the ninth, and nobody has asked me for a second line since the Tuesday of the week before last.*”

“*Somebody asked me a question on Friday evening down the lane and I have had it two days, and I said on Friday that I would answer it on Monday, and I have not thought about it since Friday and I am not going to pretend to you that I have.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety-seven days, and ninety-seven is seven hundred and ninety-five less six hundred and ninety-eight.*”

Then a woman of twenty-eight was not on that bank, and a man of thirty-one said her figure out loud, and then said the thing that is the middle of his week.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He gave a figure of days for a woman of twenty-eight every morning for about three weeks and has stopped.

“*A woman of twenty-eight has been in this county a hundred and fifty-three days, and that is seven hundred and ninety-five less six hundred and forty-two, and she is not on that bank and I am giving her figure anyway.*”

“*And I said on Friday that I would not say that again after this week, and I am saying it on a Sunday, which is a day I did not account for, and I have noticed that about four of you noticed it too.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and forty-one days, two hundred and forty-one being seven hundred and ninety-five less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ten days old, a hundred and ten being seven hundred and ninety-five less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked four times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said.

“*That is the fifth time I have given that figure for a woman who is not here, and I gave it on Monday and Tuesday and Wednesday and Friday and this morning, and it is a figure of days for a person who has never been asked one question about any of them.*”

“*And I am not going to keep saying it, and a man of sixty-one has stopped saying his too, and about four of you are going to find out this week what this bank is like without a man on it reading out the days of people who are not on it.*”

“*Here is what I have found out and I am saying it before anybody asks me for it.*”

“*I have been building a case for three days out of things. A sheet with three columns. Four leaves with a number on them. Nine days on a fourth line. A line that has never had a day. A mark. A sentence in a hand.*”

“*And not one of those has a person’s name in it, and I have put four days of asking and walking and standing at the end of things into a set of figures that would stand up in a room exactly as well as this bank does, and that is not a case, it is a set of figures about a case.*”

“*A case needs one person who can be named and asked, and I have got about nine of you on a bank and not one of you is a person I can serve a paper on, and about four of the nine of you would be refused at that room in about nine weeks.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four read a line out of a book she was in nineteen years ago, and said she is reading it from memory, and said what she will not read out.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them, and she has a name in ink on the ninth line and has never had a day against it. She asked a man of thirty-one for a day on the Saturday of the week before last and he said he could not do it.

“*I am going to read one line out of that book from memory, and I am doing it because about four of you have started saying that I know what is in it, and I do know what is in it, and that has been sitting on me for nineteen years.*”

“*It says: the fourth line is a count of days, and a count of days runs out, and nobody is to be charged for the days after it runs out.*”

“*That is the line. Nineteen words. I have not looked at that book in nineteen years and I am not going to be thanked for reciting it and I am not going to be told I was brave about remembering it.*”

“*The line above that one stays where it is, and so does the name of whoever wrote it, and nobody is getting either out of me.*”

“*If a person asks me about either of those I am going to tell them the ordinary weather, and they can make what they like of it.*”

“*I have had a name in ink on a line for nineteen years and that line has never had a day against it, and I have just read out the only sentence anybody has ever given me about what a line is for, and it is in my mouth and not on paper, and about four of you heard it and that is nine fewer than the number it is for.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts, and a boy of thirteen gave his own and said he has started counting something else, and a man of thirty-one gave the want and the mistake and the cost.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She said that what to do about a figure is somebody else’s morning and has said it for a fortnight.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter fifty-one mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter forty-five mornings with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*And three: ninety mornings of this bank, and ninety is seven hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred and five, and that is a round figure and I have been told a round figure is a thing a person does on purpose.*”

“*It is not. It is five more than Wednesday, and I have been counting since a Monday with a crumb of bread in my hand and I have never once decided anything.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and forty-two days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of last week, and held nineteen minutes at the top of ninety cut steps on the Friday of last week.

“*A hundred and forty-two days, and I counted them in the lane and it is two more than Friday.*”

“*And I have not put a seventh count down, and I have started counting something else, and I am telling you what it is because I told a man of thirty-eight on the first morning of this week that I was not going to start asking anybody for anything.*”

“*I am counting the mornings when about four of you say a figure out loud that you have said before. It is nineteen so far, and it is nineteen more than it was on Monday, and I am not asking anybody for any of them.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and has been in this county two hundred and forty-one days. The cut across that palm is a hundred and ten days old and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*The want is unchanged and I am going to keep saying it is unchanged until it stops being true, and it is going to stop being true, and I do not know how.*”

“*The mistake is the eleventh of the same mistake and it is one day old. I read that to you this morning in a list of six things and I told you the list was the case, and about four of you wrote it down, and a woman of sixty-four then read out nineteen words from nineteen years ago that are a better case than anything on my list.*”

“*Ninety-five days. Ninety-five is seven hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer and I have said that ninety-four times.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of sixty-four has put a sentence about counting days into about nine sets of ears, and she did it because I stood on a bank for ten days asking for a case, and she is sixty-four and she recited nineteen words from memory and refused to read the line above it, and nobody in this county is going to be sent for about either of those things.*”

“*And nobody is relieved of anything because of that, and nobody says a word of thanks to me for it, and nobody on that bank gets told I was brave about a week.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Sunday with about four inches of water on that cut, and nineteen words had been said out loud that had never been said in that county before.

---

The fever was seventy-three weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and forty-one days.

A woman of sixty-four recited nineteen words from a book she had not opened in nineteen years, about a count of days running out and nobody being charged for the days after it does.

She refused to read the line above it, and said she would answer any question about it with the ordinary weather.

And a man of thirty-one said he had built four days of work out of figures with no names in them, and that nineteen words out of a woman’s mouth were a better case than all of it.
