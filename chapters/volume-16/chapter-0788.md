# Chapter 788: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Sunday, And He Walks A Strip Of That Bank And Measures It, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Walks Out Of It In The Open And It Closes Behind Him

Sixth day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-eighth morning. Three hundred and eighty-eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks and one day. Three hundred and forty-two days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he named a stone on that bank that about nine of them had never been told about, and said he had kept it marked for nineteen years.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He counted fifteen of them off on the Friday of this week and the sixteenth is in six days and it will be a Saturday.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is two days of the coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and that is the working underneath it.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in six days from this morning, which is the Saturday of next week.*”

“*That is the sixteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And there is a stone on the flat of this bank at the up-water end, about eleven paces from the up-water end of that counter, which is twenty-two yards.*”

“*I have kept that marked for nineteen years and about nine of you have never been told it is there, and about four of you walk past it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-seventh morning. He asked out loud first, and gave the notice and not a want.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week, and he has said on Thursday and Friday and Saturday that he will start asking about it on Monday.

“*Sixty-seven mornings.*”

“*Sixty-seven is one more than sixty-six, and that is the working, and I have said it on Thursday and Friday and Saturday and I am going to say it on Monday as well.*”

“*A man of forty-three has just named a stone on that bank. I have been standing on that bank for about nine weeks and I did not know it was called anything.*”

“*I am not asking him what it is for. I am going to wait and see whether somebody else asks him.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day and gave the count of that sheet and the number of names, and gave nothing else at all.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has said the number of names he can see on the ninth line out loud every morning since the Friday of last week, and gave the reason once on the Thursday of this week and has not given it since.

“*Four names, and I have said that number out loud eight mornings and about nine of you know it by heart by now.*”

“*Seventy-eight is seven hundred and eighty-eight less seven hundred and ten, and I read only the ninth, because a man of fifty-four asked and nobody has asked me for another.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety days, and ninety is seven hundred and eighty-eight less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*And I said on the Friday that I was not opening it, and I have not, and that is eight mornings.*”

Then he walked a strip of the flat of that bank and measured it out loud in paces of two yards, and gave it three dimensions and gave what it is for in nine sentences.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-four days, two hundred and thirty-four being seven hundred and eighty-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is a hundred and three days old, a hundred and three being seven hundred and eighty-eight less six hundred and eighty-five.

“*It is the flat of this bank, from the stone a man of forty-three has just named to the up-water end of that counter.*”

“*It is about nineteen paces long. Nineteen times two yards is thirty-eight yards, and that is about a hundred and fourteen feet.*”

“*And it is about six paces wide at the narrow end, which is twelve yards, and about nine paces wide at the wide end, which is eighteen yards.*”

“*And it is about thirty feet off the edge where those ninety steps start. The whole of it is on ground that is nine feet above that landing.*”

“*Here is what it is for and I am going to say it before anybody asks me what a strip of a bank is for.*”

“*It is for somebody to be standing in, and then to walk out of it, and to be safe when they have. That is the whole of it.*”

“*Not safe on paper. Not safe in a count. A person goes out of it in the open, in front of about nine of them, and the room does not close behind them.*”

Then he asked a man of about fifty-two to walk out of it, and he walked out of it, and it closed behind him.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works, and every morning of his working life there have been four lines under him to be checked against. He has one thing in those four leaves he has not said out loud in about eleven years and he has now said that out loud eight mornings running.

“*I will. I have got nothing standing in the way of it and I would rather be the one that finds out than be told about it by about four of you on Friday.*”

He walked out at the wide end, in the open, past about nine people, and about nine of them watched him do it and not one of them moved.

Then the far end of the strip came back to the counter.

“*Here is what happened and I am going to say it before about four of you say it for me.*”

“*I held the up-water end myself. I walked the strip this morning with my own two hands and I was at one end of it and you were at the other, and when you went past the wide end there was nobody at that end at all.*”

“*And the far end came back to the counter. It is nine paces wide at this end now and it is nothing at the other, and I did not decide that and nobody else did either.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two said what a margin is and what it is not, and gave the check, and said the thing in the four leaves is still not said.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two. He walked out of a strip of that bank in the open on a Sunday morning and it closed behind him, and about nine people were standing there when it did.

“*I have four lines under me and I can be checked against them every morning, and that is the only reason I said I would do it.*”

“*A strip of ground you can walk out of is not a margin. A margin is a strip of ground with somebody at both ends of it, and there was one of us at the end of it this morning and one of us was the man who drew it.*”

“*Thirty-eight yards by twelve yards and by eighteen yards. I have checked all three of those and the two checks are the same.*”

“*And the thing in those four leaves is still not said. I have said that out loud eight mornings now and about nine of you have never once asked me for it.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she has been inside of for eleven years, and it is not the strip on that bank.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. She asked him on the Sunday of last week for the ground of three things.

“*I have been inside of eleven years of not having a day and about four of you were in there with me and about nine of you were not.*”

“*That is what a margin is for and I have been asking for one for eleven years and I did not know there was a word for it until a man of forty-four said one word on that bank a fortnight ago.*”

“*I want to say this carefully because about four of you will hear the wrong thing and go away with it.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine in a reed is not in a margin. She is about a hundred yards away and she cannot get out of anything, and a man of thirty-four in an ash is not in a margin either.*”

“*Nobody is in a margin who cannot get out of it. That is what makes it a margin and it is the whole of it and about four of you had it the other way round before this morning.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said what she is going to be able to hold on Monday, and said it out loud before it happened.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and she has never once said what to do about it.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-four mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-eight mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-three mornings, and eighty-three is seven hundred and eighty-eight less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a fourth, which is a figure of this bank: a strip of it that opened on one end this morning and shut on the other.*”

“*I am not going to say what to do about the eighty-three. I have said that since the Saturday of a fortnight and I am not going to find a way around it because a man of about fifty-two got his figures checked.*”

Then a man of thirty-one gave the cost out loud, and it is that the only man on that bank who can check him walked out of the thing.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-four days. The cut across it is a hundred and three days old.

“*Here is the want and it is the first one in two days that is not about a person.*”

“*I want somebody to ask me for a thing. I have said that three times in about a fortnight and I have had two of them and I can do neither, and I am going to keep saying it until somebody asks me for something I can do.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is six days old and it is the same mistake in a fifth wording.*”

“*I have been treating ground as though it were a thing that could be got hold of. I walked three pieces of it this week and counted them and said them out loud, and then I walked a fourth and tried to hold it.*”

“*Ground does not stay held. It stays held while somebody is at the end of it and not one moment longer, and I have been the end of it.*”

“*Eighty-eight days. Eighty-eight is seven hundred and eighty-eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer to a strip of a bank that shut.*”

“*And the cost is that I asked the one man on that bank who can check my figures to walk out of it, and he did, and it shut behind him, and I did that on purpose and I knew the risk and I did it in the open anyway.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for walking out of a thing and nobody is going to be told I was brave about a strip of ground nine feet above a landing.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-fifth morning, and said he would rather have heard about the strip on Friday.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-five mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-eight days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-eight less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-nine days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-eight less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-four days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-six, and that is seven hundred and eighty-eight less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I keep heads and carts, and there are four houses on a cart road three miles and six hundred yards above this landing that have a second cart which does not come the whole way down, and I still cannot tell a woman at a gate which day it comes on.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-fifth morning I have said it, and I would rather have heard about a strip of that bank on Friday than this morning.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Sunday, and a man had walked out of a strip of it, and the strip was nine paces wide at one end and nothing at the other.

---

The fever was seventy-two weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-four days.

He walked a strip of that bank and gave three dimensions in paces of two yards, and said the whole of it was for somebody to walk out of it and be safe.

A man of about fifty-two walked out of it in the open at the wide end, and about nine of them were standing there when he got to it.

The far end of that strip came back to the counter with nobody standing at it, and the man who drew it had been the only one at the other end all morning.
