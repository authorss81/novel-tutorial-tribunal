# Chapter 766: Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-First Week, And A Saturday, And The Bank Is Full Again At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step On The Fourth Morning Of It, And A Woman Of Forty Says Out Loud What Job A Man Of Thirty-One Has Been Standing In For Two Hundred And Twelve Days

Fifth day of the hundred and thirty-first week. His five hundred and sixteenth morning. Three hundred and sixty-six days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks old. Three hundred and twenty days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full again at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the fourth morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and said the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and gave the day of the week on the word.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and the three things he gives have been the same three for about seven weeks. He said on Tuesday that he is not going to change how he says things to suit a figure, and he has not.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the fourth morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and four inches a day is the working under it.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, which is the Wednesday of next week.*”

“*I am giving you that day out loud because about four of you have been writing it on your fingers wrong and because on Tuesday I said it the other way round and about four of you told me I had it wrong.*”

“*I am not going to change how I say things to suit a figure. Three things a morning and then I stop.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-fifth morning. He asked out loud first, and a man of thirty-eight said yes out loud before he read any of it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He asked for that line out loud before anybody spoke on the Tuesday of this week and has done every morning since, and he has not missed one of the about six weeks in front of that either.

“*Forty-five mornings.*”

“*Forty-five is one more than forty-four and I worked it out before I said it, same as every morning.*”

“*The man of thirty-eight said yes out loud before he read it and he did not have to. I have not asked him anything today and he read it.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me and I would rather they did not, and I am not going to stop asking for the ninth line because of a fifth thing.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-five.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight said why he said yes before he was asked, which is a different reason from the one he gave on Tuesday, and he gave the count of that sheet with the working first.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and on Tuesday he gave one reason for reading it and on Saturday he gave another and this morning he gave a third, and he is not going to say which of the three he started with.

“*Fifty-six is seven hundred and sixty-six less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*On Tuesday I said I read it because I was asked. That was true and it was not the whole of it.*”

“*Today I said yes out loud before I was asked, because a man of fifty-four has been asking out loud for four mornings and about nine of you have got used to it, and if I have to be asked every morning then the asking is a job he is doing for me.*”

“*He is not doing a job for me. He is asking for a line of a hearing that has no name on it, and I happen to be the one who can put a light on it.*”

“*And I am still not opening this coat, and there is a board in it with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and about four of you are waiting for it and I have said what a thing about four people is.*”

Then a woman of forty said what job he has been standing in, and said it in the hearing of about nine of them, and nobody asked her for a reason for saying it.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name, and she has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. On Thursday she said out loud that she wants a day with nothing against it, and this morning she said what a man of thirty-one has been standing in every morning for two hundred and twelve days.

“*Say it first, and then I will tell you what I am standing in front of.*”

“*A man of thirty-one standing on a bank for two hundred and twelve days being the thing about four people come up to.*”

“*And about four of you know that is a job and about nine of you have been treating it as a position, and it is a job, and he has been doing it every morning for about nine weeks for nobody and getting nothing out of it.*”

“*He has said a want out loud in front of about nine people about eleven times in about nine days, and then on the Wednesday of a fortnight he told me himself that a word said nine times in nine days in front of about nine people is not a want any more but a habit.*”

“*And then he has stood here every morning since and gone on doing it, and calling it a want, and about four of you have believed him and about nine of you have not.*”

“*That is what I am standing in front of and I am not going to take any of it back, because there is nothing on it to take back, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it either.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and said the fourth thing about it for the first time in six mornings, and it was two sentences.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to, and she has been refusing a fourth thing about that counter for six mornings.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-two mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter sixteen mornings, with a number on them and no room on them.*”

“*And the fourth thing, which I have refused six mornings running: the two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it, and I have now written both of them on the back of my own hand and neither of them is in that book, because that book is chairs.*”

“*A woman of thirty-four came two days and came after both of them and none of that is on the paper, and a man of about fifty-two has asked nine days from what, and there is nobody four hundred miles off that anybody in this county can be sent for.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and said the want out loud again, and said that a woman of forty has not changed it by saying what it is.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and he has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since, and there are five lines on the leaf after the last head.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and thirty-six days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and thirty-seven days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twelve days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and twenty-four, and that is seven hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other and I am not going to, and I have not added a day to them either.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. I said it on the Saturday of last week and I have said it every morning since, and a woman of forty has stood on this bank and told a man of thirty-one what he is doing every morning, and that has not changed what I want by one day.*”

“*It is about a road four miles up that cart road and about four houses that cannot come on a day, and it is not about the woman in the reed or the man in the ash or the woman of sixty-four.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said a want that is about himself for the first time in about eleven days, and a woman of forty told him what shape it was before he had finished it, and he said it back.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and twelve days, two hundred and twelve being seven hundred and sixty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. He has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether the cut across his right palm will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is mine and it is about me, and about eleven days of my wants have been about other people.*”

“*I want somebody on this bank to ask me for a thing.*”

“*Not a question about what I am. Not whether I will say a word. A thing. A job. Something with a day on it.*”

“*And a woman of forty has told me what that is before I had finished the sentence, and she is right, and I heard it.*”

“*It is the same shape as the thing on that counter. A finding is made about people who are not in the room and it says they did not object.*”

“*And I am a man of thirty-one standing on a bank in front of about nine people saying I want to be asked for a thing, and about nine of them can be here for it.*”

“*So I am going to say it back, because you said it out loud and about four of you were not going to, and that is worse than the thing I said.*”

“*I want to be asked for a thing, and the shape of it is the shape of the thing on that counter, and I am not going to pretend it is not.*”

“*Sixty-six days. Sixty-six is seven hundred and sixty-six less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer and this morning it is not an answer to her either.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with the water full again and a woman of forty standing at the counter who had said out loud what a man of thirty-one does every morning, and about four of them had heard it and about nine of them had not.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks old. He had been in that county two hundred and twelve days.

A woman of forty said out loud that a man of thirty-one standing on a bank for two hundred and twelve days being the thing about four people come up to is a job and not a position, and that about four of them know it and about nine of them have been treating it as a position.

And a man of thirty-one said he wants somebody to ask him for a thing, and said it back when she told him what shape it was, because it is the shape of the thing on that counter.

And a woman of forty-four gave the fourth thing about that counter for the first time in six mornings, which is that the two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it, and that she has written both on the back of her own hand and neither of them is in a book of chairs.
