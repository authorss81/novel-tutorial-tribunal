# Chapter 798: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Sixth Week, And A Wednesday, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Checks Eleven Figures Out Loud And Catches Himself Carrying A Day From Yesterday, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says No Nine Times And Says A Gap Is Not A Wall

Second day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. His five hundred and forty-eighth morning. Three hundred and ninety-eight days after the settlement. The fever seventy-three weeks and four days. Three hundred and fifty-two days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the first morning of it, four days of it, and the man of forty-three said so and gave the day.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the week before last and about four of them walk past it every day and about nine of them have never been told about it.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is four days since it came off, and it is a full bank on the first morning of it and it will hold there.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath the holding and I have given that every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in four days, and that is the Sunday of this week, and it will be the seventeenth time.*”

“*I said on Tuesday that it would be a Sunday, and about four of you have had Saturday written down since, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it a third time.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, seventy-seventh morning, and asked out loud first, and said he is not going to ask for the whole of it again.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He asked for the whole of a sheet to be read out loud at the top of that cut yesterday and a man of thirty-eight read all of it and gave no figure.

“*Seventy-seven mornings, and I am not going to ask for the whole of it again this week.*”

“*It was in the open yesterday. That was the whole of what I wanted and it took one morning and about nine of you were standing there when it happened, and I am not going to ask for it again because asking twice turns a thing into a habit and a man of thirty-eight has been telling us about habits all week.*”

“*And I want to say the other half of it. I have been not asked anything for about three weeks and I have worked out that the reason is that nobody wants anything from me, and that is not the same as being refused, and I have not been refused anything, and I have not had anything put in front of me with a place to put no.*”

“*I am fifty-four and I cannot read and I have nobody to be angry with and I have been telling about four of you for a fortnight that a man who is not allowed is not a man who has refused, and I am the one it is hardest for and I am not going to soften it this morning.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two checked every figure that had been given on that bank in four days out loud, against four lines under him, before any of them was used, which is what he said he would do.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works, and four lines sit under him and any one of the four can be looked at to see whether he has read the fifth wrong. He put a mark on the fourth line of a leaf eleven years ago because he was the only one of nine who was not asked what the line was for, and he said that out loud on Saturday.

“*I said on Thursday of the week before last that I would check every figure a man of thirty-one gives out loud, and say so before one of them is used. Nobody has used one. I am doing it anyway, because I said I would and not using it is not a reason for not doing it.*”

“*Here is what I can check. Two yards to a pace of his, and that came out of five thousand eight hundred and eighty yards over two thousand nine hundred and forty paces, and both of those are in the mouths of two people and I have looked at them.*”

“*Eleven paces and twenty-two yards at a turn. Eighteen yards by ten at a yard. Eight yards by eighteen at a wash-house. Thirty-eight yards by twelve and by eighteen at a strip of a bank. Three thousand five hundred and twenty yards at a house.*”

“*Ninety-three mornings on that counter. Fifty-four mornings of a sheet. Forty-eight mornings of four leaves.*”

“*And the working is the same shape on all three, because all three of them are this morning off this morning.*”

“*Ninety-three, off seven hundred and five. Fifty-four, off seven hundred and forty-four. Forty-eight, off seven hundred and fifty. And every one of those three is this morning off seven hundred and ninety-eight.*”

“*Those eleven check and I have written them on the back of my hand in the ordinary way and not in a book, because a man of sixty-one has two numbers in eleven years and is not going to want a third from me.*”

“*And I had three of those wrong by a day when I started saying them, and about four of you heard me, and I am not going to leave that in the air.*”

“*I said ninety-two for that counter this morning and it is ninety-three. Ninety-two is the figure for Tuesday. I have been carrying a day from yesterday in a thing I said I was checking, and I caught it because a man of thirteen has been standing about four feet from me counting something else, and he did not say a word.*”

“*That is the whole of the argument for doing this in the open and it cost me about four seconds of standing there.*”

“*And here is the one I cannot check, and I am saying it in front of about nine people because I said I would say it before it was used, and it was not going to be used, and I am saying it anyway.*”

“*Four hundred miles. A man of thirty-one has it in his mouth about nine times in four days and there is no line under me four hundred miles off to check it against, and it is not wrong, and I cannot catch him on it, and neither can any of you.*”

Then about nine of them asked a woman of sixty-four to say the word again, and she said she would not, and gave the reason, and was not thanked for saying it.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them, and she has a name in ink on that ninth line and has never had a day against it. She said the word once out loud on Tuesday morning and named three people it had cost.

“*Nine of you asked me between the seventh hour and the ninth hour this morning to say it again, and about four of you asked in a voice and about four asked in another one, and I said no nine times and I am not going to count them for you.*”

“*I said on Tuesday that I would say it once and I said it once. A word is not a bucket. It does not have more in it the second time and I am not a thing that empties.*”

“*And it is worse than a word being said once. About four of you know what it said back, and if nine people asked me to say it again I would be doing a thing with a bucket and I am not going to do it to anybody.*”

“*Nobody thanks me for the nine and nobody is told I was brave about the one.*”

“*I have said that three times in four days, and about four of you have stopped bringing it up to me, and I noticed that too.*”

“*What I am going to say instead is the thing I have not said, and I am saying it because a man of thirty-one said nine things about what a thing is not on Tuesday and I have been thinking about it for two days.*”

“*It is not a person and I knew that when I was forty-five. That is why nobody could be sent for about me. That is the whole protection I have had for nineteen years and it is not a protection anybody gave me, it is a gap, and a gap is not the same as a wall.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one is right that a woman of sixty-four cannot be protected from it by not saying a word, and he said that on Tuesday and about four of you heard him and nobody has done anything about what he said.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts, and a boy of thirteen gave two, and a man of thirty-one said the case is not going to be won on what he has.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She said that the numbers are of things and every one of the things is about somebody who is not in this county.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter fifty-four mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter forty-eight mornings with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*And three: ninety-three mornings of this bank, and ninety-three is seven hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And I have been asked twice this week whether the whole of a case is a thing like these and I have not answered either time and I am not going to answer this morning either.*”

“*A man of about fifty-two has just checked eleven figures out loud against four lines under him, and one of them is mine, and he checked mine and it was right, and I am not going to be thanked for a number being right.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and forty-five days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of the week before last, and has been counting the mornings about four of us do not say a figure.

“*A hundred and forty-five days, and I counted them in the lane and it is one more than yesterday.*”

“*And the third count is five, and it is the mornings about four of us do not say a figure when one of us could, and that is me and not these people, and I have worked that out and I am saying it before somebody asks.*”

“*I have been not asking anybody for anything since the first morning of this week and it turns out that is the same as asking, only the other way round, and a man of thirty-one worked that out on Sunday and told us and I got there on my own on Tuesday.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and forty-four days, two hundred and forty-four being seven hundred and ninety-eight less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and thirteen days old, a hundred and thirteen being seven hundred and ninety-eight less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked six times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said.

“*A man of about fifty-two has checked eleven of my figures out loud in front of about nine of you and every one of the eleven is right, and there is a twelfth he cannot check at all, and he said that before anybody used one of them, which is what he said he would do on the Thursday of the week before last.*”

“*And a thing said four things back to me on Tuesday in about nine people’s hearing and said in the fourth of them that it read that line, and I have had two days with it and I still cannot do one single thing with it that anybody would take.*”

“*The want is unchanged and I have stopped saying the same six words over it, because a boy of thirteen has this week counted the mornings when about four of us say a figure again, and I am not going to be one of the days he counts.*”

“*I want somebody who can be asked, and who says no, and who comes back the next morning and is asked again.*”

“*The mistake is the fourteenth of the same mistake and it is three days old, and it is the one a man of about fifty-two found this morning, and it is not mine.*”

“*I have been treating the ability to be checked as a kind of being listened to. He has four lines under him and about nine of us do not, and I have been taking his checking as the same as being heard, and it is not the same, and about four of you have let me.*”

“*Ninety-eight days. Ninety-eight is seven hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer.*”

“*The case is not going to be won on what I have. I am saying that on the second morning of a week with a full bank behind me and about nine of you on it, and about four of you have been waiting for me to say it since Wednesday of last week.*”

“*A sheet with three columns and nothing in them. Four leaves with a number and no room and no names. Nine days on a fourth line. A line with no day on it. A mark. A sentence in a hand. A name in ink.*”

“*Seven things and not one person, and I have walked about three miles and counted a strip of a bank in paces of two yards, and a man has checked eleven figures against four lines and the one figure that matters most is four hundred miles and he cannot check it.*”

“*I cannot win a case on the surviving record alone, and I am not going to stand on this bank and ask about nine of you to pretend otherwise, and nobody is going to be thanked for standing here while I say it.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of any of it, and nobody is told I was brave about ten days.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning, and I am not saying that it was, and a man of fifty-four has said that sentence better than I have and he is standing on this bank.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Wednesday with a full bank behind them, and eleven figures had been checked out loud, and one of them could not be.

---

The fever was seventy-three weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and forty-four days.

A man of about fifty-two checked eleven figures out loud against four lines under him, and said before any of them was used, and one of them he could not check at all.

Nine people asked a woman of sixty-four to say a word again between the seventh hour and the ninth hour, and she said no nine times, and then she said that a gap is not the same as a wall.

Seven things were on a list he read out loud on that bank this morning, and not one of them was a person, and the list before it had six and was read three days ago.
