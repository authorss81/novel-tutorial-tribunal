# Chapter 756: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirtieth Week, And A Wednesday, And The Bank Is About Eight Inches Over The Top Step On The Second Day Of It, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Is Still Standing At The End Of That Bank And Was Not Asked For Anything, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Came Down Four Miles And Says About Four Houses In That Village Have Stopped Sending Carts Down That Road Since Friday

Second day of the hundred and thirtieth week. His five hundred and sixth morning. Three hundred and fifty-six days after the settlement. The fever sixty-seven weeks and four days. Three hundred and ten days since the division. Two hundred and fifty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the second day of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped, and he has given two figures for a night since. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*About eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the second morning of it.*”

“*Two days. It was about four at this hour yesterday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And about ninety steps can be walked again in about four days after that bank is full.*”

“*I said that yesterday morning and said I would say it once more and then not again until Monday. This is the once more.*”

“*And there is no fourth thing in me this morning and there is not going to be one.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the thirty-fifth morning, and a man of thirty-eight read it because he was asked, and he said so before he started.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and he has given the day it was on as well. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives.

“*Thirty-five mornings and it is the second day of a week.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight said before he started that he was asked, and about nine of you had not thought about the asking and about four of you had.*”

“*The fifth thing is still not answered and nobody has put it to me and I am still here.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the thirty-five and nobody is going to be told I was brave about it.*”

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and forty-five mornings of that count had been counted by yesterday morning. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week.

“*Forty-six is seven hundred and fifty-six less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the forty-six in the dark.*”

“*And I said before I started that I was asked, and I am not going to say that again every morning or it stops being a thing that happened.*”

“*A man of thirty-one said on Tuesday morning that a second time is a rule, and I have said that sentence in my own mouth on this bank on about nine mornings since and I did not know until Saturday that I was doing it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-six and nobody is going to be told I was brave about saying one sentence before a line.*”

Then a woman of thirty-four was still standing at the end of that bank, and had been since about the sixth hour, and had not been asked anything by anybody, and said so in four sentences.

Brenna Dole is thirty-four and has walked about two days from a place four hundred miles off this landing, and she cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from. She came down that bank on the Sunday of last week and said she was one of the nine and that six were in that room.

“*Four sentences and I am not going to be asked anything about any of them and I am saying that so that about nine of you know I know it.*”

“*One: I have been standing here about an hour and nobody has asked me a question and nobody is going to.*”

“*Two: I said on Sunday what I came to say and I said I would not be here tomorrow because the water was coming off, and the water came off and I am here.*”

“*Three: that is not me making a point. I did not know the water was coming off until a man of forty-three said it on Sunday morning and I did not know what he meant by a full bank until Wednesday.*”

“*Four, and it is what I would like said about me: I am standing here about nine days’ walk from the only thing I came here about.*”

“*And I am not going to be sent for, and I am not going to ask anybody to walk it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the four sentences and nobody is going to be told I am patient.*”

Then a woman of forty-four came down four miles and gave the count first and refused the rest, and it was about about four houses.

Hester Ollerton is forty-four and has kept a bakehouse four miles up that cart road, and about nine houses in that village take her bread. She has been asked four times and answered twice and has not answered the fourth thing, and she said on the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week that it was three weeks since she said she would not answer it.

“*Nine houses, and I am giving the count first because that is how it is done on this bank, and about four of you are going to make the other half into a bigger number than it is.*”

“*And about nine of them took bread from me this morning and about four of them did not and I am not going to say which four and I am not going to make a total of the two.*”

“*Here is what has happened in that village since Friday, and nobody in this county asked it and I did not ask it.*”

“*One: about four houses have stopped sending the second of their two carts down that road in the morning.*”

“*A woman of forty-one with two carts stopped on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. Two more stopped on Friday of this week and I have walked down there and asked about one of them and the answer was that the road is not to be used for carrying on a day.*”

“*There is no day on anything about nine of us have seen. That is the part I cannot make into a figure and I am not going to.*”

“*Another one has put its own name on its own paper with a day against it, in its own hand.*”

“*I have not asked it about that either. I have been down that road twice in four days and I am not going a third time in a fortnight.*”

“*That is nine houses and I have given three of them and I am not going to give the other six.*”

“*It is the same three I gave on the Tuesday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week, and it is not the same three, because about four of them have stopped since then.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for four miles and nobody is going to be told I was brave about walking it and coming back the same morning.*”

About four of them had a woman of forty-four who had walked four miles and given three things out of nine and refused the other six.

About nine of them had four houses that have stopped sending carts down a road because of a day nobody on that bank has seen written down anywhere.

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and what it is, and said he was not going to go up that road, and gave the reason.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and two days, two hundred and two being seven hundred and fifty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. Yesterday a woman of twenty-eight said in five sentences that the last four of his wants have been about other people and not about this county.

“*Two hundred and two is seven hundred and fifty-six less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county two hundred and two days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*Here is the want and I am giving it before the shape, and I have said the word about eleven times in about eleven days now and I am going to stop saying it after this morning.*”

“*I want to go up that cart road four miles and stand in front of about four houses and read the paper out to them, and I am not going to.*”

“*A woman of forty-four just said she is not going down that road a third time in a fortnight and that about four houses have stopped sending carts on it since Friday because of a day nobody on that bank has seen. I have been in this county two hundred and two days and I have never walked those four miles in the morning.*”

“*And here is the reason I am not going, and it is not that somebody would thank me for it.*”

“*A man of sixty-one said on Tuesday morning that he wants that hearing to stand. A woman of thirty-four said on Sunday that six of the nine were in that room and were not asked.*”

“*And if I walk up that road this morning and read a paper at about four houses, then about nine people in four counties will have a man of thirty-one going up a road saying a thing about a day nobody has seen written down.*”

“*That is the third time in about two weeks that I have worked out a distance in my own head before I opened my mouth.*”

“*I am saying the number out loud this morning because a woman of twenty-eight told me on Tuesday that about nine people would rather I asked nothing than asked in that order.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about that and nobody is going to be thanked and nobody is going to be told I was right on Tuesday about the shape.*”

“*Fifty-six days. Fifty-six is seven hundred and fifty-six less seven hundred. Nobody has answered it and nobody is going to answer it for me and I am not going to answer it, and I am not going to say that word again on this bank for about four days.*”

Then a boy of thirteen carried five counts and said he was going to carry six on Friday, and gave the working for all five.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has kept five counts since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eightth week. He has carried five every morning since.

“*A hundred and three days. A hundred and three is seven hundred and fifty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*And thirty-five mornings, and a hundred and twenty-six days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and twenty-seven for a woman in a reed, and two numbers in a book.*”

“*That is five and I am going to carry six on Friday and I am telling about nine of you now so that about four of you cannot say on Friday morning that they did not know.*”

“*The sixth one is a thing a woman of forty-four said and it is not a day-count.*”

“*I said last week that I would not carry a figure that is not a day-count. I am breaking that on Friday and I am saying today that I am breaking it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for five and nobody is going to be told I was right about the two numbers.*”

Then about nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a woman of thirty-four who was not going to be asked anything and a woman of forty-four who had walked four miles.

A boy of thirteen said he was going to break his own rule on Friday, and a man of thirty-one said he was not going to walk the same four miles.

---

The fever was sixty-seven weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and two days.

A woman of forty-four came down four miles and said that about four houses in that village have stopped sending the second of their carts down that road since Friday, because of a day that nobody on that bank has seen written down anywhere.

And a woman of thirty-four who walked two days was still standing at the end of that bank and said out loud in four sentences that nobody has asked her a question and nobody is going to.

And a man of thirty-one said he wants to walk those four miles and read the paper to about four houses, and said he is not going to.

He gave the reason: about nine people in four counties would then have a man of thirty-one going up a road saying a thing about a day nobody has seen written down.
