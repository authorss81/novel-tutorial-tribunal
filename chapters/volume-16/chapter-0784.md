# Chapter 784: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Wednesday, And He Walks To The End Of What He Can Walk And Stops There, And A Woman Of Sixty-Four Says What She Is

Second day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-fourth morning. Three hundred and eighty-four days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and four days. Three hundred and thirty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the third morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and said he had held the end of nothing this week.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He offered on Monday to hold the far end of anything about nine of them measured, and a man of thirty-one did not take him up on either of the two mornings after that.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the third morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen, and it does not go on rising. Four inches a day underneath it, and that is the working.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in two days from this morning, which is the Friday of this week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Friday.*”

“*On Monday I said I would hold the far end of anything measured.*”

“*I have held the end of nothing on Tuesday or this morning, and I am saying so before about four of you notice it on Friday.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-third morning. He asked out loud first, and said he was going to go on not noticing, and gave the figure for that.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week and about four of them have been treating him differently since, and he has said so out loud twice.

“*Sixty-three mornings.*”

“*Sixty-three is one more than sixty-two, and I said on Sunday that I would not say the working again and I have said it.*”

“*About four of you have been not asking me about the fifth thing for a week and I have stopped noticing it.*”

“*The figure for that is a week, and I am giving it to you, because about nine of you have not given me a figure for anything all week.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and gave the count of that sheet and did not read a second line, because nobody asked him for one.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He read a different line on Tuesday because a woman of forty asked him for it and he has not been asked for anything since.

“*Four names. I said the number yesterday and I am saying it this morning and it is the fourth morning running.*”

“*Seventy-four is seven hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred and ten, and I read only the ninth this morning because a man of fifty-four asked and I read only that one because nobody asked me for another.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-six days, and eighty-six is seven hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*And I read a line out loud on Tuesday because a woman of forty asked me, and nobody has asked me for anything since Tuesday, and I am not going to ask.*”

Then he walked to the end of what he can walk, and stood there, and gave the whole of it out loud in the order he can walk it.

He had a woman of forty-four hold the count of it and a man of about fifty-two check the figures, and one of the figures could not be checked and he said so before anybody asked him about it.

“*Here is the whole of it in the order I can walk it.*”

“*I am giving the last figure first, because about four of you have not heard it, and it is the one that decides the week.*”

“*The third of the three is a person being put in a room, and it happened four hundred miles off this landing.*”

“*That is the figure three people in this county have given out loud, and it is not mine, and I have not measured it, and nobody here can.*”

“*The last of it I can walk is three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road to the low gate.*”

“*That is two thousand nine hundred and forty paces of mine, at two yards a pace.*”

“*And a bakehouse is about four miles up that same road, and about nine houses go up to it for bread.*”

“*And between the landing and the low gate there is a turn at four hundred yards that is not a piece of ground.*”

“*There is a house at about two miles that is two pieces of ground.*”

“*There is about two hundred yards of bank down to the water, and about ninety steps cut in that bank, and those steps are about half a mile of water below the landing on the up-water side.*”

“*So here is the map in the order it can be walked, and it is three things.*”

“*One of them is not a piece of ground, and one of them is two, and one of them I cannot get to.*”

“*I have been out farther every morning this week and I have got one day nearer to the end of it every morning.*”

“*I am going to say the end of it now, so that about four of you do not have to wait until Friday.*”

Then a woman of forty said she was not going to name the room, and said why, and said it was not what she had asked for.

Wenna Callow is forty and has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a reason instead. The third of the three things that have a day against them is a person being put in a room, and it is the only one of the five she was ever given a day for without asking.

“*I have a day against it and I am not going to name the room this morning.*”

“*That is not me stopping you. You asked me for the ground of three things and I have given you two of them.*”

“*I have given them to the yard and to the strip at the back of the wash-house.*”

“*I am not going to give you the third one out loud in about nine people’s hearing on a Wednesday morning.*”

“*And I am not going to say I will never. I have been not saying a thing for eleven years, and I have found out twice this week that not saying a thing and refusing a thing are not the same.*”

“*I am not going to say the word for what I did on Tuesday, because a man of fifty-four said it first and better.*”

Then a woman of sixty-four said what she is, and said she is not going to be the second person to say the other thing, and nobody thanked her.

Ada Renk is sixty-four. She got up out of a book of heads and carts in the open nineteen years ago and it cost her one thing a day of them. She has a name in ink on a line that has never had a day against it. She said one name out loud once on the Thursday of last week and has asked for nothing since.

“*Nobody has put a thing in front of that woman with a place to put no, and I know what that is worth because I have been one for about nineteen years.*”

“*I have said one name out loud once in this county and it cost me three things and I gave all three of them out loud, and I am not going to be thanked for saying this today either.*”

“*A woman of forty has just said out loud in front of about nine of you that she is not naming a room.*”

“*About four of you are already doing the thing with your faces. I have watched that face being made about me for nineteen years and it is not what she is doing.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two gave the check, and said which one of the figures he could not check, and said the thing in the four leaves is still not said.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. He has four lines under him and can be checked against all four of them every morning of his working life. He has one thing in those four leaves he has not said out loud in about eleven years.

“*Three thousand five hundred and twenty yards and one thousand seven hundred and sixty paces, and eighteen yards by ten and eight yards by eighteen, and all of it checks twice.*”

“*The four hundred miles is the one I cannot check, and it is not because it is wrong.*”

“*It is because there is no line under me four hundred miles off to check it against.*”

“*I have said that out loud and I am not going to say it again this week.*”

“*And the thing in those four leaves is still not said and I have said nothing about it since the Wednesday of a week and a half ago.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said the figure of the map, and said what a figure of a bank is and what it is not.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and she has never once written it in the book.

“*I held the count of it and it is three things and it is on the back of my hand and it is not in the book, because the book is chairs.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-four mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-nine mornings, and seventy-nine is seven hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a fourth, which is a figure of this bank and not of anything else: three things.*”

“*Of the three, one is not a piece of ground, one is two pieces, and one is four hundred miles off.*”

“*That is not a finding. A finding would be a thing about people and there are no people in that count except a man of thirty-one walking.*”

“*I am not going to say what to do about the seventy-nine. I have said that since the Saturday of a fortnight and I have not found a fourth thing to add and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he cannot get to the third one, and gave the two mistakes, and gave the cost, and said the word out loud that he has been not saying.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty days, two hundred and thirty being seven hundred and eighty-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-nine days old, ninety-nine being seven hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Here is the want and it is the first one in three days that is not a shape and I am not going to dress it up.*”

“*I want all three walked before this week is out, and I have two days left.*”

“*I know the figure on Friday morning, and I am saying it now so that nobody spends Thursday waiting for it.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and there are two of them and they are the same mistake.*”

“*On Monday I called a turn a field, and on Tuesday I called a house a field.*”

“*This morning I got as far as the end of a road and said the third one was four hundred miles.*”

“*Every morning I have got one day farther out and one day nearer to saying out loud that I cannot do the third one.*”

“*And I have had four days to say it and I have spent every one of them walking.*”

“*Eighty-four days. Eighty-four is seven hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to four hundred miles.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of forty has a day against a room and will not name it, and about nine of you now know that she will not name it.*”

“*That is four more people knowing a thing about her, because I asked her in the open on a Wednesday morning.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for saying out loud that they cannot get to a thing, and nobody is going to be told I was brave about four hundred miles. I have not been to any of them.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-first morning, and said he was still not writing anything down.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-one mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-four days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-five days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-two, and that is seven hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other and I have not added a name to them, and I have not written down a room that a woman of forty will not name.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-first morning I have said it, and I have not asked any of you about it and I am not going to.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour of the second morning of that week, with a full bank behind them on the third morning of it.

A man of thirty-one had walked to the end of a road and stopped there in the open.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty days.

He gave the whole of what he can walk in the order he can walk it, and the last figure of it is four hundred miles and three people have given it and none of them has measured it.

A woman of forty said she was not going to name a room, and about four of them began doing the thing with their faces at the same moment.

A woman of sixty-four told about four of them that she had watched that face being made about her own name for nineteen years, and that it was not what a woman of forty was doing.
