# Chapter 799: Third Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Sixth Week, And A Thursday, And A Notice From A Bench Says Only A Foundational Hearing Can Choose And Will Not Be Given A Day, And A Woman Of Twenty-Nine In A Reed Comes Up That Bank On Her Own, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Two Different Nots

Third day of the hundred and thirty-sixth week. His five hundred and forty-ninth morning. Three hundred and ninety-nine days after the settlement. The fever seventy-three weeks and five days. Three hundred and fifty-three days since the division. Two hundred and ninety-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour, five days of it, and the man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time and then gave a fifth thing he has not given before.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of the week before last and has not been asked about it by anybody.

“*Sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is five days since it came off, and it is a full bank on the second morning of it.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath the holding, and I have said that at the seventh hour every morning this bank has been full, and about four of you have stopped needing to hear it and about four of you have not.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in three days, and that is the Sunday of this week, and it will be the seventeenth time, and I said on Tuesday that it would be a Sunday.*”

“*And here is the fifth thing, and I have been keeping it since the Saturday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week and about nine of you have not asked me for it.*”

“*There is a man at the top of that cut who has not said a figure out loud in four mornings, and he has said one every morning for a fortnight, and I have noticed, and I am not a man who is asked to be a keeper and I am not going to start.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, seventy-eighth morning, and asked out loud first, and asked who was reading it and why it was not the same man.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and holds the gravel on that landing and cannot read. He asked for the whole of a sheet to be read out at the top of that cut on Tuesday and has not asked for it again.

“*Seventy-eight mornings, and I am asking who is reading it, because it has been a man of thirty-eight for seventy-eight mornings and this morning it was a woman of twenty-nine who came up that bank and I did not know she could read.*”

“*And I want to say that I am glad, and I am not going to say I am glad, because a man of thirty-eight stopped on Monday on purpose and a woman reading it instead of him is a thing that happened to him and not a thing that happened because of him.*”

“*I have been not asked anything for about three weeks and I worked out on Wednesday that nobody wants anything from me, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read and nobody wants anything from is a thing I have to say out loud once and then not again.*”

“*Here it is: I would like one thing, and it is a day, and it is a day against a name and I am not going to say whose name because I have not got that far and about four of you would use it.*”

Then a notice came down that cart road on Wednesday and a man of sixty-one read it out, and said what it is, and said what it is not, and refused to write it in his book.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He has stopped saying every morning that he wants that hearing to stand, and said why.

“*This came down the cart road on Wednesday behind a load of gravel and a man gave it to me at the low gate and did not say who had sent it.*”

“*It says that the Bench has considered the matter of a preliminary case against the Bench and that only a Foundational Hearing can choose between the standing of the old rules and the making of new ones by the people, and that the Foundational Hearing is not a thing this court will set a day for.*”

“*That is the whole of it. It is one side of one leaf and it has a number on the top of it and no name, and I am reading it out in the ordinary voice because that is the only voice I have for it.*”

“*And here is what it is not.*”

“*It is not a refusal. It settles nothing for a woman of sixty-four. It settles nothing for a man in an ash, or for a woman in a reed, or for me.*”

“*It is a notice that a thing is going to be decided somewhere else, later, by somebody, and that nobody here will be told when.*”

“*And I am not writing it in that book, and that is four weeks now, and I have two numbers in eleven years, and I am not going to start on a notice that a Bench sent to a gravel cart.*”

Then a woman of twenty-nine came up that bank and read the ninth line in the open because she can read, and was not asked anything, and said one word on her own and nobody repeated it.

Marda Vell is twenty-nine and has been in a reed about a hundred yards off that drain a hundred and seventy days, and there is a day against her name on that ninth line and there has never been one. She said five words out of that reed on a Saturday three weeks ago and they were that nobody asked any of us, and she answered one word to a woman of forty about ground on the Monday of that week.

“*I can read. Nobody knew that until this morning, and I have been in that reed a hundred and seventy days and about four of you have walked past me and looked down, and not one of you ever asked whether I could read.*”

“*And I came up this bank this morning because a man of fifty-four asked at the top of a cut who was reading it, and nobody answered him, and I am the one who came up, and I did not ask anybody for anything to do it.*”

“*And I am not going to be asked a question this morning. I have had one question in three weeks and it was about ground and I said one word, and I have been thinking about the other one for three weeks and I am going to say it now and then it is said.*”

“*Nobody asked any of us.*”

“*And that is what I said on a Saturday three weeks ago and nobody asked me who us is, and I am not going to say who us is, and it is four people, and I have known that since a Monday, and nobody is going to be sent for about any of the four of us.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one is not going to ask me and I can tell by the way he is standing.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave three counts, and a boy of thirteen gave two, and a man of thirty-one said what the notice is and refused to put his hand in it, and gave the want and the mistake and the cost.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She said she is not going to put a notice on that counter to make it a fifth thing.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on that counter fifty-five mornings with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing on that counter forty-nine mornings with a number on them and no room and no names.*”

“*And three: ninety-four mornings of this bank, and ninety-four is seven hundred and ninety-nine less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a notice came down that road on Wednesday and it is not on that counter and it is not in that book and I am not going to put it on my counter to make it a fifth thing.*”

“*I have been asked three times this week whether a case is a thing like mine, and I have not answered, and this morning I am going to say the reason out loud once.*”

“*Because the answer is that my three numbers are of things, and if I say a case is a thing then the case is a thing, and a thing is a thing anybody can be sent for about, and that is what has been going wrong for a fortnight.*”

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and forty-six days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of the week before last, and has been counting the mornings about four of us do not say a figure.

“*A hundred and forty-six days, and I counted them in the lane and it is one more than yesterday.*”

“*And the third count is six, and I am stopping it, because a man of forty-three gave a fifth thing this morning and it was about himself and not about a number, and I have been counting for five days and I have got what I was counting.*”

“*And a woman of twenty-nine came up that bank and can read and nobody knew, and I am the only one on this bank who saw her come up, and I have not said anything about it for about an hour, and I am saying it now because I decided on Tuesday that I would stop deciding.*”

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and forty-five days, two hundred and forty-five being seven hundred and ninety-nine less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across that palm is a hundred and fourteen days old, a hundred and fourteen being seven hundred and ninety-nine less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked six times in about a fortnight whether it will keep and nobody has said.

“*The notice says a thing is going to be decided somewhere else, later. That is what it says and I am not going to improve on it in front of about nine of you.*”

“*I have been not opening an opening for a fortnight. I stood at a ninth opening with ninety cut steps dry on a Saturday and I did not open it, and I am not allowed to until the final hearing, and now there is a notice saying that hearing will not be given a day.*”

“*So I am not opening it, and I am not going to put my hand in it, and I want to be exact that those are two different nots and I have been treating them as one for a fortnight.*”

“*I am not allowed to open it. I am not going to open it. And the second one is mine and the first one is a thing that was done to me and about four of you have been hearing me say them as one thing since the Friday of the week before last.*”

“*The want is the sixth morning of being said a different way, and it is the same want, and I am not going to get tired of it before one of these people can be asked.*”

“*Somebody who can say no to me, and come back the next morning, and be asked again.*”

“*The mistake is the fifteenth of the same mistake and it is four days old, and a man of fifty-four said it this morning without me. I have been saying not allowed and meaning refused, and he has been saying them apart for a fortnight, and I have been getting nearer to understanding it and I have not got there.*”

“*Ninety-nine days. Ninety-nine is seven hundred and ninety-nine less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer, and a notice came down that road yesterday and it did not answer it, and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*The cost is that a woman of twenty-nine came up a bank this morning to read a line she has never been asked to read, and a man of fifty-four said he would like a day and would not say whose name, and a man of about fifty-two has stopped saying a figure, and a man of forty-three has stopped saying anything at all about a thing he has noticed about himself.*”

“*And I put four of those things in the way this week by standing on this bank for ten days.*”

“*Nobody is relieved of any of the four. Nobody says a word of thanks to me for them. Nobody gets told I was brave about a fortnight.*”

“*And the right of refusal is not restored this morning. I am not going to say it was. And a woman in a reed has said four people out loud for the first time and nobody asked her.*”

About nine of them were on that bank at about the eleventh hour of a Thursday, and a notice about a hearing that will not be given a day had been read out in the ordinary voice.

The fever was seventy-three weeks and five days old. He had been in that county two hundred and forty-five days.

A notice came down that cart road behind a load of gravel and said that only a Foundational Hearing can choose, and would not be given a day, and a man of sixty-one refused to write it down.

A woman of twenty-nine in a reed came up that bank on her own, because a man of fifty-four asked who was reading it and nobody answered him.

And a man of thirty-one said those are two different nots and that he has been treating them as one for a fortnight, with a ninth opening a hundred and thirty feet under that bank and ninety cut steps dry above it on the Saturday before last.
