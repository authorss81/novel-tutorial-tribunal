# Chapter 761: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirtieth Week, And A Monday, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Is Not At The End Of That Bank, And Nobody On It Says So For About A Minute

Seventh day of the hundred and thirtieth week. His five hundred and eleventh morning. Three hundred and sixty-one days after the settlement. The fever sixty-eight weeks and two days. Three hundred and fifteen days since the division. Two hundred and sixty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the seventh morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gives three things every morning out of three he has never added to, and he said five times in a row that he had nothing to say about a night and then stopped saying it.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour.*”

“*And that is the seventh morning of it running, and a bank that is full stays at sixteen until it comes off.*”

“*Seven days of it. It came full on the Friday of this week.*”

“*And four inches a day is the working I give.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow morning, which is the first day of a new week.*”

“*I said that yesterday and I am saying it again because it is what I say.*”

“*Seven weeks of three things a morning and I am not going to give anybody a figure for how long I have been doing that.*”

Then about nine of them stood at the top of that bank, and the place at the bottom of it where a woman of thirty-four had stood for six days was not taken by anybody.

Nothing was said about that for about a minute.

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fortieth morning, and a man of thirty-eight read it because he was asked.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one of them. He said on Friday morning that he was going to stop putting a figure on how long he has been waiting for a fifth thing, because about four of them had started counting it for him.

“*Forty mornings.*”

“*Forty is one more than thirty-nine and that is the working and it is not much of a working.*”

“*I said on Friday that I was going to stop putting a figure on the waiting.*”

“*And I have not stopped, and I am not going to say that out loud to about nine of you every morning either.*”

“*A fifth thing was asked of me on a Sunday of a fortnight.*”

“*It has not been answered and nobody has asked me about it and I am still standing here.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty and nobody is going to be told I was brave about asking for the same thing forty times.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight gave the count of that sheet, because he had been asked for it, and because it is his count and he gives it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and he has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and fifty mornings of that count had been counted by yesterday morning.

“*Fifty-one is seven hundred and sixty-one less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And there has not been one of the fifty-one in the dark.*”

“*I was asked yesterday and I read it, and I was asked this morning and I read it.*”

“*About four of you know the difference between those two things and about nine of you do not.*”

“*And I have carried a board in the inside of this coat since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week.*”

“*I am not going to open it on a Monday morning when one of the about four of you is not standing on this bank at all.*”

Then a woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs came up that bank, put her hand flat on the counter, and gave the two counts in the order she has given them in for eleven mornings.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to, and she has not asked anybody to take them off that counter since either.

“*Two things on that counter and I am giving them as two.*”

“*Nobody is going to add them.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter seventeen mornings.*”

“*Nothing in any of the three columns and no column on it for a person stopping.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter eleven mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And I am not going to say one thing about the eleven this morning.*”

“*A woman was not at the end of this bank and that is not a thing about paper.*”

“*About four of you were already waiting for me to say it and I am not going to be thanked for not saying it.*”

Then a woman of forty stood at the counter and said what she was standing in front of, and gave the one thing she has said that is not a count, and it was not a rule and not manners.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them, and she said on a Monday of a fortnight that she would not and has not started.

“*Say what you are standing in front of.*”

“*Four leaves on a counter and one place at the bottom of a bank where nobody is standing.*”

“*And I am not going to add the second of those to the first of them.*”

“*I have asked for a day against a name for eleven years.*”

“*I have never once asked for a day against nothing, and there are about nine days on that paper against nothing, and I noticed that on Friday and I have not worked out what I noticed.*”

“*I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and did not add any of them to any other, and refused a fifth reason for that.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written two numbers in it in eleven years, one of them six and written on the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He stopped a count of days himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has refused the figure of it out loud ever since.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash on the low side of that landing for a hundred and thirty-one days.*”

“*A hundred and thirty-one is seven hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and thirty-two days, and that is seven hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*I have not added those two and I am not going to.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and seven days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and nineteen, and that is seven hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added those two to the other two either and I am not going to.*”

“*And there are two things on that counter this morning with no column on either of them, and I have not added those to anything, and nobody on this bank is going to ask me for a fifth reason.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the want first and the mistake after it and the cost last, in that order, because a woman of twenty-eight told him on a Wednesday of a fortnight that the order of those two things is the whole of what he is.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and seven days. The cut across it is seventy-six days old, seventy-six being seven hundred and sixty-one less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want.*”

“*I want about nine of you to go back to whatever it was you were doing on Friday morning before any of you said what I am.*”

“*And here is the mistake and it is mine.*”

“*A woman of thirty-four walked two days and stood at the end of this bank for six days while about nine of you looked at her.*”

“*Yesterday morning I said a sentence about her out loud in front of all of you, and I had not found out she was not here to hear it.*”

“*And I found out she was not here about a minute after about nine of you did.*”

“*I have been standing at the top of this bank for two hundred and seven days being the thing about four people come up to.*”

“*Two hundred and seven is seven hundred and sixty-one less five hundred and fifty-four.*”

“*That figure did not change this morning when one of them was not here.*”

“*It is still about four, and it will be about four tomorrow whether she comes back or not, and I cannot use it for anything.*”

“*Sixty-one days. Sixty-one is seven hundred and sixty-one less seven hundred.*”

“*That is what I have given every morning since the fifty-first.*”

“*None of it is an answer and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with the water still up and two things on a counter and one place at the bottom of it empty.

The man of forty-three gave his three things into the gap where nothing had been said for about a minute, and the wind came off the water and went up past about nine people who did not say thank you for them.

---

The fever was sixty-eight weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and seven days.

A woman of thirty-four who had walked two days was not at the end of that bank and nobody on it said so for about a minute, and then a man of forty-three said about sixteen inches and a figure for tomorrow, into the gap.

And a woman of forty said she has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a day against nothing, and that there are about nine days on that paper against nothing, and that she noticed it on Friday and has not worked out what she noticed.

And a man of sixty-one gave four counts and did not add any of them to any other, and said that there are two things on that counter with no column on either of them and that he has not added those to anything.

And a man of thirty-eight said that about four of you know the difference between being asked to read and not being asked, and that he has carried a board in the inside of his coat since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week and is not going to open it on a Monday.

And a man of thirty-one said he wanted about nine of them to go back to what they were doing before Friday, and said that the figure of about four of them did not change when one of them was not there, and that he cannot use it for anything.
