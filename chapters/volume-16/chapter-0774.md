# Chapter 774: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Sunday, And A Woman Of Forty Says Out Loud That A Name With No Day On It Is A Day With No Name On It, And That She Has Wanted Both Of Them For Eleven Years

Sixth day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twenty-fourth morning. Three hundred and seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and one day. Three hundred and twenty-eight days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was full again at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the first morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and said a bank that is full holds until it comes off and did not add anything to that.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He has said the working four mornings running now and about four of them have stopped saying it back at him, and he has not noticed, and he has said he has not noticed.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the first morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off. Four inches a day is the working underneath it.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, which is the Thursday of next week, and a full bank does not shorten that.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-third morning, and he asked out loud first, and nobody on that bank said anything at all about the fact that he has now asked fifty-three times.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has asked out loud before anybody speaks since the Tuesday of a fortnight ago. He has asked nothing of anybody for about six weeks.

“*Fifty-three mornings.*”

“*Fifty-three is one more than fifty-two.*”

“*I have been asking out loud for nine days and about four of you have stopped noticing and about nine of you have not, and neither of those is a figure I am going to use for anything.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered and I have not been asked about it and I am not going to say one word about it until somebody asks me.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day, and gave the count of that sheet first, and said the two of them are a pair this morning and were not a pair on Tuesday.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has said a different reason every morning this week for not opening a coat, and he said on Wednesday that he would not say which of them he started with, and a man of thirty-one read that back to him on Wednesday morning in front of about nine of them.

“*Sixty-four is seven hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read that line and I read that day this morning because two different people asked me for two different things and I have stopped noticing the difference.*”

“*On Tuesday I would have told you they were the same job. On Saturday I told you they were not. This morning I have stopped being able to say which of those two was true and I am not going to pick one so that about four of you feel better.*”

“*I have a board in the inside of this coat with thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it, and it has been there seventy-six days, and seventy-six is seven hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and ninety-eight, and I am not opening it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for a figure I have counted myself.*”

Then a woman of forty said the thing she has wanted to say since a man of about fifty-two said it out loud on Friday, and it took her eleven years to get to and she gave the working for the eleven years.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name. She has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. On the Thursday of a fortnight ago she said out loud that she wants a day with nothing against it, and it has not been given to her.

“*On the Thursday of a fortnight ago I said I want a day with nothing against it. Not a name, not a house, not a cart.*”

“*I have wanted that for about nine days and I have been standing in front of a man of thirty-one and about four of you since the Thursday, waiting for somebody to hand it to me.*”

“*And on Friday a man of about fifty-two said that a line has names on it and no day, and I stood there and worked out that the two of those are the same thing, and they are not the same, and that is worse.*”

“*Here is the working. Eleven years. I have asked for eleven years for a day with a name against it. Eleven years is how long I have been looking at names that have got nothing against them.*”

“*A name with no day on it is a day with no name on it. I have been asking for both of them for eleven years and I did not know I was asking for two, because a day with a name on it and a name with a day on it are the same object and I have wanted them the whole time.*”

“*And about four of you have been thinking since the Thursday of a fortnight that I want something softer than what I have wanted since I was twenty-nine, and I do not, and I am not going to be comforted about that and I am not going to be thanked for saying it.*”

“*And I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and I have been asked by two of you in about nine days and I have said no twice, and I am going to keep the answer in my own mouth until I have said the rest of it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and said what she is going to do about the sixty-eight mornings now that a woman of forty has answered her.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted sixty-eight mornings on Saturday with a pencil and a crumb of bread and said she was not going to say what to do about it.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-four mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And a woman of forty has just told me what she wants, and it is a day with a name on it, which is the thing I have been putting in that book for nineteen years under a chair.*”

“*I am not going to say I will do anything about the sixty-eight. I said on Saturday that I wanted somebody in about nine people’s hearing to notice that I had not said what to do about it, and a woman of forty has noticed it and has said what she wants, and those are two different things and neither of them is a plan.*”

“*And I am not going to be thanked for counting, and I am not going to be told I was brave about a pencil and a crumb of bread.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said that the two ninth days have been said out loud three times and he is not saying them again, and gave his own count, and asked for one thing for the first time in about a week.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and twenty-one days ago and keeps his own count of it. He has carried five counts on that bank since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and has told everybody he was going to carry six.

“*A hundred and twenty-one days. A hundred and twenty-one is seven hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*I am not going to say the two ninth days this morning because I have said them three times without being asked and a woman of forty-four has just counted sixty-eight mornings of people saying things out loud without being asked, and I have been doing it at the same time as everybody else.*”

“*Here is my one thing and it is the first one in about a week. I want somebody to ask me about the fifth thing that was asked of a man of fifty-four on a Sunday of a fortnight.*”

“*Not to ask me. To ask him. I have been carrying that since the Wednesday of a fortnight and I have not carried it as a sixth count, and I am not going to carry it as a sixth count, and I am saying it out loud so that it is a thing said and not a thing I am sitting on.*”

“*Nobody has asked me twice and nobody is going to be thanked for me carrying five.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he is not going to ask a man of fifty-four either, and gave the reason, and it was about the thing he wanted on Saturday.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty days, two hundred and twenty being seven hundred and seventy-four less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-nine days old, eighty-nine being seven hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is about a boy of thirteen and not about me.*”

“*I want the fifth thing to stay unasked until Monday. I want about four of you to hear that and about nine of you to hear it and I am not going to explain it, and a man of about fifty-two is going to say it is the wrong call.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is four days old.*”

“*On Saturday I wanted a woman of forty-four to be the mouth the sixty-eight stop out of. This morning a boy of thirteen has asked for the one question that would break all sixty-eight of them, and I want him not to ask it until Monday.*”

“*And the reason I want that is that I have not found out what happens to about nine of you on the Tuesday. I have wanted a person to be the one who went for about nine days and I still do, and I have not got round it, and that is the whole of what I am.*”

“*Seventy-four days. Seventy-four is seven hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And the cost is that a boy of thirteen said out loud this morning that the one question nobody has asked is standing behind a man of fifty-four’s asking, and I have just told about nine of you I hope it is not asked for four more days.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with a full water behind them and a boy of thirteen at the front of it who had said the name of a question instead of the question.

---

The fever was seventy weeks and one day old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty days.

A woman of forty said that a name with no day on it is a day with no name on it, and that she has wanted both of them for eleven years, and that nobody is going to be comforted about that.

And a boy of thirteen said he is not going to say the two ninth days a fourth time because he has said them three times without being asked, and asked for one thing, which is that somebody should ask a man of fifty-four about the fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight.

And a woman of forty said that a name with no day on it is a day with no name on it, and that she has wanted both of them for eleven years.
