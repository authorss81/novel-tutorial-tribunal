# Chapter 790: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fifth Week, And A Tuesday, And A Full Bank On The First Morning Of It, And He Gives The Four Things Away In The Order He Got Them, And Says What They Are For

First day of the hundred and thirty-fifth week. His five hundred and fortieth morning. Three hundred and ninety days after the settlement. The fever seventy-two weeks and three days. Three hundred and forty-four days since the division. Two hundred and ninety days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the first morning of it again. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he gave the day-name on the word, because it is the first morning of a week and about four of them write the day down on the day and not on the day after.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He named a stone on that bank on the Sunday of last week and about four of them have walked past it since.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the first morning of it, and it will hold there for four days.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off. Four inches a day is the working underneath it, and I have given that on every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, which is the Saturday of this week.*”

“*That is the sixteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it will be a Saturday.*”

“*And about four of you have walked past my stone this week and about nine of you have not, and I am not going to do anything about either of those two figures.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-ninth morning. He asked out loud first, and then asked the fifth thing, and about four of them did not answer him.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He said on Thursday of last week that he would start asking about the fifth thing on Monday.

“*Sixty-nine mornings.*”

“*Sixty-nine is one more than sixty-eight, and that is the working.*”

“*And I asked about the fifth thing after it was read, in the ordinary voice, the way I said I would on Thursday, and about four of you did not answer me.*”

“*I have been asked the fifth thing once and I answered it at the Wednesday of the week before last, and I am not going to be treated as a man who has been asked twice because I asked about it myself.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day and gave the count of that sheet and the number of names, and said what nobody has asked him for in ten days.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He has said the number of names he can see on the ninth line out loud every morning since the Friday of last week, and gave the reason once, on the Thursday of last week, and has not given it since.

“*Four names, and it is the tenth morning running, and I have still not opened this coat.*”

“*Eighty is seven hundred and ninety less seven hundred and ten, and I read only the ninth, because a man of fifty-four asked and nobody has asked me for another since Tuesday of last week.*”

“*Ten days. That is the longest anybody has gone without asking me for a thing, and it is not the same as nobody asking, because about nine of you have been asking me for the number every morning.*”

“*The board has been in this coat ninety-two days, and ninety-two is seven hundred and ninety less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

Then he gave the four things away in the order he got them, and about nine of them stood there and let him.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-six days, two hundred and thirty-six being seven hundred and ninety less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is a hundred and five days old, a hundred and five being seven hundred and ninety less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep.

“*One: the map. Three things, walked and counted out loud in front of about nine of you, and somebody else holding the count and somebody else disputing it.*”

“*A cart turning off a road is not a piece of ground, because the cart is not at that turn every day. A house being taken into is two pieces of ground, because there are two doors and they do not touch. A person being put in a room is four hundred miles off and I cannot get to it.*”

“*The measure is two yards to a pace of mine, and I got that out of two thousand nine hundred and forty paces over the three miles and six hundred yards a woman of forty-four named.*”

“*Two: the consent. A woman of sixty-four said the word and said what the word has to have in it, which is that she can take it back and names the day.*”

“*About four of you gave it, and about four of you gave it with a piece taken out of it, and one man took a whole piece out and said why in front of about nine of you.*”

“*Three: the margin. Nineteen paces long, six paces wide at the narrow end and nine at the wide end, on the flat of this bank, from a stone to a counter.*”

“*It shut once, on the Sunday, with a man of about fifty-two walking out of it and the far end coming back. It held once, on Monday, with a woman of forty-four standing at that end for an hour and a half.*”

“*Four: the blank interval, which is not open.*”

“*The water was off all ninety of those steps on the Friday of last week and I went down them and stood at the ninth opening and did not open it, and I am not allowed to until the final hearing.*”

“*And the whole of those four things is standing on me and I am going to say that before about four of you say it for me.*”

Then he said what they are for, and refused to put a day on it, and said why.

“*They are for the final hearing. That is what they are for and that is the only thing they are for, and that hearing is four hundred miles off in a room with nine clerks in it.*”

“*And I am not going to put a day against it, and about four of you have been waiting all week for me to produce one.*”

“*A day is the thing I have spent ten days failing to make. I have got three pieces of ground, one word out of a reed, and a woman who is not coming to a room four hundred miles off, and there is no day anywhere in any of it.*”

“*And a man of thirty-one is a person who asks. About four of you gave me something this week because I asked you for it, and about four of you walked off with a piece of it, and that is not a map and it is not a margin, it is four people doing what a man asked them to do on a bank.*”

“*That is what I am and I have known it since the Wednesday of last week and I have not said it out loud until this morning.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own count and said what he is going to do about it, and about nine of them heard it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and thirty-seven days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks, put the sixth down on the Thursday of last week, and held nineteen minutes at the top of ninety cut steps on the Friday of last week.

“*A hundred and thirty-seven days, and I counted it on my fingers in the lane, and it is nine more than a week ago.*”

“*I have not put a seventh count down and about four of you have stopped noticing whether I do.*”

“*And I told a man of thirty-eight on Monday that nobody has asked him for anything in ten days, and I said it out loud because I have been the one on that bank saying somebody ought to ask, for about two weeks.*”

“*I am not going to start asking anybody for anything. I have decided that and I am saying it out loud so that about four of you know I have decided it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said the round figure, and said what the week added up to, and said what it did not.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and eighty-five mornings is where it has got to.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-six mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter forty mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-five mornings, and eighty-five is seven hundred and ninety less seven hundred and five, and that is the figure.*”

“*And I am going to say the other one and then I am going to stop. I have said since the Saturday of a fortnight that I would not say what to do about them, and I am not saying it, and this morning I have found out why.*”

“*What to do about the eighty-five is somebody else’s morning and not mine. That is all. I have been asking about nine of you for what to do about a number for three weeks and the answer is that it is not a morning of mine, and I am not going to be thanked for finding that out late.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-seventh morning, and said he wrote nothing down all week.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-seven mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and sixty days, and that is seven hundred and ninety less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and sixty-one days, and that is seven hundred and ninety less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-six days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-eight, and that is seven hundred and ninety less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I wrote nothing down all week. Two numbers in eleven years and none this week, and I have not written a third and I am not going to.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-seventh morning I have said it, and about four of you have said yes to a thing this week and not one of you has said it about that.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said the want and the mistake and the cost, and the cost is that he asked for four things and got them and about nine of them did not go down.

“*Here is the want, and it is the same want I have said three times in about a fortnight and I am not going to say it a fourth time.*”

“*Ninety days. Ninety is seven hundred and ninety less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer and I am not going to answer it and nobody is going to answer it for me.*”

“*Ninety is a round figure and so was eighty, and I left that one alone, and I am leaving this one alone as well, and about four of you will hear that as a decision and it is not one.*”

“*The mistake is the sixth one of the same mistake and I am not going to give it a new wording at half past the tenth hour on the first morning of a week.*”

“*The cost is that about nine of you are on that bank this morning and about nine of you were on that bank on the Sunday before I started, and nobody has gone down, and I have not been thanked and nobody is going to be told I was brave about a week.*”

“*And I am not going to be relieved of any of it. I did four days of walking and one of holding and one of not opening, and every morning of it was because I asked about nine of you for it.*”

“*And I do not know what any of this is. A man of thirty-one who is not going to know what a thing he has spent ten days building is, does not get to call it a settlement, and about four of you have been waiting nine weeks for me to call it one.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour of the first morning of that week, with a full bank behind them on the first morning of it, and four things that belonged to a room four hundred miles off.

---

The fever was seventy-two weeks and three days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-six days, on his five hundred and fortieth morning.

He gave away four things in the order he got them and refused to put a day on any of them.

And he said the whole of it stands on him, and that a man of thirty-one is a person who asks, and nobody went down the bank.

A boy of thirteen told a man of thirty-eight that nobody had asked him for anything in ten days, and then told about nine of them that he was not going to start asking anybody for anything at all.
