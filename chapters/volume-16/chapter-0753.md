# Chapter 753: Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Week, And A Sunday, And The Bank Is Still Full At About Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step On The Seventh Morning Of It, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Comes Down About Two Days Off A Road With Nothing To Be Sent For About Her And Says She Was In That Room And That The Minute Is Not False And Is Not A True Account Of What Happened Either

Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. His five hundred and third morning. Three hundred and fifty-three days after the settlement. The fever sixty-seven weeks and one day. Three hundred and seven days since the division. Two hundred and fifty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the seventh morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then he stopped saying it. There are three things he gives every morning and there is not going to be a fourth thing out of them.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the seventh morning of it.*”

“*Seven days. It was full on the Monday of this week and a bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and I am not going to be thanked for saying that seven times this fortnight.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in one day from this morning, and that is tomorrow, and I have said that since Wednesday.*”

“*I am not going to give you a fourth thing.*”

Then a man of fifty-four had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the thirty-second morning, and he said the count before anybody asked him for it and nobody had asked him.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then he stopped saying it. There are three things he gives every morning and there is not going to be a fourth thing out of them. About nine of them were on that bank this morning.

“*Thirty-two mornings and it is the last day of a week and I am not going to be here tomorrow and neither is anybody else.*”

“*Tomorrow the water comes off all ninety at about the sixth hour for the eleventh time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and a man of forty-three has said that three mornings running and I am going to say it because he said it.*”

“*I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me and I am still waiting, and this morning is a Sunday of a fortnight and about four of you worked that out before I did.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the thirty-two and nobody is going to be told I was brave about any part of it.*”

Then a man of thirty-four said what he had in his coat and what he is not going to do with it, in three sentences, and the third one is the reason.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and forty-three times as of this morning. He carries a board in the inside of his own coat since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and has never read it out. He is the only person anybody in that county can be sent for about nothing.

“*Three sentences and the third of them is the reason and it is mine.*”

“*One: I have a board in the inside of this coat and I have had it since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week and I have never read it out in front of about nine people.*”

“*Two: a man of thirty-four said a word out loud on this bank on Friday of a week and last Friday and I am not going to carry a fifth thing out of a coat and open it in front of about nine people.*”

“*And I have read that sheet forty-three times in this county and forty-three is seven hundred and fifty-three less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the forty-three in the dark.*”

“*Three is the reason. About four of you have been waiting since Wednesday for me to bring it out, and about nine of you have not, and a thing that only about four of you are waiting for is a thing about four people and not about nine.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the three sentences and nobody is going to be told I was brave about keeping it in.*”

Then a woman of thirty-four came down that bank from about two days off a road, and she had been on it since about the sixth hour, and she was not one of the nine, and nobody could be sent for about her.

Her name is Brenna Dole and she is thirty-four and she has walked about two days from a place four hundred miles off this landing, and she cannot be sent for about in this county or in the one she came from, and she did not come for anything.

“*Two days on a road and I came down that bank about the sixth hour and I have been standing here about an hour and about four of you have looked at me twice.*”

“*I am going to say what I am standing in front of first, and then the thing I came to say, and then what I am not going to say.*”

“*And I am not going to be here tomorrow. The water is coming off that bank at the sixth hour and I am not going down about ninety steps with about nine people I have not met.*”

“*That thing on that counter is about a line with names on it, and I have been in the room where that was decided.*”

“*I was one of the nine.*”

“*And I am not going to say the minute is false, because it is not false. It says what it says and every word of it is a true word in the order it is in.*”

“*And I am not going to say the minute is a true account of what happened in that room, because it is not.*”

“*I want to be exact about the difference. I have been exact about it for about two days and I would like about four of you to have it exactly.*”

“*It says that nine people were heard about and did not object. That is a true thing. What is not a true thing is that nine people were heard about.*”

“*I was in the room. Six of us were in the room. Three of them were there for about four hours and the other three of them came in at the end and did not sit down.*”

“*And nobody asked me anything on that day and I said one thing, and the one thing I said was about a road, and it is not on those four leaves.*”

“*I am not going to say what it was about, because about four of you would then have the whole of what I said and about nine of you would have a piece of it.*”

“*I came two days to say that one sentence and I have said it, and I am not going to be thanked for the two days and nobody is going to be told I walked them for anything.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind with a woman of thirty-four who had been in the room standing at the end of it, and about four of them had six and about nine of them still had nine.

Then a man of sixty-one did the thing he has not done in eleven years, and said so, and refused the working.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written one number in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five.

“*I am going to write on the leaf after the last head this morning and I am going to say first that I have written one number in eleven years and that number is the last of the five.*”

“*And I am writing six and not nine, because a woman of thirty-four has just told me in about nine people’s hearing that there were six in that room and I have not seen her before this morning and I cannot be sent for about her to check it.*”

“*And I am not going to give the working for it.*”

“*The working for a figure of people is that they were there. I was not there. And she is not going to be asked twice about it.*”

“*A man of sixty-one who writes six on that leaf this morning has a figure of six in a book in this county for the rest of his life and no way of saying where it came from.*”

“*That is three times I have refused a reason in about two weeks and I am not going to give a fourth one and I am not going to be asked for one on this bank.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for six and nobody is going to be told I believe her. I do not believe anybody. I have written the number down.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and what he got wrong and what it cost, and he gave the working for his own figure first.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county a hundred and ninety-nine days. A hundred and ninety-nine is seven hundred and fifty-three less five hundred and fifty-four. Yesterday he said he wanted the woman who said that word to be asked what word it was and said he was not going to be the one who asks her.

“*A hundred and ninety-nine is seven hundred and fifty-three less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county a hundred and ninety-nine days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*Here is the want, and I am giving it before the shape, and I gave the shape first on Thursday and Friday and I am not going back to it.*”

“*I want her asked what six were in that room. Not her. The three who came in at the end and did not sit down.*”

“*And I am not going to ask her, because she came two days and said one sentence and I am not going to stand on this bank and turn a woman who walked two days into the mouth of three people she has never seen.*”

“*That is the whole of it and I am not going to put a shape round it this morning.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me on the Wednesday of a fortnight that the order of those two things is the whole of what I am. And I have been getting it wrong on purpose for about two days.*”

“*Here is the mistake. A man of sixty-one has just written six on a leaf after the last head and said he does not believe anybody and that is the first number a man of sixty-one has written in that book in eleven years.*”

“*He wrote it because a woman of thirty-four told about nine people that six of them were in that room.*”

“*And I have been on this bank for a hundred and ninety-nine days wanting somebody to say a true thing in front of me, and a man of sixty-one has written a number down instead.*”

“*That is not a finding and it is not a proof and I am not going to improve on it in about nine people’s hearing.*”

“*And here is the cost, and it is mine. On Friday of last week I gave up being nobody’s for one morning by saying a word out loud. This morning a man of sixty-one gave up eleven years of not writing anything by hearing a stranger say a number out loud on a bank, and about nine of us will carry that stranger’s number in a book in this county from this morning.*”

“*Not one of them is going to be relieved about that and not one of them is going to be thanked, and nobody is going to be told that either of those two men was right.*”

“*None of this is an answer to anything that was asked on a Wednesday fifty-three days ago. Fifty-three is seven hundred and fifty-three less seven hundred, and nobody is going to answer it for me and I am not going to answer it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen carried five counts and asked a question about one of them, and nobody answered him.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has kept five counts since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week.

“*A hundred days. A hundred is seven hundred and fifty-three less six hundred and fifty-three, and I counted it myself and nobody asked me.*”

“*And thirty-two mornings, and a hundred and twenty-three days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and twenty-four for a woman in a reed, and eleven years and a number for a man of sixty-one.*”

“*That is five and I have carried five every morning since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and I am not going to carry six.*”

“*And here is my question and it is one question and I am going to ask it once. The number a man of sixty-one just wrote down is six, and the number on the paper is nine, and the difference is three, and my mother is one of the nine that go down that cart road four miles.*”

“*Is three a number of people or is three a number of what I do not know yet?*”

“*Nobody has answered me and I am not going to ask it a second time this week.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the last morning of a week. A book in this county had a second number in it for the first time in eleven years, and a man of thirteen had asked one question and nobody had answered it.

The fever was sixty-seven weeks and one day old. He had been in that county a hundred and ninety-nine days.

A woman of thirty-four who had walked two days said in about nine people’s hearing that she was one of the nine, and that six of them were in that room, and that the minute is not false and is not a true account of what happened either. Nobody asked her what she said in that room.

And a man of sixty-one wrote six on the leaf after the last head of a book he has kept for eleven years, his second number in eleven years, and said he does not believe anybody and refused to give the working for it.

And a boy of thirteen asked whether three is a number of people or a number of what he does not know yet, and nobody answered him.
