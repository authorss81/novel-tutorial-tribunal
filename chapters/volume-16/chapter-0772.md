# Chapter 772: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Friday, And A Man Of About Fifty-Two Says Out Loud That He Read A Day Into A Line That Has Never Had One, And Gives The Working, And Nobody On That Bank Says Thank You

Fourth day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twenty-second morning. Three hundred and seventy-two days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks and six days. Three hundred and twenty-six days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was about eight inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and did not give the day of the week, because he gave it yesterday and he gives nothing twice.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He has counted thirteen of them off and has said out loud that he is not going to work out the first five in front of anybody, and a man of thirty-eight read that to him out of his own head on Thursday and did not improve on it.

“*About 8 inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is two days of it coming back.*”

“*Four inches a day, and the bank will be full on the Sunday of this week, and that is two days from this morning.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in six days from this morning.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the fifty-first morning, and he asked out loud first, and nobody thanked him, because nobody has thanked him for any of the fifty-one.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and cannot read. He holds the gravel on that landing. He has asked for that line every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week and has not missed one, and he has been asking out loud before anybody speaks since the Tuesday of a fortnight ago.

“*Fifty-one mornings.*”

“*Fifty-one is one more than fifty, and that is the working.*”

“*I gave a second figure yesterday and I am not going to give a third one this morning.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered. Nobody has asked me about it and I have not asked about it and I am not going to say one word more about it until somebody does.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and gave the count of that sheet first, with the working, and then read the day out loud because somebody asked him to, and said both of those things are the same job.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He has said he will read a day out loud if he is asked, and he said yesterday that he would not open a coat in front of anybody, and he said the reason was a different one again.

“*Sixty-two is seven hundred and seventy-two less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and about four of you asked for it before I got the ninth line read, and that is the same job as the ninth line and neither of them is a job I asked for.*”

“*I have said a different reason every morning this week for not opening this coat and I have said I will not say which one I started with.*”

“*Here is the one for today. A woman of sixty-four is on that line and has not been asked anything about it for about three weeks, and I am not going to be doing something else at the same time.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well asked for the fourth line to be read out loud, and then said the thing he had been holding since Wednesday, and gave the working for it in front of about nine of them.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and cannot see well, and he is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. He asked at the Tuesday of last week the one question nobody on that bank had asked, which is that the fourth line says about nine days and does not say nine days from which day. He has asked what a mark is worth as a figure four times in a fortnight and been answered by nobody.

“*Read the fourth line. All of it. And then read the fifth.*”

“*It says a finding is made in the absence of the persons heard about, that their absence is not to be read as consent, and that it is held open for about nine days for any person heard about to come forward.*”

“*The fifth line says the persons heard about are about a line with names against it and a day against it.*”

“*And I read a day into that fifth line at the Saturday of a fortnight ago. There is no day on it.*”

“*Here is the working and I am giving all of it. I have been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years and there is no fourth thing that comes before a fifth thing, and every morning I can be checked against the four lines under me.*”

“*On that Saturday I read the fifth line of four leaves and I read that it says a day, and it does not, and a man of thirty-eight put it on that counter the same morning with the wrong word in it.*”

“*The ninth line of a sheet of printed paper has never had a day against it. There is a day against eight of the nine lines and there has not been one against that one since the sheet was printed.*”

“*So a finding about that line was held open for about nine days for anybody to come forward on a day that is not on it.*”

“*That is not the fifth time I have asked what a mark is worth as a figure. It is the first time I have asked it about a day, and I am not going to let about four of you tell me it is the same question.*”

“*And a woman of sixty-four has a name in ink on that line. A man of thirty-four has a day against his name and a woman of twenty-nine has not, and about four of you know the second half of that and about nine of you do not.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for reading two lines and I am not going to be told I was brave about the working.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter and said what she is going to do with the thing she has been sitting on since Wednesday.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about, and neither is that book.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-eight mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty-two mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And the thing I have been sitting on since Wednesday is not about that counter and I am going to give it this morning because a man of about fifty-two has just said it first and I would rather it were his than mine.*”

“*I have counted what this bank does. Not the water. The rest of it, going back to the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, and it is a figure of mornings and I have got the working for it.*”

“*Every morning of those about nine weeks, about nine people have said a thing out loud on this bank, in front of each other, and not one of them has been asked one question by anybody.*”

“*A man of fifty-four has said so himself since Saturday of last week. A woman of sixty-four has said so since the Thursday of a week ago. That is two of them saying it in about nine people’s hearing and neither of them has been asked about it by anybody.*”

“*And I am going to say the rest of that in a woman of about fifty-two’s words and not mine, because it is his finding and I have only got the count.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave his own count, and said the two ninth days out loud again, and said he was not going to say them a fourth time unless somebody asked.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and nineteen days ago and keeps his own count of it. He has carried five counts on that bank since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and has told everybody he was going to carry six, and has not carried six.

“*A hundred and nineteen days. A hundred and nineteen is seven hundred and seventy-two less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*The two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it. A man of about fifty-two has just told you what those nine days are held open for.*”

“*And I worked them out on my fingers and both of them are right and I am not going to work them out a fourth time unless somebody asks me for it.*”

“*Nobody has asked me twice. I have said that four mornings running and I am not going to say it a fifth.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she was standing in front of, and it was a list, and the last thing on it was a line.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them. She said on the Thursday of a fortnight ago that she wants a day with nothing against it, and it has not been given to her and she has not asked again.

“*A man of about fifty-two has just said that a line has names on it and no day.*”

“*And I have asked for eleven years for a day with a name against it, and I have never once asked what happens to a name that has no day against it, because it never occurred to me and it should have.*”

“*There is a woman of sixty-four on that line and she is about nine feet from where I am standing and she has not been asked anything about it for about three weeks.*”

“*That is not one thing I am standing in front of this morning. It is nine, and about four of them are standing on this bank, and the two things we are all waiting for are a day and a reason and neither of those is what that line is.*”

“*And I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and nobody is going to be thanked for me noticing something on somebody else’s morning.*”

Then a man of thirty-one wanted the man of thirty-eight to say it before him and did not, and said what it cost him not to.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and eighteen days, two hundred and eighteen being seven hundred and seventy-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-seven days old, eighty-seven being seven hundred and seventy-two less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice that nobody say.

“*Here is the want and it is the first one in about two days that is not about a road.*”

“*I wanted to say that line this morning before a man of about fifty-two said it, and I have wanted to say it since Wednesday night, and I did not say it, and he said it.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is the same one again in a new place.*”

“*On Wednesday I wanted the finding to come back down that road to me. This morning I wanted the sentence to come out of my mouth before it came out of his.*”

“*Both of those are about a man of thirty-one being the first one to say a true thing in front of about nine of them, and I have been getting that wrong since the Wednesday of a fortnight and I have not got round it.*”

“*Seventy-two days. Seventy-two is seven hundred and seventy-two less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer.*”

“*And here is the cost. A man of thirty-eight has had a coat with a board in it for seventy-four days and he is not going to open it, and I have not asked him to, and a woman of sixty-four is on a line that has no day on it and I have not asked her about it, and about nine of you are on a bank this morning instead of on a road.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with the water two days back from the top step and a man of about fifty-two standing at the end of a thing he had carried since Wednesday.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and eighteen days.

A man of about fifty-two read two lines out loud and said he had read a day into the fifth of them at the Saturday of a fortnight ago, and gave the whole working for it, and said that a finding about that line was held open for about nine days for anybody to come forward on a day that is not on it.

And a woman of forty-four said she has counted what this bank does, and that about nine people have said a thing out loud on it every morning for about nine weeks and not one of them has been asked one question by anybody, and that two of them have said so themselves.

And a boy of thirteen gave his own count of a hundred and nineteen days, and said he worked the two ninth days out on his fingers this morning and that both of them are right, and that he is not going to do it a fourth time unless somebody asks him.
