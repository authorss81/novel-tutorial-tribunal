# Chapter 786: Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Week, And A Friday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Fifteenth Time, And He Walks Down Them With A Boy Of Thirteen Holding The Count And Stands At The Ninth Opening And Does Not Open It

Fourth day of the hundred and thirty-fourth week. His five hundred and thirty-sixth morning. Three hundred and eighty-six days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and six days. Three hundred and forty days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night, for the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Friday. The man of forty-three said the hour of it and the count of it and gave the day of the week on the word.

Then he gave the whole sequence again, because he had said on Monday that the next one would be a Saturday and about four of them wanted it said out loud.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He has counted fifteen of them off now. He offered on Monday to hold the far end of anything about nine of them measured and nobody took him up on any of the three mornings after that.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour this morning, and that is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Friday.*”

“*Eight days from the fourteenth, which was a Thursday. The day-name moves one place every cycle and I gave you the sequence on Thursday of the week before and I am giving it again.*”

“*Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday, and then it starts again on the Tuesday.*”

“*And I said on Monday morning that the fifteenth would be a Friday and it is a Friday, and about four of you had Friday written down wrong on Tuesday.*”

“*And the next one will be a Saturday, in eight days, and that is all I am going to say about it.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-fifth morning. He asked out loud first, and said what Monday is for now.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of last week and about four of them have been treating him differently since, and about four of them have not answered him since Monday.

“*Sixty-five mornings.*”

“*Sixty-five is one more than sixty-four, and that is the working.*”

“*I am going to start asking about the fifth thing on Monday and I said that on Thursday and I am saying it again on Friday so that about nine of you have it twice.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day and gave the count of that sheet, and said what he is going to do on a morning he could have done it on.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He said on the Friday of last week that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open, and on the Thursday of this week he gave the reason at last.

“*Four names. Six mornings running, and I said the reason out loud yesterday and I am not going to say it a second time.*”

“*Seventy-six is seven hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred and ten, and the line is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week.*”

“*I am not opening this coat this morning, and it is the fourth morning this week that ninety steps have been off and I have had the whole morning to do it in.*”

“*The board has been in it eighty-eight days, and eighty-eight is seven hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

“*A man of forty-three said the day-name sequence again this morning, and I have said six numbers, and about nine of you came down that bank after me.*”

Then he went down the ninety steps with a boy of thirteen holding the count of minutes, and stood in the dry passage, and did not open the ninth opening.

Corvin Tace went down first and called the times from the top, and about nine of them stood at the top of the cut and looked down it.

The dry passage is a hundred and thirty feet below that bank and there are nine openings in one wall of it. The ninth room has not been kept since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, which is fifty-six days ago.

“*Here is what is in my hand and I am going to say it before anybody asks me what I am doing down here.*”

“*Two pieces of ground two miles off, counted twice, checked twice. One turn at four hundred yards that is not a piece of ground. One room four hundred miles off that I cannot get to.*”

“*Four names on a line that has never had a day against it, and about four of you who said yes yesterday and one of you who took a piece of it out.*”

“*That is the whole of it. Five things and one word, and every one of them came out of a mouth in front of about nine of you this week.*”

“*And I am not opening this, and I am not putting my hand in it, and I have had my hand about four inches from it since the seventh hour.*”

“*I am not allowed to use any of those five things until the final hearing, and that hearing is four hundred miles off and I do not have a day for it.*”

“*And I am not going to stand in a passage a hundred and thirty feet under a bank and tell about nine of you that this is a difficulty. It is not a difficulty.*”

“*It is that I am not allowed, and I have not asked anybody to make it sooner, and I am not going to be relieved of having done the work first.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read said what that is and what it is not, out loud, and about nine of them heard a man who has been asked the hard thing all week keep the right of refusal where it belongs.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four and cannot read and holds the gravel on that landing. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week.

“*A man of thirty-one has just said that he is not allowed to open a thing he has the measure of, in front of about nine of us, a hundred and thirty feet down.*”

“*That is not a man who has refused something. A man who has refused something has had a thing put in front of him with a place to put no.*”

“*I have said that on this bank twice this week and about four of you have heard it both times and about nine of you have been waiting to see if I would say it a third time about him instead of about me.*”

“*I am saying it about him because he is standing at the ninth opening and I would like about nine of you to have it in your ears before Monday, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen gave the count he held and what the count is, and nobody asked him for a second one.

Wat Marshe is thirteen and has been in this county a hundred and thirty-three days. He carried five counts for about nine weeks and put the sixth down this week. He held the count of minutes at the top of ninety cut steps this morning.

“*A hundred and thirty-three days, and I counted it on my fingers in the lane, and it is six more than a week ago.*”

“*I held the minutes. Nineteen down and about four up, and I counted it twice on the way back up and the two are the same.*”

“*A man of forty-three called the times off the top and I did not use his. I am not going to say which of the two of us was right because neither of us was doing the same job.*”

“*And I have put the sixth count down this week and nobody has asked me what it was, and I am not going to tell them.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the three counts and said what she held this morning and who held it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and she has added nothing to it since.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter forty-two mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-six mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: eighty-one mornings, and eighty-one is seven hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And I held a thing this morning that is not a count, and it is that a boy of thirteen held nineteen minutes and a man of forty-three called the times off the top, and about four of you watched a man of thirty-one not do a thing.*”

“*I am not going to say what to do about the eighty-one. I have said that since the Saturday of a fortnight and I said it on Saturday before that.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted and what he did wrongly and what it cost, and it cost down a bank and not up one.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and thirty-two days, two hundred and thirty-two being seven hundred and eighty-six less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is a hundred and one days old, a hundred and one being seven hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Here is the want, and it is the first one in four days that is not a shape.*”

“*I want somebody on that bank to ask me to open it anyway. Not to ask me whether I will. To ask me to.*”

“*Nobody has, and I have been standing at that opening for two hours waiting for about four of them to think of it, and about nine of them were not going to.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is a week old and it is the same one as Thursday.*”

“*On Wednesday I asked nine people for something because I wanted it, and about four of them said yes because I asked, and a man of thirty-eight took a piece of it out.*”

“*I did the same thing again this morning, except worse. I have not asked anybody anything at all this morning. I have just stood at a thing I am not allowed to open and waited for a person to come and ask me to open it anyway.*”

“*Eighty-six days. Eighty-six is seven hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred, and none of it is an answer to a ninth opening.*”

“*And the cost is that I went down about ninety steps this morning, and a boy of thirteen counted nineteen minutes, and about nine of you stood at the top of that cut and looked down it, and I could not show any of you what I am not doing.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for counting the minutes and nobody is going to be told I was brave about standing at a door.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the twenty-third morning, and said the ninth room has not been kept in fifty-six days.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud twenty-three mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-six days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-seven days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and thirty-two days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty-four, and that is seven hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*The ninth room under that bank has not been kept since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and that is fifty-six days, and fifty-six is seven hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred and thirty.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the twenty-third morning I have said it, and about four of you were at the top of that cut this morning and none of you has said it.*”

About nine of them stood at the top of about ninety steps cut in that bank at about the ninth hour with all ninety steps off it for the fifteenth time, and a boy of thirteen holding a count of nineteen minutes.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and six days old. He had been in that county two hundred and thirty-two days.

The water came off all ninety steps for the fifteenth time and it was a Friday, and he gave the whole day-name sequence again because about four of them had it wrong.

He stood at the ninth opening and gave out loud the five things he had and did not use any of them, and said he was not allowed.

A man of fifty-four who cannot read put a sentence about refusing into about nine of their ears from the top of ninety cut steps, and did not want it noticed that he had said it.
