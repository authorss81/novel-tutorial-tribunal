# Chapter 777: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Wednesday, And A Woman Of Forty-Four Asks A Man Of Fifty-Four The One Question Nobody Has Asked Him, And He Answers It, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Says What The Precedent Is

Second day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and twenty-seventh morning. Three hundred and seventy-seven days after the settlement. The fever seventy weeks and four days. Three hundred and thirty-one days since the division. Two hundred and seventy-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was still full at about the seventh hour, about sixteen inches over the top step, and it was the fourth morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and gave the day of the week.

He said it was the day he had given a figure for yesterday.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. On the Tuesday of a week ago he said he had worked out the first five of the thirteen and did not believe his own figure, and he has not given another since.

“*About 4 inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is one day of it coming back.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and that is the fourth morning of it.*”

“*Four inches a day is the working underneath it, and a man of fifty-four has heard me give that on every morning of this bank being full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is tomorrow morning, which is the Thursday of this week, and that is the fourteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And about four of you have been counting the days down with me and I have noticed and I have not said anything about it and I am not going to start.*”

Then a woman of forty-four asked a man of fifty-four one question in front of about nine of them, and it was the fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight.

She said out loud why she had waited.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She counted seventy-one mornings on Tuesday with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and seventy-two this morning. A boy of thirteen said on Sunday that somebody ought to ask him.

“*I am going to ask a man of fifty-four one question and I am going to say why I have not asked it in about seven weeks.*”

“*The reason is that I have been counting his mornings instead. Fifty-six of them this morning. I have counted them every day since the Saturday of a week and a half ago and I did not count one of them.*”

“*And I am not a keeper of anything, and I have spent nineteen years writing down who sat in a chair.*”

“*There is a man on this bank who has been asked nothing for about seven weeks, and I have been counting him instead of asking him, and that is the same instrument, and I would like somebody here to notice that I have said so.*”

“*Here is the question. On a Sunday of a fortnight a thing that is not a person asked you whether you would come at first light on the days it is written on, and put two hands on a stone.*”

“*That is the fifth thing. I have not asked it before and I am not going to ask whether I may ask it.*”

Then the man of fifty-four answered it, and he gave the answer in the order he keeps things in, and he did not round it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He has asked out loud before anybody speaks since the Tuesday of a fortnight ago. Nobody on that bank has asked him about the fifth thing in about seven weeks.

“*Fifty-six mornings, and I am going to give the count before the answer because that is the order I keep things in.*”

“*Fifty-six is one more than fifty-five, and that is the working.*”

“*The fifth thing was this. Whether I would come at first light on the days it is written on and put two hands on a stone.*”

“*And I have never answered it, and the reason is that there are days written on eight of the nine lines of that sheet, and there has never been one written on the ninth.*”

“*I have had that ninth line read to me fifty-six times and I did not know until Friday.*”

“*So there is no day for me to come on. I have not refused it. I have been asked whether I would come on a day and there is no day, and that is not the same thing and I am not going to let about four of you hear it as the same thing.*”

“*And the thing that asked me is not a person. I have said that for about seven weeks in the only way I have got, which is by not answering it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the fifty-six and nobody is going to be told I was brave about the answer. About four of you are going to say that an answer is a good thing and I am not going to be one of the things you say that about.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day and gave the count of that sheet.

Then he said the thing he has had since the Saturday of a week and a half ago, and he said it in about nine lines and did not improve on it.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week. He has given a different reason every morning this week for not opening a coat.

“*Sixty-seven is seven hundred and seventy-seven less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. I read that line and that day because a man of fifty-four asked, and because a woman of sixty-four asked, and they asked on different days and I do it the same way.*”

“*Here is what I have had since the Saturday of a week and a half ago, and I have said a different reason every morning for not opening a coat, and none of those reasons is this one.*”

“*The precedent on this bank is this. A thing is a thing if it was said out loud, in a mouth, in front of about nine people, with the working said out loud.*”

“*That is what we have done here for about nine weeks. That is what I have done sixty-seven mornings of. And I have believed it every morning and I am not going to stop believing it, and I am telling you what it is.*”

“*And the four leaves on that counter are the same thing. A finding is made. Persons are heard about and they are not in the room, and what the paper says about them is that they did not object.*”

“*Nobody on that bank has ever asked one of those nine anything. And not one of those nine can come up here, and one of them has been in this county for nineteen years and she is about nine feet from where I am standing and she has not been asked.*”

“*So the precedent that made this bank worth standing on is the precedent that makes those four leaves work.*”

“*I do not know how to say the next part of that without saying something about a man standing on this bank, and I am going to.*”

“*It is the man of thirty-one. He has stood here for about nine weeks being the reason about nine of us get up this bank.*”

“*He has said the sentence out loud more mornings than anybody, he did not write it, and I have never once asked him where he got it.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said the name of the thing that has been going on for seventy-two mornings, and gave the count, and said the name is not a thing she has to be thanked for using.

Orla Fennimore counted seventy-one mornings on Tuesday of this week with a pencil and a crumb of bread and said on Saturday of last week that she wanted somebody in about nine people’s hearing to notice that she had not said what to do about them.

“*Three: seventy-two mornings, and seventy-two is seven hundred and seventy-seven less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a man of thirty-eight has just said what I have not been saying since the Saturday of a week and a half ago, and I am going to use one word for it and I am not going to use it twice in a week.*”

“*Coalition.*”

“*That is what has been going on here for seventy-two mornings. A coalition, and I am the one who has said the word.*”

“*I have kept chairs for nineteen years and I am not a keeper of anything, and I know the shape of a thing that is several people doing one job.*”

“*And here is the whole of what a coalition has got, and it is the only thing it has got, and I have got it out loud in front of about nine of you this morning.*”

“*About nine of us have stood on this bank every morning for about nine weeks and said things out loud to each other in a hearing, and not one of us has asked one question.*”

“*And that is the same instrument that made nine people four hundred miles off into nine people who did not object.*”

“*I am not going to say the word for that either, because a man of thirty-eight has said it, and I would only be saying it a second time.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the word. It is a word for a thing I have been standing in.*”

Then a boy of thirteen said that he asked on Sunday for somebody to ask, and that he did not ask, and gave the reason, and that the reason was the one thing he is not carrying.

Wat Marshe is thirteen. He came to this landing a hundred and twenty-four days ago and keeps his own count of it. He has carried five counts on that bank since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and has told everybody he was going to carry six, and has not.

“*A hundred and twenty-four days. A hundred and twenty-four is seven hundred and seventy-seven less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*I said on Sunday that somebody should ask a man of fifty-four about the fifth thing, and a woman of forty-four has asked him, and I did not ask him.*”

“*And I have not asked him because I have been standing on this bank for about nine weeks saying things out loud without being asked.*”

“*If I had asked him then I would be a person in that, and so would he, and I did not want to be a person in it on a Sunday morning when nobody else was going to be.*”

“*That is the worst reason anybody on this bank has given out loud and I am not going to be told it was a good one.*”

“*I have not carried it as a sixth count and I am not going to. Five.*”

Then a woman of forty said what she is standing in front of, and said what it is about now, and it is not about a day with nothing on it any more.

Wenna Callow is forty and asks for a day against a name and has never once asked for a reason instead. On the Sunday of last week she said out loud that a name with no day on it is a day with no name on it and that she has wanted both of them for eleven years. She has still not said which three of five things have a day against them.

“*I said on the Sunday that I wanted a day with a name on it and a name with a day on it, and they are the same, and I have wanted both for eleven years.*”

“*And what has happened since the Sunday is that I have found out there is a third thing and I have not got a word for it yet and I am not going to make one up this morning.*”

“*It is not a day with a name on it and it is not a name with a day on it. It is a person that nobody can be sent for, and I have been standing in front of about nine of those for about seven weeks and I have been asking for a day the whole time.*”

“*A man of fifty-four. A woman of sixty-four. A woman of thirty-four. A man of thirty-four in an ash. A woman of twenty-nine in a reed. A man of thirty-eight with a board in his coat.*”

“*That is a figure of people and it is the only one I have given this morning, and I have not added anything to it, and nobody is going to be thanked for me counting.*”

“*And I am still not going to say which three of the five have a day against them, and that is not a want any more. It is the only thing I have got that nobody has asked me for.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that everything said on that bank this morning was said because of him, and he said it, and nobody argued with him and nobody went down.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-three days, two hundred and twenty-three being seven hundred and seventy-seven less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-two days old, ninety-two being seven hundred and seventy-seven less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Here is the want and it is the first one in about six days that is not about another person’s mouth.*”

“*I want to be the person who says it, and I am going to say it, and then I am going to hand the morning to somebody else and I am not going to do that again.*”

“*Everything said on that bank this morning was said because of me. That is the want, the mistake and the cost in one sentence and I have been trying to give it to you in three for about nine weeks.*”

“*Here is the mistake. I said on the Wednesday of a fortnight that I want somebody on this bank to ask me for a thing. Not a question about what I am. A thing. A job. Something with a day on it.*”

“*And a woman of forty told me what the shape of that was before I had finished the sentence and she was right and I said it back.*”

“*The shape of it is a form. A form has a line with a name on it and a line with a day on it, and a person stands there or does not. A man of sixty-one said that at the Saturday of a week and a half ago and gave it as the second of six things.*”

“*I have been standing on this bank for two hundred and twenty-three days being the thing about four people come up to, and what that is, is a line with a name on it that nobody can be sent for.*”

“*And what I wanted was for somebody to put me in a column.*”

“*And there is no column. There is no day on the ninth line and there never has been, and there is a woman of sixty-four with a name in ink on it.*”

“*And the finding says the nine of them did not object, and a man of fifty-four this morning has said out loud that he was never asked anything and has not refused anything.*”

“*Seventy-seven days. Seventy-seven is seven hundred and seventy-seven less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first and none of it is an answer to any part of what I have just said.*”

“*And the cost is that about nine of you were on this bank before I said it, and about nine of you are on this bank now, and nobody has gone down.*”

“*I have not been relieved of anything. I am not going to be thanked for saying the sentence that made this bank, and I am not going to be blamed out loud for it either, because the second of those would be a finding and I do not make them.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the ninth hour with a man of fifty-four’s answer in the air and not one of the nine of them going down about ninety steps under sixteen inches of water.

---

The fever was seventy weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-three days.

A woman of forty-four asked a man of fifty-four the fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight, and he answered it.

He said there has never been a day written on that line, so he has not refused anything, and he is not going to be thanked for the answer.

And a woman of forty-four used one word for what has been going on here for seventy-two mornings, and gave the count, and said that not one of about nine people has asked one question.

And a man of thirty-eight said the precedent out loud, which is that a thing is a thing if it was said out loud in a mouth in front of about nine of them with the working.

He said the four leaves are the same instrument.

And a man of fifty-four answered the fifth thing that was asked of him on a Sunday of a fortnight, and said there has never been a day written on that line, and said he has not refused anything.
