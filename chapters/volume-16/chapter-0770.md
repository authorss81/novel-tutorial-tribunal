# Chapter 770: Second Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Second Week, And A Wednesday, And The Water Comes Off All Ninety Steps For The Thirteenth Time And Two People Are Not On That Bank, And A Man Of Thirty-One Gives Seventy Days And Says None Of It Is An Answer

Second day of the hundred and thirty-second week. His five hundred and twentieth morning. Three hundred and seventy days after the settlement. The fever sixty-nine weeks and four days. Three hundred and twenty-four days since the division. Two hundred and seventy days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water came off all ninety of the steps in the night, for the thirteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Wednesday. The man of forty-three said the hour of it and the count of it and gave the day of the week on the word.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He gives three things every morning and has never added a fourth to them, and he said five times in a row that he had nothing to say about a night and then stopped saying it, and he is not going to start again about it now that a woman is walking that bank again.

“*Off all ninety at about the sixth hour this morning, and that is the thirteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Wednesday.*”

“*Eight days from the twelfth, which was the Tuesday of last week, and the last eight of the thirteen I have counted are eight days apart one from the next.*”

“*And I am not going to work out the first five of them in front of about nine of you, because I have not found out, and I gave a figure for it once on the Monday of a fortnight ago and I took it back.*”

“*Tomorrow it will be about four inches over the top step and that is one day of the coming back.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open. It was the forty-ninth morning. He asked out loud first, and the day was said out loud afterwards by the same man who had read the line, and neither of them was thanked.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four. He holds the gravel on that landing and he cannot read. He has asked for that line every morning for about six weeks and has not missed one, and he has been asking out loud since the Tuesday of last week, before anybody speaks.

“*Forty-nine mornings.*”

“*Forty-nine is one more than forty-eight and I worked it out before I said it, same as every morning of them.*”

“*Tomorrow it will be fifty and I am not going to say anything about the fifty tomorrow, and about four of you will write fifty down tonight and about nine of you will not.*”

“*The fifth thing is not answered and I have been asked nothing by anybody on this bank for about six weeks and that is five mornings of saying so out loud.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the ninth line and read the day out loud, and gave the count of that sheet first with the working, and said that it was the last morning of a whole number of mornings and he was not going to do anything with that.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and he is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and has not read it since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and this morning is a round figure in his own count and he is not going to do anything with a round figure.

“*Sixty is seven hundred and seventy less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*It is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I have said it out loud three mornings running now because about four of you asked for it and about nine of you have not.*”

“*Sixty mornings of that count and it is a round figure and I am not going to do anything with a round figure, because a man of thirty-one gave seventy days yesterday out of a figure of seventy and nothing came of that either.*”

Then a woman of forty-four gave the two counts off that counter, and said that they were the twentieth morning and the twenty-sixth, and that she had given those two counts every morning since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years. She brought the four leaves down four miles on the bread on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and nobody asked her to, and she has given those two counts every morning since without being asked once.

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter twenty-six mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter twenty mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And there are two people who went up that cart road this morning who are not on this bank, and neither of them came down this road to say anything on their way up, and I did not see them go and I did not ask.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts he gives, in the order he gives them, and then said what he wants out loud for the eleventh morning running, and said he is going up that road and not coming back down it today.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years. He has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said so every morning since the Saturday of a week ago.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and forty days, and that is seven hundred and seventy less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and forty-one days, and that is seven hundred and seventy less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and sixteen days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and twenty-eight, and that is seven hundred and seventy less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*I have not added any of those to any other and I am not going to, and I have not added a gate to a day either.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the eleventh morning I have said it and it is the eleventh morning about nine of you have not said anything about it.*”

“*I am going up to that gate today and I am not coming back down this road today, and if you want to know what I find there then somebody will have to come up and ask me, and I am not going to promise to be here tomorrow.*”

Then a woman of thirty-four said one thing about the two people who went up that road, and it was not about the road, and she said it from the top of about two hundred yards of that bank and did not go back down it.

Brenna Dole is thirty-four and has walked about two days from a place four hundred miles off that landing. She came up about two hundred yards of that bank by herself on Sunday and said she did not know the days were being counted. She has not said a second thing about a room since the Sunday of a fortnight ago.

“*A man of sixty-one and a woman of forty-four have gone up a road and about nine of you are going to spend this morning working out what they are like.*”

“*I have been on this bank about four mornings and I have said the one thing I came two days to say, and I am not going to tell about nine of you what a man of sixty-one is.*”

“*And I am not going to be here when anybody comes back down that road, and I am not going to be asked what I think about it afterwards, and if about four of you would rather I said a thing about him then I would rather you did not ask me for it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the four mornings and nobody is going to be told I came two days for nothing.*”

Then a man of thirty-one gave seventy days, and said that none of the twenty mornings of them is an answer, and said that a round figure is not a settlement, and said he is not going to have anybody sent for.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s. He has been in this county two hundred and sixteen days, two hundred and sixteen being seven hundred and seventy less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is eighty-five days old, eighty-five being seven hundred and seventy less six hundred and eighty-five, and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say whether it will keep.

“*Seventy days. Seventy is seven hundred and seventy less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first, and that is twenty mornings of it.*”

“*And none of the twenty is an answer. I have said that on every one of them and I am saying it again with a round figure in my mouth so that nobody on this bank can tell me I said it differently today.*”

“*A round figure is not a settlement. Seventy is the easiest figure I have ever given and it is worth exactly what the other nineteen were worth.*”

“*Here is the want and it is the last one I am going to say out loud this week.*”

“*I want to be the man who goes up that road.*”

“*And I am not going to, and I said on Friday that I am not going to, and a man of sixty-one and a woman of forty-four have gone up it and neither of them went for me and one of them said so out loud before she went.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is the same one I have been making since the Wednesday of a fortnight and I am not going to pretend I have got round it.*”

“*I have wanted somebody to go up that road for about nine days. And I have not wanted them to go up it, I have wanted a person to be the one who went, so that it would be a thing about me that a person had done.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me on a Wednesday of a fortnight that the order of those two things is the whole of what I am, and I have had it in front of me since and I have been getting it the wrong way round for nine days.*”

“*Here is the cost and it is that there are two people three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road at a low gate, and nobody in this county can be sent for about them, and I am not going to have anybody sent, and I do not know what they will find and I am not going to be told by about nine of you that I will find out.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank with all ninety steps off it for the thirteenth time and two empty places on it that had been full the day before, and the four leaves of that hearing were on that counter on the twentieth morning with nothing in any column of any of them.

---

The fever was sixty-nine weeks and four days old. He had been in that county two hundred and sixteen days.

The water came off all ninety steps for the thirteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and it was a Wednesday, and a man of forty-three said the last eight of the thirteen are eight days apart and that he is not going to work out the first five.

And a man of sixty-one and a woman of forty-four went up that cart road in the morning to a low gate three miles and six hundred yards from that landing, and neither of them came back down it that day, and neither of them went because a man of thirty-one was asked.

And a man of thirty-one gave seventy days, and said that none of the twenty mornings of it is an answer, and that a round figure is worth exactly what the other nineteen were worth.

And about nine people stood on that bank with two places at the top of it not filled, and nobody on it sent for anybody.
