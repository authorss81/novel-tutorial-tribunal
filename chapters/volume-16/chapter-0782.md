# Chapter 782: Seventh Day Of The Hundred And Thirty-Third Week, And A Monday, And A Full Bank On The First Morning Of It, And He Walks Four Hundred Yards Up That Cart Road And Counts It Out Loud And Is Told By A Woman Of Forty That It Is Not Ground

Seventh day of the hundred and thirty-third week. His five hundred and thirty-second morning. Three hundred and eighty-two days after the settlement. The fever seventy-one weeks and two days. Three hundred and thirty-six days since the division. Two hundred and eighty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The bank was about sixteen inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was a full bank on the first morning of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and the working and the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked.

Then he said he would hold the far end of anything anybody measured this morning, and that he had said that on about nine hundred mornings about this sort of thing and it had never once been taken up.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. Three things every morning. He gave the day-name sequence out loud on the Thursday before last and said he was not going to say what he believes about the first five of the thirteen, and he has not said it since.

“*About sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and that is a full bank on the first morning of it.*”

“*A bank that is full holds at sixteen until it comes off, and four inches a day is the working underneath it, and I have given that on every morning this bank has been full.*”

“*And the next time those ninety steps can be walked is in four days from this morning, which is the Friday of this week.*”

“*That is the fifteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week.*”

“*And if anybody measures anything out there this morning I will hold the far end of it, and I have said that on about nine hundred mornings and about four of you have never once taken it up.*”

Then a man of fifty-four who cannot read had the ninth line read to him in the open, and it was the sixty-first morning. He asked out loud first, and the man of thirty-eight said the number of names before he read anything else.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He has asked for the ninth line to be read to him in the open every morning since the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-fifth week. He answered a thing at the Wednesday of this week and has not been thanked for it and has not been asked about it.

“*Sixty-one mornings.*”

“*Sixty-one is one more than sixty, and that is the working, and I am not going to say anything else about the fifth thing.*”

“*And a man of forty-three has just offered to hold the end of a thing for about four of us and I am going to say that I heard him, because nobody has ever taken him up.*”

Then the man of thirty-eight read the line and the day, gave the count of that sheet, and said the one thing he has said about his own coat four mornings running.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine. He said on Friday that he would say the number of names he can see on the ninth line once a morning in the open, and the only reason he gave was that a man of fifty-four asks him to.

“*Four of them, and I have said the number every morning since Friday.*”

“*Seventy-two is seven hundred and eighty-two less seven hundred and ten.*”

“*And it is the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, and I read it because a man of fifty-four asked, and I have now said it out loud on three mornings running and I am going to keep saying the number and not the line.*”

“*I am not opening this coat. The board has been in it eighty-four days, and eighty-four is seven hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and ninety-eight, and it has thirty-two notches cut in the edge of it.*”

Then he went up that cart road with about nine of them and he counted the ground out loud, and a woman of forty-four held the count and a man of about fifty-two checked it against four lines under him.

They went about four hundred yards up the cart road from the landing to a place where a cart comes off a lane and onto the road that goes down to the water. He gave the distances before he gave the count.

“*Four hundred yards up this road from the landing, and that is two hundred paces of mine, and it is about three miles and two hundred yards below the low gate, which is two thousand seven hundred and forty paces.*”

“*Here is the measure and I am giving it before the count because a count with no measure on it is just a man talking.*”

“*I paced the three miles and six hundred yards that a woman of forty-four named on the Friday before this week, all of it, on foot, and I got two thousand nine hundred and forty paces.*”

“*Three thousand five hundred and eighty yards, and two thousand nine hundred and forty paces, so that is two yards to a pace of mine, and about four of you are going to tell me two yards is not a pace of anybody but me.*”

“*A field is the word for it where I learned it. I am going to say the word once and then I am going to say ground, because I have got it wrong twice this morning before I have counted anything.*”

“*Here is the count at the turn. It is eleven paces across where the off-side wheel of a cart goes, and eleven paces is twenty-two yards, and the ground either side of that is not part of it.*”

Then a woman of forty told him that it was not ground, and gave the reason, and about four of them had already agreed with her before she finished.

Wenna Callow is forty and asked him on the Sunday for the ground of the three things that have a day against them. She gave the five out loud on the Friday of this week and the first of the three is a cart turning off a road.

“*You have measured a turn. I want to tell you what you have measured and I would rather say it before you have it written down anywhere in your head.*”

“*A cart turning off a road on a day is not a piece of ground. The cart is not at that turn every day. It is at the turn on the day, and on the other days it is somewhere else, and maybe it is broken.*”

“*So it is not tied to that turn. It is tied to the day, and the day is the thing you cannot stand on.*”

“*That is not me refusing you anything. That is me telling you what is there, and you asked me for what is there.*”

“*You have asked me for eleven years for a day against a name and I have never once said no to you and I am not saying no to you now.*”

Then a man of about fifty-two gave the count back to him and said what he can be checked against, and gave the count a second time and it was the same.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two and is the fifth line of one of two sheets of deal that have been eleven years in a place he works. Every morning he can be checked against the four lines under him. He has had one thing in those four leaves he has not said out loud in about eleven years and he has not said it.

“*Eleven paces across, and the twenty-two yards is right, and I have checked the paces against the two yards and the two yards is right.*”

“*I am the fifth line of two sheets of deal and every morning of my working life four lines have sat under me and any one of the four of them can be looked at to see whether I have read the fifth wrong.*”

“*And I have read the fifth line of four leaves wrong once, and it was about nine days ago, and I said so on that Friday out loud and nobody has asked me about it since.*”

“*I checked your count twice and the two are the same, and that is the whole of what I have got for you this morning.*”

Then a woman of forty-four said what she held and what it is not, and said the thing about two yards.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four and has kept a book of twenty-nine chairs at thirteen shillings and fourpence a year for nineteen years, and she is not a keeper of anything anybody can be sent for about. She began a count on the Monday of the hundred and twenty-second week, which is seven hundred and five, with a pencil and a crumb of bread, and not in that book.

“*I held the count. It is eleven paces and I wrote it on the back of my hand and not in the book, because the book is chairs.*”

“*One: a sheet of printed paper with three columns on it, on that counter thirty-eight mornings, with nothing in any of the three.*”

“*Two: four leaves of a hearing, on that counter thirty-two mornings, with a number on them and no room on them and no names on them.*”

“*And three: seventy-seven mornings, and seventy-seven is seven hundred and eighty-two less seven hundred and five.*”

“*And a man of forty-three said he would hold the far end of a thing and about four of us have never taken him up, and I have been on this bank seventy-seven mornings and I did not know that until this morning.*”

“*Two yards to a pace of a man who has walked two hundred and twenty-eight days on a bank is not a yardstick and I am not going to say it is.*”

Then a man of thirty-one said that he had called it a field before he had asked what it was tied to, and gave the three figures that go with it.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and twenty-eight days, two hundred and twenty-eight being seven hundred and eighty-two less five hundred and fifty-four. The cut across it is ninety-seven days old, ninety-seven being seven hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and eighty-five, and nobody has said whether it will keep and he has asked twice in about a fortnight that nobody say.

“*Here is the want and it is a small one and it is about a woman of forty and not about me.*”

“*I want her to have been the one who said it. I want about four of them to have been wrong this morning for about an hour and then her to be right, and that is not a good want and I am not going to dress it up.*”

“*Here is the mistake and it is mine and it is the same one in a new wording and it is about eleven hours old.*”

“*I have been saying field all morning. A field is a piece of ground a thing is tied to, and I have not once asked what the thing is tied to, and the thing on this ground is a cart.*”

“*A woman of forty has asked me for eleven years of asking and not one foot of it, and she has just given me a day’s worth of ground that is not ground, and she did it in four sentences and she did not once raise her voice.*”

“*And I walked up four hundred yards to find it out. She could have told me at the counter on Friday and she came up four hundred yards in the road to tell me after I had done the walking, because that is the only place it could be told properly.*”

“*Eighty-two days. Eighty-two is seven hundred and eighty-two less seven hundred, and I have given that figure every morning since the fifty-first, and none of it is an answer to two yards to a pace.*”

“*And the cost is that I have one thing mapped and it is not a field, and I have said the word out loud this morning, and about four of you heard it.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for walking four hundred yards and nobody is going to be told I was brave about two yards.*”

Then a man of sixty-one gave the four counts and said the thing about what he has not written down, and it was the second morning running he had said a version of it.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. He wants that hearing to stand and has said it out loud nineteen mornings running.

“*A man of thirty-four has been in an ash for a hundred and fifty-two days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*A woman of twenty-nine has been in a reed for a hundred and fifty-three days, and that is seven hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and twenty-nine.*”

“*A man of thirty-one has been in this county two hundred and twenty-eight days and a woman of twenty-eight a hundred and forty, and that is seven hundred and eighty-two less six hundred and forty-two.*”

“*None of those has been added to any other. I have not added a name to them and I have not written down eleven paces.*”

“*I want that hearing to stand. That is the nineteenth morning I have said it, and I have not asked any of you about it, and about four of you have said nothing back since the Tuesday of a week and a half ago.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank at about the eleventh hour with a full bank behind them and eleven paces of a turn counted twice into two sets of hands.

---

The fever was seventy-one weeks and two days old. He had been in that county two hundred and twenty-eight days.

He walked four hundred yards up the cart road and gave the measure and the count and the two distances, and about nine of them stood in the road for it.

A woman of forty told him he had measured a day and not a piece of ground, because the cart is not at that turn every day.

Nobody handed him anything to hold that Monday, and the man who had offered had been standing at the end of that bank for nineteen years.
