# Chapter 755: First Day Of The Hundred And Thirtieth Week, And A Tuesday, And The Bank Is About Four Inches Over The Top Step On The First Day Of It, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Is Asked For The First Time In About Three Weeks To Read Something Out Loud And Says Yes, And A Woman Of Sixty-One Who Has A Number In A Book In This County Wants The Hearing To Stand And Says Why, And It Is About A Road

First day of the hundred and thirtieth week. His five hundred and fifth morning. Three hundred and fifty-five days after the settlement. The fever sixty-seven weeks and three days. Three hundred and nine days since the division. Two hundred and fifty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The bank was about four inches over the top step at about the seventh hour and it was the first day of it. The man of forty-three gave the reading and said the working and gave the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and nobody had asked him for any of the three.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years. He said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then he stopped saying it. He gives three things every morning and there is not going to be a fourth thing out of them.

“*About four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, and it is the first morning of it, and it came off all ninety yesterday morning for the eleventh time.*”

“*One day. It was nothing over the top step at this hour yesterday and four inches a day is the working.*”

“*And the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in about four days after that bank is full.*”

“*It will be full on Friday, and I have said that about four times this week and I am going to say it once more and then not again until Monday.*”

“*I am not going to be thanked for it and there is no fourth thing in me this morning.*”

Then a man of fifty-four asked for the ninth line in the open, and a man of thirty-eight read it, and that is the first time in about three weeks that anybody on that bank has asked him to do anything at all.

Corvin Tace is forty-three and has kept that landing nineteen years, and about ninety steps of the work are under the bank he stands on. He has said he had nothing to say about a night five times and then stopped. He is not going to give a fourth thing out of the three he gives every morning.

“*Thirty-four mornings and it is the first morning of a week and I asked for it and he read it.*”

“*That is three weeks of asking and about nine of you have never once asked a man of thirty-eight to read anything and he has read it every morning of it.*”

“*I am not going to say thank you to a man of thirty-eight in front of about nine people and I am not going to be thanked for thirty-four.*”

“*I am still not answering the fifth thing and nobody has asked me and I am still waiting, and I asked for one thing this morning and got it and that does not settle the other one.*”

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight and has a child of nine, and is the only person on that bank who can put a light on a thing in the dark. He has read that sheet forty times in this county and forty-four times as of yesterday. He stopped deciding at the Sunday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. He said he would read it again when somebody on that bank asked him to in front of about nine people.

“*Forty-five is seven hundred and fifty-five less seven hundred and ten, and there has not been one of the forty-five in the dark.*”

“*And a man of fifty-four asked me to do it in front of about nine people this morning and I said yes, and I am not going to improve on what that is either.*”

“*I said on Saturday that I would read it when I was asked. A man of thirty-one said on Saturday that a second time is a rule and that about four of you had noticed the order things were being done in.*”

“*About four of you have noticed that a man of fifty-four who cannot read has been asking a man of thirty-eight to read for thirty-four mornings and that nobody has noticed until this morning.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for the forty-five and nobody is going to be told I was brave about saying yes.*”

Then a woman of sixty-one said what she wants, and gave the count first, and said what it is about and what it is not about.

Nevin Trask is sixty-one, and has kept a book of heads and carts in this county for eleven years, and has written two numbers in it in eleven years. There are five lines on the leaf after the last head and a number is the last of the five. He wrote the second one yesterday morning on the last day of the hundred and twenty-ninth week and has refused to give the working for it out loud three times.

“*I want that hearing to stand. I am saying it first thing this morning before the shape and before the working, because about nine of you have been waiting since Saturday for me to say it after and I am not going to.*”

“*That is four days. It is seven hundred and fifty-five less seven hundred and fifty-one, and a woman of thirty-four walked two days to say a number and I wrote it down and about nine of you heard me do it.*”

“*Here is what it is about and it is about a road and not about a person. The ninth line of that sheet is about a day, and there are about nine houses four miles up that cart road, and about four of those houses cannot come on a day because of what they are carrying or who they are carrying it for.*”

“*And if that finding comes back and somebody carries it down that cart road, then about four houses are on a day they cannot come on, in writing.*”

“*And there is no column on it for a person stopping.*”

“*And here is what it is not about and I am going to say it because a woman of twenty-four is not going to say it for me. It is not about the woman of twenty-nine in the reed. It is not about the man of thirty-four in the ash. It is not about the woman of sixty-four who was in that room.*”

“*It is about a road and about four houses and about eleven years of a book of heads and carts that about nine people can be sent for about.*”

“*And I have been the man who says a thing cannot be entered in it eleven times.*”

“*Nobody is going to be thanked for six sentences and nobody is going to be told I am right about the road.*”

About four of them had a man of sixty-one who wanted a hearing four hundred miles off to stand for the sake of a cart road four miles off.

About nine of them had a man of thirty-eight who had said yes to a man of fifty-four.

Then a man of thirty-one said what he wanted, and he got it wrong twice on the same morning, and he said both of them.

Ilyan Vester is thirty-one and is nobody’s, and has been in this county two hundred and one days, two hundred and one being seven hundred and fifty-five less five hundred and fifty-four. Yesterday a woman of twenty-eight said in five sentences that wanting to arrange for about nine people to be asked is not a want about this county but a want about him wearing about nine people’s clothing.

“*Two hundred and one is seven hundred and fifty-five less five hundred and fifty-four, and I have been in this county two hundred and one days, and nobody has asked me for anything in one of them.*”

“*Here is the want and I am giving it before the shape. I want a man of sixty-one to be asked whether he believes a woman of thirty-four, and I am not going to be the one who asks him, and I said exactly that sentence on Friday morning about a man of thirty-eight and a woman of sixty-four and about nine of you heard it.*”

“*That is the first wrong thing and I said it out loud about four minutes ago and I am going to say the second one before somebody else finds it.*”

“*A man of sixty-one wrote six on a leaf yesterday morning because a woman he has never met told him six in front of about nine people. If I now want him asked whether he believes her, I am asking him to do the thing he refused yesterday morning, which is to go into that book and put something on that leaf that has no working under it.*”

“*And the second wrong thing is that I have wanted that in about nine days’ worth of wants.*”

“*Every one of them has been a want that somebody about nine people should ask a question on my behalf. I said yesterday that that is a job I made up, and that nobody in this county can be sent for about it.*”

“*And here is the cost, and it is not the same cost as yesterday and I am not going to say it the same way. About four of you have worked out that I want a man of sixty-one to do a thing for me, and about nine of you have not, and that is a figure of about four people in this county and not a figure of the county.*”

“*I have said want out loud about ten times in about ten days and the last four of them have been about other people and not about this county.*”

“*A woman of twenty-eight told me yesterday that about nine people would rather I asked nothing than asked in that order. She was right and I am not going to improve on it in front of about nine people.*”

“*Nobody is going to be relieved about that and nobody is going to be thanked and nobody is going to be told I was right about the shape on Friday.*”

“*Fifty-five days. Fifty-five is seven hundred and fifty-five less seven hundred. Nobody has answered it and nobody is going to answer it for me and I am not going to answer it.*”

Then a boy of thirteen carried five counts and gave back two of them that were wrong on Wednesday, and said which two.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, and his mother is forty-one and has two carts and neither of them can be sent for about. He has kept five counts since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week and has carried five every morning.

“*A hundred and two days. A hundred and two is seven hundred and fifty-five less six hundred and fifty-three.*”

“*And thirty-four mornings, and a hundred and twenty-five days for a man in an ash, and a hundred and twenty-six for a woman in a reed, and two numbers in a book.*”

“*That is five and I have carried five, and I am not going to carry six this week because I said Wednesday that I would not and I have not carried six once.*”

“*And here is what I am giving back. On Saturday I said a man in an ash had been there a hundred and twenty-four days.*”

“*It was a hundred and twenty-two that Saturday, because a hundred and twenty-two is seven hundred and fifty-two less six hundred and thirty.*”

“*I said a hundred and twenty-four and I have said a hundred and twenty-four every morning since.*”

“*And on Sunday I said a woman in a reed had been there a hundred and twenty-six days and it was a hundred and twenty-four, and that one is worse because I rounded up and I do not round anything.*”

“*Nobody has caught either of them because nobody has been reading them the way I have. Nobody is going to be thanked for five counts and two of them were wrong.*”

About nine of them stood on that bank in the wind on the first day of a week with a bank about four inches over the top step.
