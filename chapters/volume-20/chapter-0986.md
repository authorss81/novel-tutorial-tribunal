# Chapter 986: The Water Came Off That Bank And All Ninety Of Those Steps Were Walkable And That Was The Thirtieth Time And There Was No Figure Of Inches And No Figure Of Steps Under And No Figure Of Days And It Was The First Morning Of A Week, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Said What A Thing With A Giver In It Does To A Landing

First day of the hundred and sixty-third week. His seven hundred and thirty-sixth morning. Five hundred and eighty-six days after the settlement. The fever one hundred weeks and three days old. Five hundred and forty days since the division. Four hundred and eighty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and thirty-two days in this county of Kell.

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh, and that was the thirtieth time. There is no figure of inches on this morning and there is no figure of steps under and there is no figure of days of the coming back, and it is the first day of the hundred and sixty-third week as well. 

That is the whole of what is on the stone, it is not a gap in the morning, and the thirtieth time is the only figure this morning carries and it is a figure of times and not of anything else.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the thirtieth morning the water came off that bank, in the first day of the hundred and sixty-third week, that he had said those three absences by name on every morning this had happened and was going to say them in the same shape again.

“There is no figure of inches on that bank this morning and I am not reaching for one out of habit, and there is not a feeling of it either, because a feeling of it is a figure in a worse coat. Not a number of steps under, and not a number of days.” He put the broom down flat on the stone.

“And I have said those three out loud by name every morning the water has come off that bank since the day I started saying them, and I am going to say them in that shape this morning as well, because a shape you change on a morning like this is a shape you are dressing up, and there is nothing here to dress up.”

“A morning with nothing on the stone is not a better morning and it is not a worse one. It is the shape of it. That is the thirtieth time.”

Nobody on that landing reached for a figure, and nobody filled the gap with one out of another morning.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour, and the water was off and all ninety steps were out, and he asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that a morning with nothing on the stone was the morning he was most careful on.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and sixty-fifth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and sixty-fifth. Nine hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and there is nothing on that stone this morning to ask it against.”

“No, and I will tell you the truth about that, since nobody has asked me for it and I have been waiting a long while to be asked for it.” He shouldered the barrow. “There is more chance of me being answered on a morning like this than there is on a morning with a number on it. Nobody can say they were busy. Everybody can see what is in front of them.”

“I have thought that two hundred and sixty-five times and I have not said it out loud before and I am saying it now and I am not saying it again.”

Nothing was said about having waited to hear it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And it is a Tuesday and the week turned in the night and nobody on this landing has said a word about that and neither am I, and the question is the same one and it is going to be the same one on every morning of this week.”

Nobody answered him on a morning when there was nothing on the stone to answer him with, and nobody told Barnaby Crove he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour to a landing with no water on the steps at all to lean his own figures on, and gave his figures anyway, and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and thirty-two days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and one days old.

“Four hundred and thirty-two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-thirty-sixth is two hundred and eighty-six days old, which is nine hundred and eighty-six less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and there is no water on these steps this morning and there is nothing on that stone to stand any of it against, and I have been giving those figures into an empty morning for weeks now and I am not going to stop now that it is the shape of the morning instead of a habit.”

He was not stopped and he was not helped.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps with his hand out in front of him and found the wall with it, and said what a thing with a giver in it does to a landing on a morning when there is nothing on the stone to put anything beside.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-third week that a landing which has run on one sentence for nineteen years and then finds the sentence is not true does not get a new sentence, it gets a hole in the shape of the old one.

“I am going to start where I have started every time, because you have all heard me start here and I would be making a different speech than I have got.” He put his back to the wall. “I cannot see that step. I am not going to pretend I can and I am not going to ask anybody to describe it.”

“And this morning there is nothing at all on the stone and a man with a broom has told me in the same words he has used every other time that there is no number to reach for and no feeling to reach for either.”

It was not described.

“That happens every time this happens and I have got four of those mornings behind me now and I have got a shape for it and the shape is that a morning with nothing on it is the only kind of morning where I can be certain about what I do not know. This morning I know precisely what I do not know. That is the whole of what I came up here to say and I have not said anything else yet.”

He turned his face towards the wall.

“Now here is the other thing and it has been sitting on me since Saturday and it is worse on a morning like this than it was on a morning with figures on it.” He put his hand out along the wall and stopped short of where he knew the step was. “The thing that has made this landing easy to be honest on is that there is no giver in anything on it. That is not a figure.”

“It is a shape, and everybody on these steps has been standing inside that shape for a fortnight and not one of you has had to say it out loud.”

“A man who finds a thing and uses it is not on this landing. A thing left by somebody who is not on the step is not on this landing. Everything on this landing runs on there being no giver in it.”

Nobody on that landing had said it out loud.

“And a man said out loud on Thursday that he put a piece of ash on that wall, and he said it because he wanted a giver in it, and now there is one. So that shape is not true any more, and it has not been true since the eighth hour of Thursday, and it is the oldest sentence on this landing and it is nineteen years old.”

He came away from the wall and stood with his hand at his side, and said the seven of them out loud on his way down, in the order they go, and nobody thanked him for saying them.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used.” His hand was still out. “And I have said those on this landing four times in a fortnight and every one of the four times it was somebody else who could see the thing I could not see. This is the first one I have said about a thing I have been near, and nobody is to thank me for it and nobody is to tell me I was careful.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had been careful.

“And here is what a thing with a giver in it does to a landing. It does not get a new sentence. I want that said because I have watched people think that a false sentence gets replaced. It does not. It gets a hole in the shape of the old one, and the hole is exactly the size of the thing and it does not close.”

“And from Thursday this landing has had a hole in the middle of its oldest sentence, and everybody who stands up here now is going to have to walk around it, and not one of you is going to be able to describe where the hole is to anybody who was not standing here on Thursday morning at the eighth hour.”

Nobody on that landing described where the hole was.

“And I cannot check it. That is the part I came up here to say and it is why I have not gone down. I have got a list of things I cannot check and this morning the list is empty of everything else and this is the only thing on the landing, and it is not a thing, and I am not going to put a person in a list to make the morning easier.”

Nobody put a person in it, and nobody told him that was the right thing to do.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his own count before the light had properly come up, and said that there was a fifth morning of this kind behind him and that he had stopped keeping a shape for them.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and thirty-three days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and thirty-three is nine hundred and eighty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and thirty-three days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He looked round the landing. “There is nothing on that stone and there is a number of times and that is all, and I have not got a shape for it this morning because I had one on Saturday and it came out wrong by lunchtime.”

How it came out wrong was not asked.

“I am not going to tell you how it came out wrong and nobody is going to tell me either. And I am going to be up this lane tomorrow when the water is up again and I am not going to ask anybody what I will see, and if I am wrong about what I will see then that is what will happen and it will not be anybody’s fault, including mine, and I would like that recorded in a voice and not in a book.”

It was not recorded, and he was not told he had it right.

Then the man of thirty-one gave the whole of what was left in him and did not go up past the bottom step, and nobody on that landing said a word to him about any of it.

“One hundred and twenty-six wrong things in one hundred and twenty-six mornings, and this is the hundred and twenty-sixth. And I have a want, and it is the same want I said out loud on this landing last week, and it is that I want to be somebody, and I have not got any nearer it since the day I said it and I have got further away, and I am not going to unsay it because a want does not have to be a good one to be true.”

He was told neither that the want was a good one nor that it was a bad one.

“And the one thing I still have is that I can walk down that bank at any hour of this morning and leave, and I have had it every morning for four hundred and thirty-two days and I have not used it, and I am not going to use it this morning, and I am not going to say a word to anybody about how near I came to it, because a figure of how far a man walked away from a thing is a figure of the man and not of the thing, and nobody on this landing is going to get that out of me either.”

How near he came to it was not asked.

The stone was bare and ninety steps were out and walkable and a man swept every one of them, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on the chair and did not touch the iron in the seam and did not touch the ash on the wall. Three absences had been named out loud in the same shape for the thirtieth time and cannot be recalled, and the thing he had put down came through a morning with nothing on that stone at all and came out the other side of it with a giver still in it, and nobody on that landing thanked anybody for anything.
