# Chapter 966: A Full Bank, And The Man With The Broom Said Out Loud That Four Days Is A Figure Of The Water And Not A Figure Of The Morning, And Then A Man Who Moves Chairs For A Living Said A Sentence About A Chair

Second day of the hundred and sixtieth week. His seven hundred and sixteenth morning. Five hundred and sixty-six days after the settlement. The fever ninety-seven weeks and four days old. Five hundred and twenty days since the division. Four hundred and sixty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and twelve days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then took the whole morning apart in four sentences so that nobody would go away thinking a full bank was a kind of day.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the second morning of the hundred and sixtieth week, on a full bank, the two things he has said before about a full bank and one of them for the first time.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the second day of the hundred and sixtieth week, and it is a Wednesday. Three figures.”

He set the broom down rather than lean it, which is what he does when he has something else.

“First thing, and I have said this before on a full bank and I am saying it again because a man came up here on Friday asking me what I do with my mornings. A full bank is four days. It is not a good morning and it is not a bad one and it is not the day anything gets decided on. It is the day the water has been in the same place for as long as it is going to be in the same place.”

“Second thing, and this is the one I have not said before. Four days is a figure of the water and it is not a figure of this landing, and it is not a figure of the morning, and nobody is to carry it off this stone as a kind of day to have a conversation on.” He looked along the top step.

“I have given three figures off this stone every morning for nineteen years and I have never once given one of them a name, and the reason is that they are about the bank and not about us. A bank does not care what a landing does on top of it and I have never claimed that it does.”

Nobody claimed that it did.

“I am going to sweep all ninety and I am not going to say anything else about the water, and whatever gets said on this landing today gets said on a Wednesday with sixteen inches standing on the step, which is the least interesting Wednesday in this county and is the only one I have got.”

Then the man who moves chairs came up the cart road without anything on his shoulder, and he had been up since the sixth hour, and he did not go to the back of the landing where he stands, and he came to the flat of the steps and stood there in front of everybody, and it took him a while to get going, and everybody on that landing let him.

Aldis Rook moves the chairs for Orla Fennimore and keeps nothing and has no stone and has no seat and no board, has never once been asked what he did, has been told not to come up that bank and has come anyway, and said out loud on the second morning of the hundred and sixtieth week a thing about a chair that made the morning harder.

“I have got one thing to say and it is about a chair and not about the people standing on this landing, and I have checked that twice on the way up here, and I would like anybody to stop me if I am wrong about it being about a chair.”

Nobody stopped him.

“A man carries a chair into a house. He puts it down where somebody works. He is not in the house, he does not work in the house, and he does not come back the next morning to put another one down.” He put his hands behind him. “And then he goes away, and he does not come back at the hour it is found, and everybody in that house has to decide what it is, and every one of them decides the same thing, and it is that he did not want to be thanked.”

The landing did not make a sound.

“And that is a way of giving that does not need him to be there. He gets the not being thanked, which is the only thing that trade ever gave him, and he gets it by leaving. And I have been on this landing a fortnight telling this step that a thing put down where somebody works has no giver in it, and it has no giver in it, and I still believe that, and I am telling you this morning that it has an absence in it instead, and an absence is a shape, and a shape can be read.”

He stopped for a moment and worked out where he was.

“So here is the sentence. Leaving a thing and not being there is not a way of giving without being thanked. It is a way of arranging to be the one who is not present at the moment the thing counts.” He said it out loud, in front of the whole landing, and did not say it again.

“And a thing with an absence in it can be read as generously as a thing with a presence in it. And I am saying that on a Wednesday with sixteen inches standing on the step, in front of about nine ears, and I am not going to say it a second time while I am on this landing, and I would be obliged if nobody repeated it back to me.”

The man of forty-three stood four steps down with the broom in his hand and did not move while any of that was being said.

“I am not going to agree with it and I am not going to take it apart,” Corvin Tace said. “That is on purpose and I would like it noticed. This landing has spent a fortnight refusing a reading and a man has just put a different reading on the step, and the right thing for a man with a broom and nineteen years is neither to agree nor to take it apart, because agreeing gives it a keeper and taking it apart gives it an answer, and it is not going to get either off this landing.”

The man who moves chairs stood where he was.

“And now the second half, and it is worse than his, and I am the one who has to say it, because I am the one who would have to do the asking.” Corvin Tace came down to the flat of the steps. “You cannot ask the person who left a thing what they meant by it. Not because they would not answer. Because asking is the one thing that would put them back.”

Nobody on that landing said anything.

“The second anybody on this step asks one of them what they meant by that thing in the fortieth step, they stop being somebody who was here and went away, and they become a person who was here and had something to say about it. And then they are on this landing. And then there is one more of us who came and went and did not say, and every one of us who came and went and did not say has to be handed round together.” He set the broom against the wall.

“And I am telling you that nobody on this step is going to ask anybody what they did. Not him. Not anybody. That is not a favour to them and it is not a favour to me and it is not the right of anything. It is the only way there is to leave a thing alone.”

Nobody said what the only way there was to leave a thing alone.

The boy came up out of the lane with his own figure and said it before anybody could ask him for it, and then said the other thing, and nobody on that landing had told him anything about the first one.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and thirteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and thirteen is nine hundred and sixty-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and thirteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.” He looked round the landing. “And a man said a thing about a chair just now and I am not going to repeat it and I am not going to work out whether I think it is right, and I want it noticed that I did not work out whether I think it is right.”

Nobody told him he did or did not think it was right.

“Because if I start working out whether a thing is right then I have got a view about it, and a view is a thing I would have to keep, and I am thirteen and I have got nothing on the step this week except a count and I would like to keep it that way.” He sat down on the wall on his own coat. “And nobody is to thank the man who said it either, and nobody is to thank me for saying I am not repeating it.”

Nobody thanked anybody for anything.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour with sixteen inches above the step he was standing on, and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and heard the sentence and was not helped with it and did not try to be.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and twelve days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-one days old.

“Four hundred and twelve days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-one days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-sixteenth is two hundred and sixty-six days old, which is nine hundred and sixty-six less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

He stood at the top of the steps with his hands behind his back and did not say anything for a while.

“One hundred and six wrong things in one hundred and six mornings, and here is the hundred and sixth, and it is the only one this week that I am not going to explain.” He looked at the floor of the top step. “A man said a thing about a chair on the step in front of me and it was not about me, and I want to say that I noticed that, because it is the first time in this county that something has been said out loud that was not about me and was still useful, and I am not going to stand here and get hold of it.”

“Nobody is going to hand you anything,” said the man with the broom.

“No, and I am not going to ask for it, and I would rather have it that way than not, and that is a sentence I have not said on this landing before and I am only saying it once.” He turned round. “I have spent eleven years wanting somebody in this county to put me in a sentence about a thing. It has happened, and it was about a chair, and it cost the man who said it something, and I got nothing out of it at all, and I am not going to be relieved about that and I am not going to be unrelieved either.”

Nobody was relieved and nobody was unrelieved.

Nobody on that landing acted on the sentence. Nobody wrote it down, entered it on a line, minuted it, recorded it, copied it, or carried it off that bank. Nobody said it back to the man who had said it. And nothing was convened, because nothing was going to be, because there was nothing to hear.

Then the man who moves chairs said the last of it, and it was not about the sentence and it was the reason he had come up.

“And there is one more thing and it is nothing to do with a chair, and I would rather say it now than have it said about me later.” He took his hands out from behind him. “The woman whose chairs these are has not been on this landing this week and she has not been up this road and she is not going to be, and nobody on this step is to say anything to her about any of it, and nobody is to go up that road and tell her that a thing was said this morning that she was not there for.”

Nobody said anything about her.

“And nobody is to thank her either, before anybody gets there, and there is nothing to thank her for, and if somebody does thank her for it then that person has turned a thing she was kept out of into a thing she did.” He looked round. “I move her chairs. That is all I have got to do with her and I have never asked her for anything else and I am not going to start on the morning a man says something about an absence.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody said they wished they had, and nobody on that landing went up that road.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety steps and went round the fortieth and went round the chair and set the broom against the wall where it has been every morning for nineteen years, and did not sit on the chair, and did not touch the thing in the seam of the fortieth step, and nothing that had been put down anywhere was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used.

And the water stood at sixteen inches on the top step and held there, which was four days and a figure of the water and nothing else, and a sentence about a thing was on that landing that had been said once and would not be said again in new words, and nobody had been relieved, and nobody had been let off, and nobody had been thanked, and the account was exactly as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour.
