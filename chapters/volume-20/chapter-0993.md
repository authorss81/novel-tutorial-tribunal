# Chapter 993: A Tuesday And One Day Of The Coming Back And A Man Of Thirty-One Stood At The Top Of Those Steps And Said Out Loud What The Thing He Put Down Is Going To Be Called By Other People From Now On And Could Not Say That It Was Not That, And Nobody Told Him Whether He Ought To Have

First day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-third morning. Five hundred and ninety-three days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and one weeks and three days old. Five hundred and forty-seven days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and thirty-nine days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said that he was not going to stand on his own stone and say a day in advance, and that the water had never once needed him to.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that the shortest of the three figures was the one that told him least, and that he was not going to improve it by saying it twice.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Tuesday. Three figures.”

He stood on the head of the broom.

“One day is the shortest figure I give. It is not a warning and it is not a run-up to anything. The water will say whatever it is going to say in its own time and I am not going to help it along by getting here early with a sentence about it.”

Nobody helped it along.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that he had asked on three mornings running now and had noticed that nobody on that landing had changed shape.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-second mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-second. Nine hundred and ninety-three less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and you have told me the shape of us has not changed, and I am not going to tell you the shape of us has changed, because that would be inventing.”

“No.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I have worked out this week that the shape of you not changing is not a thing you did. It is just what standing still looks like from the outside, and I would like it said that I know that and I am still standing here.”

Nobody said the shape of anybody had changed and nobody told Barnaby Crove he was owed an answer.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing and went all the way up to the dry strip and stood over both pieces of ash without touching either of them.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and thirty-nine days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and eight days old.

“Four hundred and thirty-nine days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and eight days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-third is two hundred and ninety-three days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-three less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it, I have not been offered a way out of it, and I am not going to put either of those two things in the same breath as what I am about to say.”

Nobody answered him and nobody offered him anything.

“One hundred and thirty-three wrong things in one hundred and thirty-three mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-third, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nothing was said to him about either of those.

Then he stood there a while, and the light came off the wall onto the stone in front of his boots, and he started.

“It is going to be called a barrow haft. It is going to be called a thing for Barnaby Crove’s barrow. That is what a woman who saws lengths said in her own mouth on this landing yesterday and I have had a whole night to work out whether a word can be got back out of a mouth once it has come out of one, and it cannot.”

Nobody said a word.

“So I am going to say what it is called now, out loud, in my own mouth, while I am the one talking, so that it is me saying the name and not just nobody saying nothing. It is a barrow haft. That is the name on it. I did not put a name on it and I have not taken a name off it and I am not going to stand up here and say that it is not that.”

He did not say that it was not that.

“And I have worked out this morning what the whole of it is, standing at the top of these steps in the rain, and I am going to say it because nobody up here is going to say it and I have had four hundred and thirty-nine mornings of not being asked for anything.”

He put his thumb against the stone of the wall.

“A thing put down and not said so is a thing somebody else gets to name. That is all. That is the whole of what I have learned this week and it cost me a night’s sleep and one morning of standing in the rain to find out. The woman who named it did not take anything away from me. She put a name on a piece of wood I could not touch, and the name is wrong, and it is going to be the name for as long as that wood lies there.”

Nobody told him the name was wrong.

“And there is no way of taking a name off it either. A name comes off a piece of wood by somebody laying a hand on the wood, and there is not one person on this landing going to lay a hand on it, including the man who put it down, including me, and I put it down.”

Nobody laid a hand on it.

“And I have been standing up here for the best part of an hour waiting for somebody to tell me whether I ought to have said something on that step yesterday while she was saying it. Nobody has said it and nobody is going to and I am not going to ask, because a thing you ask to be told you were right about is a thing you have made.”

Nobody told him he should have said something and nobody told him he should not have said anything.

“And I am not going to be unrelieved about that and I am not going to be relieved either, and I have said those two things in that shape before on this landing and they are the same thing tonight and I am carrying both of them up that bank myself.”

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him, found the wall, and said what a name on a piece of wood does to a landing where two kinds of things have been lying side by side.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that nobody on this landing had ever told the man who cannot see well that the kind of wood with no name on it was the safe kind.

“There is a piece of wood on that strip with your name in it and a piece of wood on that strip with a use on it, and neither of them has a person in it, and I have been up this landing long enough to know that one of those two is a thing people do not put down and the other one is a thing people do.”

He kept his hand on the wall.

“And nobody has ever stood on this stone and told me that the one with no name on it was the safe one. It has just been what has been there. If somebody had come up nineteen years ago with a piece of wood and said nothing at all about it, I would have found it in the dark by putting my hand on it, and I would not have known whose it was, and that would have been the safest thing that has ever happened to me on these steps.”

Nobody told him he was safe.

“And now there is a piece of wood on that strip with a name on it and I cannot check the name, and I am not going to put my hand out to find out which piece is which, and that is the third time I have said that on this landing and I have said it in three different shapes and every one of the three has been true.”

Nobody thanked him for saying it in three shapes.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Neither piece. And I am not going to be thanked for this one and I am not going to be asked to take a turn at it.”

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used.

A man of thirty-one said out loud what his own thing is going to be called by other people and did not say that it was not that, and nobody told him in either direction what he ought to have done about it. The bank held at sixteen inches above the top step and about sixty of those ninety steps were under, and one day of the coming back was a figure of the water and not of the morning, and nobody on that landing carried anything anywhere.
