# Chapter 997: A Saturday And Twelve Inches And One Hundred And Two Weeks Said Out Loud At The Sixth Hour By A Man Who Cannot Read For The Seventh Time And Somebody Asked Him What A Whole Figure Of Weeks Is For And He Gave The Same Answer He Has Given And Added One Thing To It, And A Man With A Broom Said It Was A Habit Again

Fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-seventh morning. Five hundred and ninety-seven days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks old. Five hundred and fifty-one days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-three days in this county of Kell.

Twelve inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and then a man who cannot read came up the bank at about the sixth hour and said a whole figure out loud before anybody on that landing had finished its morning.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that a whole figure produced three times is a habit, that he had said that, and that this was four and he was not going to pretend four was a different animal from three.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-sixth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“One hundred and two weeks.” He put both hands flat on the wall, which is what he does. “Not one hundred and two weeks and a bit. One hundred and two weeks, on this Saturday, and it is the seventh one of those since I started saying it out loud.”

Nobody worked that figure out for him on that landing.

“It has come out whole seven times and it has gone up by one each time, and it was ninety-six weeks the first morning I stood up here and said one out loud, and it is the fourth week running that I have come up on a Saturday and had one of these sitting in my mouth ready.”

Nobody worked it out for him.

Then the man with the broom gave his three figures off his stone and then said the rest of what he had come up to say, which was about a habit and not about the water.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, five days of the coming back, and about seventy of those ninety steps under, on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Saturday. Three figures.”

He put the head of the broom down flat.

“I said on this landing that a whole figure produced three times is a habit, and I named it, and I said at the time that I had got it out of my own mouth and that it belonged to me. Well. It is four this morning and I am not going to stand up here and pretend that four is a different animal from three. It is a habit. He has got it and he has not got it out of it and I have not got it out of mine either and mine is nineteen years long.”

Nothing was said about which of the two habits was longer.

“And here is why I say it. Not to take it off him. He puts it down at the sixth hour and I give mine at the seventh and we are two men doing the same thing in the same week and neither of us is doing it on purpose, and that is exactly what a habit is. The not doing it on purpose is the whole of it. He could stop tomorrow and so could I and neither of us is going to.”

He picked the broom up.

“And nobody is to work it out for him. Not the water and not the wall and not the fever. If anybody up here works a whole figure out for a man who cannot read, then he has got a fifth figure off that stone, and it will not be a figure about him, it will be a figure about the man who worked it out.”

Nobody on that landing worked anything out for him.

Then the man of fifty-four asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that a whole figure and his question had never once got in each other’s way and that he was glad of it and would not be thanked for being glad.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.” He said it in the words he uses. “And it is a Saturday and there is a whole figure in my mouth and neither of them has anything to do with the other, and I am not going to put one behind the other to get at the answer. I have tried that with a question before. It does not work and it makes a man look like he is after something.”

Nobody answered Barnaby Crove on that Saturday, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for being glad about anything.

Then the man who cannot see well came up the cart road and onto the flat of the steps with his hand out in front of him, found the wall with it, and asked the question nobody on that landing had ever asked him.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he was asking about the figure of weeks and not about Thursday’s figure, because one of the two was his to ask about and the other one was not.

“What is a whole figure of weeks for.” He said it without stopping, the way a man says something he has had ready since the sixth hour. “Not Thursday’s number. I am not asking about that, and I said out loud earlier in this week that I would not ask about it, and I did not ask on the morning she said it, and I am not asking now.”

Nobody told him he had better not.

“I am asking about one hundred and two weeks, because I have listened to that come out of a man’s mouth on this landing seven Saturdays running and nobody has ever asked him what it is for, and I have watched seven of them, and I am the one on this stone who is supposed to be the one who asks.”

Barnaby Crove did not turn round. His hands stayed flat on the stone.

“I do not know what a whole figure of weeks is for.” He said it into the wall. “That is the same answer I have given every time anybody has got round to asking me, and it is the only answer I have got, and I am not going to improve it for you on a Saturday because you have finally come up.”

It was the same answer he had given before.

“And I am going to add one thing to it and then that is the whole of what I have got in my mouth this morning.” He took his hands off the wall. “Nobody on this step has ever asked me what I want it to be for. Not once. Not on any of the seven. A man who cannot read is asked what a number is for, if he is asked at all, and nobody in my life has ever asked me what I would like a number to be for.”

The landing went quiet.

“That is not the same question and I know it well enough not to pretend otherwise. I am telling you that I have noticed the difference, and that I have not used the difference to get at my morning. Somebody up here ought to have asked me that other one instead. Nobody did, and I did not ask for it, and I am not going to be unrelieved about that.”

Nobody asked him that one, and nobody said that was a good thing to have noticed, and nobody told him what his figure was for.

Then the woman who saws lengths came up the cart road with her saw handle under her arm and stood at the bottom of the steps and said one thing about the wood on the dry strip, and it took her a while to get to it, and nobody helped her get to it.

Wenna Pyle is thirty-eight, who saws lengths and sets studs at a bench in an open-sided shed behind a wheelwright’s about three hundred yards down the cart road, and who carries her own cup out to that bench because there is no table in the shed, and who said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that she was not going to say a second word about that piece of wood and that the not saying was harder than the saying had been.

“I am not going to say another word about that wood this week and I would like it noticed that the not saying is harder than the saying was, and that nobody is to thank me for the difficulty because I did not do it for them.”

Nobody thanked her for the difficulty.

“I said on Monday what I thought it was and I gave the working and I said it out loud on purpose before anybody else could say it wrong. That was the right thing to do in my trade. And it has been the rest of this week and I have been at that bench with a piece of ash in my head and a barrow shaft in my head and a man who cannot see well in my head, and I have set four studs crooked.”

Nobody told her she had set four studs crooked and nobody told her she had not.

“And the reason is that a name on a piece of wood cannot be argued with. In my trade I say a thing is a haft and then somebody takes it and fits it and either the eye holds it or it splits, and either way we both find out the same afternoon. That is what a name is for. It is not for the wood. It is for the two people who are going to find out about the wood together.”

Nobody found out about that wood together.

“And on this landing there is no afternoon. There is no fitting and no finding out and nobody is going to ever put it in anything, and so my name is just sitting there on it, and I put it there, and I cannot take it off with a saw without picking it up.”

Nobody picked it up.

“And I will say the seven of them, because I have said them on this landing before and I am not going to be thanked for this one and I am not going to be asked to say them again this week. It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. That is the ash and that is the iron and that is the chair.”

Nobody thanked her for that either and nobody told her to go over them again.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stood where he could see both pieces of ash and neither of them had a giver in it that anybody was going to say out loud.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-three days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and twelve days old.

“Four hundred and forty-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and twelve days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-seventh is two hundred and ninety-seven days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-seven less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has offered me a way out of it, and I did not ask for one this morning and I am not going to ask for one on a Saturday.”

He reached the top of the steps and stood there.

“One hundred and thirty-seven wrong things in one hundred and thirty-seven mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-seventh, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nothing came back to him on either count.

“I am going to say three things and they are the same three I said yesterday and the day before, and I am going to say them the same way, because a man who says the same three things three ways has turned them into a speech and I do not have a speech.” He did not move his boots.

“My want. To be somebody. My cost. Asking is the only thing that costs me nothing and I have not asked for anything for four hundred and forty-three days. And the third thing is that a woman on Thursday said out loud what the only thing in this county that could have been checked is for, and I did not say it, and I am going to keep not saying it, and I am not going to be relieved about that and I am not going to be unrelieved either.”

Not one of them was relieved and not one of them was unrelieved.

“And I am not going to stand on this Saturday and tell anybody what my three things are worth. There is a man at the bottom of these steps who has just said out loud that nobody has ever asked him what he would like a number to be for, and I have been on this landing all morning wanting somebody to ask me a question I would have to be honest about, and nobody has, and I am not going to make a speech out of the difference.”

Nobody asked him anything.

Twelve inches on the top step and about seventy of those ninety steps under and five days of the coming back. Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used. A whole figure of weeks came out of a man’s mouth at the sixth hour for the seventh time and it did not become a meaning and nobody worked it out for him and nobody told him what it was for, and a man with a broom said out loud that a whole figure produced four times is a habit, and nobody told him he was right about that either.
