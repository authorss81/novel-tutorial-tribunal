# Chapter 998: A Sunday And Sixteen Inches Over The Top Step And Four Days Of The Coming Back, And A Man With A Broom Gave A Fourth Figure He Said On Another Morning He Was Finished With And Was Not Thanked For Giving It Again, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Put His Hand Out Along That Wall And Stopped Short Of It And Nobody Took His Hand Down

Sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-eighth morning. Five hundred and ninety-eight days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks and one day old. Five hundred and fifty-two days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-four days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and then gave a fourth, and said out loud that he had promised nineteen weeks ago he would not give that one again.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, on a full bank, that four days is a figure of the water and not a figure of this landing and not a figure of this morning.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Sunday. Three figures.”

He leaned the broom against the wall, clear of the strip.

“And here is the fourth, and I am going to give it, and I said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-second week that I was finished with it and that I would say it once and not again, and I have said it since, and I am not going to pretend I kept my word about that.” He put his thumb on the wall. “I am not going to apologise for it either. An apology is a thing you do to a person and there is no person in a figure of the water.”

Nothing was said about the two things he had said.

“Four days is a figure of the water and it is not a figure of this landing and it is not a figure of this morning and it has nothing to do with a Sunday, whatever a Sunday turns out to be like. Nobody is going to carry four days off this stone and have a conversation on it, and if anybody does I will stop sweeping for the day.”

Nobody carried it off the stone.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with a full bank behind him, asked his question on it in the same words at the same hour, and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that he had noticed the water was up and that he had asked on a morning with nothing to lean his question against as well as one with everything.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-seventh mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-seventh. Nine hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and there is a full bank behind you.”

“Yes, and I know what a full bank is, and I am telling you it has not changed my question, and I am telling you that before you tell me that a full bank is not a kind of morning.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I have asked it on a morning with a full bank and on a morning with the steps bare and it is the same question and I would like whoever answers it one day to have heard both.”

Nobody answered Barnaby Crove on that full bank, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for asking twice in one week in two shapes.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stayed.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-four days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and thirteen days old.

“Four hundred and forty-four days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and thirteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-eighth is two hundred and ninety-eight days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-eight less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has offered me a way out of it, and I did not ask for one.”

He came up the last two steps and stood on the top one.

“One hundred and thirty-eight wrong things in one hundred and thirty-eight mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-eighth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything at all to him on either count.

“Three things, in the same shape, because I said yesterday I was not going to change them into a speech. My want, which is to be somebody. My cost, which is that asking costs me nothing and I have not asked for anything for four hundred and forty-four days. And the third thing, which is that I have said nothing for five days and a wrong name is standing on that strip because of it, and I am not going to be unrelieved about that and I am not going to be relieved either.”

Nobody was relieved and nobody unrelieved.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the tenth hour with his hand out in front of him, and said out loud what he was going to do before he did it.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he was going to walk his hand along that wall to the end of the strip and stop, and that he was telling everybody at the top of his voice first because he was not going to do it quietly and get caught.

“I am going to do something on this landing I have not done in a long while and I am going to say it first, out loud, in front of all of you, because if I do it quietly then somebody finds out afterwards and it turns into a thing that happened.” He got his hand to the wall. “I am going to walk my hand along this wall to the end of that strip the way I have done a hundred times without stopping, and I am going to stop where the wood is, and then I am going to stand here with my hand like that.”

He was not stopped and nobody helped him.

He got to the strip. His fingers went along the stone and came to the wood and stopped.

His hand stayed there.

“And here I am. Nineteen years on this landing and my hand has been at the end of that strip about a thousand mornings and it has never stopped at anything, because there has never been anything on it to stop at.” He did not move his fingers. “There is something on it now and my hand has found it and it has not touched it.”

Nobody said whether that was right and nobody said whether it was wrong.

“I am not going to say what it is for. I have wanted something off that strip since I came up this bank and I am not going to say what it was, because the moment a person says what a thing on that strip is for, then it has a person in it, and there is no giver in it, and a thing with a person in it and no giver in it is the worst kind there is and I would rather have nothing.”

Nothing on that landing had a person in it.

“Take it I will not. That is the whole of what I came up to say this morning, and it is the first time I have said it out loud on this landing. Nobody is to thank me for the saying and nobody is to ask me afterwards whether I could manage.”

Nobody asked him whether he could manage and nobody thanked him.

“If there is a thing on that strip that somebody put there for somebody, I would like it said out loud in somebody else’s mouth that I do not want it. Not in mine. In somebody else’s. And I am not going to ask who put it there, and I am not going to be glad that it is here, and if it is not for anybody then that is a thing nobody needs to explain to me.”

Nobody said it in anybody else’s mouth. Nobody asked who put it there. Nobody said whether it was for anybody.

He stood with his hand at the end of that strip for a long time, and then he took it off and said the seven of them against the wall, and nobody thanked him and nobody asked him to say them again.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. That includes the thing my hand has been resting on and that is the first time I have ever said those seven about something I have had my hand on, and I am not going to be told I was careful about it.”

He went down the steps one at a time with his hand out and nobody helped him down, and nobody said one word about the whole of it.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on the chair. He did not touch the iron in the seam and he did not touch either piece of ash on the dry strip. Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used.

Sixteen inches over that top step and four days of the coming back, which is a figure of the water and nothing else, and nobody carried it off that stone as a kind of day. The name on the second piece of ash was still wrong and nobody on that landing could take it off without laying a hand on the wood, and four days from now the water comes off that bank again and the name will be standing on it.
