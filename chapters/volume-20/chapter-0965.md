# Chapter 965: Twelve Inches On The Top Step And About Seventy Of Those Ninety Steps Under And Five Days Of The Coming Back, And The Man Who Holds That Landing Worked Out Something About How Long A Thing Can Be Left And Then Refused To Say The Figure Of It

First day of the hundred and sixtieth week. His seven hundred and fifteenth morning. Five hundred and sixty-five days after the settlement. The fever ninety-seven weeks and three days old. Five hundred and nineteen days since the division. Four hundred and sixty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and eleven days in this county of Kell.

Twelve inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then worked out a thing in front of everybody and stopped one word short of the figure of it, out loud, on purpose.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and on the first morning of the hundred and sixtieth week gave a whole working out and no figure at the end of it.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, five days of the coming back, and about seventy of those ninety steps under, on the first day of the hundred and sixtieth week, and it is a Tuesday. Three figures.” He did not set the broom down, which meant he had not finished. “And I am not going to say what the morning after this one is. I told this landing on Friday morning that I do not hand out mornings and I meant it and I am not going to be the one who breaks it in a week where I said it twice.”

Nobody asked him to.

“Here is the other thing and it is about a thing and not about the water and not about any of you.” He came down to the flat of the steps.

“On Tuesday morning of last week the man who moves chairs stood at the back of this landing and told me that if a thing is put down and nobody touches it and nobody says who put it there, then after a while there is no way on earth for anybody to tell whether it is still being left or whether it has been there since the beginning. I told him he was half right and I told him he was the cleverest thing said on this step since I came to it, and then I said I had no answer to it, and I said I had to have one by Friday.”

“You did not have one on Friday.”

“I did not have one on Friday and I have not had one since and that is on the record and I would like it to stay on the record.” He turned the broom in his hands. “And here is the answer, and I am going to give you the whole of it, and the end of it is a figure I am not going to say, so do not wait for it.”

Nobody waited for it.

“The working is this. There is a point in it. Past that point, the two stop being different — whether a thing is still being left and whether a thing has stopped being left — and when they stop being different they do not go away. They go quiet. And a quiet thing on a landing is worse than a loud one, because a loud one gets looked at and a quiet one gets built into the shape of where people work.” He stopped. “That is the working.”

“Then what is the figure,” said the man who moves chairs, from the back of the landing.

“There is no figure.”

“There is a figure for how long a thing can be left before that point, and you have worked it out, and you have just said the whole of it except that.”

“I have worked out that there is one. I have not worked out what it is, and I am not going to work out what it is, and that is not a gap in the answer, it is the answer.” He put the broom against the wall at the bottom of the steps and took his hands off it and folded them.

“The figure of how long anything has been in a place in this county is zero. Not about nine. Not about a season. Zero, and I will say that out loud as often as anybody wants it said, because it is the only figure of its kind I have ever been given and I have had it since before any of you were on this bank.”

“A figure of zero.”

“A figure of zero for how long a thing has been in a place. Yes. It means that a person in this county does not know how long a thing has been there and is not going to be told, and that anybody who works out that number has not found out a fact about the thing. He has found out that he is the kind of man who counts.” He looked round the landing.

“I am not that man and I have not been that man since the first morning of this week, and I gave it up on purpose on the sixth morning of that week, in front of witnesses, and nobody has thanked me for giving it up and nobody is going to, and I did not do it for that.”

Nobody thanked him for it.

“And I know what that costs,” he said. “It costs that in about nine days one of you is going to stand on that wall and work it out by counting, and every count will be right, and the number will be good, and it will be the only number anybody in this county has got about anything that has ever happened on this landing, and it will be a number about him.”

Nobody on that landing had worked it out.

“I am going to say it once more and then I am going to sweep, and the once more is this. It is not that the figure is forbidden. It is that the figure is about the man who works it out and not about the thing, and the whole of this business has gone wrong for four volumes because people keep handing each other figures about themselves and calling them figures about the county.”

The woman who saws lengths at a bench was at the bottom of the steps with the saw handle under her arm, and she had come up on the first morning of a week and had not come up on the last day of the one before, and she said the thing that made the working work.

“I know how to do that,” she said. “Not here. In my own work. There is a stage where a cut in a piece of wood stops being a cut somebody is making and becomes a mark that is in the wood, and you cannot get it back and you cannot date it off the grain afterwards, because the grain does not keep the days.” She shifted the saw handle. “And I could tell you how long mine takes. I have been doing it fourteen years and I could put a figure on it inside a minute and it would not be a guess.”

“Thank you,” said the man with the broom, and then heard himself, and stopped.

“Do not thank me. I was not giving it to you. I was telling you that I have got one and that I am not handing it over, and if you thank me for not handing it over then the not-handing has become something I did and I would rather it stayed a thing I did not do.”

“No one on this landing is going to thank you for anything,” said the man who moves chairs, from the back of it. “I am not going to and I have never thanked anybody in my life and I am not going to start because a man said thank you and then heard himself.”

The man with the broom said nothing about that at all.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour with twelve inches above his boots and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and then worked out something of his own and got it wrong in the middle and said so.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and eleven days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty days old.

“Four hundred and eleven days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fifteenth is two hundred and sixty-five days old, which is nine hundred and sixty-five less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

He stood at the top of the steps with his hands behind his back and looked at the man who had just refused to give them a number.

“One hundred and five wrong things in one hundred and five mornings, and here is the hundred and fifth, and it is a working out and it comes out wrong.” He turned round. “I have been trying since Friday to work out what I am allowed to ask about that step. And my answer was that I am allowed to ask about it, and the reason I am allowed is that nobody works there except him, and a step is a thing people stand on, and asking about a step is asking a man about his own work.”

“That is not wrong,” said the man with the broom.

“It is wrong by the end.” He did not move his hands.

“Because the moment I ask about the step I am a man who comes up here about that step, and the morning after that there is a man on this landing who comes up about a thing he is not paid to come up about, and after that there is no way to get back to a landing where a man only talks about the weather and his own work. And that is how you end up with an account. Nobody sets out to write one. Somebody asks one question too many and then there is a thing on paper that a person can be asked about, and every person in it is a figure.”

Nobody took a note and nobody wrote anything down.

“So I am not going to ask. And I am not going to be thanked for not asking, because I am not going to tell anybody that I did not ask until afterwards, and by then it will be a thing I did not do and I would rather it was a thing I did not do.” He turned round again. “And that is the hundred and fifth and I would like the landing to notice that I have got the number right this time even though I have got the working wrong, and that nobody is going to say so.”

Nobody said so, and nobody said it was wrong, and nobody told him he had it right.

The woman who saws lengths went back down the cart road to a bench with a cup standing on it that had not been touched since Saturday. The man of forty-three swept eighty-nine steps and did not put his weight on the fortieth one at any point in the morning, and did not touch it, and nothing that had been put down anywhere was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and the water was twelve inches on the top step and going up, and nobody on that landing said what the morning after it would be.
