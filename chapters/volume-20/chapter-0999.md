# Chapter 999: Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Sixty-Fourth Week, And A Woman Who Reads For A Living Walked Up The Cart Road And Said The Ordinary Form Out Loud In Her Own Mouth And Refused To Name It And Nobody Handed Her A Word For It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asked Whether It Covered Him And A Woman Told Him It Was About Somebody Else’s Morning

Seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-ninth morning. Five hundred and ninety-nine days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks and two days old. Five hundred and fifty-three days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-five days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and three days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said that it was the last morning of a week and that he was going to be short of things to say again and was not going to fill the gap.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he had said one thing at the end of a week in this county once and had not stopped paying for it, and was going to say one thing again and it was going to cost him something, and would not say it was going to be different.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, three days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Monday. Three figures.”

He set the head of the broom flat on the stone and stood on that.

“I am going to be short today and I am not going to fill the gap. There is going to be a speech on this landing before the ninth hour and I have already worked out most of what it is going to be and I am not going to say one word about it in advance.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour for the last time in that week and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that a week ending was not an occasion and that he was not going to bring a new question for a Tuesday.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-eighth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-eighth. Nine hundred and ninety-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and it is the last asking of this week and it changes nothing.”

“No.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And it is the last one this week and tomorrow it is the same question in the same words and I am not going to change it for the turn of a week, because you have all had a turn of a week and I have had none of them.”

Nobody told Barnaby Crove he had had none of them either.

Nobody answered him on that Monday, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for the last asking of the week, and he was going to be up on the first morning of the next one with the same question in the same words.

Then the woman who reads for a living came up the cart road at about the fourth hour with the book shut against her knee, and asked for nothing, and gave the shape of the whole of her trade in her own mouth, and refused to name it.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and said on the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that she was going to say the commonest shape there is in other people’s pages, out loud, on a landing where there is no reader, and then refuse to put a word on it.

She came up the steps and did not sit down.

“I have been walking up this road since the fourth hour with a sentence and I have had it ready twice and thrown it away both times, and I am going to stand up here and say the shape of it out loud in front of all of you, and then I am going to refuse to name it, and nobody is to stop me and nobody is to thank me.”

Nobody stopped her and nobody thanked her.

“Here is the shape. A thing gets carried into a room where a person works. And the person who carried it does not come back the next morning with another one.” She put her hands behind her back. “That is not a kindness in those pages and it is not a rule either. It is simply what the person does, because a person who comes back the next morning with a second one has turned a thing into a habit, and a habit is a thing a man has to go on keeping, and in every page I have ever read there is nobody in the room keeping anything.”

Nobody made a face at that.

“And here is the half that everybody skips, and it is the half that matters and it is the half that has never once happened on this landing.” She turned round to the bottom of the steps. “The person whose work it was is never asked whether they took it. Not once. Not because the answer would be bad. Because asking is a thing you only do to a person you are prepared to owe something to afterwards, and in nineteen years on this stone nobody has been prepared to owe anybody anything, and so nobody has asked.”

Nobody on that landing had asked anybody that.

“And I am telling you this because I have read for twenty-three years, and in every page that has ever used that shape the asking has been the part the reader is moved by, and in every one of those pages the asking does not exist on the ground. It exists in the reader. It is a way of making a person feel something about a piece of wood in a room.”

Nobody made a face about that either.

“So I have said it once here, where there is no reader at all, and I am not going to name it. I have been waiting nineteen years for somebody on this landing to hand me a word for it and I am not going to take the word, because the day this landing gives that shape a name it stops being a thing that people do and it starts being a thing that somebody decided, and then it is a rule, and a rule on this landing is a thing with a person inside it.”

Nobody handed her a word and nobody gave that shape a name.

“And I am not going to call it a right. There is a word that a great many people in this county believe they are owed, and it is not that word, and I am not going to hand it out on a Monday for nothing.”

Nobody asked her what the word was.

“And it is not a form either. A form is a thing that has been written down, and a thing that has been written down can be carried to somebody else to be kept, and nothing on that landing has been written down and nothing on that landing is going to be.”

Nothing on that landing had been written down.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Seven things, and I have said them on this landing before and I am not going to be thanked for this one and I am not going to be asked to say them again this week.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to say them a further time.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and then asked the only question he had come up that bank with.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-five days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and fourteen days old.

“Four hundred and forty-five days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and fourteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-ninth is two hundred and ninety-nine days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has offered me a way out of it, and I did not ask for one this morning.”

He stood at the head of the steps.

“One hundred and thirty-nine wrong things in one hundred and thirty-nine mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-ninth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody answered either half of it.

“A woman said a shape out loud on this landing at the eighth hour and it is the shape of what I have been doing since Sunday morning, and I am going to ask the question I have been carrying up that bank since I got to the top of it, and I am going to ask it in front of everybody on it.”

Nobody stopped him.

“Does it cover me. I am asking whether that shape covers a man who puts a thing down where somebody works, says nothing, walks down a bank, and comes back up it the next morning and does not put another one there.”

He was told neither that it covered him nor that it did not.

“Because if it does not, then there is a piece of wood on that strip with a wrong name on it and nothing in this county that fits it. And if it does, then I have been doing a thing that people do since Sunday morning, and I have not worked it out once, and I put it down in the dark on a Sunday morning and I said nothing about it in the light on a Monday morning.”

Nobody told him which of those two things was true.

“And I am not going to be unrelieved about the silence and I am not going to be relieved either, and I have said those two things in that shape before on this landing and they are the same thing tonight and I am carrying both of them up that bank myself.”

Then the woman who reads for a living said one more sentence to him, and nobody on that landing stopped her.

“That shape is about somebody else’s morning.” She did not sit down and did not soften it. “It is about a person who has a place and a work and a person who puts a thing down for them and then goes. You have no place on this landing and no work here and there is nobody whose morning that shape is about when you are standing in it. That is not a kindness to you and it is not a punishment, and it is the reason it does not fit, and I am not going to be thanked for it.”

He was told that, and nobody else on that landing said one word about it.

“And I am going to say the other half of it before somebody else puts it in for me, because it is mine.” He had his hands at his sides. “A thing left beside a person is not a version of what that person has wanted. Not by a hair. I have wanted a thing to be put down beside me for four hundred and forty-five mornings, and I am not going to let what is standing on that strip get turned into that, because whoever left it there did not know what I wanted and I did not tell them, and two people not knowing a thing about each other is not a shape.”

Nobody said that was a good shape and nobody said it was a bad one.

Then the man of thirty-one gave the whole of what he had come up that bank with, out loud, in one place, and said he was not going to say what the second piece of wood was going to become, and nobody on that landing said what it was going to become either.

“An offer at two hundred and ninety-nine days, unanswered, asked on none of this week, offered a withdrawal on none of it. A figure of mistakes that nobody has told me the size of and nobody is going to. A shape said out loud by a woman who reads for a living that nobody has put a word to. A name on a piece of wood that is wrong and that I put on it by not speaking. That is everything, and I have said all of it, and I have not asked for one thing out of any of it.”

Nobody asked him for one thing out of any of it and nobody gave him one thing out of any of it.

Sixteen inches over that top step and about sixty of those ninety steps under and three days of the coming back, which is a figure of the water and not of the morning.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used. A shape was said out loud in a mouth on the last morning of that week and it was not given a name and it was not called a right and it did not answer anybody and it resolved nothing, and the man who had put a thing down on that landing on a Sunday morning was told out loud that the shape was about somebody else’s morning and nobody told him he was right about that and nobody told him he was wrong.
