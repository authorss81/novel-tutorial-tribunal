# Chapter 994: A Wednesday And The Water Came Off That Bank And All Ninety Of Those Steps Were Walkable And There Was No Figure Of Inches And No Figure Of Steps Under And No Figure Of Days And It Was Not The Last Day Of A Week, And A Man Who Cannot See Well Said Out Loud What A Thing Becomes When It Has Been Named Wrong By Somebody Else

Second day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-fourth morning. Five hundred and ninety-four days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and one weeks and four days old. Five hundred and forty-eight days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty days in this county of Kell.

The water came off that bank at about the sixth hour and all ninety of those steps were walkable by about the seventh, and that was the thirty-first time.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he had said three absences out loud by name every morning the water came off that bank and that he was going to say them in the same shape, and that a shape you change on a morning like this is a shape you are dressing up.

“Sixteen is not on that bank this morning and I am not reaching for it out of habit. There is no number of steps under and there is no number of days.” He put the broom down flat on the stone.

“And I am not reaching for a feeling of it either, because a feeling of it is a figure in a worse coat. A morning with nothing on the stone is not a better morning and it is not a worse one. It is the shape of it.”

He left the broom where it was.

“I have been asked by nobody this morning and I am going to say one more thing and it is about the day and not about the water. This is the middle of a week and not the end of one, and it is a Wednesday, and in nineteen years that has made no difference to this bank and I have never once let it make one.”

Nothing on that landing carried a figure of inches or of steps under or of days, and nobody on that landing reached for one out of another morning, and nobody put anything in the gap.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour with the stone bare behind him and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-third mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-third. Nine hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and there is nothing at all on that stone this morning to set it against.”

“No, and this is the morning I have been waiting a long while for, and I said that a fortnight ago in the same words, and I am going to say it in the same words again and nobody is to think I have improved it.”

Nobody thought he had improved it.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And there is a man on this landing who has worked out what is going to be said about a piece of wood on that wall and who is standing up here this morning with it in his mouth, and I would like him to know that it is not me he is talking about.”

Nobody told the man of thirty-one what he was talking about.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour to a landing with no water on the steps at all to lean his own figures on and gave his figures anyway and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and nine days old.

“Four hundred and forty days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and nine days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-fourth is two hundred and ninety-four days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-four less seven hundred. It is unanswered, I have not asked it, and there is nothing on that stone this morning to stand it against, and I am going to keep giving it into an empty morning for as long as I am in this county.”

Nobody stopped him and nobody helped him.

“One hundred and thirty-four wrong things in one hundred and thirty-four mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-fourth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said one word about the kind of figure it was and nobody told him he had found anything.

“And here is the half of this morning I have been carrying up the bank since Tuesday, and I am going to say it with nothing in the world to put it beside.”

He stopped where he was, well below the dry strip.

“A man who put a thing down and said nothing about it is a man who gave up the right to name it, and he knew that when he did it, and I want that said by me and not worked out by any of you. He did not think it was a fair trade. He thought it was the cheapest thing in this county that costs nothing to do, and he was right about the price and wrong about everything else.”

Nobody on that landing took his side and nobody took the other.

“Then somebody who did not put it down said out loud what she thought it was, and she said it out loud on purpose, before anybody else could say it wrong, and that was fair of her and it was careful, and it has taken the name off it and put a wrong one on, and it cannot be put back.”

Nobody said it had been careful and nobody said it had not.

“So a thing like that does not become a thing with a giver in it. That is not what happens. It becomes a thing with a giver in it and a woman in it and a name on it that belongs to neither of them, and the man who put it down is the only one of the three who can say a word about it, and he is not going to say it, and I am not going to say it, and that is not the same as saying it and being quiet afterwards.”

Nobody gave an answer to any part of that.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps with his hand out in front of him and found the wall with it and stood there a while, and then said the whole of what he had.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on a morning with nothing at all on the stone that a thing which has been named by somebody who did not put it there becomes a thing that two people are standing next to and neither of them can touch.

“I am going to start where I have started every time, because you have all heard me start here and I would be making a different speech than I have got.” He put his back to the wall. “I cannot see that step and I am not going to pretend that I can and I am not going to ask anybody to describe it.”

It was not described.

“Here is what I have come up for and I am going to say it in one shape because I have had a fortnight of learning what happens when a person says a thing twice in two shapes.”

He turned his face to the wall.

“A thing put down by a man who said nothing, and named by a woman who did not put it down, is not a thing with a giver in it. It is a thing with a giver and a namer in it, and the namer is in it as deep as the giver, and she did not know she was getting into it and she was not careful and she was not careless. She said it out loud. That is all she did.”

Nobody moved.

“And that is the answer to what a thing like that does to a landing. It does not get a giver in it and it does not get a receiver in it. It gets a third of a person in it, the one who gave it a name, and that third person is the only one of the three who did not choose to be there, and she cannot get out of it, because the way out of a name on a piece of wood is a hand on the piece of wood.”

He took his hand off the wall.

“I have got a list of things I cannot check and I am not going to put anybody on it, and I am not going to leave anybody off it, and I know exactly what I have just done and I cannot check one word of it.”

Nobody told him he was right.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Neither piece of wood on that strip and not the iron in the seam. And I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be asked to take a turn at it.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him to take a turn.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his own count before the light had properly come up and said that there was nothing on that stone and that he had stopped keeping a shape for these.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and forty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and forty-one is nine hundred and ninety-four less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and forty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He looked round the landing. “There is nothing on that stone and there is a number of times and that is all, and I am not going to stand here and tell you which of the pieces of wood is which either, because I would be guessing and you would all believe me.”

Nobody asked him to guess.

“I am not going to tell you what I said about this being a number of times and I am not going to tell you what it is for, and nobody is going to tell me I was right about it, and I am going to be up this lane again when the water is back on that bank.”

Nobody recorded it, and nobody told him that he had it right.

The stone was bare and ninety steps were out and walkable and a man swept every one of them and went round the fortieth one and went round the chair and did not sit on the chair and did not touch the iron in the seam.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and not one word was said on that landing about how any of it came to be there. A thing put down by a man and named by a woman was lying on a dry strip above bare stone with a name on it that was not true, and the thirty-first absence had been said out loud in one mouth and could not be said back.
