# Chapter 996: A Friday And Eight Inches And About Eighty Of Those Ninety Steps Under And A Man With A Broom Said Out Loud What The Figure Given Yesterday Was A Figure Of And What It Is Not A Figure Of And Said That Saying It A Second Time Does Not Make It Truer

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-sixth morning. Five hundred and ninety-six days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and one weeks and six days old. Five hundred and fifty days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-two days in this county of Kell.

Eight inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about eighty of those ninety steps under, and six days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said a great deal else, and none of it was about the water.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that a figure said out loud on that landing cannot be called back by everybody else saying it again, and that he was going to say what it was a figure of once and then leave it alone.

“Eight at the seventh hour, six days of the coming back, and about eighty of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Friday. Three figures.”

He set the broom down flat and stood on the head of it.

“A woman said a figure out loud on this landing yesterday morning and she gave the working with it, and I said on the fifth day of the hundred and sixty-third week that I would not say it and would not ask anybody else to say it, and I have kept to that and I am not going to stand up here this morning and be pleased with myself about it.”

Nobody said whether he should be pleased with himself.

“I still think what I thought on Saturday. I said that the moment a figure of that kind is out loud then the landing is a number and everything on it is a line under that number. That got said. It got said by somebody else and not by me and it got said with the working printed beside it, and I have been standing here since the seventh hour trying to find a hole in it and I have not found one.”

Nobody found a hole in it either.

“So here is what that figure is a figure of, and I am going to say it in one shape and then I am finished with it for the rest of the week. It is a figure of what is lying on this landing and has not been taken up. That is the whole of it. That is what it is a figure of.”

He held up a hand before anybody could ask him anything.

“It says nothing whatever about who put any of that there. It says nothing whatever about how long any of it has been lying where it is lying. It counts nothing at all about how many mornings any of us has stood on this stone. And it is not a figure of whether anybody on this landing is owed anything, and it is owed to nobody, and it was owed to nobody before she opened her mouth and it is owed to nobody now.”

Nobody claimed it and nobody was told they had been owed it.

“And here is the part I have wanted to say since the sixth hour and I am going to say it plainly. A figure like that does not go on the landing when it is said out loud. It goes on whoever said it. She said it and it went on her and she carried it back down that road on her own two feet and nobody can take it off her and nobody can add to it, and I am not going to be up here on Monday pretending it is ours.”

He lifted the broom off the stone.

“And saying it a second time does not make it truer. I have not got a fifth figure and I am not going to get one by agreeing with a woman who saws lengths. More than one mouth has had hold of that figure now and the second mouth is the one that turns a fact into a landing-wide fact, and I would rather be wrong on my own stone than be the second mouth.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that he had worked out that a figure on a landing does nothing for a man who cannot read and never has.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-fifth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-fifth. Nine hundred and ninety-six less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and there is no number on that stone this morning either.”

“No, and I will tell you the thing about numbers on this landing and I am not asking for anything by saying it.” He set the barrow down. “A number about things is worth having. A number about days is worth having. A number about me is worth nothing to me whatever it comes out at, and that is not a complaint about the woman who said hers, because she said it about wood.”

Nobody told Barnaby Crove that his number was worth nothing and nobody told him it was worth something, and nobody thanked him for working it out.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to go on saying it on every morning I come up here, and it is not going to be a different question on the mornings a figure comes out.”

Nobody answered him, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for the asking.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stayed on that landing until the light came off the wall, and did not go down the bank at the end of it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-two days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and eleven days old.

“Four hundred and forty-two days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and eleven days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-sixth is two hundred and ninety-six days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-six less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and nobody has offered me a way out of it, and I did not ask for one this morning and I am not going to ask for one this evening.”

He got as far as the top step.

“One hundred and thirty-six wrong things in one hundred and thirty-six mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-sixth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything to him on either count.

“I got to the top of these steps this morning with three things in my mouth and I have got to the top of them with the same three, and I am going to say all three so that a person standing here can check that I have not added anything.” He did not look along the dry strip. “My want. It is to be somebody and it has not changed and I am not going to say it in a new shape because I am bored of it.”

Nobody said the want was a good one and nobody said it was a bad one.

“My cost. Asking is the only thing that costs me nothing, and I have not asked for anything for four hundred and forty-two days, and on Thursday morning a woman gave a figure out loud and it went on her and not on me, and I have not been relieved about that and I have not been unrelieved either.”

Not one word came back to him on either of those.

“And the third thing is that a figure of things went up that bank yesterday morning with the working, and I was at the bottom of the steps the whole time it was said, and nobody has asked me since whether one of those things is mine, and I am not going to volunteer it, and I am not going to be unrelieved about that either.”

Nobody asked him whether one of those things was his.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps with his hand out in front of him and found the wall with it and said the one thing he had come up for and would not be thanked for.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he was not going to ask what the figure had been, and that the not asking was the only thing he had got right that week.

“I was not up this bank yesterday morning. I heard about it after, the way everything on this landing happens, second-hand and late and from somebody who was in a mood about it.” He put his hand flat on the stone. “And I am not going to ask what the figure was.”

Nobody told him what the figure was.

“I want it said that I would have liked to have heard it said. I have been coming up this bank a long time and I have never once heard a figure come out of anybody’s mouth on this landing, and yesterday there was one and I was not here for it.”

Nobody said he was owed it.

“And I am not going to ask, because a figure I did not hear is not a figure of mine and I have got enough of those already, and because the woman who said it is entitled to say it once and to be left alone about it afterwards, and she is not going to have anybody come up this bank next week asking whether it was true. And I have said out loud this week that I would not ask about it, and I did not ask about it on the morning she said it, and that is not a thing I did.”

Nobody asked whether it was true.

“So the not asking is the whole of what I have done today and I would like somebody to notice it, and then I would like nobody to tell me I got it right.”

Nobody told him he got it right.

“And the other thing I came up for is the only thing I know how to say. It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Seven things, said by me, and nobody on that landing has to thank me for saying them and nobody has to ask me to say them again this week.”

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his own count before the light had properly come up and said that a figure of things was a different kind of figure from a figure of times and that he was not going to be told which kind his was.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and forty-three days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and forty-three is nine hundred and ninety-six less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and forty-three days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He looked round the landing. “And somebody up here said a number yesterday and I did not hear it and I am not going to ask, because a number about things and a number of times are not the same animal and I have watched people mix them all week.”

Nobody told him which kind his was.

“That is all. I am not going to tell you what my number is for and nobody is going to tell me. And I am going to be up this lane on the morning when that bank is at the top again, and I am not going to ask anybody what I will find when I get here.”

Nobody asked him and he was not told he had it right.

Eight inches on the top step and about eighty of those ninety steps under and six days of the coming back.

Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used. A figure of things was said out loud on that landing once and the working was given with it and it was not given again on that landing this morning, and the man with the broom said out loud that saying it a second time does not make it truer, and nobody told him he was right and nobody told him he was wrong. The account was as incomplete as it had been on Thursday and it was still in use.
