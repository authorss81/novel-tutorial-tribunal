# Chapter 989: Twelve Inches And About Seventy Of Those Ninety Steps Under And The Man With The Broom Took Back A Rule He Had Given Up Two Weeks Ago Out Loud And Gave A Reason For It And Was Not Thanked, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Came Up And Talked About Going Down A Road Without Naming A Day, And Nobody Named A Day

Fourth day of the hundred and sixty-third week. His seven hundred and thirty-ninth morning. Five hundred and eighty-nine days after the settlement. The fever one hundred weeks and six days old. Five hundred and forty-three days since the division. Four hundred and eighty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and thirty-five days in this county of Kell.

Twelve inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about seventy of those ninety steps under, and five days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and then, at about the eighth hour, said a thing out loud on that landing that he had said two weeks ago he was not going to say again.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-third week that he was taking the seven things back, and gave his reason out loud, and said he was not going to be thanked for it.

“Twelve at the seventh hour, five days of the coming back, and about seventy of those ninety steps under, on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-third week, and it is a Friday. Three figures.”

He set the broom against the wall and left it there and stood in front of it.

“Two weeks ago this morning I said seven things out loud on this landing and then I said I was not going to say them again on this landing, and I have not said them since, and I meant it when I said it, and I am going to break it this morning and I would like everybody to hear me break it and I would like nobody to think it is a big thing.”

Nobody thought it was a big thing and nobody asked him why.

“The reason is a man who moves furniture stood up here yesterday and said that the rule was worth nothing and the care was worth all of it, and he was right, and being right by a man who has never once been thanked for anything is the hardest kind of right there is.” He put his thumb on the wall. “And he is right in this shape.”

“A man who cannot see well put his hand along that wall yesterday morning and it was not the rule that stopped it.”

“It was that I have been knowing where his hand was for nineteen years.”

“That is not a rule.”

“A rule is a thing a man says so that he does not have to do the work, and I gave it away two weeks ago because saying it out loud was easier than carrying it, and yesterday I found out what carrying it costs.”

Nobody said anything about what it cost.

“So here they are, in my mouth, again, in the order they go, and I am not going to change the words because I am not going to dress it up. It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Seven things, and not one of them a figure of anybody, and not a fifth figure off my stone, and I have said them twice on this landing in nineteen years and I have given them away once.”

Nobody thanked him for taking them back.

“And I am not going to be thanked for saying that giving them away was not honour. It was not honour and it was not care. It was that I wanted one thing on this landing that nobody else had to hold, and I have wanted that my whole life, and I have got it out of a rule, and I would like somebody to notice that I have just said it and not to notice it very hard.”

Nobody noticed it very hard.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that he had been right about the water on Monday and that he was not going to be told he had been right about it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and sixty-eighth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and sixty-eighth. Nine hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and I have heard what you said about the water.”

“I said the water does not know I am here on Monday and I put it down on Monday and it stays down.” He set the barrow down where he had set it on Monday, which was on purpose and which nobody mentioned. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.”

“And I am aware that a man who can read has just told this landing that he gave something away so as not to have to hold it, and I am aware that I have done that twice, and I am not going to stand up here and make them into the same thing, because they are not, and mine was about being interesting.”

Nobody said he had it right and nobody said he had it wrong.

Nobody answered Barnaby Crove on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-third week, nobody told him he was owed an answer, nobody thanked him, and he was going to be up on the fifth day of that week and the sixth and the seventh with the same question in the same words.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour, gave his figures, gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and was right about two things before the ninth hour was out, and nobody on that landing told him he was right about either.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and thirty-five days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and four days old.

“Four hundred and thirty-five days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and four days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-thirty-ninth is two hundred and eighty-nine days old, which is nine hundred and eighty-nine less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and I have not asked it on any morning of this week and I am not going to start on Saturday.”

He got to the dry strip and stopped with his boots where they were on Thursday.

“I am going to say two things that are true and then I am going to stand here and nobody is going to check either of them, and I am not going to ask anybody to.”

Nobody checked either of them.

“The first is that Barnaby Crove is going to come up this bank tomorrow morning and the morning after that and the morning after that, and he is going to ask that question in those words, and nobody on this landing is going to answer him, and that includes me, and it is going to keep not being answered until one of us can put up our hands and say why not.” He did not move his feet.

“And I have never once been able to see the shape of that and I have got it now, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and I am not going to be told I have got it right.”

Nobody told him he had got it right and nobody told him he had not.

“The second is about my own week. On Wednesday morning I said out loud that I would not be the one to say what that piece of ash turns into. Not on that landing, and not later, not when it comes out of the weather. And I have not said it, and there is a woman on this landing who did say it yesterday in her own mouth before any of us could, and I have said nothing since. That is the only thing I have got right this week and I have not got anything out of it and I am not going to get anything out of it.”

Nobody got anything out of it.

“One hundred and twenty-nine wrong things in one hundred and twenty-nine mornings, and this is the hundred and twenty-ninth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is and nobody is going to tell me I found anything.”

Nobody said one word about the kind of figure it was, and nobody told him he had found anything.

Then the man of thirty-eight came up the road and said the thing about the road, and nobody named a day, and nobody thanked him for coming up.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, came back up a road he said he would not come up and is still not naming a day, and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-third week that a man who says he will go down a road and does not go down it has not stayed, and that he was not going to name a day for anybody else either.

“I have got a boy of nine at home and I have come up this bank on a morning when I did not have to and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked why I came.” He put his hand behind his back, which is where it lives. “There is one thing I know and I am going to say it and then I am going to stand here.”

“A man who says he will go down a road and does not go down it has not stayed.”

“He has not stayed and he has not gone.”

“There is no third of it and anybody who says there is a third of it has not been up a hill with a bag.”

Nobody on that landing named a day.

“And I am not going to name a day for the man of thirty-one either, and I am not going to tell him not to go and I am not going to tell him to stay, and I would like both of those noticed in the same breath because I know what it looks like from outside and it looks like a threat and it is not one. It is the only thing I have got that is mine and I am giving it away for nothing, which is a habit I have and which I am not going to give up today.”

Nobody thanked him for giving it away.

“And nobody up here has asked me for the road I came up and nobody is going to. If somebody asks me how far it is I will not answer, and not because it is a secret. Because a figure of how far a man walked is a figure of the man and this landing has had a week of figures about men and not one of them was any use.”

Nobody came near that question.

He came away from the road and went and stood with his back to the bank and looked at the water on the steps, and nobody on that landing said anything else to him, and nobody went up that road after him.

Then the man of thirty-one said the last of it and it was about the one thing he has left, and he did not say a figure, and nobody relieved him.

“There is one thing left in this county that nobody can take off me. I can walk down that bank at any hour and leave. Not today and not tomorrow morning and not at the end of this week. I am not going to do it, and I am not going to tell anybody how near I came to doing it, and nobody is to thank me for not doing it, because a thing you do not do cannot be thanked for.” He took his hands out from behind him.

“A man of thirty-eight has just told me that is not staying and he is right and I am not going to argue with him and nobody is going to tell me he is right either.”

Nobody argued with him and nobody told him the man of thirty-eight was right.

Twelve inches and about seventy of those ninety steps under and five days of the coming back. The man with the broom had said the seven things in his own mouth again and had not been thanked for taking them back. A man of thirty-one had been right about two things and had been told nothing at all about either of them. A man of thirty-eight had talked about a road and had not named a day, and nobody had named one for him. Nothing on the dry strip had been handled by anybody, and the piece of ash had a giver in it, and nobody on that landing had thanked anybody for anything.
