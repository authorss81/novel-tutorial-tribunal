# Chapter 974: A Full Bank On A Thursday And The Man With The Broom Said The Fourth Figure Out Loud As A Figure Of The Water And Not A Figure Of The Morning And Nobody Carried It Off That Stone As A Kind Of Day

Third day of the hundred and sixty-first week. His seven hundred and twenty-fourth morning. Five hundred and seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever ninety-eight weeks and five days old. Five hundred and twenty-eight days since the division. Four hundred and seventy-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and twenty days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and that was a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said the fourth one out loud a second time this fortnight, and this time he said it because a man had already broken it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-first week, on a full bank, that four days is a figure of the water and not a figure of the morning.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour, that is a full bank, four days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the third day of the hundred and sixty-first week, and it is a Thursday. Three figures.”

He laid the broom down on the stone instead of leaning it.

“I am going to say a fourth thing and I am going to say it having said it before this week, and I want that on the record before I say it, because the second time a man says a thing on a landing it gets smaller, and I am not going to have this one get smaller.”

Nobody said they had heard it smaller.

“Four days is a figure of the water.” He said it slowly, one clause at a time, the way he says the things he means to last. “It tells you nothing about this landing. It tells you nothing about this morning. And it has nothing to do with Thursday, whatever Thursday turns out to be like.”

He looked along the top step.

“Yesterday a man told me what tomorrow was going to be like and he was right about the water. That is what he was right about. He was right about sixteen inches standing on the top step at the seventh hour, and everything else he said was him deciding in advance, and the two of those got put in the same sentence and it came out of his mouth as a Thursday.”

Nobody on that landing said a word about it.

“I have given three figures off this stone every morning for nineteen years and I have never given one of them a name, and the reason is that they are about the bank and not about us. A bank takes no notice whatever of a landing and I have never once pretended otherwise.” He picked the broom up. “So here is the whole of it and I have said it twice this fortnight now and I am not going to say it a third time on this bank. Take the four off this stone and it stops being a figure of the bank and it starts being a day, and a day is a thing people arrange themselves around.”

Nobody on that landing carried the four off that stone, and nobody said anything on a full bank that was not said on every other morning.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked on a full bank in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and he noticed that nobody had asked him to come back tomorrow either.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and fifty-third mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and fifty-third. Nine hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and I have got sixteen inches standing on that step and I am not going to say the water has made me generous.”

“No, and I have been thinking about what I said to you on Tuesday about the two of us keeping a figure to ourselves, and I have worked out that I do not know which two I meant, and I am not going to ask, and that is the first thing I have worked out in a fortnight.”

Nobody said he had it right about the two mornings and nobody said he had it wrong.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.” He said it in the words he uses. “And I have noticed that today is the third day in this week that I have asked and it is the first day in this week that somebody stood on that step and said a whole sentence out loud without anybody needing a figure of the water to stand it on, and I would like somebody to notice that.”

Somebody noticed it and nobody acted on it.

Nobody gave that question an answer and nobody gave him an answer either, on a full bank, in the words he uses, and nobody thanked him and nobody was going to be thanked for refusing him either.

Then the man who moves chairs came up the cart road with nothing on his shoulder for the fourth morning running and stood at the flat of the steps and said the thing he had been carrying since Monday, which was that a person cannot find out what is in a step without picking it up, and that picking it up is the one thing that has been forbidden on that landing for a fortnight.

Aldis Rook moves the chairs for Orla Fennimore and keeps nothing and has no stone and has no seat and no board, has never once been asked what he did, has been told not to come up that bank and has come anyway, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-first week that the only experiment anybody has on that step is one nobody is allowed to run.

“It is still there,” he said. “That is what I have come up to say and it is the whole of what I have come up to say. It is still there on a full bank, in the same seam, in the same chip, and it has not been touched, and I have not touched it, and I am not going to.”

Nobody looked at it.

“Now here is the rest and I would rather somebody sat down for it.” Nobody sat down. “There is exactly one experiment anybody in this county has for that step and it is a bad one and it is the only one. You pull it. Or you turn it over. Or you get a thumb under it and lift, and you find out what it is holding, and you put it back, and then you know.”

He put both hands behind his back.

“And that is the only road in this world from not knowing to knowing about a thing left in a place. There is no other road. Not asking, because there is nobody here to ask. Not waiting, because nobody is going to put themselves on that step by standing still. Not a wise man, because there is no wise man on this landing and there is not going to be one, and a wise man is the same shape as a person who is allowed to do it.”

Nobody said they had known there was no wise man on that landing.

“And a man who works here has told this landing out loud on Monday that his work has got easier and cannot say who did it, and the whole of what he wants now, and I know he does not want it, and I want it too, and it is one pull.”

He stopped there and left it.

“So the rule is not a kindness and it is not me being careful and it is not nineteen years of a man sweeping ninety steps a day being noble about a piece of iron.” He came down one step, which is as far as he has ever come. “It is that there is one experiment and we are not allowed to run it, and every one of us is going to have to carry that, and I would like it said out loud on a full bank, on a Thursday, in front of everybody, so that nobody on this landing can go a fortnight without knowing it.”

Nobody on that landing said it out loud after him.

Then a man of thirty-eight came up the cart road at about the ninth hour with nothing, having left the board where he had left it, and said the seven of them in one breath and would not say them again that week, and nobody thanked him.

Garrin Tolley is thirty-eight, has a child of nine, came back up a road he said he would not come up and is still not naming a day, and on the third day of the hundred and sixty-first week said the whole of the rule out loud for the first time in his life and had worked out two days beforehand what it was going to cost him to say it.

“It has not been touched,” he said. “Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Seven things, and I have not worked out why it is seven, and I have not asked anybody, and I am not going to.”

He did not say anything else and he went back down the road and he did not come back up it that day.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his count before anybody could ask him for it, and then said out loud that he had a figure of his own about that step and was not going to give it to anybody.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and twenty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and twenty-one is nine hundred and seventy-four less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and twenty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.” He looked round the landing. “And I have got another figure, and it is not mine, and I am not giving it to anybody, and I am saying that I have got it so that nobody on this landing can say afterwards that there is one figure in this county that has not been offered.”

The landing went quiet.

“It is a figure of how long that step has been sitting still.” He said it flat and he did not go into it. “And I have worked out that it is not a figure of a thing and it is not a figure of a man, and that anybody who works it out properly has not found out a fact about that step. He has found out that he is the kind of boy who counts. So I counted it and I am not giving it and I would like nobody to ask me.”

Nobody asked him.

“And I am not going to be told I was right to keep it either,” he said, “because there is nothing on this landing to be right about this morning, and I am thirteen, and I have got one figure of mine and one I am sitting on, and that is the whole of what I have got, and I would like it left there.”

Nobody told him anything about either figure.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and stood on a morning that had four days on it and used none of them.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and twenty days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-nine days old.

“Four hundred and twenty days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-nine days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-twenty-fourth is two hundred and seventy-four days old, which is nine hundred and seventy-four less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and I have not asked it on any morning there has been a full number on this step to stand against.”

He got to the bottom of the steps and stood on it, which nobody else does.

“A hundred and fourteen wrong things in a hundred and fourteen mornings, and here is the hundred and fourteenth, and it is about four days.” He did not move his hands. “A man of forty-three has spent this morning telling a landing that a figure of the water is not a figure of the morning. I have been doing the opposite since Monday. I have been standing on this bank finding out what a figure of the water is going to let me say, and it has let me say four days, and I am not going to say what for.”

Nobody asked him what the four days were for.

“And I am not going to be thanked for saying so, and I am not going to be unrelieved, and I have spent a whole morning choosing not to use a morning that was handed to me, which is the only kind of skill I have got, and there is nobody on this step to hand me another one.” He looked up the top step. “And the offer is at two hundred and seventy-four days and it is unanswered and I have not asked it and I am not going to ask it on a morning like this one.”

Nobody asked him about the offer and nobody offered him anything.

He swept his ninety and went round the fortieth one and round the chair, and left both alone, and nothing on that landing was pulled, or turned over, or weighed in a hand, or read aloud, or carried inside, or put back where it was, or used. The bank stood full over the top step, and four days was a figure of the water and nothing else whatever, and not one person on that landing carried it off that stone as a kind of day to have a conversation on.
