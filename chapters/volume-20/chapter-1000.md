# Chapter 1000: A Tuesday And Sixteen Inches That Hold And Two Days Of The Coming Back And A Woman Of Forty-Four Stood At A Wall And Said Out Loud, Before Anybody Thanked Her, That She Is Not In Any Of It And Nobody Thanked Her, And A Man Of Thirty-One Gave An Offer At Three Hundred Days Out Loud With The Subtraction And Nobody Answered It

First day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week. His seven hundred and fiftieth morning. Six hundred days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and two weeks and three days old. Five hundred and fifty-four days since the division. Five hundred days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-six days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then said that there was a word going round about this morning and that he was not going to act on a word.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he had heard a word going round in a kitchen and had not been in a kitchen in nineteen years, and that a landing does not change because a word has done a journey.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and it is a Tuesday. Three figures.”

He set the broom against the wall, clear of the strip.

“There is a word going round about today. I heard it said about three days ago and it has travelled since and it is now on this landing, and I am telling you I have heard it and I am telling you I am not going to do anything with it.” He looked along the top step. “A word gets to a place on a Tuesday and everybody starts behaving as though the stone had been moved. This stone has not been moved. I swept it before the light came up. Sixteen inches. That is the morning.”

Nothing on that landing was moved by a word.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that he had asked the same question on the last morning of one week and the first morning of the next and that he had noticed both of those mornings being called something by other people.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-ninth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is one thousand less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-ninth. One thousand less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and I would say the same thing in the same way today as I said it in the same way the day before.”

“No.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I have asked that on two mornings now that other people have given names to, and it has made no difference to the question and I am not going to let a name make a difference to it. I do not hold with names.”

Nobody answered Barnaby Crove on that Tuesday, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for the asking, and nobody disagreed with him about names.

Then a woman stood at the wall at the bottom of the cart road before the seventh hour and said out loud, before any of the morning had finished itself, that she was not in any of it, and nobody thanked her for saying so.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, keeps twenty-nine chairs at a house that is not in this county, was asked once what she did and has not been asked since, and was at the wall before the seventh hour and said what she was not part of out loud before any of it was done.

“Nobody is to come up this road and tell me anything.” She said it without coming past the wall. “Not about the water. Not about a name on a piece of wood. Not about a shape a woman read out loud to that landing yesterday morning, and not about the piece of ash on that strip with a man’s name in it, and not about the other piece on that strip with nobody’s name in it at all.”

Nobody on that landing had said a word to her.

“The second one has got no name in it. Not one of you has said a name in it, and I have heard all of you talk for a week and not one of you has got a person in it, and I am going to say that out loud because somebody was going to and somebody was not going to and I would rather it were on the record from a wall than worked out afterwards.”

Nobody corrected her and nobody told her she had it right.

“He said it out loud, the first one. He put it down in front of everybody and said the words. That one has a man in it. The one that came up on Sunday has nobody in it at all and that is not the same as being mine or his or anybody’s, and I am not going to have it made into mine by standing at a wall.”

She was not thanked.

“I have twenty-nine chairs in a house that is not in this county and I have been asked about them once in my life and never since, and that is the life I have, and I am not going to have it made into a thing this week because a man who carries furniture into rooms has said out loud what he has been doing for nineteen years.”

Nobody thanked her.

“And here is the part I am saying before any of you finishes that morning and walks up here and decides I stood at a wall on purpose. I have one question. I have had it a long while and it is not about that landing.”

Nobody at the top of those steps made any sound at all.

“It is this. What is a person to do with a place they are not in.” She did not come past the wall. “Not a wall they are not on the other side of. A place they have stood next to every day of their working life and have never once been in and have never once been asked whether they would like to be in.”

Nobody said one word.

“That is not the question a shape answers. It is not the question a name on a piece of wood answers, and it was not meant to, and I am not going to stand here and tell you it was nearly answered, because it was not. It is my question and I have brought it down this road and nobody answered it and nobody is going to.”

Nobody told her she was owed an answer.

“Nobody thanks me. Not for being at the wall, not for staying out of it, and not for saying either of those things out loud this morning.”

She was not thanked then and she was not thanked afterwards.

“None of you is to come up this road and find out what I said to that wall. None of you is to tell me they wished I had been on the landing. I have not come down this road to be wished for. I am going back down it now at the pace I came up.”

She went back down that cart road at the same pace she had come up it, and not one of them went up after her, and nothing whatever was said on that road behind her back.

Then the man who cannot see well came up onto the flat of the steps at about the eighth hour with his hand out in front of him and found the wall with it and said the whole of what he had left, and it was about the end of that strip and not about anything else.

Simon Rook is about fifty-two, cannot see well, has a list of things he cannot check and has said out loud more than once that the list is a list and not a complaint, and said on the first day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week that he was going to keep the thing that was at the end of that strip on his list and that he was not going to cross it off.

“My hand was at the end of that strip yesterday morning and I did not take anything. Today I shall do exactly the same, whatever a woman read out loud on this landing yesterday.” He put his hand flat on the stone. “That shape is about somebody else’s morning. It does not fit me and I am not going to bend it until it does, because a shape that gets bent is a shape that somebody wanted.”

Nobody said a word about whether that was right and nobody said a word the other way.

“And I have got a thing on that strip in my head where it has been since I found it. No name goes on it and none comes off it. If there is a thing there that somebody put down for somebody then somebody put it down for somebody and I am not going to be the one who works out who. And if there is not, then there is not, and that is a thing I can live with for as long as the wood lies there.”

It was not worked out, and he was not told he had it right.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. I am the last one likely to be thanked for saying that and I am the one most likely to be asked to say it again, and I will say it again while I can still make out the wall by daylight.”

Nobody thanked him for it and nobody told him to say them again this week.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour with sixteen inches above his boots and gave his figures and gave the figure of the standing offer with the subtraction out loud, and it was at three hundred days, and it was unanswered, and nobody was relieved.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-six days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and fifteen days old.

“Four hundred and forty-six days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and fifteen days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-fiftieth is three hundred days old, which is one thousand less seven hundred.” He said it without stopping on it. “It is unanswered.”

He got to the top of the steps and stood there.

“I have not asked it this morning. I have not asked it on any morning of this week. Nobody has offered me a way out of it and I did not ask for one, nor will I, and I am going to be neither relieved nor unrelieved about that, and I want both halves of that said out loud on this landing.”

Nobody said he was relieved and nobody said he was unrelieved.

“One hundred and forty wrong things in one hundred and forty mornings, and this is the hundred and fortieth, and it is a whole figure and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody put a word to him about what kind of figure that was and nobody told him he had found anything.

“My want. I want to be somebody, and I said it out loud on this landing in this county and I am not going to unsay it because the morning has gone round. My cost is that asking is the only thing that costs me nothing and I have not asked for anything for four hundred and forty-six days, and this week I spent that instead of asking and it cost me the same, which is nothing, and that is the whole of my trouble.”

Nobody said anything about the whole of his trouble.

“And the third thing, and it is the one I am going to be careful with. A woman gave a figure of things out loud on this landing on Thursday morning with the working beside it. Nobody is to be given that figure again. I am not going to count anything out loud this morning and I am not going to ask anybody else to, and that is not me being careful with her. It is that a figure of that kind says it once and the second time somebody says it, it stops being a figure of the things and it starts being a figure of the person who said it first.”

Nobody counted anything and nobody asked anybody to.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his own count before the light had properly come up and said he was not going to say what it was for and that nobody was going to say it for him either.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and forty-seven days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and forty-seven is one thousand less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and forty-seven days, and it is one more than the morning before.” He looked round the landing. “And somebody on this landing said a number about things on Thursday and somebody else said what it was a figure of, and I did not hear either of those and I am not going to ask. I have got a number of my own and it is a number of times and I am not going to turn it into a number of things on a Tuesday morning because somebody else has.”

Nobody told him which kind his was and nobody told him he was right.

“I am going to be up this lane again. I am not going to say when and I am not going to say what I will find, and if I am wrong about what I find then that is what will happen and it will not be anybody’s fault, and I would like it noticed that I said that and that nobody has to do anything about it.”

Nothing was noticed and nothing was done about it.

Then the man of thirty-one gave the whole of what he had come up that bank with, in one place, out loud, and asked for nothing.

“A woman of forty-four stood at a wall this morning and said she was not in any of it and that nobody was to thank her. Nobody thanked her.”

“A man who cannot read has asked the same thing at the sixth hour for two hundred and seventy-ninth mornings and nobody has answered it and nobody has told him he was owed an answer and nobody has thanked him. A man who cannot see well has had his hand at the end of that strip and has taken nothing and is not going to. A shape was said out loud on this landing yesterday and it was not given a name. An offer stood at three hundred days this morning and was not answered.”

He had his hands at his sides and he was not waving them.

“A piece of ash with my name in it. A piece of ash with a wrong name on it that I put there by not speaking. And a figure of my own that nobody has told me the size of. That is everything I have brought up that bank. I have not asked for one thing out of any of it and nobody has given me one thing out of any of it.”

Nobody asked him for one thing and nobody put one thing into his hands.

Corvin Tace swept all ninety steps, though about sixty of them were under, and went round the fortieth one, and went round the chair, and did not sit on the chair. He did not touch the iron in the seam of the fortieth step and he did not touch either piece of ash on the dry strip and he did not say who had put the second one there. Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used, and not one word was said on that landing about how any of it came to be there.

Sixteen inches over that top step and it holds, and two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, which are three figures about the water and not about the morning. The thing on that strip with the wrong name on it was still lying there and still unclaimed, and the offer was unanswered, and the account on that landing was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour, and it was still in use, and the water came off that bank four days later and the name was standing on it.
