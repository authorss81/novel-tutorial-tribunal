# Chapter 984: A Boy Asked A Man Of Thirty-One One Question About The Thing He Put Down And It Was Not Why And It Was Not What For, And The Man With A Broom Refused It And Gave The Working, And The Man Answered It Out Loud To The Landing And Not To The Boy, And Nobody Helped Him With Any Of It

Sixth day of the hundred and sixty-second week. His seven hundred and thirty-fourth morning. Five hundred and eighty-four days after the settlement. The fever one hundred weeks and one day old. Five hundred and thirty-eight days since the division. Four hundred and eighty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and thirty days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and two days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-second week that there was a question on that landing that nobody was entitled to ask and that he was going to say so before it was asked rather than after.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-second week that he was not going to hand out mornings and that a question was a morning somebody else wanted to have.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, two days of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the sixth day of the hundred and sixty-second week, and it is a Sunday. Three figures.”

He leaned the broom up against the wall and stood in front of it rather than beside it.

“There is going to be a question asked on this landing today about the wood up on that flat step, and I am saying now, at the seventh hour, with nobody else on these steps, that I have thought about who has the right to ask it and that the answer is nobody, and that when it comes I am going to refuse it. Not because of who asks it. Because of what a question asked on a landing turns into.”

Nobody asked him what it turned into.

“And that is not mine to decide and I am not deciding it. It turns into whatever the person who asks it decides it turns into, and I have watched a man on this landing spend a fortnight putting his own shape on things and then be surprised when other people found the shape and used it. So a question is a morning, and I do not hand out mornings, and if a question gets asked about that wood it is going to get asked with me standing at the top of my own steps being part of it.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said that a Sunday was a day people made things worse by saying them out loud.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and sixty-third mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and sixty-third. Nine hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and a Sunday is not a different question.”

“No, and it is a day when a man who has been quiet all week opens his mouth, and I have watched two hundred and sixty-three mornings of that.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am going to say that I do not want the wood up there to get into it.”

“If somebody asks him about that wood today, I want it written on that landing that I did not put the wood in front of it.”

Nobody wrote it down, and nobody told him he was owed the writing.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stood at the bottom of the steps with a boy in front of him, and a boy asked him one question.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and thirty days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and ninety-nine days old.

“Four hundred and thirty days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and ninety-nine days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-thirty-fourth is two hundred and eighty-four days old, which is nine hundred and eighty-four less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

He had not asked it and the boy came up out of the lane and stood at the bottom of the steps with his hands behind him.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and thirty-one days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and thirty-one is nine hundred and eighty-four less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and thirty-one days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.” He looked up the steps at the dry strip along the foot of the wall and not at the man. “I am not going to ask you why you put it there and I am not going to ask you what it is for.”

“I have got two questions and they are the wrong two and I have thought about it all morning and I am going to ask the third one instead, and I would like it recorded that I gave up the two I wanted.”

Nobody recorded it.

“What did you do at the bottom of the lane before the light came up.”

Nobody on that landing breathed.

And the man with the broom said no, from the top of the steps, and gave the working.

“No.” He did not come down. “I am going to refuse that and I am going to do it in the seventh hour’s language, which is the only language I have got. That is not a moral and it is not me protecting you and it is not me protecting anybody. Here is the working, and you can check every part of it.”

He put his thumb on the broom handle.

“A question about what a man did at the bottom of a lane before the light came up is not a question about this landing. It is a question about the man. And a question about a man is a thing you put to a man, and not a thing you put to a landing, and this morning one of you has just put it to a landing, at the foot of these steps, where I stand at the top of them.”

“The moment it is asked on these stones it stops being his and it becomes a thing that happened here, and then I am the man it happened on. I have worked this stone nineteen years and I have never once been a thing that happened.”

Nobody there said anything at all.

“And the second half of the working, which is the half that actually refuses it. There is nobody on this landing entitled to ask him that. Not you, not me, not the woman who reads for a living, not the man who moves furniture. Entitlement is not a thing anybody on these steps is holding. The only person who can ask a man a question about himself is somebody who has already stood at the bottom of that lane, and not one of you has, and asking him is not the same as having been there. So the question is not refused because it is cruel. It is refused because there is no one here to ask it.”

Nobody asked it.

The boy stood at the foot of the steps and did not say anything else for a while.

Then the man of thirty-one answered it out loud, to the landing, and said first who he was answering.

“I am going to answer that and I am going to say in front of everybody that I am not answering it to the boy who asked it, and that is not a slight on him and it is not me dodging. He asked a true thing in the right words. He is owed nothing by me and I am not going to pretend that a boy of thirteen gets an answer off me because he got the shape of the question right.”

He did not go up the steps.

“At the fourth hour I could not sleep. I went down the lane as far as the fallen ash and I could not sleep there either, and there was a branch off it that had come down in some wind and had been lying in the wet long enough to go soft on the underside. And I did not have a saw that was any good and I did not have anybody to hold it for me. And I sat down on the wet ground at the fourth hour with a wedge and a block of wood and I split it down the middle.”

“That is the only thing I have ever been able to do with my hands that comes out right.”

Nobody helped him with any of it.

“And I cut one end square with a saw that went off at the corner. And I put my coat round it and I carried it up that bank and I stood at the bottom of these steps for a quarter of an hour working out whether to put it down. And I knew at the bottom of the steps that a thing put down where somebody works is not a given thing, because I had heard the man with the broom say it on Thursday morning before he had looked at what was under my arm.”

Nobody on that landing told him anything about that.

“And I put it down anyway. And I am not going to tell anybody I have been carrying it in my coat for the length of a morning like that is an excuse, because it is not an excuse, it is just what happened, and I said it out loud on Thursday because I wanted a giver in it and I have wanted a giver in it since the day I came into this county.”

He stopped there and stood still.

“One hundred and twenty-four wrong things in one hundred and twenty-four mornings, and this is the hundred and twenty-fourth, and it is a figure of mistakes and not a figure of a man and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure it is, and nobody is going to tell me I found anything.”

Nobody named the kind of figure it was, and nobody told him he had found anything.

Then he reached out towards the foot of the wall with his right hand, quite openly, and stopped short of where the ash was lying, and put his hand down at his side again.

“I want to say that I was going to pick that up just now to show the boy the end I cut, because it is the only part of it I am proud of and I have not looked at it since Thursday. And I am not going to. And the reason I am not going to is not the seven things and it is not the man with the broom. He has never once told me not to do anything.”

“It is that I put it there, and the only thing I am allowed to do to it now is nothing, and that is not a rule anybody has given me, that is just what putting something down turns out to be.”

Nobody had told him not to do anything, and nobody touched the ash.

Then the boy said the rule out loud, in his own words, in the shape he had been carrying it in since Saturday, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody told him he had it right.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used.” He said it looking at the wall and not at the step. “Seven things. And I am going to say the half of it that is mine.”

“Nobody on this landing has asked me whether I have looked at that wood and I am going to say out loud that I have looked at it and I have not asked him one thing about it, because he said out loud on Thursday that he put it there, and a thing with a man in it does not need asking about.”

Nobody told him that was right and nobody told him that it was wrong.

Sixteen inches and it holds, about sixty under, two days of the coming back. A question had been refused with the working given. The man who put the thing down had answered it out loud to a landing and not to a boy, and nobody had helped him with one word of it.
