# Chapter 969: Sixteen Inches And It Holds And One Day Of The Coming Back And About Sixty Of Those Ninety Steps Under, And A Man Who Cannot Read Gave A Whole Figure Of Weeks Out Loud For The Third Time And Was Asked What It Was For And Said He Did Not Know

Fifth day of the hundred and sixtieth week. His seven hundred and nineteenth morning. Five hundred and sixty-nine days after the settlement. The fever ninety-eight weeks old. Five hundred and twenty-three days since the division. Four hundred and sixty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and fifteen days in this county of Kell.

Sixteen inches on the top step at about the seventh hour and it holds, about sixty of those ninety steps under, and one day of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and then heard a whole figure come out of a mouth that cannot read and did not go in front of it.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the fifth morning of the hundred and sixtieth week that a whole figure in a morning is a figure with an offer attached to it.

“Sixteen at the seventh hour and it holds, one day of the coming back, and about sixty of those ninety steps under, on the fifth day of the hundred and sixtieth week, and it is a Saturday. Three figures.”

He stood the broom up against the stone of the wall instead of leaving it lying.

“I am not going to say anything about what is coming,” he said. “I have not said it for three mornings and I am not going to say it on the morning when it would be easiest, because one day of the coming back is a morning that everybody can do the arithmetic on and every one of them will do it wrongly.”

Nobody did the arithmetic out loud.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and said it before he had got the barrow against the bottom step, which he has not done in this whole business.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and forty-eighth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Ninety-eight weeks,” he said.

The landing went the other quiet, the one it goes when a man who cannot read has said a whole figure and is waiting to see whether anybody picks it up.

“Ninety-eight weeks. On this Saturday. And that is the third time I have said a whole one of those out loud on this step and the second time this week, and I said the first one myself before any of you worked it out, and I am saying this one myself too, and if I have got it wrong then I have got it wrong out loud and not in my head where a man can work at it.”

Nobody told him he had it right and nobody told him he had it wrong.

He put both hands flat on the wall and left them there.

“Now I know what you are all going to do and I am going to save you the morning and I am going to do it before it gets started. One of you is going to ask me what a whole number of weeks is for. Not because it is a good question. Because it has been sitting in somebody’s mouth since the man who cannot read said a whole figure on a Saturday and everybody on a landing wants to hand a man something to hold.”

Nobody had asked him.

“So I am going to answer it now, before it gets asked, and I am going to answer it out loud and I am not going to be thanked for answering it in advance, and I am not going to be asked it again afterwards, and if anybody asks it anyway then I am going to say the answer again exactly the same.” He took his hands off the wall. “I do not know what a whole number of weeks is for. I have not known since the first time and I have not worked it out in between and I have had a fortnight to work it out in and I have not done it.”

“That is not an answer,” said the man of thirty-one.

“It is the answer to the question you would have asked,” Barnaby Crove said. “That is a different question and you did not ask it and I do not have to have one.”

Nobody took him up on it.

“And here is the second half and it is the half I actually want said on this landing and I have wanted it since the first time and I have never had anywhere to put it.” He turned round and looked up the steps at the whole of that landing standing in front of him.

“You all keep asking what it is for as though the figure were a thing that had a use waiting for it. It has not. I have carried ninety-eight weeks all morning as a figure because it is a figure of weeks and it is a whole one, and I cannot read, and it is the only whole figure I have ever been handed by anybody in this county that nobody asked me anything about.”

“That is not a thing to be proud of.”

“I am not proud of it. I have said on this step before that nobody is thanked for asking on this landing and I am not going to start being thanked for carrying a figure.” He set the barrow against the bottom step.

“And I am not going to be told it means anything. Not by a man with a broom and not by a woman with a book and not by a boy and not by a man who is nobody’s. I have been on this landing nineteen years and I have never once had it explained to me and I am not going to start explaining it to nine people this morning because they have decided a whole number is a door.”

Nobody said what a whole number was a door to.

“Two hundred and forty-eighth. Nine hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.” He put both hands back on the barrow. “And I am going to ask my question now and you are going to refuse it the same way you have refused it all week, and if you refuse it differently I would like to know why, because a different refusal is a thing a man has to think about.”

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it.” He put the barrow handle down against the step while he said it, which is not how he usually says it.

Nobody answered him. Nobody said it was a good question. Nobody said it was a foolish one, and nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for the whole figure or for the asking.

Then the boy came up out of the lane and gave his own count before the light had properly got onto the steps, and nobody on that landing asked him a single thing about it.

Wat Marshe is thirteen, has been in this county three hundred and sixteen days, and counted the figure in the lane before the light came up. Three hundred and sixteen is nine hundred and sixty-nine less six hundred and fifty-three.

“Three hundred and sixteen days, and it is one more than the morning before, and I counted it in the lane before the light came up.” He looked at the man with the barrow. “And you have said a whole number out loud and I am not going to ask you what it was for either. I have watched the whole of you start to and then stop, and I am not going to be the one who starts it.”

“Nobody has asked you and nobody is going to.”

“Nobody has asked me anything all week and that is not going to change today and I want it noticed that I noticed it on the third week this morning and not on the first.” He put his hands in his pockets. “And nobody is to tell me I was right about that. It is not a thing anybody can be right about.”

Nobody told him he had it right.

Then the woman who reads the ninth run came up the road and entered the morning, and had the whole figure of weeks in the air by the seventh hour, and did not put it on anything.

Sena Dorr is forty-nine, has read for a living for twenty-three years, holds the ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone, and has entered a morning this week on which a whole number of weeks was said out loud and has not entered the number.

“I entered this morning and there is a whole figure standing in the middle of it and I have not written it down.” She had the book shut against her knee. “That is a thing I have done twice in twenty-three years and the first time was a morning when a man in another county died at a number that went in a book and came out of a book and by the middle of the afternoon there were six people in a county who had a thing they could do.”

“Nothing is going to be done with it.”

“I know that. You know that. The man who cannot read knows that, because he is the one who said it and he has been standing at the bottom of your steps refusing to let anybody hold it.” She did not open the book.

“And I am not writing it because the day a whole number of weeks is on a line in a book in this county it stops being a number a man said and becomes a number this county holds. And then in about nine days there is a man who knows it, and a clerk who knows he knows it, and that is the whole of how an account starts. Not with a lie. With a figure somebody was careful about.”

Nobody asked her to write it down and nobody asked her not to.

“And I am not going to say what a whole number of weeks is for either, and I want that noted, because I have read for twenty-three years and it is the first question of my trade and I have decided this week that I do not know what it is for and I am not going to find out on a landing.”

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and had the whole figure of weeks sitting in his own mouth as well and would not give it back in a sentence.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and fifteen days. The cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-four days old.

“Four hundred and fifteen days in this county, and the cut across that palm is two hundred and eighty-four days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-nineteenth is two hundred and sixty-nine days old, which is nine hundred and sixty-nine less seven hundred, and it is unanswered, and I have not asked it.”

He stood at the top of the steps and looked down at the man with the barrow for a while.

“One hundred and nine wrong things in one hundred and nine mornings, and here is the hundred and ninth, and it is about a whole number of weeks and I am not going to say one thing about it.” He kept his hands behind him. “I have had a sentence ready since the sixth hour. It has been sitting in my mouth behind four hundred and fifteen days of this county and a hand and an offer, and I am not going to say it, and nobody is going to notice that I am not saying it, and I would like both of those to be true at once.”

Nobody noticed.

“And here is the reason, and it is the reason I have been carrying all week. A whole figure that nobody can explain is the only kind of figure this landing has ever respected. The man who cannot read has got one. I have got one, and I gave it out on Thursday morning of that week in my own mouth, and nobody on this step has told me what it is for since, and that is the best thing that has happened to me in this county.” He turned round.

“So when ninety-eight weeks comes out of a mouth that cannot read this morning, the correct thing is for the whole of this landing to be quietly pleased that a second person has one, and for nobody to ask either of us what it is for, and that is what I am doing. That is what I am doing right now by standing here saying nothing.”

Nobody said he was doing it and nobody said he was not.

The man of fifty-four went down the bank with the barrow and asked again halfway down, to nobody, in the same words, and said that he would be up on the Sunday morning and that the Sunday was the morning the water would not be there at all if it went, and that he would rather have the Sunday. The man of thirty-one went down the bank on foot at about the ninth hour with his hands behind his back and did not put his weight on the fortieth step.

Corvin Tace swept his way up eighty-nine steps and went round the fortieth and went round the chair and set the broom against the wall where it has been every morning for nineteen years, and did not sit on it and did not touch it, and nothing that had been put down anywhere was pulled, turned over, weighed in the hand, read aloud, carried inside, put back where it was, or used.

A whole figure of weeks was on that landing in about nine ears and had not been written on anything, and one day of the coming back was a figure of the water and nobody on that landing had said a word about the morning it was going to be.
