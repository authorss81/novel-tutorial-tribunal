# Chapter 987: The Water Came Back On That Bank At Four Inches And About Ninety Of Those Ninety Steps Were Under, And There Was Nothing On That Landing To Sweep For Four Days, And The Thing He Had Put Down Went Under The Water Where Nobody Can Reach It, And He Spent The Whole Of His Mistake Out Loud In One Mouth And It Resolved Nothing

Second day of the hundred and sixty-third week. His seven hundred and thirty-seventh morning. Five hundred and eighty-seven days after the settlement. The fever one hundred weeks and four days old. Five hundred and forty-one days since the division. Four hundred and eighty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and thirty-three days in this county of Kell.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures, and then said that there was nothing on that landing for him to do until the water came off, and that he was going to say out loud what that was, because it is the fourth time in nineteen years.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-third week that about ninety out of ninety is the whole of what he has and that he was not going to be a man standing at the top of his own work for four days in a row.

“Four at the seventh hour, seven days of the coming back, and about ninety of those ninety steps under, on the second day of the hundred and sixty-third week, and it is a Wednesday. Three figures.”

He looked down the flight of them.

“Ninety of ninety. That is the whole of what is under me this morning and there is not one stone of this landing I am allowed to put a broom on until it comes off again.” He set the broom against the wall, upright, in the place it stands when there is nothing to do. “This has happened four times in nineteen years. And on the three previous times I have said something about it on the first morning and the something has cost me the rest of the day.”

“So this morning I am going to say the whole of it in one sentence and then I am going to have nothing to say until it comes off.”

He stood with his hands empty.

“There is no work on this landing and I am going to be on it anyway. That is the sentence.”

Nobody on that landing said anything about it, and nobody said he ought to go home, and nobody told him he was needed anywhere.

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour, which he could not have done at the seventh, and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that a man of fifty-four standing on the top step in a foot and a half of a stranger’s water was the most honest he had ever been.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and sixty-sixth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and sixty-sixth. Nine hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and you are standing in my water.”

“I am standing in your water,” said Barnaby Crove, “and I have been coming up this bank two hundred and sixty-six mornings and I have never once been able to say that I have got nothing standing between me and this county, and this morning there is a foot and a half of a stranger’s water between me and everything I have come up here for, and I am not going to pretend I do not like it.”

Nobody told him he ought to.

“Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I am not going to say it from down there in the wet because I have never said it from anywhere but the top and I am not starting now.”

Nobody answered him from the top and nobody answered him from the bottom, and nobody told Barnaby Crove he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for standing in the water to say it.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the eighth hour, and there was nothing to put his boots on, and he stood on the flat with the water round the stone, and gave his figures and the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud, and asked for nothing, and then spent the whole of what he had done this week out loud in one mouth, in order, and nobody on that landing did anything with it.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and thirty-three days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and two days old.

“Four hundred and thirty-three days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and two days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-thirty-seventh is two hundred and eighty-seven days old, which is nine hundred and eighty-seven less seven hundred. It is unanswered, and I have not asked it, and I am going to spend the rest of that morning saying the other thing out loud from the beginning to the end, and I would like anybody who wants to write any of it down to go ahead, and I am not going to thank them and I am not going to ask them to.”

Nobody took out anything to write on.

“Thursday morning of last week. I got up in the dark because I could not sleep and I went down the lane as far as a fallen ash and I sat down in the wet with a wedge and a block of wood and split a length down the middle because that is the only thing I have ever been able to do with my hands that comes out right.” He was not looking at anybody. “I cut one end square with a saw that went off at the corner.”

“And I carried it up that bank in my coat and I stood at the foot of these steps for a quarter of an hour working out whether to put it down, and I knew before I put it down that a thing put down where somebody works is not a given thing.”

The water moved on the stone.

“And I put it down anyway. And I said out loud that I put it there, and I said it so that there would be a giver in it, and I said the reason out loud as well, which was that I have wanted to be thanked for something since the day I came into this county. And the man with the broom had told me the shape of that out loud before he had looked at what was under my arm, and I put it down anyway, and I said it out loud anyway.”

He stopped and let it sit.

“Friday. A man who moves furniture told me that saying it out loud does not make it a given thing and that in fact it takes the last thing away from a thing that has no name on it yet. And he gave his trade as the reason and he did it without being unkind to me and he was not thanked either. And I worked out that morning that I had wanted the refusal, and I said so, out loud, on this landing, and nobody gave me a piece of it.”

Nobody gave him a piece of it.

“Saturday. A man who cannot see well said out loud that this landing rests on there being no giver in anything standing on it, and that it stopped being true on Thursday at the eighth hour, and that a landing which has run on one sentence for nineteen years and then finds it is not true gets a hole in the shape of the old one and not a new sentence.”

Nobody said that was true.

“Sunday. A boy asked me one question about it and a man with a broom refused the question and gave the working, and I answered it out loud to the landing and not to the boy. Monday. A woman who reads for a living refused to give a figure of three hundred days any shape and refused to put it beside the iron in the fortieth step. Tuesday. That man came back and said the same thing about a hole and said it differently, because he is a man who works things out on his own and gets them different each time.”

He looked down at the water on the stone.

“And that is all of it. In order. That is the whole of what I have done in this county in a week. And I have just said it from the beginning to the end in one go, which is the first time I have been able to do that about anything, and nobody on this landing has given me anything for it.”

Nobody on that landing gave him anything for it.

“One hundred and twenty-seven wrong things in one hundred and twenty-seven mornings, and this is the hundred and twenty-seventh. And I want to say the thing I have not said out loud yet and then I am going to stop for the day. Whatever that piece of ash is going to be on this landing, I am not going to be the one who says what it turns into. I said on Thursday what it was cut for. I am not going to say what it is going to be. Not to this landing and not later, not when it comes out of the water, not when it goes hard.”

Nobody asked him what it would turn into.

Then the man of fifty-four said the rule out loud from where he was standing, in the water, in the words he uses, and nobody thanked him for it and nobody helped him with it.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read, and said the whole of the rule out loud on the second day of the hundred and sixty-third week and gave the working for why he knew it in that order.

“I have never read it. I cannot read and there is not a person in this county who has ever offered to read it to me and I have never asked.” He shifted the barrow in the wet. “And I know all seven of them in the order they go because a woman who reads for a living said them once on this landing in the fourth hour of the day when I was standing behind her, and I have had them in that order in my mouth for a fortnight and I have said them out loud four mornings running now and nobody has stopped me and nobody has thanked me.”

Nobody stopped him and nobody thanked him.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. Seven things.” He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. “And I am going to say the half of it that belongs to me and nobody else. The water is the only thing on this landing that has been in any of them all week.”

“The water has been over it, and under it, and round it, and it will be over it again on Saturday.”

“Nobody put the water there.”

“And that is the only sentence I have got that is worth anything and it is about the weather and not about anybody.”

Nobody on that landing made anything of it.

Then a woman at the wall said the whole of it in her own shape from below the wall, and did not come up the steps, and nobody thanked her for it.

Orla Fennimore is forty-four, keeps twenty-nine chairs at a house that is not in this county, was asked once what she did and has not been asked since, and said on the second day of the hundred and sixty-third week, from the wall and not from the steps, that the seven things had been said four times that week by people who were not being thanked for it, and that she was going to say them once more from where she was standing.

“Nobody is to come up this road and tell me anything about that landing.” She said it without coming past the wall. “And I am not coming up it, and I have not come past this wall, and I would like whoever is up there counting the mornings to notice that I am not one of them and that whatever is in the water at the top of those steps is not in my house and is not my business and did not come from me.”

Nobody on that landing counted her.

“And I will say the seven from here because I can say them from a wall and it costs me the same as saying them from a step, which is nothing.” She did not raise her voice to do it. “It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used.”

“Seven things, and I am not going to be thanked for saying them from a wall and I am not going to be told I have no standing here either, because I have been at this wall twice this week and I have not been up that road once and that is the whole of my claim and I am not making more of it.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody told her she had no standing.

Four inches and about ninety of those ninety steps under and seven days of the coming back. The thing he had put down was lying on the dry strip along the foot of the wall, where the water had not been and had not been on Tuesday and would not be on Saturday, and it was not touched and nobody had rescued it and nobody had needed to. 

He had spent the whole of his mistake out loud in one mouth from beginning to end, and it had resolved nothing, and nobody on that landing was relieved.
