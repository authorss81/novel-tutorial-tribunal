# Chapter 995: A Thursday And Four Inches And About Ninety Of Those Ninety Steps Out And A Woman Who Saws Lengths Stood At The Bottom Of The Steps And Gave The Figure Of How Many Things Are Lying On That Landing And Nobody Is To Be Given It Again, And A Man Stood At The Bottom Of Those Steps And Was Not Asked

Third day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week. His seven hundred and forty-fifth morning. Five hundred and ninety-five days after the settlement. The fever one hundred and one weeks and five days old. Five hundred and forty-nine days since the division. Four hundred and ninety-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county, and four hundred and forty-one days in this county of Kell.

Four inches on the top step at about the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days of the coming back. The man of forty-three gave those three figures and said that the number of days was the largest one he had given in a fortnight and that he was not going to make anything of that either.

Corvin Tace is forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years, gives four figures off one stone, has never once given a fifth, and said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that he had known somebody was going to say a figure out loud on that landing one day this week and had not known which morning, and that he was not going to be the man who stopped it.

“Seven days of the coming back is the whole of what I have got and it is a big number and it means the water is low and it does not mean anything else. Four at the seventh hour, about ninety of those ninety steps under, and seven days, on the third day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week, and it is a Thursday. Three figures.”

He set the broom against the wall.

“I have not said anything about the wood on that strip. I have swept round it every morning this week and I have not said one word about it and I am not going to this morning, and if somebody up here says a figure about those steps I am not going to stop them and I am not going to agree with them either.”

Then the man of fifty-four came up at about the sixth hour and asked in the same words at the same hour and was refused in the same way and was not thanked, and said out loud that a big number of days was still a number of days and not a place for anybody to live.

Barnaby Crove is fifty-four, holds the gravel on that landing, and cannot read. He carried two hundred and seventy-fourth mornings of asking for a day against the ninth line, which is nine hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one.

“Two hundred and seventy-fourth. Nine hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred and twenty-two, and then one. Nobody asked me for it, same as every morning I have come up.”

“Nobody asked you for it, and there are ninety steps under my arm this morning and I could not walk them whatever I had to walk them for.”

“No.” He set the barrow down. “Whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it. And I have worked out this week that a man who sweeps ninety steps is going to have to look at those steps longer than anybody else does, and that is not me getting at anything and I am not going to use it.”

Nobody answered Barnaby Crove on that Thursday, nobody told him he was owed an answer, and nobody thanked him for working a thing out.

Then the woman who saws lengths came up the cart road with her saw handle under her arm, and did not come up the steps, and asked for nothing, and gave the figure.

Wenna Pyle is thirty-eight, who saws lengths and sets studs at a bench in an open-sided shed behind a wheelwright’s about three hundred yards down the cart road, and who carries her own cup out to that bench because there is no table in the shed, and who said on the third day of the hundred and sixty-fourth week that she was going to give the figure of how many things were lying on that landing and nobody was to be given it a second time.

She did not come up the steps and she did not point at anything.

“I am going to say this once and I am not going to say it again on this landing in any week, and nobody on these steps is to ask me for it, and if a figure cannot be asked for twice then it has to be right the first time or it is not worth saying. I am not going to be thanked for it either and I am not going to be told I have got it right.”

Nobody asked her for it a second time and nobody thanked her.

“Here is the working, and I am going to be exact about it because there is no use in it otherwise. I did not count people. I did not count mornings. I am not going to tell you how long anything has lain where it is lying, and if anybody up there wants that figure they can go and find it somewhere else in the world.”

She turned the saw handle over once in her hands.

“What I counted is things lying on that landing that nobody has taken up. Three. The iron in the seam of the fortieth step. The chair standing where it was put. And the second piece of ash on that dry strip.”

Nobody on that landing made a sound.

“And I left the first one out and I am going to say why in front of everybody, because if I do not say why then somebody is going to find a way of saying the figure is small.” She stopped and then went on. “The first piece of ash has a giver in it. A man stood on that step and said out loud that he put it there. So it is not lying there not claimed. It is lying there claimed, by the man who left it, and I am not going to move him into a figure or out of one to make it come out at a number anybody would rather have.”

Nobody moved anybody into it or out of it.

“So that is the whole of it and it counts wood and iron and a chair and it counts nobody at all. It is not a figure of how many people put any of them down. There is no giver in any of the three and I am not going to say a number of nothing, and I am not going to stand here and tell you who put them there, and neither is anybody else.”

Nobody said who put any of them there.

“And it is not a figure of how long anything has been there and it is not a figure of how many mornings any of us has stood on these steps. I want that in the same breath as the figure, because a figure of things that has got a length standing next to it is a figure of something else by the end of a week.”

She did not come up the steps.

“And here is the last part and it is why I can only give it once. The only thing that would check any of those three is the person who put them there. And there is no way of putting that question on this landing without putting a giver into a thing that has not got one, and it would not be me putting it in. It would be whoever answered.”

Nobody on that landing put that question.

Then the man of thirty-one came up at about the ninth hour and gave his figures and gave the figure of the offer with the subtraction out loud and asked for nothing, and stood at the bottom of the steps while he said all of it, and did not come up.

Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody’s, and has been in this county four hundred and forty-one days. The cut across that palm is three hundred and ten days old.

“Four hundred and forty-one days in this county, and the cut across that palm is three hundred and ten days old, and the standing offer asked at the seven-hundred-and-forty-fifth is two hundred and ninety-five days old, which is nine hundred and ninety-five less seven hundred. It is unanswered. I have not asked it this morning and I am not going to ask it before the water is off those steps again.”

He said all of it from the bottom of the steps and did not look at the dry strip once.

“One hundred and thirty-five wrong things in one hundred and thirty-five mornings, and this is the hundred and thirty-fifth, and nobody is going to tell me what kind of figure that is and nobody is going to tell me that I found anything.”

Nobody said anything at all about the size of that figure and nobody told him he had found anything.

“And I am going to say the last of it and it is the only part of this morning I have been thinking about since the sixth hour, and I am not going to be unrelieved about it and I am not going to be relieved.”

He put his hands behind his back.

“A figure of things was given on this landing this morning with the working, and the working says what it is a figure of and what it is not a figure of, and one of the things it is not a figure of is a figure of how many people put any of them down. And I have been standing at the bottom of these steps for an hour with the only thing in this county that could have checked one of them inside me, and nobody has asked me, and nobody is going to ask me, and I am not going to say it.”

Nobody asked him, and nobody told him he should have said it, and nobody told him he had been right not to say it.

“Because the second it comes out of my mouth it is not a thing nobody put down any more. It is a thing I put down, and there is a giver in it, and a figure of things becomes a figure of me, and that is how a landing turns into an account.”

Nobody turned that landing into an account.

He came away from the bottom of the steps and went down the bank. He did not stop at the bottom of it, and nobody went down after him.

She waited until his boots were off the stone, and then she said the seven of them out loud, and did not come up the steps to say them.

“It has not been touched. Not pulled, not turned over, not weighed in the hand, not read out loud, not carried inside, not put back where it was, and not used. All of it, on that landing, including a thing that has a name on it that is not true and including a thing that has a giver in it. And nobody is to thank me for this one and nobody is to ask me to say them again this week.”

Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to say them again.

Four inches on the top step and about ninety of those ninety steps under and seven days of the coming back, which is the largest figure of days on that stone in a fortnight and is a figure of the water. A figure of things was given once on that landing with the working beside it, and it counted a thing in a step and a chair and a piece of wood, and it counted nobody. The person who could have checked one of them walked down that bank without being asked and without saying anything, and the account on that landing is exactly as incomplete as it was at the sixth hour, and it is still in use.
