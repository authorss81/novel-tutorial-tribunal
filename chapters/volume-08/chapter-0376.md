# Chapter 376: What A Woman On A Stool Said To The People In A Hall

The last day of the seventy-fifth week was his hundred and twenty-sixth morning and his twenty-fifth day in a city he came to on foot, and the woman on the stool at the low hall at the end of Tallowgate had told him two days before that she was going to say four things to whoever was standing in that hall, and she had said three of them to him and had not yet said the fourth to a room.

She did it at the second hour, standing where she stands, with a boy of sixteen, a man of thirty-one with a bad ear, and two people on boards near the pail who could hear everything said in that room and had been awake for two days.

There were no other people. He had walked down the row before her and there was nobody at any door on it, and that is not a thing anybody arranged.

---

“*I am going to say it once,*” she said, “*and then I am going to sit down, and I have been on this stool since the eighth morning of the fever and it is not my stool and nobody gave it to me, and I am fifty-eight and I have a house two streets off this and I am not going to it this morning.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*The sixth part of the order stands. It was filled in before yesterday and the word in the margin has not changed and the date in that line does not come. Any person inside the protection of this order who is worse than every other person in the place they are sleeping in may be put in a bed in any hall in this city, and a hall that takes a person that way does not need that person's name on the roll of its ward, and that is the whole of it and there is no more of it than that and there is not going to be any more of it.*”

---

“*Three things you are not going to be told by anybody else, and I am telling you, and I am not asking you about any of them, and you are not going to be asked about any of them by anybody in this city, and there is nobody I can send to ask.*”

The boy of sixteen had the barrow outside the door and he had not moved.

“*One. It was a man with a bad leg who asked for it. He is not a magistrate and he is not in this ward. He said the price of it out loud in a room at the top of the hill on the fourth day of the week with four people in it, and a clerk wrote it down, and there is no name at the top of the paper that went up, and there is no name at the bottom of the page in the book either, and that is on purpose, and he did that on purpose, and he is not in this city on anything and there is no form in this city on which he could be asked about it.*”

“*Two. The rule will move the worst first. The worst are the ones who are already inside, and being inside is the fever, and the fever is in the people whose names are on a roll of their own ward. So the first people it moves are people from a hall in a low ward, and the rule does not care which low ward, and it will not ask you.*”

---

And then the third one, and she took longer over it, and the man with the bad ear turned round on his stool and looked at her, which he had not done in three weeks.

“*Three, and this is the one I would not have believed a month ago and I am fifty-eight and I have been in this city since I was nine. There are three hundred names in four low wards that are on a paper and not on a plate. Before yesterday morning those were a thing with a price on it, four shillings the hundred, and every one of them could have been put right by one person with eight shillings. Since the morning before yesterday a hall does not need the roll of its ward, and so the roll of a ward is not what puts a person in a bed, and so those three hundred people are not a gap in anything. They are not outside. They are not inside. They are a second way of keeping a record, and a second way of keeping a record is not a mistake, because there is nothing for it to be a mistake about.*”

She sat down.

“*That is the whole of what I have,*” said the woman on the stool. “*And I am not going to say that it is bad, because it is not a bad thing, and I sat on this stool and worked out in my head that the worst thing about a boy at the end of a run of boards was that he was inside, and somebody took that wall down before yesterday with three sentences, and I am glad about it and I am telling you the rest of it at the same time and I am not going to take either of them back and you can do what you like with both.*”

---

Nobody thanked her. She was not thanked and she is not thanked now and there is no form in this city that would record a thank.

The two people on the boards near the pail did not say anything, and one of them was awake, and he lay there and looked at the ceiling of a hall for a while and then said one thing, and it was not to anybody.

“*That is the first time anybody has told me anything at all.*”

“*It is the first time I have had anything to tell anybody,*” said the woman on the stool, “*and I have had it for two days and I sat on it for one of them, and I am going to tell you why, and it is not a good reason. I have been on this stool three weeks saying one sentence to everybody who came through that door and I have never had anybody in this hall who could do anything about the sentence. Before yesterday a woman came through that door who could. And I did not say it to her, and I said it to you, and I have thought about that for two days and I cannot get it any better than that.*”

---

And that is where he came in, at about the third hour, and he stood in the doorway of a hall at the end of a low ward and heard the last of it, and what he had come to do was to say a price out loud, because that is the only instrument he has, and that is the method, and it is his whole of what he has ever been able to do.

He did not do it. He stood in the door with his hand on the frame and he worked out straight away why he was not going to, and the reason was not that she had done it first. The reason was that there was nobody in the room to say a price to. A price said out loud is a thing said to a room, and it is how a man makes sure he is not the only one who knows, and the room in the public room at the foot of the hill had four people in it and one of them was a clerk whose job it is to write it down.

This hall had the people in it who it was about. And a man who says the price of a thing to the people the thing is being done to is not saying a price, he is standing in front of them while somebody else does it, and there is no name on the paper either way, and both of them said the truth, and the woman on the stool had said it to the people and he had said it to a room, and the only difference between the two of them was who was standing in the room when it was said.

He stood in the doorway and did not say it, and that was not a thing he had done before, and he did not know yet whether it was the same instrument used better or the same instrument put down, and the man with the bad ear looked at him and then looked away, and the boy of sixteen had gone out to the barrow.

---

The mother came at about the fifth hour, and she comes at the second hour every morning now, and nobody has said she may not come, and there is a bench against the wall outside the door of this hall that anybody may sit on, and she sits on it, and nobody inside the hall asks her in.

The woman on the stool goes to the door and says it to her, and it is a short thing to say, and it is the same short thing every morning, and here it is in full, once, because it is the shape of what she is going to be saying for the rest of her life.

“*He is in a bed at the top of the city and he is better. You cannot bring him home and here is why, and I am going to say why every morning for as long as you come to this door, and I am not going to be asked to stop. A hall has a line for every person who comes in it and no line at all for anybody who goes out, and the discharge is a document, and there is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one, and a person who is in a hall is at no door, and a name comes into this ward's roll at a door, and so his line is at your door and he is not at it, and there is no form in four hundred miles that moves a name from one place to another. That is all of it. Come back in the morning.*”

“*Right,*” said the mother, and she did not go, and then she said the thing that made the man with the bad ear put his chalk down.

“*Say the part about the second line on the board.*”

“*His line is on the board at the end of the beds and it is not going to come off, and there is a second line in a hall at the top of this city, and the man keeping the column of nineteen has been asked and has said no and he is right to say no.*”

“*Then he is in two places.*”

“*He is in two places and one of them is a board,*” said the woman on the stool, “*and a person cannot be got out of either of them and there is nobody in this city who can be asked whether that is all right. That is what the morning before yesterday was. You can go home now and I will be here in the morning.*”

---

The four bells went at the last of the light over nine hundred doors in a ward a mile and a half long, and the man with the bad ear was at the end of the beds rubbing out a number on the wall board and chalking a number that was less wrong, and the boy of sixteen had the barrow against the wall of a shed, and the woman on the stool was on the stool, and a man with a bad leg went up the row with a leg that will not clear and nothing at all in his hands, and nobody in that hall had been thanked, and nobody had been relieved, and nobody had been asked.
