# Chapter 399: Who Is Going To Ask You About Her, And The Pen That Was Not Used

The second day of the seventy-ninth week was his hundred and forty-ninth morning and his forty-eighth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was sixteen weeks and four days old, and the man with the bad ear asked him a question at the end of a row at the second hour, and the clerk of thirty was standing close enough to hear the whole of it with a pen out, and did not use it.

There were two of them thirty-one. He had not known that until that week and he had been in the ward since the second week of the fever.

---

“*I have got two lines of chalk over my nineteen,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and I have got a form with my name at the top of it in a book four floors up, and I am the only man in this city who is both of those things, and neither of them is a person, and I have been going round asking people whether either of them is a person and I have got no from nine.*”

“*They are not.*”

“*Then I will stop asking,*” he said. “*I have been at the end of this row since the light and I have come out to the end of it to ask you one thing and then I am going back up to a board.*”

The clerk of thirty had a pen out. He had it out because a question was being asked at the end of a row in a ward and he had come down the hill on the ordinary errand of a return and had stopped, which is a thing a clerk of thirty does.

“*Ask it,*” said the man with the bad ear.

“*Who is going to ask you about her.*”

---

Ilyan had an answer ready and it was a true one and it was about a thing that was not his, and the whole of what he had for forty-eight days was the fact that a true thing said out loud in a room, in front of other people, about a thing that is not yours, goes into them and stays there, and it has gone into a gate, a lane, a yard, a hall, a door, a step, a form and a page, and the last of those has his name at the top of it and a woman's name at the foot of the fourth and fifth clauses, and the clerk of thirty told a room, on the morning you said your own name out loud twice in it, that there was nothing after that, and that was true, and he had said it before he did it, in a room, so that it would not be him afterwards.

He could have said it. He had the shape of it. It would have gone into the man with the bad ear and into the clerk of thirty and into the woman on the stool at the low hall and into every person who has ever gone past that end of the row, and it would have been true, and it would have cost something, and saying the price of a thing before the doing is the only method he has ever had and it has never once stopped him.

He did not say it.

“*I do not know,*” he said.

“*That is not an answer.*”

“*It is the answer,*” he said. “*There is nobody. That is not a thing I have not got round to. I have been in this ward forty-eight days and I have asked every person in it who can be asked anything and there is no post in this city with the care of a name at the top of a page, and there is no form that says a person shall go and ask a man what he did with a woman's work, and there is no form that says a woman shall be asked whether she agreed to it, and both of those are the same form and neither of them exists, and the woman who would do the asking is a mile and a half down a row with a lamp on and she is the only person in this city anybody can be asked about that roll and she cannot be asked whether she agreed, because nobody has ever thought of asking her that and I have asked her four things and three of them were about figures.*”

The man with the bad ear said: “*That is a thing about a city and not about me.*”

“*Yes,*” Ilyan said.

“*Then ask me a better one.*”

---

“*No,*” Ilyan said.

The clerk of thirty had the pen out the whole time. He had it out and he had the flat look of a man waiting for a sentence to write down, and he had written four hundred lines a day for nine years and he had a blank page and he was four feet from a man who was about to say something, and he did not put the pen on the page.

Nobody in the row said anything for a while, and the low ground was under water at the sides and the water was going down, and the woman on the stool at the low hall said the same short thing she said every morning to a boy of eight's mother when she came down the row for water, which was four words, and then said it again to nobody, and the man of fifty-two was at a door at the end of the row with a slate and was not stopping.

“*You are not going to write it down,*” Ilyan said to the clerk of thirty, after a while.

“*No,*” said the clerk of thirty.

“*Why not.*”

“*Because it is not a form and there is no line for it,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and I fill lines and I do not make them up, and I have been on this floor nine years and I have never once written down a thing a man said at the end of a row because it was worth writing down, and I am not starting at the end of a wet week in a ward that has had nothing on a wall in it since a bill went up.*”

He put the pen in his coat.

“*And I am not going to tell you why I did that either, and you are not going to ask me again, because if you ask me again I will have to think about it and I have not got the hour.*”

Nobody asked him why.

---

And the one person in that row who the order had never touched came down his nine steps while that was going on, because he comes down them every morning, and his light is better than it was and there is nobody in this city who put anything there.

He is forty-four and his head is his sister's husband and he is not on the roll of this ward and has asked twice and was told to come back when he has the paper, and he is not asking a third time. He stood at the bottom of his own steps and listened to a man be asked a question he could not answer, and he did not say anything, and then he said the only thing he had come down for.

“*Is my door in it.*”

“*I do not know,*” Ilyan said, and it was the second true answer he had given in a row at an hour.

“*Right,*” said Ivo Serrel. “*Then I will tell you what it is with both and I will not be on a list about it. My door is in it if you go to it and it is not in it if you do not, and there is nobody in this ward who has come up nine steps, and I am not complaining, because I came down them this morning on my own two legs, and if you put me in a book about that I will be a person in a record for being well, and I have watched what that does to people this month.*”

Nobody thanked him for it and he did not wait to be.

---

“*I am going to ask you the same question in a year,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and in two years, and when the round is done, and I am going to ask it at this end of this row and you are going to give me the same answer, and I want to tell you now that I have decided to keep asking and that I am not going to tell you why and it is not a threat and it is not a job.*”

“*It is not a job.*”

“*It is not a job,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and there is no form for it and that is the first thing I have ever wanted that there is no form for, and I have had a form twice since the end of last week and I have not asked for a third.*”

He went back up the row. He has a bad ear and he is thirty-one and he is paid nothing and he has two lines of chalk over nineteen dead that are about a ward and not about the dead, and a name at the top of a form in a book four floors up, and he asked a man in a city a question and got a true answer and did not get what he came for, and he went back to a board.

---

And the man with the leg stood at the end of a row in a city he came to on foot and put his hand in his coat and touched a piece of paper that was not there any more, because it had gone up a hill on a tray thirteen days ago with his name at the top of it, and it was eight days since he had last said a price out loud in this city, and everything he had said in this city had gone into somebody, and one of the things had gone into a jug of water, and he was not going to be asked about any of it by anybody, and nobody asked him.

The four bells went over Tallowgate and the water at the low end had gone down a hand's width since the morning, and the fever was sixteen weeks and four days old, and the boy of eight was still in a bed at the top of this city and was still better, and still could not be discharged, and still had not been named out loud in any room in his life, and his mother would be at the standpipe in the morning.
