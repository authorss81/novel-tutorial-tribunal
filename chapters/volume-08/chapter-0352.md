# Chapter 352: Five Parts And A Half, And The Half Is The One She Left Out

It was his hundred and second morning and the second day of a fever in two low wards of a city he had been in for one day, and there is no figure on any wall in this city and there is going to be none for as long as he is in it, so the count that is running in his head this week is a different one and it is running in a book at a gate and in three places where a person is standing all day counting something else.

He went and asked to hear the order read out.

---

There is a room in this city, on the ground floor of the black building at the middle of the nine bridges, with a bench in it and a long table and a door that is open all day, and anybody may come into it and ask to hear a rule that governs them read aloud, and it is a room with four people in it at any time and two of them are clerks.

He was in it at about the second hour.

A clerk of thirty read it. Not because he was asked nicely. Because a rule that governs a person is read to that person, and the room exists for that, and a stranger who comes in and says *I would like to hear the shelter order read out* has asked for the one thing in the building that costs nothing to give.

It took eleven minutes and it was read the way that anybody reads a thing out in a room where people have not heard it before, which is slowly, and twice for the numbers, and once for the words that do the work.

Here is the whole of it, and the order is six lines long and a covenant has six parts, and this one has five.

*THE SUBJECT* is the residents of the four low wards, which are Tallowgate, Weir Lane, Nethergill and the Ninth Weir, and the four are named one under another at the head of it.

*THE CONDITION* is a fever declared by a magistrate, which has stood since the cold came off the water.

*THE BURDEN* is the person. And here is the whole of what the line says, and it is eleven words and every one of them is doing work: a person is inside the protection when the roll of the ward has the name of that person in it. Not their household. Their name.

*THE WITNESS* is the keeper of the roll, and the master of the hall, and the person of the house in which the person sleeps.

*THE REMEDY* is a board in a hall, water at a standpipe, and a wage at the end of the week. There is a figure in the ward's own book for a wage and the figure is not in the order, and the clerk said that was correct and that the order did not fix wages and the ward's book did, and nobody has brought the ward's book.

And the sixth line is a line with a date in it, which is when the order ends, and the line is empty, and in the margin beside the empty line there is one word, and the word is standing.

It is the only word on the page that is not part of a sentence.

The clerk read the word. He read it the way a man reads the last thing on a page when he knows what the room is going to do about it.

Nobody in the room did anything for nine seconds.

Then Ilyan said: “*There are six parts to one of these and this one has five and a half.*”

The clerk said, “*There are.*”

“*And the half is the line with the date in it.*”

“*The half is the line with the date in it,*” said the clerk, “*and the word beside it is the reason there is no date in it, and I read it because I am required to read the whole of it, and I would be the one who did it wrong if I read round it, and if you want to argue with the word you want the magistrate and not me, because I did not write it and I would not have written it and I could not unwrite it.*”

---

She came into the room while he was standing there, and nobody announced her, and that is a thing about a city that a country does not have: nobody announces anybody.

She was working. She had a sleeve pushed up and a sheaf under her arm and she was going somewhere else, and she stopped because the clerk said one sentence to her and the sentence was *this one has read the sixth line, magistrate*.

“*Then that is fair,*” said Magistrate Rell. “*I would have read it round it myself and I am glad somebody did not.*”

She put the sheaf on the table. She looked at the stranger for about a second and a half and then stopped looking at him, in the way of a person who has decided he is not the matter in front of her.

“*You have read the order,*” she said. “*Do you have a question about it or do you have an objection to it, because those are different and the second one is a thing I have to write down and the first one I have to answer.*”

“*Both,*” he said. “*And the second one first, because it is shorter.*”

She said: “*Go on.*”

“*The burden is named,*” he said. “*The line says a person is inside the protection when the roll has the name in it. That is a name. Your own wall has a sentence on it — I read it coming up the stair, it is cut in the stone over the door, and it says that there is no burden without a name, and I have been a person who has spent seven months in a country where that sentence is the truest thing anybody has ever written down, and I have come four days to tell you that your order obeys it perfectly and that I do not think you can hear what it is saying.*”

Nobody in the room moved. The clerk's pen was on the table and it was not on the paper.

“*Go on,*” said the magistrate, and her voice did not change at all.

“*A name is a person,*” he said, “*and a person can be asked about. You have not named a person. You have named a kind of person, and everybody inside your protection is a person, and everybody outside it is a person, and the line does not say which is which — it says the roll says, and the roll is a book, and a book is a thing somebody keeps, and I have not been in this city a day and I can tell you that the sentence on your wall is the reason your order is a good one and the reason it is going to be the worst thing that happens in it.*”

There is no version of this that was a speech. It came out in thirty seconds and it was said in a room with four people in it and it was not loud.

The magistrate looked at him for a while.

“*That is a good argument,*” she said, “*and it is eleven years old, and I have heard it nine times, and I have answered it nine times, and I am going to answer it once more and then you will not want to hear it again this week.*”

She turned round and put a hand flat on the table, on the paper, on the word *standing*.

“*A covenant with a person's name in the burden is a covenant about a person, and I have four in this city, and every one of them has a household, and every household has somebody in it who is not the person and is carrying the burden anyway, and if I name the burden in person I have to name it in four people and the other ninety thousand are not in the order at all. That is what happened the last time. I was here. I read the order after it and I was the one who had to sit in a room with a woman for an afternoon and explain to her why the name on the paper was her husband's and not hers, and I did it competently, and it took an afternoon, and it is correct, and it does not scale and it is not supposed to scale, because an emergency is a thing that happens to a city and not to four people in it.*”

“*Then how many people does the roll have,*” he said.

“*Fewer than it has,*” she said. “*And I know that, and I know it is the worst sentence in the order, and I wrote it in the fifth week when I had about nine hundred of them dead and a bench of four who wanted one legible thing by the end of the day.*”

---

He asked her the other question then, the one that had nothing to do with her and everything to do with why he had come four days on a road, and she answered the first half of it in three sentences and the second half of it in nine words.

“*The board came to the building at noon yesterday,*” she said. “*Nine things on a board the size of a door, on the back of a sack, in nine hands. Six of them standing. One burned. Two not found, and one of the two is this city. There is a question on the docket about it in a chamber upstairs and there is a date on it, and the date is the fourteenth of this month, and a witness has been asked for and there are two names on the sheet for it and one of them is yours.*”

“*I did not send for it.*”

“*No,*” she said. “*You did not, and I want that said where it can be read, because a document arrives from four hundred miles off with a question attached to it about this city, and the man who carried the last part of making it is standing in a room with no line on any book in it, and a bench of four is going to be asked on the fourteenth by a person who will say the man made it up.*”

That was the end of the magistrate's time. She took her sheaf and went, and she was gone before either of them said anything else, and she did not say good morning and he did not say it to her.

---

He went back to the table where the clerk was still sitting, because there was one thing left and he had come four days for it and it was not the order.

“*Who reads it to the people it governs?*”

The clerk put the pen down.

“*Whoever comes and asks,*” he said. “*That is the rule. A person may come to this room and ask to hear a rule that governs them, and we read it, and it takes eleven minutes, and there are four of us and the room is open all day, and the order has been up since the second week.*”

“*How many people have come and asked?*”

The clerk was honest about it, and he was not embarrassed, and there is no reason in the world he should have been.

“*I could not tell you and I am not going to guess at you,*” he said. “*I have read it out to about forty people since the third week of it. Some of them came twice. There are about ninety thousand people in this city and the order has been up eight weeks, and I do not know how many of them know it exists, and neither does the bench of four, and I will tell you the honest reason for that, which is that nobody has ever been made to tell anybody.*”

Then he said the thing that the whole of the next nine days of his life turned on, and he said it as a clerk saying a fact about his own work.

“*It goes out to the ward by the ward clerk on the day it is signed, and the ward clerk puts it on a board, and after that it is a piece of paper on a board in a corridor. There is no part of this that says a person must be told. A rule that says a person is inside a protection when a book says so is a rule that works by the book, and the book is the doing of it. The book is in Tallowgate and it is very good work and I have read it and it is correct, and I have never once thought about what happens to somebody who is not in it, and I have thought about what happens to the order about twice a day since the second week, and those are two different things and one of them is my job.*”

The stranger said nothing at all for a moment.

“*Do you know a person who is not in it,*” he said.

The clerk thought about it, and it is a fair question and it is a hard one, and the clerk answered it the way an honest man answers a hard question in a room where four people can hear it.

“*Yes,*” he said. “*And I cannot tell you who, and that is not modesty. I know that there are people in this city who are not in that book, because the book is made at doors by householders and about a third of the people in this city do not sleep in a house that anybody calls theirs. I have known that since I was twenty-six. I did not know what to do about it until eleven minutes ago, and I am going to go and do my afternoon, and the afternoon is four hundred entries, and I am telling you that I am going to do my afternoon and not that I am going to do anything else.*”

Nobody thanked the clerk and the clerk was not thanked and he went and did his afternoon.
