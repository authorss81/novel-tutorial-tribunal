# Chapter 374: He Did It, Knowing, And Nobody Stopped Him

It was his hundred and twenty-fourth morning and the fifth day of the seventy-fifth week, and he had been in this city twenty-three days, and a runner came down the hill at the first hour with a piece of paper in his hand and nobody had sent for the runner and there is no form in this city that says a man may be told a thing in his own street.

The paper was a sheet with the order on it, and the order was five parts and a half, and the sixth part had three sentences in it that had not been in it on the day before.

Under the sixth part there was a date, which is the date the thing was made, and under the date there was a name in a hand that is not the hand of the clerk of forty and is not his own, and the name was that of the magistrate who had written the first five parts and the word in the margin. He read it twice in a lane at the top of the low ground. He did not see the person who had written it and nobody in this city is going to see her.

---

And here is what he said to himself, standing in that lane, and it was the price, and it was the same price he had said out loud in a room the day before, and he said it to himself in the flat voice of a man reading out a line off a list he has read before, because that is the only voice he has for it.

Three hundred names in a fair hand upstairs and not on a plate, and four shillings the hundred, and after this morning nobody in this city has to cut a line for anybody, and those three hundred people are somebody's arithmetic for ever. The people who will be moved first are the people already inside. A person moved out of a hall at the end of this row is at no door in this row and his line is still at his door. He has not asked one of them. He asked a room with four people in it and the price went into a book with nothing at the top of it.

He had it in the order it was said in. It had not got any smaller standing in a lane. He had said it out loud in front of four people on purpose, and the reason he had said it out loud on purpose was that he believed, in three weeks, in a country four hundred miles off and in a house in this city, that a man who says a price before he does a thing has not done the thing yet, and that a thing said at a price is a thing that can be stopped.

The thing was not stopped. It was on a sheet of paper with a magistrate's name under it and a runner had carried it down a hill at the first hour.

He found out at about the fourth hour that the price had not worked, and he found it out in a lane, on his own, with nobody in it, and he has said a price out loud in a room four times in this city and this is the first time one of them has come to nothing.

---

He went down to the low hall at the end of Tallowgate and the woman on the stool was on the stool and the run of boards at the end was short by one.

“*That one at the end is worse than the other nine,*” she said, to a man with a bad leg who had come in at about the fifth hour, and then she stopped, which she has never done in three weeks.

He waited.

“*That one at the end is in a hall at the top of this city in a bed that was free this morning,*” said the woman on the stool, “*and I am going to say four things in this hall and I am going to say them to whoever is standing in it, and then I am going to go back to sitting on this stool, because I have been on it since the eighth morning of the fever and it is not my stool and nobody gave it to me.*”

---

“*One. I did not ask for that. I have said that boy is worse than the other nine every morning since he came in and I have never once asked anybody for anything about him, and I was not asked this morning, and if anybody in this hall thinks I did it, they should say so to me and not to the man, and I will stand here while they do it.*”

Nobody said it.

“*Two. It is right. I have sat on this stool for three weeks and I have worked out in my head at the rate of two doors a day that the worst thing in this city about that boy was not the fever and it was not the cold and it was not a bed, it was that he was inside, and being inside was the wall, and somebody in this city went and took a wall down this morning with three sentences and a pen, and whoever did it did not have to touch him to do it, and I have not been asked for anything and I have not been thanked and I am not going to be.*”

---

“*Three. The woman who is his mother is not in this hall,*” she said, “*because a hall is not where people are, it is where the beds are, and she is at a standpipe at the end of the row and she is not coming here, and I have not told her and I am not going to, and somebody in this city is going to have to tell her and it is not going to be me because I have said the same sentence to her for three weeks and if I say a different one she will find out what has happened before she has been told what has happened to her boy.*”

“*Four.*”

And the woman on the stool looked at the door for a while before she said the fourth one, and there were people in that hall and one of them was a boy of sixteen who works in the yard next to the plate-cutter's and who had a barrow outside the door.

“*Four is that the line at the foot of that board is not going to come off,*” she said. “*It is chalk and there is a man here who has been keeping a column of nineteen of them for a month and he is not going to rub out mine, and I have asked him, because I am on a stool in a room and I ask, and he said no and he was right to say no, because a hall has a line for every person who comes in it and no line at all for anybody who goes out, and there is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one, and so that boy has two lines in two halls now and one door, and I do not know of any form on earth that fixes that, and I have been on this stool for three weeks and I have got very good at knowing what there is not a form for.*”

---

The man with the bad ear had written a second line on the board at the end of the beds and had not rubbed out the first one, and he said so, to a room, before anybody asked him, because he is thirty-one and he has a bad ear and he has been keeping a column with nothing over the top of it since the second week of the fever.

The boy of sixteen took the boy out of the hall on the barrow at about the sixth hour with a blanket round him and nothing else, and it is a mile and a half to the top of this city and he took the long way round because the low ground is under water at the sides, and it took about an hour and forty minutes, and there was nobody with them.

And nobody in that hall thanked anybody, and the woman on the stool did not, and the man with the bad ear did not, and the man with the leg did not, and nobody was thanked.

---

Tarin Keel was at the end of the row at the change with a piece of lead in his coat and chalk on the back of one hand, and he had been at the low hall at the end of this ward since the beginning of last week counting what came through the door, and nobody had asked him to and nobody had told him to stop, and both of those were the worst part of it.

He looked at the barrow going up the row and then he looked at the man with the leg.

“*You are a man who says prices,*” he said. “*I count things. It is what I have been for nineteen years and there is no form in this city that says I may stop. I have counted four of yours in three weeks, because I count them, and every one of them was a true thing and every one of them cost somebody something, and the last one was in a room at the top of the hill with a clerk in it and I was not in that room and I have not slept properly since the change.*”

“*I know.*”

“*I have one question and it is the only one I have and I am not going to ask it twice,*” said Tarin Keel.

And he asked it, and it was the question he was going to ask, and it was in a street at the end of a low ward at the change on a day when a boy of eight was going up a row on a barrow in a blanket.

“*Who is in the room that is paying it.*”

---

He did not answer it.

There was nobody to answer it with. There is no person in this city he could have said the name of, and there is no ward he could have said, because a ward is not a person and a ward cannot be asked, and the whole price of what he had done was that the people paying it were in four wards in rooms and yards and halls, and none of them was in a room, and the room had been the point.

So the question stood in a street at the change and he did not answer it, and he had said a price out loud four times in three weeks and had never once been asked the one that mattered, and he was being asked it now by a man with a piece of lead in his coat who was right nine times out of ten and had told him in a doorway that both their ignorances were the same ignorance in a different coat.

Tarin Keel waited, and then he said: “*Right,*” and that was all, and he has never asked him it again, and neither of them ever mentioned the notice again either, and the notice is not written, and a rule with no end in it went up a hill in a runner's hand on the fifth day of the seventy-fifth week, and the man who had been going to write the notice stood in a street at the end of a low ward and watched it go and said nothing at all about it, and he was right about that too and he will not be thanked for it.

---

The mother of the boy was at the standpipe at the end of the row at the last of the light and the woman on a stool at the low hall had not told her and he told her, and he said the price of it out loud first because he has no other way of doing anything, and she listened to all of it standing up with her hand on the pail, and then she asked him one question and it was not the one Tarin Keel had asked.

“*Can I bring him home.*”

“*No,*” he said, and then he told her the whole of why in the plainest words he had, and it took about a minute, and none of it was a kindness and all of it was true. A bed in a hall is a place a person cannot be discharged from, because the discharge is a document, and there is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one. A person who is in a hall is at no door, and a name comes into this ward's roll at a door, and so the line at her door has a name on it that is not coming back to it, and there is no form in four hundred miles that moves a name from one place to another. And the woman who keeps the book cannot take a name out, and cannot make one up, and has not been asked to, and would not be.

“*Right,*” she said.

And then she said the rest of it, standing at a standpipe at the last of the light, and it was short and she had not been asked for it and she said it to a man who had asked her nothing in three weeks.

“*I knew that before you came down the row, and I am not going to hold you to it, and it is a good bed.*”

She filled the pail and went home, and the four bells went over the low end of Tallowgate, and a boy of eight was in a bed at the top of the city with a blanket round him and a line in chalk at the foot of a board at the end of a run of boards, and his mother was at the standpipe in the morning and would be at the standpipe in the morning, and nobody in this city had been asked anything, and nobody had been thanked, and nobody was relieved.
