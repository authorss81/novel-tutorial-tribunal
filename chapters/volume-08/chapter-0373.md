# Chapter 373: The Price Of It, Said Out Loud, In A Room, Before The Doing

It was his hundred and twenty-third morning and the fourth day of the seventy-fifth week, and he had been in this city twenty-two days, and he said the price out loud in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour, and there were five people in the room and two of them were clerks, and the order was on the wall behind him with nine inches of blank under it for a notice that is not the order.

The clerk of thirty read the order out. He was asked to. That is a thing a clerk of thirty does four hundred times a day and he did it standing at the end of the long table with the flat voice of a man reading a thing he has read before and is not going to be asked about afterwards, and he read the five parts and then he came to the sixth and read a date and then read the word in the margin.

Nobody in the room said anything when he had finished, which is what a room in this city does at the end of the order.

---

“*I would like the sixth part of that filled in,*” said Ilyan, “*and I am going to say what it will cost before I ask for it, and it is going in the book, and I have thought about saying it for two days.*”

The clerk of forty put his hand on the book before the stranger had got to the middle of the first sentence, because he has done this before.

“*I am writing,*” he said.

And here is the price, in the order he said it, and nobody in that room interrupted him and he did not ask to be interrupted.

---

“*Three hundred names are in a fair hand in a book on the second floor of the building at the middle of the nine bridges,*” he said. “*They are not on a plate. A plate is four shillings the hundred, and this ward's own two hundred is eight shillings, and the three hundred is twelve shillings, and I have had all three of those figures said out loud to me in this city by two people who are not lying to me. After the sentence I am asking for stands, a hall does not need the roll of its ward, and so a plate is not what puts a person in a bed, and so nobody in this city is ever going to pay four shillings the hundred again. Those three hundred people are going to be somebody's arithmetic for ever, and I am the reason, and I am not going to find out whether they would have said yes.*”

He stopped for a breath and went on.

“*And that is the half of the price that can be counted. Here is the other half.*”

---

“*The people this will move first are the people who are already inside,*” he said, “*and I want everybody in this room to hear that sentence in the mouth of the man who is asking, because it is true and it is the whole of what I am doing. Not the three hundred. The three hundred are not in a hall to be moved out of. The three hundred are in rooms and yards and doorways in four wards and a hall that does not need a roll of its ward does not need them, and they are not going anywhere today or any other day, and the rule I am asking for will not touch one of them and I know it will not.*”

“*Then it moves nobody.*”

“*It moves everybody who is inside,*” he said, “*and inside means worse, because inside is a fever, and a fever finds the people whose names are on a roll of their own ward because that is where the houses are. A person moved out of a hall at the end of this row is in a hall at the top of this city, and is at no door in this row, and his line is still at his door, and there is no form in four hundred miles that moves a name from one place to another, and there is no discharge paper and no office that issues one. So it works. It works every time anybody uses it, and it is going to be used in a week, because a rule is not an argument, it is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper does not stop being true because a boy at the end of a run of boards is better.*”

---

“*And I have not asked one of them,*” he said. “*Not the three hundred. Not the people in the halls. Not the woman of twenty-eight at the end of the row, who found the boy and has been going to nine hundred doors on her own account for ten days. Not the woman in the room behind the chandler's shop, who told me yesterday that she has read a wall for twelve weeks without knowing she was reading it. I have asked this room, and I am saying it in this room on purpose, and the reason I am saying it in this room is that this room is the only thing I have got and there is nothing else in four hundred miles I could have used.*”

He put his hand flat on the table, the way a man does when he has finished saying a price.

“*That is the price. It is said out loud. Now I am going to ask for it, and the clerk is going to write it, and it is going up the hill, and I am not going to be able to take it back and neither is anybody else.*”

---

Then he said the three sentences, and they were plain, and there is no ornament in them of any kind, and they took as long to say as it takes to say a thing once, and four minutes to write down.

“*Any person who is inside the protection of this order and who is worse than every other person in the place they are sleeping in may be put in a bed in any hall in this city. A hall that takes a person under that sentence does not need that person's name on the roll of its ward. That sentence stands with the rest of the order and it may be ended only by the person who may end the rest of the order.*”

Nobody stopped him. The clerk of forty wrote for four minutes and did not look up and his hand did not stop.

---

Here is what the room did with it, and it did it in the order it did it, and it was four people in a room being useful, and none of them is a villain and one of them is a man of thirty-one with a bad ear who had come down off a stool at the end of a run of boards because the beds are his business and he has had them since the second week of the fever.

---

The man with the bad ear asked the first one, and he asked it flat, standing up, with his hat still in his hand.

“*That empties halls.*”

“*That is the first thing everybody says and I will say what this room thinks about it before anybody else does,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*It does not empty a hall. It takes a bed out of a hall, and those are different things, and the difference is the whole of what a rule is for. A hall with a bed in it and a man in the bed is a hall with no beds free. A hall with a bed in it and nobody in the bed is a hall with a bed. You want the second. Everybody in this room wants the second. The only people in this city who do not want the second are the people who are in the bed, and they are not in this room, and they have not been asked, and they are not going to be asked, and that is the sentence you have just said out loud twice and I have written both of them down.*”

---

The second one was his own, and he put it himself, and a person of a place answered it, and the person of a place was the man with the bad ear.

“*Then it takes people out of their own ward and puts them in wards they are not on the roll of, and they can never come back, and a man came down off a stool at a quarter past the first hour to say that this is true.*”

“*It is true,*” said the man with the bad ear. “*And I will tell you the half of it that you have not thought of, and then you can decide whether to have said the three sentences. A person in a hall at the end of this row is in that hall because his name is in a book, and a person in a hall at the top of this city is in that hall because of a piece of paper on a wall. The first of those can be got out on one condition: somebody with a duty decides that a thing in a book is not true, and there is no post in this city with that duty and there has not been one in the four hundred years of this ward's roll. So neither of them can be got out. You have not made that worse. You have made it permanent, and I have been keeping a column of nineteen lines in chalk with nothing over the top of it and nothing at the foot of it for a month, and nobody has ever asked me what the words over that column would be, and after this morning I know one of them.*”

---

The third one was the boy of fourteen, who had not said anything since he came in and had been listening the way a boy listens when he is alone in a room with two clerks and a stranger, which is to say entirely.

“*There is no name on it,*” he said.

And the room stopped, and the clerk of forty stopped writing for about a second and then went on.

“*That is not an argument against it,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*That is the only thing in this room that is in favour of it. I have been in that building nine years and everything that has come out of that bench has had somebody's name at the foot of it, and all of it has been argued with by somebody who was aggrieved and who had a right to be. That is not a fault in the arguing. That is the whole of what a rule is for. And you have put three sentences in front of me with nobody at the foot of them, and that is either the finest thing anybody has brought into this room this year or it is the worst, and I am a clerk and it is not my job to say which, and I have been in this room long enough to know that the answer is whichever of the two happens afterwards.*”

---

The fourth was the last question in the room and the clerk of thirty answered it before it was finished.

“*And who ends it.*”

“*The person who may end the rest of the order,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*That is a magistrate of this city. There is one, and she is the person who wrote the whole of it including the word in the margin, and she put a date in a line and then told the date not to come, and she is entitled to do that, and it is the same pen and the same woman and I am not going to stand in this room and pretend that is anybody's fault.*”

And then he said the last of it, and he said it to the form, and not to the room, and it is the one rule he has got and he said so.

“*There is a line at the top of that for who is asking, and I am not going to enter your name in it. I have not put anybody's name on anything in this city in three years that was not off a document in front of me, and I am not going to start on a morning like this one. That is not a kindness to you and it is not a kindness to me and it is the same sentence I said to you on the eleventh morning of your being in this city.*”

---

“*Then it goes up with nothing at the top of it.*”

“*It goes up with nothing at the top of it,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and the request is a thing five people in a room have heard a stranger say in his own mouth, and a fair hand on the second floor will have all of it, and there is no line at the top of the fair hand either. You have spent twenty-two days making sure that nobody could put a name on you, and you have just said a thing out loud in a room with a clerk in it that is going into a book, and if you are looking for the place where that is recorded, it is not on a form, it is in the five people, and four of them are in this building every day of the week and the fifth has a column of nineteen dead and is owed nothing.*”

He picked up his tray.

“*That is not me being cruel,*” he said. “*That is me telling you the price of the thing you have just done, and you have not asked me for it, and I would have said it if you had asked.*”

---

And at about the fifth hour the clerk of thirty read the sixth part of the order out loud again, with the three sentences in it, in the same flat voice, in a room with four people in it, and he read the date and he read the word in the margin, and nobody said anything, and the four people who were in the room had all been in the room when the three sentences were said.

That is the whole of what happened in the public room at the foot of the hill on the fourth day of the seventy-fifth week, and it is in a book on the second floor and a tray going up a hill, and the request has nothing at the top of it, and the price is in the same book as the request, word for word, in the order he said it, and he said the price before he asked and he said it in a room and both of those are the things he has done every time for twenty-two days, and it did not help, and he went down the hill at the change with a leg that will not clear and four bells went behind him and a runner went up it with a piece of paper and no name on it.
