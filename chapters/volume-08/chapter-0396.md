# Chapter 396: What A Door Costs, Said Out Loud At A Door, Before The Doing

The sixth day of the seventy-eighth week was his hundred and forty-sixth morning and his forty-fifth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was sixteen weeks and one day old, and the fourth line of a return was on a press in a chamber four floors above a room, and the fourth line said that the difference between two kinds of ward in this city is a door, and nobody in Orison had ever written a list of the doors.

That was the morning's whole business and it took about an hour to find out that nobody could.

---

“*Then somebody has to write it,*” said the one with the chair nearest the door, who had come down the hill with three of the others and had not sat down.

“*Somebody has to write it,*” said Magistrate Rell.

“*Then it is a thing with a name at the top of it,*” said the clerk of thirty, from the end of the long table, “*and I will tell the three things that have to be true of it before anybody picks it up, and then nobody in this room is going to pick it up and I have said that in advance so that it is not a trick.*”

He held up three fingers the way a clerk does and put them down one at a time as he got to them.

“*One. It has to be written by somebody with a duty, because there is no form that lets anybody else do it, and a duty in this city is a magistrate or a bench of four, and a bench of four is four men who cannot be sent to nine hundred doors and would not go if they could, because they are a bench and that is not their employment.*”

“*Two. It cannot be the woman with the book. Say that out loud if anybody in this room is going to say it.*”

Nobody said it.

“*Two,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*It cannot be the woman with the book, because she keeps the book, and a list of doors that the keeper of the book writes is a list the keeper of the book can be asked about and cannot answer, and she is in two records already and one of them has my name at the top of it.*”

“*Three. It cannot be the woman who goes to the doors.*”

The woman of twenty-eight was standing at the end of the long table with her slate against her hip, and she had been in the room since the first hour, and she said: “*Say why, and say all of it, and do not do it to be kind.*”

“*Because there is one of you already,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and a person anybody can be asked about the doors is a thing this city has one of and one is enough, and a second one is a form, and there is no form.*”

---

“*Then it is me,*” said Magistrate Rell.

Nobody had asked her. She said it in the flat voice she used for everything, and then she put her pen on the table and left it there, which was the first thing she has done since the order went up that a clerk in that room has not seen her do.

“*I am not going to be praised for it and I am going to be argued with, and I would rather have both of those than a fortnight of nobody doing it, because on the day that the order ends on the date in the sixth part there is going to be a review, and a review of what, and there is nothing in this city to review but a roll and a board and a plate and a block in a gatepost, and none of the four of them has ever been to a door.*”

“*You have not asked whether you can do it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

“*I have not asked because I could not refuse,*” said Magistrate Rell, “*which is a different reason and I would like it written down that it is a different reason, and the clerk will not write it down because it is not a form, and I have been in this city since before you came down off a road and I have learned that the difference between two true things in this city is that one of them has a form.*”

And then the woman of twenty-eight said the price, out loud, in that room, in front of everybody in it, about a thing that was not hers, before the person she was saying it to had done it.

---

“*You are going to carry a list of nine hundred doors,*” she said, “*and I am going to tell you what that is, in the order I found it, and then you can do whatever you like with it, and I am not going to say it twice and I am not going to be thanked for it and there is nobody in this room who could thank me and there is nobody in this ward who ever will.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*The person who carries the list is the person anybody can be asked about the list. That is what a list is. That is the whole of what a list has ever been in this city and I have found that out in thirty-five days at nine hundred doors and you did not have to tell me anything, you wrote a fourth line on a return and I read it at the bottom of a row at the first hour this morning.*”

She put the slate down on the table, which she had not done in a room before.

“*There is one of those people in this ward and I am her, and I am standing in this room, and if there is a second one then one of us is a lie or one of us is a spare, and I am not going to be a spare and I cannot be a lie, and that is not an argument, that is just the two things that are left.*”

The clerk of thirty wrote nothing. Rell did not look at him.

“*Your name goes at the top of it. Not mine. Yours, because a magistrate is the only person in this city anybody can be asked about an order, and if your name is not on the list then the list is a thing with a kind of person at the foot of it, and that is how all of this started, and a man with a bad leg told you in a room that a name is a person on the morning you wrote it, and you were right, and I was not in that room and I have been told about it since.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*You will be asked about it every day. There is no form in this city that ends it. You have had an end in it since the day before yesterday and you did it and it is on a return in a press, and this is the part of the settlement that has no end in it, and I want you to hear those two sentences next to each other and I want you to hear them from me and not find them out in a year.*”

She picked the slate up again.

“*And if the fever comes back — and it will, you said so in this room two days ago and you were right and nobody has argued with you about it since, including me — then you will be standing in a row with a list in your hand instead of a pen, and the bench of four will be asking you what is on it, and there will be no order, because you will not have written one, because you will have been doing this.*”

“*Say the last of it.*”

“*Nobody can be sent for you. There is no runner and no tray and no line on any form in that building that will put a magistrate in a row, and that has been the shape of the whole time I have been writing orders in this city and it is the only good thing anybody can say about you. This ward has had one person nobody can be sent for since the eighth morning of the fever and she is fifty-eight and she is on a stool, and after this morning it has got two, and I do not know that is a good thing and I am the one who is saying it, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be forgiven for it either, and I have been at this thirty-five days and I have said one short thing at a door every morning and this is the longest thing I have ever said anywhere.*”

---

Nobody in that room said anything for a while, and the man with the bad ear was in it and had heard the whole of it, and a man with a bad leg was standing at the end of the long table and knew exactly what he had been looking at.

He had said a true thing out loud in a room, about a thing that was not his, in front of people, four days in a row to a woman he had met once, and he had never once known while he was doing it that it was a method, and he had taught it to nobody and had not been asked to teach it, and it had been standing in this room since the first morning he came down the row in a city with a fever in it, and he had never seen it until a woman of twenty-eight picked up a slate and put it down on a table and did the same thing to a magistrate for the same reasons, and neither of them said one word about it, and he was not going to, ever, and he understood that he was not going to because he was the third person in the arrangement and there was no form for him in it.

Magistrate Rell picked her pen up off the table and put it in her bag and did the thing that was left for her to do, which was to not be kind.

“*Say the number of doors,*” she said.

“*No,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, and she was not angry and she had it ready, which he had expected and had told her about in advance and had been wrong to expect anything else. “*A number of mine is a number about how many doors I have been to and a door I have been to is a visit, and a visit is the only thing in this city that is not a shilling, and a visit is not a thing a person can be held to. You cannot ask me how many and get a figure that means a person agreed to something. The round is the keeper's and I am not going to quote it at a door and I am certainly not going to quote it to a magistrate in a room.*”

“*Then who owns the number.*”

“*Nobody,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*That is the answer and it is the correct one and it is the only one in this city, and the woman with the book has the figure and she is a mile and a half down that row and she does not come up here for me, and you can ask her, and I am not going to tell you what she will say, and I am not going to be thanked for any of it and I have said so four times in two minutes and I would like to say it once more at the end.*”

Nobody thanked her.

Magistrate Rell said: “*Then I will ask her, and I will write down what she says and I will not put a word to it, and I will carry what she gives me and I will not choose what is on it, and if she gives me a figure I will carry the figure and if she gives me a sentence I will carry the sentence, and I am telling you in this room that I am not going to end up with one hand on this and one hand on the pen, because the settlement says two hands on the order and there is only one order and it is not finished.*”

She stood up, and put the bag on her shoulder, and said the last of it to the room and not to the man with the leg.

“*Nobody is going to thank me and I am not going to wait for it, and I am not going to be forgiven and I am not going to ask for it, and I have lost the right to end a city by myself between the first hour this morning and the second, and I still have the pen, and I have the work, and the work is a list of nine hundred doors and a person who will not give me the number, and I did not ask for it, and I am not going to be able to put it down, and there is no form in this city that can.*”

She was at the door when the clerk of thirty said, in the flat voice, without looking up: “*Magistrate. The line at the top of a return is filled from the paper in front of me, and I fill it from the paper and not out of a man, and I have been doing it for nine years, and there is nobody in this city I can fill one out for.*”

And that was all he said, and he did not say it kindly, and she looked at him, and neither of them said anything else, and she went out into a row with nothing in her hands, and the fever was sixteen weeks and one day old and did not stop for any of it.
