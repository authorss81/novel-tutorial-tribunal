# Chapter 390: Seven Things On A Table, And A Row That Is Not A Join

The last day of the seventy-seventh week was his hundred and fortieth morning and his thirty-ninth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was fifteen weeks and two days old, and there were seven things on the long table in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour, and there were nine people in the room, and none of them had been asked to come because there is no form in this city that calls a meeting of anything that is not a ward.

The order was on the wall behind them with nine inches of blank under it for a notice that is not the order. The bench of four is upstairs and has not come down.

He put them down one at a time and he did not join them, and he said so before he started, and nobody in the room argued with him about it, and that is the whole of what happened in that room on that morning.

---

“*I am going to put seven things on this table,*” he said. “*Every one of them is true. None of them has ever been in this room at the same time as any of the others. I am not joining them and I am not going to pretend at the end that I have, and I want the clerk of forty to write that sentence down first, because it is the only one I am certain of.*”

The clerk of forty wrote it down without looking up.

“*One. Four numbers off four walls and two doors that would not open. Two, four, eleven and thirteen. Two and four is six, and six and eleven is seventeen, and seventeen and thirteen is thirty. Thirty beds free this morning in four halls out of six, and the other two halls have nobody in this city who has read their boards, and the two in this list with a cross beside them are spoken for according to a woman on a stool who was not asked to be asked.*”

---

“*Two. Nine hundred doors, and a round of thirty doors a fortnight, and four hundred and twenty days to get round them, and a year here is three hundred and sixty-four, and the fifty-six days over that are the part nobody has ever paid anybody for.*”

“*And who is going?*” said the boy of fourteen.

“*That is the fourth thing,*” said Ilyan, “*and I am not going to get to it for a bit, and I am telling you that I am not getting to it, so that you know I have not forgotten it.*”

---

“*Three. A line in chalk at the foot of a board at the end of a run of boards, and a second line on the same board in a different hall, and the man who wrote the second one did not rub out the first one and has said so out loud to a room before anybody asked him.*”

The man with the bad ear said: “*That is mine and I will answer for it. It is chalk. There is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one, and I have been asked and I have said no and I was right to say no.*”

“*Four. Nineteen lines in chalk in a column in this ward, with nothing over the top of it and nothing at the foot of it, kept by a man who is thirty-one and is paid nothing and has been keeping it since the second week of the fever.*”

---

“*Five. A roll of a ward with a lamp on it, and two names in it that cannot be put right. One of them belongs to a person who is alive and in a hall at the top of this city and is at a shut door in a row above a plate yard, and one of them belongs to a person who died in the fourth week of the fever and the name is correct and he is dead. And three hundred names in four low wards that are on a paper and not on a plate, which are not a gap in anything, because a hall does not need the roll of its ward, and which are a second way of keeping a record, and there is nothing for a second way of keeping a record to be a mistake about.*”

The woman with the book, at the far end of the long table, said nothing at all, and she had walked a mile and a half to be in the room and she had not opened the door for it either, and there was a paper in the wrong hand folded in four in the inside pocket of a coat, and nobody in the room had been told that and nobody in the room was going to be.

---

“*Six. Four records that make a ward's record a record, and they do not agree with each other. A plate at the low end of the row, and a paper, and a keeper, and a block in a gatepost at the end of a passage with a mark on its face that nobody in this ward can read, and the second clause of a paper I filed four days ago says that the four of them are one record and that where they differ the paper is the account and the plate is the working.*”

“*There is no meeting of the ward,*” said Bevin Tarr, from the end of the table, where he had come up a mile and a half with a plate under his arm, because he could not leave a plate.

“*There is no meeting of the ward,*” said Ilyan, “*and there has not been one in Tallowgate since the autumn before the water came, and there is no form in this city that calls one, and a ward is not a person and cannot be asked anything.*”

---

“*And seven. One order, on that wall, with six parts, and a date in the sixth line with the word standing in the margin beside it so that the date does not come, and three sentences underneath it, and the sixth part is an exit and not a refusal, and it is not a refusal now and it was not one on the day it went up, and nobody in this city has one of either.*”

The clerk of thirty read the sixth part out. He did not have to be asked. He read it in the flat voice of a man reading a thing he has read before, and he read the date and he read the word in the margin, and nobody in the room said anything, which is what a room in this city does at the end of the order.

---

And then the man with the bad ear stood up, which he does when he is going to say the half of the objection nobody else has thought of, and he said: “*That is seven things. I have been in this ward for two months of a fever and in every room I have been in, and I have never once had two of them in the same room, and I want to say the thing that is true about that and I want to say it flat because you have asked me to and I am not going to make anything of it.*”

“*Say it flat.*”

“*They have all been true at the same time. All seven of them, every day, for longer than a week, and not one of them has ever been in a room with another one of them, and the reason is not that anybody is hiding anything, and there is no man in this city who could tell me that anything has been hidden from me, and I have looked.*”

He put his hand flat on the table, on nothing, next to the second thing.

“*I have kept a column of nineteen with nothing over the top of it and nothing at the foot of it since the second week, and I have wanted a heading over it every day of that, and nobody has ever asked me what the words over it would be, and there is no form in this city that asks a man for a heading. And there are seven things on this table and not one of them is a heading.*”

Nobody in the room answered him.

---

The man of fifty-two had come up the hill too, and he said the thing that is the finding of the whole of it, and he said it the way he says everything, in a street, in a room, without weight.

“*There are nine people in this room and a bench of four upstairs, and there are about forty people in this city who live under all seven of those, and there is not one of the seven that anybody has ever been asked about,*” said Tarin Keel. “*And a room like this one is where they would have to be. That is the whole of it and I have been counting for nineteen years and I am telling you that the number of the things is seven and the number of the rooms is one and the two numbers are not the same kind and the thing that is missing is not a number.*”

“*Say what is missing,*” said the clerk of thirty.

“*Somebody whose job it is to ask,*” said Tarin Keel. “*And there is no post in this city that has the care of a piece of paper on a wall, and there is no post in this city that has the care of a board, and there is no post in this city that has the care of a column of nineteen dead, and there is no post in this city that has the care of a roll, and a man at a wheel and a woman with a lamp and a man on a stool are three people in a city of ninety thousand who are holding four records in agreement by hand, and they cannot be asked about it, because being asked about it is not their employment.*”

---

So the seven things stayed on the table for about an hour, and people came and looked at them, and nobody in the room connected any of them to any of the other ones in front of anybody, and he watched that happen and did not help it along, and it was the hardest thing he has done in thirty-nine days and it cost him nothing and bought him nothing and he has said the price of that out loud in this city six times.

The bench of four did not come down. There is no form in this city that says a bench must come down, and there is no form that says a room must be called, and nobody sent for anybody, and at the change the room emptied the way a room in this city empties, and the seven things were still on the table when the woman of twenty-eight came past the door with a slate against her hip and no chalk in her apron, and she looked in, and she did not come in, and she went on down the row.

And a boy of eight was in a bed at the top of this city, and had been there a fortnight and two days, and was better, and could not be discharged, and his line was in chalk at the foot of a board at the end of a run of boards and a second line was in chalk on a board at the top of this city, and nobody had said his name out loud in any room in this city in his life, and his mother would be at a standpipe in the morning, and the four bells went over the low end of Tallowgate and the fever was fifteen weeks and two days old and it did not stop for a table with seven things on it.
