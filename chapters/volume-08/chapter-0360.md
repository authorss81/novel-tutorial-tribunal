# Chapter 360: The One Panel, And The Price He Said Out Loud Before He Paid It

It was his hundred and tenth morning and the fourteenth was nine days off, and he had a sheet in his coat with four clauses and a half on it and a fifth one that was wrong, and he was going into a yard at the low end of Tallowgate at about the second hour to do one thing, and he had been awake since the first bell working out how to do it without a form.

There is no form in this city that says a man may put a person's name into a record, and there is no form anywhere in four hundred miles that says a person may be taken back out of one once it is there. He has spent seven volumes learning that. He has also learned that in a country the second half of it is nearly harmless, because a record of a name is a piece of paper and a piece of paper is a piece of paper.

In a city it is not a piece of paper. In a city a name is the only handle there is.

---

There were nine people in that yard at the second hour and they were there before he was and they are all of them people of Tallowgate.

The woman of twenty-eight was at the door with her bundle of string and her slate and nine days of chalk. There was a man of thirty-one with a bad ear who keeps a column of nineteen names with nothing over it. There were two men from the low ward who had been on the books of a hall and were sleeping at the Ninth Weir. There was a boy of about sixteen who works in the yard next to the plate-cutter's. There was a woman of sixty with a shawl. There were two more and they were both in the street and they stayed.

Nobody had been sent for and nobody had been asked to be there.

He did not say the name. He is careful about that for the rest of his life and he will be asked about it once and he will give the true answer, and the true answer is that he did not say the name, he asked a question, and a person of a place answered it in front of nine people, and he spent it four seconds later.

“*Who keeps the roll of this ward.*”

It is the plainest question anybody asked in Tallowgate that week. He asked it in the voice of a man who has been in a city three days and has a leg, and there was nothing in it, and the woman of about twenty-eight looked at him and understood the whole of it in two seconds, which he could see her do, and then she said:

“*She is called Fenna Rusk. She is forty-four. She has been in that room behind the chandler's since before I came to this ward, and if you want me to tell you the rest of it you can ask me on a different day, because there are nine people standing here and four of them cannot get in out of a street this cold.*”

---

“*Stop,*” he said.

Nobody stopped, because he was not talking to the room.

He stood in the middle of a yard in a low ward and he said the price out loud, before he did it, which is the one thing he has done three times in a hundred and ten mornings and has never once done wrong on purpose.

“*I am going to use that name. In about an hour I am going upstairs in that building and I am going to say it there, and it is going in a book, and when a name is in a book the person is somebody who can be asked about, and after today the only person in this city anybody can ask about the roll of this ward is a woman of forty-four who has never asked to be anybody's answer to anything.*”

He waited, because he has waited before, and the difference this time was that he was waiting in front of nine people in a yard instead of in front of a kitchen.

“*Here is the price and I am saying it out loud so that it is hers and not mine. In about an hour the people who wrote the order are going to have the one thing they do not have, which is a person. They will be able to ask her whether the roll is kept properly and they will be able to ask her all day, and she has been doing the work of nine people for eleven years and she is on four shillings, and there is no form in this city that will take her out of it afterwards, and it is me that has done that, and I have not asked her, and I am not going to be able to give it back.*”

Nobody said anything. The man with the bad ear looked at the ground. The boy of sixteen did not understand any of it and stayed, which is the correct behaviour for a boy of sixteen in a yard.

“*Now say it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

So he said it. He said *Fenna Rusk* out loud, in a yard, in front of nine people, at the second hour of a morning, in a ward he had no business in, on a country where his own name is on a sheet he has never seen.

That is four times in a hundred and ten mornings. The first of the four cost a woman of thirty-four at a bar eleven miles off a lane four hundred miles from here, who was asked for a sentence and said *go on* and then said it back in her own mouth and worse, and he stood at that bar in the last week of that country and neither of them said one word about any of it, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that lifts her out of it.

And the thing he learned about it in a yard in Tallowgate, standing in the cold with nine people, is the second half of a thing he has known since a kitchen, and the second half is this. A person who is in a record is not only somebody who can be asked about. A person who is in a record is the only thing about that record that can be asked about. Putting a name in does not open a thing up. It closes it down to one person, and it picks which person, and if the person is right then the thing can be defended for ever by the person who made it.

He put Fenna Rusk in a record in order to attack the roll, and in doing it he handed the roll's defenders the only handle they needed, and he said the price out loud first, in a yard, to nine people, and nine people heard it, and it did not stop him, and that is the whole of what happened and it happened at the second hour on a morning with no market in it.

---

He left the yard and went up the hill and did not go to the roll-room, and that was the only part of the day he is proud of.

The chamber is on the second floor and it is a room with a table and four chairs and a box of blackglass plates in a press at the end of it, and the chamber records what it is sent. There is one clerk in it at half past eleven.

“*You are on the sheet,*” said the clerk.

“*I know that I am on a sheet. I have not seen the sheet.*”

“*No,*” said the clerk, who was thirty, and who was the man from the public room with the four hundred entries in the afternoon, and who had read an order aloud to a stranger on a second day and had not enjoyed it. “*You have not seen the sheet, and your name is on it, and it is not your name that put it there. It is mine. There was a written request for a witness and it came down from above with a room on it and I entered two names off two documents that were in front of me, which is what I do, and the second document is the one that says who can speak to a board that came from somewhere else, and it had a name on it and I wrote it out, and I have not put anybody's name on anything in this city in three years that was not off a document in front of me.*”

“*Can I take it off.*”

“*No,*” said the clerk. “*And there is no form that says a man may take his own name off a piece of paper once it is on it, and I have looked, and I looked properly, and I am telling you that because you would have asked me and because I would have had to say no anyway.*”

He said it without any pleasure in it at all.

“*There are nine things in this chamber that have not been sent to it,*” he said, “*and there is a column on the wall for them and it is the only column in this building that anybody reads, and yours is not in it.*”

---

At about the noon the light in that room came in at a low angle off the river and lay along the edge of a blackglass plate the clerk had half out of a sleeve, and in his sight, plainly, once, and not a second time that day, was this:

**CLAIM: A roll of a ward is one record and the order treats it as one.**
**EVIDENCE: The paper has one thousand five hundred. The plate has one thousand three hundred. The block in the gatepost weighs the plate as it was two years ago.**
**BURDEN: Somebody must go to the doors. There are nine hundred.**
**CHOICE: Amend the roll, hold the order, or name the person who keeps it.**

It was four lines and it hung in his sight clean and useless, the way the only other one he has ever seen hung in his sight, which was a long time ago and in a different room, and he had not missed them and had not known until this one arrived that he had. It did not say what amending the roll would cost, and it did not say who would go to the doors, and it did not say when the order ends.

He read it twice. He did not choose anything out of it. He has never chosen anything out of one of those in his life and he is not going to start, and there is no instrument in this city that can make a man choose and there is one at the end of the third line of it that he had already done at the second hour of the same morning in a yard, on his own account, to nine people, having said the price out loud first.

Nobody in that room remarked on the fact that it had been shown to a stranger with a bad leg who was on no list of anybody. There were four people on the floor below and nine clerks in the building and a bench of four upstairs who had written a request for a witness, and the question had been put to a man on a slate and not to any of them, and he noticed that, and he did not ask, and there is no way on earth to ask it without a room, and he had promised a woman of twenty-eight not to say one word about anything to anybody, and he was going to keep that before he went and did anything else.

He wrote one line on the back of a candle bill that evening, under the fifth clause, in a hand that was not his best, and there is no hand in this country he could have given it to, and it is not a question.

---

The roll-room door was shut when he came back at about the eighth hour and there was a light in it.

She opened it, and she was not surprised, and she had been in that room since the second hour and she had not gone out, because there are nine people at that door most days and she does not leave a room with a book in it.

“*They came at the fifth,*” she said. “*A woman from the magistrate's office and a man with her, and they had a paper, and they asked me eleven questions, and nine of them were about the paper and the plates, and two of them were about me.*”

“*What were the two.*”

“*Where I was born,*” said the woman at the table, “*and whether I had ever been paid anything other than four shillings a year.*”

She stood with her hand on the door and she did not ask him a single question about what he had done, which he had earned, and which was going to cost him more than anything else he has ever done.

“*You said the price out loud in a yard,*” she said, “*which is a thing I have heard about from two people already today and one of them was a woman of twenty-eight and one of them was a man of thirty-one with a bad ear, and neither of them had any business knowing what I said or what you said, and both of them told me anyway, and that is the shape of a city and not the shape of a fault, and I am not going to stand here and be angry with you about it, and I have got nine people at this door and it is nearly the last of the light.*”

“*I know.*”

“*Then sit down for four minutes,*” she said, “*because you are going to be in a room on the fourteenth with me in it, and there is a bench of four and nine clerks and a magistrate and a room of forty people, and I am going to be asked things for a whole day, and you are going to have to sit in that room while I am asked them, and that is the price, and I have been told it already, this morning, in a yard, by the man who charged it.*”

He sat down for four minutes. She went back to her table. At about the ninth hour he left, and the door was shut behind him, and nobody in Tallowgate said anything about a name, because nobody in Tallowgate had been in the black building and nobody in the black building had come to Tallowgate, and those are two separate facts about a city of ninety thousand people and they are the whole of what the tenth morning of his hundred and ten cost.

The four bells went. The fever did not stop. Three hundred names were outside a protection that a magistrate put there on purpose, and there were nine days between that and the fourteenth, and nine hundred doors, and one woman with a book and a slate, and one woman with a board of a hundred and forty marks and no names on it, and one man with a bad ear who keeps nineteen names with nothing over them, and a boy of sixteen in a yard who did not understand any of it and stayed.
