# Chapter 361: A Hearing That Cannot Be Produced, Held Anyway

It was his hundred and eleventh morning and the sixth day of the seventy-third week, and the fourteenth was eight days off, and he went up the hill at about the second hour to do the one thing a man in a city can do that a man in a country cannot, which was to put a thing into a record by saying it out loud in a room with a clerk in it.

He had a sentence ready at the bottom of the hill. The sentence was about a book at the back of a chandler's shop in a low ward and it had been ready since the first bell.

He got it out in the public room at about the fourth hour and it took him four minutes and a clerk of thirty wrote nothing at all.

---

The room is open all day and there are four people in it at any time and two of them are clerks, and on that morning there were five, because it was the hour when the runners come in from the wards.

He stood at the end of the long table and he said it plainly, because he had learned in ten days that there is no arrangement in this city for saying a thing more than once.

“*I would like the roll of Tallowgate read out in this room.*”

The clerk of thirty put his pen down, which is a thing he does before he answers a thing and does not do before he answers a thing he is going to refuse.

“*That I cannot do,*” he said, “*and I want to be exact with you about why, because you have come in here twice and you have never once asked me for something I could not do.*”

“*Read rules to people.*”

“*Read rules to people,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*That is what the room is. A rule that governs you is read to you, and the reading is a record, and the record is why the room is here and not a room with a man in it being helpful. The roll of Tallowgate is not a rule. The roll is a book. There is nothing in this building that reads a book out, and I have looked, and the reason there is nothing is that a book is a list of people and a list of people is a thing a man can carry.*”

He said the last four words the way he said the order in four hundred entries a day, which is to say without any weight at all, and then he looked up, because he knew what the four words were for.

---

“*Then put a question about the roll to the bench of four.*”

“*I can put a question about the roll to the bench of four,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and I will, and it will go up this afternoon in the tray with the nine other things, and it will be read out in a chamber upstairs by a man who is not me, and four people will look at it, and here is the whole of what four people can do about a roll.*”

He put his hand flat on the table, the way a man does when he is about to be helpful and knows it.

“*They can order the roll produced. They can order a person asked questions about the roll. They can order the roll read in this room, which is the thing you asked for and which I have just told you is not a thing I do, and they cannot order it, and I have seen them try about a water.*”

“*They cannot do the other things.*”

“*They cannot go to nine hundred doors,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*A bench of four does not do doors. They cannot weigh a block in a gatepost, because that is not a thing anybody in that room knows how to do, and because a block is not a record, it is a weight, and there is no proceeding about a weight. And they cannot make a ward send them anything, and that is the one that matters and I will come to it.*”

---

He came to it.

“*A thing that goes up from here goes up from a person or from a body. A person goes up with a complaint. A complaint about a book is a thing that happens to a person who keeps a book and not to the book, and I will tell you why that is, because it is not the rules being unkind, it is what a complaint is. A complaint is a thing somebody is doing to somebody. You are asking me to send nine hundred people up to a room because a book is correct.*”

“*The book is not correct,*” said Ilyan. “*The paper has fifteen hundred and the plate has thirteen hundred and the block says what it said two years ago, and two hundred people are outside a protection because of it.*”

“*I know that,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*I wrote a fair hand about it in the seventh morning of your being here and it took me eleven minutes and a man at a rail corrected a figure in it. It is in a book on the second floor. Nineteen people have read it in the four days since the morning of the seventh day. I am one of them and it is the correctest thing anybody has said in this room since the order went up.*”

He stopped, and it was not for effect.

“*And nothing came of it, and I want to tell you why, because you have come in here with a face on you and you have not found anything, and the reason you have not found anything is that there is nothing to find. The roll is right. The roll has been right every day for eleven years. A person who is not on the roll is not on the roll because the roll is wrong. The roll is right, and being right is what stops anybody being able to say anything about it.*”

---

“*Then who sends it.*”

“*Nobody,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*That is the answer and it is the reason you came four days on a road for, and I have never said it out loud in this room because there has not been a reason to until now.*”

“*Say it properly.*”

“*A ward sends matters. A ward is a body when a ward says it is a body, and a ward says it by meeting, and a meeting of a ward is called by a form, and there is no form in this city that calls a meeting of a ward. There has not been one in Tallowgate since the autumn before the water came. So Tallowgate cannot send the bench of four anything, and cannot be asked to, and cannot be fined for not doing it, because a body that cannot be asked to do a thing cannot be fined for not doing it. A person can send a complaint. I can put yours in the tray. It will go up this afternoon and it will come back down in a fortnight with a mark on it, and the mark will say that a person has asked about a book and that the book is kept properly, and that is a true document and it is worth exactly nothing to the two hundred names on a paper and not on a plate.*”

He said the last part to the table.

“*And there is a form for sending a matter up and it has a line at the top of it for who is sending it, and I am not going to enter your name in it, and I am not doing that as a kindness to you. It is the one rule I have got and I break it about twice a year and not in this.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because you have not put it in front of me,*” said the clerk of thirty.

---

He stood in the middle of a public room for eleven seconds.

“*There is no way to make this hearing,*” he said.

“*No.*

“*Not by you and not by her and not by a bench of four and not by the magistrate.*”

“*Not by the magistrate either,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*I will tell you that because you will go and ask her and she will tell you the same thing in a better coat. There is no form. A magistrate can write a notice about a fever. She cannot manufacture a form for a meeting of a ward, because a form is a thing this bench makes and there are about nine thousand of them and none of them is that, and if she wrote one in her own hand it would be a notice with a magistrate's name at the foot of it, and then it would not be a meeting of a ward, it would be a thing a magistrate came to.*”

Nobody thanked the clerk and the clerk was not thanked. He went back to his tray.

---

Here is the rest of it, because a chapter about a thing that cannot be done is not finished by the not-doing.

He went down the hill and across the low ground and along a mile and a half of Tallowgate at about the sixth hour, and the woman of twenty-eight was at the end of it with a bundle of string and a slate and a piece of chalk, and the chalk was the whole of nine days she had bought with a penny and had not used any of it yet, and she had been in this ward before he came into it.

She did not ask him anything. He told her what the clerk of thirty had said, and she listened to the whole of it standing up, with her back to a wall.

“*Say the part about the form again.*”

“*There is no form that calls a meeting of a ward.*”

“*There is not,*” she said, “*and there has not been one in this ward since before the water came, and I know that because my mother used to say that a ward was a street with a book in it and nothing else, and she was not being clever.*”

“*Then it is held anyway.*”

“*It is not held,*” she said. “*It is nine people in a passage agreeing with each other, which is free, and I have been in this city nineteen years and I have watched men do it four times and it has never once moved a block.*”

And then she did the thing, and she did it without asking him, and he understood afterwards that she had been waiting two days to see whether he would understand it.

She took the chalk out.

She went up the row and she put a mark on nine doors, and the mark was not a word and it was not a figure and it was the same mark on all nine, and it meant one thing, which was: be in the passage behind the second house at the change. She did it in an order she has not written down. She did not tell him the order and he did not ask and he could not have helped if he had.

It cost her a penny's worth of chalk and there is no form for a mark on a door.

---

Nine people came, and every one of them had been at the door they were asked from, and nobody had been sent for.

The woman of thirty-four or so from the standpipe, who is the head of her own house and has been nineteen years. The man of thirty-one with the bad ear who keeps Tallowgate's column of nineteen dead with nothing over it. The woman of sixty with a shawl. The boy of sixteen who works in the yard next to the plate-cutter's. Two men off the books of the low hall who were sleeping at the Ninth Weir. A man and a woman who had been in that hall and were out. And a householder he had not met, who lived nine doors from a door he had been inside twice.

They stood in a passage behind two houses and it was not warm and there was a smell of lime off the low ground.

They agreed about everything.

The book was right. Two hundred of her names were on a paper and not on a plate. The block in the gatepost says what it has said for two years, which is a mark cut into a face, and nobody in this ward can read it and nobody has read it since the day it was cut. Four shillings the hundred would fix two hundred names, which is eight shillings, and there is no money in this ward and has not been for two years, and the two hundred that were cut in those two years were paid for out of two women's own hands and neither of them would say what for.

A meeting of a ward that is not called by a form is not a meeting.

And the block does not get re-weighed, and here is why, and he said it and he said it out loud because he had said a price out loud in a yard yesterday and had learned exactly what that does and does not do, and this was not that, because there was no woman in this passage and nobody in it was going to be a person in a record tonight.

The block gets re-weighed when a form says so. A form has a name at the top of it. A name at the top of a form is a person, and a person is somebody who can be asked about the block for the rest of their life, and every one of the nine people in that passage understood that in their own way and in their own time, and one of them had understood it before he arrived because it had been said out loud in a yard at the low end of this same ward on his tenth morning in this city, and two of the nine had been in that yard.

So the nine people stood in a passage and agreed that the block was wrong, and did not change it, and did not put a name on anything, and went home.

That is the whole of what was done in Tallowgate on that afternoon and it was the first of its kind in four hundred years of that ward, and nothing came of it, and not one of the nine said anything to him about it, and he did not ask.

---

At the door on the way back up the row, the woman of thirty-four asked him a question, which was the first question anybody in that ward had asked him in eight days, and it had nothing to do with the fever.

“*Are you going to write any of this down.*”

“*No,*” he said.

“*Right,*” she said, and went in, and the door was shut, and that is the whole of what he had been given by ten days in a city, and the four bells went, and the fever did not stop for the passage or for the form or for the mark on nine doors, and three hundred names were still outside a protection that a magistrate put there on purpose.
