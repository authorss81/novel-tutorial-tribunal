# Chapter 364: A Doorway, And Two Men With Incompatible Methods

It was his hundred and fourteenth morning and the second day of the seventy-fourth week, and the fourteenth was five days off, and a man of about fifty-two came down off the hill into Tallowgate at about the second hour and stood in a doorway until the stranger came down the row, and neither of them had been asked and neither of them sent for.

Tarin Keel had a piece of lead in his coat and chalk on the back of one hand, and he had been counting what came through the door of the low hall at the end of Tallowgate since the second week of the fever, and nobody had asked him to, and he had told nobody to stop him, and both of those were the worst part of it.

“*I have been at the low hall at the end of this ward four days,*” he said, “*and I am here because your man with the bad ear says I am the only man in this city who counts anything that is not in a book, and he is right, and he said it to me in a doorway and he did not have to.*”

“*What are you counting.*”

“*People coming in,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and people going out, and the number of beds free on a board, and I put the three of them side by side this morning and the three of them do not agree, and they did not agree yesterday either, and there is no form for putting three numbers side by side, and nobody has ever asked me to.*”

---

They stood in the doorway because it was the one place with a door in it that a man with a leg can stand in, and because neither of them would stand in a street for eleven minutes at the second hour in this cold, and neither of them said so.

“*Say your thing,*” said Ilyan, “*and then I am going to tell you why it is right and why it is wrong, and you are going to say the other half, and then one of us is going to be standing in this doorway for no reason at all.*”

“*The notice,*” said Tarin Keel.

---

“*There is a magistrate in this city who can write a piece of paper in ninety seconds,*” he said, “*and I know that because I have watched one do it, and I have watched four do it, and it is the fastest thing that has ever happened to me and I have been doing this for nineteen years.*”

“*What paper.*”

“*A notice that a meeting of the ward is called,*” said Tarin Keel, “*on a day, at an hour, in a room. And when there is a notice there is a meeting, and when there is a meeting there are nine people in a room because there have to be nine, and when nine people are in a room the block in the gatepost gets re-weighed, because that is the only thing a meeting is for, and then the plate and the block say the same thing again, and then the two hundred names on your woman's paper and not on Bevin Tarr's plate are two hundred names that are on the plate, and it is eight shillings, and eight shillings is the whole of it.*”

“*And the notice has a name on it.*”

“*The notice has a magistrate's name on it,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and that is what I am telling you and you have known it since you came down this row, so do not waste my morning on it.*”

---

“*Then say the rest of it and I will tell you why it does not work.*”

“*It works,*” said Tarin Keel. “*That is the whole of my argument and I have had it for seven days and I have been waiting for you to be in a doorway where you cannot walk away. I will go up the hill this evening and I will say it to her in ninety seconds and she will write it, and it will be on a board in this ward by the morning, and there will be a meeting on the day she names, and I have watched this exact thing happen in a place two hundred miles from here and it took nine days from the corridor to the block and nobody in the room understood what they were agreeing to.*”

“*Say the last part of that sentence again.*”

“*Nobody in the room understood what they were agreeing to,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and I have never understood it either, and it is the only thing I have that you have not got, and I would like it noted that I have noticed the shape of this conversation and I have not enjoyed it.*”

---

Ilyan put his hand flat on the doorframe of somebody else's house, which is not a thing he did often, and which Tarin Keel watched him do without comment.

“*It does not work,*” he said, “*and here is the whole of why, and it is one sentence and you have heard it before in a room with nine people in it. A notice with a magistrate's name at the foot of it is not a meeting of a ward. It is a thing a magistrate called. And when a magistrate calls a meeting of a ward, the meeting is not the ward's, and the nine people in the room are not nine people who have come together, they are nine people who have been called, and whatever comes out of that room is whatever the person who called it says comes out of it, and she wrote the order. She wrote the order in the third week of the fever with five parts and a half and a word in the margin, and the three hundred names in four wards that are outside that protection are outside it because of a line she left empty on purpose, and she is right about that, and if she calls the meeting of this ward then this ward gets a block that agrees with a plate because a magistrate said so, and it agrees for as long as she is magistrate.*”

“*You have said that to me before in a room and I agreed with none of it.*”

“*Because the document outlasts the man,*” said Ilyan. “*That is what I said in the public room on the seventh morning and I have not stopped saying it, and I have not stopped hearing the other half of it either. In nineteen years you have gone to nine counties and in six of them the one person who could do the thing was gone by the time anybody had checked, and I have never been in nine counties in my life, and I learned that from a man at a rail, and I do not think it is a bad argument. A document in a book upstairs does not go anywhere. A magistrate's notice on a board in a ward does not go anywhere. And if the magistrate goes out of office in a year, the notice is still on the board and the meeting is still a meeting of a ward and nobody is going to be able to say who called it.*”

“*Yes,*” said Tarin Keel. “*And the two hundred names are still on a paper.*”

“*Yes,*” said Ilyan.

---

Here is the whole of the disagreement and it is four sentences long and neither of the four sentences is wrong.

Tarin Keel's method is a thing said to the one person who can act, by morning.

Ilyan's method is a thing said in a room, in front of other people, which becomes a document, and a document is a place somebody can be asked.

Tarin Keel's is faster and it works more often than it ought to, and it is made of one person, and when that person is gone the thing is gone with them, and the people it was for are the last to know it was ever going to happen.

Ilyan's is slower and it is made of a book, and a book stays, and a book is only as good as the room it was said in, and the room is full of people who did not ask to be in it and one of them is a woman of forty-four who keeps a roll and did not consent to anything.

They both know that. They have both known it since the seventh morning, in a public room, with a clerk of forty writing one of it down in a fair hand.

And neither of them is going to win, and this doorway is not going to say which one of them is right, because both of them are right, and the answer to it is not in this doorway and will not be.

---

“*I am going to do it in the morning,*” said Tarin Keel.

“*You are going to do it in the morning.*”

“*I have said that I am going to do it in the morning and I am going to do it in the morning, and you are not going to talk me out of it, because I have been right about this nine times and wrong about it once, and the once was four years ago and there is a village in it, and I have not been to the village in a while and I am not going to talk about the village.*”

“*I am not going to talk you out of it.*”

And then Tarin Keel asked him a question, and he asked it the way a man asks for a thing to be carried, and it was the first time in this city that he had asked anybody for anything at all.

“*How do you know a corridor is faster than a room.*”

“*I know it because I have been in nine counties and six of them did it and it worked,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and you know it because you have never once been in a corridor with anybody who could do a thing, and both of those are the same ignorance with a different coat on. That is the whole of what I have for you and I have not got any more of it.*”

“*Then say the last of it.*”

“*The last of it is that a corridor needs a person at the end of it and a room does not, and that is the whole of the difference and there is no third thing between the two of them,*” said Tarin Keel. “*A room in this city has a clerk at the end of it who writes down what was said in it and it goes in a book upstairs. A corridor has a person at the end of it who can do a thing by the morning. That is all of it, and I have been saying it badly for seven days because I have never before had to say it to a man who already knows what a clerk is.*”

---

And then they stopped standing in the doorway, because a doorway is a place to say a thing in and not a place to be in, and they went up the row together at about the seventh hour, and the row is a mile and a half long and there is nothing at the end of it, and he asked the other question about four hundred yards from where the first one had been in a lane four hundred miles back, where he had told himself in a second voice that he was not going to ask it, and he had said that to himself twice since.

“*What were you the only person in.*”

“*There was a gate with a levy behind it once,*” said Tarin Keel.

He said it to the middle distance, in a street, in the cold, to a man walking beside him, in the voice of a man reading out a line off a list he has read before.

“*A levy on loads. The loads were counted by a man who was not paid. There is a dead man in the fourth line of that list and the dead man stays dead, and there is no road behind that gate and there never was one, and there are eleven feet that stay where they are, and there is a second notice of five shillings and fourpence that nobody has ever paid, and that is the whole of it.*”

He stopped, and he did not stop at a doorway.

“*I am not going to say another word about it,*” he said. “*You did not ask me, and that is the only reason I have said it, and I have said it in nineteen years three times and twice of them were in a doorway and once of them was in a counting, and this is the third, and it is in a street, and I am fifty-two years old and I have never once said it in a room.*”

“*I am not going to ask you about the village,*” said Ilyan.

“*I know you are not,*” said Tarin Keel. “*That is the only reason I am walking up a row with you instead of counting a door. If you asked me about the village I would have gone back up the hill and I would have been right to go, and I have not gone.*”

---

And that is the whole of the morning, and here is what came out of it.

Neither of them changed his mind. Tarin Keel went up the hill at the change and put the lead back in his coat and stood at a rail outside the black building and counted what came in, which is what he has done every day since the first morning of this city and which he has been useful at and hates.

And Ilyan went down the row and held a door open in the cold for a woman of twenty-eight who did not ask him a single question, because the argument in that doorway was that both of the things they do are correct, and he had been thirteen days in a city where the only person who could decide between them was a woman of forty-four with a book, and she was not going to be asked, and he was not going to ask her, and neither was Tarin Keel.

The block in the gatepost was still wrong at the last of the light. The two hundred names were still on a paper. The notice was not written, and there was no notice, and no meeting, and nobody had been thanked, and the four bells went, and the fever did not stop for a doorway either.
