# Chapter 395: A Bell At A Landing, And A Hand That Is Not Hers Alone

The fifth day of the seventy-eighth week was his hundred and forty-fifth morning and his forty-fourth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was sixteen weeks and no days old, and the return came down the hill in the tray at the second hour and the clerk of thirty read it out in the public room at the foot of it standing up, in the flat voice, and there were nineteen people in the room and the order on the wall behind them.

“*One form,*” he said. “*Written by me on the second floor. Four lines and a heading, and I am going to read the heading last, and I am telling you that I have put it last on purpose and that I have been argued with about it.*”

---

“*One. An order may be added to. An order is a magistrate's and it is written in a magistrate's hand, and that has not changed this morning and is not changed this morning. And an order does not stand until it has been read into the sitting of a bench of four and entered on a return, and a return has a line at the top of it for the name of the person who wrote it and a line for the name of the person who read it, and both of those lines are filled, and that is the second hand, and it is not a magistrate's and it never will be.*”

“*Say the second hand out loud,*” said the man with the bad ear, who had been in a chamber four floors above a room for the first time in his life that week.

“*A bench of four,*” said the one with the chair nearest the door, and he had said it himself yesterday and he said it again standing up with his hat in his hand, because he was the kind of man who says a thing twice when it is going on paper. “*Four men, and one of the four can stop it, and that is the whole of the second person in this settlement and I am not going to stand in a room and tell anybody that four is a person. It is four, and it is better than one, and it is not a person and it will not be.*”

“*Two. The sixth part of the order is filled in. There is a date in it and the word standing in the margin comes off, and the order stops on that date, and on that date the whole of it goes back to being an argument that has to be had in a room with the people in it, and there is no form in this city that says an order has to be argued again, and I have had this morning's return printed and copied and the copy is going into a press in that building, and it is the first return this city has ever had printed.*”

“*A copy is a place somebody can be asked,*” said the man of fifty-two, from the end of the table.

“*A copy is a place somebody can be asked,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and that is why I printed it and not you, and it is three pence at a scrivener and a penny a page, and it is in a press upstairs and it is a penny a page, and nobody has ever in this city been able to put that in a room, and it is on the return because a bench of four asked for it and not because anybody in this room asked for it.*”

“*Three. A review. Any person of the ward may ask for a thing to be read to them, which is the oldest form in that building, and a person of this ward may use that form about the order, and if the thing to be read is the order then the order is read in this room, and a bench of four is at this table, and anybody who comes is not asked who they are and not asked why they came.*”

That last one was her, and she said it herself, and she said it from the end of the long table with her pen out and her bag on the floor beside her, and nobody in the room thanked her and she was not thanked.

---

Then the clerk of thirty read the fourth line, and the fourth line was short, and it is the only line anybody in that room has ever been given.

“*Four. Where a rule in this city is enforced in some wards and not in others, and the difference is a door, and no document in this city mentions a door, then the difference is a finding, and the finding goes over the column of nineteen, and the nineteen is entered on this return, and it is entered with the finding over it, and the finding is the finding and the nineteen is the nineteen and neither of them is the other.*”

He read the heading last, and the heading was the whole of it.

“*The room, the street and the book are three places and one of them has never been in the other two.*”

The clerk of forty wrote it fair on the same morning, and the clerk of thirty read the fair hand back to the room, and then the man with the bad ear stood up and said the thing that everybody in that room was waiting for him to say, and it was not what anybody expected.

“*That is not a heading,*” he said.

Nobody said anything.

“*That is a sentence, and it is a sentence about a ward, and it is not about the nineteen,*” he said, “*and it is the only words anybody in four hundred years is going to put over nineteen dead in this city, and I have wanted them every day since the second week of this fever, and I am going to have them, and I would rather have them and be angry about them than have nothing, and I want it read out in this room a second time, and then I am going back down the row to a board.*”

The clerk of thirty read the heading out a second time, because a man in that room asked him to, and that is a thing a clerk of thirty does four hundred times a day and has never once done twice for the same line.

And the man with the bad ear put his hat on and went down the row, and the words went over his nineteen in chalk that afternoon because there was nothing else to write them in, and he wrote them small, and they took up two lines, and the woman on the stool read them from where she was sitting and did not say anything, and she is going to say the same short thing at that door every morning for the rest of her life, and the nineteen are at the end of it now and are not in it.

---

The woman with the book came up the row at the fifth hour and did not open the door for it, and he was in the room and had told her in advance on the morning he filed the paper that he was going to do it anyway, and she had told him not to tell her either way, and so nobody sent for her and she came and she stood at the end of the long table with her hands behind her back.

“*Say the whole of it,*” she said, and he made the clerk of thirty read the return out again from the top, all four lines and the heading, and she listened to it standing up.

“*Then that is what it is,*” she said, when it was done. “*And nobody has shown me a bill for any of it, and I am not going to ask in a room at the foot of a hill, and I am forty-four, and I have been in two records since a man with a bad leg put me in one, and there is a third thing coming and I can see it coming, and it is a thing with my name at the top of it, and I am not going to be thanked for any of the three of them and I am not going to be forgiven and I am not going to be relieved, and I would like it said in this room that I said all of that first.*”

“*It is in the fair hand,*” said the clerk of forty.

“*Then it is in a book,*” said the woman with the book, “*and I have been in this city thirty-one years and that is the first time anybody has written down a thing I have said in a room before somebody else has said something, and I am going to go down that row now and I am not going to wait for the rest of it.*”

She went down the row, and the room let her, and nobody thanked her.

---

And then the part of the morning that nobody has ever put in a return, because returns are not for that, and the woman of twenty-eight said it and it was the finding and it was also the wound, and she said it standing in the doorway with her slate against her hip and no chalk in her apron.

“*You have given this ward a way to ask for a review of that order, and the way to ask for it is to fill in a form and be in this room at the second hour on the day it is read, and I have been in this room, and I know what is in it, and what is in it is a long table and a clerk and forty people who came because a boy of sixteen said it in a doorway.*”

“*That is how a room works,*” said the man with the chair nearest the door.

“*That is how that room worked this week,*” she said, “*and there are people in this ward who will not be in that room on that day, and one of them is a woman of sixty-eight whose name is at a shut door in a row above the plate yard's and who is in a bed at the top of this city, and she is not going to be in this room, and if somebody in this city writes a review of this order and asks whether the ward agrees, the answer on the paper will be whatever the people in the room say, and she will be counted in it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*So that is what the settlement is,*” she said. “*It has an end and it has a review and it has a second hand on it, and the way to get at all three of those is to be in a room, and that was true of the order before you made it better and it is true of it now that you have made it better, and I am not thanking you for it and I am not saying it to be unkind, I am saying it because I am the only person anybody can be asked whether the doors are being gone to, and I cannot refuse, and so I am the one who has to say it out loud, and I have been saying things out loud at doors for thirty-four days and nobody has ever once asked me whether I wanted to.*”

Magistrate Rell put her pen down on the table, and nobody saw her do it and nobody has ever said that she did.

“*Write it in the return,*” she said. “*Not as a concession. As an objection that is mine, in my own name, against a settlement I am bound by, and put it under the fourth line and not under the heading, because the heading is about nineteen dead and this is about a woman of sixty-eight and they are not the same thing.*”

“*That will make the return two returns,*” said the clerk of thirty.

“*Then it will be two,*” said Magistrate Rell. “*I am not going to hold the pen up for one afternoon of the whole time I have been writing orders in this city and I am not going to pretend that a settlement which reaches the people in the room is a settlement. Write it and let the second one be ugly.*”

He wrote it, and the clerk of forty copied it fair, and the two returns went up the hill together in one tray, and nobody sent for the runner, and a bench of four is four and not a person, and the woman of twenty-eight is the only person in this ward anybody can be asked about the doors and she cannot say no.

The low ground was under water at the sides again at the noon bell and the bell at the landing was rung for the ferry, which is the sound a thing makes in this city when the way across is shut, and it was rung once and it was not rung twice, and the four bells went over Tallowgate, and the fever was sixteen weeks and no days old and did not stop for a return, and nobody in that room was thanked.
