# Chapter 384: A Man Of Fifty-Nine With A Wheel, And A Name That Goes In For Nothing

The first day of the seventy-seventh week was his hundred and thirty-fourth morning and his thirty-third in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was fourteen weeks and three days old, and the yard at the low end of Tallowgate had a plate on the wheel at the second hour with two hundred and eleven lines in it, every one of them cut by the man who owns the wheel, and nobody in this city had paid for one of them in two years.

Bevin Tarr had the candle bill in his hand before the man with the leg was ten paces into the yard, and he had had that bill three days and had not been able to get it out of his head, and he had not read it, because he can read a line and he cannot read a hand.

“*You are going to read it to me again,*” he said, “*and this time you are going to read the third one twice, because I have had it three days and I have not been able to get anywhere with the third one and I do not send a wheel back for nothing.*”

So he read the third one twice.

---

“*Right,*” said Bevin Tarr, and put the bill face down on the bench, which is the way that yard treats a thing that has been read out and is being worked on.

“*A name said by a person of the ward goes in without a fee. That is the third one. I have been cutting this yard's lines for thirty-one years and the money for a line has come out of a woman's own hand twice in thirty-one years and both times out of two women, and neither of them would say what for, and there has not been a year in this yard in which I did not cut at least one.*”

“*The money for a line is the part you told me on the sixth day of the week before last. The third one is new this morning and I have not had it until you read it out.*”

“*That is what I told you and it is a price and a price does not move,*” said the plate-cutter, “*and what has moved since then is that there is going to be no price, and that is the first good thing anybody has done for this yard in two years and I have not been able to be pleased with it for about an hour and a half, so I am going to tell you exactly why, and you are going to stand there and take it, because you have had that paper in a coat for twenty-six days and you have read it out in my shed once and you have not come here to give me anything.*”

He turned the wheel a quarter and stopped it.

“*Without a fee means without a line. That is what it means. A fee on a plate in this ward has always been a line, and a line has always been the record of a person being in this ward, and that is the only thing a line has ever been in my yard. You have written down in a fair hand that a person can be in this ward for nothing, and if he is in it for nothing then he is in the book and not in my yard, and then the second clause you have written makes the book the account and my yard the working, and the working is not the account, and it stops at the next meeting of the ward.*”

“*There is no meeting of the ward.*”

“*There is no form in this city that calls a meeting of a ward and there has not been one in Tallowgate since the autumn before the water came,*” said Bevin Tarr. “*So the working stays behind and the account goes on without it, for ever, and the third thing that makes a ward's record a record — the block in the gatepost at the end of the passage with a mark on its face that nobody in this ward can read — gets weighed at a meeting that is never going to happen, and the mark says what it said two years ago, and it has a mark on it that says something and there is no money in this ward and has not been for two years and nobody has ever been told what the mark says.*”

---

He took a plate off the rack and put it on the wheel and set it up with his thumb the way he has done four thousand times, and then he did not cut it, and he left it there.

“*And here is the part that has taken me an hour and a half,*” he said, “*and it is not the money and it is not the block. It is that you have made the book more important and not less, and you have done it by making it free, and I want you to sit with that for a minute because a stranger from four hundred miles off has just made the woman in the room at the back of that shop the most important person in this ward and he did it in a clause he thinks is a gift.*”

He looked up.

“*A name may be put in by any person of the ward who can say it. So who decides whether a person of the ward is a person of the ward. Not the clerk, not the ward, not a form, not a meeting. The person who keeps the book, because the person who keeps the book is the only one anybody in this city can ask. And so every name that goes into that book now comes in free and comes in on the word of somebody in a street, and the whole of what stands behind those two hundred and eleven names in front of you is one woman and a lamp, and that was true before you wrote it and it is truer now, and she is on the same four shillings a year she was on two days ago, when you asked her what one day of a round was worth and she told you what a year of it came to.*”

---

“*Say the part about me,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have not got a part about you,*” said the plate-cutter. “*I have got a wheel and I have got a plate with two hundred and eleven lines in it and nobody in this city has needed a line cut since the morning a sheet came down a hill with three sentences in it, and I have been told what a line is for, and I asked out loud in this yard whether I was to stop and I was answered by nobody and I went back to the wheel, and I would like it noticed that I went back to the wheel and not to a door.*”

“*It is noticed.*”

“*Then the rest of it is that you have written five clauses about a ward and not one of them is about a plate, and there is a man in this shed who has been the working half of this ward's record for thirty-one years, and your second clause has just said in a fair hand in a room that the working is not the account, and that is a licence, and I am going to take it, and I am not going to be thanked for taking it, and nobody is going to tell me it does not matter, and I want both of those in the yard again this morning so that they are in it.*”

“*They are in the yard.*”

“*Right,*” said Bevin Tarr.

Then he did the thing he does at the end of a morning, which is to take a plate off the rack and set it on the wheel and cut one line, and it took him the better part of ten seconds, and then he turned the plate round so that a man with a leg could read it, which is a thing he has not done for anybody in thirty-one years.

It was a name, and he knew it, because he cuts the roll of this ward and the roll of this ward is the paper on the table in the room at the back of the shop, and he cut that line in the autumn before the water came and he was paid for it out of a woman's own hand and she would not say what for.

“*There,*” he said. “*That is what you have put on a table in a room, and it is not a figure. It is a name in glass that I cut eight months ago for money out of a woman's hand, and it is the only line on this plate that anybody in this city has had any use for, and there are two hundred and ten more behind it that nobody has needed since the morning a sheet came down a hill with three sentences in it.*”

“*What would I have to have to say to you to make you stop.*”

“*Nothing, and that is the answer and it is the same one you got,*” said Bevin Tarr. “*There is no form in this city that says a man of fifty-nine shall stop cutting plates and there is no form that says he shall not. I asked out loud in this yard and I was answered by nobody and I came back to the wheel. I am going to keep on coming back to the wheel until somebody with a duty tells me not to, and there is no post in this city with that duty, and if there were one, and if it were filled, and if it said stop, I would stop on the morning it was said and I would not argue about it, because I am fifty-nine and I have been doing this for thirty-one years and I am tired of being the reason a record is a record.*”

He put the plate back in the rack.

“*And you have put a woman in a book and I have been a man in a yard, and neither of us was asked, and I have had your five clauses in front of me for three days and the only part of them I have any feeling about is the one that says a name goes in without a fee, and I want you to understand what that is, coming from me and not from anybody else. It is the first line anybody has cut in this yard in two years that anybody might ever need, and it cost nothing, and it went in on your say-so, and I did not have to do one thing about it, and I am the man who gets to say that out loud and I am not going to say it twice.*”

---

And he cut, for four hours, with a wheel in a shed at the low end of a low ward in a city of ninety thousand people, on a morning of the seventy-seventh week, with a plate in front of him that nobody had ordered and a mark in a gatepost at the end of a passage that still said what it had said two years ago, and he is not relieved and nobody has forgiven him anything and he has still not been told what a line is for, and by the time he had finished the four hours he had not cut a name of anybody who was dead or gone, because he does not do that and nobody ever asked him to, and that is the only part of his trade in this city that is not in anybody's book.
