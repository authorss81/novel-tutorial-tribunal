# Chapter 351: A Road, And A Board The Size Of A Door Going The Other Way

There is no figure for this morning and there is not going to be one in this country, and the reason this time is not a ladder and a wall at the end of a lane. A weight is a weight in the county the plank is in. The plank is on the north wall of a tower in a village two days of road behind him, and he has been out of that county since the second morning, and a stroke does not travel on a road. There is no figure on any wall between here and the city. There is not going to be one.

It was his hundred and first morning, and he had been four days on a road with a leg that is the leg, and the road was the first thing in a long while that had cost him nothing at all.

---

A carter came up behind him at about the fourth hour with a cart and a beast, and on the back of the cart, under a strap and a sacking, was a board the size of a door.

He did not ask about it. The carter did not offer it. They walked a mile and then the carter said it, without being asked and in the flat voice of a man handing over a fact that is not his.

“*That is a board of nine things with a map on it and I have got no opinion about one line of it, and I am taking it to a city because a city wrote and asked for it and paid the carriage, and the carriage is eighteen pence a day and this is the fourth day, so it has cost whoever sent me seventy-two pence, and the board is not heavy and the road is the expensive part.*”

Nobody had told the carter what was under the city. He said that in the same breath, before it could be asked.

“*Nobody has told me and I have not asked and I am not going to. I have a beast and four days and I am going to be at the gate at the second light tomorrow and the board is going to be where the man who wrote to me said it is going to be, and that is the whole of what I know about it, and I would be obliged if you did not make it into a thing.*”

The carter was fifty or so and had a bad cough and was not a fool. He had read the board on the top of the sacking where one line of it showed, and he had read nothing else, and he had a beast to feed and four days of road behind him and one of them done.

---

The gate into this city is a bar across a road and two posts and a hut, and it is shut, and it is not shut because of a war or a debt or a plague in the ordinary sense. There are nine people standing outside it at about the seventh hour in the shade of the western post.

They are not waiting to be let in. They are waiting to be counted, and the counting is the gate's whole work this morning, and it is being done by a man of sixty or so with a book on a board across his knees.

The book has a line for a name in it.

“*Stand off the step,*” he said, before anything else, to the nine, and then to the stranger, in the same voice, because it is the voice you use on a road. “*If you are on foot you are a traveller and a traveller is a line and a line is a name and where it is going.*”

The nine at the post did not move off the step and did not say anything, and one of them is a woman with a bundle and a child asleep against her shoulder and the child has the fever and has had it for two days, and there is nowhere on this side of the bar for a child with the fever, and the man of sixty knows that and wrote it down this morning, and he cannot do anything about it with a book on his knees.

Then he looked at the stranger and read the line out loud, which is what a man does with a form in front of a person who has not seen one.

“*Your name and the ward you are going to.*”

The stranger did not give him one.

It took him two seconds, and in those two seconds he went through everything he has not been able to do for a hundred and one mornings, and the thing he went through was not the rule. It was the arithmetic of what a name on a line turns a man into, and he has a book under his arm about a hundred miles behind him with two hundred and nine names on a wall and no figures and no heading, and a sack in nine hands with nine things on it and no name at the foot of it on purpose, and a woman at a bar eleven miles off a lane who is a person in a record because he said a price out loud before he did it, and he is not going to be the reason there is a name on a line in a city because a man of sixty with a cough needs a line filled in.

The man of sixty read the shape of the refusal and did not argue with it, and he did not write anything, and he did not ask twice.

“*Then you are not on the book,*” he said. “*I am going to let you in and I am going to tell you that I have let you in and nobody asked me to, and I am going to tell you that you will be at this gate on your way out, because everybody goes out of this gate eventually, and I will ask you then.*”

He put his thumb on the line and left the line empty and the four days of the fever did the rest.

“*Go on. Keep to the middle. There is a fever in the two low wards and it is in Weir Lane and in Tallowgate and it is not in Cresset Street, and if you have come to work you will find about a third of it is shut, and if you have come to a person you will find them cold, and I am sorry about both of those and I am not able to do one thing about either of them and I have a book.*”

---

There is no figure on any wall in this city and there is no ladder and there is no woman of forty-one on one, and by about the ninth hour he understood the shape of the place, which is that it is a low place on a river with nine bridges over it and one black court at the middle of the nine, and that a man coming in off a road in this city has about ninety thousand people around him and every one of them has a paper of some kind, and that he is the only one of them who has none.

The man of sixty said *about* and meant it, and the book on his knees has a column in it for the morning and the column is not finished and will not be finished before the last of the light.

There is a fever in the two low wards and it is not in the four rich ones, and the man of sixty said that the way a man says a thing he has written down four times this week and cannot make come out differently, and it is the only sentence anybody said to the stranger in the first day that he wrote a line about, and he wrote it in his own head, and there is no hand in this country he could have put it in.

---

Here is the whole of the first day.

A board the size of a door went to a building of black stone at about the noon and came back at about the last of the light with nine things on it and a mark in the corner of it that is a clerk's mark and says that a city received a document and does not say what it is going to do with it.

A man of about fifty-two stood at a rail outside that building for about an hour and a half and said nine words to nobody, and he was a carter, and he was counting what came in and what went out, and he had been somebody in nine counties in nineteen years and nobody had sent him and nobody thanked him.

And a boy of eleven was carrying water past the stranger in the ninth hour from a standpipe that had a queue at it, and the boy was carrying it for nothing, because somebody gives him a penny at the end of the week, and he said the whole of his day in five words without being asked, and none of the five were about the fever.

It was his hundred and first morning, and for the first time in a hundred mornings there was no figure on any wall he could reach, and there was a figure of a kind on a book at a gate that had a line in it for him, and the line was empty, and it was not going to be filled in by him.
