# Chapter 358: A Draft, And The Reason He Does Not File It

It was his hundred and eighth morning, and he wrote a thing, and the thing was four clauses and a half, and it is the first one he has ever written and it is not the last, and he has not told anybody in this city that he wrote it and nobody asked him about it and one person has read it.

He wrote it on the back of a chandler's bill for candles, because that is what was on the bench in the yard at the low end of Tallowgate, and he wrote it with a stub of pencil that was blunt, and he wrote it in forty minutes, sitting on the bench where plates are cut, with a man fifty-nine years old cutting two hundred lines of somebody's in front of him and not asking what he was writing.

---

Here is the whole of it.

*ONE. Every person sleeping in the four low wards is inside the protection of the order from the moment the order stands, and not from the moment a roll has their name in it.*

*TWO. The paper and the plate of a ward are one record, and where they differ the paper is the account and the plate is the working, until a meeting of the ward has weighed the block again.*

*THREE. A person may be put into the record of a ward by any person of that ward who can say the name, and a name said by a person of the ward is entered without fee.*

*FOUR. Somebody must go to the doors.*

*FIVE — and this is the half, and it took him forty minutes to get to it, and there is a line struck through before the fifth clause and not afterwards — the somebody in the fourth is the keeper of the roll of that ward.*

There is no sixth line. He did not write one, and he noticed that he had not written one, and the reason he had not written one is that the fourth clause is the end of the order as far as this goes, and the only person who can be in it is the person who keeps the book.

It is a good draft. He has been doing this for seven volumes and he knows the difference between a draft that will not hold and a draft that will hold, and this one will hold: the four clauses are plain, the words are the words people in that ward use, it costs almost nothing, and it can be read out in a room in four minutes.

It is also the first one he has ever written that is finished, and he has been in a hundred and eight mornings in this country and a hundred and seven of them had nothing in them, and this one has a person at the bottom of it.

---

He went back to the roll-room at about the fifth hour and she was still at the table, and he said what he had come to say, which was that he had written four clauses and a half and he was going to read them to her and he was not going to file them and he wanted her to tell him the fifth one was wrong.

He read them. She let him get through all five and she did not interrupt him once, which is a thing about her and not a thing about the draft.

“*Say the fourth one again,*” she said.

He said it again.

“*And the fifth.*”

He said it again.

She put her pen down and turned round in the chair, and she did it slowly, and when she spoke she was not angry and that was much worse.

“*You have put me in it,*” she said.

“*Yes.*”

“*Not in a clause. In a document. There is a difference and you know there is a difference, because you have come four hundred miles to not do that to anybody and you have done it to me on the back of a candle bill at about the fifth hour with a stub of pencil and a man cutting plates in the next shed.*”

“*Yes,*” he said.

“*Say why. Not the reason for the draft. The reason you put it in a clause.*”

“*Because a draft has to name a burden,*” he said, “*and there is no other person in this city who can be the somebody in the fourth, and I have been in seven days and I do not know eleven names in four wards and I could have learned eleven names in seven days, and I did not, because learning them would have taken the seven days I spent learning about plates.*”

“*You did not ask me,*” she said. “*That is the whole of what you have done and I am going to say it the way I would say a figure in a book and I am not going to be talking about it afterwards. You have written a document with my work at the bottom of it and you have read it to me after you wrote it, and there is no form in this country that will take me out of it, and there is no form in this country that will take you out of having done it, and both of those are true and I am the one who is in it.*”

---

She was quiet for eleven seconds, and then she said the thing he has carried for seven days, and she said it because he had asked her a question, which is the only way anybody in this city says a true thing.

“*Nobody has ever shown me a bill for that,*” she said. “*That is the whole of my answer and I have been trying to get to it since you came through that door. Eleven years of going to thirty doors a fortnight and nine hundred doors, and not one single person in this city has ever said to me: here is what it costs, here is the bill, here is what you are worth. I have been paid four shillings a year, which is forty-eight pence, which is about a farthing a day and not quite, and I have never once been able to tell anybody what the work is worth, because I have never seen it written anywhere, and if you had come in here on the second morning and put that in a document then I would have known what my work was worth, and I would have said: it is worth that, and I want that, and pay me that.*”

“*I have written that in a clause. Somebody must go to the doors. The keeper of the roll.*”

“*And you have not written a penny in it,*” she said, “*and you have not written a year, and you have not written that it is a year and fifty-six days, and you have written the person and left out the bill, and I have been left in a document with my name at the bottom of it and no figure, and there are four hundred years of that in this city and I am not going to be one of them in a sheet written by a stranger on a candle bill.*”

He looked at it for a while.

“*Then tell me what to put in it,*” he said.

“*I am not going to tell you what to put in it,*” she said, “*and that is not modesty and it is not a refusal to help a stranger with a bad leg, and I want it written down that I understood the difference, because you have been in a country where everybody says the modest thing. Here is what is the matter with it. You have come in here and you have found a hole in a city three hundred people wide, and you have written the only thing you could think of to fill it, and it is me. And in nine days somebody will be sensible and say: she is the keeper, of course she is the keeper, it was always going to be the keeper, and there was never any question about it. And that will be said in a room by somebody who has never been to a door, and it will sound reasonable, and it will be a thing that is true, and I will be the person who goes.*”

“*You might not.*”

“*I will,*” she said, “*and I will do it well, and I will not be thanked, and I will be forty-five next year and I will be fifty when the round is done, and that is the whole of my answer and you may write it down or not.*”

---

“*I am not going to file it,*” he said.

“*No,*” she said. “*You are not, because you would have to put your name to it, and you are not going to put your name to anything in this city, and I have watched you not do it at a gate and I have watched you not do it in this room and I did not think anything of it until about a minute ago, when you handed me a sheet and did not put your name on it, and I understood the whole of the arrangement at once, which is that you will not be a person in this and she will, and you have found that out in seven days and I have been finding it out for thirty-one.*”

He said: “*You are not going to be in it.*”

“*I have already been in it,*” she said, “*and the way out of it is the same way as the way into it, which is that somebody with a duty has to decide that a thing is not true, and there is nobody in this city with a duty, and you have said that out loud in a room two days ago and it is in a book upstairs, and I have read the book, because everybody in this building reads that book, and it is the first time in my life that anything anybody said about me is in a place I can be asked about.*”

He put the sheet on the table, face down, and then turned it over and looked at it, and the fifth clause was in his own hand and it was legible and it was short.

Then he folded it in four and put it inside his coat, and he did not tear it, and that was a decision and not an impulse, and the reason was that in about a week there was going to be a child in a hall in this ward who was worse than the others, and he had not the faintest idea what he was going to do about that, and he was not going to do it without the piece of paper in his coat.

Nobody thanked her. She was not thanked. He was not thanked for asking her, which is the first time in a hundred and eight mornings that asking somebody has cost him nothing at all and it did not feel like anything, and he took that with him and it did not improve.

---

Here is the whole of what he had learned by the last of the light, and he learned it in a shed and a room and it did not go in a book, because it was not said in a room, and he has been in a hundred countries' worth of rooms and he knows exactly what a thing has to be said in to become a record.

A burden named in a draft is not a burden that has been agreed to. It is a burden that has been made visible, and being made visible is not the same as being carried by choice, and what naming it does is put a person underneath it so that the paper shows a person did it.

That is what he has spent seven volumes learning, from the other end. A name at the foot of a thing is the only part of it anybody can be asked about. A name at the foot of a thing is a person. A person is somebody who can be asked about, for the rest of their life.

He has built a document with a person at the bottom of it and no figure beside the person, and the person read it and told him what was wrong with it in her own mouth in her own room, and he is not going to file it, and he has put it in his coat, and the reason he is going to need it again is that he has not the faintest idea what else to do.

That is the whole of the hundred and eighth morning. It is not a resolution and it is not a discovery. It is a man with a bad leg and a sheet in his coat walking back across a mile and a half of low ground with this city around him, of whom three hundred are outside a protection that a magistrate put there on purpose, and the number of them is not going down, and the four bells went, and the fever in the two low wards did not stop for anybody.
