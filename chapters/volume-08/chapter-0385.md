# Chapter 385: The Flaw, Said Out Loud, In A Room, Before The Doing

There were nine people in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour on the second day of the seventy-seventh week, and he counted them himself from the long table before he said anything, and the order was on the wall behind the long table with nine inches of blank under it for a notice that is not the order, and he stood in front of the blank and said the price out loud, and it was the fifth time he had said one in this city and the first time he had said one about a thing he was about to do.

The fever was fourteen weeks and four days old. The paper in his coat was twenty-seven days old and folded in four with two holes in it where the pencil had gone through, and he put it on the long table face up, and the clerk of forty moved the book off that part of the table without being asked, and the clerk of thirty put his pen down, and the boy of fourteen stopped doing whatever he was doing with a string at the end of the room.

Nobody left. That is the thing about a room in this city at the second hour and it is the only reason anybody has ever said anything useful in one.

---

“*I am going to read out a piece of paper and then I am going to say what is wrong with it, and then I am going to file it, and I have written the wrong part of it down at the top of this list before I start, which is that I am the man who filled in the sixth part of the order on that wall and everybody in this room knows that, and everything I say this morning is going to land on top of that, and I would like the people who came in here to hear me say that first. And I have counted this room, and there are nine of you in it, and I know what that is worth, which is nothing, and I would rather have said it out loud than go up a hill afterwards and find out that I did not know.*”

He said: “*It is a draft. Four clauses and a half. It is the first one I have ever written and it is on the back of a bill for candles and it is twenty-seven days old and it is the only thing I have got.*”

---

“*What does it do.*”

The man with the bad ear asked that, from the end of the table, with his hat in his hand, and he had come down off a stool at the end of a run of boards because the beds are his business and he is thirty-one and he is unpaid and owed a heading.

“*Three things. The first: everybody sleeping in the four low wards is inside the order from the moment the order stands and not from the moment a roll has their name in it. The second: the paper and the plate of a ward are one record, and where they differ the paper is the account and the plate is the working, until a meeting of the ward has weighed the block again. The third: a name may be put into the record of a ward by any person of that ward who can say it, and a name said by a person of the ward goes in without a fee.*”

“*And the two that are not about being in a ward.*”

“*Somebody must go to the doors,*” said Ilyan. “*And the somebody is the keeper of the roll of that ward.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*There. That is the whole of it, and I have read it out in a shed and in a yard and in a row and in two rooms and this is the fifth time and it is the first time in a room that writes things down.*”

---

And then he said the flaw, and he had thought about it for three days and he had it in the order he was going to say it in, and the clerk of forty wrote all of it down without looking up and his hand did not stop.

“*The third clause is a gift and it is the reason I wrote the thing. Three hundred names in four low wards are on a paper and not on a plate, and a plate is four shillings the hundred, and the three hundred is twelve shillings, and there is no money in this ward and there has not been for two years, and a name that goes in without a fee goes in without a plate, and the three hundred stop being a thing anybody has to pay for. That is the whole of the good in it and it is worth what I said it was worth on the fourth day of the week before last, which is nothing in shillings.*”

“*And the fourth and the fifth.*”

“*And the fourth and the fifth put a woman of forty-four in a document,*” he said, “*and in the enforcement of it, for four hundred and twenty days, and there is no third clause and no sixth line and I wrote a line under the fifth and it is not a question and I do not know what it is for.*”

---

He stopped and let the room have it, and then he said the part that is the finding and that nobody in the room had asked him for, and it had been in his own head for ten days and he had got it out of a yard and a wheel and a gate.

“*Now I am going to say the part that is wrong, and I want to be exact, because I have said a price out loud in this room before and it did not do anything, and the reason it did not do anything is the thing I am about to say, and I have known it for ten days and I have been carrying it round this ward waiting for somewhere to say it that writes things down.*”

Nobody in the room said anything.

“*Naming a burden does not stop the harm. It puts a person under it, so that the record shows that a person did it.*”

He said it once and he did not say it again.

“*That is what the fourth and the fifth are. They are not a mistake. They are the thing I have spent seven volumes learning, said back to me by my own hand on a candle bill at about the fifth hour with a man cutting plates in the next shed, and I am filing it on purpose, and I am not going to be able to put it down afterwards, and if the third clause does what it does then the three hundred get what they were owed and the only thing anybody will ever be able to say about that week is that a man with no standing put a woman of forty-four in a book so that he could do it.*”

---

The clerk of thirty said: “*Say the part about the three hundred out loud with the figures in it, because this goes into a fair hand on the second floor and a figure in a book is a figure anybody can check and a feeling is not.*”

“*A plate is four shillings the hundred. Two hundred names in one ward is eight shillings. Three hundred names in four wards is twelve shillings. A name that goes in without a fee goes in without a plate and the three hundred stop being anything anybody in this city has to pay for. I have had every one of those figures said to me in this city by two people who are not lying to me, and the first of them has her name at the foot of the fifth clause and she is sitting at a table a mile and a half from this room with a lamp on and six people at her door and she did not consent to any of it.*”

The clerk of forty wrote that down too, and the clerk of thirty said, “*Do not tell me what the clerk is writing. I have been in this room nine years and I know what she writes.*”

---

“*Then the price,*” said the chandler.

He sold candles at the end of the row in Tallowgate, and the paper on the long table was his paper, and he had not been asked what it was for, and he said that in the room in his own mouth, which is what a man does when somebody has said a price out loud in front of him.

“*Four hundred and twenty days,*” said Ilyan, “*at thirty doors a fortnight, over nine hundred doors, and a year here is three hundred and sixty-four days, and the fifty-six days over that are the part nobody has ever paid anybody for. And it is not hers to accept, because nobody in this city has ever shown her a bill for it, and she has never been asked what it costs. The third requirement of a thing like this is that the people who will live under it say yes, and I have not got that and I cannot get it, and I have not asked, and I am going to file it anyway.*”

The room was very quiet and somebody at the end by the door said *oh* in a voice that had nothing in it and then stopped.

---

And then the man with the bad ear said the half of it, standing up, in the flat voice he keeps a column of nineteen dead in.

“*You have said the price out loud five times in this city in front of people and the first four of them cost somebody something and this one costs a woman of forty-four four hundred and twenty days, and you have said out loud that saying it does not stop it, and you have said out loud that you are doing it anyway, and there is nobody in this room who can stop you and I am not going to try, because the thing you are filing is the thing that will get a name into a ward's record for nothing, and I have been keeping a column of nineteen lines in chalk with nothing over the top of it and nothing at the foot of it for a month, and after this morning there is going to be a fair hand in a room four floors up that has got a number in it that somebody in this room said out loud on purpose.*”

He sat down.

“*Say the next bit out loud and do not skip it. Somebody is going to have to be able to ask somebody about that number in a year and I would like to know who, and I would like to know it in a room, and not in a book.*”

---

Ilyan said: “*Me. I am the only person in this city it can be asked about, and I am going to have my name at the top of the first line of the fair hand, and there is no form in this city that takes a name off a page or a person out of a record, and I have known that since the first time a man with a tray said no to me about a name, and I have been using it, and I have stopped using it as of this morning.*”

And then he said the last of it, and it was to the whole room and not to the clerk.

“*And nobody is going to be thanked. Not the woman with the book and not the woman at the end of the row and not the man at the wheel and not the two of you and not me, and I have said that in this city before and I am saying it in a room that writes things down now.*”

The clerk of thirty picked up his tray and did not leave, because he has never left a room early in nine years and he said so.

“*I am not going to ask you for it this morning and I am going to tell you what I am going to do,*” he said. “*There is a form and the form has a line at the top of it and I fill that line from the paper in front of me, and there is nothing at the top of that paper, and when you put it in front of me in this room I am going to ask you for the name, and you are going to have to say it or not say it.*”

He put the tray down again.

“*There is no third thing, and nobody in this room is going to help you with it, and I have said it out loud before you do it so that it is not me afterwards.*”
