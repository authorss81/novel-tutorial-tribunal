# Chapter 365: The Third Requirement Of A Draft, And What It Is

It was his hundred and fifteenth morning and the third day of the seventy-fourth week, and the fourteenth was four days off, and there was no notice on any board in Tallowgate, and neither he nor the man of fifty-two at the rail said one word about that, and that was the whole of what had come out of a doorway, and the day was not about it.

The day was about a thing he had written on the back of a candle bill on the eighth morning of his being in this city and had put in his coat, and it was still in his coat.

He went up the hill at the second hour and stood under the blank nine inches on the notice board in the public room and said the price of it out loud, because that is the only instrument he has and it costs him and it is the only one.

---

The notice board under the order was still empty, and it was eight mornings since he had said twelve shillings and a pound three and sixpence out loud under it, and nothing had come of either figure, and he was going to stand under it again and say a worse thing.

There were seven people in the room. Two ward clerks. A man with a barrow who had come about a lane and not about the fever and who had been in the room on the seventh morning and had been in it on the first. A boy of fourteen. A runner in a magistrate's colour. A woman of sixty with a bundle and a child asleep against her shoulder. And a clerk of forty at the end of the table, whose job it is to write down what is said in that room, and who put his hand on the book before the stranger had got to the middle of the first sentence, because he has done this before.

---

“*There are three things a draft needs,*” he said, “*and I have two of them and I have not got the third, and I am going to say all three in this room, and it will be in your book, and I am saying it in this room on purpose and I have thought about that for two days.*”

The clerk of forty said: “*I am writing.*”

“*A draft needs witnesses. It needs a public cost. And it needs acceptance by the people who will live under it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I have the witnesses. There is a fair hand in a book on your second floor with three hundred names in it and a pound three and sixpence in it and nine people in this room on the seventh day of my being in this city, and a map with a clerk's mark in the corner, and a woman's name that I said in a yard in front of nine people on my tenth morning, and the yard heard it and the yard is not a record and the book is. Those are witnesses. That is the first thing and I have got it.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*I have the public cost. Twelve shillings of plate for the names that are on a paper and not on a plate in this ward, and a hundred and thirty-eight pence between that and a date at this bench, and both of those are in that book and both of those are true and a person with a duty could act on them tomorrow and will not, and that is not my business and it is the shape of the city.*”

He stopped there, and the clerk of forty waited, and the room waited, and a man in the room who had come about a lane put his barrow down against the wall, which is what a man does when he thinks he is going to be in a room for a while.

“*That is two,*” he said. “*And the third one is the reason I am standing here and the reason I have been in this city fifteen days.*”

---

“*The third one is acceptance by the people who will live under it,*” he said, “*and I have not got it, and I am going to name what it is, because it is a number and a number can be said out loud in a room, and the number is four hundred and twenty days.*”

Nobody in the room said anything.

“*Thirty doors a fortnight on about nine hundred doors is four hundred and twenty days. A year in this country is three hundred and sixty-four days. So it is a year and fifty-six days to go round the ward once, and every one of those days has the same person at the end of it, and the person is a woman of forty-four who keeps the paper roll of Tallowgate and is paid four shillings a year, and she gave me that working in her own mouth at the first hour two days ago and did not know she was giving me the price of a draft, and neither did I.*”

He let it stand.

“*So the third requirement is four hundred and twenty days of one woman's life. And a draft cannot bind anybody with that, and I am not going to write it down and call it a burden, because I have already done that once on a candle bill in a yard at the low end of this ward and I did it to the same woman and she told me what was wrong with it in her own mouth in her own room and I did not tear it up.*”

The clerk of forty wrote for about a minute and a half and then stopped and put the pen down and said:

“*You have said the third one out loud in this room.*”

“*I have.*”

“*Then it is in a book on the second floor and nine people in this room heard it, and there is no way anybody in this building can unhear it, and I would like you to know that I have not done anything to you by writing it down. I have done my job and I would have written it if you had not said it, four hours later, in a worse sentence.*”

“*I know,*” he said. “*That is why I am in this room.*”

---

And then a woman of sixty with a bundle said the thing that was the reason he had come up the hill, and she said it to him and not to the room, and he had not asked her and it is a fact about the room and not a want, and in a city a thing gets asked and this got asked, because he had been talking in front of seven people for four minutes when she decided she was one of them.

“*I am going to say a thing and then I am going to go,*” she said, “*and you are the second man in this city who has stood under that board and told me a true thing and then not asked me anything, and I am tired of being the woman in the corner of a room.*”

He said nothing, which is what the room required of a man who has just been spoken to by a person of a place.

“*My sister's boy is on the roll,*” she said. “*I put him on it. I stood at our door in the autumn and I said his name out loud to the woman with the book, and I said my own name and my sister's name, and there were four of us and there are four names, and I have never once wished there were five. And nobody asked me whether I wanted to be a person in a book and I did not need to be asked, and that is the whole of the difference between me and the woman at the back of the chandler's shop and you have been in this city fifteen days and you have not seen it and I would like you to see it now because you have just said her price out loud in a room in front of seven people and it is the only thing you have said in this city that was worth saying.*”

“*What would I have to do to see it.*”

“*Nothing,*” said the woman of sixty. “*You would have to ask her. And you are not going to ask her, and I know you are not going to ask her, because you said the price out loud in a yard five days ago and seven of the people in that yard have been in this ward since and I have heard four of them say so at a standpipe. And I am telling you that I would have said yes. That is the thing you have not got. You have got a woman who has said no to you about being a person in a record and a woman who would have said yes, and they are the same door, and you cannot tell them apart from outside, and there is no form anywhere in this city that asks a person which one they are.*”

She picked up her bundle and shifted the child against her shoulder.

“*My sister's boy has the fever,*” she said, “*and I am not going to stand in this room about it, and you are not going to ask me about it, and I am going.*”

And she went, and the room let her, and nobody stopped her, and she was not thanked and she was not relieved and she had not been thanked for the first one either.

---

The man with the barrow, who had been in this room twice and had come about a lane both times, waited until the door was shut and then said one sentence to the middle of the table.

“*You have three things and two of them are about a room,*” he said, “*and the room is where you put a thing so that it lasts, and the third one is about a woman, and the woman is where you put a thing so that it is true, and you have been in this city fifteen days and you have only just worked out which of the two you are holding.*”

He lifted his barrow and went out.

---

Here is the whole of what he had, said out loud in a room with a clerk in it, at about the fifth hour of the third day of the seventy-fourth week.

A draft needs witnesses. A draft needs a public cost. A draft needs acceptance by the people who will live under it.

He had two of them and the two of them were in a book.

The third one was four hundred and twenty days of one woman's life and a woman at a table had not been asked and would not be and could not be, because the only way to ask her is to say a true thing out loud in front of other people about a thing that is not his, and he had done that on his tenth morning in this city and it had cost her the only position in this city anybody can be asked about, and there is no form in four hundred miles that lifts a person back out of a record once she is in one.

So the third requirement could not be got. Not by asking, because asking is the instrument and the instrument is the thing that takes it. Not by waiting, because she goes round her own nine hundred doors once in a year and fifty-six days, and you have four days before the fourteenth, and after the fourteenth the whole of it is a year and fifty-six days and there is nobody in this city who will pay for any of it. Not by writing it down, because writing it down is what he did on a candle bill and she told him what was wrong with it.

And there was one thing left and he did it, and it cost him nothing and it is the reason there is any point to the room at all.

A copy is a place somebody can be asked. The clerk of forty's fair hand is a copy, and it is in a book on a second floor in the black building at the middle of the nine bridges, and on the fourteenth a bench of four is going to sit in a chamber and a clerk is going to read out what it is sent, and one of the things it is going to be sent is a fair hand containing a public cost, a difference of a hundred and thirty-eight pence, and a number that four hundred and twenty days of one woman is the price of a draft.

He did not need a copy of his own. He needed the copy that was made without him, in a room he was standing in, by a man whose job it is, out of his mouth.

The draft was still in his coat. He did not file it. He was not going to file it in this city at all, and the reason was a woman of sixty with a bundle who had walked out of a room and a sentence of nine words she had not been asked for, and he was going to be carrying the piece of paper for the rest of the volume.

The four bells went. The fever did not stop. Nobody in that room was relieved, and nobody in that room was thanked, and there was a copy in a book that said the third requirement out loud, and it was there whether he liked it or not, which is the only difference between this city and a country and it is not a small one.
