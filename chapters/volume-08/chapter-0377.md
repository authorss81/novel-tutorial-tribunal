# Chapter 377: A Man Of About Sixty At The End Of A Row, And Nine Words

The first day of the seventy-sixth week was his hundred and twenty-seventh morning and his twenty-sixth in a city he came to on foot, and it was the day he went to the end of the ninth row in Tallowgate, where there is a man who has been at the end of that row for fourteen years and whom nobody in that ward has asked anything in as long as he can remember, and who is about sixty, and for whose job there is no post in this city.

He has a bench, a board, and a shallow tin, and the tin is not medicine and he has never said that it is, and what he does at the end of that row is look at people and tell them what he thinks, and about half of what he thinks is that they need water and a fire and fewer of the other people in the room, and the other half is that he does not know, and he says the other half out loud, which is not what anybody comes to the end of a row for.

---

The woman of twenty-eight told him about him on his way down, without being asked, in the voice of a woman giving a direction.

“*That is the man the row calls a healer,*” she said. “*He has been at the end of that row since before you came into this city and I have walked past him two hundred times going to a door and twice going to a standpipe, and he has never once asked me a question about my work and I have never once asked him one, and that is how this ward works.*”

“*Is he a healer.*”

“*He is what this row has instead of one,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and I have had four people in my row on boards this month and he has been to two of them and not to the other two, and I asked him about the second pair on the fifth day of the week and he said the second pair were at the end of the row and would come, and one of them came and one of them did not.*”

---

He asked the question the way this city requires, which is that you ask it and then you stop talking, and he asked it at the third hour, standing at the end of the ninth row with a man of sixty in front of him and a bench between them.

“*What happens to a person who is in a hall and is worse than anybody else in it.*”

And the man of sixty looked at him for a while and then he said nine words, and he said them in the voice of a man reading out a line off a list he has read before, and he said them once, and he did not say them again.

“*The worse they are, the longer they keep it.*”

---

He said nothing else for a long time, and in that time a woman of about thirty came down the row with a child on her hip and stopped a few yards off and stood there, which is what people do at the end of that row, and the man of sixty did not turn round.

Then he said: “*That is nine words and I have said them and I am not going to say any more of them, and I am not going to explain them and I am not going to soften them, and you are going to want me to and I am telling you now that I will not.*”

“*I did not ask you to explain them.*”

“*You are going to,*” said the man of sixty, “*because that is what everybody does with nine words. They ask you what you mean and then you say it again in more words and it gets worse. What I have said is what a man says who has been sitting at the end of a row for fourteen years and looking at the low hall from about a mile and a half away, and the low hall is at the end of this same row and I have not been in it in three weeks.*”

---

“*Why not.*”

And here is the whole cost of him, and it took him a while to get to and he got to it and it was not a confession and it was not a defence and it was the same arithmetic everybody in this city has been doing for twenty-six days.

“*Because I have been asked three times this month to go up to that hall,*” he said. “*A woman on a stool in it sent a boy. A woman with a pail at a standpipe came down this row and asked me in the street. And a man with a bad ear at the end of the beds sent a boy as well, and I know about that one and I am not going to say who told me.*”

“*And you did not go.*”

“*I did not go,*” said the man of sixty, “*and here is the reason and it is a good reason and it is the reason you have been using on other people for three weeks, and I would like you to hear how it sounds coming out of a man of sixty at the end of a row. A man in this ward who is called in by name goes. And when he has gone in he is a person about a bed, and that is a thing a whole row knows by morning, and there is no form in this city that takes a man back out of being a person about a bed. And I have been at the end of this row for fourteen years and I have never once been in a room in this ward about anything, and that is the only thing I have, and I sat on it three times in three weeks and there are ten children in that hall and one of the ten is worse than the other nine.*”

---

He stopped there, and he did not go on, and the four minutes and the sentence are the whole of what he is.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan. “*Say it and then tell me why the nine words cannot be acted on, because I have been in this city twenty-six days and I have just used a rule that does something about the worst person in a hall, and I would like to know what your nine words have to say about that.*”

“*I have got nothing to say about your rule and I have not read it and I do not know it exists,*” said the man of sixty, “*and if I had read it I would not know what to do with it, and here is why, and it is your own arithmetic and I have watched you work it out at a gate at the end of this row for two nights.*”

---

“*Your nine words say the worst keep it longest,*” he said, “*and the rule you have put in moves the worst to a bed. And a rule that moves a person is not a thing that makes them better, it is a thing that makes them somewhere else, and somewhere else is where you go to be kept, because the beds in this city that are free are free because nobody comes out of them. So the rule works and the nine words are still true, and both of them will be true in a month, and there is nothing anybody can do with either of them, and I would like that written down somewhere by somebody who is not me.*”

“*Then say what would make the nine words false.*”

And he said it, and it was the ninth sentence he had said in ten minutes, and it took no time at all to say, and it is the whole of what he has ever worked out and he has never had anybody to work it out for.

“*A discharge paper.*”

---

Nobody said anything for a moment. At the end of the row the woman with the child on her hip had not come any closer and had not gone.

“*Say more than two words about that,*” said Ilyan.

“*There is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one,*” said the man of sixty. “*That is not a secret and it is not a fault and it is not anybody's doing. It is that a bed in a hall is a place a person cannot leave, and it is a place a person cannot leave the same way a door is a place a person cannot be fetched through, and the two things are so different that nobody in four hundred years has needed a word for both of them. And the only thing that would make my nine words false is a piece of paper that says a person has gone home, and there is nothing to write it on and nobody whose job it is to write it, and I have been at the end of this row for fourteen years and I have never once been asked for my name.*”

“*You are not going to give it to me.*”

“*I am going to give it to you, and I have not given it to anybody in this ward in fourteen years,*” he said, “*and here is the price of it and I am saying the price out loud before I do it, because a stranger stood under a board in a room at the foot of the hill four days ago and said a price out loud before he asked for something, and four people in this city have told me about it and I have not been able to work out for a week why a stranger would do that, and I have worked out.*”

---

“*Say the name and then the price.*”

“*Hadden Roote,*” he said. “*And the price is that the name is in this ward now, and half of this ward is at a standpipe this morning, and in three months somebody is going to write it in a book because a person in a record is the only person anybody can be asked about, and I will have gone into a book about a bench at the end of a row, and there is no form in this city that takes a man back out of a book, and I have thought about that for as long as it takes to think about it and I am doing it anyway, because you asked me a question instead of telling me a thing, and nobody has done that at the end of this row in fourteen years.*”

The woman with the child came forward then, and he looked at her, and he said the one thing he was there for, and it was the ordinary work, and it was not about nine words.

Then he said, to her and not to the stranger: “*Water. And a fire. And take the other three out of the room and let him sleep, and if he is worse in the morning bring him back down here and do not go up to a hall about it.*”

And she said *right*, and went, and the man of sixty sat on his bench at the end of the ninth row in Tallowgate in the middle of the afternoon and did not say anything else, and nobody thanked him, and he was not thanked then and he is not thanked now, and nine words of what he said will still be true in a month, and there is no paper on earth on which anybody can write that a person has gone home.
