# Chapter 386: The Line At The Top Of The Form, And A Name He Has Carried For Thirty-Five Days

He put it on the long table in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour on the third day of the seventy-seventh week, and it was his hundred and thirty-sixth morning and his thirty-fifth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was fourteen weeks and five days old, and the paper was twenty-eight days old and folded in four with two holes in it.

There were six people in the room this morning and the man with the bad ear was one of them and the boy of fourteen was one of them and the chandler was one of them and he had not asked any of them to come and there is no form in this city that calls a meeting of anything that is not a ward.

The clerk of thirty had the form out. He had it out before the stranger was in the room, flat on the table with the line at the top of it showing, and the clerk of forty had the book and a pen and did not look up.

“*Say the form out,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*For the room. It is the first time in nine years I have had to read one of these with people standing in the room while I fill it.*”

---

“*A sheet sent to the chamber on the second floor,*” said the clerk of thirty, in the flat voice of a man reading a thing he has read before and is not going to be asked about afterwards. “*For keeping. The thing sent is copied in a fair hand and the fair hand keeps it. A line at the top of this form for the name of the person who is sending it. A line for the date. And nothing else, and there has never been anything else, and I do not fill the top line out of a document and I have never filled the top line out of a man.*”

He put the pen on the line.

“*There is nothing at the top of that paper. So I am asking you, in this room, with six people in it, and you may take it off the table and go down the hill and I will not say one word about it, now or afterwards.*”

---

Nobody said anything, and he understood, standing at the end of a long table in a city of ninety thousand people, that there was nobody in this room who was going to do one single thing about it, and that the woman of twenty-eight had told him that at the end of a row three days ago in her own mouth, and that the man of fifty-two had asked him a question about it in a street a week ago and had been given nothing and had said *right*, and that this is what a man who has spent thirty-five days building a thing out of other people's refusals finds when the refusals run out.

The clerk of thirty was not being cruel. That was the part that made it unanswerable. He had told him on the fifth day of a week what he was going to do, in front of four people, before he did it, and he had said that it was not a kindness to him and not a kindness to himself, and he was keeping to it, and he had put the pen on the line and stopped.

So Ilyan said his name.

He said it once, in the flat voice of a man reading a line off a list he has read before, because that is the only voice he has ever had for giving a thing to a room.

“*Ilyan Vester.*”

---

“*Again,*” said the clerk of forty, without looking up, and his pen was not moving. “*It goes in a fair hand and a fair hand is one person's writing and there are more people in that building than there are doors in this one, and none of them has heard it, and I am going to write what you say and not what I think you said.*”

He said it again.

The clerk of forty wrote it at the top of a page in a book, and the clerk of thirty wrote it on the line at the top of the form, and neither of them spelled it and neither of them asked him to spell it, and the six people in the room heard a stranger say his own name twice in the morning and not one of them wrote it down anywhere, and that is the whole of what happened to it in a room with people in it.

---

Then the clerk of thirty read the line back, and it was a thing he did four hundred times a day, and he did it flat.

“*There is a name at the top of this,*” he said, “*and it goes up the hill this morning, and the fair hand on the second floor will have it in a book, and a book in that chamber is a thing that is kept, and a thing that is kept is a thing anybody in that building can be asked about. Including you. In a fortnight there will be a person on the second floor who has read that page and does not know what your name is and will go and ask somebody what your name is, and there is nobody in this city you can tell not to.*”

“*I know.*”

“*Then I am going to tell you the two things I have to tell a man when this happens, because I have told four people in nine years and I have never once told one of them anything they wanted,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and the first one is that this is not a protection and you have been treating it as one since the first time I said no to you about a name. A name at the top of a thing is a person, and a person is somebody who can be asked about, and you are now a person in a record about a woman of forty-four and a round of four hundred and twenty days over nine hundred doors, and she is at the foot of it and you are at the top of it, and that is the only arrangement there is.*”

“*And the second one.*”

“*The second one is that you did not do it to yourself, and I am not going to let you have that either,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*I have put one name on a thing in nine years that was not off a document in front of me, and it is yours, and I did it in an ordinary room at the second hour because there is a form and a line and the line was blank and you were standing in front of it. There was no kindness in it. If I had been unkind I would have done it on a bad morning and you would have gone down the hill. There is nobody in this city to whom I can hand that back and there is no form in this city that takes a name off a page, and you knew that on the fifth day of a week when I told you, and you came back up the hill this morning anyway, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be blamed for it and those are the two things I have got left.*”

---

The boy of fourteen said, from the end of the room, in a voice that had gone up and then come back down: “*So somebody can ask him about her.*”

“*Somebody can ask him about her,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*That is the whole of what a name at the top of a page is for and it has been the whole of it in this city for four hundred years, and there is a name on the top of that page now, and it is not the clerk's and it is not mine.*”

And the man with the bad ear said: “*Then somebody can ask me about my nineteen as well, because I said a number out loud in this room on the fourth day of the week before last and I have been waiting thirteen days for somebody to ask me for it and the only reason nobody has is that there was nobody in a book about it.*”

Nobody wrote that down either.

---

And then the clerk of thirty did the ordinary part of it, because there is an ordinary part and he had never in nine years had to do it with anybody standing in the room.

“*Here is what happens to it and I am going to say all of it now so that nothing about it is news to you at the end of a month. It goes up the hill in a tray. The tray goes on a bench on the second floor. A woman of forty copies it into a book, and she will not do it this morning, she does things in the order they come, and there are things in front of yours. The book stays on that bench. There is a column on the wall of that chamber for the things that have been sent to it and it does, and there is a column for the things that have not been sent to it, and yours will be in the first of those two in about a week, and it is the first time anybody has put anything in that column in nine years with a name at the top of it from a person who was standing in a room when he did it.*”

“*And after that.*”

“*And after that there is nothing, and that is the honest end of it and I am the man in this city who tells people that. A bench of four upstairs can be asked about that book and cannot be asked to do one single thing with it. There are nine lines entered in a book on that floor and kept, and they have been there since before you came down off a road, and nobody has read them out in a room. There is a question on a slate on that floor and it has not been answered and it has not been asked again, and both of those are in a book and neither of them is about a person, and I have never told anybody that either and I am not going to start.*”

The man with the leg stood at the end of the long table and had the whole of the month in about four seconds.

*He had said a price out loud six times in this city. The first five had gone into people — a gate, a lane, a yard, a hall, and a room with a clerk in it — and this one had gone onto a page with his name at the top of it, and the whole difference between those two things had turned out to be a form, and a line, and a man with a tray, and nobody in particular.*

He did not say that out loud. There is no form in this city that would put it anywhere, and there is nobody in the room he could say it to who is not already in it.

---

The tray went up the hill a little before the change, with a runner, and the runner was not sent for and there is no form in this city that says a man may be told a thing in his own street, and a sheet with five clauses and a half on the back of a bill for candles went up it with a name at the top of it and a woman's name at the foot of the fourth and fifth.

The fever did not stop. The four bells went over the low end of Tallowgate at the change and the noon and the last of the light, and a mile and a half away a woman of forty-four was at a table in a room behind a shop with a lamp on and six people at her door and a pen in her hand, and there was no form in this city that says a person is to be told anything, and nobody had told her, and nobody was going to be sent, and she was going to find out from a book like everybody else, and he had asked her on his hundred and eighth morning to be told by him either way and she had told him not to and he had done it anyway.
