# Chapter 354: Sera Quill Says What She Is Tired Of

It was his hundred and fourth morning and the sixth day of the seventy-second week, and he went to a room over a lock-and-ledger shop near the courts because there is one person in this city he is allowed to ask for a thing, and because the one thing he has needed for six days is somebody who reads.

She was at the table with four documents open and a lamp on and the shutters half shut, and she had been at it since before the light, and she did not get up.

---

“*You are wet,*” she said. “*And there is a thing on the second table under the stone, and it is bread, and I am not going to watch you not eat it while you stand there being interesting about a fever.*”

He ate it standing up and she went on writing for eleven minutes, and neither of them said anything, and it was not a good morning and it was the first morning in four days that had not been a road.

Then she put her pen down and turned her chair, and that is when it started.

“*Say it,*” she said. “*You have come four days on a road with a leg and you have got a face on you and you are not going to be able to sit down again once you start, so say it and I will tell you the parts that are wrong before you have finished.*”

“*There is an order in this city that puts its protection behind a roll, and about a third of the people in two wards are not in the roll because the roll is made by whoever answers a door, and there is a man of forty-four at the top of nine steps in Weaver's Row who has not been down them in six years and has asked twice and been told to come back with a paper, and there is no paper in this city that would do it, and nobody had noticed before a clerk said it out loud to a stranger in a room, and I have been four days in this country and I have worked out that the whole of what I have is a question and a cost, and I have come here to say it in a room to one person, and I am not going to have you fix it.*”

She listened to all of that without moving.

“*Right,*” she said. “*Now I am going to tell you what I am tired of, and I have been not tired of it since the second week, and I am going to say it in nine sentences and then I am going to go back to my table.*”

---

“*I am tired of being the place where your plans go to be right.*”

She said it without any weight on it at all, which was worse than saying it with weight.

“*You go out and you find a hole and you come back here at the last of the light and you lay it on this table, and I find the precedent, or I find the clause, or I find the two hundred words that make it a matter of this court and not of a man in a room, and then your plan is a good plan and you go off with it and I am left with a set of documents and a lamp and four weeks of an afternoon I did not choose. And that has been the shape of it for six years and I have let it be the shape of it because I am good at it and because the first time you asked me it was for a child under a bridge and I would have done that for a stranger with no coat.*”

“*I know.*”

“*You do not know, and I am not going to hand you the rest of it in a morning, but I am going to say one more sentence and then it is over: I have read four pages this month for men who came in off a road and had no business in this city, and I have not read one page for the woman who keeps the roll of Tallowgate, and that is not because she has not asked me, it is because she has not asked me, and she has not asked me because she is the fourth person in a fortnight that has been in that building and looked at a form and asked me the same three questions in the same order, and I am the one who cannot answer the same three questions twice in the same morning without starting to be one of the things I am.*”

Nobody said anything for a moment. Downstairs, somebody was shutting a shop.

“*Then do not,*” he said. “*I will not put the roll on this table. I will not ask you to find the clause for it.*”

“*You will bring it here anyway,*” she said, “*not on this table, in three weeks, in a worse shape, because you cannot help it, and I will look at it, because I am not a saint and it is a roll. But I am telling you the shape of the thing now so that when it happens you do not get to be surprised and I do not get to be blamed for having said so.*”

---

Then she said the part that was about the work, and her voice changed and this was the part of the morning she had been doing all the time.

“*Now. What you are allowed to have for nothing, because it is a fact about a form and it costs me four minutes and no judgement at all.*”

She turned back to the table and put her hand flat on the left-hand document.

“*The roll of a ward is entered in four places and the four have to be kept in agreement, and that is not a local custom, that is what a public works ledger is — a set of plates, paper, and keepers, and a stone, and a stone cannot say a name, it can only say how much. So: a plate, a paper, a keeper of record, and a block in a gatepost. In this city the four are kept by four different people in four different rooms, and there is a yard at the low end of Tallowgate that cuts the plates, and the man who cuts them is called Bevin Tarr, and he is fifty-nine, and he has been cutting them since before I was licensed.*”

“*And the paper.*”

“*And the paper. And a keeper of record, whose name I am not going to give you this morning, because you have not asked for it and I am not going to hand it to you because you are going to be in this city for a fortnight, and the moment you have a name you will have a use for it and the use will be a kindness and the kindness will be a thing I did.*”

He took that without arguing, because it was exactly right and he would have done exactly that.

---

Then he asked her the question, and he asked it because he had to know, and she answered it because he asked it.

“*If you had to write it,*” he said, “*the order. What would you have done differently.*”

She thought about it for longer than he expected.

“*I would have put a date in the sixth line,*” she said. “*And I would have lost this city for about a week over it, and a hundred and forty people would have died while the argument was being had, and the bench of four would have said I was protecting a procedure at the expense of the living, and they would have been right in the way that people in a room are right when there is a number on a wall in front of them.*”

“*So you agree with her.*”

“*I agree with her about the fever and I disagree with her about the order and those are two separate pieces of work and nobody in this building has the time to do both, which is the whole of what a city is.*” She turned the chair back to the table. “*And that is the sentence I have been saying to myself for six years and I would like to be left alone now, please.*”

---

She picked up her pen and then put it down again, which she did perhaps twice in a year.

“*Two other things and then I am going to work.*”

“*The fourteenth,*” he said.

“*The fourteenth,*” she said. “*Upstairs, in the chamber, and the question is on a slate and not on a paper because it is a question, and it is: what is the Bench to do with a document it did not make, that says a thing is under this city and not found, and who is to be asked about it. There are two names on the sheet for a witness and one of them is yours, and the other one is a man of forty-four who works in a yard at the low end of Tallowgate and is not a witness to anything and has never been asked to be.*”

“*They want the plate-cutter.*”

“*They want anybody who can say what a plate is for,*” she said, “*and he is the nearest one to this city, and the difference between those two sentences is four hundred years and it is not a thing this city is going to settle in a fortnight, and I am not going to be the one who settles it.*”

And the last of it, said flatly, and not improved on by anybody.

“*My family's claim is six years old and it is not filed and it is not going to be filed this month, and I am not going to use a fever to file it, and I would like that said once and then never again this year, including by you, and especially by a man who has a way of saying a true thing out loud in a room and then carrying it somewhere it does him good.*”

Nobody said anything.

She went back to her four documents. He took the second piece of bread because it was under the stone and she had put it there, and he did not thank her for it, and she had not put it there for thanks, and that is the whole of that and it is not going any further than that this morning.
