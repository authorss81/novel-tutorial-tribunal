# Chapter 378: A Bed Is A Place, And There Is No Form For Either Half Of It

He was twenty-seven days into a city he came to on foot, on the second day of the seventy-sixth week, and his hundred and twenty-eighth morning was taken up with two people he had gone to see and had not got. He saw the first of them at the second hour, and it is a mile and a half across the low ground, and a leg that will not clear.

She is sixty-one and she is in a hall at Weir Lane and she has been in that hall since the eighth morning of the fever, and the number of days is not in a book, because a hall does not keep days. It keeps lines. There is a line for her at the end of the beds and the line has not been rubbed out, and there is a line for her in the book at the door, and she is the only person in this city who is in two of those without having been asked to be in either.

---

He stood at the end of the beds and said the thing out loud, because that is the only instrument he has and it costs him and it is the only one, and he said it in a hall with a door on it and people in it and he did not say the price of it first, and he noticed that and let it go.

“*There is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one,*” he said, “*and I have said that out loud four times in twenty-seven days in four different rooms and four different wards, and none of those four times did anything, and I am saying it a fifth time because I would like it in a room where the people it is about are standing in it.*”

Nobody in that hall said anything, and he went on, because the instrument is a question and a question is not a speech.

“*Is there anybody in this hall who has asked to go home and has not been let.*”

Four people said so. It was four. He did not write it down and he did not count it a second time.

---

“*Bess Carrow,*” said the woman on the second bed, and she did not raise her hand, and she said it as a person gives a thing that has been in a book for a long time and has been checked.

“*I asked in the first week for a sheet of paper,*” she said. “*Not for a voice. A sheet of paper with a date on it that says I have gone home, so that when I do go home there is something that says I went. And a man came and listened to me and wrote something in a book in another building, and he was not unkind about it, and nothing has come, and I have not asked again, and I am not going to.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because the only thing anybody can do about a sheet of paper in this city is put my name at the top of a form,*” said Bess Carrow, “*and I have been in this hall since the eighth morning and I am sixty-one and there is a line for me at the end of these beds and a line for me in the book at that door, and I am as much a person in a record as anybody in this city, and I have found out in a fortnight that being one is not the same as being asked after. I would rather have the paper in a year than be a person about a request today.*”

---

And then she said the sentence, and it is the sentence, and it took her about a second and a half to say and she had not been asked for it and she said it to the room, because he had asked a question in the room and in this city that is what a room is for.

“*A person cannot be got out of a hall, and a person cannot be got out of a ward either, and the two of them are the same piece of paper, and that is the whole of what last week did.*”

Then she lay back on the board.

“*That is all I have about it,*” she said. “*You can put that in your book and you can say it to the bench of four, and it will be a true thing in a fair hand, and it will not get anybody out of a hall, and I have been here since the eighth morning and I am better and I am not going anywhere, and I am not going to be asked to be a person about a request.*”

---

He used the instrument twice in that hall, on a thing that is not his, in front of people who live under it, and it cost him nothing either time, and he wrote down both reasons, in his own head, and they were the reasons and neither of them was good.

The first is that she is already a person in a record. There is a line for her in the book at the door of her own hall. Whatever he says about that hall now falls inside a thing that is already about her and cannot make the record any bigger and cannot make anybody look at it who is not already looking.

The second is that he is the man who filled in the sixth part of the order, and everything he said in a room in the last five days landed on top of what he had already put in, and there is no way to ask a hall to separate the two, and a hall cannot be asked.

---

The second person was a mile and a half off, up nine steps, in Weaver's Row, and he got there at about the fifth hour and he had to be let up, and the man whose house it is said the same three sentences he has said for six years, which are that he is in and that the light is better than it was and that he is not complaining.

IVO SERREL is forty-four and cannot leave his own room. He is not on the roll. He has asked twice. The answer he has been given twice is that he should come back when he has the paper, and the paper is the thing nobody in this city can give him, and he is not asking for it again.

He was told there was a man with a bad leg in the room, and he asked him to come up, and Ilyan went up the nine steps, which took a long time and which he was not going to do twice.

---

“*There is a thing happened four days ago,*” said Ivo Serrel, before he was asked anything, and he had been told about it by a neighbour who had been told about it by a woman at a standpipe, and that is how a thing travels in a ward, and it travels in about a day.

“*I know.*”

“*Then you know it does not do anything for me,*” said the man in the room. “*That is not a complaint. I have worked it out. You have made a rule that a person can be moved out of a hall to a bed in any hall in this city, and I am not in a hall, and I have not been in a hall, and I have not been in a fever, and I am forty-four and my sister is downstairs and her husband is the head of this house and he puts himself in the book twice a year, and he has been doing it for six years.*”

“*I know.*”

“*Then say the rest of it,*” said Ivo Serrel, “*because I have not got the rest of it and you have come up nine steps and it has taken you a quarter of an hour and I am not going to be the reason a man with a leg stands in a room being polite.*”

---

“*The rest of it is that the rule does not touch you at all,*” said Ilyan, “*and that it was never going to, and that I knew that when I asked for it, and I asked for it anyway, and the reason I asked for it is a boy of eight at the end of a run of boards who was worse than the other nine. I did not do it for you. I am telling you that so that you do not have to find it out.*”

The man in the room did not say anything for a while and the light came off the low ground and went up the wall the way it does.

“*I have been in this room six years,*” said Ivo Serrel, “*and the head of this house has put himself in the book twice a year for six years and never once put me in it, and I have asked twice and been told to come back when I have the paper, and I have not asked since, and you are the first person who has come up those steps in six years who has not asked me for anything, and you have told me the rule is not for me, and that is the correct answer and I have no complaint about it and I am not going to say anything that makes you feel better about the nine steps.*”

---

He came down the nine steps at the last of the light, one at a time, and stopped at the bottom of them for a while, and here is the whole of what he had on the second day of the seventy-sixth week, and it is not a feeling and it is not an argument, and he did not say it out loud because there was nobody in the lane.

A bed in a hall is a place a person cannot be discharged from, and a discharge is a document, and there is no document and no office in this city that makes one, and that was true before the sixth part of the order was filled and it is true now, and it will be true when the fever goes down, because nothing in this city was ever going to be about a fever.

A ward is not a person and cannot be asked, and a rule that moves a person from a hall in a low ward to a hall in any ward in this city does not move a person between two places. It moves a person from a place they are in to a place they are not, and a person in a hall is at no door, and a name comes into a ward's roll at a door, and there is no form in this country on which anybody could move a name from one address to another.

So since the fifth day of last week, a person cannot be got out of a hall, and a person cannot be got out of a ward, and the two of those are one piece of paper, and the piece of paper is in a book in a chamber on the second floor of a building at the middle of the nine bridges, and nobody's name is on it, and there is no form anywhere on earth on which anybody could be asked to take it out.

That is not a lament and it is not a finding and nobody is relieved by it and the rule is not undone and the block in a gatepost at the end of a passage is still wrong and still gets nothing, and about a hundred and forty people are still dead in four columns in four wards that have not been added together and cannot be, and a woman of sixty-one in a hall at Weir Lane is better and is not going anywhere, and a man of forty-four at the top of nine steps has a better light and no paper, and a boy of eight is in a bed at the top of the city with two lines in two halls and one door.

The four bells went and the fever did not stop, and Bess Carrow lay on a board at Weir Lane with a line at the foot of it and a line in a book at a door, and Ivo Serrel had a better light and no paper, and neither of them was thanked and neither of them was asked for anything, and the sixth part of the order is on a wall at the foot of the hill and is not coming off.
