# Chapter 379: A Rule About Everybody, And A Group That Is Not One

Five days after a part of an order stood, on the third day of the seventy-sixth week, which was his hundred and twenty-ninth morning and his twenty-eighth in a city he came to on foot, he had walked up and down a mile and a half of Tallowgate on two nights not sleeping, working out what he had done, and he had not got it.

He got part of it at the seventh hour on the sixth day of the week, from a woman of twenty-eight with a slate and a string and a paper of chalk, and she had not been asked and she had been going to doors since the first hour and she had found something.

---

“*Say it in the order you found it.*”

“*In the order I found it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and I am going to give you the finding and the finding is not a feeling and I have had all day to get it into a shape. There is a door in the row above the plate yard's and there is a woman of sixty-eight at it, and there has been one name at that door for eleven years and it is hers, and she was the head of her own house, and she went into the low hall in the third week of the fever, and she is not at that door, and she has not been at that door since the morning before the part of the order stood, and that is five days.*”

“*Where is she.*”

“*At the top of this city, in a bed, because she is sixty-eight and she was the worst person in that hall on the day they looked,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*And the man with the bad ear did it and he told nobody and there is a second line on the board at the end of the beds and he has not rubbed out the first one. And I have been at that door nine times since the morning he came down the row, and I knocked and I stood, and the door is shut and the shutter is across the front and there is nobody behind it, and I have not put a mark on it because I have run out of the kind of mark that means that.*”

---

“*There is no mark in this city for the other thing,*” said Ilyan.

“*There is no mark in this city for the other thing and no form in this city for the other thing and no paper,*” she said, “*and that is what I have found out in five days and it is the whole of what this week has done to this ward, and here it is, and you may have it for nothing because I am not going to be thanked for it and there is nobody in this ward who could thank me.*”

She put the slate against her hip.

“*That name at that door is a claim. It says a person is in this ward. It was true on the morning before the part of the order stood and it is not true now and it is not a lie and nobody in this city can say which of those two things it is, and the only person who ever could have said it is the woman with the book, and she cannot take a name out, and there is no form in this city that says a man may ask a woman to take a name out of a roll, and if there were one, then the next thing anybody would ask her for is to put one in.*”

---

“*He said that to me in a hall on the second day of this week,*” said Ilyan, “*a man with a bad ear, almost word for word, about a man who died in the fourth week of the fever and whose name is in that book and is correct and is dead and nobody can take it out.*”

“*Then the book has one of each now,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and you are the man who put the second one there and the second one is alive, and I have been trying all day to work out which of the two is worse and I cannot do it and I have given up and I am going to tell you the thing I can do instead.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*Nobody in this ward is going to be able to prove that woman is in this city,*” she said, “*and nobody in this ward is going to be able to prove she is not. And that is the same as being nobody, in a book, in four hundred years, and I have got a name at a door and the door is shut, and if you want to know what your three sentences did to a group of people in this row, that is what it did, and it did it to one of them, and I have found one, and there are three halls in three low wards and I have been in one of them for five days and I have not got to the other two.*”

---

And that is the whole of what the rule did, and here it is, and he did not get it at the seventh hour. He got it at about the ninth hour, standing at a gate at the end of a row, and the woman of twenty-eight was right that it is not a feeling.

The rule moves the worst person in a hall, and the worst person in a hall is not a group. There is no name for them. There is no meeting of them. There is no ward that contains only them, and a ward is not a person in any case. They are whoever is worse today, and next week it will be whoever is worse then, and there is no list and there is no form and there is no person at the top of anything, and therefore nobody can be asked whether they agree and nobody can be asked whether they object and nobody can be asked whether they had been told.

And the three hundred names in four low wards that are on a paper and not on a plate are not in a hall at all, and have not been in a hall, and are not going to be in a hall, and the rule does not touch one of them, and the price of the rule is a thing that was a price two years ago and is not a price now.

So the rule is good. It is good in the way that the argument for it was good, and he made that argument himself in a public room in his third week in this city against the whole of it, and he heard it, and it was right, and the thing at the foot of it was a magistrate's name and a date and a word in a margin, and a permanent rule survives the person who writes it and cannot be argued down at a gate in a cold street by nine frightened people.

And the group whose consent was not obtained is not a group, and that is the reason nobody can object to it, and that is the reason it will keep working, and it will keep working for as long as there is a fever and for as long as there is a fear of one, and the group that cannot object is the group that gets nothing, and the group that gets nothing has always got nothing, and so a rule that moves the worst out of a hall in a low ward can be defended to the last person in this city for ever, and every single time, by pointing at the group it does not reach.

---

He went up the row at the change and Tarin Keel was at the low hall at the end of it with a piece of lead in his coat and a slate, and the man of fifty-two has been counting what comes through a door in a city for nineteen years and is being useful and hating it, and he has been at this door since the second week of the fever and nobody asked him to and nobody told him to stop.

“*You have not been up the hill,*” said Tarin Keel.

“*No.*”

“*I have not written the notice,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and I have not written it for fifteen days, and a rule with no end in it has been standing for five and the fever is the same fever and I have thought about that a great deal and I have not said it to you and I am not going to.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because you know,*” said the man of fifty-two. “*You are standing in a street with a face on you and you have been walking this row for two nights and you have worked it out and you worked it out the same way I did, and if I say it to you we are two men agreeing about a thing in a street, and I have had four of those with four different people in nine counties and every one of them was a thing I was doing instead of a thing I was counting.*”

---

Then he asked the question, and it is the only one he has left, and he asked it in a street and not in a doorway and he has never done that before.

“*How many.*”

“*How many what.*”

“*How many people have been moved,*” said Tarin Keel, “*out of a hall at the end of a low ward into a hall at the top of this city, since the part of the order stood. I count what comes through a door. That is the whole of what I have been for nineteen years and it is the only instrument either of us owns that does not put a person in a record, and I have been at the low door since the second week and I have got one hall and I have not got the other five and I cannot get them, and I am asking you, because you wrote the thing, and if you have a number then I will go and count it myself and I will be right about it.*”

And there is no number. There is a man of thirty-one with a bad ear with a column of nineteen dead in chalk and nothing over the top of it, and a board at the end of a bed with a line for every person who has been in that hall, and neither of those is a count of what the sixth part of an order has done since the morning before yesterday, and no office in this city is keeping one, and there is no form that would ask for one.

“*I do not have it,*” said Ilyan.

“*No,*” said Tarin Keel. “*Then I will count it. I have counted a door for nineteen years and I have never once been asked to count a thing that mattered and I am going to count this, and it will take me a fortnight, and I will get a number, and it will be a number about halls and not a number about this rule, because that is not the kind of thing a number is.*”

“*That is true.*”

“*I know it is true and I am doing it anyway,*” said the man of fifty-two, “*and I would like you to have one thing said to your face by the only person in this city who is going to be able to tell you anything about it in a fortnight: you have built a rule that nobody can be asked about and there is not one person in this city who is able to say whether it is working. That is not a fault in the rule. That is the rule.*”

---

The four bells went over the low end of Tallowgate and the fever did not stop, and a woman of sixty-eight was in a bed at the top of this city with a second line in a board and a name still at a shut door in a row above a plate yard, and a boy of eight was four days better and was not going to be discharged and his mother was at a standpipe in the morning, and a man of fifty-two was at a rail with a slate, counting what comes through a door, in a city where nobody had been thanked, nobody had been relieved, and nobody had been asked.
