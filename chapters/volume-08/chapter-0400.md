# Chapter 400: The Fever Goes Down, And The Dead Stay Dead

The third day of the seventy-ninth week was his hundred and fiftieth morning and his forty-ninth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was sixteen weeks and five days old, and it went down that week, and it went down because a fever goes down.

Nobody in Orison said anything about it for two days, and the first person to say it out loud was a man of thirty-one with a bad ear at a board at the end of a run of boards at the fifth hour on the first of the two days, and he said it the way he says everything, which is flat, and he said it to a boy of sixteen who had come up with a barrow.

“*Two beds free this morning on this wall and the same two yesterday afternoon, and the run of boards has not gone up by one since the eighth morning of the fever.*”

“*Say that again for the people behind me,*” said the boy of sixteen, because that is a thing a boy of sixteen does at a doorway and at a board and in a row, and nobody stopped him.

He said it four times before the noon bell, in three doorways and one yard, and a woman with a pail at a standpipe at the end of the row said it out loud to a woman coming down the row, because that is also a thing a person does at a standpipe, and nobody stopped her either, and by the last of the light on the second day four halls out of six had two beds free instead of none, and nobody in this city could say why, and nobody was told a reason.

---

The man of sixty was on his bench at the end of the ninth row at the change and had been on it for fourteen years, and he had nine words in him that he had said once, twenty-three days ago, in a room at the end of his own row, and he had said in the same room that they would still be true in a month, and it was not a month yet, and they were still true and he was not going to say them again.

“*It has gone down,*” said the man with the bad leg, because he had come up the row and he was going to say it out loud and there was nobody else in the row who was going to.

“*It has,*” said the man of sixty.

“*Then was it the order.*”

“*No,*” said the man of sixty. “*I cannot tell you that it was the order, and neither can the four men who were in that room, and neither can you, and I have been asked three times this month by people who wanted a thing they could put in a book, and I am not going to give it to them today either. A fever goes down. I have watched four go down in forty years and I have never once known why and I have never once been wrong about the going down.*”

“*The worst keep it longest,*” said Ilyan, because the man had said it and he had been in the room.

“*The worst keep it longest,*” said the man of sixty, “*and the worst are still the worst, and half of the people in that hall at the end of this row are still going to be the worst in a month, and the beds that came open this week opened because there were fewer of them, and there were fewer of them because a hundred and forty-odd people in this city are dead, and that is not a cause of anything. That is a number. Put it in a book and it will still be a number.*”

“*You have never been asked for your name.*”

“*No,*” said the man of sixty, “*and I was not asked this week either, and there was a bench of four in this city for two days and not one of them asked me, and I am not going to say that is a fault. I have never wanted to be in a record and I have never been in one, and that is the only thing I have that nobody in this ward has, and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be.*”

Nobody thanked him.

---

The dead are not a thing that gets better in a week and the clerk of thirty was the one who said so, on the second day, standing in the room at the foot of the hill with his tray, in the flat voice, because a rule that is not read out is a thing that hangs on a wall and he had spent nine years with a wall and a bench of four had gone the whole of a week without one.

“*The count of the dead is in two wards and not in four, and it has not moved in the three days since it went down and is not going to move this week, and I am not going to be asked to put a number of dead in a return, because a return is a form about a thing that was asked, and nobody has asked me for it, and if somebody asks me for it I will put on it what the man with the bad ear has got on his board in chalk and the rest of it will be nobody's.*”

“*What is on his board.*”

“*Nineteen names, and two lines of chalk over the top of them that are about a ward and not about the nineteen, and a woman of sixty-eight who is alive at the top of this city and whose name is at a shut door in a row above a plate yard's, and a boy of eight who is better in a bed he cannot be discharged from, and nobody has said either of their names out loud in a room in this city in their lives.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And the other hundred and twenty-odd are in four books in four wards and there is no office the four of them can hand a book to, and there is no column for them and there is no heading over them, and there has not been a heading in this city for a person who died of anything since before the water came, and I have looked.*”

---

And the clerk of thirty said the other thing, and he said it because it was his employment and not because it was a lesson, and he said it in the flat voice, and nobody in that room thanked him for it.

“*You are all in this room asking me what has not been put back, and there is one piece of paper in that building and it is a return, and a return cannot carry a thing that is four hundred miles off and is in another county, so I am going to say it and then I am going to stop.*”

He put his hand on the tray.

“*A porch in a valley of sixty people is gone. There is no copy of it in four hundred miles and there is not going to be one. A corridor is standing in a valley of a hundred and forty people where a man of forty named Corvin Hale signed it out loud with the working printed beside it, and he is alive in his own field, and nobody has bought the bell he is owed, and he is not redeemed and there is no form in this city that redeems anybody.*”

“*Say the bell.*”

“*The bell in that county is still not hung,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and it is a hundred and forty days of a keeper's wages and a tower and two carriers and no third, and it is four hundred miles off, and I have never seen it and I have sent a thing to that county four times in nine years and I cannot do one single thing about any of it, and neither can you, and it is in a book upstairs and it is the first time I have ever said any of it out loud in a room and I am not going to say it again.*”

Nobody asked him why.

“*And a block in a gatepost at the end of a passage in this ward has a mark on its face that nobody in this ward can read and that the meeting of a ward that would weigh it again does not exist,*” he said. “*I have said that last part in this room before and I am saying it again because this week it was the fourth line of a return, and the fourth line is the only new thing anybody in this ward has got, and I am not going to be the one who says that is a settlement.*”

---

The boy of eight was still in a bed at the top of this city on the second day and he was better, and he had been in it since the night a boy of sixteen took him up the row on a barrow in a blanket, and he could not be discharged, and there is no discharge paper in this city and no office that issues one, and his line was in chalk at the foot of a board at the end of a run of boards in a low ward and a second line was in chalk on a board at the top of this city, and he has two lines in two halls and one door, and no form in four hundred miles moves a name from one place to another.

His mother was at the standpipe at the last of the light and the water was down a hand's width again and the row was quiet, and she filled the pail and she did not say anything, and she will be at that standpipe in the morning, and nobody had said his name out loud in any room in this city in his life and nobody said it in this one.

And nobody in this city has one of the words that would let a person say no to the order. The order stands. It has an end in it now and a date and a review and a second pair of hands on it, and the right to refuse it has not come back to anybody, and an exit is not a refusal, and it was not a refusal on the day it went up and it is not one now, and the sixth part is a way out of a thing a person is in and not a way of not being in it, and there is no form in four hundred miles that lets a man in Orison be outside it.

And there is a bill on the wall at the foot of a hill with a heading on it about a bell keeper in a county four hundred miles off, and nobody can pay any of it, and a line on it is blank, and the fee is four shillings the hundred names, and the three hundred names that stopped being a thing anybody had to pay for are not on a plate and are not going to be on one this month or the next.

---

On the third day of that week the woman of twenty-eight went down the row at the first hour with her slate against her hip and a stub of chalk in her apron that a man at a wheel had not asked her why she had, and she said a name out loud at a door, and the name went into the record of that ward without a fee, because a name said by a person of a ward goes in without a fee, and nobody thanked her.

Magistrate Rell was at the end of a different row at the same hour with a sheet in her hand and a figure on it and a sentence under the figure, and she was going to write down what was at a door and there was no form for the sheet and there is not going to be one, and nobody thanked her, and she is a magistrate and nobody can be sent for her and she cannot be sent for by anybody at all.

The woman with the book was at her table a mile and a half down the low ground with a lamp on and a roll under it, and she was going to go round nine hundred doors at thirty a fortnight and then go round them again for the ones that were shut, and that is four hundred and twenty days and then four hundred and twenty days more, and there is nobody going to be standing in that room at the end of it to be asked whether it was done, and she is forty-four and she is in two records and she is not thanked and has stopped expecting it and has told a room and a magistrate and a bench of four that she will not be thanked and is not going to stop saying so.

The man with the bad ear had two lines of chalk over nineteen dead, and the four bells went over Tallowgate at the first, the change, the noon and the last, and the water at the low end went down another inch in the night, and about a hundred and forty people were dead in this city and they were dead before the first morning a man with a bad leg came down off a road into it, and they are remembered as dead, and none of them is coming back, and nothing that happened in this city brought any of them back, and the fever went down and nobody was thanked.
