# Chapter 391: A Form For Asking A Bench Of Four A Question In A Room

The first day of the seventy-eighth week was his hundred and forty-first morning and his fortieth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was fifteen weeks and three days old, and the order was on the wall of the public room at the foot of the hill with nine inches of blank under it, and he wanted seven things read out in that room in front of a bench of four, and there was nothing in this city that would let him do it.

He had known that since the last day of last week, when he had put seven true things on a long table one at a time and had watched the ward come and look at them and join none of them to any other. The bench of four had been upstairs the whole of that morning, and it had not come down, and nobody had decided that it should not.

---

He went up the hill at the second hour and the clerk of thirty was on the ground floor of the black building at the middle of the nine bridges with his tray and a runner waiting, and he was a man who had been in that room nine years and had never once in nine years left it early.

“*You have come to ask me how a bench of four is asked about a thing,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*I am going to tell you the whole of it, and then you are going to go back down the hill and do something else, and I want the something else to be a thing you have thought of and not a thing I have suggested.*”

“*A bench of four is sent things.*” He turned the book round. On the wall behind him were two columns ruled in a fair hand, and the left one said that a thing had been sent to that chamber and the right one said that a thing had not. “*Nine lines in the left, in nine years. That is the whole of what four men and four stools do up there. They read things, and what is done about them is written down and sent back down the hill, and they never go anywhere, because nothing in this city says a bench of four must.*”

“*So a bench cannot be brought to a room.*”

“*A bench cannot be brought to a room and a room cannot be brought to a bench, and the only thing that has moved between that room and this floor in nine years is a tray.*”

He put his hand on a form under the window. There were nine of them in the press and they were used about forty times a year.

“*A person of the ward may ask for a thing to be read to them. The oldest form in this building and the only one a person can fill in without a magistrate, a bench, or a paper from upstairs. Three lines: the thing to be read, the room it is to be read in, and a line at the top for the name of the person asking.*”

“*You have filled it before.*”

“*Nine times in nine years, and every one of the nine was answered by a clerk, and every one of the nine was a book, and a book I can read.*” He put the pen down. “*If it is not a book, the person who can answer it has to come down and read it themselves, and none of the four of them is a magistrate, and a magistrate is not one of them and does not sit with them, and not one of the four has come down that hill in nine years, because nobody in this city has ever filled that form in about a thing a bench of four has to answer. There is no way of finding that out from this room except by asking me, and you are the first person to ask me.*”

“*Fill it in.*”

“*I will fill it in the ordinary way at the ordinary hour, and if there is nothing to read I will send the tray back down with the line empty, and that has happened before.*”

---

Down at the long table at the fourth hour there were nine people in the room and the order on the wall behind them, and he said the price out loud, which is a thing he has to do whether he wants to or not, and the price was about somebody else, and that was the whole difficulty with it.

“*There is a form in that building a person of this ward can fill in on their own, and it has a line at the top of it for the name of the person asking, and the only person in this room who can fill it in and get what he has wanted for longer than a man with a bad leg has been in this ward is the man who keeps a column of nineteen. I am not going to fill it in, because I have no standing in this ward and my name is on two things in a chamber four floors up and I am not going to put it on a third.*”

The man with the bad ear put his hat on the table.

“*Say the price,*” he said. “*Not the form. The price of my name on it.*”

So he said it, with the whole of it in front of nine people, and it was the seventh time he had said a price out loud in this city and he was not going to say afterwards whether it had worked.

“*A thing read out in a room is a thing asked about in a room, and a thing asked about in a room is a thing a person can be held to. Your name at the top of that form goes into a fair hand on the second floor this afternoon, and into a book, and the book stays on a bench, and anybody in that building can be asked about it, and nothing in this city takes a name off a page or a person out of a record, and I have used that every time I have wanted something. You would be a person in a record about your own nineteen, and you would be asked about it for the rest of your life, and I would be the man who asked you, and nobody in this room would be thanked for it. I am telling you the price before the doing because that is the only thing I have got and it has never once stopped me.*”

---

The man with the bad ear did not take a long time over it, and nobody in the room interrupted him, which is a thing about a room in this city.

“*I have wanted a heading over that column every day since the second week of this fever,*” he said. “*I asked a room for one on the last day of last week and the people in it looked at seven things, and not one of them was a heading. Then I worked out that I was not angry with the room. Nobody in that room can write words over a thing. It is not their employment.*”

He turned his hat round on the table.

“*You are asking me to put my own name on a line so that a thing I have asked for nine times gets written down, and you have told me what it costs, and the cost is that I am a person in a record, and I have been a person in a record my whole life, and there is one name at a door in this row that is a person in a record and is dead. Fill it in.*”

“*You are not going to be thanked for it,*” Ilyan said.

“*Do not,*” said the man with the bad ear. “*Nobody has thanked me and I have not expected it. I want one thing, and you are about to tell me I cannot have it, and you should tell me now so that I can be standing up when you do.*”

---

And Ilyan did not tell him then, and that was the part of the morning he did not go back to, and the reason is that he did not know yet, and a thing he does not know yet is not a thing he is allowed to say out loud in a room to make it true.

There was a fourth thing in that room that nobody had on the table and nobody had asked for, and he had been carrying it for seven volumes and four hundred miles, and it was four words, and it was the only instrument in this city that would have worked in the next ten seconds.

A person inside the order could say no to the order. Not in a week, not by a magistrate, not by a bench, not by him, because the words that would put it in were written in no form in this city and were not in the head of the plank in a county four hundred miles off, and he was the only person in that room who had ever read them, and he could have said them out loud on the end of that table, and a rule said out loud by a person who lives under it, in a room where it is being talked about, stops being a thing on a wall in this city. That was the whole of what he had ever wanted.

Tarin Keel was at the end of the table with a piece of lead in his coat, and he had been looking at him for about a minute.

“*Do not,*” said Tarin Keel.

“*I have not said anything.*”

“*You have had it in your face for a minute and a half, and I have been counting what goes into rooms in this city for nineteen years, and every price you have said out loud in one of them went into a person, and the one that went into a page is the reason a woman of forty-four is in a book. If you say those four words on that table they go into every person in that room, and not one of them has asked you for them, and you would be the only man in this city who could be asked about them for the rest of his life.*”

Nobody said anything.

Ilyan took the jug and drank a cup of water, which he had not wanted, and put it down, and his jaw did the thing first and the water came second. The clerk of thirty, who had come down the hill with the form and was at the end of the table with his tray, looked at him and looked away and said, in the flat voice of a man reading a thing he has read before:

“*The form wants the thing to be read, and the room it is to be read in, and the name of the person asking. I have the first two. I am not in a hurry, and there is no version of this afternoon in which I do it badly.*”

---

So the man with the bad ear said his name to a clerk of thirty on a form in a public room at the foot of the hill, and the clerk of thirty filled the line from the paper in front of him, which is what he fills every line from, and the clerk of forty wrote it fair, because a fair hand is one person's writing and more people will read it than have heard it. Nobody in that room wrote the name down in a book of their own, and the man with the bad ear watched it go into the tray and said nothing about it, and he has said nothing about it since, and he was not thanked and did not expect it.

The tray went up the hill a little before the change with a runner, and nobody sent for the runner.

The return came down at the seventh hour in the tray, and the clerk of thirty read it standing up and then read it again, because he is a man who does things like that.

“*A bench of four will be at the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour tomorrow, and the order is to be read to the person who asked and to anybody who comes, and anybody who comes will not be asked who they are and will not be asked why they came. That is the whole of what is on it.*”

“*Read it again.*”

He read it again. Nine people in a room at the foot of a hill and the order on the wall behind them with nine inches of blank under it, and nobody said anything, which is what a room in this city does at the end of an order.

---

And a man with no standing in this city had found, in the ordinary way, through a form that had been in a press under a window for nine years, the only door in Orison a bench of four can be walked through, and had walked a man of thirty-one with a bad ear through it, and had not walked himself through anything at all.

He sat down on a bench at the end of the long table and put his hand flat on the wood, and understood that a man who says a thing out loud in a room does not get to choose which side of the record he ends up on, and that it had cost the person who answered a thing that could not be given back, and that was the instrument, and it was the seventh time.

*And there was a name for what he had become, and nobody gave it to him, and nobody in that room was ever going to use it about him. A Remedy Drafter is a person who proposes a real covenant, and his had been read out in a room that writes things down and had gone into a book, and from that morning the flaws in it belonged to him in front of people. There was no power in any of it. It was a piece of work and not a rank, and the whole of the difference between a man who drafts and a man who files is that the man who files has somebody to answer to.*

He did not say any of that out loud, and there was nobody in the room he could have said it to, and it did not go in a book, and it is the last thing in this city that has not been read out to anybody in a room.

At the last of the light the bell at the landing was rung for the ferry, which is what it is rung for when the water is over the flat way, and the four bells went over Tallowgate, and the fever was fifteen weeks and three days old and it did not stop for a form.

The boy of sixteen was in the doorway of that room at the last of the light with a barrow outside it, and he said it out loud to four people in the doorway, which is how a thing travels in this ward: that a bench of four was coming down the hill in the morning, and the room was to be full. Nobody stopped him and nobody sent for him.
