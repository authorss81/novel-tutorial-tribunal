# Chapter 397: Thirty Doors A Fortnight, And Nobody Standing There At The End Of It

The seventh day of the seventy-eighth week was his hundred and forty-seventh morning and his forty-sixth in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was sixteen weeks and two days old, and Magistrate Rell came down the mile and a half of low ground at the fifth hour and stood outside a chandler's shop at the end of a row in Tallowgate with four people at the door of the room behind it, and there is no form in this city that says a magistrate may come to a door, and she came anyway, and nobody sent for her and nobody stopped her.

The woman with the book had the lamp on and the roll under it and a chair turned round to the table, which is what she does when she is going to do something with her hands, and she did not get up.

“*You have come to ask me for the number,*” she said. “*There was a woman in a room at the foot of a hill yesterday who would not give it to you and said it was mine, and she was right, and I would have said the same and I would have said it better because I have been saying it for eleven years.*”

“*I have come to ask you for the number,*” said Magistrate Rell.

“*Then sit down, and I am going to give you the whole of it with the working in it, because you are the first person in thirty-one years who has come to this door and asked me for a figure, and nobody has ever shown me a bill and I have never been able to say what the work is worth.*”

---

She gave it the way she gives everything, in the voice of a woman reading a thing out of a book, and she did not put her pen down and she did not stop.

“*Nine hundred doors. Thirty of them a fortnight. Thirty lots of fourteen days is four hundred and twenty days. A year in this city is three hundred and sixty-four days, so a round is a year and fifty-six days, and I have said that figure eleven times in thirty-one years and eight of those were to men who wrote it down and not one of them ever gave it back to me.*”

“*And the correction.*”

“*And the correction is not one round, and this is the part I have never had to say to anybody, because there has never been a correction.*” She turned the chair and looked at the lamp. “*A round goes to nine hundred doors and sets down what is at them. When I come to a door that is shut, I cannot set down what is at it. There is no mark in this city for a house that has emptied and there is no form that says a door was shut, so a shut door is not on my list and not off my list. It is a door I went to. And between the first time I went to it and the second time, somebody may have come back into it or gone out of it, and I will not know, because I went past it.*”

“*How many doors are shut.*”

“*I do not know, and I am not going to guess at it in my own room,*” said the woman with the book, “*and that is the second part and it is worse than the first. The first part is four hundred and twenty days. The second part is that the four hundred and twenty days has to be done again for the doors that were shut, and one round does not find them, it only counts them, and then the round has to be done over them.*”

She put two fingers on the roll.

“*Four hundred and twenty days, and then four hundred and twenty days over the doors that were shut the first time. Four hundred and twenty twice is eight hundred and forty days. A year here is three hundred and sixty-four. That is two years and a hundred and twelve days, and that is the figure, and I have given it to you with the working in it so that you can check it and not believe me, and I have never been able to do that for a figure in my life.*”

Magistrate Rell wrote it down. She wrote it exactly as it was said, in a small hand, on the back of her own hand, because there was no paper in that room but the roll and a candle bill.

“*Say the next part out loud,*” she said, “*because I can see you deciding whether to say it.*”

---

“*There is not going to be anybody standing in this room in two years and a hundred and twelve days to be asked whether it was done properly,*” said the woman with the book, “*and I am not saying that to be sad. I am saying it because it is the shape of a round and I have been inside the shape for eleven years and nobody has ever asked me about it.*”

“*Explain it to me.*”

“*The people who will know whether the roll is right are the people who went to the doors. That is me. And in two years and a hundred and twelve days I will be somebody who did it, and I will be asked about it by people who did not, and I will have to say I did it, and they will have to take it.*” She shut the roll. “*And a person in this city who keeps a book and is asked about the book and says I did it is a person who cannot be argued with about anything else. That has been true of me since I was thirty-three and it is the reason there is nobody in Tallowgate who will tell me a thing they have not already put in a book, and I have made my own arrangements about that and they are not anybody else's business.*”

“*I am not going to argue with you about any of it,*” said Magistrate Rell.

“*I know,*” said the woman with the book. “*You have not argued with me about a single thing in the whole of this, and I have noticed it, and I have noticed it the way I notice a figure that does not come out, and I do not know what to do with it and I am not going to do anything about it in front of you.*”

---

Then she said the part about the third thing, and she said it in her own room, and it is the only time in this city anybody has said it.

“*Two days ago, at the foot of a hill, I said there was a third thing coming and that I could see it coming and that it was a thing with my name at the top of it. I want to take that back and put the true one in its place, and I want to do it now, in a room with four people at the door, and not in a return.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*The third thing is not a document. I am in two records and I know exactly what both of them are, and I have said that to a fair hand and it is in a book four floors up with a name at the top of it, and that is enough. The third thing is four hundred and twenty days and then four hundred and twenty days more, and there is no form for it and nobody has to write it down, and there is nowhere for me to put it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*So the third thing cannot be asked about. It cannot be produced. Nobody can stand up in a room in two years and put it on a table and say *this is the round* and nobody can say it is wrong. And I have spent thirty-one years inside a document, and a document can be produced, and the one thing I have always been able to do is be produced, and I am going to be the first person in this city whose work is the one thing that cannot.*”

She turned the chair back to the table and put her hand flat on the roll, which is what she does before she says the last of it.

“*I am not relieved and I am not thanked and I am not going to be. If anybody in this room thanks me I will not answer and I will think about it for the rest of the day.*”

Nobody thanked her.

---

“*And the two names,*” said Magistrate Rell, because she had come a mile and a half and she was not going to leave it there.

“*The two names are wrong and they are going to be wrong in two years and a hundred and twelve days, and one of them belongs to a woman of sixty-eight who is in a bed at the top of this city and one of them belongs to a man who died in the fourth week of the fever, and I cannot take either of them out and I cannot make either of them true, and I am not going to be asked to and I would not do it if I was.*”

“*That is not an answer about a review.*”

“*It is the only answer there is about a review and I will give you the whole of it for nothing,*” said the woman with the book. “*A review of that order is going to ask whether the roll was right, and the roll will not be right at the end of two rounds because of those two doors, and the person who is asked about the roll is me, and the answer I will give is that there is no form in this city that moves a name from one place to another and that this has been the case for thirty-one years and I am not going to pretend that two years of somebody else's good intentions has changed it.*”

“*That is a finding against the settlement and not for it.*”

“*It is a finding and it is in a room and there are four people at the door and two of them have heard it,*” said the woman with the book, “*and if a magistrate wants it on a return she can come back and ask me again and I will say exactly the same nine words and it will cost her another mile and a half of low ground.*”

---

And then the woman at the door who had been standing in it for the whole of the hour, on her own account, with her slate under her arm, said the only thing in that room that was about the rule rather than about the work, and she said it to the magistrate and not to the keeper, and the keeper let her.

“*I have been at a set of doors since the morning this started and I have a mark against most of them and it is not a name and it is a claim, and I am not going to tell you how many, and you have already had the answer to that from a room at the foot of a hill and you did not like it.*”

“*Say the claim.*”

“*That somebody said so and did not go and ask,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*A mark against a door is not a name in a book. It is a person of that ward saying that a person of that ward can be asked about that door, and nobody has to take my word for it and nobody has to believe me, because the name goes in when the name is said by somebody in the ward and it goes in without a fee, and that is your third clause, and it has been in force since a man with a bad leg read it out in a room at the foot of a hill and sent it up a hill on a tray four days before the end of last week, and I have been saying names at doors ever since, and three hundred of them are in a book and not on a plate and that is the whole of what I have done.*”

“*And the rest of the hundred and forty.*”

“*And the rest of them are a woman on a bed, and a woman of sixty-eight at a shut door, and a number of doors I have not been inside and am not going to be until somebody with a duty stands in a row and says so,*” she said, “*and your fourth clause made me the somebody, at a door, in a row, without asking me, and I have said that already in a room and I am not going to say it again.*”

“*I am not asking you to say it again. I am asking whether the hundred and forty can go in the roll.*”

“*Yes,*” she said. “*All of them. Tomorrow, and the ones after that, and the ones after that, and I will not be thanked and I have stopped waiting for it and I have told a magistrate and a bench of four and a room of forty people that I will not be thanked and I am not going to stop saying it because I have said it a lot.*”

Nobody thanked her.

“*And it will not take anything off the round,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, to the keeper, and the keeper said: “*No. A rule that lets anybody put a name in a record does not let anybody find out whether a name is still true, and a name in a book is a claim that somebody was at a door, and a claim that is out of date is worse than a blank one, because a blank one can be found and an out-of-date one will be believed. It will go in, and I will put the day on it, and in two years and a hundred and twelve days there will be more names in this book than have ever been checked, and that is the trade, and somebody with a duty has made it, and I am not going to pretend I have a way of stopping it.*”

Magistrate Rell wrote that down too, on the back of her own hand, and she did not argue with any part of it, and she went out into the row at the last of the light with a figure and a sentence and no paper, and the four bells went over the low end of Tallowgate, and the fever was sixteen weeks and two days old and it did not stop for a round, and nobody in that row thanked anybody.
