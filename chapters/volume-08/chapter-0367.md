# Chapter 367: A Debt Of Four Hundred And Twenty Days, Priced

It was his hundred and seventeenth morning and the fifth day of the seventy-fourth week, and the fourteenth was two days off, and a boy of eleven who carries water past a standpipe in this ward for a penny at the end of the week told him the names of three people who are not his family, in a street, at about the second hour, because nobody had ever asked him anything else.

The boy had been in that street before he came into this city. The stranger had watched him carry two pails past him on his first morning here and had been told the whole of the boy's day in five words and none of the five had been about the fever.

---

The boy put the pails down at the standpipe end and came over and wiped his face with his sleeve, and Ilyan asked him the question that a man asks a boy of eleven in a street, which was what his name was and whether he went to a school.

“*Halden,*” said the boy. “*No.*”

“*Halden, that is a good name for a street.*”

“*It is my mother's brother's name and he is at the yard at the low end and he is not here,*” said the boy, “*and I am the head of the house, so if you want to say a name at my door you are saying it to me and not to my gran, and my gran is the one who gives me the penny at the end of the week, and my gran is sixty-eight and has bad knees and cannot get to the door, and that is the whole of why it is me and not her.*”

He said all of that in one go, in a cold street, to a stranger, and he was not showing off and he was not frightened. He said it the way a person says a thing they have had to say.

---

The boy is the head of the house because he is the oldest in it and because the roll of Tallowgate is made at a door by the person of the house, and the person of the house is the person who answers the door, and the person who answers that door is a boy of eleven because his mother has been in a hall at the low end of this ward since the middle of the fever and has not been discharged and is not going to be, and his grandmother is sixty-eight and has bad knees and does not get to the door.

There are six people in that house and the roll has three names in it and has had three names in it for two years.

“*You said three,*” said Ilyan. “*Is that everybody.*”

And the boy looked at him, and understood that he had been asked a question, and discovered that he had not been asked one before.

“*No,*” said Halden.

---

He said it quickly, in a street, at the second hour, to a stranger, and he did not stop to think about it and there was nobody to stop him, and it took about nine words.

“*There is the old man in the passage. He sleeps there and he gives my gran nothing and he gives me a penny a week for the fire. There is the woman from the next house, her name is not hers, she sleeps in our front room three nights in the week because she goes to the yard. And there is the little one from number nine, his mother is at a hall and he sleeps in our kitchen and he is not on the book either.*”

“*Halden—*”

But the boy had said the three names and he was eleven and there was no taking them back, and he looked at the stranger's face and then said the thing that made it worse, and he said it because there was finally somebody standing there who had asked him something.

“*Nobody has ever asked me anything,*” said the boy. “*My gran says the names and I say the names and that is all anybody has ever wanted off me. You asked me if that was everybody and nobody has ever asked me that.*”

---

“*Do not tell anybody else.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because I have not got anywhere to put them,*” said Ilyan, “*and because I am not going to be the reason a boy of eleven is a person in a record about a book in this city, and there is no form in this country that takes a person back out of one once they are in it, and I have been here sixteen days and I have watched four people get into records because somebody said a true thing near them.*”

“*You are not going to tell.*”

“*I am not going to tell anybody anything,*” he said, “*and I have promised a woman of twenty-eight the same thing eight days ago at the corner of a low ward and I have not broken it and I am not going to start on a boy.*”

“*Right,*” said the boy, and picked up the two pails, and went to carry water, and did not say one word more about it, and did not look back, and was gone round the end of the row.

And three names went into the world at about the second hour of the fifth day of the seventy-fourth week and they went into a street and not into a book, and they cost nothing, and they cannot be spent, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can do one single thing with them.

He stood in the street for four minutes and worked out the whole of what his one instrument is worth, in a city, in sixteen days.

In a country it had cost a woman of about thirty-four at a bar a thing she cannot put down, and it had cost him twice, and every time it had made somebody a person in a record.

In this city it cost the same, and that is the finding and he had been sixteen days finding it.

Used on the woman at the back of the chandler's shop it had put her name in a book she did not consent to. Used on a woman of sixty with a shawl it had cost her nothing, because she had a name in a book already and it had not made her anything new. Used on a boy of eleven at the second hour it had cost nothing at all and had got him three names that he could not use, from a person who had no way of knowing she was saying them to a man with a leg.

His instrument does not ask a question. It puts a person in a room. And the answer it gets back is never the answer, because the person in the room is a different person from the one who is hurt.

And that is what a man with a question and no other one has, on the fifth day of a week, in a low ward, in a cold, at sixteen days in a city of ninety thousand.

---

At the fifth hour he was in the room at the back of the chandler's shop, and there were four people at the door and a lamp on, and he asked her a money question and he asked it as a man asks a person of a place for a price, and it was the second time he had asked her for one in four days.

“*What is a round of this ward worth.*”

“*You asked me that yesterday morning,*” said the woman at the table.

“*Yesterday morning you told me the count. I want the price.*”

“*The price is a visit and the count is a year and fifty-six days and you have had both,*” she said, “*and now you want me to put a shilling on it, and I am not going to do it in the abstract, so tell me what the number is for and I will tell you what it costs.*”

“*It is what a draft costs when it does not get the third of the three things a draft needs.*”

---

The woman at the table put both hands flat on the book and did not say anything for nine seconds, and then she said a number, and she did not say his number.

“*A date at this bench, bought, is two hundred and eighty-two pence,*” she said. “*My year is forty-eight pence. Two hundred and eighty-two less forty-eight is two hundred and thirty-four pence, and that is the number and I have given it to you and I am not going to do anything else with it.*”

“*What is it.*”

“*It is the difference between what this city can pay for one afternoon of a room and what it pays me for every day of a year,*” said the woman at the table, “*and it is the only money there is in this, and I have had it in my head for ten seconds and I would like you to take it and go and do something with it.*”

He did not do anything with it. He stood in a room with a book in it and understood, for about the fourth time in sixteen days and the first time properly, that he was holding two numbers that did not touch.

---

“*Four hundred and twenty days of you is not a division,*” he said.

“*No,*” said the woman at the table.

“*Say why, because I have it in my head as a division and I want to be wrong.*”

“*Because the arithmetic of a person is not a division,*” she said. “*You have got a round of four hundred and twenty days and you have got a year of forty-eight pence and you have got a date of two hundred and eighty-two pence, and if you do that division you will get a figure, and the figure will be right, and I will be a figure. And I have been in this city thirty-one years and I have watched four people be turned into figures by people who were not being cruel, and one of them is still paying for it this week. And you have come sixteen days and you have said a price out loud three times and every one of the three you have said it to a room and not to me, and I have let you, because I have been in this room since the second hour this morning and I have had a lamp on and four people at my door.*”

She turned the chair round and picked up her pen and then put it down again, which she does perhaps twice in a year.

“*And here is the other half and it is the half that matters and it is not a number. Four hundred and twenty days of my life is not a debt of mine. It is a debt of this ward. And a ward is not a person. A person can be asked about a thing — that is the whole of what a person is, a person is somebody you can ask about the record. A ward cannot. A ward has no name at the foot of anything, and a form needs a name at the top of it, and there is no form in this city that has a ward at the top of it, so this ward owes four hundred and twenty days and there is no one on earth that can be asked whether it has paid them.*”

“*So the round is unpaid and uncollectable.*”

“*The round is unpaid and uncollectable and it has been that for eleven years and nine rounds,*” said the woman at the table, “*and I have never said it out loud before and I have known it every single day of it, and it is the reason a boy of eleven can be the head of a house with three names in it.*”

She opened the door and there were four people at it and two of them were not there before, and she did not look at them.

The four bells went that night over nine hundred doors, and the debt of four hundred and twenty days was still a debt, and the difference between a date and a year was two hundred and thirty-four pence, and nobody was relieved, and nobody was thanked, and a woman of forty-four went on doing a round she was not being paid for, at a rate of two doors a day, in a ward that is not a person and cannot be asked.
