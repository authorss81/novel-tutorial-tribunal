# Chapter 382: Four Hundred And Twenty Days, And A Thing That Cannot Be Got

There is a number in a room at the back of a chandler's shop that nobody in this city has ever been asked for, and he asked for it on the sixth day of the seventy-sixth week, which was his hundred and thirty-second morning and his thirty-first in a city he came to on foot, and the fever was fourteen weeks and a day old and the paper in his coat was twenty-four days old.

He asked it the way a person of this city asks a question, which is that he asked it and then he stopped talking, and there were six people at the door and she did not open it.

“*Say the question again and say it properly, because you have asked me four money questions since you came into this city and this is not one of them.*”

“*What does one round of this ward cost.*”

The woman with the book did not put her pen down. She wrote for another eleven seconds, and then she put it down, which is a thing she does before she answers a thing and not before she answers a thing she is going to refuse.

“*You have had that. I told you in my own room on the morning you read me your five clauses.*”

“*You told me the shape of it. I want the price of it in the only unit this city has and I have never asked anybody for it.*”

“*Then there is no answer and I will tell you why there is no answer, and then I will tell you what I have got, and you may have it, and I am not going to be thanked for it and there is no form in this city that could record a thank.*”

---

“*Thirty doors a fortnight,*” she said. “*Nine hundred doors. Thirty lots of fourteen days is four hundred and twenty days, and a year here is fifty-two weeks of seven, which is three hundred and sixty-four days, and four hundred and twenty less three hundred and sixty-four is fifty-six, and so one round of this ward is a year and fifty-six days.*”

“*And you have done it nine times.*”

“*I have done it nine times in eleven years and the last complete one finished in the autumn before the water came, and the one I am in the middle of is a bit more than a third of the way through, and I have not been to a door since the water came, and I am not going to while there is a fever in this ward.*”

“*How much of it is left.*”

“*If I do not go, all of it.*”

---

He wrote nothing down, and she watched him not write it down, and neither of them said anything about that.

“*What you have not got,*” she said, “*and what nobody in this city has got, in any unit, is what one of those days is worth. My year is four shillings. Four shillings is forty-eight pence, and forty-eight pence over three hundred and sixty-four days is about a farthing a day and not quite, and that is the whole of what anybody has ever put against that work. Nobody has ever shown me a bill for it. Eleven years, and not one person in this city has ever said to me: here is what it costs, here is the bill, here is what you are worth.*”

“*That is what you said to me on the morning I read you the five clauses.*”

“*That is what I said to you and you have been carrying it about for twenty-four days like a man carrying somebody else's shopping, and I would like to know what you have done with it.*”

“*Nothing.*”

“*Right,*” said the woman with the book. “*Then I am going to say the other half of the answer and it is the half you did not come for, and I am saying it because you asked me a question and I have never once in this room not answered one.*”

---

She turned the chair round, which is what she does when she is going to do something with her hands.

“*The third requirement,*” she said, “*is the people who will live under it saying yes. You have told me that in your own mouth, in this room, seventeen days ago, and I have written it nowhere and it is not going anywhere, and I am not going to pretend you did not say it because saying it out loud is the only thing you have ever done well and I am not going to be the one who tells a man that is a trick.*”

“*I know I cannot get it.*”

“*You cannot get it and you are going to file it anyway,*” she said. “*That is what I want to say and I have been waiting since the morning you brought it into this room folded in four to say it, and I have not said it and you have not asked me whether you are going to, and so it has been in this room for twenty-four days without anybody in the room knowing it was in the room. I am saying it now because a thing in a room is a thing somebody can be asked about, and I have learned that from a book you cannot see, and that is the whole of what I have to teach a man with a leg in a month.*”

---

He did not say that it was true, and she did not ask him to.

“*Say the rest of it,*” he said.

“*The rest of it is that I am one of the people who will live under it, and I have been in this room for eleven years and there are four people at this door, and if that paper goes up that hill with my name at the bottom of the fifth clause then the answer to the third requirement is going to be me, alone, in this room, and there is no form in this city that says a man may ask a woman to answer for nine hundred doors, and there is no form that says I may answer for anybody but the people in this book, and the people in this book are not nine hundred doors, they are two hundred names, and it has been eleven years and I have got two hundred out of nine hundred and there is no mark in this city at all for a house that has emptied.*”

“*You could say no.*”

“*I could say no in this room on an ordinary morning and four people would hear it and it would be in nobody's book at all, and I have said a true thing out loud in this room exactly once in eleven years and I know precisely what that is worth, and it is worth nothing, and I have not said it again and I am not going to start on your account.*”

---

“*Then what would it be worth.*”

And she told him, and it took her a moment, and he understood that she had been carrying it since before he came into this city and had never once had anybody to say it to.

“*One thing at a time, at a door, to one person, and the person hears it and does what they do about it and I hear what they do about it from being the next one at that door,*” she said. “*That is the whole of what a sentence is worth in this ward and I have been finding that out for thirty-one years and nobody has ever put it in a book, and if you take it up that hill and put it in a fair hand then it is not thirty-one years of a woman saying a thing to a door, it is one woman in a room having said a thing, and in six months there will be a clerk who has read it and a clerk's clerk and a woman at a table in a room over a lock-and-ledger shop near the courts, and there will not be one door in this ward where anybody can say: she said that at my door.*”

She put the pen back down the other way up, which is a thing she does when she has finished a thing.

“*And I am not going to stand in a room on a hill and be asked to say that a year and fifty-six days is a price I accept,*” she said, “*because I have never been asked what it costs.*”

---

“*One more thing and then there are six people at my door,*” she said.

He waited.

“*If you file it, then from that day there is a person in this city who can be asked about a thing in a book, and it is not going to be me. I have been a name in a sheet in a building at the middle of the nine bridges since the tenth morning of your being in this city because of a yard, and that was before, and I did not consent to it, and I have not been able to get out of it, and you did that on purpose and I have never once said it was a kindness.*”

“*I know.*”

“*Then you know there is going to be two of us and that neither of us asked for it,*” said the woman with the book, “*and that is not a fault in the paper and it is not a fault in the city and it is not even a fault in you, and it is the price, and you have come in here and asked me what a round costs and I have told you, and it is not four hundred and twenty days. It is a room, and a fair hand, and a person at the top of the page, and no way back.*”

She got up and opened the door, and six people were at it, and she did not look at any of them.

“*Say the thing out loud before you do it,*” she said. “*Not to me. I have had it. To whoever it is you are going to do it in front of, and I want it in a book that is not mine, and I am telling you to do that as a person who is not going to be thanked for telling you anything.*”
