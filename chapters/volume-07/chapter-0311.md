# Chapter 311: The Six, Put Beside Each Other, And Not Joined

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-three thousand eight hundred and ninety-six at the second light on the fifth morning of the sixty-sixth week, and he was standing at the foot of the ladder and read it himself, which is the only way he has ever got a figure in this country, and the day it stood for was four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is the number most of the days in a year are.

It was his sixty-first morning. He went up the lane before the second bell with a leg that was the leg and nothing to put in it, and he went three miles to a village of about a hundred and ten people where there is one kitchen anybody will let a stranger stand in.

---

The man of sixty-three was at the end of the lane with a barrow and a crowbar and a drain that had wanted cutting out since the autumn, and he came in at about the third hour with his hands black to the wrist and put the barrow against the wall and did not say good morning, which is correct, and four people in that kitchen noticed that it was correct.

Nine people were in the kitchen. There is always nine people in it, because it is the kitchen at the end of the lane and it is where a village of a hundred and ten people does the thing it does instead of having a hall, and a man who has been in it three times knows that the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes.

He said the price first.

That is the shape of it. He has paid it four times in this county now and it does not get easier and it does not stop being worth saying, because a man who has said a thing three times does not stop saying it because the fourth time costs more.

“There is a thing I am going to do in this room in a minute,” he said, “and it is going to cost somebody, and I am saying the cost out loud before I do it because that is the only order that is any use. *Nobody in this kitchen is going to be asked anything. Nobody is going to have to answer for what I say and nobody is going to be on anything and nobody is going to be thanked and nobody is going to be relieved.* What happens instead is that a person in this village is going to know a thing about a place he has never been to, and he is going to know it because I said it in a kitchen, and there is nothing in this country that takes a knowing back and I have looked.”

The woman of about fifty-one at the churn said, “That is a great deal of warning for a man who has come up a lane to talk about a hedge.”

“It is,” he said. “I have come up a lane to talk about six things and I am going to say all six in nine minutes and I am not going to join any two of them, and I do not know yet whether I can do that and I am going to find out in front of you.”

Nobody said anything. The churn went round.

---

The six.

A boundary of four hundred yards of hedge in the third village up this lane, walked in nine days, by eleven names on the back of a flour bill in this kitchen, of whom nine come.

A set of beam scales on a stone plinth at the end of a main street in a market four days up this lane, in a county where nobody living has stood, with a man of seventy on it and a woman of about fifty-eight doing his sums, and four hundred loads a week in a season.

A line of nine diggers on a rise behind a hamlet eleven miles down the lane and off it, and a woman of thirty at the end of it who does not want to be.

A widow's fund in a room over a smithy in the tenth village, three pence a load, every penny of it out in the week it comes in, and one man of about thirty-eight reading a column and going four valleys away in about a month.

A mile of road, nine householders, a length apiece, ninety years, nobody paid.

A porch in a valley two miles off a track, and eleven names on a board on the end wall of it, and one woman of about seventy-four who is alive and has never been asked.

He said them in that order and he said each one the way a person says the name of a place they have been to, and he did not say one word about how they fitted together, and he was aware the whole time of the man of sixty-three standing at the table not looking at him, and of a room that had gone the way a kitchen of nine people goes quiet, and of the fact that he had just put six places in a room in the Briar March out loud, in order, in nine minutes, and that not one of the six is in this room.

Then he looked for the join, because he cannot help it, and it took about a second and a half, and what he found is the whole of what he has got and the whole of what it is worth this morning.

There is no join between them. He has spent seven weeks telling four people in nine yards that the line where one arrangement stops being true and another one starts is the only instrument he has ever had, and he has just put six arrangements in a row in a kitchen in the order he came up a lane, and a row is not a join. *Six words on one side of a mouth have no feet.* The thing he has been telling anybody is that a name at the foot of a thing is the only part of it anybody can be asked about, and there is nothing at the foot of this, and there is no form in this country that says a man may put six places side by side and call the side-by-side an arrangement, and he did it anyway, out loud, in a kitchen, because it was the only instrument he had and it is not an instrument.

Then he did the first pair, and it took him four minutes, and that is where the price came.

---

“Here is the thing I have got,” he said, “and it is not much, and I am going to say it about a place I am not in, and then somebody in this room is going to do something with it, and I will not know when.”

He put his hands flat on the table.

“There is a join in that market. *A thing stops being true and another thing starts, and I can see where, and I cannot tell anybody what either side of it is for.* I saw it on a weighbridge and it took about a second and a half and it is still there. On one side of it there are two people on a stone plinth in a street and on the other side there is a market with four hundred loads a week in it and a queue and a season and people who will tell two others before supper. And I cannot tell you what the first side is for and I cannot tell you what the second side is for.”

He stopped there. That was all he said about the market and it took four seconds.

“And what I am telling you is this. The join is not a thing that is broken. Nothing is wrong. The join is where it always is and it has been there about forty-four years. The join is what happens *when the day comes that one of the two of them is not standing on the plinth*, and there is no form anywhere in that town that says what fills a hole the size of a man, and the answer for forty-four years has been that a family stands in it. That is not a secret and nobody is hiding it and nobody there knows I can see it and the woman of fifty-eight has told a stranger no in nine words and has not been thanked and is not going to be.”

Nobody said anything.

“I have never been in that market. I have stood on that plinth for about an hour. I told nine people on a bridge that I can do this, and I cannot do one single thing with it, and it cost me the only thing I have, and nine people in that town know something about me that was not true at breakfast, and the version of it that is going round that town is bigger than what I said and there is nothing in four hundred miles that can put the right one back.”

He looked up.

“That is all of it. That is the whole of what I know about that market and it is a join I can see and cannot use. I am telling a kitchen in a village of a hundred and ten people because you are the first room in about a month where I have ever said a whole thing out loud and got to the end of it. *And now four people in this room know a thing about a market four days up a lane, and one of you is going to go and tell somebody, and that is the price, and it arrived in four minutes and it is not going back.*”

---

The man of sixty-three put his cup down.

Nobody had put the question. He had put it himself, in his own kitchen, in a voice with no weight on it, the way he had put the other one the day before a stranger walked a hedge, and it had taken him thirty years to happen twice.

“No,” he said. “No, I have not. Not in about thirty-one years and I have had the chance four hundred times.”

He looked at the man of thirty-one for the first time that morning.

“You have told me a thing about a place I have never been,” he said, “and it is the first time anybody has ever said anything to me in that kitchen that was not about the flour or the drain. And I am sixty-three and I have a knee and I walk four hundred yards in nine days and I can do eleven miles in a day and back if I start in the morning. There is a bar eleven miles down this lane and off it with a woman at it who has stood at that bar for twenty-two years. I have never asked her one thing. I am going to ask her one thing about that market and I am going to walk there in the morning and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to want to be.”

He did not say he was going to ask her what. He stood up and went out to his drain.

And that is how the price of that morning was paid, and it was not paid by a stranger and it was not sent by a stranger: *a man of sixty-three who had never asked anybody anything in thirty-one years asked a woman a question on his own leg, eleven miles, in the morning, and the asking was his and it had a cause, and neither of those two things can be taken back, and he is not a villain and he has hidden nothing and he is never going to be told that a stranger with a bad leg is the reason a woman at a bar has been asked a question for the first time in twenty-two years.*

---

Then he said the rest of it, standing up, in nine seconds, to nine people, and it was a decision and not a speech and he had made it four minutes before he said it.

“I am going to say one thing about each of the other five,” he said. “One thing, in a room, to a person, about the arrangement in their own place. I am not going to give anybody a paper and there is nowhere to give one. And I am not going to be in the rooms when what I said arrives. *If I am in the room, then what happens in that room is a thing a stranger did, and the only thing I have learned in sixty-one days is that the second time somebody is asked is the thing that matters and a stranger makes the second time impossible.*”

The woman of fifty-one said, “That is a strange thing to be proud of.”

“It is not pride,” he said. “It is arithmetic. I said in a gateway four days ago that I cannot be in two of these places at once, and nobody asked me about it, and I have been carrying it four days, and I have not thought of a way round it, and I have not looked for one, and there is no form in this country that says a man may send anybody.”

He picked his cup up and it was cold and he drank it anyway.

“Nobody is thanked. I am not thanked and she is not thanked and he is not thanked and the man of sixty-three has just told you he is not going to want to be, and that is the correct arrangement and I would not trust any of the six of you who said otherwise.”

He went out into the lane and it was raining a little and the drain was going and the man of sixty-three was in it up to the knee, and they did not say one word to each other for about an hour, and the day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is the number most days in a year are, and there was nobody at the bar at the bottom of that lane who was told anything about any of it.
