# Chapter 347: A Man With A Cart Says The Seventh Thing Out Loud And Nobody Asked Him

No figure for this morning. Two miles up a lane at the far end of it there is a wall, and the day goes on it at the second light and at no other hour, and at the second light this morning a woman of forty-one was on a ladder against it. He was a mile off at the top gate of a road with nine houses on it with a leg.

The day came to four bells and a stranger, which is seven, and the stranger is him, and there has not been a stranger in that lane since he came through it with a sentence in his mouth in the middle of the last month and was stopped by a man with a cart.

It was his ninety-seventh morning. That is eight mornings after he stood at the top of a bridge with one rail on it in a valley of sixty people, and seven days after a woman of about thirty-four at a bar said a thing at a cross, and fourteen days after a man of thirty-four said out loud that nobody in a room believed the stranger was going to be here in a year.

---

He went up the mile at about the sixth hour because there was nothing else to do with the morning, and the third of the nine was at the third length with a scythe out, doing the way a man does a thing he has done every year of his life, and the scythe had not been out on the length above and the beast was not in the second field.

“You have come to the wrong end of it,” said the man of thirty. “*I do not need telling on this morning. I have had a fortnight of it and I have not said a word about any of it and I am not going to start because you have turned up with a leg and nothing to do.*”

He went on mowing. He has not been thanked for that length in ninety years and he did not say so and he is not going to be thanked for it.

---

The stranger stood at the top gate for an hour and did not ask him a single question, and that is also the way of it, and at about the ninth hour the man of thirty stopped and put the back of the scythe into the ground and said the thing nobody had asked him for.

It is not about a hedge and it is not about a mile of road.

“You have been asking everybody what the sixth is,” he said. “In a kitchen and in a room over a smithy and in a yard behind that smithy, and a boy of seventeen has told you there are seven, and I have listened to it twice and I have not said a word because I am the third of nine on a mile of road and I do not get a vote and I have said that before.*”

He looked at the top gate and not at the stranger.

“So I am going to say it and then I am going to mow my length, and you can do what you like with it.”

He did not stop mowing to say it. He said it flatly, at the rate of a man reading a length off a board, with the scythe going.

“*The seventh thing in that round is the porch.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*It is not a place and it is not a stone and it is not a thing anybody laid. It is eleven names on a board in a porch in a valley of sixty people, and it has been in that porch since before her mother was born and it has not been used since before her mother was born, and there is one woman of about seventy-four who goes down there four mornings a year and does the whole of it herself and has done for forty years, and she is not on the list, and she is not one of the eleven, and she is not a name at the foot of anything.*”

He shifted the scythe and a foot of the length went past.

“And there is nobody in this county who has ever counted her. Not the man who reads things out, not the man of sixty-three, not the boy with the book, not you, and not her. And I know that because I have been on that road ninety years and the road goes past a gate that goes down to that porch and I have never once seen anybody come up it but her, and I have not stopped on it in forty years because I did not know that was what it was.*”

---

And the stranger stood at the top gate of a mile of road with a leg and did not say one word for nine seconds, and then he said the thing, and the thing was one word.

“Right,” he said.

And the man of thirty said: “*You are not going to say anything about it and that is the right answer and I want it noted that you did not say anything about it, because I have been waiting nine days to see what you were going to do about it and I have watched two of your mornings from this length and I have not worked it out and I have not got a better way of saying it than that.*”

He went back to the scythe.

“And do not go up there,” he said, “and that is not me telling a stranger what to do with his leg. That is me telling you what I have seen from a length of a mile of road, and I have seen smoke off a porch twice in nine days and I have not gone up it, and that is a thing about me and not about you.*”

---

He did not go up there.

He had it in four seconds and the four seconds were the right four seconds and he wrote them down in his own head in a lane and did not say them to anybody, and here they are in his own words and not in anybody's mouth.

*She asked me on a bridge eight mornings ago whether I was going to say anything about that porch, and she asked it in the way a person asks a thing they have already decided the answer to, and I said I was not, and she wanted it said out loud, and I said it. She has been the instrument for forty years and nobody has ever asked her whether she would rather not be, and she said that to me, and she said that if she stopped in the spring then it stopped in the spring and that would be correct. And there is no form in this country that says a man may put a question to a woman of seventy-four about a thing in her own porch, and I am not going to be the man who invents one at eight days' notice because a man with a scythe has told me what is in it.*

And the second half of the four seconds, which is the half he did not say and will not say in this county.

*And the last time I asked a person of a place a question in a room in front of other people, it cost a woman of thirty-four at a bar a thing she cannot put down and is never going to be thanked for and cannot say no to, and I said the price out loud first and it did not make it smaller, and there is nobody in four hundred miles that can take her out of it. And a porch is not a room and a porch has been shut since before her mother was born and there is nobody in that valley who is going to say anything to me about anything.*

---

So the round has seven turns in it and the seventh is a porch, and he did not go and look for it and he did not send anybody, and the man of thirty-four went up the lane on six mornings with no order in his head and got four of the six and has not got the other two, and the boy of seventeen has not said a name and will not, and a woman of about thirty-four at a bar is a person in a record, and a man of thirty-eight is four days up a road with a bag and a bad back and nobody has asked him what he wants.

And nothing about the porch is settled. That is the honest shape of the morning and it is the first time in this country that he has had the answer to a thing and been unable to use it, and being unable to use it is not the same as being right about it, and he has not got a sentence for that either.

---

The stranger in that day's weight is a man with a bad leg who stood at the top gate of a mile of road for an hour and did not ask a question, and a man of thirty mowed the third of nine lengths and did not say thank you or do not thank me and there is nobody in that lane who is going to say either, and two miles off a track a porch with eleven names on a board in it was not visited and was not looked at and was not asked about, and a woman of about seventy-four did not know that any of it had been said, and that is the way of it.
