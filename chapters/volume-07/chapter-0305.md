# Chapter 305: The Offer

There is no plank at the end of this street, and there is no figure on a wall at the second light, and nobody in this market town has ever heard of a board at Coldharrow or a woman of forty-one who keeps one, and a man of thirty-one did not come four days up a lane to talk about it.

It was his fifty-fifth morning in this country. The sixth day of the sixty-fifth week came to four bells, and a burial at the far end of the street, and a fire in a yard behind a row of carts that was out before anybody got to it, and none of the three was anybody's business, and about nine loads had gone over the bridge by the time the sun was off the roofs.

---

The scales are the whole of the town and the town is the whole of the market.

There is a weighbridge at the end of the main street with a set of beam scales on a stone plinth, and the plinth has been re-cut three times in a hundred years, and a man keeps them. The man is about seventy and his name is on nothing and he has been at that bridge for about forty-four years and he does not do the sums any more and has not done them for about nine years.

His daughter does the sums. She is about fifty-eight. She has been doing them since she was forty-nine and she does them in her head, and she does them while she is doing four other things, and four hundred loads go over that bridge in a week in market season and every one of them is weighed and every one of them is written. *He weighs them and she knows what they are, and that is the whole division of it, and there is no paper in this town that says which of the two of them is the arrangement.*

Nobody is paid. There is no wage and no fund and no form and no day anywhere in it, and the two people who do the work are the only two anybody would ask about, and there is nobody in this town to ask them except each other. The bridge is a set of stones and two beams and a plinth, and the plinth belongs to nobody in particular and the beams were his father’s and his father’s before him, and it is kept the way a lane is kept, which is by everybody and by a man in particular.

He will not let anybody else over the bridge. That is the arrangement and it is the whole of it. She has asked him twice whether he would let a man in who knew what he was doing, and he has said no both times, and she has never once asked him a third time.

---

He stood at the plinth for about an hour and did not say anything, and at about the second hour a carter came up with nine quarters of good chalk and would not go over it until his own mate came out of the shade and moved the carriage by about a finger’s width with his heel, and the carter looked at the beam and nodded and went on, and nobody in that street had said a word about it.

He waited for the ninth load and then he said the price, out loud, on the bridge, in about nine seconds, to about nine people who were standing about it because it is a market and there is nowhere else to stand.

It was not the price of the technique. It had never been the price of the technique; the price of the technique is a person finding out about themselves in a room, and he had paid that twice in this county and once in a valley four hundred miles behind him, and he said it out loud anyway, because a man who has said it three times does not stop saying it because the fourth time is worse.

Then he said the other thing, and he had never said it to anybody, and he had known he could do it for about two years.

“There is a line,” he said, “where one arrangement stops being true and another one starts. I can see where it is. I cannot write it down and I cannot show it to anybody and you cannot check it, and it is the only thing I have got, and I have never said it out loud to a living soul in about two years and I am going to say it out loud to about nine of you now, on this bridge, because you are the first place in two months where nine people can be told a thing at the same time and it is the only chance I am going to get.”

Nine people at a bridge at the end of a market morning.

“I can do this for the town. I will stand here on a market day and I will tell you where your arrangements meet, and I cannot tell you what either side of them is for, and I will not pretend to, and that is what I am offering. I am offering it now and nobody asked me to and nobody in this country knows I can do it, and in about four seconds nine of you will know, and there is nothing in the world that can take a knowing back. Not a form, not a word, not a fee. *You will know it for the rest of your lives and I cannot un-know it and I knew that when I said it.*”

About four of the nine people understood the whole of that and the rest heard a man talk.

---

She said no before he had finished the word that came after the second sentence of the offer, and she did not say it unkindly, and she did not say it quickly, and the whole of it was nine words and she got all nine of them out before he had finished the second sentence of what he was offering.

“A thing nobody can do,” she said, “is a thing nobody has to pay anybody for.”

Nobody said anything.

“You have done your arithmetic,” she said, “and you have done it faster than I have ever done one in my life, and I am going to finish it for you because you have left it in the middle and because I am the person who does it. *If one person in a market can do it, then the day he is not here is a day with no scales, and four hundred loads a week go over somebody else’s guess.* And I have watched a man of about seventy try to guess at a beam for nine years and he gets it right about nine times in ten, and I have got a child coming in the spring, and I am going to be stood up here at the spring instead of doing the sums, and that is the whole of what I am afraid of and you took it out of my hands in about nine seconds and you did not even know you were doing it.”

He did not say anything.

“You said nobody asked you to offer,” she said. “I believe you, and it does not help, and I want you to hear the shape of it. *You have offered this to a market and not to me, and about nine of you heard it, and there are about nine people in this town who will now go home and tell two others before supper.* In a week there will be a man in a tavern saying there is a man on the bridge who can see a join, and in a month somebody will be standing on this bridge waiting for you, and the day after that they will be standing on it when you are not here, and my father will be standing on it with a beam and no sums, and that is not a market, that is a queue for a man with one leg from another country. I did not want to be asked and now I have been asked, and you are a stranger, and strangers are the people who are hardest to say no to in a market where everyone watches.”

He said, “That is the best no anybody has given me in fifty-five days.”

“It is the only no,” she said. “Do not make it better than it is.”

---

Her father had not turned round once. He was seventy and he had his hands behind his back and he had been at that bridge for about forty-four years and he had heard nine words and about four hundred loads and he did not turn round now.

“We call it an anchor,” he said, to the stones, in a voice that was not for anybody. “That is what the people in this town call the two of us on a market morning. *An anchor is not a thing. It is the plinth, and the day, and the two of us, and the nine times in ten I got it right before she took it off me.* It is not a relic and it is not in a box and there is nothing to find. When I cannot stand on this bridge there is a hole in this market the size of me, and there is no form anywhere that says what fills a hole the size of a man, and the answer for forty-four years has been that my family stands in it.”

And the man of thirty-one stood about nine feet off that plinth and looked at the two of them, and he could see the join.

He could see it perfectly. It was there and it was clear and it was not a place. It was where the arrangement stopped being a father and a daughter on a stone plinth in a street and started being a market, with four hundred loads in it and a queue and a season and about nine people who would tell two others before supper, and there is a join there the way there is a join at the end of a hedge where a field begins.

He could see it and he could not say what either side of it was for.

He had said, four hours ago, on a bridge, in front of nine people, that he would tell them where their arrangements met, and he could do that. He could do that at the end of the street, at the gate of a bar, in a kitchen, in a tower, on the back of a flour bill. And he could not tell one person in this town what a single thing on either side of that join was *for*, and he had spent fifty-five days and four hundred miles finding that out, and the whole of what he had come here with was a way of seeing where things join and no way whatever of saying what a thing is for, and there are about nine people in this country who can say exactly what theirs is for and not one of them can see the join.

Nothing happened on the bridge. Nobody was relieved. No instrument improved. Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him to stay and the market went on.

He did not apologise, and she did not ask him to, and the two of them agreed about that without discussing it, which is a thing that happens between people who have both been in a market.

And on the fifth day of the walk, in a town four days up a lane, a man of thirty-one walked out of the end of the main street with a leg and a boy of seventeen and a man of fifty-two, and about nine people in that town knew something about him that had not been true at breakfast, and he could not take it back, and it had cost him the only thing he has and the only nine people who know about it, and there is no instrument in this country that puts either of those back, and he knew the price before he said the sentence and said it anyway.

He had been in this country fifty-five days, and he had found out what he was, and it was not that he can see a join.
