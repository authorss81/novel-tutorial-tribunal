# Chapter 306: Nine People Know

He came down the lane on a cart, and the cart was a two-wheeled timber thing with the load taken off it, and the beast was a grey one about fourteen hands with feet that were bad in the wet, and it did four days of walking in a day and a half, and the man of thirty-one sat on the shafts for the last two miles because the shafts were the only thing that did not hurt.

It was the last day of the sixty-fifth week and his fifty-sixth morning in this country, and the day at the bottom end of the lane, where he had been five days before, had come to four bells and one more that was on nobody's list.

He was not going to find out what the one more was. The woman of forty-one would know by the second bell, standing on the third of the four rungs with a cloth over her shoulder, and she would not tell him, and nobody was going to ask her, and that is the correct arrangement and he was not going to be the one who broke it.

---

He had done the arithmetic of the last four days in about nine minutes a day, standing up, on a cart, in a lane, to nobody, and the whole of it came to this.

The morning after the offer, the town knew. He had said out loud on a bridge that nine people would know, and the ninth person had told two others before supper, exactly as she had said he would, and by the time he got to the cart at the far end of the main street there were about forty people in that market town who had heard that a man with a bad leg could see where two things join.

By that evening there was a version of it that was larger than what he had said, and two men had already begun the two days of riding that it was going to cost them. He heard the version from a carter on a hill and he wrote it down in his own head so that it could not grow: *he can tell you what a thing is hiding and he has not charged anybody and there is a man in a market four days up the lane that nobody has to pay.* That was the version. It was wrong in three places and it was being repeated by people who had never been in the room, and there was no instrument in four hundred miles that could put the right one back in, and he could not have corrected it if he had stood in every yard in that town at once.

And on the morning after the offer, which was the morning he left, two men had come up the lane and gone back down it again, and they were in the market street at about the first hour.

---

They overtook the cart two miles above the bar.

There is no bar up there. There is a gate, and a hedge, and a place where the lane bends for a pond, and two men on a pair of horses came past him at about the sixth hour at a walk, and they did not go past him, they stopped, which is the worst thing a rider can do.

The older one was about forty-six. The younger one was about thirty and had his father’s face and none of his father’s patience.

“You are him,” said the older one.

“I am.”

“We have been up,” said the older one, “and we have been down, and it is two days’ riding and we are doing it on the strength of a thing a carter’s boy said in a yard.”

He did not say what the carter’s boy had said.

“We asked her,” he said. “We went to her straight away, before we came down, because she is the one people ask and we did not go to anybody else. We asked her whether you were what they said you were.”

“And what did she say.”

“She said it was a wet road,” said the older one.

He waited, in the way a man waits who has expected better and has got the ordinary thing and is not going to complain about it.

“That is all she said. *It is a wet road.* And she said it to us in a street with about nine people in it and then she went back to the scales, and my brother here has been carrying it for two days and he is not the man to be honest with you, I am. He wanted to come up a third time.”

“I did,” said the younger one.

“That is the whole of it,” said the older one. “I am not here to tell you that you are a fraud, because you did not say you were anything except a man who can see where two things meet, and my brother here thought you said you could tell us what a thing is hiding. And I have thought about that for two days and I have decided that it does not matter, and here is why, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to ride home.

“*There is no use in a thing that is not a day.* A wet road is a wet road. A thing is a thing on a day, and a man is a man on a day, and if you are here on a market day then you are here, and if you are not here then there is a man of about seventy on a bridge with a beam and a woman of fifty-eight doing his sums, and that is what there is, and it has been what there is for forty-four years, and it will be what there is on the day you are not here, and nobody is going to write anything down about you on either day.”

He touched the horse’s neck.

“So there is nothing to have,” he said. “I have come two days to tell you there is nothing to have and I have said it and I am going home. I am not being kind. I have nine quarters of chalk at home that came over your bridge two days ago and I did not know whether to believe them and now I do, and that is worth two days on a horse. My brother here is going to keep saying that you can tell a man what he is hiding at a market, and he is wrong, and I am going to tell him he is wrong for the rest of his life, and you did not cost me anything to do it.”

They rode on down the lane. The cart went on behind them. And four days up the lane, a woman of fifty-eight stood at a set of scales in a market street and put a weight on a beam she had not read for nine years, and nobody in that town ever knew she had said nine words to two men she had met once.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody will ever know. She said no to a whole market instead of to nine people, in a street, in daylight, alone, and the price of that is the price of everything in this country, which is that nobody finds out.

---

The man of fifty-two did not speak for about a mile after that, and then he did, and he did not stop walking, and there was a road on his right and a hedge on his left and nobody in it.

“I am going to say a thing once,” he said, “and then I am not going to say it again this year, and I have been not saying it for nineteen years and you may as well have it.

“*A place under a corridor cannot be the scene of a hearing about its own standing.* That is the finding of a valley four hundred miles behind us and a man of fifty-two has been carrying it about a week and has not got the rest of it. A district that has been set aside cannot hold its own hearing, because a thing that is set aside has no standing and standing is what a hearing is made of. So a place under a corridor has to send somebody. *And the somebody has no standing either.*”

He shifted the reins.

“And I have been the somebody in nine counties in nineteen years, and there is no form for it anywhere, and nobody sent me the first time and nobody has sent me since, and I have gone every time, and nobody has ever once asked me whether I would go, and I have not been asked whether I would stop.”

He was quiet for about nine seconds.

“Nobody has ever asked me what I am the only person in.”

He said it in the voice of a man reporting a fact about a beam, and he did not look round, and he was not inviting a question, and about four hundred yards of lane went past in which nothing whatever was said.

Ilyan asked him the first question anybody had asked him about that in fifty-six days, and he asked it badly, and he asked it out loud, and it was the only thing in four days of walking that he had got ready.

“What were you the only person in.”

The man of fifty-two did not answer and did not refuse, and about half a mile went by.

“That is a different road,” he said, “and a longer one, and you will have to come and find me when you have time to sit down for it.”

The cart went on down the lane. The lane came to the bar, and the bar was not shut, and there was a woman of about fifty with a board under her arm who did not take it out, because the man of thirty-one was not a stranger and had not been a stranger for about a month, and about four people in that yard noticed that she did not take it out and about nine did not.

Four hundred yards further on, a woman of forty-one came down off a ladder at about the second hour, and put a cloth over her shoulder, and went to do her day, and there was a stroke on the plank that neither of them could account for, and neither of them said a word about it, and he got down off the cart about a mile short of his bed with a leg that was the leg and the lane behind him that he had walked four days up in a country that counts bells.
