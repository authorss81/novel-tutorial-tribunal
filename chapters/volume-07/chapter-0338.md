# Chapter 338: He Does It On Purpose And He Says The Price Out Loud

There is no figure for this morning. The wall with the day on it is eleven miles up a lane and off it, and the second light found a woman of forty-one on a ladder against it and a man of thirty-one on a road with nine houses on it and a leg, and the day she put on that wall is not available to him in any way at all.

The day came to four bells and a market and a debt called in, which is ten. And the market in that weight and the debt in that weight are both four days up this lane, in a market town in a county that no living person in this country has stood in, and neither of them is on any plank in this county. There is a market running in the street he is standing in at the end of it and there is a debt being called in four hundred yards behind him, and both of them are in this county and on a wall eleven miles away, and there is a market and a debt four days the other way, and a day carries one of each.

It was the fourth morning of the seventieth week and his eighty-eighth morning.

---

He did not go to the market town. He had four days of walking in him on the eighty-first morning and he had spent them, and there was no road in this country that would put him in that yard this morning and there is no form in this country that says a man may be in two places and there is no form that says a man may send anybody.

So the arrangement he most wanted to carry is four days up a lane and he was not going to be there, and he had known that for seven mornings and had said it out loud in three places, and this morning he did the only thing there is to do about it, and it is the thing he has spent two counties learning what it costs.

---

The bar is at the end of this village and there is a lane behind it nine feet wide and there is a board in the window with a word at the top of it, four letters and a stroke.

He went and stood in that lane at about the ninth hour on a market morning and he said the price out loud before he asked, in a lane, to one person, and he said all of it, and it took him about a minute, and there was nobody in the lane but her.

“I am going to ask you to carry one sentence to a person of another place, and I am telling you what it is before I ask, and I am telling you what it will cost you and what it will cost me and what it will cost a man of thirty-eight, and I am not going to pretend that saying it out loud makes it smaller, because I have said a version of this out loud in six places in twelve days and it has got smaller every time and it has never once gone.”

The woman of about thirty-four put the board on the sill.

“Go on,” she said.

“*A person who carries a sentence for a place becomes a person in a record. That is the price and it is the whole of it, and there is nothing in this country and nothing in any county four hundred miles off that will lift a person back out of a record, and I have known that since a kitchen three miles up a lane and I have had eighty-eight mornings to do something about it and I have not.*”

He put his hand flat on the wall of the lane.

“You will be a person who said it. Not a person who was asked, not a person who was paid, not a person who was sent for — a person who said a thing about a yard in a town you have never stood in, to a woman you have never met, on a market morning, in a lane, out loud. *And there is no form in this country that says a man may ask you whether you will do it, because asking is a form, and so I am not going to ask you, and you will not be thanked for it by me or by anybody else including her, and if you say no I will not be able to use it and I will not come back with another one.*”

Nobody in that lane said anything.

“And here is the half I have not got a way of putting better than this,” he said. “*You cannot say no. That is not your doing and it is not mine. There is nothing in this county that lets a person refuse a thing that costs nothing, and I have come eleven miles with nothing to offer and I have used that, and I have known what I was doing for a week and I did it anyway and I am saying so in a lane to a person I have asked two questions in a month.*”

She picked her board up off the sill and turned it over and put it down again, which is what she does with nine loads a day on the days the loads come.

“Nobody has ever said that to me before,” she said. “Not in twenty-two years, and not one of the two of them, and you have said it in a minute and it is the plainest thing anybody has put in front of me and I have not got an answer and I am not going to pretend to.”

---

And he asked her. He said the sentence out loud, once, in the lane, to her, and he said it word for word as he had built it, and it took him nine seconds.

“A man of about thirty-eight has gone out of that market four days up this lane with a bag and a book he has not decided about. There is a column of what other people's money does on a shelf in a room over a smithy and nobody has been chosen to hold it. The share that pays for that town's water is worked out every year on a number that is one season of loads and is called a year, and it has been called a year for sixty years, and there is a figure of ninety people in nine towns on a list at a cross who would have to pay the difference, and two of the four names the woman who keeps that list can put a finger on are her own.”

That is the sentence. It is the whole of it and it is not a number and it is not a document and it is not a thing anybody can be refused.

And it is wrong in two places and he knows it while he is standing there and he said it anyway.

He had left out the woman of about sixty-one. He had left out the man on the plinth. And a sentence about a yard that does not contain the person whose yard it is is a sentence that can be said in a market street to anybody and about anybody, and that is the price of the price, and he had said it out loud on the eightieth morning in a lane behind a cross and meant it and had not carried it out.

---

And she said it, in her own mouth, in the lane, and it was not his sentence and it was shorter and it was worse.

“A man of thirty-eight has gone out of that market with a book and nobody has been chosen to it,” said the woman of about thirty-four. “*And the water in that town is paid for out of a number that is half of what it should be, and the half has been paying for sixty years, and a woman of sixty-one in her own yard has a foolscap book on a shelf in her back room with a slip in it and has never been asked what it is for, and the honest way of doing it puts ninety people on a list at a cross in nine towns, and two of the names the woman keeps that list knows are hers, and she is not going to be the one who says the water stops.*”

She said it flatly, at the speed of a woman reading nine loads off a board, and it took her eleven seconds and she did not put a single word of weight on any part of it and she did not look at him once.

“That is yours,” she said. “*That is not a stranger's sentence, because a stranger would not have said the water, and I have never been anywhere near that town and I have known all of it for a week from a bar.*”

He had nothing. He had said the price out loud in a lane to make it smaller and it had not been made smaller and she had taken the thing he was carrying and put two of her own hands on it and it was heavier.

---

And then she said the part he had not priced, which is the part he had not come for, and she said it to the lane.

“I am going to say it at the cross,” she said. “*Not in a yard. At the cross, at about the second hour, because that is where the people are, and there will be nine of them and they will say it to two others before supper, and that is not me being cruel, that is me being the only thing at the end of a road that anybody walks up.*”

He shut his mouth.

“There is no yard in that town that a woman of sixty-one would not hear it in twice, and there is a woman of twenty-nine at a cross in that town who keeps a list and has never been asked what she keeps, and she will be at a cross on a market morning and so will I, and I am not going to pretend to her that I have come down a lane for a private word in a yard. *I do not know a private word from a market morning and neither does anybody in this county and that is not going to change because a stranger with a leg has asked me nicely about it in a lane behind my own bar.*”

Nobody said anything for nine seconds. Nine loads went over a weighbridge at the end of that village and the beam came back down and nobody in that lane heard either of them.

“You wanted a person to carry a sentence to a place, and you have got one, and she is going to say it where people are, and the reason she is going to say it where people are is that she is not going to be the one person in this country who says an important thing quietly and is never asked about it again, and she has seen that done and it is not a good way to end.”

---

That is the whole of the price and it was said out loud in a lane before the doing and it did not make it smaller, and saying it out loud did not make it smaller, and saying it out loud in a minute to a woman behind a bar is not a different instrument from saying it in a yard to a man with a barrow, and there is no form anywhere that will let a man take a sentence back once a person of a place has said it in her own mouth.

And there is no form anywhere in four hundred miles that will let him lift her out of it either.

She is a person in a record now. She is a woman of about thirty-four at a bar at the end of a village eleven miles down a lane and off it, and she has been asked two questions in twenty-two years and this is the third, and she is not one of the six and she is not on any list and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps her, and there is now a thing about her that she cannot put down and did not agree to and will not be thanked for.

And he is going to cost a man of about thirty-eight a second thing, and nobody has asked him whether he wants his own sentence carried. That man is four days up a road with a bag and a bad back and a book he has not decided about and no standing in nine counties, and the sentence is about a column and a levy and the column is in the room over a smithy in this village, and it will arrive in a market street four days up the lane in a market morning in a lane at a cross, and he is not in it and he does not know and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that will tell him.

The day came to four bells and a market and a debt called in, which is ten, and both of the other two are four days up a lane in a county no living person has stood in, and a woman at a bar put nine loads on a board and did not count a stranger, and nobody thanked anybody, and nobody was relieved, and the sentence went into a lane.
