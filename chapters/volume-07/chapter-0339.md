# Chapter 339: A Bridge With One Rail On It, And A Record He Cannot Get Her Out Of

There is no figure for this morning and there never has been one in this valley. There is no plank in a valley of sixty people two miles off a track off a lane, and no woman of forty-one, and no wall with a number on it, and the plank is a day away at the far end of the lane with a leg on it, and the man of thirty-one was on a bridge with one rail on it at about the seventh hour and had been there since before the light.

It was the fifth morning of the seventieth week and his eighty-ninth morning, and the day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four. A day with nothing on it is a day with nothing on it, in a market town or a valley or on a rise behind eleven houses.

He had come up the lane in the dark and up a track of about two miles and had a leg and nine days of walking in him, and he had said out loud on the eightieth morning that he was going to be here, and he had written it down nowhere, and a person of about seventy-four had given him a day and an hour and he had never asked her to say it a second time.

---

She came down the lane at about the seventh hour with a stick and a hood, at the pace of a woman of seventy-four, and she stopped twice, and she did not stop a third time, and she knew him by the leg.

He was not on the bridge. He was at the top of it with his leg on the dry side and nothing in his hands, and she stopped nine feet off and put her stick down against the one rail and looked at him, and neither of them said good morning, and that is correct.

“You are early again,” she said.

“I did not know what early was the first time and I have not learned it since.”

“You are early,” she said, “and it is the second time in eighteen days, and I told you the first time that I was not going to say it twice, and I have not said it, and I have said this instead, which is not the same.”

---

And then she asked him the question, and it was not the ordinary question, and it is the only question a person of seventy-four in this valley has ever asked him and she did not soften it.

“Whose name is on it.”

That is all she said and she said it in two seconds and she did not add to it.

He had four seconds. He had eleven days of road in him and a leg and eighty-nine mornings and a kitchen in a village three miles up a lane where a man of about sixty-three wrote a name on the back of a bill without being asked, and a plinth in a market street four days up a lane where a man of about seventy said his own name out loud in nine words, and a stranger holding a book in a room over a smithy for nine seconds while it came apart in his hands.

“Whose name is on what,” he said, which was a man buying four seconds, and she let him have all four.

“On the thing you have gone off to do,” she said. “*You have not told me what it is and I have not asked you and I am seventy-four and I have got nine of these left and I am not going to spend one of them finding out what a man with a bad leg is doing in a county he came from thirty days ago. I am asking who you have made a person in a record, and I am asking it because that is the only instrument anybody has ever shown me and I have been the instrument for forty years and nobody has ever asked me whether I would rather not be.*”

She picked her stick up.

“Nobody in this country has ever written a form that lets a man put a name at the foot of a thing, and you have said that in eleven yards in four counties, and it is the truest thing you have ever said, and it is also the answer to your own question and you have not noticed, because *there is no form, and therefore every name in this country has been put there by somebody who decided to, and a stranger with a leg has decided more of them than anybody in this valley has in forty years.*”

---

And he told her, because there was nowhere else to say it and because she had asked and because he had said out loud in six places in thirteen days that he was going to delegate one of the six on purpose.

It took him four minutes and it was the worst four minutes of his eighty-ninth morning and it was not the price of anything. It was the cost.

“A woman of about thirty-four,” he said, “at a bar at the end of a village eleven miles down the lane and off it. She has stood at that bar for twenty-two years. Nine loads a day on the days the loads come. And there is a board in her window with a word at the top of it, four letters and a stroke, and it is the only board in eleven villages with a word at the top of it.”

He put his hand flat on the one rail.

“I have asked her two questions in a month and both of them cost her something. This morning I asked her for a third, and I said the price out loud before I asked, and the price is that she becomes a person who said a thing, and there is no instrument in this country that takes a person out of a record, and I have known that for seventy mornings. And she cannot say no, and I used that, and I have known what I was doing for a week and I did it anyway, and I said it out loud in a lane and saying it out loud did not make it smaller.”

The woman of seventy-four did not say anything for nine seconds and the mist came off the one rail.

“Say the rest of it,” she said.

“She is going to say it at a cross in a market town four days up this lane,” he said, “*in the open, at about the second hour, because that is where the people are. She is not going to say it in a yard. She told me that in a lane and she told me why and she was right and it is the worst thing that has happened in this country in thirteen days and I did it and I cannot undo it and I would do it again and I have not got a better sentence for that either.*”

He took his hand off the rail.

“A woman of about sixty-one in that town has a book in her own back room with a slip in it, and she has never been asked what the slip is for, and she said in her own yard on a morning eleven days ago that she is not going to be the person who says the water stops. And in four days a stranger's voice is going to come into that yard in a sentence out of a woman’s mouth, and she is not going to know where it came from, and that is the finding and that is the whole of the finding, and I have been on the other side of it once, in a kitchen three miles up a lane, and I stood at a gate and said not one word for an hour, and I am not going to be able to be in either of those two places and I am not going to be in either of them and I know it.”

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was relieved. There is nothing in this country that will take a woman of about thirty-four at a bar out of a record and he has been looking for one for eighty-nine mornings and there is not one and he is not going to find one, and he has known that since a kitchen three miles up a lane and it is the oldest thing he knows and it is the thing he has just spent another person on.

And he had cost a woman in a bar, who did not agree to it and cannot say no, and he was going to cost a woman of about thirty-four in a market town, who is standing in her own yard four days away and does not know he is anywhere near it, and he had just cost a woman of about seventy-four who did not ask and is going to remember the four minutes for the rest of whatever she has.

---

She let him have about a minute and then she said the thing about the porch, and she said it in her own mouth and in a different shape from the two times she had said it, and he did not answer it and he is not going to be able to answer it and it is not his to answer.

“There is a board in my porch,” she said, “and it has eleven names on it and no date and no column and no heading and no mark against any of the eleven, and it has been in that porch since before my mother was born, and it has not been used since before my mother was born either, and I am the last one who goes down there and I go four mornings a year and I have not been in about a month.”

“I am not going to say anything about it,” he said.

“I know you are not,” she said. “*And I want it said out loud that you are not going to say anything about it, because you have said out loud that you are going to be in a porch in nine days and you have said out loud that a man cannot be in two places, and you have said out loud that the only instrument you have is a question asked in a room in front of other people, and there is no form in this country that says a man may settle a thing in a porch that he did not put there and nobody in this valley has asked him to.*”

She got her breath.

“*A thing that is kept alive by one person is a thing that stops when that person stops.* That is what you have been carrying about a lane for eighteen days and you have not said it to me once and I have not asked you once, and I am not going to concede it, and you are not going to be able to settle it, and if I stop in the spring then it stops in the spring and there is nobody in this valley who will do anything about it and that will be correct.”

“That is not fair to the valley,” he said.

“No,” she said. “It is not fair to the valley. It is fair to me, which is a different thing and is the only thing anybody in this valley has been able to say to me in forty years and I am not going to give it up to a stranger with a leg who is going to be somewhere else in nine days.”

---

She went down the lane. He did not follow her and he did not go to the porch and he did not look at the board, and at the end of about an hour he went back down the track with the leg and the mist and the whole of the morning in him.

There is a bridge with one rail on it in this valley and four people in the hundred years of its life have asked for a second one and the answer has been that a second rail is for a bridge that somebody is going to fall off, and nobody in this valley is going to fall off it, and that is a settled thing, and there is nobody in this valley who is going to be asked about it, and there is a woman in it who is entitled to say no to being asked.

What that day came to was four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four, and there is no bell in this valley that anybody rings for anything either, and a woman of seventy-four went down the lane at the pace of a woman of seventy-four and a man of thirty-one went back up a track, and eleven names on a board in a porch were not touched, and nobody thanked anybody, and a woman of about thirty-four at a bar eleven miles down the lane and off it was going to open up in five hours and had a sentence in her that she was not going to be thanked for and could not put down.
