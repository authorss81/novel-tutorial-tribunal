# Chapter 336: A Thing That Stops When The Ninth Stops, And He Is Not The Ninth

There is no figure for this morning and there never is one in this place. There is no plank in this hamlet and there is no woman of forty-one and there is no wall in this county with a number on it, and the plank is a lane and a day away and the man of thirty-one was not at the foot of it at the second light because he was on a rise behind eleven houses with a leg and a dry morning.

It was the second morning of the seventieth week and his eighty-sixth morning, and the day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four, and there is nothing on that day at all, and a day with nothing on it does not care what county a man is standing in, and he was on a rise above forty people four hundred miles from a valley with a corridor standing in it.

---

There is a line of nine.

Not a written one. Nobody has ever written it and nobody can say who dug the first hole, and the ground is deeper on the side the family came in from, and the near gate is at the low end of it, and the first hole anybody in that ground can point at is at the near gate and there is nothing under it and no marker and nothing at the foot of it.

There are forty people in this hamlet in eleven houses and a burying ground on a rise behind the last of them, and nine of them dig, and a man of about fifty-one with a spade said on the sixty-seventh morning in the wet that a line is not a list and a man cannot join one, and he is right, and nothing in this field has made him less right.

---

And this morning was dry.

He had walked eleven miles off the lane and up a rise and he had come for one thing and he got to the near gate at about the eighth hour with the light long and the ground hard and the mist off it, and there was nobody in the field at all.

There is no line to stand in. He looked at the near gate and the eleven houses and the ground beyond them for four minutes and there was nobody digging, and there was not going to be anybody digging until it rained, and the whole of the arrangement in nine words is that a line of nine people turn up when the ground is wet, and it had not been wet for nine days.

He stood at the near gate and tried it. That is the honest word for it and it is the only time in thirteen days he tried anything in the open. He put his weight on the leg and took the leg out of the wet corner of it and stood the way the man in the second house stands, and he stood there for nine minutes.

Nothing happened. Not a refusal, not a door, not a form, not a look from a window. The field did not know. A thing that is kept alive by nine people turning up in the rain cannot be carried by the eleventh person who turns up because he decided to, on a dry morning, in a hat.

---

“You are standing in my brother's wet corner.”

The woman of thirty was on the step of the fourth house with a bucket and she had been there about a minute and a half and she had not said anything while he was doing it, which is the way she says everything.

“There is a wet corner,” he said. “On the other side of it there is a dry one and I have got a leg.”

“Yes,” she said. “*And a man can stand in it and there is nobody in the rest of it, and I have been in this field since I was nine and there has not been a wet morning in this month, and the last one was the last morning of the sixty-eighth week and the man of about fifty-one dug and the eight and the ninth were on it and the man of about seventy-four in that house went out for the first time in about a year and came back in before the light went, and that is the ninth day and that is the only ninth day there has been in this month.*”

She put the bucket down.

“You have come eleven miles on a leg on a dry morning to stand in a corner of a field that is not doing anything,” she said, “and there is nothing to be refused, and that is the whole of it. *There is nothing here to say no to. If you had come in the wet there would have been nine of us and you would have been the tenth and I would have said four words at you and you would have gone, and instead you have come on a dry morning and there is nobody to say anything.*”

---

The man of about fifty-one came out of the second house with a spade over his shoulder that was not his and looked at the near gate and at the stranger standing in a corner of a field, and he did not say good morning.

“You have understood it,” he said. “*You have understood it in nine minutes and it took me fifty-one years and a wet morning in the spring and I am not going to take that off you and I am not going to give you anything for it either, because there is nothing to give.*”

He looked at the ground.

“Eleven houses and forty people and nine of them dig and nobody knows why and there is a man of about seventy-four in that house who has not been out of it since the last day of the sixty-eighth week and will not be asked, and he is the one who does the most when it rains, and he is seventy-four, and he is entitled to stop, and I am not going to be the man who tells a stranger that a thing he is seventy-four in cannot be asked about his own field.”

“And you are right,” said the man of thirty-one.

“I am right on the facts,” said the man of about fifty-one, “and I have been right on the facts since the spring, and being right on the facts is not a thing you can carry across a gate.”

---

Nobody in that hamlet asked him anything else and nobody thanked him and he asked nobody anything, and he was there about an hour and a half on a rise with forty people in eleven houses and got the whole of it in four minutes.

Here is what he could not do and could not have done and would have paid for the rest of his life to do.

A thing cannot be delegated to a stranger. He had said in a lane the day before, in a kitchen in a village three miles up the lane, that a person who is handed a thing for a place becomes a person in a record. That was true and it had cost him nothing, because a man of about sixty-three had simply gone on walking four hundred yards of hedge on his own and had not been asked and had not had to be.

This was worse than that, and it was the same finding with the price on it, and he worked it out standing at a near gate in a hat.

Eleven names on the back of a bill and nine come and a man of sixty-three can hand his length to nobody because there is nobody who was ever in the field. A line of nine in a wet field and the man of thirty-one can hand his place in it to nobody because there is no place in it to hand, because a line of nine is nine people who turn up and the eleventh person who turns up is a man who decided to. And a man who decided to is not the same as a man who was asked, and everybody in that field can tell the difference in four seconds and every one of them can.

And the other half of it, which he wrote in his own head and did not say and has not got a better sentence for.

And that is the answer to the count I did on a road four days ago. Not that I carry one and not that I get out of the way. That the only person who can hold one of these things is a person of that place who was already in it, and if there is not one then there is not one, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that makes one, and I have spent eighty-six mornings learning that a word is not a person and a name is not a person and a book is not a person and now I have learned that a stranger with a leg is not a person either, and that is the fourth thing and it is the cheapest of the four and it has taken me the longest.

---

The woman of thirty picked the bucket up off the step.

He said he was sorry about the wet corner and that he would not come in it again, and she said the thing that is the ninth word of what she has said twice now and once more.

“It is the ninth of nine. It is mine.”

Nine words. Nobody had asked her for them, she was thirty and she was the ninth in a line of nine people in a field her family has been in longer than anybody can date, and she had said what she wanted twice in a fortnight and she was entitled to say it a third time and she was not going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to thank her.

He did not ask her a second question. He had learned in a kitchen three miles up a lane, at about the eleventh hour of the previous day, that a person of a place will ask you the shape of question you have been asking all your life, and he had not got an answer to it then and he had not got one now, and the man of about fifty-one went back inside with a spade that was not his.

The weight of the day was four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four, and four bells is the one number on that plank that does not belong to a county, and the ground was dry, and the ninth of nine went back in with a bucket, and nobody was relieved and nobody was thanked and the man of about thirty-one walked eleven miles off a lane on a dry morning and had learned the cheapest thing anybody learns in this country the hard way.
