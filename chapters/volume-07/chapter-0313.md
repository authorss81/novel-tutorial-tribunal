# Chapter 313: A Market Morning In The Tenth Village, And A Woman With A Bag

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-three thousand nine hundred and seven at the second light on the seventh and last morning of the sixty-sixth week, and the man of about thirty-eight had stood at the foot of the ladder that same morning on other business and had said the number out loud in a lane with nobody in it and had got it right, which he has done four times now and does once a week, and the day it stood for was four bells and a market, and the tenth village was running its market, and it was his sixty-third morning.

He was in the tenth village at about the eighth hour and he was not in the room over the smithy, and he had made up his mind about that at about the fifth hour in a doorway and had not told anybody, and there was no form in this country that says a man may be told anything.

A room over a smithy is a room over a smithy. Nine people go into it who have business with a fund and a tenth person who does not have business with a fund is a thing that has happened in this county before, and a man who stands at the window of it is a man who has come to take something, and everybody in the room can see the shape of what he has come to take, and if he stands there long enough they will start to arrange for it. He had watched a man of thirty-eight work in that room for a morning four days before and he had seen the exact place where the money stopped being eleven villages' and started being one man's, and it had been there for about thirty-one years, and it had not mattered to anybody, and he was not going to stand in the room.

So he was at the bar, and at the smithy's yard door, and in a lane behind the smithy, and he was not in the room, and that was the shape of the whole of that day.

---

The woman of about thirty-four said the money out loud because a market morning is the only morning in this village when nine people can be told a thing at the same time, and she said it to nobody in particular, which is how she says everything.

“Three pence a load,” she said. “Nine loads a day on the days the loads come. A hundred and forty days in a year in this county whatever anybody says about it. *A hundred and forty days of nine loads at three pence is fifteen pounds fifteen a village a year, and that has been the figure since before I stood at this bar, and every penny of it goes out in the week it comes in, to nobody who is a person in a record.*”

Nine loads went over that morning before the sun was off the roofs and she read every one of them off her own board and asked nobody anything, and a man of thirty-one stood nine feet off and said nothing, and the whole of the money in it is the unit and the count and the figure, and the figure is the one everybody in that lane could work out for themselves and nobody has.

---

The woman with the bag was in the lane behind the smithy at about the ninth hour, and she had been there before.

She is thirty of her own and she has a bag and there are thirty people who come when she says there is something to come for, and she had lost the whole of the movement at the end of the winter before last and had kept thirty of it anyway, on her own account, and nobody sent for her and nobody thanked her and nobody was going to.

She had carried that book to this smithy and back nine times a year for six years. The man of thirty-eight could not carry it. He has a bad back and a hand that does not close properly on a spine in the cold, and he would not ask her, and she offered, in the first year, in a lane, and he said yes, and that was the whole of how it was arranged and there is no paper about it.

Nobody in this village knows that she does it. The nine people in the room know that a book goes to the smithy and comes back. Nine people in this village have watched a woman with a bag go up that lane and come down it nine times a year for six years and not one of them has ever asked her why.

He was standing in the lane. She was thirty feet off with the bag at her feet and her back against a wall, waiting for the room to be empty, which is a thing a person does who has been doing a thing six years.

He could see the join.

---

It was there and it was the same join, and it was the only one he has ever got out of a thing rather than gone into it.

The fund's own join was where the money stopped being eleven villages' and started being one man's, and that one was thirty-one years old and it was in a room and it had never mattered to anybody in the room. This one was six years old and it was in a lane. It was where a favour stops being a favour and starts being a habit. *Not asked, and offered, and then done nine times a year, and then done so many times that nobody would now call it a favour, because you cannot be thanked for a habit and she has not been thanked once in six years and the arrangement has survived on that and on nothing else.*

And the woman with the bag was standing inside the join. Not in the middle of it. Inside it. She was the load side of a habit, and a habit does not break when the person in it stops, it just goes on being a habit for about a year, and then somebody else finds themselves in a lane at the ninth hour with a bag at their feet and no arrangement, and no form, and nobody to say anything to.

He looked at it for four seconds.

It was a fact. It was a true fact and it was the most useful thing he had ever seen, and it was about a woman's unpaid labour, and there is not one instrument in this country or in any county four hundred miles off that can be applied to a fact about a woman's unpaid labour, and he could not compel a person and could not join accounts and could not produce a record, and there was not a single thing in a lane behind a smithy on a market morning that he could do about it.

He had a bad leg, a name on the back of a flour bill three miles up a lane, and nine days of walking in him.

He did not go over. He did not say good morning. He did not say the sentence he had been carrying about things nobody knows the reason for, and he had it ready and he had it ready in that lane and he did not say it, because saying it to a woman of thirty with a bag and thirty people of her own, in a lane behind a smithy, would have been a man explaining to her what she had been doing for six years so that he could leave with his own sentence said in a room.

---

Then the woman at the bar went into the room over the smithy and did not come out for about half an hour, and what she did in there was the whole of the day.

Nobody sent for anybody. A man of sixty-three had walked eleven miles to ask her one question about a market four days up a lane, and a stranger with a bad leg had put six places in a row in a kitchen eleven miles up the lane behind her, and between those two facts and that room there is nothing at all. A person of a place told a person of a place. That is the only transport this country has and it has no weight and it has no date, and it is the reason a debt at the fourth house travels nine miles faster than a man on foot, and it is the reason a room over a smithy can be full at the second hour of a market morning without anybody in eleven villages having decided to fill it.

He does not know how she put it. He was in a lane. What he was told afterwards, by three people who were not in the room, was that she had asked the man of about thirty-eight a second question, about the book, and had asked it in front of the other eight, and that he had answered it.

And he said the sentence.

That is all of it. It is nine seconds and it is the sentence he had been carrying since the second morning of the sixty-sixth week in a different lane, and the cost of it is what that morning said the cost would be, which is that a man of thirty-eight has to stand in a room in front of nine people and say in his own mouth that he cannot do it any more. He said it. Nobody stopped him. Nobody argued with him. One man of about fifty-one said *noted*, which was wrong, and one woman said nothing, and the smith's wife put a cup down on the sill of the window and did not pick it up for about a minute, and a man of about thirty-four who reads things out for a living said, out loud, that there was no form in this country that says a man may stop, which is a true sentence and was not asked for and made the room worse, and then everybody stood about.

“He is not a villain,” the woman at the bar said, at the bar, in the afternoon, to a man of thirty-one who had not asked. “Do not go writing him as one. He has done that count for about eleven years and he is going four valleys away in about a month and there is a house and a woman who has been patient about it for two years, and he is not frightened of anything in this room. He is going to say the same thing at his own hearth inside a month and half of those nine will be stood in front of him again and he will say it again. *That is what it costs. Not once. Twice, and then for the rest of his life at kitchen tables, and there is no form that says a man may stop saying it.*”

---

The book is on the shelf in a room over a smithy and it has one reader in the world for it and he is going four valleys away in a month.

That is the decision nobody made, and it is a decision all the same, and its shape is the whole of what the day was, and it is the only thing in that lane this morning that was not about a person.

Nobody has been chosen. The smith’s wife does not read a column. The woman at the bar reads loads off a board in her head and has never once said she can do anything else and would tell you she cannot, and she said so in a lane in nine words when it was suggested to her, which is twice in one day. The man of about thirty-four who reads things out cannot follow a sum that runs down a page, and everybody knows that and has known it for years. A man of about forty-one would come for the water once a week and would be frightened of a book.

And the woman with the bag is not going to be chosen, and the reason is in the room and not in this lane, and it is the only sensible reason anybody has. She is thirty and she has thirty people of her own and she has not lived in this county for six years and she would still be a stranger at that bar in ten, and a book that is held by somebody the village has not chosen is a book the village cannot ask anybody about, and that is the same hole that is in the floor of everything else in this county: *a word is not a person, and the instrument for turning a word into a person is a name at the foot of a thing, and the instrument only works inside about a day's walk of the kitchen it was written in.*

So a month of notice is left in it and nobody is going to be chosen in a month, and everybody in that room knows it, and one of the nine said out loud that a fund with one reader in it and no second reader is a fund that stops, and a man of about thirty-eight said, “Yes,” and that was the only word he said for an hour, and it was not a dramatic word and it was the whole of his life for eleven years.

The book went back on the shelf at about the last of the light, in a room, on a shelf that has had it for about thirty-one years. Nobody carried it down the lane that evening, because it goes to the smithy nine times a year and not on a market day, and the woman with the bag had gone up the lane at noon and had not been asked to do anything else and had not been thanked and was not going to be.
