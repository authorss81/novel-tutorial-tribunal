# Chapter 341: A Round Of Turns That Has A Person In It

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-four thousand and ninety-one at the second light on the last morning of the seventieth week, and he read it himself off the wall at the foot of the ladder, which is the only way he has ever had a figure in this country.

The day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four. That is the number most of the days in a year are, and a day with nothing on it is a day with nothing on it wherever a man is standing.

It was his ninety-first morning. There is no market in this lane today and no stranger in it but him, and no fire, and no debt called in, and no boundary that anybody walked, and no burial, and that is all of the seven weights and four of them did not happen.

---

Three miles up the lane. There is one kitchen in the third village that anybody will let a stranger stand in and it is the kitchen at the end of the lane, and there are always nine people in it and the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes.

He got there at about the seventh hour with a leg and nothing in his hands, and a man of about sixty-three was at the table with his hands black to the wrist, and the flour bill was face up on the table where it had been left on the previous afternoon, and eleven names were on it and a twelfth in a straighter hand that a man with a drain in his hand wrote because a stranger offered to walk a hedge, and nobody in that kitchen has ever been told and the man who wrote it does not know it is there.

He did not look at the bill. He looked at the nine people and the boy of seventeen, who comes into that kitchen every morning with a book under his arm and has never been asked about it by anybody in nine years.

---

He said what he had come for, out loud, in a kitchen, in front of nine people, and he had it ready on the road and it took him nine seconds.

“I am not going to ask the boy anything,” he said. “I am going to ask the room whether a round of turns can be walked, and if it cannot then the hearing is not a thing anybody can hold and I have wasted two counties on it.”

Nobody said anything.

“And the answer is no,” said the man of sixty-three, “and it is no because of where they are, and you have been in this country ninety-one mornings and you have not once asked me where anything is.”

He had said it flatly and he had not said it unkindly and there were always nine people in that kitchen and the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes.

“That is fair,” said the stranger.

“It is not fair,” said the man of sixty-three. “*It is my bill and I have carried it thirty-one years and you have not asked me a question in this kitchen in a month and now you have come and asked one, and it is the wrong one, and I am going to answer it because you are a stranger and strangers do not get talked out of it.*”

---

So the room walked it, and the room is the kitchen, and there are nine people in it and none of them is the man with the cart or the woman of thirty or the man of thirty-eight or the woman of fifty-eight or the woman of seventy-four.

That is the whole of the finding and it took them a quarter of an hour and the man with the crowbar did most of it.

The first turn is his and he can say it. It is the woman of thirty in a field behind eleven houses eleven miles down the lane and off it, and she said in that field on a dry morning that if he had come in the wet there would have been nine of them and he would have been the tenth. She is the second turn and she is in a hamlet and she is not in this kitchen.

“Eleven miles and off it,” said the man of sixty-three. “*I can say her name and I cannot put my hand on her shoulder and neither can anybody in this room, and that is nine words and one of them is a distance and the other eight are a man of sixty-three telling you what he can and cannot do from a table.*”

He put his hand flat on the flour bill and left it there.

“And I have been trying to walk that bill since the day it was written and I have got as far as this kitchen and I have not got one step further, because the third name is a man with a cart who is a mile the other way and a fourth is a woman four days up this lane and there is no road from this village to a place four days up a lane that a man on foot can do in a day, and the fifth is a man on a road with a bag and nobody in this county knows which road.*”

---

And the boy of seventeen said the thing, and he said it in his own mouth and he had not been asked and it took him nine seconds, which is what he takes.

“A round of turns is not walked,” said Bram Orren. “That is what I have been trying to make it do for five years and I have never been able to and I stopped trying a year ago. You do not walk a round. You carry one turn at a time, and the only thing in this county that can carry a turn is a person who is going that way anyway, and there is a woman of thirty-two who goes up this lane twice a week for salt and she does not know she is carrying anything and I am not going to tell her.*”

He shut the book halfway and put a finger in it.

“It goes round at the speed of the road. One of them is four days off and one of them is eleven miles and one of them is two miles down a track. A round of turns is nine weeks or a year. There is no other speed for it in this country and I have not been able to make it any faster and I have not been asked to.*”

Nobody said anything.

“And that is the whole of what a book is for,” he said. “A book is the only thing in eleven villages that can say who is after whom. It cannot say when, so the road says when, and the road is made of everybody who is going somewhere already, and none of them are mine.*”

---

And the stranger stood in the middle of a kitchen and found out the thing he had come four days up a lane to find out, and it is not the thing he expected and it is worth more than the thing he expected.

A hearing cannot be produced. That is not news; that has been true since a woman of fifty-one who cannot write said it at a table. What is new, and it is new this morning, in a kitchen, in front of nine people, is what carries one.

Not a form. Not a book. Not him, and not a man with a bill. A person going where they were going anyway. A woman going for salt. A man with a cart who is a mile the other way. A man of thirty-four who is in this village one day a week and in Coldharrow another and has never in nineteen years been in both in the same week and who is going to the other one tomorrow.

And that is why there is no instrument in four hundred miles, and it is not because nobody has thought of one. It is because every one of them is a person, and a person is the only thing in this country that can carry a turn, and a person cannot be told to.

He said that part out loud, once, and then stopped, because he had said the price out loud in six places in a fortnight and it does not get smaller and he knows what it costs.

---

And then a want arrived, and it arrived in the mouth of a boy of seventeen, and nobody in that kitchen had asked for it, and that is the whole of it.

He shut the book and got the strap over it and did not put it under his arm yet.

“You read it out on a morning in this kitchen and nine people listened and nobody asked you and one man put two fingers on the cover and shut his mouth,” he said, “and I have not got a way of saying that was well done that is not a reward, so I am not going to say it.”

“All right,” said the boy.

And then, because the man with the leg had said it in the way a man says a thing that is finished, and because there were nine people in that kitchen and none of them had anything else to do:

“I would read it out again,” said Bram Orren. “*On any morning. To anybody. I have been running it since I was twelve and I have read it out to my mother and to a cat and once to a man who came to look at a gate and did not stay, and nobody in eleven villages has ever asked me to and I have never said that out loud to a room and I am saying it now because you have said a thing that is finished and that is the only way I know how to say a thing.*”

He put the book under his arm.

Nobody thanked him. He did not want to be thanked and would not have known what to say to it, and the stranger did not reward him because he had no way of doing it that was not a reward.

---

The man of sixty-three turned the flour bill over at about the ninth hour, face down, the way it was on the morning before yesterday, and there is no reason on earth for him to have done that and nobody asked him.

And at about the tenth hour the stranger said the small thing, out loud, in that kitchen, to the room, and it was the first of several and it is not the hearing and there was nothing about a day in it.

“Every one of you is going to say your own turn, out loud, in your own place, to whoever is standing near you when you say it,” he said. “*Not to me. There is nobody in this country who is entitled to hear it and I am a stranger and I am going to be gone. And nobody is going to write any of it down, and nobody is going to be thanked for it, and the woman of thirty is going to say hers in her own field and the woman of fifty-eight is going to say hers four days up this lane and the man of thirty-eight is going to say his on whatever road he is on, and if none of them ever says theirs then the round stops and that is the answer and the answer does not have to be a good one.*”

Nobody agreed and nobody disagreed.

“And there is no day in it,” he said, “and I am not going to be the one who sets a day, because a book can order things and cannot date them and I have had two counties of a morning in which that was the only true sentence anybody said to me.”

The man of sixty-three put both hands flat on the table on the flour bill and left them there for the length of a cup going cold.

“That is a rota,” he said. “*You have described a rota and there is one on the back of that bill and eleven names on it and I have never once read it out in this kitchen, and you are a stranger and I have known you a fortnight and I am not going to say the next part of that in front of nine people.*”

He did not say the next part. Nobody asked him for it.

---

The boundary was four hundred yards of hedge and was not walked and nobody had walked it on any of the nine mornings except the nine men who walk it, and the twelfth name on the bill was under the table face down and the man who wrote it does not know it is there, and a boy of seventeen went out to the yard at about the eleventh hour with a book under his arm and a strap on it, and nobody was relieved and nobody was thanked, and the round of turns went out of that kitchen at the speed of the road.
