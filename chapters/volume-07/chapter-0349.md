# Chapter 349: What A Hundred And Forty People Build In Nine Weeks Because They Have To

There is no figure for this morning. The plank is at the far end of the lane, nine miles down it, and a woman of forty-one was up a ladder against it at the second light, and he was at the end of a lane eleven miles off the main one with a leg, and the day she put on that wall is not available to him in any way at all.

Four bells, and nothing whatever on top of them, which is four, and that is the whole of what this morning weighs. There is no market in this village today and no stranger in it but him, and no debt called in, and no boundary walked, and no burial and no fire, and the day after a fire in this county is a day with nothing on it and that is not a mystery and nobody in it is thinking about it.

It was his ninety-ninth morning. The day before, a porch in a valley of sixty people had gone out between the fourth hour and the last of the light, and nobody in four hundred miles had put it out and nobody in four hundred miles was going to.

---

The woman of about twenty-nine came up the lane at about the seventh hour and she had a list on a board and she had counted something and she said the number before he asked, which is the way a person of a place says a thing they have counted and can be asked about.

“A hundred and forty,” she said. “That is not a guess and it is not a village. It is a hundred and forty people in eleven villages who have said they would take a turn, and I have written it on a board and I have not written a name against any of them, because a name at the foot of a thing is a person and a person can be asked about, and you have said that in four counties in a fortnight and I have heard you say it in a yard and I have agreed with it and that is not the same as doing it.*”

She put the board against a gate.

“Nine weeks,” she said. “*That is my count and not a stranger's and it is the same count twice. Nine weeks, because I have watched this county try to tell nine people something in a day and I have watched it take a fortnight to tell one. Nine weeks and it will be about as good as we can get and it will not be good, and it will be built by a hundred and forty people, and not one of them has been asked to be in it and not one of them has been thanked and forty of them are going to hate it by the fourth week and be right to.*”

---

And he did not go in, and he said so out loud, in a lane, to one person, in eleven words, and nobody had asked him.

“I am not going to be in it,” he said. “*There is no form in this country that says a man may be in two places and I have been finding that out since a road four days off and I have just spent a morning standing in a lane two miles off a track and I am not going to be in two things at once again in the same week, and if I am in this then I am a man who has put a woman of thirty-four at a bar into a second thing in a month and there is nobody in four hundred miles who can take her out of the first one.*”

The woman of twenty-nine did not argue with him and did not thank him and said the half of it that mattered.

“That is right,” she said, “and I would have said so if you had not. There is a hundred and forty of us and you are not one of them and there is no form in this county that would let a man add himself to a list of people doing something, and that is why the list has got no names on it and why it is going to be a hundred and forty and not a hundred and forty-one.*”

---

Here is what it is, and it took four people an hour to say out loud in a lane, and none of it is a repair.

It is not a bell in a tower. There is a bell in a tower at Coldharrow and it has not been rung for want of a wage and nobody is buying that wage and nobody is going to, and if somebody hung it tomorrow and paid a person to pull the rope it would be one person again, and one person is what a porch was, and a porch is what went out yesterday between the fourth hour and the last of the light, and nobody in this county is going to build another one person and call it a network.

So it is a hundred and forty of something that can be struck, in eleven villages, by anybody who happens to be near it.

A goat's bell on a gate bar in the second village. A handbell that belongs to a woman of about thirty-four and that she has said she will have back on the days it is not wanted. Two of the harness bells off a dead cart, which somebody's grandfather bought. A hoe handle against a wall, which makes a noise you can hear half a mile and is not a bell and is not going to be a bell and is going to be used every morning anyway.

And no keeper. That is the whole of the design and the man of thirty-four said it and it is the only sentence anybody has said about it that was worth writing down.

“It does not need anybody to hold it,” he said. “Anybody near a thing can hit it. That is the entire difference between it and a tower with a wage in it and a woman of seventy-four going down four mornings a year, and it is the only reason we are doing this in nine weeks instead of not doing it at all.*”

---

And a man of about fifty-two was at the bar at the end of that road counting what crossed it, because that is what he does, and he has been the somebody in nine counties in nineteen years and nobody has ever sent him and nobody sent him this morning, and he put the bit of lead on the sill and said the thing that finished the argument before it started properly.

“It is a list,” he said.

Nobody said anything.

“*A hundred and forty people who take a turn is a count. A count is a list. And I have been in nine counties in nineteen years and I have watched a list come in that had a thousand on it, and I am not going to say what came out of it because I am not going to say it to any more people than I have to and I have said it to nine.*”

He picked the lead up.

“I am not against it,” said the man of fifty-two. “*I am telling you that you are building a thing that will be used to make a person work, and you will not know that for two years, and by then there will be a hundred and forty people who cannot get off it and forty of them who will not want to, and there is nobody in this county whose job it is to notice that first. That is all I am going to say about it and nobody asked me and that is the only reason I have said it.*”

He went back to the bar and put the lead in his coat and the counting went on.

---

And the cost is not money, because there is no money in it at any point, and the woman of twenty-nine said so before anybody asked her, in nine words, in a lane, at about the tenth hour.

“There is no money in it and nobody is being paid and nobody is going to be,” she said. “A hundred and forty people with their own work, and nine weeks of it, and the ones with beasts and the ones with a trade will find it out in a fortnight and the ones who cannot will not find it out until the fourth week, and I am not going to be the one who decides which of those it is and I am not going to be asked.*”

The cost is a man of about thirty with a cart and a beast, who is the third of nine on a mile of road, and who has spent a month saying that a count is a list and a list is the thing that comes before a bill.

He was at the bar at about the ninth hour and he had come in because a bar at the end of a road is where a man with a beast stops, and he had not been sent for, and he was not thanked for coming, and he said the thing that nobody else was going to say, in a lane, in his own mouth, flatly.

“*I have been telling a stranger for a month that this is the thing that comes before a bill. I was right. It is the thing that comes before a bill. And I am on it, and I did not agree to it, and there is no form in this county that says a man may take himself off a thing he is on, and I am the third of nine on a mile of road and I have not been thanked for that in ninety years and I am not going to be thanked for this either, and I want off it and I am not going to be.*”

Nobody took him off it and nobody said he had to stay and nobody thanked him, and he is on the board of a hundred and forty with no name against him and he is one of the forty who is going to hate it by the fourth week, and he said in the lane afterwards that he would be right about that as well, and he has been right about things in this county before.

---

And a woman of about thirty-four who cannot write said the last thing of the morning, and nobody had asked her, and it is a want and it is ordinary and it is not going to be thanked and it is not the stranger's and he cannot do one single thing about it.

She had been in that kitchen in the third village for nine years and she had shut a gate bar for four seconds one time because a hen was out, and she is the one who said in that kitchen, in the middle of a morning about a hearing, that a thing that has to be held in six places at once is a hearing and that a book is the only thing in eleven villages that can put six things in an order and cannot say when.

And a hundred and forty people are now going to have to be able to say whether they have had a turn.

“I cannot write,” she said. “*I have said that in front of strangers more than once in this month and I am saying it now because we have just worked out that a hundred and forty people have to be able to write one word on a board and I am one of the hundred and forty and I am the only one of them who cannot.*”

She put her thumb on the edge of the board.

“*Somebody in this county ought to teach me to write. I have not asked anybody in nine years and I am not going to ask anybody about it in the next nine weeks either, and I have said it out loud in a lane today because I said a thing in a kitchen a fortnight ago that started all of this and it is finished and there is nobody to leave it with. Nobody is going to answer me and I am not going to ask again and that is the whole of what I have come down here for.*”

Nobody answered her. The stranger had an instrument for four seconds and it was a question asked in front of other people and it would have cost her something he could not pay, and he has used it twice in a month and both times it made somebody a person in a record, and he shut his mouth and said nothing and it is the only thing he has got.

---

The nine weeks started at about the eleventh hour in a lane at the end of a road with a goat's bell on a gate bar and a hoe handle against a wall and a woman of twenty-nine with a board that has a hundred and forty marks on it and no names, and nobody was thanked and nobody was relieved and a man with a cart and a beast said out loud that he wants to be taken off it and is not going to be, and a woman who cannot write said out loud that somebody ought to teach her and that she is not going to ask.
