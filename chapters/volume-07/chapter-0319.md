# Chapter 319: Six Rooms, And A Thing That Cannot Be Produced

The man of about thirty-eight had said sixty-three thousand nine hundred and forty-eight out loud in a lane at about the seventh hour on the sixth morning of the sixty-seventh week, having been at the foot of the ladder at the second light, and the day that figure stood for was four bells and a stranger, and the stranger was him, and the woman of fifty at the bar had read him off her own board in four seconds and had not asked his business.

It was his sixty-ninth morning. He got nine people into a yard in Coldharrow at about the ninth hour, which is the only thing this country has that is worth the name of a room, and the nine of them were the woman of forty-one, the man of about thirty-eight, a man of twenty with a scythe, a woman of about fifty-one who cannot write, the man of about thirty-four who reads things out, the man of about forty-one who comes for the water once a week, two men he had not met who had come with the second, and him.

Nobody was sent for. That is the whole of it and it is the only way anything happens in this county.

---

He said it in nine seconds, standing in the yard, in his own mouth, to nine people, because a yard is the only instrument in this country that can hold nine people who have to be told and he was not going to spend it on anything smaller.

“A document is a thing you produce,” he said. “A hearing is a thing you have to carry. *A sheet can be put in front of a room on a day. A hearing cannot be produced at all, and it is not a bad thing that cannot be produced, it is a hearing, and the only way one exists is if six people who have never met each other agree to be in it.*”

The woman of fifty-one said, “Who are the six.”

And that is the whole of the mechanism, and it took him the rest of the morning, and it is the first time in this country that a piece of machinery has been used in front of people who had to be told, and it failed, and it failed on something true.

---

It failed on the paper.

The man of about thirty-eight said it first, and he said it flatly and without any heat, because he is a man who counts other people's money and has never once in his life made a speech.

“There is no sheet,” he said. “That is not an opinion. I have been in this county nine times in two years and I will go through it. The fund has a book and the book stays on a shelf. Your bread has a bill and the bill is at the baker's and the baker does not sell paper. *A sheet is three pence at Sallowby and three pence at any scrivener over a chandler, and there is no scrivener in this county and no chandler in this county, and a man who copies is a penny a page and there is nobody in eleven villages who copies anything for anybody.*”

“Write it on the back of a bill,” said the man of twenty with the scythe.

“Whose bill.”

The man of twenty did not have an answer to that and it was the correct question and the yard went quiet for four seconds, because there is one piece of writing paper of any kind in Coldharrow and nine people in that yard had worked out what it was in the same four seconds.

It is the back of a flour bill in a kitchen in the third village up this lane, and it has eleven names on it, and a twelfth in a stranger's hand, and the twelfth is his.

Nobody said that out loud. The man of thirty-eight looked at the man of thirty-one for about a second and a half and did not say it and the man of thirty-one did not say it, and four people in that yard understood all of it and nobody spoke.

“So there is no sheet,” said the woman of forty-one, from the wall, without moving. “I have been the only one anybody could ask about for thirty-one years and I am telling you that there is no sheet, and that is not an excuse, it is a fact, and it has been a fact for thirty-one years, and everything in this county that anybody could be asked about is a thing somebody is holding in a room.”

“And a copy is three pence,” said the man of thirty-four who reads things out. “Three pence at Sallowby. *And a copy is not the thing, and I want that said in a yard, because everybody in this county thinks the paper is the thing.* A copy in a place where nobody has read it is a piece of paper. A copy in a place where somebody has read it is a place somebody can be asked, and that is worth more than the paper and it is not the same as the paper, and the difference between the two is nine days of a cart, and if you have not got the nine days then you have got a sheet and no hearing.”

---

The six, then, because he had said six and had to produce them or lose the room.

A man of about sixty-three in a kitchen three miles up this lane, who walks a boundary and has never been asked.

A woman of about fifty-eight at a set of scales in a market four days up this lane, who was told no in nine words by a stranger with a bad leg and has not been thanked and is not going to be, and who is not being asked for anything today and is not being asked for anything this month.

A man of about thirty-eight in a room over a smithy in the tenth village, with a month of notice left in him and a column on a shelf with one reader in the world for it.

A woman of thirty at the end of a line of nine diggers on a rise eleven miles down the lane and off it, who does not want to be, and who has now said the thing she wants out loud twice in a week to two different men and has been given nothing by either of them.

A man of thirty with a cart and a beast, at the end of a lane eleven miles down and off this one, whom a woman at a bar has called out loud in a lane a man who wants paying, and who told her in nine words that she was half right, and who has not been thanked for that either.

And a woman of about seventy-four in a porch in a valley two miles off a track, with a day and an hour in it that she gave him and that he did not write down.

He said all six out loud in four minutes, in a yard, and he did not join any two of them, and four of the nine understood every word of it and the other five heard a man in a yard. And a woman of about thirty-four at a bar in that same village, who has stood at that bar for twenty-two years and had never been asked a second question about anything until this month, is not one of the six and is not on the list, and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps her, and that is the first time anybody has put six of a thing beside each other and come out with a seventh on the same morning.

---

Then it failed on him, and it failed in the plainest sentence available, and he had said it once already in a gateway in a field and he said it again because it is the sentence and not a speech.

“There is no way for me to be in two of those six places at once. *The market is four days up this lane. The bar is eleven miles down and off it and the porch is two miles past that. And there is no room in this country that will let a man be in two places and no form that will let a man send anybody, and I have looked, and there is not one.*”

The man of about thirty-eight said, “Then what is the six for.”

“That is the whole of the finding and it took me twelve days to get it,” said the man of thirty-one, “and it is this. *A hearing is not a place. It is six people agreeing to be somewhere on a day, and carrying it between them, and the carrying is the only instrument there is.*”

He put his hands flat on the wall of the yard.

“I cannot do it and there is no version of me that can do it, and if the six of you can do it then it will go on without me and that is the correct outcome and not a defeat, and I am saying it now, in a yard, with nine people in it, because I said the same thing in a gateway in a field twelve days ago to three people who had not asked me anything, and because a man who says a thing once and then cannot get away from saying it again is building a case instead of doing a thing.”

Nobody laughed. The man of twenty with a scythe said, “Then where does it go.”

---

And it went to a bar, which is the whole of the last hour of that day and the reason the morning went the way it did.

The woman of about thirty-four at the bar in the tenth village had not been asked to carry anything and was not going to be, and the reason she carried it is that she is the only person in four counties that anybody goes to when they want to know a thing and does not want to be seen wanting to know it, and she had been that for twenty-two years without ever once being asked whether she would.

He did not ask her. He has stated that four times and it is true. The man of about thirty-eight asked her, in a room over a smithy, on a market morning, in front of nine people, in a sentence of his own that cost him what it cost him.

The woman of thirty was not asked, and she is one of the six, and she is the reason the six works, and this is the shape of it and nobody designed it.

They agreed a time and a place. Then they changed the time twice, in a bar, in a lane, and in a gateway in the wet, and the changing of it is the whole of what happened.

The first time was because a man of about thirty-eight cannot be in a room in the tenth village on a day in the middle of a move, and a month is what he has and nine days of it is travel. The second time was because somebody had said, in a bar, without being asked and without stopping what she was doing, that a woman of about fifty-eight is six months gone with a child and is going to be stood up at the spring instead of doing the sums, and the sixth morning of a week is not the spring but nobody in that bar could say in advance which morning of a month it would turn out to be.

The third time was because the woman of thirty said so out loud, in a gateway, in the rain, to a man of thirty-one and a man of thirty-eight and a woman of thirty-four, and what she said was not about herself.

*“You cannot fix a day for it. Not one of you. Whatever day you six agree on, if somebody in this hamlet dies on it then somebody in this hamlet is standing in a field and not in a room, and that has been the arrangement here for four hundred years and you cannot have a day and have that.* So take a week. Take a week and put it wherever it goes, and do not ask me to be there, because I will be there, and I will not be able to say that I came.”

And the woman of thirty-four at the bar, who has never been asked a second question in twenty-two years, said the fourth thing, which was the only thing said in that yard and in that bar and in that gateway that nobody had thought of, and she said it with a board under her arm.

“Then do not settle a day,” she said. “Settle an order. Put them in an order and let each one of them say who comes next, and the day is the day of whichever one is doing the asking that week, and the one doing the asking is the one it is easiest for, and the one it is easiest for is the one everybody turns to first, and that is a market and it works on a market morning and it works when nobody is standing about.”

Nobody said anything for four seconds.

“That is the arrangement of six places and I have been at the end of this lane for twenty-two years,” she said, “and it is a rota, and there is a rota on the back of a bill in a kitchen three miles up your lane and it is the only one in this county and nobody has ever read it out.”

---

Nobody agreed to anything in writing, because there is no form, and there is no room, and there is no officer, and the woman of about fifty-eight is not being asked for anything.

A boy of seventeen was in that yard at about the fourth hour because he was going that way and had not got to where he is going, and he said one thing and he did not open his book.

“There is a third place in it I have never been able to make out,” said Bram Orren. “And I have not got it and I am not going to get it this year, and I want to say that out loud once in a yard with nine people in it, because nine people in this county have been told about it by somebody who was not me, and I would rather the first time it was said in a yard it was said by me and it was said all the way through.”

Nobody asked him about it. He did not want to be asked two things at once and he has never wanted to.

The man of fifty-two stood at the gate of that yard for about an hour and did not say one word, and nobody asked him what he was the only person in, and four people understood that they had all wanted to and had not.

---

At about the last of the light he said the last thing of that day out loud, in a yard, to nine people, and it was a decision and it was nine seconds and nobody thanked him for it.

“I am not going to be in it,” he said. “I have said that in a field and I am saying it in a yard and it is the same sentence. *There are six people who have agreed to be somewhere on a day, and I am not one of them, and I am going to be four days up a lane or eleven miles down it or two miles past a bridge with one rail on it, and I will not be in the room, and there is no form in this country that says a man may be in two places, and I have been sixty-nine days in this county and I have not found one and I am not going to.*”

The woman of forty-one put the cloth over her shoulder.

“Nobody is thanked,” she said, in the voice she uses for a stranger at her bar, which is the only voice she has got for one. “*And nobody is relieved, and I would like it noticed that this is the fifth morning this week that nobody has been relieved about anything and that is the ordinary way of this place and has been for thirty-one years.*”

And that was the whole of it, and nobody was in it, and six people had agreed to be somewhere on a day and had changed the arrangement twice and were going to change it again.
