# Chapter 333: He Tries To Carry A Book And It Comes Apart In His Hands

There is no plank in this village and there is no figure on any wall in it, and the plank is nine miles up the lane at the far end of it where a woman of forty-one keeps the only seven weights in this county in her head. A market was running in this street this morning and a market is a market in this county and it weighs four whether it is in a street or not, so the day is in the March and the day is on that plank, and the wall with the day on it is nine miles up a lane, and he had been eleven miles down that lane and off it in the dark, and the second light came and went where he was not.

It was the sixth morning of the sixty-ninth week and his eighty-third morning, and the day came to four bells and a market, which is eight, and there is no second market in a day however many villages are having one.

---

The book is on a shelf in a room over a smithy and it has been on that shelf for thirty-one years.

The room is the smith's wife's because her grandmother's was before her, and there is a bed in it with the straw still in it, and nobody sleeps in it, and it is not let and has not been let since before she came here, and a book about other people's money has been sitting in a dead woman's room for thirty-one years because that is where the shelf is.

He came in at about the eighth hour on a market morning with nine people in the lane outside and nine loads on the bar at the end of the road, and the man of about thirty-four who reads things out for a living was already in there, because it is the day he is here and he is here most weeks, and he read the stranger off his own board in a second and a half and said what everybody in that room was going to say inside a minute.

“You have come for the book,” said the man who reads things out. “And I am going to say the thing before you ask it, because I have said it once and I have not been thanked for saying it once, and there is nobody else in this village who could do it and there is no point in us taking a quarter of an hour about it.”

He put his hand flat on the table, on nothing.

“I cannot follow a sum that runs down a page. *I have been the man who reads things out at three tables in this county for nineteen years and there is not one figure on that shelf that I have ever been able to take in my head and put back, and that is not a thing I have found out this month, and everybody in this village has known it since I was fifteen.*”

---

The smith's wife said her half and she said it with the cup in her hand and did not put it down.

“I do not read a column,” she said, “and I have never pretended to, and there is a woman of about forty-one comes to the pump in this house once a week and she has a good head and she could read a column, and I asked her about it once, four years ago, in this room, and she told me she would be frightened of a book in this house at night and would I not ask her that again.”

Nobody said anything.

“So there it is, and you have come eleven miles, and the man who can do it is on a cart and the man who could do it and would not is on the road with a bag, and there is nobody in this county at all, and I have not got a bad feeling about you and I would not let a stranger take it off that shelf and I would not let a man I have known thirty years take it off that shelf.”

He did not ask for it. He asked the ordinary question, in the ordinary four words, standing the way he stood.

Nobody answered it in that room. That is the finding and it took him four seconds.

---

And then, in the lane outside, at about the ninth hour, a woman with a bag went past with a book under her arm.

She was going up the lane and not down it, and the book was a foolscap book with a strap on it and the strap had been mended twice with a length of different stuff, and she was not going slowly, and she did not stop, and there is not one word of hers in that lane that is about him.

He stood nine feet off the wall and let her get on with it.

Nobody sent for her. She has thirty of her own and a bag and she has carried that book to a smithy and back nine times a year for six years and she did it on her own account before he came into this county and she has not been asked to keep it since, and she is not on any list of his and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps him.

She was nine feet past him when she said it, and she said it to the road.

“*It goes nine times a year and it is a quarter of a mile each way and it is not a book anybody is keeping, it is a book being moved, and there is a difference and I have known the difference for six years.*”

Then she was gone up the lane and he did not say one word to her and she did not look round and he is not on her list and nobody is going to thank her and she is not going to be thanked by anybody including him.

---

He went back into the room over the smithy and he did the thing he came eleven miles to do, and he did it on purpose, and nobody stopped him because there was nobody who could.

He reached out and took the book off the shelf.

It came apart in his hands in nine seconds. Not in the room. In his hands. A foolscap book with a strap on it that has been mended twice, and the strap went first, and the front board came away from the back board at the stitching that has been done four times by four different hands, and the whole of it is a thing that has been carried nine times a year for six years by one person and put down on a shelf thirty-one years, and there is not one page in it that is fastened to anything.

That is what happened and it took nine seconds and four people in that room watched a stranger hold a man's share in his hands and let it come apart in them.

“A book on a shelf is not a weight,” he said. He put the two boards down on the table, gently, the way you put a child down. “It is an answer to a question somebody has to ask, and the question has not been asked, and a stranger holding a book is a man holding somebody else's week.”

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that finished it, and he said it flatly and he was not being cruel and he had known it for nineteen years.

“You are not going to be here in a year,” he said. “*Nobody in this room believes you are going to be here in a year, and you know it too, and it is the one thing in this room that everybody has got the same opinion about and it is correct.*”

Nobody argued. The smith's wife picked the two boards up, put them back in the order they came off in, which she knew, and strapped the book, and put it on the shelf at the height her grandmother had it at, and did not say one word while she did it.

And the man of thirty-one stood in the middle of a room over a smithy with nothing in his hands and understood, in nine seconds, the whole of the failure: he is the only person in four counties who could learn that column in a year, and he is a stranger with a bad leg going four valleys away in about a fortnight, and the four people in that room are right, and he is right, and there is no version of it in which one of those two things stops being true.

---

He did not try to fix it. He had come eleven miles to fix it and he had broken a strap in nine seconds and he stood four minutes and did not try to fix it, and then he asked the one question he had left, which was the one from the eighty-first morning, out loud, in that room, to four people who had not asked him.

“I have to put somebody's name on something,” he said, “and I have not got one, and there is no form, and there is no sheet in this county and there is no scrivener within nine miles of this smithy, and I have been using the absence of a form as a reason to do nothing for eighty-three mornings and that is a use and it is not a good one.”

The man of about thirty-four did not answer it and could not have.

---

And at the end of the lane, at about the eleventh hour, the woman of about thirty-four at the bar told him one ordinary fact about that room that he had not asked her for.

“That room is not a room for a book,” she said. “*It is my husband's mother's and she died in it in about the winter before I came here and they left the bed in with the straw in it, and nobody has ever swept it out, and I go in there about twice a year and I open the shutter and I shut it again, and that is what the room is for, and a man with a leg and a book in his county is not going to get anything out of it.*”

She put her board on the sill.

“I am not going to be thanked for that and you are not going to use it,” she said. “That is the third thing I have said to you in a month that nobody asked me for and it is the third one I am not going to be thanked for, and there is a man of about thirty-eight sitting on the step of that cross with a slate and he asked me one question in the spring and I gave him a straight answer, and that is all he got, and I would do it again, and *I am not the seventh of you and I am not on the list and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps me.*”

The market that figure stood for was a market in a village nine miles up the lane in the county the plank is in, and the one running in that street is nine miles from the wall, and a book went back on a shelf at the height a dead woman's grandmother had it at, and nobody in that village was relieved and nobody was thanked.
