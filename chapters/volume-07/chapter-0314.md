# Chapter 314: A Debt Called In At The Ninth House

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-three thousand nine hundred and fifteen at the second light on the first morning of the sixty-seventh week, and he was at the foot of the ladder while she put it there, and the day behind that figure was four bells and a debt called in, and the debt had been carried in at the bar at about the third hour the previous afternoon by a man of twenty with a scythe over his shoulder who had come up the lane to say nine words and had answered none of the four questions that were asked of him and had gone back down it.

It was his sixty-fourth morning and he was at the ninth house at about the eighth hour and the door was shut.

---

Nobody in Coldharrow pays a debt on the day it is called in. That is not a rule and there is no form for it and it is not a custom anybody has ever been asked about. It is what happens, the way it is raining.

The woman who keeps that house is about thirty-four and has four children and a man who is not there any more, and the man of twenty stood in her yard at about the third hour the previous day and said nine words and went back down the lane, and nine people in that yard heard the nine words, and four of them had worked out in four seconds that the reason a man with a bad leg exists at all in the mind of the woman of forty-one is the reason the woman of the ninth house knows that a man with a bad leg exists, and neither of those groups said anything, and that was correct, and it had been correct for a week.

He knocked. Nobody came to the door, which is not the same as nobody being in.

He stood in that yard for four minutes.

There is a join in that yard and it took him about a second and a half to find it, and it is in the one thing he has actually been watching since the second morning of the sixty-sixth week, which is a thing kept alive by a person. A debt at Coldharrow stops being two people in a kitchen at about the seventh hour of the previous afternoon and starts being nine other people's estimate of the two of them, and the estimate is worth more than the money, and the money is two on a plank and the estimate is a shut door and a man with a scythe standing in a yard saying nine words, and nobody in the village has ever been asked which of those two a debt actually is.

It told him nothing. It is a place and not a bargain and there is nothing on either side of it that anybody could be entered against, and it is the fourth time he has got one out of a thing and there is not going to be a fifth, and he stood in a yard in the rain and did not say any of it out loud, which is a decision and not a virtue, and which cost nothing at all.

He stood there for four minutes and then he went and looked at the bar, because a bar in this country is the only thing in a village that anybody in a village can be asked about, and the woman of fifty at the bar had the board out and read him nothing at all, because he has not been a stranger at that bar for about a month and she does not count people she has seen before and has not told him and is not going to.

---

At the second light, before any of that, he had asked her a question on the ladder and got an answer, and the answer was in a kitchen of nine people in a village of about a hundred and ten people three miles up the lane, and he has not got over it and does not expect to.

“Which of the two is the debt.”

“There are two strokes on here and I put them both,” said the woman of forty-one, without stopping, “and the second one is the debt, and it has been the second one since about the seventh hour yesterday, and you are asking me the wrong question. The right one is what a stroke is for. I am going to tell you that, and then you can decide what to do about it.”

He waited.

“A day’s figure is a weighted number,” she said. “*Four bells is four, and a stranger is three, and a market is four, and a boundary is seven, and a debt called in is two, and a fire is two, and a burial is one.* And a weighted number cannot be turned back into what was done. *A six on that wall is four bells and a fire, or four bells and a debt called in, or two strangers, and on nine of the last hundred days I could not have told you which, and I have never once been asked to, and it has never once mattered, and if anybody in this village ever wants to know what a stroke was for I am the one person in two hundred and ten who has the seven weights, and seven weights is not a day, and a day is what a stroke was for, and there is nothing on this plank that says which of the seven a person was doing it for.*”

She went down the ladder and went to do her day and left him standing at the foot of it with a leg and a morning and nothing to do with either.

---

He was at neither place at the hour it happened, and that is the whole of the shape of that day, and putting it in a sentence took him the whole of that morning, and the sentence is not a good one.

In Coldharrow he was in a yard at the eighth hour and the door was shut and a thing had been done the previous afternoon and there was nothing in that yard about it. Eleven miles down the lane and off it, at about the same hour, a woman of thirty was in her own kitchen and somebody told her something, and the somebody was her mother, and the something was that her mother's brother, about seventy-four, who has shifted more wet earth in that field than anybody in it and who got into the line on a wet day about a hundred and forty years ago and has never once been asked about it, fell in his own yard in the wet on the last day of the sixty-sixth week and has not been out of his own house since.

It took her nine seconds to say what she said, and she said it standing at the table with her hands flat on it, and nobody in that kitchen had expected to be saying it that morning.

“Then it is the man with the spade,” she said.

Nobody said anything.

“He is not in the line,” said her mother.

“He has never been in the line,” she said, “he has dug in the rain for three generations and nobody asked him and he was never asked and if he digs one now then he digs one now, and it is the same wet day, it is the identical wet day, it is a hundred and forty years and a day.”

And then she said the thing that is the reason there is no form for stopping in this country, which is that she is thirty and she is the ninth and there is no ninth after this and nobody in that hamlet has any idea what happens if the ninth stops, and it is not a form, and there is not going to be a form, and the only thing anybody has ever offered her about it is a man of about fifty-one who is right.

Both of those things went on without him. He was eleven miles away with a leg and a shut door and a bar that had nothing on it for him, and the woman of thirty was told a thing at about the eighth hour on the first morning of the sixty-seventh week and he did not know about it until three days later, from a person of that place, in a lane, in passing, and nobody thought to tell him and nobody was going to and nobody was thanked.

---

Two things set side by side is not a room.

That is the honest finding and it is worth having in a band whose engine is putting two things next to each other, because the whole of the thing he has been able to do for about a month is to say one thing about one arrangement to one person in one room, and the price of it has been that a person has to be in a room, and the reward of it has been that the person goes home changed, and both halves of that need a room.

*A reader is not a room.* Nobody in this country is going to answer a question in a chapter. Nobody in a chapter is going to shut a door because of what was said in it, and nobody in a chapter is going to have to stand in a yard afterwards and find out whether saying it was worth it. A yard is an instrument. It has nine people in it who have to be told, and the telling costs the person who is told, and there is no way round that and it is not a design fault. A page is a different instrument from a yard and a worse one, and it is the one that exists, and the whole of the difference is that in a yard somebody has to be in it.

He wrote that down in his own head, in a lane, in the rain, and it took him nine minutes and it did not help and it was the only thing he had to add to anything.

The man of fifty-two was on the lane at about the ninth hour going about his own business, which is counting what crosses, and he had not been asked to be there and did not say so and did not offer. Nobody asked him what he was the only person in, because that question belongs to a longer road and he said so eight days ago, and four hundred yards went past in which nothing whatever was said.

---

And at about the eleventh hour, at the bottom end of a lane eleven miles down and off the March, a person of that place asked a person of that place a question about one of the six, and neither of them knew that a man with a bad leg had said anything to anybody about it in a kitchen eleven miles up the lane behind them, and neither of them was ever going to find out.

The man of about fifty-one with a spade had been at a ditch at the end of a lane of nine householders, and the man of thirty with the cart and a beast was on the shafts of his own cart outside a bar, and they had known each other for nine years and had never once had a conversation.

The man of fifty-one put his spade down.

“If somebody paid you,” he said, “for one length. Just one. Would you take it.”

The man of thirty took four seconds and he did not make a joke of it, which is not what a man of thirty does when somebody asks him that.

“No,” he said.

“You have not thought about it.”

“I have thought about it every day for eleven years,” said the man of thirty, “and the answer is the same every day and it is not about the money, and I will give it to you in nine words because you have asked me and because you are the second person in a month to make me say it. *Nine men doing each other a favour, and if one is paid then eight are working for a man, and the man can go, and then eight men are standing in a lane on their own and there is nobody in this county with a form for that.*”

He put his hands in his pockets.

“I am not being right,” he said. “I am telling you what the nine of us are standing in. And you may tell anybody you like and nobody will repeat it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am going to mow my hundred yards this afternoon and you may watch me do it.”

The man of fifty-one picked his spade up and carried it back down the lane to a ditch he had been at since the second hour, and the two of them had not asked each other one single question about what the other one was for, and that was correct, and nobody thanked anybody, and nobody was relieved, and it was a week like any other week.
