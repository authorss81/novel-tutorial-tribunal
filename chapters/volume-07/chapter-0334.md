# Chapter 334: A Rota With Eleven Names On It And A Twelfth In A Stranger's Hand

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-four thousand and forty-one at the second light on the seventh and last morning of the sixty-ninth week, and he read it himself off the wall at the foot of the ladder. Four bells and a debt called in, which is six, and the debt was called in in this village, and the boundary is four hundred yards of hedge and three miles up the lane away.

It was his eighty-fourth morning. There is a boundary in the third village up this lane of four hundred yards of hedge that is walked in nine days by eleven names on the back of a flour bill in a kitchen, of whom nine come, and the nine days of it begin at about the seventh hour tomorrow, and the nine mornings of that walk run from tomorrow to the ninth day, and he was going to be standing at a bridge with one rail on it in a valley of sixty people on the fifth of them, and the two of those things cannot both happen and he had known that for about a month and had said it out loud in three places and had not done one single thing about it.

---

The debt was at the ninth house and it was being called in by a man who had been to that door four times.

He was thirty-four and he reads things out for a living at three tables in this county, and he is the man of about thirty-four, and he had come up the lane from the village eleven miles down and off it because that is the day he is in this village, and he had a paper with him and he was standing in a lane outside a door with four children behind it and no man in the house, and a stranger with a bad leg came down the lane at about the ninth hour, stood off, and did not say one word for four minutes, which is the correct treatment and is the whole of the technique he owns.

Then he asked him one question, because the man of thirty-four was a person of a place and had answered questions in lanes before.

“How many times.”

“Four,” said the man who reads things out. “*And I have a right to be at that door four times and not five, and there is no form in this country that says a man may stop being allowed to stand on a step, and I will be at it again in six weeks and there is nothing else in a week that I have to do.*”

The door was shut. It had been shut since the fourth time and it was not going to open and nobody in that lane thought it was.

“There are four children behind that door and there is no man in the house and there is a list at a cross in a church nine miles off that she has been on for nine years, and I have got the sum right and there is nothing whatever wrong with it, and I have not got a way of saying that to a shut door, and I have not said it, and I am not going to say it, and there is nothing in four hundred miles that would let me.”

He folded the paper and put it in his coat and went up the lane, and the man of thirty-one let him get a field ahead of him, and nobody thanked either of them, and the day was a debt called in and the debt was called in and nothing came of it, which is what a debt called in is.

---

Three miles up the lane. There is one kitchen in the third village that anybody will let a stranger stand in and it is the kitchen at the end of the lane, and there are always nine people in it and the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes.

He got there at about the eleventh hour and the man of about sixty-three was at the table with a barrow and a crowbar and his hands black to the wrist, and there is a flour bill face down in front of him and it has been face down since a stranger with a bad leg said he would walk four hundred yards of hedge on his own, in that kitchen, and nobody has turned it over in thirty-one days.

“Right,” said the man of about sixty-three, and turned the bill over, and did not say a word while he did it.

Eleven names. Four hundred yards. Nine days. Nine of them come.

And under the eleven, in a different hand, in a straighter hand than a man with a drain in his hand usually has, there is a name that the man of about sixty-three wrote because a stranger offered to walk a hedge, and the man of about sixty-three does not know that he has done it, and nobody in that kitchen has ever been told, and it is the only piece of paper in this country with a name of a man from off the world on it.

---

“You have not walked it,” he said.

“I have walked it,” said the man of about sixty-three. “*All nine days of it, and I have walked it since the day you offered and I have never once walked it with anybody, and six of the nine days I stood at one end of four hundred yards of hedge by myself and talked to a hedge, and the man before me did the same and the man before him did the same, and nobody has ever written that down anywhere and I have not written it down either.*”

He put the crowbar against the wall.

“And a stranger came on the seventh morning and walked half of it with me and did not say nine words the whole way, and I have not told anybody that either, and there is nobody in this kitchen to tell.”

“Where is the man who used to come with you.”

“Gone. Gone since the spring, and there is a woman of about thirty-four who came in the spring and said four words at this table and I have not seen her since, and I have not asked where she went, and there is a foot of that walk with nobody on it now and it is still walked every ninth day, and nobody in this village has noticed, and I have noticed and I have got no way of saying it to anybody that is not a stranger.”

---

And that is when he tried to carry it, and he tried it properly, in front of nine people, and it is the only time in thirteen days he tried anything in a room.

It did not take long and it was not the leg and it was not the distance.

A boundary is four hundred yards of hedge walked in nine days by eleven names of whom nine come. That is the whole of what it is. There is nothing on it that is a fact except the hedge and the nine days and the coming. Four hundred yards is half a mile of a man's walking and it can be done by anybody in this county in an hour including the man of thirty-one with the leg, and he has done it, on the fifty-third morning, in half an hour, and he saw the join on it twice and it told him nothing.

A man who carries it is a man who has to be there on nine mornings. Not once. Nine. Four hundred yards nine times, in the order, on the day, in the weather, for four hundred yards, and the ninth morning is four hundred yards and the man of sixty-three is sixty-three and there are nine of them that come and a twelfth that a man of about sixty-three wrote by accident on the back of a bill in a kitchen because a stranger offered.

He looked at the bill on the table.

And I have said out loud in two yards in eleven days that I am going to be standing at a bridge with one rail on it in a valley of sixty people in five days, and I did not write it down, and a person of seventy-four gave me that morning and she is entitled to stop and I cannot put her back, and I have not got a leg that gets me out of this one. The nine mornings and the ninth morning are the same week and they are four hundred miles apart and there is no road.

---

He said the price out loud before he asked, in a kitchen, in front of nine people, and he had been saying a version of it in five places in eleven days and this was the first time he had said it before doing the thing instead of after.

“Before I ask him, and I am asking, and I am going to say what it is first because I have learned that the price is smaller out loud than it is in and smaller is not the same as gone,” he said, “*a person who is handed a thing for a place becomes a person in a record, and there is no instrument in this country that takes a person back out of one, and nobody in this kitchen is going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be thanked for asking, and I am going to do it anyway because the alternative is that four hundred yards of hedge are walked by a man of sixty-three on his own for another year and I cannot stop that and I cannot take it over and I do not get to be sad about it in a kitchen.*”

He said it to the man of about sixty-three and not to the room.

“Do it,” he said. “Or do not. But I have to have said it out loud before I said the other thing, because that is the only instrument I have ever had and I am not going to use it the other way round.”

---

The man of about sixty-three put both hands flat on the table on the flour bill and left them there.

He did not say yes and he did not say no, and that is not a fence and it is not a courtesy and it is not a man of sixty-three being careful with a stranger. He was asking a question, and he had been holding it since about the seventh hour of the morning, and he had not known he was holding it until a stranger with a bad leg said the word anyway out loud in his own kitchen.

“How many of the nine come.”

That is what he said.

“You have been in this country eighty-four days,” said the man of about sixty-three. “*I have never asked you a question in this kitchen and you have never asked me one, and I have just asked you one, and it is the same question I have wanted to ask for thirty-one years, and I have asked nobody, and I am asking you because you are a stranger and strangers do not get talked out of it.*”

Nobody in that kitchen said anything. There are always nine people in it and the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes.

“How many of the nine come in a year,” said the man of about sixty-three. “*On the nine days, in a year, how many of the nine of us are on that hedge. Not how many walk it. How many are there.*”

He waited for it.

And the man of thirty-one, who had come up a lane eleven miles and then three miles more to ask a man of sixty-three for something, stood in a kitchen with a leg and nine people in it and could not answer it, and it took him four seconds, and nobody helped him with any of the four.

Because there is no answer. Nobody has ever counted. I have been in this country eighty-four days and I have not written a line in eighty-four days and there is no hand in this county I could have put one in, and the last time anybody asked a person of a place a question about a number was a man of about seventy at a plinth in a market town four days up this lane on the seventy-fourth morning, and he asked me how many loads go over that bridge in a year, and I said I cannot do that, and it is the only thing I have got to give anybody and I gave it to a man of seventy and I have been carrying it for ten days.

And I am carrying it to a kitchen now. A stranger with a leg comes eleven miles and asks a question that is not a question, and gets a question, and takes it away, and that is what a delegation is before it is anything else.

---

The bill stayed face up on the table for another two hours and nobody turned it over and nobody read a name out loud, and the tenth never came, as the tenth has never come.

The day came to four bells and a debt called in, which is six, and that debt was in the ninth house of this village and not in any other, and the hedge was four hundred yards and was not walked, and the walk begins tomorrow at about the seventh hour, and nine of eleven people know it does, and one of the eleven is a man of thirty-one whose name is on the paper and who is not going to be there for any of the nine.
