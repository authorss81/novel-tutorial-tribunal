# Chapter 325: About Ninety People

Sixty-three thousand nine hundred and eighty-four was what the plank said at Coldharrow at the second light on the fifth morning of the sixty-eighth week, and the man of about thirty-eight put the number into a lane behind the cross at about the second hour, having stood at the foot of that ladder four days down the lane on his way through. The day behind the figure was four bells and a burial, which is five, and the burial was in this market town, and the man of thirty-one was not going to it.

It was his seventy-fifth morning. There was a coffin in the main street at about the seventh hour and it went to the churchyard behind the wall at the other end, and nine people went behind it, and he did not go because nobody asked him to.

---

A coffin going down a street at a market hour is the fastest thing anybody has found for making a figure mean something, and it happened four feet from him at about the seventh hour with nobody arranging it.

The ground behind that wall is paid for out of the over-and-above. That is not a figure anybody said to him. He worked it out at about the eighth hour standing at the plinth with his leg out in front of him, and it took him nine minutes, and it is the only sum he has done in seventy-five mornings that came out where he wanted it to.

The over-and-above pays for the water and the bridge and the wall and the cross. The wall is what that coffin has just gone behind. So a man who has been dead for six days has been buried, this morning, out of a number on a slip in a foolscap book kept in a house at the end of this street, and there is nobody in this town who has ever thought about that, and the number is wrong in the way that has made it possible.*

He wrote it in his own head and it did not help and it was the only thing he had, and it is not a discovery, because he did not find it out. It was standing in front of him at the seventh hour with a lid on it and nine people walking.

---

The man of about thirty-eight did the sum.

He did it in a lane, out loud, on the end of a slate he had borrowed from a man at the cross, and he did it the way he has done sums for eleven years, and there were four people in that lane who heard most of it and nine who heard some of it, and he stood nine feet off and listened to all of it.

He worked out what a share of the cost of a town comes to when it is divided by four thousand eight hundred loads, and then he worked out what the same share comes to when it is divided by nine thousand, and then he worked out what the market takes in the two cases across a season, and then he stopped and put the slate down.

Nobody is going to be given those figures. Neither is he and neither is the stranger, and that is not a favour to anybody. It is the thing that happened.

---

“Do not write it down,” said the man of thirty-one.

“I am not going to write it down,” said the man of about thirty-eight. “I have been not writing it down for eleven years. That is the entire profession. *A number in a hand is a thing a person can be entered against, and this county has been getting on without figures on things for ninety years, and a figure on this thing is the end of that, and it is the end of it because the moment you have the difference in a hand then somebody is going to work out how to spread the difference, and the way you spread a difference is you spread it on the people who cannot refuse it.*”

He put the slate back against the wall of the cross.

“You have heard the sum,” he said. “I have not given you the figures and you are not going to get them off me, and that is the one favour I have ever done anybody and I am not going to be thanked for it.”

---

And then the person who had been in that lane since about the eighth hour, at the cross, with a broom, said the whole of what the morning was, and nobody had asked her, and she had been listening to four people say a number out loud for an hour.

She was twenty-nine and she looked after the cross and she kept the list, and the list had been at the cross for longer than she had, and nobody in that market town had ever asked her what she kept or what was on it, and she had not been asked in nine years and had not mentioned it in any of them.

“You are all talking about this as though it is a price,” she said. “It is not a price on ninety people,” she said. “Those ninety people are not going to be charged more. They are going to be told that the thing they have been counting on was never theirs.”

Nobody said anything, and nine people in that lane had stopped what they were doing, which does not happen often.

“The water comes out of a standpipe at the end of Church Lane and it has come out of that standpipe every day of my life,” she said. “And four of those ninety have children in this town, and one of the four is a woman of about thirty-two with a pitch at the cross who is expecting her second and has counted on that standpipe every day for nine years, and she is one of the four, and she is not going to be able to pay you for water in a year where the farmer cannot, and she is not going to be able to refuse either, because you cannot refuse a standpipe.

“*And there is no form in this country that says a person may say that a thing was never theirs. A price you can argue with. A rate you can ask about. A thing that was never yours is a sentence, and a person who says the sentence has nowhere to put it.*”

She picked the broom up again.

“That is all I have got and I have not had a question about it in nine years and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and somebody ought to have asked me about it a long time ago and not this morning with a stranger standing in the lane.”

---

The man of thirty-one went and stood at the plinth for about an hour and did not say one word.

The number ninety was said out loud in a lane at about the tenth hour by a woman of twenty-nine who keeps the list, and it is a real count and she made it and anybody can ask her about it and it is the only number anybody has said in three days that anybody can be checked on, and nine people heard it and two of them wrote nothing down and one of them has written it in his own head and that is the whole of the record of it in the world.

The man of thirty-eight has said that he cannot count the ninety. He has not been in seven of the nine towns. He has been in two of them on business and he has seen two of the nine lists and he has not seen the other seven and he is not going to guess at the other seven, and the difference between the number on a list at a cross and a number of people is a thing that nine people in this lane had not thought about before the ninth hour and will not think about again.

*About ninety is not ninety. About ninety is a woman of twenty-nine with a broom, and a list she has kept for nine years, and four names she knows and one of the four with a pitch at the cross and a child coming in the spring, and the other eighty-six are a piece of paper in a church in a town nine miles off, and there is no version of a morning like this in which a stranger with a leg goes and stands in front of the other eighty-six.*

---

At about the last of the light he made a decision, and it was the wrong one, and he made it out loud in a lane to a man of about thirty-eight and to nobody else, and he knew it was the wrong one four seconds before he said it and said it anyway and finished it.

“I am going to go and tell the woman of about fifty-eight,” he said.

The man of about thirty-eight put the slate down.

“She said no to you in nine words on a bridge, and she was right, and you have not found one word that makes it less correct.”

“She did, and she was right, and I have looked for it for twenty days, and I am going to go and say a thing to her that nobody has asked her for.” He put his hand flat on the plinth. “*That is the whole of the technique. That is the only thing I have ever been able to do and I am about to do it to the one person in four counties who has already said no to it, and I know what that is, and it is not a repair of anything, and I am doing it anyway because a figure came out of a yard this morning and there is nobody in this town who is better at a figure than she is, and that is the wrong reason and it is the reason I have.*”

Nobody thanked him. Nobody stopped him. Nobody is going to thank him for the first time he went four days up a lane and nobody is going to thank him for this.

The day came to four bells and a burial, which is five, and a coffin went behind a wall in the main street at the seventh hour, and nine loads went over a bridge, and a woman of twenty-nine went on sweeping at a cross with a list in a church nine miles off, and this market town has never had a figure of its own on any wall.
