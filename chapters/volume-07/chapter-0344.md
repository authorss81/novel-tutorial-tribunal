# Chapter 344: Six Things Laid Beside One Another And No Join Between Any Of Them

At the second light a woman of forty-one was on a ladder against a wall eleven miles up a lane and off it, and there is no plank in this village and no figure on any wall in it. The stranger with the bad leg was in a room over a smithy with four people in it and was not one of them, and the day she was putting on that wall is not available to him in any way at all.

The day came to four bells and a debt called in, which is six, and the debt was called in at a door in this village and not at any other, and four bells is four and a debt called in is two, and a boundary is four hundred yards up a lane in another village and is not on this day.

It was his ninety-fourth morning.

---

The room over the smithy is a dead woman's room and there is a bed in it with the straw still in it and nobody sleeps in it and it is not let, and the smith's wife was in it with her sleeves up because it is the only room in eleven villages with a table in it long enough for six things.

He asked her for the table at about the seventh hour and she said no to the first thing he asked, which was to move the bed, and then she said yes to the second, which was to put the bed in the corner and take the board off the wall trestles, and that is how he learned the order she does things in.

The foolscap book is on the shelf where her grandmother had it, strapped, and it is not a thing anybody in that room can do anything with except the stranger, who took it off the shelf in a fortnight and had it come apart in his hands in nine seconds and put it back.

Nobody touched it.

---

They laid the six out on the table. Out loud, in the room, with four people in it and the door open, and they laid them out in a row because that is the only way six things fit on a table.

The man of about sixty-three's four hundred yards of hedge, walked in nine days, nine of eleven, unpaid, thirty-one years.

The woman of thirty's line of nine diggers on a rise behind eleven houses, a rule written nowhere, nobody able to say who dug the first hole.

The man with the cart's mile of road, nine householders, ninety years, a length each, unpaid.

The woman of about fifty-eight's scales on a plinth at the end of a main street four days up this lane, a toll in a box, a beam, a share, a man of seventy who is entitled to stop.

The man of about thirty-eight's column on that same shelf, a levy that nobody has been chosen to hold, a book that came apart in nine seconds.

And the woman of about seventy-four's porch. Eleven names, undated, no column, no heading, no mark against any of the eleven, a board in a porch in a valley of sixty people two miles off a track.

They were on the table for four minutes.

---

And then the man of about thirty-four said the thing that made the rest of the morning, and he said it in the voice he reads things out in and he had not been asked.

“Put them side by side and it does not join,” he said. “I have been saying this in four counties for a fortnight in one form or another and I have got it wrong twice. A row is not a join. A column is not a join. Six things laid end to end on a table are six things laid end to end on a table, and what I have been calling a link for nineteen years is me being able to see the top of a page.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“And a hearing is not a document. A hearing is six people saying a thing in six places and the document is nothing and the document was never going to be the thing that made it work.*”

---

And the stranger said the other half, and he had it in four seconds and it was the answer to a question he had been carrying since a yard behind a smithy.

“The thing that has to be in six places at once,” he said, “is the round. *Not a copy of the round and not a book of it. A copy is threepence at any scrivener over a chandler and a scrivener copies at a penny a page, and there is no scrivener within nine miles of this smithy and there is no sheet in this county and there has never been one, and a thing that has to be in six places at once in this country can only be written out six times by six people who are not going to be thanked for it.*”

The smith's wife said the half of it that mattered and she said it with the cup in her hand and did not put it down.

“Then there are six pieces of paper,” she said, “and a piece of paper in my house that a person in my house has read is not a piece of paper, and a piece of paper in a house where nobody has read it is a piece of paper. I do not read a column. If you write six of them out, you will have six places somebody can be asked, and there is no way on earth to have six places somebody can be asked without six people knowing about it, and that is the whole of what you have and it is not very much and I am not going to pretend it is more.*”

Nobody argued with her and she had not been asked.

---

And a woman with a bag went past in the lane below with a book under her arm, going up the lane, and she did not stop, and she has carried that book to a smithy and back nine times a year for six years and she has done it on her own account since before he came into this county and she is not on his list and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps her.

She was thirty feet past when she said it, and she said it to the road.

“Six copies is six more things to move, and I have moved one thing nine times a year for six years and I am not going to be asked to move six, and you are all writing as though the making of it is the hard part.*”

Then she was gone up the lane and he did not say one word to her and she did not look round and nobody is going to thank her and she is not going to be thanked.

That was thirty feet of a lane and it was the truest thing anybody said in that room all morning, and nobody wrote it down.

---

So they wrote six out. On the backs of flour bills, because a piece of paper in this county is the back of something else. The smith's wife had eleven of them in a drawer because the mill brings them, and there is one mill, and she said so, and the man of thirty-four said that was the first time anybody in eleven villages had given him a supply chain for anything.

And nobody signed any of the six.

That is the finding and it is his and he said it in his own mouth at about the eleventh hour with four people in the room.

“There is no form in this country that says a man may set a name at the foot of a thing,” he said, “and therefore every name in this country has been put there by somebody who decided to, and the man who puts a name at the foot of a thing is a man who makes a person, and a person is a thing a person can be asked about for the rest of their life, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that takes a person back out of it. So there are six pieces of paper in six houses and none of them has a name on it, and if that means nobody can be asked who holds one then I am going to have to do it that way, and I am not going to put my own name on one and neither is anybody in this room.*”

The man of thirty-four said: “Then what is the good of six pieces of paper.”

“The good of them is that somebody in six places can be asked to say who comes next,” he said, “and that is all the good there is and I have spent ninety-four mornings looking for a better one.”

---

A man of fifty-two had been at the bar at the end of that road counting what crossed it at about the seventh hour, because that is what he does and he has been the somebody in nine counties in nineteen years and nobody has ever sent him and nobody sent him this morning and he was not asked to be there and he did not say so and he did not offer.

He came in at about the ninth hour because the smith's wife sent for him, which is the only time anybody has sent for anybody in this room in nine years, and he put the bit of lead on the table and looked at the six pieces of paper upside down and did not touch them.

“I have been the somebody in nine counties in nineteen years,” said the man of fifty-two, “and I have never once been sent for, and I am not going to be sent for now, and I will tell you the one thing I know about a table with six things on it and then I am going back to the bar.”

Nobody said anything.

“You have got six pieces of paper and no name on any of them and that is right and I would not put a name on one either. And I am telling you the price of it now so that it is not a surprise to you in a month: the day one of those six papers wants a name on it, there will be a man in this county who has one, and he will not be you, and he will put it there, and he will not tell you for a year, and there will be four of you who find out at the same time in different kitchens.*”

He picked the bit of lead up.

“That is all I am going to say about it and nobody asked me and that is the only reason I have said it.”

Nobody asked him anything and nobody thanked him and he went back to the bar and put the lead in his coat.

---

And then the man of thirty-four asked him for the thing, and he asked in a room with four people in it, and the stranger heard it happen from the step.

Not a favour. He asked it flatly, the way he asks things, because asking questions is what he does for a living.

“Give me the order of the six,” he said. “*Six words in a row. I cannot follow a sum down a page and everybody has known it since I was fifteen and I have told a stranger in this room so once, and I have never tried to do a thing by memory in front of people because I always had a page. I want six words in a row and I will carry them up the lane and I will not put them in anybody's mouth.*”

The stranger said no.

And he said the whole of it, in a room, in about a minute, to one person, and it is the shape of every price he has said out loud in a fortnight and it is the first time he has refused to give something he was not going to be thanked for.

“No,” he said. “*A list of six is what a man with a cart has spent a month telling me not to make, and he was right on the facts and right on the conclusion, and I have not found one word that makes either of those less correct. You have been the man who reads things out at three tables for nineteen years and you have said in this room that the top of a page is the only thing you have ever had, and I am not going to be the man who hands you a page for this. There is no form in this country that says a man may send anybody, and there is not going to be one, and a round of turns that has been read off a page and carried up a lane is a bill, and a bill is the thing that comes before a man saying that five other people are working for him.*”

He shut his mouth and left it there.

“And that costs you a week,” he said. “*I am not going to pretend it costs me anything. You are going to go up the lane on six different mornings with no order at all, and you are going to have to remember a round of seven turns in your head, in the order, in the weather, and the seventh of them is a person nobody in eleven villages has counted, and if you stop on the fourth morning then the round stops and there is nobody to blame except the man who would not write it down.*”

The man of thirty-four did not argue. He said that it was fair and then he said something else, which was that he had been going to read a column on his next morning at a table in a village eleven miles down the lane and off it, and that there was now no column, and that he would read six sentences out loud in six yards instead and would do it on his own account and would not be thanked.

That is the cost and it is a week of his mornings and nobody paid for it and nobody is going to.

---

And there was one more thing in that room and it came from a boy of seventeen with a book under his arm, and nobody asked him, and he had been in the yard listening and had not come in.

“There is a seventh one,” said Bram Orren.

Nobody said anything.

“It is in the round and it does not link,” he said, “and the round I read out in that kitchen has seven turns and the seventh of them is a person that nobody in eleven villages has counted, and I am not going to say who, and I said in that kitchen that if I said one then somebody would go and look for it and find a person and that that is worse than the round stopping.*”

He shut the book halfway.

“So I am telling you now, in a room, and not in a kitchen, and I am telling the room because you have all just written six things down and there is no order on any of them: there are seven, and the seventh one is not written on any of your six pieces of paper, and one of you is going to go and look for it, and I am telling you now so that it is on the table that one of you looked, and not on the paper.*”

Nobody said anything for nine seconds.

He looked at the table for a while and then he said the thing, and he said it out loud in a room with four people in it and he did not say it to the boy.

“No,” he said. “*I am not going to look for it and I am not going to send anybody and there is no form in this country that says a man may send anybody, and a boy of seventeen has just told me that the fastest way to spoil this is to go and find a person, and he is seventeen and he has been running a book since he was twelve and he is right.*”

He put his hand flat on the table next to six pieces of paper with no names on them.

“And it costs the round,” he said, “and it costs the woman of thirty, who said a name in a yard yesterday, and it will cost that woman her whole life and she is thirty and she is the ninth of nine and she is not going to be thanked for any of it, and I am not going to be the reason a person in a valley of sixty people finds out what is in a porch because I could not leave a thing alone for nine seconds in ninety-four mornings.”

The boy of seventeen put the book under his arm and went out to the yard, because he had said it in the way a man says a thing that is finished, and a boy of seventeen can tell the difference.

---

Six pieces of paper went out of that room in six hands in an hour and none of them had a name on it, and the debt was called in at a door in this village and the door was shut and it is the third time and there is no form in this country that says a man may stop being allowed to stand on a step, and a woman of about thirty-four at a bar at the end of that road said one thing to the man of thirty-four about it and was not thanked, and nobody was relieved, and the seventh thing was not looked for.
