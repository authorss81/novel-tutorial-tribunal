# Chapter 340: The Ninetieth Morning Is A Third One And Nothing Is Built On It

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-four thousand and eighty-three at the second light on the sixth morning of the seventieth week, and he read it himself off the wall at the foot of the ladder. Four bells and a market, which is eight, and there is a market on the green by that tower this morning and it is in this county and it is on that plank.

It was his ninetieth morning in this country. Nobody in that lane said anything about that. There is a market on the green one day in a fortnight and there is a bell in that tower that has not been rung for want of a wage and it was not rung this morning either, and four people were in the lane at the second light and not one of them looked at the wall.

---

He had a leg and a lane and a day, and he was going to go and find the sixth.

That is the whole of the plan and it lasted nine minutes. There are six arrangements and there are six people, and he has been in five of the six in thirteen days: a man of about sixty-three who walks four hundred yards of hedge in nine days and has done for thirty-one years; a man of about thirty-eight who went out of a market on a cart with a bag; a man of about thirty with a cart and a beast who is the third of nine on a mile of road; a woman of thirty who is the ninth of nine in a field behind eleven houses; and a woman of about seventy-four in a porch in a valley of sixty people.

The sixth is a woman of about fifty-eight at a set of beam scales on a stone plinth at the end of a main street four days up this lane, and she is six months gone with a child, and her father is seventy and has been on that plinth forty-four years, and she said no to him in nine words on a bridge on the sixty-fourth morning and she was right and he has never found one word that makes it less correct.

On the eighty-eighth morning he had decided to go and tell her a figure, and on the eightieth morning he had decided not to, and on the eighty-ninth morning at a bridge with one rail on it he had been told by a person of seventy-four that a man cannot be in two places and there is no form that says a man may send anybody.

And this morning he was in a lane with a leg and a market on the green and a woman of about fifty-eight four days away, and he stood in the lane and worked out which of those two things he was going to do and it took him longer than nine minutes and it came out the same both times.

---

The man of about thirty came up the lane at about the eighth hour with the cart and the beast and stopped at the market because a market on the green is where a man with a beast stops, and he came out of the third house of a mile of road nine men keep.

He did not say good morning and he had not said good morning for a fortnight and he was not going to.

“I have been up the road and back and I am going to say one sentence and it is not about you,” he said. “*On the fourth morning of the seventieth week, at about the second hour, a woman of about thirty-four at a bar at the end of the road said a thing in the open at a cross about a town four days up this lane, and I was at the third house when I heard it, and I have had a cart and a beast and a pair of ears and I have had it four days.*”

He put the beast's head round.

“And here is the sentence. *A sentence put into a lane gets in front of the person who put it in there. You are eleven miles off and I am nine and there is a man of about thirty-four who came through on that same morning and did not get to where he was going, and I do not know what that costs and you do, and you are going to be asked about it at a bar, and the bar is going to be the place you are asked about it in, and there is no form in this country that says a man may be asked about a thing in a place that is not a room.*”

Nobody said anything.

“Do not thank me,” said the man of about thirty. “And do not go up there. I have been up that road for nine years and there is a woman of fifty-eight in that market who has had a hand at a plinth in the rain at seven in the morning since she was forty-nine, and whatever it is you have come up here with, it is not what that plinth needs, and I am the third of nine on a mile of road and I have not been thanked for it in ninety years, and I know what a thing that cannot be put down costs, because that is my own trade.”

---

And then the man of about thirty-four came through the market with a strap on a bag and stopped at the cross, because he is in Coldharrow one day a week and has been in Coldharrow one day a week for years, and he is a person of that place and he is a person of the village eleven miles down the lane and off it, and he reads things out for a living and has not been thanked for it and does not want to be.

He said one thing at the cross, in a lane, standing next to his own bag, about a thing he had stood next to, and it is the only time anybody has said it and he is not going to be asked again and neither is anybody else.

“There is a number on a slip on a shelf in a back room in a market town four days up this lane,” he said, “*and nobody in that town has ever written down how many loads go over that bridge in a year, and it is not written down anywhere in that county and it is in no hand in it, and I have been in that room twice and read out loud in it and I could not tell you the two figures because I have never had them and I have not got a head for a column and I have said so in this county for nineteen years.*”

He shifted the bag.

“*Somebody has to write down a year of loads on a slip in that room. That is the whole of the honest version and it is about a week of a clerk’s work and there is no clerk in that county and nobody there has anybody to do it and they have not known for sixty years that they needed it, and it is not a mystery and it is not a discovery and there is nobody in four hundred miles whose job it is.*”

He looked at the man with the leg and then he did not look at him again.

“I am not going to ask you for it and you are not going to be able to do it, and I have said it out loud at a cross because I have read things out for nineteen years and that is what I do with a thing, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to be thanked.”

---

And the man of about thirty said the rest, and he was right on the facts as he always is and this time he was also right on the conclusion and neither of them noticed.

“*A thing that was kept alive by one person is now being kept alive by a person who has been asked to carry a sentence. That is not the arrangement that was there the day before yesterday. That one cost one person, which is a man of seventy who was eight years old when a number was said to him in his own yard, and he is seventy and he is entitled to stop.*”

He took the beast's head again.

“This one costs a woman of thirty-four at a bar who is not going to be thanked for it and cannot put it down, and it is not yours to change and it is not hers to change, and the only person in this county who could change either of those is a man of seventy four days up a road who said one thing in his own yard and has not been asked since and is not going to be, and he is the last one who can, and he is entitled to stop, and that is a worse arrangement than the one you had and you went and made it on purpose.”

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was relieved. Nobody said that it had been worth it and nobody said that it had not, and he was not going to say either, and the six were still standing, and the slip was still on a shelf in a foolscap book four days up the lane, and the beam was still on the plinth, and four hundred loads a week went over that bridge in a season and would go over next week, and a woman of about thirty-two with a pitch at a cross in that town was counting on a standpipe for a child coming in the spring and did not know that there was a figure, and ninety people in nine towns could not pay the difference and nobody at any of those tables wanted it, and the man of about thirty-eight was somewhere on a four-day road with a bag and a bad back and a book he had not decided about, and a woman of about thirty-four at a bar was a person in a record because of a stranger.

And six people had agreed to be somewhere on a day. The day had been changed twice and a person of that valley had said on the seventy-first morning, at a bridge with one rail on it, that the day was three days off as of that morning and that it would change again. He was not going to be in it, and he had said so in a yard and to one person and in a lane and twice at a bridge with one rail on it, and none of those had been an ask and none of them was going to become one now, and he knew it, and he had known it for a long time, and saying it out loud had never once made it smaller.

He was not going to be able to undo it. He was not going to pretend the price was worth paying and he was not going to pretend it was not, and nothing in this country lifts a person out of a record and he has looked for a way to do it in ninety mornings.

---

The bell in the tower was not rung. The market went on and came down by the ninth hour. Nine loads went over a weighbridge eleven miles down the lane and off it and the beam came back down, and a man of about thirty took a cart and a beast back down a mile of road that nine men keep, unpaid, ninety years, and he did not mow his length that morning and nobody asked him why.

And at about the eleventh hour a boy of seventeen came out of a gate at the top of the lane with a book under his arm and the strap on it, and he had somewhere to be that he had not got to yet, and he went off down the lane at a walk without saying anything to anybody, and he was not asked, and he did not want to be, and there is a question in that book with a mark on the end of it that nobody has answered and a third place in it he has never been able to make out, and both of them are still in there.
