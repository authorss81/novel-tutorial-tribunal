# Chapter 328: The Arrangement Did Not Stop

The first morning of the sixty-ninth week came on a morning when the plank at Coldharrow said sixty-four thousand and seven, and the man of about thirty-eight was at the foot of that ladder at the second light four days down that lane and said the number out loud at the plinth in this street at about the second hour on his way out of a county. The day behind it was four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is the ordinary number, and which is the number this market town has been on for a hundred and forty years and has never once had a figure of its own about anything.

It was his seventy-eighth morning and it was the first morning of the sixty-ninth week. He had been in this market town for five days and he was going to be in it for two more mornings, and the man of about thirty-eight was at the cross with his cart still unloaded and three days of road in him and a fortnight of notice behind him.

---

Nothing had stopped.

That is the finding and he had come four days up a lane to get it and it took him about an hour on the first morning and it is the answer to the only question anybody would have wanted answered, and it is not the answer anybody would have wanted.

The plinth was where the plinth is. The beam went up and went down. Four hundred loads go over that bridge in a week in the season and the season has been running four months, and nine went over in the hour he was standing at it. The toll was being taken at the plinth, in a wooden box with a slot in it, by the man whose hands are behind his back, and the money in it went into a basket and the basket went into a room at the end of the main street on market nights and nobody counted it in front of anybody and nobody has counted it out loud in that town in nine years.

The standpipe at the end of Church Lane was running. The wall behind the churchyard was standing with four feet of it put in again by a man of about forty-one with his own lime on the last morning of the sixty-eighth week, which is two days ago. The cross was where the cross is. The woman of about twenty-nine was sweeping round it at about the seventh hour with a list in a church nine miles off, and she did not look at the man with the leg.

The market was running. Four hundred people live in this town and four hundred loads go over a bridge in a week in a season and the two numbers have never been in the same sentence in the whole of the sixty years the slip has been in that book, and they are not in the same sentence now.

And the woman of about fifty-eight is doing her father's sums in her head this morning in a doorway at the end of this street, and she is six months gone with a child, and she was asleep at the second hour on the morning of the third day of the sixty-seventh week and was not woken, and she is not going to be woken today either.

---

The man of about thirty-one stood at the plinth for about an hour and worked out the shape of what he had found, and it is the only thing he has got, and it took him the length of a load going over and coming back, and he did not say it out loud to anybody and it is in the state of his own head and nobody can check it.

*The untruth is not the structure.*

He has been in four counties in seventy-eight mornings and every one of the things he has looked at has had the shape of an arrangement, and an arrangement is a thing a lot of people turn up for, and the theory he has been carrying since a kitchen three miles up a lane is that a thing of that kind is held up by what people believe about it, and by nothing else.

This one is not. This one is held up by four hundred people and a beam and a man on it and a woman in a doorway, and if the number on the slip came out tomorrow, and it came out in a yard with nine people in it, and every one of the nine told two others before supper, the beam would still be on the plinth at the seventh hour and nine loads would go over it in the first hour and somebody would still have to say what they weigh.

The number is the paper. Everything else is the town.

*He has spent seventy-eight days telling anybody who would listen that a word is not a person and that the instrument for turning a word into a person is a name at the foot of a thing, and he has never once in this country asked the obvious question, which is what a thing looks like when you take the name off the foot of it. It looks like a market. It looks like a hundred and forty years. It is the only one of the six he has been in that would still be standing on the morning after the figure came out, and it is the only one of the six in which the figure was never the thing holding it up in the first place.*

---

Then the man of about thirty-eight counted a market day, because he is a man who counts things and carts had been going over all day and he had three days of road in him and nothing else to do with them.

He counted for eleven hours off his own memory on the end of the same slate, sitting on the step of the cross, and he did not tell anybody what he was doing and four people in that street asked him and he said he was doing nothing, and he came out with a number at about the last of the light and he said it out loud to the man of thirty-one and to nobody else.

“That is a day,” he said. “And a season here is twelve weeks, and there are forty weeks in a year of which this market is shut most, and I have been in nine counties in two years and I am not going to work it out in the street. *It is not four thousand eight hundred. You can go and ask the man on the stones what it is not, and he will tell you the same number he told the woman in his yard, and I have got a number of my own and I am keeping it until I have a second day, and I have got a fortnight of notice and a market that is shut two days a week.”

He put the slate down.

“That is the first count of my life that I have not been paid for and not asked for and it is the worst one I have ever done because I cannot check it against anything,” he said. “*A number in a hand is a thing a person can be entered against. That is the rule and I have lived on it for eleven years and I have broken it twice in four days in a town I am leaving in three, and I would do it again tomorrow and I am not going to be thanked for either.*”

---

At about the eleventh hour he asked the man of about seventy the question, and it was not about a figure, and it was the last thing he had come four days up a lane for and it took nine seconds to ask.

They do not use a name at that plinth. There has not been a name at that plinth in about nine years, and everybody in this market town says *the man on the stones* or *her father* or nothing at all, and it has been a hundred and forty years of that and a hundred and forty years is not a complaint.

He asked him what he was called.

It is the first question anybody has asked him in forty-four years that was not about a figure and was not about the toll and was not about the water, and it went across a plinth in a street in nine seconds, and he was not standing off the stones when he asked it and he did not say it in a yard and he did not say it in a room.

The man of about seventy did not turn round for four seconds.

“Not once in forty-four years,” he said, “and I am Wenlock.”

Nine words and the ninth of them was his own name and he had not said it in that street in forty-four years and he said it in the open air, in the rain, at the end of his own main street, at about the eleventh hour of a market morning, and nobody was in a room and nobody was going to be asked to say it again and he is seventy and he is entitled to stop and he did not stop and that was the whole of it.

---

The woman of about sixty-one heard it. She was in her own doorway with a bucket and she had been in that doorway for a year and she had never once used a name for the man on the stones in fifty years of living over the plinth, and she said it out loud afterwards, once, to nobody, in a yard, and four feet of wall had been put in two days before by a man of about forty-one who does not take anything for it.

That is the only name the man of thirty-one has that is not on the back of a bill.

It is not on a thing. There is no book, no shelf, no room over a smithy, no foot to it and nothing to be entered against and no copy of it anywhere in four hundred miles, and a name said out loud at a plinth in a street is not a name in a place, and he has known that since a kitchen three miles up a lane and he asked for it anyway.

He is not going to be thanked for it. He has been on a bill in a kitchen for twenty-five days because a man of sixty-three did not know what he was doing, and now he has one that is not on anything at all, and the two of them together do not make a person, and he has found out in seventy-eight mornings that a person is not made out of places somebody can say their name.

The day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, and a load went over and the toll went into a box, and the water ran at the end of Church Lane, and the wall was standing, and the number was on a slip in a foolscap book in a back room, and nobody in that market town had stopped anything and nobody was relieved and nobody was thanked.
