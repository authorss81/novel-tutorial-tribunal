# Chapter 331: He Starts To Count And The Count Will Not Close

There is no plank on this road and there is no figure on any wall in the county he is walking out of, and there is no woman of forty-one within four days of where he is standing, and a stroke does not travel. There is no instrument in this country that will carry one off a wall and into a lane in another county, and the only man in this country who could have had a figure this morning was a man standing at the foot of a ladder at the second light, and he was not one.

It was the fourth morning of the sixty-ninth week and the eighty-first morning of a man of thirty-one in this country, and the day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is the number most of the days in a year are, and a day with nothing on it is a day with nothing on it wherever a man is standing.

He had a leg and a road and the whole of the day in front of him and nothing to do with any of it.

---

The man of about thirty-eight was up that road somewhere ahead of him and could not be overtaken by a man on foot, and that was not a matter of the leg. A cart on a made lane does a day's road in a morning and a man with a bad leg does a quarter of one, and one morning of that arithmetic puts a day and a half between them, and there is nothing in this country that takes a day and a half of a cart back off a man on foot, and he was not going to try it.

The man of about thirty-eight had said he had four days of road in front of him. He had a bad back and a bag and a book he had not decided about, and four days had been the figure on the eightieth morning and there was no reason on earth to believe it was the figure now.

And there is nobody in between. That is the other half and it took him the length of a field to get it. Not a carter, because a carter is four days behind a man who is going this way. A shepherd. A woman going to a mother. A man with a load of lime for a wall. A boy sent for a horse. The whole of the transport in this country is a person going where they were going anyway, and if a person is not going your way then nothing in the world is.

He stopped at a gate and put his weight onto his leg and worked it out and got the same answer, and it did not improve the second time, and that is the part he had to get used to in eighty-one mornings.

---

The arrangement he most wanted to carry was four days behind him and it was going to stay four days behind him.

That is the whole of it and there is no more to it than that. There is a set of beam scales on a stone plinth at the end of a main street with a man of seventy on it and a woman of about fifty-eight doing his sums in her head, and a man of about thirty-eight knows that the number on a slip in that woman's back room is half what it has been called for sixty years, and he is four days of road away with a bag, and he does not know what is going to happen in that street now and neither does anybody else, and there is nobody in the world who can be told.

And he had stood at that plinth in the rain for four hours on the seventy-fourth morning and had watched nine loads go over in an hour, and the woman of about thirty-two at the cross had told him in a market street that she was counting on a standpipe at the end of a lane the way a person counts on a wall, and the woman of about sixty-one had said in her own yard that she was not going to be the person who says the water stops, and none of those three women is going to be asked to choose and he cannot ask them and must not.

---

He did the count at the side of the road at about the fifth hour, in the open, out loud, on his own, and it took him the length of a field, and he is the only person in four counties that has ever heard it.

*One. A boundary of four hundred yards of hedge in the third village up the lane. Eleven names on the back of a flour bill in a kitchen, walked in nine days, of whom nine come. It is a morning and a half and a bad knee and it is a morning and a half I cannot do in nine days and I have never once asked whether the man of sixty-three would do it if I asked him.*

*Two. A market with a set of scales on a plinth, four days up the lane. I have been in that street for eight mornings running and the man of thirty-eight has gone and the woman of fifty-eight is six months gone and the man of seventy is entitled to stop and nobody has been chosen.*

*Three. A field. A line of nine diggers on a rise behind the last of eleven houses, eleven miles down the lane and off it. A woman of thirty is the ninth in it and she has said twice that she wants it to be hers and not to be out of it and she is not going to be given it by me.*

*Four. A village with a fund in it, same eleven miles. A column on a shelf in a room over a smithy that nobody has been chosen to hold, and a woman with a bag carries it nine times a year and is not going to be asked and is right, and I am not on her list and there is no arrangement in this county that keeps me.*

*Five. A mile of road with nine householders on it and a man of about thirty with a cart and a beast who is the third of the nine and who has never been paid and never asked and who told a stranger to go and stand at his own bridge. Eleven miles the other way from the field, and I have not been in either of those two places this week.*

*Six. A valley off a track off this lane, a quarter mile past a bridge with one rail on it, sixty people, and a woman of about seventy-four who goes down there alone and has said out loud that she is not going to be the person the valley loses. Nine days from now, on a morning she named, and I did not write it down.*

Then he stopped, because that is where the count stops, and it has always stopped there, and it did not stop today either.

Four days up a lane. Eleven miles down and off it, and eleven miles down and off it again, and eleven miles the other way. Three miles. A track of two miles and a bridge with one rail on it. And two legs.

---

Here is the arithmetic of it, and it is arithmetic and not a mystery, and it took him four seconds and it had been true for eighty-one mornings.

Carrying one of them means not carrying another one. That is not a rule anybody made. It is what a man with a leg is, in the sense that a man with two arms is a man with two arms. He has spent eighty-one mornings learning that a person is not an instrument, and he has spent them in five counties, and the sentence he is standing in the middle of a road saying to himself at about the fourth hour of a morning on a lane is that he is not one either, and that a man who decides that other people can be in two of them at once is a man who has decided that a person is a thing that can be in two places.

And then the other half, which is the half the count is actually for.

So somebody else has to be in them. Not I have to be in all six. Somebody else. That is arithmetic and it does not go away and it is not a difficulty and it is the arrangement every one of these six has been running on for years without anybody writing it down: a thing only stays alive in a place because a lot of people in that place turn up on a particular day and do a particular thing, and if you stop turning up it stops, and nobody wrote the arrangement down because nobody who was in the room thought of a time when it would stop.

And every one of the six of them has at least one person in it who is already doing that. That is not a solution. That is the discovery. The man of sixty-three walks four hundred yards in nine days and has done it for thirty-one years and nobody paid him. The man of thirty has been the third of nine for eleven years. The woman of thirty is the ninth in a line and stays in it in the wet. The woman of seventy-four goes down alone.

So the answer is not that I carry it. The answer is that I get out of the way and let the person who is already in it keep being in it, and there is no instrument in this country that lets a man do that to somebody on purpose.

He put his hand flat on a gatepost and left it there for a while.

Which is the whole of it. Eighty-one mornings, and the only thing he has found is a way of being in fewer places than he was at the start.

---

He said it out loud anyway, in a lane at the side of the road, at about the fifth hour, to nobody, because he has said the price of his own method out loud in six places in eleven days and has not improved on it and does not intend to stop.

“A person finds out about themselves,” he said, “and nobody asked him, and that is why it is the price and not a fee. And I have worked out this morning that I am about to stop doing it to myself by making somebody else do it, and a person who is handed a sentence for a place becomes a person in a record, and I have spent two counties and a good many mornings finding out what that costs a person, and I am going to do it on purpose, and I am saying so out loud in a lane with nobody in it, and the lane is the only place in this country where a thing like that can be said at all.”

The wind took most of it.

---

He decided at about the seventh hour, in a lane, on his own, with a leg and no instrument, and nobody in the world knew he had decided it.

He did not write it down. He has written nothing down in eighty-one mornings and there is no hand in this country he could have put one in, and the one name he has is on the back of a flour bill in a kitchen three miles up a lane and it is a rota for a boundary and it is not about him.

Nine days. That was the price and it was the only figure he had, and it was not a figure anybody could check: if he is going to hand a sentence to somebody in a place he is not in, then in nine days that sentence is going to arrive in a place where he has never stood, in a mouth that is not his, and there is no form in this country that says a man may take it back, and there is no instrument in four hundred miles that can.

And the part that frightened him, which he said out loud to the same empty lane because he had learned eleven days ago that the price said out loud is smaller than the price not said, and not very much smaller, and smaller is not the same as gone.

“The thing I am not saying,” he said, “is that the man of thirty-four says I should not do it. He said it on the seventy-ninth morning in a market four days up this lane and he said I did not need to, and he was right on the facts, and I have gone on four days anyway, and I have not got a better reason than that I have run out of mornings and he has run out of ones he is willing to spend on me.”

Nobody argued with him. There was nobody there to.

---

The road went on down. There is nothing at the side of it in this county that anybody has ever written a figure on, and a stranger on a road is not a weight on a plank in another county four days away, and the man of thirty-eight had a cart and a bad back and four days of road in front of him, and there is nothing in this country that puts a day's road back on a man on foot.
