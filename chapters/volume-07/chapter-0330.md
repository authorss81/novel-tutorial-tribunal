# Chapter 330: The Eightieth Morning Is A Count Of Mornings, And A Person Is Still At A Plinth

“Eightieth morning,” said the man of about thirty-eight.

He said it from the bottom of a wall at the end of the main street, to nobody, with his back against a plinth that is not his, at about the sixth hour, before the light was properly on the roofs, and four people in that street heard it, and one of them is the stranger with the leg.

“It is a round number and there is nothing in this country that turns on a round number,” said the man of thirty-one, in the voice he uses for a stranger at a bar. “*I have heard that one. Mornings are not days.*”

Nobody argued about it. Neither of them was right and both of them were right, and the man of about thirty-eight got his cart up at the cross and the stranger got his leg under him, and neither of them said one word about it again that day, and nothing is going to be built on it.

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-four thousand and eighteen at the second light, and the man of about thirty-eight brought it about the second hour because he was going that morning and had been at the foot of the ladder before he was at the cross, and the day it stood for was four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, which is the ordinary number, and the whole of the last nine days has run on it.

It was his eightieth morning in this country, and it was the third morning of the sixty-ninth week. He had a leg and a lane four days down it to walk and a valley two miles off a track at the end of it, and he was going to be in a porch in nine days, and six people had agreed to be somewhere on a day and the day had been changed twice and would change again, and he was not going to be in it, and he had said that in two yards and to one person and once in a lane, and none of those had been an ask.

---

The man of about thirty-eight said the finding in a lane behind the cross at about the seventh hour, with his back against the wall and his cart up and nine minutes before he went, and it is in the mouth of a person of that place and it is going to stay in his mouth and it is not going anywhere.

“Not ninety who know it,” he said. “About nine know it and about ninety are on a list. The number that knows has not stopped going up all week and the number that is on the list is not going down at all, and that is a different arrangement from the one there was on the fourth morning, and it costs more, because the nine have to go on saying it and the ninety have to go on being told about it and neither of the two has anywhere to put a sentence about it.”

He shifted the slate under his arm.

“It was not that on the fourth morning. On the fourth morning it was a plinth and a man and a number on a slip in a book and four hundred people who did not know. *It is not the lie that is holding it up and it was never the lie. It is a toll on a load and a beam on a stone and a man of seventy and a woman of fifty-eight and four hundred people who cross a bridge because it is the way to the next town, and the paper came out on the fourth morning and the paper was never the wall.*”

He looked at the man of thirty-one.

“And the only person in this county who can change any of that is a man of about seventy who was eight years old when it was said and who has been the only one who could look at the other side of it for sixty years, and he is seventy, and he said it once, in a yard, in the rain, and he is entitled to stop and he is not going to be asked again. *That is not yours to change. It is not the woman of fifty-eight's to change. It is not mine to change and I am going four valleys away this morning. It is his, and it was his before you came up the lane, and it will be his in a month after you have gone down it.*”

Nobody said anything for nine seconds and nine loads went over.

“I said it in a room over a smithy eight days ago and I was wrong one half of it,” he said. “I said the count does not stop because a count is not a man. *The count does not stop and neither does the thing, and neither of them stops because of a man, and that is a harder sentence than the one I had and I have been carrying it for eight days and there is nobody to carry it to except a stranger with a leg.*”

---

They walked to the cross together at about the seventh hour and neither of them said anything on the way, and it is the whole of what happened in that lane.

At the cross he put the slate on the step and did not wipe it and left it, which is a thing a man who has been counting things for eleven years does not do, and the stranger watched him do it and understood that he had left the number on it or had not and there was no way to ask.

“Two days of road in me and four days of road in front of me,” he said. “There is a room over a smithy with a book on a shelf in it and a woman with a bag who carries that book to a smithy nine times a year and has done for six years and is not going to be asked to keep it, and I am going to walk four days and I have not decided about the book and I am not going to decide in a lane at a cross at the seventh hour with a man I met in a room about a levy.”

He got the cart.

“If you are ever in a county with no bar in it,” he said, “do the thing you do. That is not advice and I am not giving it to you as advice, and I have got no idea whether it works, and I am going to find out for four days with a bag and a book in it and a wife who has been patient about it for two years. *Nobody is thanked. You are not thanked and I am not thanked and there was never going to be any of it, and that is the correct arrangement and I would not trust either of us who said otherwise.”

He went out of the market at about a quarter past the seventh hour with a cart and a bad back and did not stop at the plinth and did not look at the man on it, and the man on it did not turn round, and that is the last time a cart went out of that street in front of him.

---

The price is not the yard.

The yard was a quarter of an hour on the fourth morning of the sixty-eighth week and nine people were within hearing of it and four of them understood it and the man of about seventy said a thing out loud once, in his own ground, in the rain, and it is done and it cannot be unsaid and nobody is going to ask him to say it a second time.

The price is that a thing which has had to be said out loud in a yard is a thing that can be said again.

He found that out on the seventh night and it took him nine minutes in a lane. Two men in a tavern at the end of the main street had been saying all week that the water in this county ought to be paid for by the people who use it, and neither of them had been in the yard, and neither of them knew either of the two numbers, and one of them has said it four times this week to anybody who will stand still, and he is a decent man and he is not a villain and he has a reason of his own, and his reason is that his own house is at the wrong end of the standpipe and has been for thirty years, and that is a real reason, and it is a different reason from a man of seventy standing in his own yard, and it went into that tavern in four days out of a yard that four people were within hearing of.

*That is the price of the technique and it is the only price it has ever had and I have been saying it in five places for ten days and I have never once said the whole of it. He said it in a lane, alone, to nobody, in nine seconds, and then he wrote nothing down, because he has written nothing down in eighty mornings and there is nobody in four hundred miles he could hand it to.

---

The woman of about fifty-eight was on the step at the end of the main street at about the ninth hour with a basket, and she was not coming to the bridge, and he had got a thing to say to her and had said out loud two days before that he was going to say it and knew it was the wrong one.

He did not say it.

He stood nine feet off the step for four minutes and she let him stand there, and then she said one sentence, and it was not about him, and it was the only sentence anybody said to him in nine days that he did not have to do anything with.

“My mother drank from that standpipe for fifty-one years,” she said, “and she is behind that wall, and she never knew, and that is not a thing that can be gone and told to her. *And my father is the only man alive who could go and tell her, and he is seventy, and he is not going to be asked, and I am not going to ask him, and I am not asking you.*”

She went in with the basket. He did not follow her. He has had one sentence in nine days that was not about him and he is not going to be able to use any of it and nobody is going to thank him for it.

---

The market opened and did not stop.

There is no bell in this town that anybody rings for anything. There is a man of about seventy on a stone plinth at the end of the main street with his hands behind his back, and nine loads went over the bridge in the first hour, and a toll went into a wooden box with a slot in it, and a beam went up and went down, and a standard of the load came out of a box on that plinth about once a year and went back in, and nobody in that market town has ever been asked what the number on the slip in the back room at the end of the street is for, and about nine people know it and four hundred loads a week go over a beam that is not what everybody in this town has been told it is.

A woman of about thirty-two has a pitch at the cross and is expecting her second in the spring and is counting on a standpipe at the end of a lane and does not know that there is a figure. A man of about sixty-three came in with eggs and went out and did not buy anything and is one of the four the woman of about twenty-nine can name. A man of about forty-one is in a lane with a barrow and his own lime putting four feet back into a wall. A woman of about twenty-nine is sweeping round a cross with a list in a church nine miles off.

And on a step at the end of the main street, in the rain, a woman of about fifty-eight is standing with a basket and is not coming to the bridge.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was relieved. The number is on a slip in a foolscap book in a back room and it is not right and it has not been taken out and about ninety people cannot pay the difference, and a man of about thirty-eight is on a cart on a road four days long with a bag and a bad back, and six people have agreed to be somewhere on a day and two of them have changed it twice and he is not going to be in the room, and he knows it.

The day came to four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, and that is the number most of the days in a year are.
