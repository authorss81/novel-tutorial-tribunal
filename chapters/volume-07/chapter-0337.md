# Chapter 337: The Tenth Person Is Found By A Person Of That Place And Not By Him

There is no plank in this road and no figure on any wall in this village. The wall is a lane away at the far end of it, and a woman of forty-one was on a ladder against it at the second light, and the man of thirty-one was a mile off on a road with nine houses on it and could not have got to the foot of that ladder with a leg.

It was the third morning of the seventieth week and his eighty-seventh morning, and the day came to four bells and a stranger, which is seven, and the stranger was not him. He was the third day running that the stranger in a day's weight had not been him, and he was the ninth, and he noted it in four seconds in a lane and made nothing of it, because this county does not count a man's mornings and neither did he.

---

The road is a mile. It leaves the lane at a gate and goes down past nine houses and comes back into the lane at a gate the other side, and it has been kept up for ninety years by the people who live on it, each of them a length, and the lengths are not written down and are not equal and are decided every year in nine minutes of ill temper at a gate.

The man of thirty is the third of the nine. He has a cart and a beast and he has been the third for eleven years and he is the only one of the nine who has ever thought about asking to be paid and he has never asked and nobody has ever offered.

He was at the third length at about the seventh hour and he had a scythe out and he was doing it the way a man does a thing he has done every year of his life, and the stranger came down the mile with a leg and did not say good morning and stood nine feet off, and neither of them was a surprise to anybody because the man of thirty had told his own father about a stranger with a bad leg in the spring and had said so in that lane in front of anybody who cared to stand and listen.

---

“You are early,” said the man of thirty. “*And before you say anything, I am going to say the thing I have been waiting to say to you since the middle of the last three weeks, and then I am going to mow my length.*”

He did not stop mowing.

“You said there was a tenth. In this lane. In front of my gate. *And I stopped you, and I was right to, and I have thought about it every day since the middle of the last three weeks and I have not found one thing wrong with stopping you, and I have not found one thing right with saying it either, and both of those are the same thing and it is the whole of what I have got.*”

He shifted the scythe.

“A tenth is a count. And a count is a list. And a list is the thing that comes before a bill, and a bill is the thing that comes before a man saying that eight other men on a mile of road are working for him. That is what is four years away for me and everybody else and I have seen it on a man.”

“Yes,” said the stranger. “You were right on the facts and you were right on the conclusion and I have not found one word that makes either of those less correct.”

“Then say the other half.”

“I have not got the other half. I have not had it since the sixty-ninth morning and I have been in four counties looking for it and I found a boy of seventeen with a book that can put things in an order and cannot say when, and a woman of about fifty-eight who has said no to me in nine words and was right, and a field with nine people in it that I stood in on a dry morning yesterday and was not one of and got nothing for, and a man of about fifty-one who is still right.”

The man of thirty stopped mowing for the first time.

“Say that again,” he said. “The bit about the field.”

---

And that is how the man of about thirty-one found out that the count had almost happened in this lane, and that is how he came to be standing in a room with a thing he had done, which is the third time in a fortnight and the worst of the three.

It was not in a room. It was a gate and a length of a mile and nine people who live on it, and by the tenth hour there were four of them standing about and the man of thirty-one was in the middle of them with a leg and the thing he had done was this.

In the middle of the last three weeks, in this lane, on a day he has counted and got wrong, he said the words there is a tenth, out loud, in the open, in front of a gate, because he was right on the facts and had the number in his head and had been told the number by a person of a place. And a man of thirty had stopped him. And a woman of about forty-one at the seventh house had heard him say it from a gate and had said nothing and had gone back in.

And she has been doing two lengths in the dark ever since, and she was doing two lengths in the dark before, and neither of those is the same thing and the difference is eighteen days and eleven words.

Nobody in that lane had ever mentioned it to him. He had not asked. He had walked down this mile twice in the time he has been in this country and had asked a man of thirty one question about being paid and had got an answer and had not asked one single question of anybody else on nine lengths of road.

---

The man of about thirty-four found her, and he found her on his own account, and it took him no part in any of this.

He was going that way. He reads things out for a living at three tables in this county and he is in the tenth village one day a week and in Coldharrow another, and the two of them never fall in the same week, and there is a road between the two of them, and it is eleven miles, and it goes past a gate at the top of a mile of road that nine people keep, and he had not got to where he was going, because he had stopped at the gate at about the fifth hour to get his leg over a rut and there was a woman of about forty-one coming out of the seventh gate with a barrow.

He watched her do a length. Then he watched her stop, and go back, and do the other one, and go back in.

And then he asked her one question, because asking a question is what he does for a living and he had not been asked one and he did not know there was a question about it and he did not know there was a stranger in the county with a bad leg.

And she told him, because he asked it in a lane like a man asking about the weather, and because she is a woman of about forty-one and has been doing this for eleven days and had told nobody and had not done it in front of anybody.

He came up the mile at about the ninth hour and found the four of them at the gate and told the man of thirty-one, in a lane, flatly, and it was not a favour and he did not want one and he did not know what he was handing over.

“*There is a widow at the fourth house with a bad hip, and she had that barrow for nine years, and she put it down in the spring and told nobody, and I have been past that fourth gate for nineteen years and I did not know until this morning. And a woman of forty-one at the seventh house takes it up at the fifth hour and puts it down at the fourth gate before the widow is up, and if the fourth house ever finds out then that is the end of it.*”

---

He asked her the ordinary question, in the ordinary four words, standing at her gate at about the tenth hour on his own leg, and he had asked it five times in this country and it had never once got him anything.

“What is it for.”

She did not tell him about the widow, because the man who reads things out had already told him about the widow and she knew he had and she has not got a way to complain about that.

“It is not for anything,” she said. “*It is for a hip. She carried it nine years and she has not carried it since the spring and she has not asked and she will not ask, and there is a barrow in that lane going rotten and a hip going in that house, and there is no form in this country that says a man may put a barrow down.*”

She looked at the ground.

“You wanted to know if I had stopped,” she said, and she said it in the voice of a person checking a thing she has been checking for about a fortnight. “*And I have not, and I am not going to, and I did not start, and I cannot now say no, and I have known that since a fortnight and there is nobody to put it to and there is no form and there is not going to be one.*”

“Who put you on this road.”

“Nobody. A stranger said the word tenth in this lane and a man with a cart stopped him, and I was at my gate, and I have been the tenth since that morning, and I have never once thought that a stranger with a bad leg knew my name and he does not, and neither of us is going to find out, and that is the arrangement and I did not agree to it and I am not going to.”

---

He had about an hour of it left after that and he spent it standing in a lane with a leg and did not do one single thing.

And there is nothing I can do about that and there never has been and that is not new. What is new is that it happened in a lane in front of four people because I said a word.

He had said the price of that out loud in five places in eleven days and had improved on it in none of them, and the price was always the same shape: a person of a place finds out about themselves, and nobody asked, and there is no instrument anywhere that takes a knowing back.

Except that this is not a person finding out about themselves. This is a person finding out about somebody else because of me, and there is a widow at the fourth house who will find out about it in about a year, and there is a man of thirty who has been standing in a lane for eleven days with a thing he has not been able to put down either, and there is me, and none of the three of them is going to be thanked and two of them cannot get out of it.

---

And at about the last of the light he made a decision, out loud, in the lane, to the man of thirty and to the man who reads things out and to nobody else, and he knew it was the wrong one four seconds before he said it and said it anyway and finished it, and this is the second time in twelve days and he is going to keep a count of that if he can get one out of a county.

“I am going to put a sentence into this county,” he said. “About a man of thirty-eight who is four days up a road with a bag, and a column on a shelf, and a levy that nobody has been chosen to hold, and the fact that the honest version of that levy costs ninety people something they cannot pay, and I am going to say it to a person of a place and let her say it to a person of another place, and I am not going to be in either room, and I am not going to be able to call it back.”

Nobody said anything. The man of thirty-four read him off his own board in about a second and a half and put it down.

“A sentence put into a lane is a delegation,” he said, “and you have not authorised it and you cannot call it back, and you have spent four counties finding out what happens to a person who finds out about themselves in front of somebody else, and you are about to do it to a woman at a bar who has been asked two questions in twenty-two years and this is the third.”

“I know what it is,” said the man of thirty-one. “I said it in a kitchen yesterday before I asked a man of sixty-three for something, and I have said the price out loud six times in twelve days and it has got smaller every time and never gone, and I am not going to do it because it is the right thing. I am going to do it because the man of thirty-eight is four days up a road and the ninety are on a list and I am on a mile of road with a leg and a fortnight, and if I do not do it this week it does not get done at all, and that is a reason and it is not a good one.”

Four bells and a stranger, and the stranger was a woman of about forty-one at the seventh house who had not been asked, and a barrow went rotten in a lane and a hip went in a house and nobody in that lane was relieved and nobody was thanked and the tenth person got up at the fifth hour again the next morning.
