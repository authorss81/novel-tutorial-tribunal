# Chapter 316: A Fire In The Market Town

There is no plank at the end of this street. There is no figure on a wall at the second light, and there is no woman of forty-one here, and nobody in this market town has ever heard of a board at Coldharrow or a lane that goes from village to village, and a man of thirty-one came eleven days up a lane to look at a set of scales and is not going to stand in a street on a market morning and talk about a wall in a county that is a week's walk from here.

So there is no figure here at all, and that is not an omission. The plank is thirty-one years old and there is one of them and she was on a ladder at the second light this morning at the bottom of a lane four days the other way, and a stroke does not travel, and there is no instrument in this country that will carry one off a wall and into a street four days away, and a man who wanted a figure this morning would have had to be a man standing at the foot of the ladder at the second light, and he was not.

It was his sixty-sixth morning, on the third morning of the sixty-seventh week, and the day it belonged to came to four bells and a fire and a debt called in, and a fire in a market four days up a lane is not a March weight and is not on that plank at all, and the debt is in a village he has not been in since the morning before last, and he is standing in the middle of the one of those two things and he will hear about the other one never.

---

The yard went up at about the fourth hour behind a row of carts, and it was out before the water came, and it took nine minutes.

There is no bell in this town that anybody rings for anything. That is not a thing anybody has ever had to say in a market and it is a thing a man from another county notices in four seconds, standing at a bridge, on a morning when a yard is burning nine streets off, because in the county he came from a fire is two strokes on a plank and the second one goes whether or not the fire is out, and here there is nothing on any wall anywhere in this market that says a yard went up at the fourth hour.

Nine people were standing at that bridge because it is a market morning and there is nowhere else in a market town to stand, and the whole market knew about the fire inside four minutes, and nobody had to tell anybody anything, and nobody wrote it down.

Four hundred loads went over that bridge in a hurry that day.

---

The man of seventy had not done the sums for nine years.

That is the arrangement and it is the whole of it and it is a piece of the town: he weighs them and his daughter knows what they are, and there is no paper in this market that says which of the two of them is the arrangement, and he is seventy and he has been at that plinth for about forty-four years and he has his hands behind his back and he does not look up when forty carts come up the street and stop.

A fire in a yard behind a row of carts at the fourth hour means that nine carts that were going to be loaded are not going to be loaded, and the ones that were loaded have to be weighed anyway, because a cart that is standing in a street at the fourth hour on a market morning is a cart somebody wants to have said what it weighs. Four hundred of them in a week in the season, and a fire takes a morning out of it and puts a morning's worth of nine carts through in about one hour, and that is a sum, and the man of seventy could not do it at seventy at one o'clock in the morning with his hands not steady.

He did it anyway.

That is the whole of what happened and nobody will ever know it happened. He went in at about the last of the light and put the book on the table and did the column himself with a pencil because there is no other pencil, and he is seventy and he is right nine times in ten, and on that night he was right about seven times in ten, and there was nobody in the room. His daughter is six months gone with a child and she was asleep at the second hour and he did not wake her, and there is nobody else in that town who can follow a column that runs down a page, and she has said so and everybody knows it and it has never had to be said.

Nobody was asked to be there. Nobody was paid to be there. There is no wage for a second reader at a set of beam scales in this country and there has never been one, and that is not a scandal, it is a market.

---

He was in a lane four streets off at about the eighth hour, sitting on a wall with a leg out in front of him, when the father of seventy came out into the street and stood nine feet off and said the thing he had been carrying for a week.

“A man came through in the spring with a cart,” he said. “About thirty-eight, from the villages behind you, four days down your lane. He had a book under his arm and he did not go over the bridge. He stood at the plinth for about half an hour and he asked me one question and it was not about the beam.”

“What was it about.”

“He asked me what my daughter is paid,” said the man of about seventy, “and I said nothing is paid anywhere, and he asked me how I knew that about a man I had never met, and I said that in nine counties I have been the only person who could do a particular thing and I have never once been the only one who was paid for it.”

He shifted his weight.

“That is the whole of it. Nine days. And then the rain came and he went, and I have not thought about it since until this morning, because a man came into a street at about the eighth hour and asked me what my daughter is paid and it is the same question and he did not know that I had been asked it in the spring by a stranger, and I am telling you that because *a thing is not a mystery in a market. It is a man on a bridge at the eighth hour who says the one sentence he has been holding, and if two men say the same sentence then it is not a mystery, it is a market, and I have been standing on this bridge for forty-four years and I have never had two of them at once until this year.*”

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was going to.

---

The woman of about fifty-eight was in a street at about the ninth hour with a basket and she did not come to the bridge and she did not speak to the man of thirty-one and he did not speak to her.

He had one thing to say about this place. He had carried it up a lane and down a lane and across a week, and it was the sixth of the six, and it was the only one of the six he had not yet said out loud in a room.

He did not say it.

That is the decision and it is the whole of that day, and it took him nine minutes on a wall in a lane four streets off a market, and eleven days of walking in him, and it is the only decision he has made since he came up this lane by not doing something.

*A woman of fifty-eight said no to him in nine words on a bridge eleven days ago and the no was right, and he has been in this county sixty-six days and has not found one word that makes the no less correct, and a man of seventy is going to find out tonight what it is like to be the only reader in a market at seventy at one o'clock in the morning, and the man of thirty-one is not going to be in that room, and the reason is not manners.*

He could see the join. He could see it perfectly well, standing in that lane with his back to a wall: it is where the arrangement stops being a father's trade and starts being a daughter's, and it has been there since she was forty-nine, and it is not a place and it is not a thing anybody can be shown, and on the far side of it there are four hundred loads a week and a season and a child coming in the spring.

He could not say what either side of it was for. He had said on a bridge in this town eleven days ago that he could tell nine people where their arrangements met, and he had been four streets off a burning yard since the fourth hour, and the one thing he had come back to do was to say it again to a woman who had said no to it.

The people of that town call the two of them on a market morning an anchor, and an anchor is not a thing, it is the plinth, and the day, and the two of them, and there is nothing to find.

---

The man of fifty-two was at that market three days running, counting what crosses, on his own account, and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him anything, and he said one sentence in a yard at about the tenth hour and it was about a gate and about nineteen years and it was true, and nobody asked him what else he was the only person in, and he was not going to say it anyway, and four hundred yards of yard went past in which nothing whatever was said.

The woman of thirty-four at a bar eleven miles down a lane read eight loads off her own board that afternoon and the day came to a figure she could not do anything with and told nobody.

Nobody was relieved. Nobody was thanked. A yard was out in nine minutes and nine carts went over a bridge in a hurry and a man of seventy did a column by himself at one o'clock in the morning and got seven tenths of it right, and a man of thirty-one sat on a wall four streets off and did not say the one thing he had come eleven days up a lane to say, and there is no form in this country that says a man may say a thing nobody asked him for.
