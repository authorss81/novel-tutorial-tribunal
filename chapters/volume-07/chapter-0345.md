# Chapter 345: Four Hundred Yards Of Hedge On A Morning That Was Not One Of The Nine

The wall with the day on it is nine miles up this lane at the far end of it, and at the second light a woman of forty-one was on a ladder against it, and he was not nine miles up this lane. He was at a north gate at the north end of four hundred yards of somebody else's hedge, with a leg and a spade he had not asked for, and there is no figure for this morning.

The day came to four bells and a boundary, which is eleven, and a boundary is a boundary in this county and there is one four hundred yards long at the end of this village, and it is not on that plank, and the market of a town four days up the lane is on nothing at all.

It was his ninety-fifth morning. The nine mornings of the walk ran from the first morning of the seventieth week to the ninth, and the ninth was the second morning of the seventy-first week, and it was done, and nine of eleven walked it in the last of the wet, and two of them did not, and that is all of it.

---

Nobody asked him to go out onto it. He went out onto it at about the seventh hour because he had a morning and a leg and nothing to do with either, and the man of about sixty-three was at the north end with a barrow and a crowbar and his hands black to the wrist.

That is eleven that is four bells and a boundary and it is the only eleven in this county that is not four bells and three strangers, and nobody in that lane is going to say so and nobody in that lane is going to notice it, and that is how a number works in a place.

They walked it. Four hundred yards from the north end, where the boundary and the edge of the lane's own ground come together, to the south end, where the boundary stops being a line and becomes the edge of a field that belongs to a man who is asleep. Two men, and a leg, and an hour and a bit, because two people stop.

A boundary is half a mile of a man's walking and it can be done by anybody in this county in an hour. He had done it in half an hour on the fifty-third morning, alone, and he had seen the join on it twice and both times it had told him nothing, and the truth was not the difficulty then and it was not the difficulty this morning.

---

The man of sixty-three talked for a mile of it and then stopped and then started again, and he was not performing anything and he did not know the stranger was there for anything but a leg.

“I have a thing,” he said, “and I have had it since you were in this kitchen four mornings ago and I have not said it and I am saying it because you are a stranger and strangers do not get talked out of it.”

He put the crowbar over his shoulder.

“*I want the person after me to be somebody I have met.*”

He walked four yards and then he said the rest of it, which is how he says everything.

“There is a round. The boy read it out. I am the first of it and after me is a woman of thirty in a field eleven miles down the lane and off it, and I have not seen that woman in my life and I know the length of her field because you have said it in my kitchen, and she is nine years younger than me and she dug the first hole of her own line when I was carrying a barrow for a man who is dead, and after her is a man with a cart who is a mile the other way and after him is a woman four days up this lane and after her is a man on a road, and after her is a woman of about seventy-four who is the only one of the six I have ever heard speak about any of it.”

He stopped at the corner where the hedge turns and looked at the field of the man who is asleep.

“*Thirty-one years of walking four hundred yards with nobody and I have never once had to think about who was coming, because there was never anybody coming, and now there is a round of it and I have found out that I do not want the last one to be a stranger. That is all. That is the whole of what I have been carrying since you stood at that table and it is not about the six and it is not about you.*”

Nobody asked him for it.

---

He did not answer it, and he wrote down in his own head in four seconds why he was not going to answer it, which is the ordinary method and the only one he has got.

The answer is no. There is no form in this country that says a man may introduce two people. There is no form that says a man may put a person in a place they have not chosen, and the one time he did that he went eleven miles with nothing to offer and used it, and a woman of about thirty-four at a bar said the word *go on*, and she is a person in a record now and there is nothing in four hundred miles that lifts a person back out of one.

A man of sixty-three wants to know the person after him and the only way he has of finding out is to be given one, and being given one would be a stranger making a person, and that is the instrument this country has and he has been holding it wrong-side-up for ninety-five mornings.

So he said the ordinary thing instead, out loud, in a hedge, at about the eighth hour, and it took four seconds and it was not a price and he did not say it as one.

“You have got eleven of them on that bill and I have never seen it read out,” he said. “And there is a man of about thirty-four in this county who reads things out for a living at three tables and who has never read that bill in your kitchen, and that is not a thing I can do anything about and that is the only thing I have got.*”

The man of sixty-three said that was true and that he had not thought of it, and then he said the second half of it, which was that a bill is a scrap of a flour mill's sack and the mill is four miles off and a mill stops.

---

The bill was on the kitchen table face up when they got back in, and nobody had turned it over and there is no reason on earth for a wind to have done it.

Eleven names, and four hundred yards, and nine days, and nine of them come.

And under the eleven, in a straighter hand than a man with a drain in his hand usually has, there is the twelfth name, and the man of sixty-three wrote it in a kitchen in the middle of the last month because a stranger with a bad leg offered to walk a hedge, and he does not know it is there, and nobody in that kitchen has ever been told and he is not going to be told and he was never going to be.

He looked at it for a second and a half and then looked at the rest of it.

He did not cross it out.

He had it ready. That is the honest shape of the morning: he had it ready in four seconds and the whole of it came to him in a hedge at about the eighth hour and it was not difficult. There is no form in this country that says a man may put a name at the foot of a thing, and there is a form in his own head that says if a name is a person and a person can be asked about for the rest of their life then a name that is not wanted is better gone, and the argument for crossing out a hand is that it is the one thing he can do this morning that makes the world smaller, and he has been in this county ninety-five mornings and has never once made anything smaller.

He did not do it, and the reason is one sentence and it is the only reason and it is not clever.

*If a hand is crossed out then it is gone, and a hand that has been crossed out is a thing nobody in that village will ever find out about, and the whole of what I have learned in this county is that a name on a piece of paper is a person and a person is somebody who can be asked about, and I have not been able to find one single person in ninety-five mornings who would stop me putting a name on six pieces of paper and one of them is that bill, and I am not going to start by making one.*

He said none of that out loud. There were four people in the kitchen and the ninth is the smith's wife and the tenth never comes, and the man of sixty-three had his back to him and was putting the crowbar against the wall.

---

They went back out at about the ninth hour and finished the four hundred yards at the south end, and the man of sixty-three said one thing at the south end that he had not said at the north end and that he did not say to anybody in particular.

“There is a foot of this walk with nobody on it,” he said, “*and it has had nobody on it since the spring and it is still walked every ninth day and I am the one who walks it and I am not going to be the one who says that out loud in my own kitchen, and I have just said it out loud to a hedge, which is the second time this month and I am going to stop.*”

Nobody asked him for the rest of it and the rest of it was about a woman of about thirty-four who came in the spring and said four words at that table and had not been seen since, and that is the fourth time that woman has been in that kitchen in a fortnight and she is not in this county's business and she has not been asked for anything and she is not going to be thanked.

---

The boundary was walked on a morning that was not one of the nine and nobody came out on purpose and no bill said so and the mill four miles off did not know. The day came to four bells and a boundary, which is eleven, and a stranger walked four hundred yards of hedge with a man of sixty-three and was not one of the eleven and was not on the paper and was not going to be thanked, and that man said out loud that he wants the person after him to be somebody he has met, and nobody asked him for it, and a twelfth name stayed on a scrap of a flour mill's sack, and the man who wrote it does not know it is there.
