# Chapter 335: A Book That Can Order Things And Cannot Date Them

There is no figure for this morning. The plank is at the far end of this lane, three miles down it, and a woman of forty-one was on a ladder at the second light putting a day on it, and the day she put on it is in a county the man of thirty-one was not in at the second light, and he was in a kitchen at the end of a lane in the third village with nine people in it and a cup of something he had not asked for.

It was the first morning of the seventieth week and his eighty-fifth morning, and the day came to four bells and a boundary, which is eleven, and a boundary is a boundary in this county and there is one four hundred yards long at the end of this village, and it is not on that plank that the market of any town four days up the lane is on anything.

---

Nine of them went out at about the seventh hour.

There are eleven names on the back of a flour bill in a kitchen and nine of them come, and the ninth is a man of about thirty with a scythe who has mended a churchyard hedge at the other end of the lane since he was twenty and has never been asked what that hedge is for, and the man of about thirty-four who reads things out for a living was in Coldharrow and not there, and one name on that bill is in a hand that is not any of theirs and the man who wrote it does not know he wrote it.

Four hundred yards of hedge from the north end, where the boundary and the edge of the lane's own ground come together, to the south end, where the boundary stops being a line and becomes the edge of a field that belongs to a man who is asleep. It takes about half an hour at a walk. It took about an hour and a bit with nine people on it, because nine people stop.

He did not go out onto it. He had said out loud on the previous afternoon, in that kitchen, that he was not going to be there for any of the nine mornings, and he was not going to be there for the first of the nine either, and a man with a bad leg standing at the north end of four hundred yards of somebody else's arrangement is a man who has been added to it, and the man of about sixty-three had not been asked.

And I could do it. Half an hour. I did it in half an hour on the fifty-third morning and I saw the join on it twice and both times it told me nothing. He wrote that in his own head in four seconds and then stopped, because the thing he had just written was the truth and the truth was not the difficulty.

---

They came back at about the ninth hour and the man of about sixty-three put his barrow down and said one thing about it, and he said it in the way he says everything, which is flatly, to the table.

“Nine days,” he said, “and I have walked it since I was thirty-two, and the first six of the nine I walked it with nobody, and yesterday a stranger asked me a question I have wanted to ask for thirty-one years, and I am not going to say the question back in front of nine people at my own table, and that is not modesty, that is the fourth person this month who has asked me a thing, and there is going to be one morning where nobody asks me anything.”

Nobody said anything.

He sat down. He put both hands flat on the table on the flour bill and left them there for the length of a cup going cold and the kitchen was the quietest it had been in fifty days.

---

And then the boy of seventeen came in from the yard with his book under his arm, which he does every morning and which nobody has ever asked him about, and he was nine feet into that kitchen before nine people knew he was in it, and he said the thing.

Nobody had asked him. There is no form in this country that says a man may ask a boy of seventeen a question about a book, and there has never been an ask in this kitchen in his life, and he had come in on his own account and he said it on his own account and it took him nine seconds.

“Put them in an order,” said Bram Orren, “and let each one of them say who comes next. *That is the only thing anybody has proposed about the six in a month and a man who reads things out said it in a yard and nobody agreed and nobody disagreed, and it is the only thing that works, and a book can order things and cannot date them, and I have never been able to make it do a date and I have stopped trying.*”

He put the book on the table with the strap still on. There are four bells at the top of every page and two pages lost in the ninth year, not copied and not shown, and he has run it since he was twelve.

“It is not a list of places,” he said. “*It is a round of turns. It goes round, and every one of them says who is after them, and if a person will not say then it stops at them and that is the answer, and there is no other answer in this country and there is not going to be one.*”

---

He read it out. He read it the way he reads things out, which is flatly, at the speed of a man reading a list of loads off a board, and nine people in a kitchen listened to a boy of seventeen say the order of six arrangements that nobody in that village had ever heard of except four of them.

“The man of about sixty-three says the woman of about thirty, and she says the man of about thirty with the cart, and he says the woman of about fifty-eight four days up the lane, and she says the man of about thirty-eight, and he says the woman of about seventy-four, and she says — ”

He stopped for four seconds and it was the only time he stopped.

“*— she says a person that nobody in eleven villages has counted.*”

Nobody said anything at all.

“She does not say who,” said Bram Orren. “*And I am not going to read a name out of it, because there is not one and because if I say one then somebody in this kitchen is going to go and look for it and find a person and that is worse than the round stopping.*”

---

And then, before anybody could get a word in, which is the way a boy of seventeen keeps a kitchen in order, he said the other two things.

“There is a question in my book,” he said, and he put his hand flat on it, “and there is a mark on the end of it and nobody has ever answered it and it is the oldest thing in here and I am not going to say what it is this morning.”

The man of about sixty-three opened his mouth and the boy put two fingers flat on the cover of the book, and the man of about sixty-three shut his mouth again and did not ask it, and that is the only time in five years that a question in that book has come within a foot of being asked, and it is still in there, and nobody knows what it is.

“There is a third place in it as well,” said Bram Orren. “I can see the third place and I have never been able to make out what is at it, and I have been at this for five years, and I am seventeen and I am not going to be asked about that either, and if anybody asks me about both of those things at once I am going to go out to the yard.”

Nobody asked him about either.

---

And then, at about the eleventh hour, with the kitchen back to being a kitchen, a woman of about fifty-one who cannot write and had shut a gate bar for four seconds one time because a hen was out said the thing that made it an instrument.

“Then it is a hearing,” she said. “*I have been in this kitchen nine years and I have not known what this county is for, and a thing that has to be held in six places at once is a hearing, and there is no paper in this county and there has never been a paper, and a book is the only thing in eleven villages that can put six things in an order, and it is the only thing in eleven villages that cannot say when, and that is the whole of what we have got and I am fifty-one and I cannot write and I have just said it.*”

Nobody praised her. The boy of seventeen did not look up.

---

He did not thank the boy. Nobody thanked the boy. The man of about sixty-three did not thank him and could not have, because the boy had not done it for him and had said so, and the boy did not want to be thanked and would not have said what to.

The man of thirty-one said one thing to him, at about the twelfth hour, at the end of the lane, and it was not a question and it was not about the six and it was not about the order.

“You read that out in a kitchen with nine people in it and nobody had asked you and you said the two things you are not going to be asked about before anybody could ask them, and you did that on your own account, and there is no form in this country that says a man may do that, and I have not got a way of saying it that is not a reward, so I am not going to.”

The boy shut the book and put it under his arm.

“All right,” he said, and went out to the yard, because the man of thirty-one had said it in the way a man says a thing that is finished, and a boy of seventeen can tell the difference and can be relied on to.

---

That eleven is the only one in this county that is not four bells and three strangers, and the hedge was four hundred yards and nine people walked it and a tenth name on a bill in a kitchen was not on it, and the round of turns goes round and stops at a person nobody in eleven villages has counted, and a question in a book with a mark on the end of it is still in there, and a third place is still in there, and nobody was relieved and nobody was thanked and the boundary was walked.
