# Chapter 318: A Mile Of Road, And The Sum Is Still Not Written Down

The plank at Coldharrow said sixty-three thousand nine hundred and forty-four at the second light on the fifth morning of the sixty-seventh week and he read it himself at the foot of the ladder, and the day it stood for was four bells and nothing at all, which is the number a road is usually run on, and he was standing in a road eleven miles down the lane and off it with a leg and a boy of seventeen's weather and nothing to do until the second hour.

It was his sixty-eighth morning.

---

The mile is a mile. It leaves the lane at a gate and goes down past nine houses and comes back into the lane at a gate the other side, and it has been kept up for ninety years by the people who live on it, each of them a length, and the lengths are not written down and are not equal and are settled every year in nine minutes of ill temper at a gate.

Nine householders. Each one mends his own length. Unpaid.

He came up the lane from the hamlet in the wet with four days of walking in him and one fact from a market town four days up the other way, which is that a man of fifty-two stood in a market street and watched four hundred loads go over a bridge in a hurry and said one true sentence about a gate and was asked nothing at all. He had been carrying that for three days and it had not told him anything, and he had come down this lane because it is the fifth of the six, and because a man of about thirty in it had said the true thing about it out loud in front of four people and had not been thanked for it.

The man of thirty was in his own hundred yards with a barrow, mending, in the wet, without a coat on, at about the seventh hour.

---

He said the thing about paying him out loud, because he had decided on the way down the lane that he would say it, and he had nothing to give and had known that for a week and had said it anyway.

“I have got nothing,” said the man of thirty-one.

The man of thirty did not stop working.

“No,” he said.

“There is a carter’s day in this county and it is eighteen pence and I know it is eighteen pence because a woman at the end of this lane has said it out loud in this road in front of four people and has never been thanked for it, and I am not going to do the sum, and you are not going to make me. What I am going to say is that I have come to find out whether paying you is the thing to do, and I have found out, and it is not, and I want you to tell me what happens instead, and I want the cost of it in particular and not the principle.”

The man of thirty put the barrow down. He took four seconds and then he gave it, and it was the best thing anybody said in that road all day, and it had been in his family for a long time.

“There was a man in a tavern in the ninth year of my father’s time,” he said. “At the end of this lane, and he was not a stranger, and he was a decent man and he is not a villain and it is not anybody’s fault. He said he would pay for the road. He paid for two years, out of his own pocket, at the bar, in front of anybody who cared to look.

“*We mowed for him for two years. All nine of us, and we did it well, and we were proud of the road for the first time in seventy years.* And in the third year he took a farm four miles off with a boy on it, and he was gone by the spring, and we had all been standing in a lane for a man for two years and not one of us had said so out loud, and nobody could look at anybody.

“It took four years to get back to what we had. Four years, and one of those years we did it at four men because two of them had gone off to a quarry, and nobody said anything to anybody, because the one thing you can say to a man who is paying for a road is that he does not have to, and he does not have to, and he knows he does not have to, and that is the end of it and there is no second half on it. *That is the cost and it is not a principle and I have lived inside it.*”

He picked the barrow up.

“You wanted the cost in particular,” he said. “That is the cost in particular and it happened before I was born and I have not got anything cleverer and you are not going to get it out of me by standing in my road.”

---

The man of fifty-two was in the road at about the ninth hour, going about his own business, on his own leg, and he had not been asked to be there and did not say so and did not offer, and he stood nine feet off for about an hour and did not say one word, and four people in that lane noticed that he was there and nobody asked him anything, and that is correct and it is the price of the road and he does not know he is paying it.

A carter went over at about the tenth hour and stopped because a cart had gone in to the second house, and he said, to nobody, that a cart is eighteen pence a day, and that he had said it before in that road and would say it again, and that he did not know what the road cost and was not going to work it out standing up in the wet.

The man of thirty said then, and he said it flatly and to the man of thirty-one and not to the carter:

“The woman at the bar has said nine a day in this road. If the two of them are ever in the same breath then somebody is doing a sum, and I have watched a stranger nearly do it twice this week and I am telling him now that if he does it in front of nine men it will be in nine heads by the last bell, and *a number in a hand is a thing a person can be entered against, and this road has been free for ninety years and the one thing it has never needed is for anybody to have written a figure on it.*”

Nobody did the sum. Two prices were named in two mouths in a wet lane and neither of them was multiplied and there is no paper anywhere in this county that says what a mile of road costs, because the whole of the arrangement is nine men and a barrow.

---

The join in the road is at the first gate.

Not at the far end, where the road comes back into the lane, and not in the middle where the ruts are worst. At the first house, a hundred yards or so in, where a man's own length starts and the first thing anybody in that lane does about the road gets done.

He could see it perfectly. It is where a favour becomes a duty, and it is the same join he has seen in a fund over a smithy and in a market street between a father and a daughter and on a rise behind a burying ground between a family and a hamlet, and it is the sixth time and it is the last one he gets for nothing. It does not tell him what to do. It is a place where two things meet and he can see the meeting and he cannot say what either side is for, and a man who can see that and cannot say it is going to be a man who stands in a lot of gates for the rest of his life.

He had one thought about it and it was a bad one and he had it before he knew it was a bad one, and it is worth putting down because it is what the whole of these ten days are made of.

*A thing that nobody has been asked about is a thing that stops when the person stops. And the only way to stop a thing stopping is to put a person in it on purpose, and the only way to put a person in it on purpose is to have a place where you write the name.*

That is the whole of the technique he has. He has said it in nine yards to four people in a county for seven weeks, and fifteen days ago a man of sixty-three wrote his name on the back of a flour bill in a kitchen without being asked, and the cost of that has not landed yet and he knows it will.

And there is nobody on that road to write anything, and there is no form for it, and there is no room and no officer, and a hundred and forty years ago a man of this family put a spade in a hole in a wet afternoon in a field eleven miles away and off the lane and put himself in a line of nine and consulted nobody, and that is the only instance of the instrument working in this county in living memory, and it worked by accident on a day when the ground was soft.

---

And the tenth person was already on the road and had been for eleven days and nobody had asked her.

He found it in the second house's length, and he found it because he had walked that hundred yards four days earlier and the ruts had been in a different place.

There were barrow marks in the wet going in at the second gate, and forty yards of the second house’s own length had been taken up and filled in, and the second house is a woman of about sixty-eight who is a widow and has a bad hip and has not mended her length in four years, and nobody in that lane had said one word to her about it, and there is not one of the nine who does her length, and the whole of the finding of this road is that there are nine men on it and there never have been nine men on it.

“There is a tenth,” said the man of thirty-one, because he could not help it, and it was the wrong thing to say and he knew it in four seconds and after he had said it.

The man of thirty looked at the ground for a while.

“A tenth is a count,” he said. “I know what you are doing and I know why and you have been doing it for seven weeks and I am not going to let you do it in my road. *The moment one of us is a number, the road has got a list, and a list is the thing that comes before a bill, and a bill is the thing that comes before a man saying that the other eight are working for him.*”

“I did not say anybody was working for anybody.”

“You said there is a tenth,” said the man of thirty, “and you said it in a lane, and four people heard you, and one of them is going to go and tell the second house’s neighbour, and by the last bell there is a tenth.”

He put the barrow down again.

“And then what. Then she has been asked, and she has not said yes, and she cannot say no without saying that she has been doing a widow’s length in the dark for eleven days, and the widow is sixty-eight and would have to hear about it. *That is what your number does to her. You have not counted her. You have put her in a place where being found out is worse than being short.*”

Nobody said anything. It was raining.

The tenth person is a woman of about forty-one at the seventh house who has a barrow of her own and a husband in the second field, and she did it at about the fifth hour on eleven days over eleven days, and she did not tell anybody, and she did not do it in front of anybody, and she was not asked, and nobody has thanked her, and she is not going to be thanked, and she cannot now say no.

And the road is still nine men. It has been nine men for ninety years and it is nine men this afternoon and the tenth is not on it and the barrow marks are in it and the rain will take them out by the end of the week, and nobody counted the woman, and he had tried, and the man of thirty had stopped him, and the price of it is not going to be paid by her.
