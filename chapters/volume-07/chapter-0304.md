# Chapter 304: Four Days Up The Lane

The figure the woman of forty-one wrote on the plank on the fifth morning of the sixty-fifth week, at the second light, was sixty-three thousand eight hundred and sixty-three, and he had it from a man of about thirty-eight who had been at Coldharrow that morning on other business, who had gone up the lane afterwards in a cart because he was going this way as well, and who had stood at the foot of the ladder at the second light, because he is a man who counts other people's money and a wall with a figure on it is a thing he cannot walk past.

He said the number out loud in a kitchen in Ashgill without looking at anything, in the manner of a man who has been adding things up in his head for eleven years, and then he said that it was that morning's and not yesterday's, that nobody had told him the second figure yet and that he would be back down the lane in a week to get it.

It was his fifty-fourth morning in this country and he was on the fourth day of a walk to a market four days up the lane, in a country further from the Seat than this one, and the day at the bottom end of that lane was four bells and a debt called in at the fourth house, and it was a day he was not in, and he heard about it before the noon bell from a boy of seventeen who had come down to tell him something else and had stayed to tell him that as well.

“A debt at the fourth house,” said Bram Orren. “Carried in at the bar at about the third hour and not paid, and the woman has shut the door and about nine people know and nobody is going to say anything about it to her, and the last bell will tell everybody.”

“You walked eleven miles to tell me a debt is not being paid.”

“I walked eleven miles because I am going that way,” said Bram Orren, “and I have been going that way for about nine days and I have not got to where I am going, and you are the only person anywhere I have ever met who has asked me a question about the order of a thing and I am not going to waste a good question on a debt.” He shifted the book under his arm. “And I will say the rest of it now and then I will not say it again this month. Everything I write down is in the order it happened. Eleven places. I have got eleven places I can read in order and one more I cannot, and I have been trying since the fourth day of the fifty-eighth week and I have not got it and I am not going to get it before the weather turns, and it does not matter, because you cannot ask anybody about the eleventh thing when you cannot say which of the first ten came before it.”

That is the whole of what the boy said and he said it standing up and then went to sit on a wall with his book on his knees and did not look at it for the rest of the morning.

---

The debt got up the lane faster than he did, which is a thing a lane does and which is the only real difference between a lane and a road anybody has ever found.

A man of about twenty came up the track at about the ninth hour with a scythe over his shoulder, going up to cut a length of hedge he has owed a man at the top since the spring and has not cut, and he stopped, and he said, in the voice a man uses when he has come four miles to deliver nine words and has already been asked for three of them:

“The fourth house at the bottom. Debt called in before the middle of the day and the door shut and the man not paid. That is all of it. I have brought it up the lane and I am going back, and there is nothing else in Coldharrow this morning.”

He said it to a yard with four people in it, and the four of them asked him four questions, and he answered none of them, and he went back down the lane he had come up. That is the whole of how anything travels in this country and there is not a form for it and nobody is paid for it and the man with the scythe is not going to be thanked.

Nobody in that yard said the word *debt* twice. About four of them had worked out in nine seconds that the man of thirty-one who went up this lane on the second morning is the reason anybody at the fourth house knows that a man of thirty-one exists at all, and about nine had not, and neither of those groups said anything.

He was not there. He was four days up a lane with a leg and had not asked, and there is no form in eleven villages that says a man may ask, and so a debt was called in at a bar and a door was shut and it was eleven miles back and it belonged to nobody and it was going to be all right, and the whole of what he learned from it was that a thing goes up a lane on a man's own legs faster than a man goes up a lane on his own.

---

The man of fifty-two came up the lane on the second day of the walk with a cart and a beast and a reason he did not explain, and he was going that way, and he did not offer, and he did not mention it, and the offer was in the air for about four hours across two days like weather.

What he did was put the beast between them and the load and then unhitch it, which is not an offer, and then eat, which is not an offer, and then say, on the third day, entirely about the beast:

“Her feet are bad in the wet and there is a stone on the third rise and I am going to walk her up the first two.”

That was the sentence. It was about the beast, and it was in the fourth hour of the third day, and it was the whole of what either of them said about it, and neither of them ever referred to it again, and a man of fifty-two has been in nine counties in nineteen years and has not met anybody else who does that.

He denies doing the other thing. He has denied it twice, in about nine words, unasked, and both times the denial was the proof, and nobody in either county has ever thanked him for either half of it.

---

He saw the join twice more that day and it did him nothing whatever.

It was at a gate, where the arrangement that keeps a village in a county and the arrangement that keeps a family in a village come up against each other and neither of them gives way and the gate stands there. And it was at a boundary mark on a stone by the side of a road, about a mile further on, where a man had cut two lines a long time ago and the mark is still doing the only job it has ever done, which is to be a place to stand.

Both times it was a place and not a bargain. He could see it perfectly well. He could not have told a woman of fifty-one who cannot write what either side was for, and she would not have wanted to hear it, and both of those facts are the same fact.

*He can see a join anywhere, and it means nothing, and a man who can see a join everywhere and understands nothing has no technique at all.* He wrote that down in his own head, on the side of a road, in the plainest words he could find, and it took him the whole of the fourth hour of that day to accept that it was the true description of the only thing he has.

---

The villages got shorter.

That is not a mystery and it is not a mood and it is not the ground rising, although the ground rose as well. It is arithmetic, and it is the same arithmetic that decides where a mill goes and where a lane goes, and it took four days of walking and about nine stops to work out and he worked it out by asking a person of a place, which is the only way it has ever been worked out in this country.

A village of about thirty people cannot carry nine arrangements. It cannot carry a bar and a lane and a rota and a fund and a boundary and a burial ground and a bell and a man who counts the loads and a woman who keeps the figures, because there are not enough hands. So what happens in a village of thirty is that the arrangements get fewer and bigger, and one person is in more than one of them, and a person in four arrangements is not a busy person. A person in four arrangements is a person who cannot stop any of them, because each of the four was a favour to somebody and every favour has somebody in it who expects it back.

A woman of about thirty-four stood at a gate with a bar across it that she cannot lift by herself, and she was trying to lift it by herself when he came up, and he did not offer and she did not want him to, and she got it up on the fourth try with a thing she had put under it for a lever and which was a stick.

“Thirty-one in this one,” she said, “and the same thirty-one have the bar and the lane and the fund and the burying, and I do the bar and the fund and I am the one they come to when somebody has to be told something, and I have said that to two men this year and both of them asked me what I wanted and there is no such thing in this village to want.”

She went on to her work with her shoulder still under the bar, and she had said nine sentences and had not stopped for any of them.

---

The market town was visible by the second hour of the fourth day.

It is a bigger thing than anything he has stood in in two months and it is not a mystery: a long street, a cross, a market cross with a stone step worn in the middle of it, a great deal of noise, and at the end of the street a weighbridge with a set of scales on it and a man standing beside it with his hands behind his back.

The man is about seventy. He did not look up when forty carts came up that street and stop at that bridge, and he did not look up when the man of thirty-one came up the street with a leg and a boy of seventeen and a man of fifty-two and no standing whatever.

“You have come a long way,” said Tarin Keel, unasked, at about four hundred yards out, and he did not stop walking while he said it. “Nineteen years, nine counties. And in every one of those nine counties there was one man who was the only person who could do a particular thing, and I could not tell you in three of the counties what the thing was, and I have never once been able to do it myself. *And in this county there are about four.*”

He waited for the question. It did not come, and he was not disappointed by that, and he carried on.

“I have never known which four. Not in one of the nine. Nineteen years of counting what crosses and not one single day when somebody told me which four. And nobody sent for me to find out and I did not go and find out, and that is going to be the whole of my winter, and you may as well have it now as at the end of it.”
