# Chapter 310: Eleven Names On A Board

“Round number,” said the woman of about thirty-four at the bar.

She said it before she had got the board out from under her arm, and she said it to nobody, and about four people in that lane had heard it, and the man of thirty-one was one of them.

“Sixtieth morning. That is a round number and there is nothing in this country that turns on a round number. *A round number is a thing people are pleased with and the day after it is an ordinary day and it does not mean a thing.*”

“It is a number of mornings and not a day,” said the man of thirty-one, and that was nine words and it was the whole of his answer and he did not improve on it, and about four people in that lane heard him refuse it and did not agree with him and were not going to argue either.

It was the fourth morning of the sixty-sixth week and his sixtieth morning in this country, and he had a bad leg and a boy of seventeen and a man of fifty-two who was not asked, and they went down a track of about two miles and over a bridge with one rail on it into a valley with about sixty people in it.

---

She gave him the figure in the lane, on the way, without being asked, in her own mouth, and she said where it had come from before she said the number, which is the way she does everything and is the reason anybody believes her.

“A woman of forty-one at the bottom of this lane writes it on a plank at the second light for anybody who comes up this way, and the man of about thirty-eight brings me the number about the second hour, and it is eleven miles and he has a cart and he has done it about four times. That is this morning’s. *Sixty-three thousand eight hundred and eighty-eight.*”

Nobody wrote it down. The boy of seventeen looked at it for about two seconds and then said, “That is a number and not a date,” and went on, which is a thing he says.

“You have it now and it is worth nothing to you,” she said. “You cannot do anything with a figure off a wall. *You have been in this country sixty days and you still cannot do one single thing with it and I have watched you not try, and that is the only thing I have got to give you this morning.*”

---

The valley has a lane about a quarter of a mile long and nine houses and a bridge and about sixty people, and the whole of it is on the ground the same way a village is on the ground anywhere: badly, and without anybody arranging it.

And there is a porch.

It is the porch of the first house, and it has a bench in it, and on the end wall of the porch, at about the height of a man's shoulder, there is a board.

It has eleven names on it.

They are not in a column. They are not dated. There is nothing at the head of it and nothing at the foot of it and no mark against any of the eleven and no rule and no wage and no office, and it is not on a door that anybody has to open to get at it, and it is a board, and it is old.

It has been there since before the mother of the woman of seventy-four was born. That is not a story. Everybody in that valley of about sixty people can tell you the porch is older than the oldest of them, and about half of them can tell you when the plaster above it was done, and none of them has ever seen anybody cross one of the eleven names out, and one of the eleven is a woman who went out of this valley in a year that nobody in that valley can name, and nobody is going to get one.

“You have come about that,” said a person of the valley, at about the third hour, in the lane by the porch, unprompted and in about nine words and without any particular feeling at all. “Everyone who comes up this track comes about that. *It is for the thing at the bottom of the valley.*”

He did not ask what the thing was.

That is the whole of the trap and it is the reason the day is what it is. The person of the valley had already told him the answer, which was that nobody says, and everybody has known it in about nine words for as long as that porch has been here, and if he had asked what the thing at the bottom of the valley was, he would have become the man who asked, in a valley of sixty people, in a porch, about a thing that has been not-asked for a hundred and forty years.

He said, “Thank you,” and meant it, and went and stood in front of the board.

---

The join was there and it was the only one he has ever got exactly right and it was no use whatever, and he has not got over it and will not.

He could see where the eleven names stopped being eleven and started being one.

It is not a bargain between two arrangements. There is no arrangement on either side of it. There is no paper, no day, no wage, no form, no officer, no name at the foot of anything, and nothing whatever on either side of the join that anybody could be entered against, and there is nothing there at all except a board with eleven names on it and a woman of seventy-four who is alive.

Forty years ago there were eleven of them.

He could see the whole of it in about a second and a half and it was perfectly clear, and a technique that can find a join has found the only thing in this country that cannot be joined, and he stood in a porch in a valley of about sixty people and had nothing whatever to do with it, and he did not have one technique for it and this country has no instrument for it and there is not going to be one.

---

He asked her the only question he was allowed to ask, and he asked it at about the fourth hour, and he asked it plainly, and he had to go down the lane about forty yards to do it because she was at the bottom end of it with a stick and a hood and was doing something to a gate that had come off its pin.

“What are you for.”

She did not stop. She got the gate up onto the pin with her foot and put the stick under her arm and stood there.

“What I am for,” she said. “Nobody has ever asked me that.”

And then she said it, and it was nine words, and it was the answer to a question nobody in that valley had thought of asking, because the board was in the porch and it had eleven names on it and it had been in the porch for a hundred and forty years, and the board had answered the question before anybody thought to put it.

“There is a thing at the bottom of this valley,” she said, “and there has to be a person who sees to it, and that is me, and it has no name and no wage and nobody has ever written it down, and it has been about forty years, and nobody has ever asked me.”

Nobody in that lane heard it. He was the only one, and he did not understand about a third of it, and the last part of it she said without any weight at all, which is the worst way anybody can say a thing.

---

He did not say the sentence he had carried since a field in a third village three miles up a lane on the fourth morning of the sixty-fifth week.

He had it. It was ready. *My father did it, and his father did it, and nobody ever said out loud what it was for, and if a person did not ask then nobody knew, and a thing nobody knows the reason for is a thing that stops when the last one stops.* It was nine words and it was the whole of what a man of sixty-three had given him, and it would have fitted that porch exactly, and it would have been true, and saying it in there would have been a man telling a woman of seventy-four what she already knows so that he could go away with his own sentence said in a room.

So he asked the ordinary questions instead, and he asked them in the order a person asks them, and the man of fifty-two and the boy of seventeen stood about nine feet off and said nothing at all.

“Who else knows.”

“Nobody,” she said.

“Who has ever known.”

“Nobody,” she said, and then, because he had asked it properly and because nobody had asked it properly in about forty years: “There was a man of about sixty about ten years ago who came through on a cart and asked me one question, which was whether the water at the bottom of the valley was the same as it was, and he did not get an answer and he went away and he did not come back and I have not thought about him since until this morning.”

“What happens on a day you do not come down.”

“Nothing happens,” she said.

She said it the way she has said everything else that morning, without weight.

“Nothing has happened for about two years. There was about nine days when my hip was bad, about two years back, and nobody came and nobody noticed and I could not have told you on the ninth day what had been missed, and there was nothing missed, and then the tenth day I came down and it went on. *That is the whole of it. It is a thing that is not a thing that stops when a person stops, because it is not a thing anybody can see stopping, and I have been the only one who could see it, and it has been about forty years, and nobody has ever asked.*”

And here is the whole of what ten days of walking up and down a lane came to, in the plainest words available, and it is a thing a woman of about seventy-four has just contradicted and is right to. The finding is a man of sixty-three’s and he did not have it out of anybody, and it is hers as much as his now and he cannot tell which of them it belongs to, and he has been carrying it about two miles and has not been able to put it down. She says it is not a thing that stops, because nothing in this valley can be seen stopping, and she has been the only one who would have known either way for about forty years, and she is not a person who is given to hearing a large claim because a stranger made it. He has not got the rest of it this morning and he is not going to get it in a porch, and it is not a mystery and it is not a scandal and nobody in that valley is doing anything wrong, and no instrument in this country can put a person back on a list and call it a restoration, and she is entitled to stop. He cannot fix it. He does not try. He has no technique for it and this country has no instrument for it, and there is not going to be one.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was relieved. Nobody is going to be thanked or relieved anywhere in this country and a person who tells you otherwise has not been to a porch.

The board has eleven names on it, and nobody crossed two of them out, and nobody is going to.

---

He said what he had to say and he said it standing up, in about nine seconds, in a lane, in front of a woman of seventy-four and a man of fifty-two and a boy of seventeen, and he said it out loud, and there was no form in any country that says a man may do it.

“I am going down this lane and I am going up that one and I am not going to be here for about nine days. And I am going to come back, and I am going to be one of the people who asks you again. That is all I have got. I have got nothing else and I have not got a way to do anything for you and I am not going to pretend I have.”

She looked at him for a while.

“Then come on the day I come down,” she said. “I go down at about the seventh hour and I am slow and I stop twice, and if you are standing at the bridge I will know you by the leg, and if you are not I will not know you at all and I will not stop.”

He did not write it down. There is no form in this country that says a man may write a thing down and there is no hand in a valley of sixty people that anybody could be asked about, and he would not have wanted one, and he did not have a book.

Nine days. And a day, and an hour, given by a person, to a stranger with a bad leg, in a lane, in the last of the light.

---

She went down the lane at about the seventh hour to do whatever it is she does on that day, at seventy-four, with a stick and a hood, and she stopped twice, and she did not stop a third time, and the pace of her was the pace of a woman of seventy-four and nothing anybody says about it is going to change that.

He was not with her. He did not follow her down that lane, and he stood in a porch with a board of eleven names on the wall of it and let her go, and the man of fifty-two did not follow her either and the boy of seventeen did not, and the last of the light went off the valley about two hundred yards below him.

And neither of them knew what the other was for.
