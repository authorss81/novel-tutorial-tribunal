# Chapter 289: The One Thing The Technique Cannot Do

A man of fifty-six with a barrow and a bundle of hurdle frames on his shoulder came into the yard at Coldharrow at about the second hour on the fourth day of the sixty-third week and would not sit down, and he had walked the whole length of the eleven-village lane in a day and a half with a man of fifty-two and had come back the whole way with the barrow empty, and he said four words and then stood by the cart and did not say any more of it. The number on the wall at Coldharrow that morning was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and seventy and the day was the ordinary one and there was nothing on the plank about a man with a barrow.

“There is no road at the end of it.”

---

The bar is at the end of the lane and the lane does not go north or south, it goes from village to village, and beyond the last village there is about two miles of hedge and a field and a boundary nobody has walked in about forty years, and then there is a road.

“The road is four miles off,” said the man of fifty-six. “I have known that since I was a boy. Your man here has never been past the field and I have never been past the field either, and we got to the hedge at about the fourth hour yesterday and the man with the lead stood in it for about a minute and a half and then we came back, and I am telling you the whole of what we found, and what we found is a hedge and a field and a boundary nobody has walked in about forty years, and after that a road, four miles off, going somewhere else entirely.

“A bar across the end of a lane. *Not a gate. There is no road behind it. There has never been a road behind it, and I have been in this lane sixty times a year for about twenty years and I have never once thought about it, and neither has the man with the cart, and neither has the woman with the board.*”

---

Ferrow Hask had a hand on the side of the ninth cart and had said nothing for about four minutes, and what he said was not what anybody in that yard expected, and it was one sentence and it is the whole of the thirty-ninth day.

“Nineteen years,” he said. “I have wanted somebody to ask me what a bar is for since I was nineteen, and nobody has asked me, and I have said so out loud in this yard twice, and there is a man at the end of a bad road nine miles off who was going to be the first one to ask me and he has not asked me either, and I have found out in a day and a half what a bar is for, and I am not going to say it in this yard, and I am not going to thank anybody for it, and I am going back to the ninth cart.”

---

They argued about it for about half an hour and it closed inside about a quarter of an hour and nobody won anything, and this is how.

The four of them were the carter who wanted a gate shut, the woman of about fifty who counts loads and has a word at the top of her board and has never been asked a second question in twenty-two years, the man of fifty-two, and the man of thirty-one. That is three of the four people in the middle of it, and the fourth is a man of about sixty-one with a chisel in a bag who had been asked nine years ago to put marks on two elms and had put a second set of marks on them on the second day of the sixty-first week for a stranger who wanted a day, and who was not in the yard and had not been asked.

A bar at the end of a lane is not a gate, and there is no road behind it, and therefore there is nothing at that bar for a gate to be the gate of. So the woman of about fifty has been counting loads at a thing that is not collecting anything for anybody, which she has said out loud once, and the carter has been wanting a thing to be shut that has nothing behind it, which he has said out loud twice, and the man of fifty-two has been doing arithmetic about a margin of a levy on a bar that is not a gate, which is all the whole of his trade and the whole of this county's civic arrangements and is about four hundred years old and does not connect to a road.

“All four of those are true at once and they are not in conflict and that is why it has taken me three days and a walk of two days to find out,” said the man of fifty-six. “*It is not a gate and it is not not a gate. It is a bar at the end of a lane, and a lane does not have a gate, and every one of you has been arguing with the other three for about a year and the thing you have all been arguing about is a piece of wood that a man put across a gap in a hedge.*”

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

“I am a hurdle-maker,” said the man of about thirty-one who owns the tower at Nettlebed, from the wall, where he had been standing with his frame. “I make gates. I make about four a year and I sell three. *That has been the whole of my trade for eleven years and I have never once been asked what a gate is for either, and it is a bar, and you can put one across anything.*”

---

And the man of fifty-two did not do the sum, and it is worth saying why, and the reason is the finding of the whole morning.

“There is a figure I could put on that board in about four seconds,” he said, “and I am not going to, and I want the reason said out loud, because the reason is the most interesting thing anybody has said in this yard in about a month and it came from a woman who counts loads.

“*Threepence a load. About nine loads on the days the loads come, and there are about a hundred and forty days in a year in this county whatever anybody says, and I have printed the working on that in this yard before and I am not printing it again. It is a sum. It is a sum I could do standing up.*

“And I am not doing it, and here is why, and it is not modesty. That woman has been at that bar for twenty-two years and she has been asked about the threepence twice and about nothing else ever, and everybody in this yard has just spent three days finding out why, and the reason is four letters and a stroke that her father wrote at the top of her board, and a heading is a category, and a category is the only thing a person can be asked about.

“If I do that sum out loud in this yard then that bar has got a figure on it in a person’s head, and a figure with a sum under it is a column, and a column is a thing a person can be put in, and *she is the last person in this county I would want to be put in a column and she does not even know that she is the last one, and I would be doing it for four seconds of satisfaction and she would have it for the rest of her life.*”

The woman of about fifty, who had heard none of it because she was on the far side of the bar, said afterwards that she had not been asked anything and that was correct and that she would like it noticed.

---

And at about the ninth hour the man of thirty-one said the thing, in the yard, in his own mouth, in about nine words, and he said the price of it first and then he said it, and about eleven people were in that yard and about four of them understood all of it and the rest heard a man of thirty-one talk.

“Four accounts, put side by side, in order, and not joined. That is all this is. *You can put them next to each other and you cannot make them agree, and a method that cannot make four villages agree is a method of evidence and is not a method of government, and if you could make them agree it would not be four villages any more, it would be one village with four names on a door.*”

He said it to the yard and not to anybody and about four people in it understood that he had just said out loud, in a yard, on a day of four, the thing he had come into this country to find out, and it is that the method is a method of evidence, and there is no instrument in eleven villages that turns evidence into government, and there is not going to be one, and a man who says that out loud is not a man with a plan.

“I have been in this country thirty-nine days,” he said, “and I have put four things on a piece of paper out of the back of a bill, and I have put a boundary next to a list and a bar next to a list and a lane next to a bar, and every single time the two people in the middle of them found out about each other and neither of them had been asked, and I announced the price every time in a yard beforehand, like a man selling a hat, and the price is paid anyway.

“I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to stop and I do not want anybody to write it down. *The four of them do not fit and they are not going to fit, and the not-fitting is not a failure of the method, it is the method, and a man who tells you four villages are going to agree is selling you something and it is not evidence.*”

---

*And there is a thing in the room where he keeps the things he is not allowed to want, and it has not said anything for about nineteen days, and he has been telling himself for nineteen days that a thing which says nothing is a thing that has agreed with him, and that is the sentence he cannot finish, and he has now been not finishing it for nineteen days and he has got quite good at it.*

---

And then he counted what four days had produced, out loud, in about nine seconds, in a yard, because it is what a yard is for and because nobody else in eleven villages was going to.

“Four things. A bar that is not a gate, and it is closed as of about an hour ago and it closed by itself and none of us won it.

“A lane that goes from village to village, and it is in a book on a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off in four words that a man said in his own doorway, and it is in three instruments that do not know about each other, and it is not in a map, and nobody has said who wants it and nobody has said who laid it.

“Ninety acres and eleven families, and eleven families know about them as of the last day of the sixty-second week, when a woman of about sixty told them in a yard in four minutes, and they have nothing to be right about, and that is exactly what everybody said would happen and it happened in four minutes.

“And a hundred and forty-one names on a door, a figure set against every one of them, and not one word over the top of that column, and nobody has added it up, and the man of fifty-two has refused to in front of about twenty people twice, and what the figures count is not known to me and is not known to a woman of fifty-one or to a recordwright of twenty-eight or to a clerk of forty-four four hundred miles off who has handled about four thousand documents in twenty-two years, and I am not going to find out this month.

“*One of those four is in a book. One of them is in a ledger at a press and it is not going in. One of them is in a woman's head and she is about sixty. And one of them is a bar at the end of a lane that a man of fifty-six with a barrow has told us about after a day and a half of walking, and I have been in this country thirty-nine days and that is the whole of what I have done and it is not nothing and it is not a settlement and I am not going to stand in a yard and pretend it is either.*”

---

And at about the fourth hour a man of thirty-four came into that yard from up the lane on foot with two men behind him, and there were about twenty people in the yard and not nine, because it was the last of the day and about two hundred and ten people had nowhere else to be, and the man of thirty-four had never stood in front of more than nine in his life and about four people in the yard understood that he had come back up the lane a second time to say one sentence in front of more than nine, and the recordwright of twenty-eight said so out loud in about nine words to nobody in particular, which is a thing she does.
