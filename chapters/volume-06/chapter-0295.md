# Chapter 295: The Arithmetic Of A Bell, In A Room, Out Loud

Sixty-three thousand eight hundred and seven was on the tower wall at Coldharrow at the second light, put up by a woman of forty-one who had a debt called in at the bar in front of her and did not put the debt on it twice, and a day of six is four of the clock and two of a debt called in and there is no column on that plank and there never was one, and the forty-fifth morning of a man of thirty-one in that country was his forty-fifth and he was four hundred miles off and a man of fifty-two who had cut nine sheets out of the back of a bill was not in that county to see it.

The three of them came back down the lane on the fourth day of the sixty-fourth week and they were gone four days, and a woman of forty-one at the end of a lane had been in her own tower at the second light that morning and had wiped a cloth over a figure and had not known that anybody was anywhere near her.

---

The day after a thing is signed is not a day of anything.

That is worth a paragraph because fifty days in a county where a day is a weighted number and a weighted number cannot be turned back into what was done has produced one flat finding, and the finding on the second day after is that a day after is not heavy at all.

The corridor stood. The woman of about fifty-six had gone out of the valley on the second morning with her two men and the flat board and the copy, and had said one thing at the gate on her way out, and it was that the office that deals with the water is four hundred miles away and it is not her.

Nobody in the valley was relieved. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was thanked. The bargain was standing and it was going to go on standing, and there is no seam in it and nothing written on the back of it and nothing anybody could appeal to, because the only instrument anybody has ever had for telling an honest document from an abusive one is a name at the foot, and there was exactly one name at the foot of this one, and that name belonged to a man of forty who was in the valley and would be in the valley tomorrow.

---

Corvin Hale did the arithmetic again at about the fourth hour of the third day, out loud, in his own yard, to about nine people, and about eighty other people heard it second-hand by the last of the light, and the whole of it took him about four minutes and he did it with the working and he stopped when it was done.

He was not asked to. Nobody asked him anything. That is the first fact and it is the fact the whole of the second half of that day is built on.

“Nobody has asked me what I signed and nobody is going to,” he said, “and I have noticed that, and I have been the one who is asked for in this valley for about nine years, and the reason nobody has asked me is that I have been the one who decided, and when a person decides, everybody in about a hundred and forty people looks at the shape of their face and finds a reason in it.

“So I am going to say the working out loud in my own yard and I am going to stop afterwards and I am not going to answer a question about how I feel, because I have not got that either.”

He put a flat board on the top of a post, which is what a man does in a yard when he has got nothing to write with.

“A bell is thirty-one pounds of metal at four shillings a pound. Thirty-one times four shillings is a hundred and twenty-four shillings, and a hundred and twenty-four shillings is one thousand four hundred and forty-eight pence. The frame and the rope is eleven shillings, and eleven shillings is a hundred and thirty-two pence. One thousand four hundred and forty-eight and a hundred and thirty-two is one thousand six hundred and twenty pence.

“A keeper is ninepence a day. A hundred and forty days a year, because a cart does not go out in a winter and nobody has ever counted the days it does not, and that number is wrong and everybody who uses it knows it is wrong and we are not going to correct it tonight. Nine times a hundred and forty is one thousand two hundred and sixty pence.

“One thousand six hundred and twenty less one thousand two hundred and sixty is three hundred and sixty pence. Three hundred and sixty pence is a pound and ten shillings. And that is a year. And it is a year every year for as long as somebody rings it and somebody is alive to be paid for ringing it.

“*That is what I have signed for.* Not a bell. That.”

He took his hand off the board and stopped, and about nine people in that yard were quiet for about four seconds and the other eighty were not in it.

---

And then a recordwright of twenty-eight said the thing that is the finding of the forty-fifth day, and she said it in a yard in a valley four hundred miles off, in front of about nine people, before anybody had asked her anything.

“There is exactly one name at the foot of that sheet,” said Sera Quill. “I have read it. It is in a hand that is not a clerk's hand and it is not good and it took him a while and nobody helped him and I watched all of it.

“I have been in two counties in seven weeks and I have read nine things in this valley that were kept by ordinary people, and I am going to put them beside that one sheet, out loud, because I have got about nine minutes and about nine people, and because I have found out something and it is about me.

“The plank in the tower in a village nine miles off a lane in my own county is four hundred years old by the hand and it has a hundred and forty-one names on it and it has no signature and no seal and no witness line and nothing whatever at the foot of it. The wall in a village nine miles up the same lane has two hundred and nine names on it and nobody struck out and nothing at the foot of it. The register in a press eleven miles off is four sheets cut out of the back of a bill and nobody's name is on it and there is a man of fifty-two who will not put his own name at the foot of anything and never has.

“*And this sheet has one name on it. One. And the one name is the only part of it anybody can be asked about.*”

She put her book down on the flat of the post, which is a thing she has never done in a yard.

“That is not a reason to undo it and I am not going to stand here and make one. It is the finding, and it is a finding about the direction of the paperwork, and I have been a recordwright for six years and I have never had it before and here it is.

“The honest documents went the other way. Every one of them is a list of people and nobody signed it, and there is not one signature in four hundred years in any of them, and that is *why* nobody has ever been able to say who decided, and the reason nobody has been able to say is that about four hundred people each said a thing in their own yard and every one of them could be asked about their own yard and about nothing else.

“This one has a name at the foot, so there is one person in four hundred miles who can be asked, and he is standing in this yard with a bit of nothing in his hands, and I have spoken to him for two days, and he is not a villain and he is not a good man and he is a man of forty who did arithmetic out loud and then signed his own name to it.

“*An abusive bargain and an honest one are the same document with a different person at the bottom of it, and I have been waiting six years for an instrument that could tell them apart and the instrument is a name, and there is a name on this one and there was no name on any of the other ones, and that is the whole of the difference and it is not a fair difference and I am not going to be able to think about it again for about a week.*”

---

And Corvin Hale said the thing everybody in that room was waiting for him to say and he did not say it, and said the other thing instead, and it took about two minutes and it was in his own mouth and he was not asked.

“You are all waiting for me to say that I have been made a fool of,” he said. “And I am not going to, because it would be the easiest thing for me in the world and it would be about me, and I have had three days to get used to the idea of a thing being about me and I do not want it.

“I am not going to be told I did a wicked thing and I am not going to be told I did a good one, and I have got four reasons and I am going to give you the first three and I am keeping the fourth.

“The first is a pound and ten a year and I have said the working out loud in this yard twice and it is correct both times.

“The second is nine children under about nine years old in a valley with one road out of it that a man can take, and I have been over that count twice since the start of the week, and a count is the only thing in this valley that nobody will argue with, and that is not a coward's reason, it is the only reason there is. If you have got a hundred and forty people and nine of them are children and something is coming that nobody will describe, then the fastest thing anybody has ever put in front of me is a sheet of paper, and I have not got a better one and I have looked, and a man of fifty-two looked for nine minutes and got a pound and ten a year.

“The third is the channel cut a foot wide that runs four hundred miles and that about a hundred people drink out of, and that is the only reason in the whole of this that I am not standing in that room saying I regret it. *The water is not the corridor and the water is mine, and I have got that, and I got it out of the one sentence a man of thirty-one said in about four minutes after I had signed, which is that the two of them were four things and not one.*”

He looked at the man of thirty-one.

“The fourth one is not for you,” he said. “The fourth one is that in the nine years I have been the one who is asked for in this valley nobody has ever once asked me what I am for, and a man of thirty-one said that out loud in my own yard nine days ago, in about nine words, before he said one single other thing. *And I did not know that was what was wrong with me until a stranger with a bad leg stood in my yard and said it.* And that is the whole of what is behind my signature and it is about him and it is not about the valley and I am not going to be thanked for it because I have not earned it and he has not given it.”

---

And on the fourth day of the sixty-fourth week, back in the Briar March, a woman of about fifty called a debt in at the bar at Coldharrow, and nobody paid it, and the last bell went and everybody in two hundred and ten people knew before the noon bell that there was a debt at the ninth house and knew by the last bell that it had not been paid.

And she put the six on the plank and went up the lane with the board under her arm.

And about nine yards up the lane a man of thirty-eight was standing at the end of the ninth cart, and he had a hand on the side of it, and he said one thing to nobody, and it is the only sentence anybody in the Briar March said in that week about anything four hundred miles off, and it is not about a corridor and it is not about a bell.

“She has called in the same load account nine times in nineteen years,” said Ferrow Hask. “That is the ninth time. And I have called it in nine times and I have never once been paid, and I go on calling it in, and I have never once worked out why I do, and I have had four days to work it out and I have not worked it out and I am going to go back to the ninth cart.”

---

And at about the last of the light in a valley four hundred miles off, a woman of about thirty-one with a child on her hip said a question in a yard to about nine people, and it was not a question about a corridor and it was not a question about a bell and it was the question everybody in that valley had been carrying for three days without anybody saying it out loud.

“Who is going to be responsible for it,” she said. “Not who signed it. Who is going to be responsible for it. The water, the valley, the children, the people that are still here in three years if there is anybody in three years — who is going to be responsible for that.”

And somebody said *the water*.

And somebody else said *a bell*.

And a man of about sixty who owns a house in that valley and has owned it since before he could lift anything said *the road*, and there is no road within nine miles and there has not been one in his lifetime.

And a recordwright of twenty-eight said nothing at all, because all three of those are words, and there is not one instrument in eleven villages or in any valley four hundred miles off that has ever turned a word into a person, and she has been a recordwright for six years and she knows exactly what a name at the foot of a thing is for.
