# Chapter 293: What The Leader Is Going To Ask For

Nine strangers came into the county on the first day of the sixty-fourth week, three at each of three gates, and the woman of about fifty counted all nine of them off the only board in eleven villages that carries a word at the top, in about four minutes and had them on the plank before the second hour, and a figure of thirteen at the head of the wall that morning was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and eighty-six.

The woman of forty-one was in the yard and had seen all nine of them go past her gate and had stood at her own door and counted them herself, and about four people in that yard worked out that there were two people in eleven villages who had counted the same nine strangers on the same morning about a mile apart, and neither of them knew it, and the woman of forty-one did not find out for about two days.

---

They were not the three men from four hundred miles off. Those had gone down the lane and out of the county on the fifth day of the sixty-third week and had not come back, and a man of fifty-two said that out loud in a yard in Draymoor two days later and had a good reason for it, which was that a man who says a sentence in a yard three times in one county and then goes home for a while is not a man anybody needs to keep an eye on, and the fact that this county had nobody to keep an eye on anybody with was not a thing he was going to be thanked for and he was not going to be thanked.

These nine were in coats that had been bought for something else, and four of them carried a flat board between two of them, and they came up the lane in three lots of three and stopped at a bar that is not shut at each of three places and stood there for about four minutes each, and then went on up the bad road at the Stennick end and did not come back that way.

Nobody stopped them. Nobody in two hundred and ten people had ever stopped anybody at a bar that is not shut, because a bar at the end of a lane is not a gate, and about four people in that county had worked out in the last two weeks why.

“They are not counting,” said the woman of about fifty, at the bar, to nobody. “That is what I have been watching for four minutes. They have got a board between two of them and they have not once looked at my count and they have not once written on it, and the woman of about fifty who has been at this bar for twenty-two years can tell the difference between a man counting and a man looking. They are looking. Somebody has told them this bar is the only one in the county with a word at the top of it.”

She put the board under her arm.

“I would like it noticed that I have told two people that in about four minutes and I have not been asked it once, and I have not said it to them, and I have said it to a yard, and about four people in it had known it already and about five had not and one of the five was a man of thirty-one who had known it since the sixth day of that week.”

---

Two days in Draymoor is a long time when you have a thing to say in a room and about nine people who might want to say something first, and what happened in those two days was mostly nothing, and the nothing is worth about four minutes of that day.

Corvin Hale sat down with four people in a house that is not his wife's and is not his and belongs to a man of about sixty, and he wrote his sentence out in a rough way, and he read it out four times, and about nine people in that valley said things about it and about four of them were right, and he took about two hours over nine sentences and got it down to one, and then he would not let anybody else in the valley near it.

The one sentence is this, and he gave it to them in about four seconds and then went and did his own work, because he is a man of forty who has a valley.

“Does a corridor have anything to say about the water, and if it has, who does the water belong to.”

He said that he had had it in a longer form and had thrown the longer form away, and that the reason was that a longer form sounded like a claim and a claim is a thing somebody can be entered against, and this was a question and a question is the only kind of sentence he trusts himself with.

“You may write that down,” he said, when a woman of about thirty offered to, “and I will not be thanked, and if I am thanked in that room tomorrow I will get up and walk out, and I have said that out loud in this house so that nobody can say afterwards that it was arranged.”

Nobody in that house thought it was arranged. About four of the people in it had heard a better sentence than his in their own lives and two of them had said so, out loud, and one of them had said the whole of it was not worth the walk and would not have come.

---

The recordwright of twenty-eight said the thing that nobody in a room four hundred miles off has ever asked for, and she said it on the second evening in a kitchen in Draymoor in front of about nine people, before anybody had asked her anything, and it took about four minutes and it is the whole of the second day.

“A corridor is a document,” said Sera Quill. “I want that said first and I want it said plainly and I do not care in this valley what anybody thinks of me for saying it.

“*A document is a thing that can be produced. In a place, on a day, to a person.* That is the whole of the trade and I have kept other people's records for six years and that is the one sentence out of all of them that has never once been wrong. And a corridor is a document with a handle built into it that you cannot see from the other side, and it is going to be produced in a room, and every one of you is going to hear it read out, and about four of you are going to understand all of it and about nine are not going to be able to tell afterwards what was decided.

“And I am going to say the part that is worse, and I have thought about whether to say it and I am going to, because the reason I am going to is that I have been in two counties in six weeks and the whole of what I have learned is that a thing gets in by being said out loud in front of people, and I have said so out loud about nine times and I have not been thanked for it once.

“*The only reason a corridor cannot be produced here is that nobody in this valley can produce a day.* That is the whole of the defence. It is not a defence. It is the reason, and the reason is the same in both counties, and the same reason is that a bell does not keep years and a place that is not on a road has never had anybody agree about when anything happened in it.

“I have got a county at home where a woman of forty-one has kept a count on a wall for twenty-five years, and the count is real and it is checkable and it is the only number in eleven villages anybody can check anything against, and if you gave it to a clerk in a building with a yard in it he could not enter it, because there is no place on the form that says a figure of rings goes in that box. *I have tried. I have been in a stair in a tower four miles from her gate about nine times in six weeks and I have said so out loud in a yard and about four people in every yard have understood all of it and not one of them has been able to do one single thing about it.*”

---

And then she said the second half, and it is the half that belongs in that day, and she said it to about nine people in a kitchen and about four of them had spent six weeks learning it and about five had not.

“There is a thing in my county about ninety acres,” she said. “About eleven families have been grazing it for nine years and they pay nothing for it and they were never told about it until about three weeks ago, and there is a man in a room four hundred miles off whose whole trade is producing documents, and he has said that a thing he heard about a cannot-be-proved has come all the way up that lane on a barrow in a bundle.

“I want that said out loud because it is the same failure in two coats and there is not one word of difference between them, and one of the two is nine hundred miles from this valley and the other one is in the next county. *A claim about a place and a place nobody can prove are the same thing. A man says a place is a place, and a man says a place is not a place, and both of those are a figure with nothing over the top of it, and neither of them can be entered anywhere, and the reason they cannot be entered is that nobody knows when either of them was said.*”

Nobody in that kitchen had ever heard the ninety acres explained and about four of them had spent six weeks learning why it mattered, and the man of fifty-two said out loud, in about nine words, that a claim about a place was a thing you could produce in a room and a place nobody could prove was a thing you could not, and that he had been wrong for three weeks and had now got it right in a kitchen four hundred miles from his own yard, and that he was not going to enjoy it.

And Tarin Keel said the thing that made the day, and he said it flatly, and it is the same working he printed in a yard at Trope a fortnight ago with three answers, and the reason he could say it four hundred miles off is that he had it in his head and not on a cart.

“*And the day in this valley is not on a wall either. Nothing is on a wall. There is no board in this valley and there is no figure and there is no heading, and the tower has a bell in it and the bell has not been rung for about nine years because the money for the person who rang it stopped, and the money stopped in a year nobody here can name.* So I will say the thing that nobody in a room four hundred miles off has ever asked and it is nine words and it is about money, and then I will shut up about it for the rest of my life.

“A date is a pound three shillings and sixpence. Two days' carrying at a shilling and sixpence is thirty-six pence, two nights at threepence is six, a clerk's fee of a pound is two hundred and forty; thirty-six and six and two hundred and forty is two hundred and eighty-two, and two hundred and eighty-two pence is a pound and three shillings and sixpence. *And it is fifteen days of the man who walks the road and one shilling, and I have given that figure to a yard in my own county more than once, and four people there get the whole of it and five do not.*”

He put his hand flat on the kitchen table.

“*And a copy is threepence.* That is the whole price of a copy of the arrears, and the account is four hundred miles off, and I have not got threepence, and neither has anybody in this valley, and nobody in this valley is going to be asked for it, and I have said that out loud in a kitchen instead of in a yard because a yard is the only place this country prints working and I am not in my own county.”

---

He said the price out loud the next morning, on his own account, in a yard, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, of whom about four were from Draymoor and about five were not and had come down the lane with them and had no business there at all.

“I am going to be in a room tomorrow where about a hundred and forty people are going to hear a piece of paper read out and I am going to be one of about four people in it who understand the whole of what the paper is,” he said. “And I am not going to say one single thing to the man whose valley it is, and here is what that costs, and I am saying it out loud before I do it, because that is the only honest act left and I have got very little else.

“The price is that he and I are going to find out about each other inside about four minutes. Not about a paper. About each other. *I am a man of fifty-two with a lead in my pocket and I have been in nine counties in nineteen years and I have spent six weeks finding out that a method that puts two things next to each other has no instrument anywhere in the world that lets a person keep a thing to himself, and I have gone looking for one twice and there is not one.* He is going to learn what I am for and I am going to learn what he is for and neither of us is going to have been asked, and I have now paid that four times in six weeks with four different pairs of people and I am going to pay it a fifth time on purpose, and I would like it said in a yard that I said it first.”

Nobody in that yard thanked him and about four people watched him decide to do it, and one of the four was a recordwright of twenty-eight who has not looked at him in three days except twice and both times about a bell.

---

And at about the last of the light on the first day of the sixty-fourth week a man of fifty-two put his hand on the kitchen table again, and said the sentence, and it is the fourth time it has been said in a yard in this county and the first time it has been said in a yard anywhere else, and nobody had sent for him and he is four hundred miles off and he is not coming back up this lane.

And the sentence is nine words and it has been in about nine heads in this county since the sixth day of the sixty-first week and about forty more in a county four hundred miles off.

“If it cannot be produced then it has not happened.”

He said it to about nine people in a yard in Draymoor, and every one of the nine was a person of this valley, and not one of them had ever set foot in the Briar March, and not one of them had ever heard the sentence before, and about four of them understood what it was and the rest heard a man of fifty-two talk.
