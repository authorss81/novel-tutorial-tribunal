# Chapter 276: The Panel

Nothing comes up this lane on an ordinary morning, and the fifth day of the sixty-first week was a market, and that made it the one day in seven when the bar has a use, and the board at Coldharrow read sixty-three thousand six hundred and seventy-six at the second light, and it went up eight before the noon bell, and four of the eight were the clock and four of them were the market, and every soul in two hundred and ten people knew what the other four were for without being told, which is the only thing in this county that has ever worked.

Nine loads came through the bar between the second light and the noon bell and the woman of about fifty had her board out in the lane and called the count off it as each one passed, and it is threepence a load and it has been threepence a load for as long as anybody has been alive, and there is a word at the top of that board, four letters and a stroke, and it is the only heading in eleven villages and it is the reason nobody in two hundred and ten people has ever been able to ask her a question about anything except the threepence.

“Twenty-seven,” she said, at the noon bell, to the yard, to nobody, to a man of thirty-one who had been standing at the bar for about an hour and had not offered to help with anything. “Twenty-seven loads and one man asked me for a day and I have not got a day and I told him so and he said that was all right and went off up the lane, and that is the fourth time that has happened to me this month, and it happens about forty times a month, and I have been at this bar twenty-two years and I have said no to a date about nine hundred times and I have never once said the word no to anything else.”

She put the board under her arm.

“That is what the heading on it buys me,” she said. “Twenty-seven. *Nobody has ever asked me a second question.*”

---

And in the middle of that yard, at about the fourth hour, with the market going on round the edge of it and about nine people in it, two lines stood in front of him.

He read them twice. They said one thing and they said it in about forty words and they did not say what anybody should do about it, and they were the shape of a thing and not a thing, and it had now happened about twice in two years and it had never once taken anything out of him and had never once put anything in.

**A COLUMN WITH NOTHING OVER IT AND A FIGURE IN IT AGAINST EACH OF ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE NAMES IS THE ONLY RECORD IN THIS COUNTY OF WHAT ANY PERSON PAID, AND NO PERSON ALIVE CAN SAY WHAT THE FIGURES COUNT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A THING KEPT FOUR HUNDRED YEARS IN A VILLAGE THAT HAS NO BELL MAY BE READ BY ANYBODY, AND BY WHOM.**

Nothing came of it. He had long ago stopped expecting anything to come of it, and there is no way this side of about a year of knowing how to talk to people that a thing like that can be made to do a job, and about four people in that yard noticed that he had stopped writing on the cart's side and looked up, and he put the lead down and told them it was a figure that did not add up and that he would come back to it, and the woman of about fifty at the bar said that was the third time in ten days he had stopped writing on a cart and had never once come back to any of them, and he agreed with her, and she went back up the lane.

And the ledger of a market day is this: it told him a thing he already had from a woman of fifty-one at a tower two days before, in better words, out of a mouth, for nothing.

---

The recordwright of twenty-eight was in the yard, and she had been in the tower at Nettlebed twice since the second day of that week, and she had not said anything about it to anybody, and on the fifth day of the sixty-first week she said it in a yard in front of about nine people before she was asked, in about two hundred words, and it is worth putting down because it is the only time in this country that anybody has said it.

“I have read a document that no bench in this country has ever seen,” said Sera Quill. “I want that said first and plainly and I want it said in a yard and not in a room, because there is no room and everybody here knows there is no room and the whole of what I have been for six years is that a room is where a document becomes real, and I have just come back from a stair of eleven worn steps with a plank on the inside of a door, and I read it, and I cannot tell anybody in a room what it is for, and there is no room in this county for me to tell in, and that has never once stopped me before and I find that I do not know what to do about it.

“This is the fourth time in my life I have read something I could not account for. Two of them were in rooms with a bench over them and one of them was a page with a crease in it that I have never been able to get hold of in eleven years, and in all three of those cases there was a person in the room who wanted something from the document and told me so inside about a minute. *And there was nobody in that stair but five people and a woman of fifty-one and a man of forty-four, and not one of them wanted anything from it, and that is the first time in six years that a document has been read out to people who wanted nothing from it.*”

She put her satchel down on the cart's side, which is a thing she has never done, and it is the first time in twenty-six days that she has put anything down in a yard without being asked to.

“It is four hundred years old,” she said. “It is not a charter and there is no seal and there is no name at the foot of it and there are a hundred and forty-one names in the middle of it and a figure against each one and nothing whatever over the column. And I have read the four words at the top of it about nine times in three days and I have had it two ways, and I told a man of fifty-two on the second day of that week that the two ways were both right, and I meant it, and I have not slept properly about it since, and *I have wanted it. That is the whole of what is different and it is not a small difference and I am a recordwright and I have spent six years believing that wanting a document is a thing you get over.*”

“Two hundred words with no name at the foot of it,” he said. “In a kitchen, eight days ago, with a woman of sixty reading out a copy in her own hand.”

“Yes,” said Sera Quill. “And no day on it on purpose. And I have thought about those two hundred words every day since and I have thought about that plank twice, and I would like to be honest about why, because the reason is not flattering and I have not got anybody to flatter me.

“Those two hundred words were a document nobody could check. This is a document that four people in a stair could not check either, and it has been unchecked for four hundred years, and it has a hundred and forty-one people in it, and every single one of them is somebody who can be asked except one, and I have spent six years believing that the mark of a real record is that it can be produced in a room, and I have got a plank on the inside of a door in a village of about ninety people that nobody has ever produced anywhere, and it has outlasted every room I have ever been in.”

She picked her satchel up again.

“And I am not going to say what it is for,” she said, “because I do not know, and because that is not mine to say, and because a recordwright who puts a use to a thing she cannot account for is the exact thing that happened to the four lines about a river when they came into this county, and I have been here twenty-six days and I have not forgotten it and I am not going to start.”

---

And a carter of thirty-eight was at the end of the ninth cart with a slip of paper in his coat he has not shown anybody, and the whole of the market day went on around nine people who had been told about a plank and had not repeated it to anybody, and about four of them told each other about the word at the top of a board in a yard, which was a different thing and smaller and was the only conversation anybody had in Coldharrow that afternoon.

The last bell went and the four of it went on the plank and the day went to eight, and the man of fifty-two read the four market strokes off the wall and did not add them to anything and did not write on the cart, and the man of thirty-one was at the bar at about the ninth hour with a woman of about fifty who had a board under her arm and a word at the top of it, and neither of them said anything for about four minutes.

“They are three,” said the woman at the bar. “At Trope. Three men, four hundred miles off, and they have come on the lane and not on a road, and the miller's boy has seen them and the miller's boy tells everybody everything he sees, and they came in at about the last of the light and they have not been to a house.”

“Three at each of three gates,” said the man of thirty-one.

“That is not what I said and you are not going to make it what you said.” She shifted the board. “I said three men and I said Trope and I said the miller's boy. And I have been at this bar twenty-two years and I know what a stranger is worth at a bar and a stranger is three rings, and three of them is nine, and there has not been nine in this village since a timber cart and two barrows and a man with lime came through in the fourth day of the fifty-ninth week, and about four people in this yard remember that day and they remember where they were standing, and it was about nine, and I was at this bar.”

She looked up the lane.

“They have come about that door,” she said. “And I am telling you now, on a market day, with about nine people behind me, that the argument about that door is going to happen at Trope on the day after tomorrow, and it is going to be the third one about that door anybody has had in a fortnight, and it is not going to be the one you have been having.”

---

And at about the last of the light on the sixth day of that week there were nine of them, and the man of thirty-one was in a yard at Coldharrow with a lead in his hand and no lead in his pocket, and about nine people in it, and he was in the middle of it, and nobody had asked him anything, and the four of the strangers had got as far as Trope and had not got as far as the bar, and the whole of what those twenty-six days had been for was about to be argued a second time in front of him by four people who had never waited for him.
