# Chapter 267: Two Cases Side By Side, And The Price Of It

Seven went onto the board on the morning of the third day of the sixtieth week, and it had read sixty-three thousand six hundred and eighteen at the second light, and three of the seven were a stranger. The stranger was a man of about forty with a handcart and a grey coat who came the other way at about the fourth hour, said his name when a woman of about fifty at the bar asked him to, and went down the lane, and a woman of forty-one rang three times, off the end of her fingers, with her eyes on the lane and not on the wall, and said nothing whatever about what the three of them were for.

The man of thirty-one had been in the country seventeen days. He had walked nine miles up the lane the evening before, said about eleven words in a doorway in Ellerby, slept about four hours in a house belonging to a woman of about sixty who had not offered it and had not been asked, and walked nine miles back in the dark with a recordwright of twenty-eight and had reached the yard at Coldharrow at about ten minutes past the second light, which is early, and had not mentioned that it is early.

He was in the doorway of the tower at about the ninth hour, and about nine people were within thirty feet of it because it was a market day gone and because a stranger had been through and because about four people in this village had established over the last two days that a man standing in a doorway is not to be hurried past, and had done so without a vote.

The stranger of the fourth hour was a subject. So was the man of about fifty-one with a satchel. So was the woman of about twenty-nine at a press eleven miles off who had four lines in a drawer. So was a woman of about sixty nine miles off who was standing in a doorway holding a piece of paper she had not asked for.

And Ottily Roke came out of the yard with the cloth because it was a morning and there was nothing to wipe, and stood against the tower wall about four feet off, and waited, because she had been waiting for this for about a day and a half and had not said so to anybody.

---

“I am going to say what I am about to do before I do it, because I have learned in two weeks that the only honest thing left to do in this country is to say the thing out loud first,” said the man of thirty-one, “and because there are about nine people in this yard and I would like them to be able to tell later that they were told.

“I am going to put two accounts side by side. That is the whole of it. I have done it about four times in two years and it has worked about four times. What I am not going to do is join them together, and I could not if I wanted to, and I am not going to pretend that the not-joining is a difficulty. It is the only shape it can be in. *You may put two things next to each other. You may not make them one. And the price of putting them next to each other is that the two people in the middle of them find out about each other, and neither of them was asked, and there is no instrument in this country that stops it happening, and I have looked for one, and there is not one, and I am going to do it anyway, because the alternative is two things in two counties nine miles apart that are each worth nothing.*”

He looked at her.

“It takes about nine minutes and I am going to do it in this doorway, and you are in it, and I am not going to ask you.”

“No,” said Ottily Roke. “You are not.”

---

The first account was hers, and he had it the way a man has a thing he has heard once.

“In the twenty-second year of that board, which is nine years back, a man walked a boundary on a hedge between this village and Ellerby with four men. He put a mark on two elms. There is a woman of about sixty in Ellerby who was twenty-one and who was somebody's aunt that day. She can say where it started and she cannot say what day it was. Your father counted seven out loud the whole way, because that is what a boundary is, and it is seven, and it is always been seven. It went on that board as a number and it is a number on a plank and there is no column and there was never a column, and your hand is not in the grain there because your father made it. That is your account. It took four minutes at a board in the shade on the first day of that week and I have got all of it.”

“And hers?”

“The first half of hers I got on the seventh day of last week from you and it is a woman of about sixty saying she is the only one who can say where it started and that being the only one who can say it is not an honour but a job nobody gave her. The other half I got yesterday in about eleven words in a doorway in a village of about a hundred and forty people. *There are two hundred and nine names on a wall in Ellerby and no figures anywhere on it and nobody has ever struck a name out, and every single one of those names is a person somebody can go and ask, and there is one name on it in a blue coat that there is nobody here for, and they have left it there because a name with nobody at the end of it is still better than a number.*”

He let it stand in the doorway for about two seconds, which is longer than he meant it to.

“Those are the two. And here is what is true of them, and it is one sentence, and it is the first thing I have produced in this country that anybody can check, and I did not check it, a woman of sixty checked it against a wall yesterday.

“*The only proof in this county that a strip of ground was ever decided is a number one village keeps and a name another village keeps, and the two of them are nine miles apart, and neither village can use what the other one has.* Coldharrow has a number and cannot produce a person. Ellerby has a person and cannot produce a day. The same afternoon is in both and there is no instrument anywhere in eleven villages that can lay the two of them side by side, and I have just laid them side by side in a doorway, and that is all I have done, and I cannot take it back and I would not, and that is the ninth minute.”

---

And the price came inside the same minute, and it came without a sound, and it is worth saying exactly how, because it is the whole of what this technique is.

Ottily Roke had learned, in seventeen days, that a strip of ground between her village and Ellerby might be a boundary, and that eleven families were grazing it, and that there is a woman of about sixty who can say where it started, and that the woman's name had been said out loud in a lane the day before for the first time in nine years.

She had not learned, and could not have learned, that a woman of about sixty standing in a doorway in Ellerby knows her name, and knows her wage, and knows about a figure on a wall and a number that went up by seven on an afternoon nobody can name, and knows that the man of thirty-one has been in this country seventeen days.

Nine miles. A doorway and about four minutes on foot.

And there was no instrument. He had said so himself a minute before he did it, in front of about nine people, and he had been right, and about four of the people in that yard had heard the rule and none of them could have stopped him and all of them could have heard the same rule at a gate in Ellerby in about nine hours if anybody had thought to go and stand at the gate.

“You have made me a person in a room,” said Ottily Roke.

She said it to him and not to the yard, and then she said the rest of it, and the rest of it was the price and she did not soften it and did not pretend to be angry and was not angry at all, which was the thing that made it land.

“You have made me a person in a room and I did not ask to be one. And I have been one for nine years. I have been a person in a room in this county since I was thirty-two and nobody in this county has ever come near the room, and I did not know it was a room until a man of thirty-one stood in a doorway and put me in it in front of nine people this morning. *I did not know it was a room until you did it.*”

She put the cloth over her shoulder.

“I am not going to be angry with you, and I want that noticed, because I know what it looks like from the outside and it looks like a woman of forty-one being magnanimous in a yard. I am not being magnanimous. I am being accurate and I have been being accurate for twenty-five years and it is the only thing I have got.

“I would like it written down somewhere that is not a wall that I found out this morning that I have been in a room for nine years, and I would like it written down by somebody who is not in it, and I am telling you that I have said *somebody who is not in it* and I have not said you, and you know why I have not said you, and so do I.”

---

And here is the sentence the man of thirty-one did not say in the doorway and wrote down afterwards, in a hand of his own, on a leaf he took out of a book, and did not give to anybody, and which is the true shape of the thing that happened to him in this country on the eighteenth day:

*He was right about the mechanics. The mechanics were right, the two accounts do sit side by side, the sentence about the number and the name is true, and the woman of twenty-eight is right that it is a waste. The human meaning of it arrived late, as it always does, and it arrived on two women in a county of two hundred and ten people, and neither of them was asked, and he did not find out that he had done it until the second of the two had already said it back to him.*

*He has had this before. Twice. Both times it was a hand on a table and both times he took the hand away again. This is the same failure in a different medium and the medium is the only thing that has changed, and the reason he wrote it down is that the third time is the one where the pattern is worth having.*

He did not write any of the rest of the day down. He had learned that once, in a market town four hundred miles off, in about a month, and he had written it down then as a fact about a thing he had done and not as a rule, and the fact was that there is a place where a thing you have written in your own hand to be careful with goes, and who is holding it, and it is not him.

At the last bell, a woman of about forty with a barrow came through the bar that is not shut, on her way up the lane, and stopped in the yard of the last house at Ellerby for long enough to look at the two hundred and nine names on the wall of the last house, and a man of about thirty-five with a barrow put a name on a wall.
