# Chapter 298: The Status, And What It Does Not Have In It

Sixty-three thousand eight hundred and twenty-five was what a woman of forty-one put on a wall in Coldharrow on the sixth day of the sixty-fourth week, on a market morning, with the market at four on top of the four, and the woman of about fifty took nine loads at her gate before the middle of the day, at threepence apiece, and read them off her own board under the four letters and a stroke, and nobody asked her a second question, and it was the forty-eighth morning of a man of thirty-one in that county and he was in it.

He had been back in the lane two days, and a leg that was the leg, and a thing in a bag that was off nothing, and a figure in chalk at the foot of a plank on a wall that had not been added to in forty-seven days and that nobody had told him to take off and that he had not offered.

---

The status is one sheet and it is short and it is the first document anybody in this county has ever been able to produce, and about four people in a room understood what that meant when it was read out and about nine did not, and the four were a recordwright of twenty-eight, a man of fifty-two, and a man of thirty-one who was on no part of it, and the fifth of the four was a woman of fifty-one who cannot write.

Tarin Keel wrote it out on the fifth of nine sheets cut out of the back of a bill, in his own hand, and he put nothing at the top of it and nothing at the foot of it, and it took him about four minutes and he read it out twice and did not improve it.

It says this, and this is the whole of it.

*A place may be named in a register. A person of that place may say a sentence in that place, and a recordwright writes down what was said and nothing else.*

*Any person of any country may go to any place named in the register and read the register, and may not be stopped.*

*Where a copy of an instrument concerning a named place is produced to a room, a person of that place may be in the room, and it shall be said aloud in the room that a person of that place may be sent for.*

*Nothing in this register shall be held to be a right, a power, a remedy, a court, a bench, a seat, a vote, a toll, a gate, a bar, a rate, an officer, a meeting, a fund, or a day.*

*There is a place named in this register that cannot be found. It is left named.*

---

“There,” said Tarin Keel. “That is the whole thing and it took me four minutes and I have been carrying it in my head for eleven days and I am not going to say a word about how I feel about it and I would like it noticed that I have not said a word.”

He put the lead on the cart's side.

“*There is no gate on it. There is no toll. There is no bar. There is nothing to be shut and nothing to be opened.* There is no date and I want that said, because about four of you have already heard the word register and put a day in it in your heads.

“No bench sits on it and no seat, and no chair. The only chamber it has is a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off, and a woman of twenty-nine who sells days out of that press is not letting the shelf go and is not charging anybody for it and has not been asked what it costs her, and I have not asked her either. *No vote. No meeting. No officer. And not one penny in it anywhere, because I cut it out of the back of a bill with a knife and a knife is what I had.*”

“Who is in it,” said the man of thirty-one.

“*You are not.*” Tarin Keel did not look up. “A recordwright of twenty-eight keeps it and is not in it. A woman of twenty-nine holds the shelf and is not in it. A man of fifty-two cut it and is not in it and has never put his name at the foot of anything in this county and is not going to start. The eleven villages on this lane and about forty other places are in it, and a valley four hundred miles off is the fifty-second, and about nine of its people said a sentence in their own yard in four days and about a hundred and twenty did not and nobody asked them.

“A woman with nine years of a bag is in it in no capacity whatsoever. She is not on it. She has not been asked and she did not ask. *And a clerk of forty-four is not in it and is four hundred miles off and sent a letter.*”

---

And the sentence that took it off her was not said in a yard in Coldharrow, and it was not said by a man of thirty-one, and it was not said in a market.

It was said four miles up the lane, in Nettlebed, in Nettlebed, by a person of Nettlebed, standing in his own doorway with the door on the hasp behind him and a hurdle frame against the wall.

He is about thirty-one and he makes four gates a year and sells three and he has a key on a nail he has never used and a tower he has never been up, and he is not the man of thirty-one from four hundred miles off and has never been confused with him by anybody except in a state file, and he has said one thing in this county that took a shape off a thing and one thing that was four words long, and neither of them was asked of him and both of them were his.

“It is not hers,” he said. “It is a thing places are in.”

That is ten words and about four people in a doorway took the whole of it and about nine did not, and the woman with the board heard it, and she was standing in the doorway, and nobody had put the question to her and nobody was going to.

---

And she agreed before it had finished.

“That is right,” said Sable Venn, “and nobody put the question to me, and I would have got there in about four days on my own, and I am glad of the four days that somebody else spent instead.”

She shifted the bag on her hip. She had been in this county about sixteen weeks. Nobody has thanked her for anything in nine years and nobody thanked her now, and she said so in the same breath, which is her habit and has been since the sixth day of the sixty-first week.

“I am not going to be relieved and I am not going to be thanked and I have said the first of those in this county twice and I am not going to say it a third time because it does not improve. *It is not a defeat and I am not the villain of it and nobody is going to make me one, and if anybody in that doorway wants to make me one I have been in this country nine years and I know exactly what that is for and it is not this.*”

She put her hand flat on the frame of the door beside the sheet in the tower, and did not touch it.

“Thirty people, more or less. Some of them would come up a lane with me tomorrow and some of them would not, and I know which is which and I have never once counted and I counted once in a market yard a month ago and I am not going to do it again today.

“What I am going to do with them is the thing I have done for nine years and I am going on doing it and nobody has asked me to stop. *I go up lanes on my own, I put a paper in front of somebody who has never had one, and I do not ask them what they want, because I cannot do that any more and it would take a year to get back and there are about four hundred doors to knock at.* And that is the shape of the door in that tower and a man with a hurdle has told me so twice in a yard and he is right both times, and it is not going to stop, and nobody has asked me to stop it.

“And what has changed is not the carrying. What has changed is that I know what it is, and a woman of fifty-one told me in a yard about a month ago, and a man with a hurdle told me again on a market morning, and about four people in every one of those yards understood all of it.

“*Nobody is in a room. I am not a room. I am a woman with a bag who goes up lanes. And there is now a sheet of paper on a door in this tower that says a place can be in a thing, and a room is what it is not.*”

Nobody thanked anybody, and the third hand in the chalk is still in the chalk, and about nine people in this county know whose hand it is in and not one of them has ever said so, and this time about four of them were in a doorway four miles up a lane and one of them was a recordwright of twenty-eight and she looked at it for about a second and a half and said nothing.

---

And then a man of about nineteen with a hurdle frame on his shoulder said the thing nobody in that doorway had said in eight weeks, and he said it to the man of thirty-one and not to the sheet, and he had come up the lane from a valley four hundred miles off with four others and had not said a word for two days.

“You have been in two counties,” he said, “and you have got a sheet of paper out of the back of a bill with nobody's name on it, and a book on a shelf with one place in it and then two, and a woman who is not in it, and a valley with a corridor on it, and a man who signed it and is alive and is not on any of it.

“*Is the man who signed it on any of this?*”

“No,” said Tarin Keel. “And I want that said out loud in a doorway in front of about nine people and I want it said by me and not found out in a fortnight by somebody who is annoyed. *He is not on the register. He is not in the fifty-two places. He is not on the sheet in that tower and he is not in the book on the shelf and nobody was asked to put him on any of it and nobody is going to be asked.*”

“And he is owed a bell.”

“Yes,” said Tarin Keel. “He is. Six pounds and fifteen is the price of the bell, hung, with a rope, and the keeper's wage is five pounds five, so a pound and ten stands between them year on year, and the bell is not hung and nobody has bought it and nobody is going to buy it this month, and the city put it in a document as the price of a signature and the price is still standing and the bell is owed and there is no money for it anywhere in this county or in his.

“And a woman of forty-one in Coldharrow has said in a yard about a week ago that she would rather there were one. *She would rather there were one and there is not one, and I have put that in here because it is a fact and a fact is the only thing I have ever been allowed to write down.*”

---

Nobody asked the man of thirty-one to be on it and he did not offer and he was not going to be asked and there is no space for his name on that sheet and there is no dash either, and a man of fifty-two cut it out of the back of a bill with a knife about a fortnight ago and there is no name at the foot of that bill either.

The last bell went, and the day at Coldharrow came to eight, and the woman of forty-one put the eight up and did not know that four of the eight were a market, and there is no column on that plank and there never was one, and a corridor is standing in a valley four hundred miles off, and a status is standing on a door in a village of about ninety people, and one of them has a signature on it and the other one has not, and neither of them has a date, and one of them has cost a hundred and forty people a bell they are owed and the other one has cost nothing at all, and it is not known in this county yet which of the two was dearer.
