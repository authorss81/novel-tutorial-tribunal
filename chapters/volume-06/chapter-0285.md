# Chapter 285: A Sentence In A Yard As The Rules Require, Which Is A Dispute And Not An Entry

A debt was called in at the bar at about the third hour on the last day of the sixty-second week, and it was a load account and it was the ninth time in nineteen years that the same load had been argued over, and a carter of thirty-eight did it in about nine seconds the way he does it, and the woman of about fifty took her board out and called the count off it as the two of them finished, because that is the order those two things happen in at that gate and has been for twenty-two years.

The four bells went on the plank and the two of the debt went on the plank and the day came to six, and what the cloth came off the tower wall that morning was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and forty-five, and nobody in two hundred and ten people had asked the woman of about fifty what a load was in the whole of the twenty-two years she has stood there.

---

They went up the lane at about the fourth hour, four of them, because the rules of the register say that a sentence is said by a person of a village in that village and there is no other place it may be said, and nobody in eleven villages has ever found a way round that and nobody wants to.

The four were the man of thirty-one, a man of fifty-two with a bit of lead in his pocket, a recordwright of twenty-eight with a book, and a woman of about sixty who walked nine miles to do it and would not be carried and would not be waited for.

There were about nine people in the yard of her own house at the second hour, and about four of them were the people the sentence was about.

---

“Right,” said Wenna Skell. “I have had this in my mouth for about a fortnight and I have had it ready since a woman of fifty-one said my grandmother's name out loud in a yard three days ago, and I am not going to pretend to you that you did that for me. I have had it ready longer than that. I have had it ready nine years.

“The rule is that I say a sentence in my own yard and it goes on a sheet, and I have read the sheet, anybody in this county can read the sheet, and there is no heading on it and no day on it and no money in it and nothing at the top of it. So here it is, and it is a whole sentence, and I am not shortening it because I am sixty and it takes me a while.

“Ninety acres between your bar and mine is ours. Eleven families have been grazing it since before I was sixty and paying nothing for it and being asked nothing about it. There is a hedge on it and I have wanted that hedge out of the ground for nine years, and I am not going to tell you why this year. A man walked a line through that ground in the twenty-second year of the board at Coldharrow and put the marks on two elms, and that man is four years dead, and there is one seven on that board in his hand and there is no column on that plank that says what the seven was for, and there is not going to be one.

“That is my sentence. That is the whole of it. I have said it in my own yard to about nine people, and I am not going to say it again this year, and if anybody asks me what year the twenty-second year of that board is I will tell them what I have told two men already, which is that I cannot, and I have been keeping that board's numbers for eleven weeks through another woman and I have never once been asked which of them anything was for and I am not going to be now.””

---

That is the four minutes.

It took four minutes and there were about eleven people in that yard by the end of it and it is the most expensive thing that has happened in the Briar March in about a month, and it was not a decision and it was not a victory and nobody in it won anything, and the only reason it happened at all is that there is a rule in a book on a shelf in a press eleven miles off that says a person of a village may say one thing in her own yard, and that the rule made a woman of about sixty say a thing out loud that she had been carrying for nine years and had no reason whatever to say.

What she said was that there is ninety acres, and that eleven families have been grazing it, and that nobody has told them.

And then the four minutes were the four minutes of eleven people finding out.

---

The first man asked where the hedge was. She told him, and it is about four hundred yards off the lane and it is not a hedge anybody in that yard had ever noticed, and about four of them said so, and one of them said it had been there all his life, and she said it had and that was the point of it.

The second thing was asked by a woman of about forty with a child on her hip, and she asked how long, and Wenna Skell said she did not know and could not find out, and would not be told, and had not asked.

The third question was the one that mattered and it came from a man of about fifty who had a cart of his own outside the yard and who said, in about nine words, that he had been grazing two acres of that strip for eleven years and had never known whose it was and had never been asked and had never asked, because there is nothing to ask anybody with, and he said that out loud in a yard in front of eleven people as if it were an ordinary thing to say, and it was the most ordinary thing anybody said in that yard, and about four of the eleven went quiet.

And then there was about a quarter of an hour in which eleven people asked nine questions and got four answers and the four answers were all the same answer, which is that a man walked a line in the twenty-second year of a board and the man is dead and the board has a number on it and not one stroke of that board says what any stroke of it was for.

And the last thing that was said in that yard, and it was said by the man of thirty-one, and he had said nothing for the whole four minutes and about nine people had watched him not say anything:

“There is no day on any of it. That is the whole of what I can tell you and I am telling you so that nobody goes away this afternoon thinking that a man of thirty-one has a day. I have been in this country thirty-five days and I have looked for a day the way you would look for a gate in that hedge, and there is not one, and the only two things anybody has ever been able to give this county are how many times and which came first, and neither of those is a thing you can be right about.”

---

And then the recordwright of twenty-eight sat down on the second step of that house with her book open on her knees and wrote down what was said and nothing else and did not write who said it, because that is one of the eleven lines, and a man of fifty-two read all eleven of them out loud in a yard at Trope a week ago, and she has not improved on them and is not going to.

She wrote it in about a minute and a half. It took her a minute and a half because she read it back to herself twice and there was one part of it she could not get the same way twice and she left it and moved on.

And then she said the thing, out loud, in that yard, before anybody had asked her, in about nine seconds, and it is the whole of the thirty-fifth day and it is not a small thing.

“I cannot tell you what I have done,” said Sera Quill. “And I want that said here and I want it said by me and not found out in a fortnight by somebody who is annoyed.

“The rule is that a sentence said by a person of that village in that village puts that village in. That is what it is for, that is the whole of what the book is, and a week ago a man of fifty-two read eleven lines off a cart's side in a yard and one of the eleven was that, and nobody has said a sentence in a month, and I have kept the book for a week, and I am not in it.

“And I have written down what she said, and it is a dispute. *It is a complaint about a hedge and a line and eleven families, and there is nobody in this county that can settle a line, and a thing that nobody can settle is not a thing a village has joined, it is a thing a person has said out loud in a yard.* And I have written the words and there is not one thing in them that says which of those two it is, and the reason there is not is that the rule tells me not to write who said it, and I wrote the rule.

“I have kept records for six years and in six years the only reason I have ever been right about a thing is that I have been able to go and look at who wrote it. *That is the whole of my trade. And I have just written the first thing in six years that I cannot look at.*”

---

Nobody in that yard told her she had done it right, and nobody told her she had done it wrong, and about four people in it had understood what she had said and the rest had not, and the man of thirty-one had understood all of it and had something available that would have settled it in about four seconds.

It was the sentence he had said out loud to a hedge on the fourth day of the fifty-eighth week, about a mile outside Coldharrow, to nobody, and he had carried it since without once putting it down in front of anybody. The whole of it is this: that where a person cannot pay for a hearing and cannot be heard in a room, the answer is a person who can be asked, and that a village needs a second such person, and that nobody in this country has ever asked what happens to the first one once there are two.

It would have settled it, in about four seconds, in a yard, in front of eleven people, and it would have turned a dispute into a mechanism. And it is the sentence on which every room four hundred miles off is built, because a person who can be asked is cheaper than a hearing, and a building with a yard is cheaper still, and the sentence is what keeps the two apart.

And the person it would have moved was standing in that yard with her arms folded, and she is about sixty, and she has been the only one who can say where a boundary started for nine years, and he had spent thirty-five days in this county learning what happens to the first person you can be asked.

So he did not say it, and about nine people in that yard watched him decide not to, and about four of them knew what it was, and one of those four was a recordwright of twenty-eight who did not look at him and has not looked at him since except twice and both times about a bell.

---

And at about the last hour of the day the woman of about sixty came back to the step where the book was and said the last thing, and it is worth putting down whole because the whole of the last ten days in this county has been about it and the whole of it is in nine words and a yard.

“I want it off the sheet.”

“No,” said Sera Quill, and said it kindly. “I cannot, and I will tell you why in four sentences and then I will not mention it again. One of the eleven lines says that a place goes in on a sentence said by a person of that village in that village. There is no line about a place coming out. *A man of fifty-two read all eleven of them out loud in a yard at Trope in front of nine people and I wrote three of them and I have not improved on them, and there is no twelfth line, and there is not going to be a twelfth line, and that is my whole opinion and I have had it for about a fortnight and I have not said it to anybody because I did not want to be the one who said it.*”

“Then say it now,” said Wenna Skell, “because I have just gone nine miles and stood in my own yard in front of the eleven people the sentence is about and told them there is ninety acres, and it is the truth and it is all I have got, and take it off the sheet before I go home, and I want it to be somebody in this county who is the one who says there is no way.”

And the recordwright of twenty-eight wrote down the asking.

That is in the book as well. That is the whole of what is in the book. Two lines in a hand that has never had a name at the foot of it, one of which is a dispute about ninety acres and eleven families, and one of which is a woman of about sixty asking in a yard for it to be taken off, and neither of them says who said either of them, and the eleven lines have no twelfth line, and the day went to six on a plank on a wall in a village nine miles away and the six is four of the clock and a debt called in and nobody in two hundred and ten people has been told what the six was for.
