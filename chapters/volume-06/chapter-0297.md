# Chapter 297: A Question Asked Of Him, For The First Time

A stranger went through the bar at Coldharrow on the fifth day of the sixty-fourth week, going up the lane and not stopping, and a woman of about fifty counted him off her own board because she counts everybody, and a man of about seventy-four at the second house died before the noon bell and was buried before the last bell, and the two of them together made four on the plank, and the figure at the head of it was sixty-three thousand eight hundred and seventeen.

That is a day of eight and it was not a market, and there is no column on that plank and there never was one, and the woman of forty-one knew exactly which two had gone onto it and has not got a single thing she could show anybody.

---

Here is the finding, and it is the last one the instrument has, and it is arithmetic and not opinion.

Eight is four bells and a market. Eight is also four bells and a stranger and a burial, because a stranger is three and a burial is one, and three and one is four.

*A day of eight and a day of eight, and they are not the same day, and nothing on that plank will ever say which is which.* About four people in Coldharrow worked that out inside about nine minutes and the woman of forty-one worked it out standing on a ladder with a cloth over her shoulder before any of them did, and there is no room in eleven villages or in any county four hundred miles off where anybody could be told, and she has been the only person anybody could ask for twenty-five years and there is nobody to ask her to.

She wrote both of them down. Not on the plank — on the end of her cloth, in pencil, in a hand that is not her counting hand, which is a thing she has done twice in seven weeks and has told nobody about and does not intend to do again.

---

A man of about seventy-four died at the second house in Coldharrow on the fifth day of the sixty-fourth week and he is not on anything.

That is the whole of his afterlife and it is worth a paragraph, and about four people in that village have worked out the whole of it in the same four minutes and have not said one word to each other about it.

He is not on the plank on the tower wall, because that plank is a count of rings and it is not a count of anything, and neither hand that has ever written on it ever wrote down what any ring was for, and a man of about seventy-four who died before the noon bell was one ring and there is no column and there never was one.

He is not on the wall of the last house in Ellerby, because that wall has two hundred and nine names on it in the order things happened and nobody struck out, and he was not in Ellerby and nobody struck him out because he was never on it, and about seventy of the two hundred and nine are people in four other villages who can be got at and asked, and about a hundred and forty of them are not in that wall at all and about a hundred of them are dead and nobody has ever gone to find out which.

He is not on the plank in the tower at Nettlebed, because that one is a hundred and forty-one names in a hand four hundred years old with a figure against every one of them and nothing whatever over the column, and a man of about seventy-four who was born in this century is not on it and could not be, and a bell is the only thing anybody in eleven villages has ever gone and looked for and there has not been one in that village for about two hundred years.

His name was on four hands and a woman of forty-one has it, and she gave it out loud at about the fourth hour to about nine people standing outside his own door because that is what she does and nobody asked her, and nobody in that yard had heard it before.

And the second half of the matter is this. *The stranger that morning had a name too, and the woman of about fifty at the bar does not have it, and she has been asked about one thing and one thing only in twenty-two years, and she was never asked who he was and he did not stop and she did not ask, and that is correct and it will stay correct.* Two people went through that village on the same morning and one of them died in it and one of them did not, and the only difference anybody can prove is three strokes and one, and three and one is four, and four is what a day of nothing says.

---

In Draymoor, on the fifth day of that week, a woman of about fifty-eight with a stick and nothing under her arm said a question in a yard at about the ninth hour to about nine people, and it was fair, and about nine people heard it, and about a hundred and twenty did not because they were not in the yard and were not going to be asked.

“Your turn,” she said. “I have listened to the leader do his own working twice and I have listened to that man of fifty-two do his three times and I have listened to the woman of twenty-eight do hers, and every one of you has been the one who knows something and every one of you has been careful about it in the way careful people are careful.

“You have been in this valley seven days and you have not once said what you are for, and you said it to the leader on the first night in about nine words, and he wrote it on a board and read it back, and that was you being careful in the way you are careful, and I have noticed it and I have not mentioned it and I am mentioning it now.

“*What are you for.* And I want an answer that is not about a village, because I have lived in this one for fifty-eight years and I know what villages are and I have got two of them out of the way already.”

---

He answered it, and it took him about nine words, and the answer was not good enough, and about four people in that yard understood every part of it and none of them could do anything with any part of it.

“I have been in this country forty-seven days,” he said, “and I have a figure in chalk at the foot of a board in a village I am not going to be thanked in, and the second thing I have said out loud this month is also a thing nobody has asked me for. *That is the whole of it and I do not know what I am for, and I have said the not-knowing out loud in about nine yards in seven weeks and I have never once been asked to say anything else.*”

And then he stopped, and what the stopping cost him is the rest of that afternoon, and it is not about him.

Answering it in a yard in front of nine people is a man becoming a person in a room. It is the exact thing that happened to a woman of forty-one in a yard about a month ago, over a figure in pencil at the foot of a sheet she did not ask for, and she said in a yard that she was not going to be told she was sorry for it, and he was standing four feet off at the time and had said nothing.

He knew it. He had known it for four days, since a woman of forty-one in her own yard had refused a nine-word sentence of his and had told him the reason, and the reason was that a word is not a person, and he had said thank you and had been told that he was refused and that it is a different sentence.

And a boy of seventeen had put a mark on the end of the same question in a doorway forty-three days ago, and had been told *I do not know*, and had said that made two of them, and about four people in a yard knew that the mark was still on it and nobody had done one thing about it.

“*There are two of us*,” said a woman of about fifty-eight, in that yard, in about four seconds, without being asked and without any idea what she was talking about.

He looked at her.

“I do not know what that means,” he said.

“No,” she said. “You do not. And I am not going to explain it, because it is not mine and I have had it out loud once in this yard and I am about fifty-eight and I do not hand things round.”

---

And then the woman of forty-one said the sentence, and it is the whole of the volume's argument, and it is not his sentence, and it is better than his, and she said it to about nine people in her own yard on her own ground and she had not been asked.

“You have all been asking me a question for four days,” she said, “and the woman with the stick has just asked it out loud and the man from four hundred miles off has answered it with a figure in chalk in a village he is not going to be thanked in, and that answer is better than anything I could have said, because he has admitted that he does not know, and I have watched about nine men in this valley not admit that for about nine years.

“So here is mine, and it is not better than his, and I am going to say it anyway, and then I am going to go and do my day.

“*A hundred and forty-one people said no in this valley, one at a time, in their own yards, and then somebody kept a bill, and there is no name over the column and nobody has ever added it up, and it is still there.* That is what happened here about four hundred years ago. Not a right. Not a law. *A bill that somebody kept.*

“And I am the answer to the question the woman with the stick asked, because I am the one who is asked for in this valley and has been for nine years, and I am the answer for a reason that has nothing to do with me being the right person. I am the answer because there is nobody else to ask. And there is nobody else to ask because nobody was ever paid to be asked, and the wage was ninepence a day on a hundred and forty days, and that is five pounds and five shillings a year, and it stopped, and I cannot tell you in which year it stopped and I have asked twice and both times I was told it was before I was here.”

She stopped, and about four people in that yard understood all of it.

“And you cannot put that in a yard with a name at the bottom of it,” she said. “Not because it would be wrong. Because it would not be true. *I am a woman in a valley. I am not a name at the foot of a paper, and if I were, then in four years somebody could ask me what I said on this afternoon and I would have to have an answer that fits on a sheet, and I have never in my life had an answer that fits on a sheet and I am not going to start.*

“You want to know what you are for. I will tell you what I am for, since you have not managed it: *I am for asking people in this valley what they want, and I have never once been asked that myself, and it is the whole of my life and I would like somebody in this yard to notice that and say nothing.*”

Nobody said anything.

“And that is the thing you have been carrying for fifty days,” she said, “and it is better than mine, and I am not going to pretend it is not, and I am not going to be the one who signs it either, and that is a separate decision and I made it four days ago in front of about sixty people and I would do it again in front of about nine.”

---

And a recordwright of twenty-eight said the arithmetic out loud at about the last hour of that day, and she said it because somebody had asked her, in a yard, whether the man's sentence could be had cheaply, and it is the last money anybody has counted in two counties and the working is in it.

“A sentence in a yard costs nothing,” said Sera Quill. “A rule on a string costs nothing. A thing said out loud by a person of a place, in that place, costs nothing at all, and there is not one of us in this valley that is going to be asked for a penny and I have written that in a book on a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off my own county and I am not in it and I am not charging anybody for it.

“A name at the foot of it is a different thing. *A name is a place, and a place is a day, and a day in this county is a pound three shillings and sixpence.* Two days' carrying at a shilling and sixpence is thirty-six pence, two nights at threepence is six, a clerk's fee of a pound is two hundred and forty. Thirty-six and six and two hundred and forty is two hundred and eighty-two. Two hundred and eighty-two pence is a pound and three shillings and sixpence. *And it is fifteen days of the man who walks the road and one shilling, and I have said all of that out loud in a yard at home, and four people there took every word of it and five did not.*

“So that is the answer to your question, and it is the whole of it, and nobody in this valley has got a pound and three shillings and sixpence and nobody in this valley is going to be asked for it, and that is why a man with a sentence and no paper and no date cannot do one single thing with it, and I have watched him try for four days and he has tried it honestly and he has not managed it and nobody is going to be blamed for it including him.”

---

And at about the last of the light, in a yard in a valley of about a hundred and forty people, a man of fifty-two with a bit of lead in his pocket took the lead out and put it down on a table and did not use it, and he said one thing about it and then he did not say anything else.

“I have had that in my pocket since the sixth day of the sixty-first week,” he said, “and I have used it about nine times and I have not used it tonight, and I want it said that I noticed, because in nineteen years in nine counties I have never once not used it.”

He put his hand flat on the table beside it and left it there.

“That is the whole of the fifth day of the sixty-fourth week,” he said, “and the day in a county I am not in went to eight, and I am not going to be told about it by anybody, and if somebody tells me about it I will not believe them.”
