# Chapter 290: Four Accounts Side By Side, And They Do Not Agree

A hen got out at Trope on the fifth day of the sixty-third week and walked about two hundred yards down a lane that has not been shut since before a woman of fifty-one was born, and nobody in about a hundred and twenty people heard it happen, and the board on the tower wall at Coldharrow had sixty-three thousand seven hundred and seventy-four on it when the cloth came off that morning, which is four strokes and nothing whatever on top of them, and that morning was the fortieth morning of a man of thirty-one in this country.

---

They had the four accounts side by side in a yard in Coldharrow for about half an hour on that morning, in the order they came up, on a piece of paper out of the back of a bill, and there was no heading over any of them and no number against any of them, and they did not agree.

That is the finding and it took four days to arrive at and it is not a disappointment. It is a thing a method of evidence does.

A bar at the end of a lane, with nine loads a day at threepence and four letters and a stroke at the top of the only board in eleven villages that has a word on it, and no road behind it and never one.

A lane that goes from village to village and does not go by the ground, in four words written in a book eleven miles off, said in a doorway by a person of a village, and in three instruments that do not know about one another, and in a boy's book that has got a third place in it he can see and cannot read.

Ninety acres and about eleven families, out loud in a yard five days ago in four minutes by a woman who cannot name a day, and nothing to be right about.

A hundred and forty-one names on the inside of a door, a figure set beside every single one, and nothing at all over the top of the column of them, and the sum not made, twice refused, out loud, on purpose.

---

The man of thirty-one stood in front of them for about a quarter of an hour and did not try anything, which is a thing he has not done in about a month.

There were about nine people in the yard and about four of them had been in it for three days, and one of the four was a woman of about sixty who had said a sentence in her own yard five days ago that a recordwright of twenty-eight wrote down and could not classify, and one of the four was a man of fifty-two with a bit of lead in his pocket, and one of the four was a woman of fifty-one who cannot write, and the fourth was the man of thirty-one, and he had a thing available and he had had it since the fourth day of the fifty-eighth week.

It is one sentence and it took him about four years and a valley and a printed form to arrive at. It is the sentence on which a building and a room are kept apart, and it is true, and it is the reason there is a building with a yard in a town four hundred miles off instead of nine people in a lane, and it is the reason this yard has got nobody in it who can be asked about anything except the threepence.

*That the answer to a person who cannot pay for a hearing and cannot be heard in a room is a person who can be asked, and that a village needs a second one of those, and that nobody has ever asked what happens to the first one when there are two.*

It would have moved her. She was nine feet away with her arms folded and she is about sixty and she has been the only person anybody could ask about for nine years, and that sentence would have told her, in a yard, in front of nine people, that she is not going to be the last one — and a boy of seventeen had put a mark on the end of that same question twenty days ago in a doorway and had been told by a man of thirty-one that he did not know, and the boy had said that there were two of them now, and that had been true for three weeks and it was not a thing anybody had done anything about.

He had watched it happen to a woman of forty-one in a month. It was a figure in a stranger's hand at the foot of a piece of paper, and it had made her a person in a record for one morning, and she had said in a yard that she was not going to be told she was sorry for it, and he had been standing four feet away at the time and had not said a word.

So he said nothing, and about four people in that yard watched him decide not to, and about nine of them did not notice, and one of the nine was the man of thirty-four, and one of the four was a recordwright of twenty-eight with her eyes in the middle of that yard, and she has kept them off him for five days now and the only two times she has turned towards him in that time were both about a bell.

---

And then the man of thirty-four said his sentence, in front of about twenty people, which is the second time he has said it in this county and the first time anybody could count them.

“If it cannot be produced then it has not happened.”

He said it in about four seconds and he said it to the yard and not to anybody, and about twenty people in that yard heard a man of thirty-four who reads things out to about forty people a week and cannot read the thing he reads out say the only sentence he has, and about four of them understood what it was and the rest heard a stranger.

Then he asked a question, which nobody expected, including the man of thirty-four.

He walked about nine yards to the bar and asked the woman of about fifty a question about loads, and she put her board down on the cart's side, and that was the first time in twenty-two years that anybody had asked her a second one.

“A load is what,” he said. “I have been up this lane three times and I have been told about a bar and a count and a figure on a wall and I have not been told what a load is, and you are the only person in this county who has got one of those boards with anything written on the top of it.”

She thought about it for about nine seconds, which is longer than she takes about anything.

“A load is a thing that came up the lane,” she said. “That is the whole of it and I have known that for twenty-two years and nobody has ever asked me. *I have got three for a stranger and four for a market and two for a debt called in and seven for a boundary walked and one for a burial, and I could tell you every one of them and how long I have kept them, and there is no stroke in this county for a load, because a load is not a thing that happens. It is a thing a man has decided is a load, and he decides it about four hundred miles off in a building, and I find out when it stops at my gate.*”

The man of thirty-four wrote it down.

He wrote it on the back of his own hand, in a hand that is not good, in about forty seconds, and about nine people in that yard watched a man who reads things out to about forty people a week write down a thing that had been said to him by a woman at a bar, and it is the only thing that man has written down in about eleven years and he did not know that was true until somebody said so, and somebody did, and it was a recordwright of twenty-eight.

“That is the only thing anybody in this yard has said this month that anybody else is able to check,” she said afterwards, to nobody. “A thing that happened, and the day it happened on, and he has got both of them on the back of his own hand. I have been a recordwright for six years and the only day anybody in this county can put on anything is a stranger, and I did not know that until about a minute ago and I would like it noticed that I have said so in a yard.”

---

The man of thirty-four did not do anything else.

He did not go up the lane to the tower and he did not ask about the door and he did not ask about the sheet in the press or the four words in the doorway or the figure in a stranger's hand, and he stood about nine yards off for about an hour with two men behind him and a thing written on the back of his own hand, and at about the ninth hour he went down the lane on foot and out of the county, four hundred miles, to a house with a yard, and there is no corridor in this county and no document in this county and there is nobody in this county who is going to do anything about a sentence a man of thirty-four said in a yard, and that is a fact and not a mercy.

Nobody in that yard said that either.

---

And the woman of about sixty said the thing at about the last hour, and she said it to about nine people in a yard in Coldharrow having walked nine miles for the fourth time in ten days, and it is the whole of the fortieth day and it took her about two minutes and nobody interrupted her and nobody had asked her.

“I am going to say the last of mine and then I am going home and I have got about four years in me and I am not being dramatic, I am being sixty.

“*I am the only one who can say where it started. Not the only one who thinks she can. The only one.*Nine years I have had that, and eight days ago I found out I had a second one, and it is a name on a list, and a woman of fifty-one said it out loud in a yard, and I cannot get it back.

“They are both going when I am. They are not going before I am, because I am sixty and there is nothing wrong with me, and that is the part nobody in this yard has understood for ten days. *Nobody is coming. There is no second woman nine miles up this lane with a grandmother on a door and a hedge in her ground. There is me and there is going to be me for about four more years and then there is going to be nobody who can say where a boundary started in this county.*”

She put her hand flat on the cart, which is a thing she does.

“And I am not asking anybody to do anything about it, and I have not asked once in ten days, and I would like it in a book, and I have said a version of that twice in ten days and I am going to stop saying it.

“I am not relieved and nobody is going to thank me and nobody is going to rescue me and nobody is going to redeem me, and I have a bar across my own gate that has hung open my whole life and it will hang open this week as well.”

---

Nobody thanked anybody. The register on the shelf in the press at Coldharrow-edge has one place in it, in four words, about a lane, and a second line under it that nobody can classify, and about thirty people are in a movement that a man of about thirty-one with a hurdle frame has said is the same shape as a door with a list behind it, and the woman who carries the papers is on no part of anything and said in a yard on a market day what she is for and has not been thanked and is not relieved and has said so out loud and is not going to say it again.

The column of a hundred and forty-one figures has not been added up. The door in a printed form has not been opened and has not been refused and nobody was asked. The third hand is still in white chalk at the foot of a board on a wall in Coldharrow and has not been added to since the first morning of a man's being in this country, and nine people in a yard could tell whose hand it is without being told, and not one of them has ever said so, and nobody has asked him to take it off and he has not offered.

Two dates are bought in a press eleven miles off and two names are on one thing and two names on one thing is not two witnesses and no room four hundred miles off has asked about either.

And the board on the tower wall said sixty-three thousand five hundred and ten on the first morning of the first day of his being in this country, and is two hundred and sixty-four higher this morning, and two hundred of it was on the board before this week began and nobody in two hundred and ten people chose that either, and the sixty-four is ten days of a village doing things, and there is no column for any of it and no heading over it and no name against it and no year at the top of it and there never was one.

---

And at about the last of the light on the fifth day of the sixty-third week a woman of fifty-one in a village of about a hundred and twenty people went out of her own gate and shut it.

She has not shut that bar since before she was born. She shut it because a hen had got out and was about two hundred yards down the lane going towards the wheel of the mill, and she went out after it and put one hand on the bar and brought it about four inches across the two uprights and went down the lane at a run.

She was gone about nine seconds.

Then she came back up the lane, and she opened the bar again, and it took her about two seconds, and the whole of the time the bar was shut was four, and nobody in about a hundred and twenty people heard any of it, and a miller's boy saw a hen and a woman and did not think anything of it because there is a handbell at that lane end and about two hundred of those things a year go on that walk.

And the last bell went at Coldharrow, and the woman of forty-one put four strokes on a plank and there was nothing whatever on top of them, and the day was the ordinary one.
