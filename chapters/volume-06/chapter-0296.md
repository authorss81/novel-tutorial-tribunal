# Chapter 296: The Word That Is Not A Person

It stood at sixty-three thousand eight hundred and thirteen that morning at the second light in Coldharrow, and it was the ordinary number, and the woman of forty-one said so to nobody, and the fourth day of the sixty-fourth week went up four bells and nothing whatever on top of them, and the forty-sixth morning of a man of thirty-one in that county was four bells and nothing on top of them too, and about nine people in two counties who had never met would have said those two numbers were the same fact.

They were not the same fact. One of them was a count and one of them was an absence, and that is the whole of that morning and it takes about nine minutes to say and about four days to be sure of.

---

A recordwright of twenty-eight said the thing out loud on the first morning after the working, in a kitchen in Draymoor, to about nine people and unasked, before anybody had put anything to her, and that is the whole of the fourth day of that week.

“I have been in this trade for six years,” said Sera Quill, “and I have kept other people’s records for every one of them, and I am going to stand in a kitchen four hundred miles from my own county and read out the whole of what exists, and it takes about nine seconds, and then I am going to stop and let about nine of you be quiet.

“The plank on a tower wall in my county. Four sheets cut out of the back of a bill. A barrow-load of paper with a figure at the foot of it in a hand that is not mine. A letter, sealed, about the size of a hand. And a thing written on the back of a man’s own hand in a hand that is not good, in a yard, in the rain, in about forty seconds.

“That is the whole of it. *Four of those five were never produced anywhere. One of them has been produced twice and the second time is the one that cost.* There is nothing else. Six weeks of a method and forty days of a man and the entire archive of it is a plank, four sheets, a barrow-load, a letter and a hand, and four of the five have never been in a room, and a room is the only place a record becomes real, and I have said that out loud in about four yards in my own county and about four people in each of them understood all of it and not one of them could do one single thing with it.

“And here is the half that is worse and I have not said it out loud anywhere until this morning. *That is the whole of the archive of a hundred and forty-one people who said no about four hundred years ago, and it is not a document, and there is no version of it that was ever a document, and it is not in anybody’s hands and it is not in anybody’s drawer and it is on a door.*”

Nobody in that kitchen said anything, and about nine of them had spent their whole lives in a valley with a tower in it and a bell in the tower and no board, and about four of them had never been in a building with a door that anybody in it could refuse them at.

---

The answer to it was the thing that came in over the following four days, and it is worth being exact about what it was, because it is the whole of the difference between what was asked for in that valley and what was actually needed.

It is not a document.

It is the same shape as a book on a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off, and a man of fifty-two said that out loud on the second morning, in a yard in Draymoor, in about nine words, and everybody in that yard understood what it had cost him to say it and nobody thanked him.

“It is the same shape as the register,” said Tarin Keel. “I have been carrying the rule of it on a string round my neck for seven weeks. Any person of any country may walk into any yard in any of the places and read all of it. Nobody goes in without a sentence said by a person of that place in that place. And a recordwright writes down what was said and nothing else, and does not write who said it. And nobody comes out, and there is no twelfth line.

“*There is no gate on it. There is no toll. There is no bar and there is nothing to be shut and nothing to be opened. And there is no day on it, and I want that said, because I have watched about nine people in the last three days hear the word register and put a day in it in their heads.*”

He put his hand flat on a wall.

“I have cut that book out of the back of a bill with a knife and it stands on a shelf in the only room in eleven villages, which is a press, and a woman of twenty-nine who sells days out of it is not letting anybody put anything else on it, and she is not charging anybody and she has not been asked what it costs her and I have not asked her either. *And that is the whole of the government of a thing that four hundred years of a lane has been doing without any of it.*”

---

Draymoor is the fifty-second place.

That happened over four days and it was the ordinaryest thing anybody has watched happen in a fortnight, and there was no form for it and no vote and about nine people were in a yard each time a sentence was said and about a hundred and twenty were not in any yard at all.

About eleven of a hundred and forty people said a sentence in their own yard in Draymoor on those four days. Not the leader. The leader was asked and the leader did not, and nobody has ever told anybody that that was a refusal and a recordwright of twenty-eight wrote down the fact that he did not say one and nothing else, because the rule does not say she may write anything else.

A woman of about twenty-nine with a child on her hip said four words about the channel. A man of about sixty who owns a house that is not his wife’s said four words about a boundary nobody has walked since before his father. A girl of about nineteen said four words that a recordwright could not classify and did not classify and said so out loud in a yard.

And the hundred and twenty did not say anything, and nobody asked them, and nobody in that valley is going to ask them, and the not-asking is the finding and not an oversight.

“That is the second time in seven weeks that a book has got a place into it and the whole of what came with the place was a person standing in a yard,” said Sera Quill. “*In my county it was four words about a lane, said by a man in his own doorway, and the whole of what came with that was a man who had a key on a nail he had never used.* And I want it said that I have got a rule on a string with eleven lines on it and not one of the eleven says anybody may come out, and about a hundred and twenty people in this valley have just found out that not saying anything is a thing they have done that cannot be undone, and I have not asked one of them and neither has anybody else, and that is not going to be in the book because it is not a thing that was said.”

---

And on the third of those four days a woman of forty-one in Draymoor asked the man of thirty-one what he was for, and she asked it in a yard in front of about nine people, and he answered it, and it was the first time he had ever answered one to a person whose valley it was.

He had been asked the same question eight days earlier in a yard in the Briar March, by a woman of forty-one who had walked a day for it, and he had said he did not know, and about four people in that yard had understood that he had not answered, and Tarin Keel had not said so and had said it out loud four days later in a kitchen, which is a thing he does.

“A stranger’s question and a person’s question are two different instruments and you have had both of them inside a week,” he said, in about nine words, in a yard, to nobody. “I have watched you answer one of them and I have watched you not answer the other one and I have got no more idea than I had before, and I have said that out loud because I keep saying things out loud in yards and it is a habit and it is not a virtue.”

---

And then he offered it, and it is the only time in fifty chapters that he has said it in a room with his name still not on it, and he offered it because a woman of about thirty-one with a child on her hip had asked who was going to be responsible for the water and the valley and the children, and three people had answered with words, and he had a sentence and he gave it.

“Where a person cannot pay for a hearing and cannot be heard in a room, the answer is a person who can be asked,” he said. “And a village needs a second one of those. And nobody has ever asked what happens to the first one when there are two.”

And then he stopped, because that is the whole of it, and about nine people in that yard heard the whole of a thing that had been assembled out loud on a hillside about a mile outside a village of two hundred and ten people on the fourth day of the fifty-eighth week, and nobody in this valley had heard it before, and about four of them took the whole of it and the rest heard a man of thirty-one talk.

And the woman of forty-one said no.

“No,” she said. “Not that.”

She did not say it unkindly and she did not say it quickly and about nine people in that yard watched a man of thirty-one be turned down by a woman of forty-one in her own yard, and about four of them had not expected it.

“You have been carrying that for fifty days,” she said, “and I know how I know, because a woman of forty-one in your county kept a count for twenty-five years and did not know she was the only one anybody could ask about until about a month ago, and I have been the one who is asked for in this valley for nine years and I have not got a board and I have not got a figure and I am the only one anybody can ask and I have known it the whole time, and I have not got a single sheet of paper with a name at the foot of it either.

“*That is nine words. It has got nobody’s name at the foot of it.* That is the whole of what is wrong with it and you know it is the whole of what is wrong with it because you have spent seven weeks telling four people in yards that a name at the foot of a thing is the only part of it anybody can be asked about, and you have told that woman from the city that her corridor is unfair and you were right, and about four people in that room knew you were right, and one of the four was me, four days ago, and I did not say so then because I was signing something.”

She put her hands behind her back, which is a thing she has seen a man of thirty-one do in about nine yards and had never once done herself until that afternoon.

“A word is not a person,” she said. “I am not being hard. I am telling you the thing I have learned in nine years of being the one who is asked, and it is this: if I say a thing in my own yard, then I am in it, and I can be asked about it in four years when I am sixty and the valley is a different valley, and that is the whole of what a sentence in a yard is worth, and it is worth a great deal, and it is not worth anything at all if the person saying it is a stranger four hundred miles off with no standing in the valley and nothing at the bottom of it.

“You could have said that in a yard at home about a fortnight ago and about four people would have heard you and nothing would have happened. *You have waited until there were a hundred and forty people and about eighty of them in a yard in the cold who had not been told anything, and you have said it here, and I have listened to it, and I am not going to be the name at the foot of it.*”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“Thank you,” said the man of thirty-one, and she said the other half of it before he had finished.

“You are not thanked,” she said. “You are refused. It is a different sentence and you have been in this country long enough to know that a different sentence is not the same as a worse one.”
