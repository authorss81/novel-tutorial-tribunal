# Chapter 288: What The Clerk Of Forty-Four Says When He Gets Back

Three strangers came through Coldharrow at about the second hour on the third day of the sixty-third week, going up the lane on foot, and the woman of about fifty counted them off her board and did not stop them and did not say where they had started from, and one of the three was the man of thirty-four and the other two were his, and they had been in this county for three weeks out of the six they have and they were not going to the tower and nobody asked them why. The number at the head of the board in Coldharrow was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and sixty-three and the day was going to be a day of seven, and the woman of forty-one had not yet been told what any of the three were for and nobody was going to tell her.

The letter came back down the lane on the same barrow at about the ninth hour. It was about the size of a hand, it had a seal on it, and it was nothing anybody in that yard had a word for, and nine of them stood round it and not one of them had held one in his life, and the man of thirty-one picked it up and turned it over twice and could not tell what any of it was.

---

Two people read it out, and neither of them wrote it down, and neither of them was in the room where it had been written.

The first was a woman of fifty-one from Trope, who cannot write, and who was in that yard because she had come down the lane with a handbell under her arm and had been in about four of the last four days' yards. The second was a recordwright of twenty-eight, who can read, and who said before she started that she was going to read it exactly as it was written including the parts that were wrong about this county, and that if anybody in the yard wanted to stop her they had about four seconds.

“There is nothing in it about this county,” said the recordwright, “so there is nothing in it that is wrong about this county, and I am going to read all of it, and it took a man of forty-four four days of walking in a leg he does not enjoy and he is not a person of this county and he is not coming back, and it is the only paper anybody four hundred miles off has written about a thing that happened in a yard in this lane without being asked by anybody here.”

---

She read it.

It was written in a hand that had been taught rather than taught into somebody, in a town that keeps weeks the writer has never learned to keep and did not say what they were called, and it began with the ordinary things a letter begins with, and then it stopped being ordinary and went on for about nine hundred words, and about four people in that yard followed all of it and about five followed about a third of it, and the woman of about fifty at the bar followed none of it and stood at the edge with her board under her arm and said afterwards that she had not missed a word of it and could not have told anybody one thing it said, and that is a fact about her and not about the letter.

“Here is the first half, in about his own words,” said Sera Quill, “and I am going to give it to you the way he gave it to me, which is flatly and with the numbers in, because he wrote it for a room and we are not a room and he would not want it done any other way.”

---

“The form you have heard about came back.

“Not to me. It was never addressed to me and it was never in my hand. It was in a drawer in a building with a yard in it, in a stack of things that came in, and it came back unsigned, and there was a paper folded inside it with about nine words written on it in a hand I do not know and I have been in twenty-two years of rooms and I know about four hundred hands.

“The nine words say that the district in which the acknowledgment would be entered has no road to it and has never had.

“That is all the note says. *Nine words. No signature, no seal, no name, and nothing else on the paper at all, and the paper is half the size of the one it was folded into and it has been torn out of something larger and folded, and whoever wrote it did not want to be written back to and I am not going to pretend to you that I know why and I have thought about it for four days on the road and I have not got one reason, I have got four, and three of them are ordinary.*”

---

“The second half is about the form and not about the note, and it is the half I came four hundred miles for and I did not know that on the day I left, and I am going to say it flatly because you have heard a man of fifty-two say a number out loud in a yard in this county and this is the same kind of thing and there is no clerk in the world who is prouder of his work than I am about this.

“I have been at the Seat nine years. I write acknowledgments. A copy goes out of a district and it comes back, and the coming back is the whole of my trade, and about four hundred of them come back in a year, and about thirty of us do it, and there is a bench of four over us, and every one of those four hundred has been done properly by somebody who was trying to do it properly.

“*And in nine years nobody in that building has ever once been asked to look at what a form does after it is entered.* I have been waiting nine years for that sentence from a bench of four and it has not come, and I have stopped expecting it, and I am not complaining about it, and I want to be exact about why: because it is not anybody’s fault. There is no wicked clerk. There is no room in which anybody could have said no.

“Here is the thing I have got, and I have had it for two years and I have not had anybody to say it to.

“A copy of a thing goes out of a district, and the acknowledgment of it may be entered without a day. That is not a secret and it is not a scandal and it is not in anybody’s pocket. It is a printed clause and it is on the form and it is the fourth line of it, and it says that where a day cannot be shown the entry goes in without one and is marked as made without a day. *A woman of about fifty at a gate in this county read that line out loud in a yard on the seventh day of the sixtieth week, unasked, and said that whoever built that door in was being fair, and that being fair and being useful are two different things, and that she was not opening it. I heard about it in a lane. I have not told her I heard and I am not going to.*

“In nine years, nine forms have been entered without a day. Nine. Not nine hundred. *And every one of the nine was entered by a person who had been in a room, and every one of the nine was asked what day it was, and every one of the nine said that there was not one.* Nine clerks, nine rooms, nine questions asked in the ordinary way by a man or a woman doing a job, and nine answers that were true.

“I do not know what a form does with those nine answers. I have been nine years in that building and I have never been asked, and this week I have tried and there is nobody to ask, and I have looked at the drawer the nine are in and I have looked at the column, and the column has a head over it.

“The head is two syllables. *I am not going to write it out and pretend I have not, because one of the two of you who is standing in that yard can read and one of them cannot, and the one who can read is going to say it out loud in about four seconds and the whole of what I am saying is going to arrive in a lane that has never had a building in it through a word that is on a column in a building four hundred miles off, and that is the most ordinary thing in the world and I have thought about it for four days and I could not make it any other way.*”

---

The recordwright of twenty-eight read the last line of the letter out and then read a word that was not in the letter, and the word was two syllables, and the man of thirty-one knew it, and about four people in that yard knew it, and about five of about nine did not and said so afterwards.

“That is the ordinary word,” she said. “Every one of us in this yard who has been in a room four hundred miles off has seen it over a column. *It is the word that goes over the list of the things that did not go, and it is the only word I have ever had to explain to a person who had never been in a room, and I have explained it four times in six years and I have never once had to explain it to somebody who had not come out of a village.*”

Nobody in that yard said anything about it and about four people in it went off up the lane about two hours afterwards and one of the four was a woman of fifty-one who cannot write and who said on the way up the lane that she had not known that a word could hold a thing up.

---

And the letter had a last paragraph, and it is the whole of the thirty-eighth day, and it was the clerk of forty-four's own hand and it was four lines long, and he was not in this county when he wrote it and he was four hundred miles off by the time it arrived and he is not coming back.

“There is one more thing and I am going to put it in a letter rather than say it in a yard, because I have been in about four of them in this county and every single one of them was better than a letter, and because I have noticed that the two of us who have done the most in this county are the two of us who have written things down, and I do not want to be the third.

“A copy of a thing goes out of a district. The district has no road to it and has never had. The acknowledgment comes back and is entered without a day, because the person in the room said there was not one and it was true.
“There is no road to it. There is no road to your lane either. It goes from one village to the next and it goes by nothing, and no cart in that county will take it, and I have read about four hundred forms about districts this year and not one of them has ever had a word in it about a lane.

“Is there a road inside your lane?”

And then: “I am a clerk and that is my whole trade and nobody has asked me for an answer and I do not expect one. Do not write back. — A clerk of forty-four, of the Seat.”

---

The yard was quiet for about nine seconds and about four people in it looked at the man of thirty-one and about five did not, and he had the letter in his hand and he could not read what was on it and he had had it read out to him by two other people in about four minutes.

Nobody answered it.

That is the fact and it is worth putting down exactly, because about nine people were in that yard and the question was a question that eleven villages could have answered between them and had never been asked: *There is no road to your lane either. It goes from one village to the next, and it goes by nothing at all, and no cart in that county will take it, and eleven villages have been walking it for about four hundred years.*

Nobody answered it, and nobody said they could not answer it, and nobody said it was not their question, and the woman of fifty-one who cannot write said, on the way up the lane, that she had been asked a thing and had not understood it and was not going to pretend to anybody that she had, and the recordwright of twenty-eight said that the word at the head of the column in a building four hundred miles off had come into a yard in the Briar March in a letter from a man who had walked in on his own account, and that this is the first route out of this county that anybody has chosen since the eighteenth day, and that it is not going anywhere, and that she is not going to let it, and that nobody thanked anybody and that the last bell went and the day was a day of seven, which is four of the clock and three strangers, and the woman of forty-one put the seven up without being asked and there is no column on that plank and there is not going to be one.
