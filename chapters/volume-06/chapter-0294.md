# Chapter 294: The Corridor, In A Room, In Front Of People Who Have To Be Told

The number on the plank at Coldharrow that morning was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and ninety-nine, and a market was the four on top of the four, and a man of thirty-one was four hundred miles off with about a hundred and forty people in a room and neither of those facts had ever touched the other one and about nine people in the Briar March would have said the two of them had nothing to do with each other and they would have been right.

The forty-fourth morning of his being in that county was the second of the sixty-fourth week and the day there was a market and the woman of about fifty was busy at the bar from the second hour to near noon, and nobody in two hundred and ten people asked her why there was a market, because a market is a thing that is not asked about, and four of the eight on that plank were the clock and the other four were the market, and there is no column on it and there never was one.

---

The room in Draymoor is over a cart shed and it is the only room in that valley with a door on it, and about sixty people can stand in it, and the hundred and forty cannot.

That is the whole of the arrangement and it is worth a paragraph, because everything that happened in that valley over two days happened inside that fact.

About eighty people stood in a yard in the cold outside the door, and they were there because the shed door was open and the shed is where the warm is, and not one of the eighty was told anything about anything until the second hour of the afternoon, and every one of the eighty had to be told, and about nine of the hundred and forty were under about nine years old and about nine of those nine were in the yard and not in the room.

A city sends about nine people. There was a woman of about fifty-six with a form and a flat board and a bench of four somewhere about nine hundred miles off that she works under, and she had come in a cart with two men and had been in the valley for a day and a half waiting for somebody to be in a room, and she was polite, and she was correct, and she had done this eleven times and it is her trade, and nobody in that valley thought anything about her at all except that she was the one who read.

The corridor was produced at about the fourth hour of the afternoon.

---

A recordwright of twenty-eight read the nine lines out, standing up, in a room over a cart shed, in front of about sixty people and about eighty outside the door, and the woman of about fifty-six handed her the copy and stood four feet off with her hands behind her back, and it is a thing this county has never had and will not have again: a piece of paper being read out in a room to a roomful of people, in a place, on a day.

Except there was no day. That is the whole of the difficulty and it came later and it cost about nine minutes.

She read it out and she read it a second time, and the two readings were the two the man of fifty-two had said in a yard in a county four hundred miles off nine days ago, and this time the room had sixty people in it and about four of them understood both readings and about nine understood one and about forty heard a woman of twenty-eight read nine lines out loud in a voice she uses for things she means.

Then Corvin Hale stood up in the middle of the floor, and nobody had asked him to, and he said the sentence he had been working on for two days.

“Does a corridor have anything to say about the water,” he said, “and if it has, who does the water belong to.”

And the woman of about fifty-six said that the instrument made no provision for water and that she was not able to speak to any other instrument and that she had brought the only one she had been authorised to bring.

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds, and it was the most useful four seconds of the whole of it.

---

And then the man of thirty-one did the only thing he knows how to do, which is to take a document apart in a room in front of the people it is about, in about nine minutes, out loud, and he announced the price first and he paid it.

“I am going to say the price before I say anything else because that is the only honest act left and I have said it in a yard in my own county four times in six weeks and this is the fifth and there is no instrument in the world that stops it. *The man whose valley this is and I are going to find out about each other inside about four minutes and neither of us is going to have been asked.*”

The room was not a yard. About four people in that room had heard him do this before and about nine had not, and nobody moved.

“The instrument says it takes effect on the day it is signed,” he said. “That is the fourth line and it is nine words and I have got the working of it. *It cannot be signed here.* This valley has no day. It has a tower and a bell that has not been rung since before a woman's mother died and a woman of forty-one who has told me, in her own yard, that she cannot say in which year the money for the ringing stopped, and she has asked twice and both times she was told it was before she was here. There is nothing on a wall in this valley. There is no figure. And a date the city keeps is not a day that anybody in this valley has ever agreed with, and I am not going to let one be put on it, and if I did that would be the worst thing I have done in forty-four days.”

The woman of about fifty-six said: “The day may be any day the city keeps.”

“Yes,” he said. “That is the whole of your answer and it is the right answer and it does not help. *Give me a day that the women of this valley would recognise, and you have nothing, and you have known that since you got off the cart, and you came anyway, because a thing that can be produced does not have to be produced to somebody who agrees with it.*”

Nobody in that room argued with him. About nine of them had not understood a word of it and the four who had understood all of it did not look at him.

He went on for another nine minutes, and it was ordinary, and it was the best civic work anybody did in that valley in six weeks, and it did not work.

He asked who the corridor affected. The woman of about fifty-six said the district.

He asked whether the district was a person. The woman of about fifty-six said a district is a district.

He asked what a district is, and the answer, given by a man of about forty in a coat, in a room of about sixty people, without any embarrassment whatever, was that a district is a set of rolls a city keeps, and that this one has about a hundred and forty names in it, and that some of the names are old, and that the city has been keeping the roll since the names were put in it and has never had a reason to check whether the people were still there.

He asked how the city had come to be making an instrument about a valley at this time of year, and the woman of about fifty-six gave the whole of it in about four seconds and it is the plainest thing anybody said in the valley in nine years.

“There is a report,” she said. “It is not this instrument. It is a different paper and it is in the hands of about nine people in a building with a yard in it and I have not read it and I am not authorised to describe it and I have not been asked what is in it and nobody in that building has asked me whether I would describe it if I could.

“*I have not come here to tell you why. I have come here because I was sent to put a paper in a room, and I can do that, and I have done it eleven times.*”

And the man of thirty-one said, in front of about sixty people: “Do you know what is in it.”

“No,” said the woman of about fifty-six. “And I am not going to find out on the road, and I have thought about it for a day and a half and I have not found out, and if you had asked me yesterday I would have said I did not want to know, and I have changed my mind about that and I want you to know that I have changed my mind about it, and I would still rather you did not have it.”

---

Nobody in that room could say what the corridor was for.

That is the finding, and it took two days and a half in a valley of about a hundred and forty people and it is not a mystery and nobody hid anything and about nine people went and stood outside and looked at the water while it was happening.

The fastest and the cheapest thing available to a city with a report it will not describe, in a valley it does not control, was to put the district off every map it keeps and call the place a corridor, because a corridor can be produced in a room and a corridor takes about nine minutes and a corridor costs a sheet of paper and a signature, and a thing that has to be done by moving a hundred and forty people down a bad road with about forty of them under about twenty takes nine weeks and about nine accidents.

Nobody in that room argued that a corridor was the wrong instrument. It is the right instrument. That is the whole of the moral shape of the whole of this and it has to be said in the plainest words available: it works, it is the fastest thing available, it is the cheapest thing available, about nine of the hundred and forty are children, and the city would have signed anything else anybody had brought it.

---

And then a man of fifty-two with a bit of lead in his pocket did a sum out loud in a room in front of about nine people who had to be told, and he did not do it to anybody, and he said so before he did it.

“I am not saying this to you,” he said, to the room. “There is a man in this room whose valley it is and I am not saying it to him and I am not saying it to the woman with the form and I am saying it in a room, in about nine seconds, and it is the same working I have printed in a yard at home twice and I am going to print it again because there is nobody in this room who has heard it and about nine of you are going to be asked to sign something.

“A bell is thirty-one pounds of metal at four shillings a pound, and that is one thousand four hundred and eighty-eight pence, and the frame and the rope is eleven shillings, which is a hundred and thirty-two, and one thousand four hundred and eighty-eight and a hundred and thirty-two is one thousand six hundred and twenty pence. And one thousand six hundred and twenty pence is six pounds and fifteen shillings, and that is a bell, hung, with a rope, and I did that sum in a yard in Coldharrow on the third day of the fifty-eighth week and I have printed it twice since and it has not moved.

“A keeper is ninepence a day. A hundred and forty days. That is one thousand two hundred and sixty pence. And one thousand two hundred and sixty pence is five pounds and five shillings, and the woman of forty-one at home has had that for twenty-five years and is paid the first of them and it is ninepence a day and she is the only one in eleven villages anybody can ask anything of.

“*And one thousand six hundred and twenty less one thousand two hundred and sixty is three hundred and sixty. And three hundred and sixty pence is a pound and ten shillings.*”

He put the lead down on the flat of a bench.

“That is a year,” he said. “Every year, for as long as it is rung, and for as long as somebody is alive to ring it. A bell is a keeper's year and a pound and ten, and that is the price of the thing, and I have now said it in three rooms in two counties and I am not going to say it in a fourth, and nobody is to thank me for it and nobody is to say I said it to them.”

---

And then Corvin Hale stood up in the middle of the floor of the room over the cart shed and said a thing out loud, in his own mouth, before anybody had asked him, and everybody in that room and about eighty people in a yard outside the door heard it, and nobody could stop him.

“Not that,” he said. “No. Wait.”

He held one hand up, and he did it the way a man does it in a yard, and there were about sixty people in a room and about eighty in a yard and it was the first time in nine years that anybody in that valley had held a hand up in front of both.

“The woman with the form has not asked me anything,” he said, “and she told me a day and a half ago that she was not authorised to describe the reason, and she told me this afternoon that she has changed her mind about wanting to know and would still rather I did not have it, and she said that in front of about sixty of you and she did not have to.

“*And I have been the one who is asked for in this valley for about nine years, and I have never once been asked what I am for, and there is a man four hundred miles off who walked into a yard of mine nine days ago and said that out loud before he said one word about anything else, and about four people in that yard understood it.*”

He put his hand down.

“There is an arrears. There have been arrears for about nine years and nobody in this valley can say in which year they started, and I cannot put a figure on them, and neither can anybody else, and the city has them and I have not got them and I am not going to guess.

“If we sign the corridor and do not contest it, the arrears are paid and a bell is hung. *A bell, hung, with a rope, is six pounds and fifteen, and a keeper is five pounds and five, and the difference is a pound and ten every year for as long as it is rung.* That man has just printed it in this room and he printed it at nobody, and I have heard it, and I am going to do it again because I am the one signing and a man who signs a thing he has not said out loud in a room is a man who is being asked to sign something by somebody else.”

And then he did it again, in his own mouth, in about nine seconds, with the working, and he got it exactly right, and he stopped.

“Six and fifteen. Five and five. One and ten,” said Corvin Hale. “That is it. That is the whole of what I have to say and it took me about nine years.”

Nobody said anything.

“I am signing it,” he said. “I am signing it now, before anybody has asked me to, and I want that in whatever you are keeping, and I want the water question answered separately and I want it answered in a sentence, and I am not signing the water. Nine of the hundred and forty of us are under nine and I have counted them twice this week because I am a man who counts things when I am frightened, and there is no road out of this valley that a cart can take and there is one road a man can take and about nine of them are not nine years old.

“And I want the last of it said out loud in this room and I want the woman of twenty-eight to write it down in whatever she writes in: *I have been asked for things in this valley for nine years and nobody has ever asked me what a corridor is, and I did not know what one was three days ago.*”

---

The woman of about fifty-six produced the instrument. There is a space at the foot of it about two inches long and it is the widest thing on the sheet, and it took him a while over it because he is a man of forty and not a clerk, and the man of fifty-two did not help him and the man of thirty-one did not help him and neither of them said a word, and about nine people in that room watched a man sign a thing that cannot be produced to anybody in this valley and about four of them understood that they had just watched that happen.

And in the middle of it, once, with the pen in his hand, two lines stood in front of the man of thirty-one in the flat white nothing that has put things in front of him in about two years, in a room over a cart shed in a valley four hundred miles off, saying one thing at most, in the language of a clerk, and deciding nothing.

**A CORRIDOR IS AN INSTRUMENT, AND AN INSTRUMENT IS PRODUCED IN A ROOM, AND THE PRODUCTION OF IT ASKS NOBODY IN THE PLACE IT IS ABOUT.**
**WHAT IS NOT DECIDED: WHETHER A PLACE THAT CANNOT BE NAMED ON A DAY CAN BE SET ASIDE BY ONE THAT CAN.**

Nothing came of it. It has never once taken anything out of him and it has never once put anything in.

---

And after it was signed he worked, because that is what he is, and he got one thing.

He asked, in about four minutes, in front of about sixty people and about eighty outside the door, for the corridor, the arrears, the bell and the water to be four separate things and not one, and he gave the woman of about fifty-six four sentences to refuse, and she refused three of them and allowed one, in about nine seconds, without any difficulty at all, and it was the water.

“The corridor does not touch the water,” she said. “It does not touch the water and it never did and nobody in the building that made this paper has ever thought about your water, and if you want to ask about the water there is a separate office and it is four hundred miles away and it is not me.”

That is the whole of what forty-four days got out of a room. One sentence, about a channel that belongs to a valley and not to a district.

“Thank you,” said Corvin Hale, to the woman of about fifty-six, and to nobody else, and he did not thank anybody else and nobody thanked anybody else.

And Corvin Hale said the last of his two days out loud to about sixty people and about eighty outside a door, and he said it to the man of thirty-four's sentence, which had been said in a yard in this valley nine hours before by a man of fifty-two and which he had been turning over since.

“Ours could be produced,” said Corvin Hale. “Yours has never been.”

And then he said, because he had a day and a half of it and because he is a man of forty with a valley and he does not waste a room:

“That is not a good answer and I know it is not, and I have been in this valley all my life and it is the only answer anybody in here has got, and I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to go and do my day, and if it is a bad argument I would rather it was a bad argument in a room than a good one in a lane where nobody hears anything.”

Nobody in that room was relieved. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was thanked. The corridor was not torn up and it was not going to be torn up, and the bell is owed and is not hung, and a keeper's year is five pounds and five shillings and the difference between a bell and a keeper's year is a pound and ten, and about nine people in a valley of a hundred and forty stood in a yard in the cold and found out about a thing they had not been asked about, and the price of that was paid and not by the man who paid it.
