# Chapter 277: The March Argues The Pressure Again

The sixth day of the sixty-first week went up thirteen on a board in Coldharrow, and the figure at the head of it at the second light was sixty-three thousand six hundred and eighty-four, and thirteen is the largest number that has gone onto that plank in about four years, and there is no column and there was never a column and nobody in two hundred and ten people has ever known what any of the other thirteen was for except the woman who put it there.

Thirteen on a plank, on the last day of a quiet six weeks, and a woman of forty-one stood at the foot of her own ladder with the cloth over her shoulder and told nobody what any of the thirteen was for, which is the one thing about that plank that has not changed in thirty-one years and which three strangers in a county of two hundred and ten people each took about four minutes to work out on their own.

What had come through were three men from off, up the lane rather than in on any road, and they had come up it for the door.

---

The carter said it first, and he said it differently from the way he had said it in this yard on the first day of the sixtieth week, and about four people in that yard heard the difference and one of them was the man of thirty-one.

“I am not going to say the word I said last time,” said Ferrow Hask. “I have said *use it* in this yard twice, to a wall and to nine people, and I meant it, and I have found out since what it costs, and I have done the sum in my head about nine times in twelve days and it is my wage and hers, and I am not going to stand in a yard and ask a woman of forty-one to be the price of a word again.

“So I am going to say the other thing and I have never said it. *I would give the whole of it up.* Nineteen years. The whole of the wanting. I have wanted this lane to be a road since I was nineteen and I would still say it to anybody. Give me a road that has a day on it and I will hand you the wanting back and I will not ask for the bar to be shut and I will not ask for a gate again in my life, and I would say that in this yard in front of the man of sixty-three who sells me nothing. *A road that has a day on it. I have been the man at that bar for nineteen years wanting a thing that cannot be dated, and a road that cannot be dated is a lane with more mud in it, and I want to say that out loud before somebody tells me I have been arguing for a gate instead.*”

He put his hand flat on the cart.

“And I found out the day before yesterday that the nothing at that bar is worth ten pounds ten shillings a year and that it is mine and it is nobody's, and I have not worked out what to do with that, and I have not slept, and I am not going to talk about it in a yard.”

---

And the woman of about sixty said the second one, and she had walked nine miles to be in that yard, and she was the only living person who had been standing on the strip when it was decided, and she had been the only one of that for nine years and was now the only one of two things.

“Take it out,” said Wenna Skell.

The yard went quiet in the way a yard does, and about nine people in it had heard her say it once before, on the first day of the sixtieth week, and had watched a man of thirty-one ask her why and had watched him stand there for the answer.

“I want to say the rest of it this time and then I am going to stop,” she said, “and I have thought about who I am saying it to and I have not worked out a better set of people, and I am not going to be helped with it.

“Nine years ago I could say where a boundary started. That was the thing that was mine. I said it in this yard twelve days ago and I said it well and I meant it and I was right. And then I was in a stair in Nettlebed with about nineteen other people and there were a hundred and forty-one names on a door, and a woman of fifty-one put her thumb on the last nine figures in a column, and about eleven of us looked down the list, and the fourth one from the bottom is my grandmother.

“I do not know that it is my grandmother. Nobody has told me. I have known it since the morning of the third day of that week and I have not told a soul and I am not going to be asked, and I am about sixty. *So you can all have that: there is not one thing I can say about this any more. There are two, and they are about a hedge and about a woman I buried, and they are both going when I am, and there is nobody coming.*”

She put her hand flat on the bar of the gate that is not shut, which was the nearest flat thing, and it is hers to do that and always has been.

“Take the door out,” she said. “Not the hedge. The door. Because a thing on the outside of a village is a thing you can be asked about, and I have had nine years of a thing I could say and I loved it, and I am not going to be one of about nine people in a lane deciding in the next ten years what four hundred years of a list of names is for, with one of us being fifty-one and one of us being sixty and the two of us being the only two who can say anything.”

---

And the woman of forty-one said the third one, and she had thought about it for two days, and it is the furthest she has ever got from the position she held on the first day of the sixtieth week, and it is not a reversal and she did not soften it.

“I said in this yard twelve days ago that I wanted the eleven families told,” said Ottily Roke. “I have not stopped wanting it and I have thought about it every day and I have not done anything about it and neither has he, and the reason has not changed, and the reason is that I cannot give eleven families a thing to be right about and neither can anybody else in this county, and I have now had thirty-one days to try and I have not found one.

“*And now I have got the other thing and I am going to be honest about it in front of nine people, and it took me two days and a woman of fifty-one to do it.* There are a hundred and forty-one names on a plank on the inside of a door and there is not one of them in this village's mouth. Not one. Nobody in two hundred and ten people has ever said any of those names out loud, and I could say them all, and I have not, and I have had two days.

“I wanted the eleven families told in the first day of the sixtieth week. I have wanted them told for nine years. I have been a person in a record for one morning, on the sixth day of that week, because a woman of twenty-nine at a press wanted a number and had nothing else, and I have told that yard and I am telling this one. *A name that is said out loud in a yard is a person in a room, and there is no room in this county, and a hundred and forty-one names is a hundred and forty-one people, and I will not do it, and I would rather be a coward about it for the rest of my life than be the woman who said a hundred and forty-one names out loud in a yard in Coldharrow.*”

Nobody in that yard argued with her and about four people in it were ashamed and about four were not, and the man of thirty-one was one of the four who were not, and he did not put his hands behind his back and he did not say one word, and he had a sentence available that would have moved the whole of it about four minutes' walk up the lane and he did not use it and about nine people in that yard could not have said why.

---

And then a woman of about fifty-one who had walked about four miles from Trope said the fourth thing, and it was not a position, and about nine people in that yard could not tell by the end of it whether she was for it or against it, and neither could he, and it took her about four minutes and she said it standing at the bar with a cold hand on the wood.

“You have all three of you said your three things and every one of them is right and every one of it is a price, and I have listened to all of it and I have got nothing to add to it and I am not going to try, and that is the first time in my life that has happened in a yard.

“But there is a fourth thing in this yard and it is not a price and it is the reason I walked into a yard I did not have to walk into, and none of the three of you has said it, and I do not think one of you is going to, so I am going to.

“That plank has been on the inside of that door since before the mill was built. It cost a hundred and forty-one people a bell each and about two hundred years of a wage that nobody was ever paid, and every one of those people said no out loud, on their own ground, in the open, and then they kept a list, and the list is four hundred years old and it is the only thing in this county that anybody has ever been asked to keep. *It is not ours. That is the whole of it and it is a plain thing and I cannot make it any plainer than that.*”

“That is not a thing anybody can say,” said the man of fifty-two.

“It is the only thing in this yard that is a thing,” said Isel Cardew. “You cannot use a thing that is not yours and you cannot expose a thing that is not yours and you cannot take a thing out that is not yours, and there are three men standing in a field at Trope this minute who have come four hundred miles to lay a thing in front of a room, and there is no room, and the reason there is no room is on the inside of a door in a village of about ninety people, and *I am not going to be the reason a hundred and forty-one people get read out because it would be tidier if they had been.*”

---

And the woman with the board said the thing nobody in that yard had asked her for, in about nine seconds, standing behind the three of them and not in front of any of them, and about four people heard it and it was the whole of what eleven weeks of her life had been for.

“I have been in this country about eleven weeks,” said Sable Venn, “and I have been in it nine years. I have carried about four hundred pieces of paper into about four hundred places and I have never once asked a single person in any of them what they wanted, and nobody has ever noticed that I did not, and one of the reasons nobody noticed is that not asking has worked four hundred times.

“I have not asked in eleven weeks. I would not have taken an answer to it. *The eleven villages have been deciding this for four hundred years without a road and a bell and a bench and they are not waiting, and there is nothing in that for me to carry, and I have been standing in this county since about the first week of my life treating it as a place where papers do not work, and papers work, and I have not asked, and I am not going to start asking now, and I would like that written down by a woman of forty-one because I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be on anything.*”

The woman of forty-one did not write it down and did not say she would and did not say she would not, and nobody in that yard was thanked, and the bell for the end of it went and was four more on a plank, and the day stood at thirteen with nothing whatever written beside the thirteen.

---

And the three of them were still in the field at Trope at about the ninth hour, and the clerk of forty-four went up and did the only thing anybody in this county is paid for, which is to tell three men what a document is, and he did it to them and not to the village, and it took about nine minutes and about four of them understood it.

“A document is a thing that can be produced,” said the clerk of forty-four. “That is all. It is not a true thing and it is not a false thing and it is not a fair thing and it is not a law. *It is a thing that can be produced, in a place, on a day, to a person.* And you three have come four hundred miles on a lane because somebody three hundred yards from this field wrote a sentence on a door, and the sentence is four hundred years old and it is not a document, and there is nobody in this county that it can be produced to, and the reason there is nobody is not that this county is poor. The reason there is nobody is that the people who would have been the room are on the list.”

The three of them said nothing, and one of them was a man of thirty-four who reads things out to about forty people a week and cannot read the thing he reads out, and he had walked four days, and he said, at the end of it, the only sentence any of the three of them said in this county:

“If it cannot be produced then it has not happened,” he said, “and I have been saying a version of that out loud for eleven years and I did not know that there was a door in this country with the opposite of it written on it.”

---

And at the last of the light the man of thirty-one and the man of fifty-two and the recordwright of twenty-eight and the clerk of forty-four came back down the lane into Coldharrow, and the man of fifty-two was carrying four sheets of paper he had cut himself with a knife out of the back of a bill because there is no paper in this county that anybody will write anything on, and he had cut nine of them and he gave four of them away, and what was on them was not a rule and not a request.

It was a list. And on the list there were eleven villages, and above the eleven, in a different hand, there were about forty more names of places that a man of fifty-two had got out of two carters and a miller and a woman of fifty-one in about four days, and beside the fifty-one places, in the same hand, there was nothing whatever at all, and that was the point of the whole of it.

“It is a list of places,” said Tarin Keel. “That is all. Any person of any country can walk into a yard and read it, and there is not one person in this county that is on it, including the four of us, including the woman of fifty-one, and it is not entered anywhere, and it is not owed to anybody, and it does not have a gate on it and it does not have a toll on it and it does not have a day on it and it is not a bench and it is not anybody's. *And there is a recordwright of twenty-eight standing here who has kept six years of other people's records, and I am not asking her to keep it and I am not asking her to be in it, and I would like it said out loud that I am not asking, in front of her, so that if she says no in a yard in about four days I cannot say I did not hear.*”

“It is a list,” said Sera Quill. “I will keep it and I will not be in it and those are two different sentences and I would like the second one written down as well as the first, and I am not being brave, I am being a recordwright, and a recordwright who keeps a thing and is in it has a thumb on the scale and I have watched it happen in about nine counties and I have never once watched a person put their thumb down on purpose and it is always the record that suffers and never the person.”
