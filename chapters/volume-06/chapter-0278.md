# Chapter 278: A Register Anyone May Read And Nobody May Enter

The seventh day of the sixty-first week was four bells and nothing on top of them, and the figure at the head of the board at Coldharrow was sixty-three thousand six hundred and ninety-seven at the second light, and the fourth day running of there being no column and no heading and no name of anybody's on anything anywhere in eleven villages.

The argument finished at Trope at about the fourth hour, in a yard, with about nine people in it, and the man of thirty-one was in the middle of it and was not asked a question for the fourteenth time in twenty-eight days, and what happened in that yard was not a decision and there is no form in eleven villages for what happened in that yard.

There were four sheets of paper on a cart's side. They had been cut out of the back of a bill with a knife about two days before, and there are four lines on each of them in a hand that has counted carts on roads for nineteen years and a second hand on the fourth that is a recordwright's and a third hand on the first, which is the hand of a woman of fifty-one who cannot write and put a cross against the six of them she had said out loud.

And there were fifty-one places on them.

Coldharrow, Ellerby, Trope, Nettlebed, Wrenny, Ashgill, Sallow, Cadgerow, Henthill, Birrow, and the one that has never been named in this county in twenty-eight days, and then five that came off the lane: Lythe, Ashcombe, Nether Wray, Pyle, and a place at the end of a bad road called Stennick that a carter of thirty-five had put on because he lives in it. And thirty-five more that a woman of fifty-one had got out of a miller and two carters and about nine conversations in a week, of which four had not been checked by anybody and are printed on the sheet with a cross against them, meaning that a person of that place said the word once and has not been asked twice.

Beside the fifty-one there was nothing at all. No column and no heading and no figure and no name, and four of the sheets are blank all the way down, and that is not an oversight and it is the whole of the design and the man of fifty-two said so about nine times in two days.

---

And the woman of fifty-one from Trope said the thing that is the reason the next two days are going to be the way they are, and she said it in a yard in her own village, and she said it to a woman of nine years' standing who is her country and not his and whom nobody had asked, and she said it in about eleven words and then went on for two minutes.

“You have thirty people in this country who will do what you tell them,” said Isel Cardew, to Sable Venn. “I do not mean thirty bad people and I do not mean they are yours. I mean thirty people who have been somewhere you were, and did what you said, and came back. And in eleven weeks you have not asked this village one single question about what it wants, and I have been watching you not ask since the third day of that week, and I would like it noticed that watching you not ask is the only work I have got and it is not much.”

“That is a thing you should not have said in a yard.”

“It is a thing I have said in a yard,” said Isel Cardew, “and it is a thing a person of this village says about a person of this country, and there is a woman of forty-one standing four feet away and she has heard both of us and neither of us is talking to her and that is also correct. Go on. I have not finished and I have not asked you anything and I am not going to.

“A thing that eleven villages decided four hundred years ago cannot be carried by one woman with people who follow her. That is all. It is not a thing about you and it is not about what you have done and I am not taking anything off you, and I am saying it because a register is a thing that gets carried, and if one person is carrying it then it is a party, and a party in a lane of about nine hundred people is a thing that happens in about four years, and I have watched one happen in my mother's time and it did not cost anybody a bell.”

She stopped, and about four people in that yard looked at the woman of nine years' standing, and the woman of nine years' standing spoke before anybody had put the question to her, which is the fact and the only fact anybody in that yard will remember about the whole of it.

“That is right,” said Sable Venn. “And I agree with it, and I have not been asked, and I would have said it in about four days and I am glad I have not had to sit on it. I have got about thirty people and about four hundred pieces of paper and nine years of a way of doing things that has never once needed me to ask anybody anything, and it is the best method I have ever had and it has just been described to me in eleven words by a woman of fifty-one in a yard and I am not going to argue with it, and I am not going to be thanked, and I am not going to be relieved, because I am not relieved. I am on a lane in a country I was born in and I have been here about eleven weeks and I have just been told what I am for, by somebody I would have carried a piece of paper for nine years without asking her a question.”

She put her hands behind her back, and about four people in that yard had seen the man of thirty-one do it about four times in a week and had not seen anybody else in the county do it, and about two of them noticed.

“I am not going to start asking now,” she said. “I have said that I will not and I am not. There are fifty-one places on four sheets of paper and not one of the eleven villages on this lane is in the register, and that is right, and it is going to stay right until a village says a sentence in its own yard and nobody has said one, and I have been here about eleven weeks and I have not earned the right to be asked to do anything at all.”

---

And the man of fifty-two read the rules out of his own list, and they are eleven lines, and about nine people in a yard heard them, and they are the whole of what the thing is and they were written by a man who counts carts and are not good and are enough.

“Any person of any country may walk into any yard in any of the fifty-one places and read all four sheets,” he said. “That is the first rule and nobody may stop them and nobody has to be told that they are allowed, and I have walked into about nine yards in two days and read a list out loud in them and nobody has asked me a single question about it, and one of them gave me a cup of something.

“The register is a second sheet and it is in a book and the book is at Coldharrow-edge on a shelf in the one room in eleven villages, and the room belongs to a woman of twenty-nine who sells days, and she is not letting it and there is no figure on it and she is not charging anybody and she has not been asked what it costs her, and I have not asked her either. *She is keeping the register. She is not in it. And a recordwright of twenty-eight is keeping the register. And she is not in it.*”

“The clerk of forty-four is not in it,” said Sera Quill, “and he has not asked and I have not told him, and he is the only person in this county that has read the four words at the top of that plank out loud in a yard and I have not put him on a sheet and he is not going to ask me why.”

“Nobody may go in the register without a sentence said by a person of that village in that village,” said Tarin Keel. “Not asked for. Not sent for. Said. And I write down what was said and nothing else, and I do not write who said it, and if a village says nothing then nothing goes in it, and that is not a rule I invented in this yard, that is the two hundred words in a kitchen ten days ago with a woman of sixty reading out a copy in her own hand, and I did not write them either and I am not going to be the man who improved on them.”

---

And then the man of fifty-two read the rest of the eleven lines off the cart's side, and it took about a minute, and they are the half of it that everybody in that yard came to hear and that is the half the register is not.

“There is no gate on it,” he said. “There is no toll on it and there is no bar and there is nothing to be shut and nothing to be opened. *There is no day on it.* There is no date, and I want that said, because I have watched about eleven people in the last three days hear the word register and put a day in it in their heads, and there is not one, and there cannot be one, and a date on that book would be a lie about four hundred years and about two hundred years and about a week.

“There is no bench on it. There is no seat and no chair and no room with a door on it except the one it is standing on a shelf in, and that room is a press and a woman of twenty-nine is selling days out of it and that is the whole of the government of it. There is no vote, and there is no meeting, and there is no officer, and there is no money in it anywhere, and I have cut it out of the back of a bill. There is not one name of the four of us on any of the four sheets, and there is not one name of yours, and the eleven villages on this lane are not in it and have not asked to be, and the forty places that are not villages are in it because a person of that place said one word once in a lane to a carter or a miller and has not been asked a second time, and I would like that written down as it is and not as eleven villages and forty others, because the eleven are the only ones in that list that anybody in this yard can check.”

He put the lead down.

“That is the whole of it,” he said. “There is no more and there is not going to be any more, and I have got a rule on a string and I have got a list of fifty-one places and a woman of fifty-one who cannot write and a recordwright of twenty-eight who is not in it, and that is the entire machinery of a thing that four hundred years of this lane has been doing without any of it.”

---

And the man of thirty-one was in that yard and had been in it for about an hour and had not spoken once, and about four people had noticed that he had not spoken once, and one of them was the clerk of forty-four, who said so at the end of it in about nine words and to nobody in particular and in a tone of a man remarking on the weather.

“You have not said anything for an hour,” said the clerk of forty-four, “and you have got a way of putting two things next to each other, and there is nothing on four sheets of paper that anybody would call yours, and I have been in this county nine days and I have watched you not do it about nine times, and I am a clerk and I am the wrong man to say this and I am going to say it and then I am going to walk down the lane. *A register is a document. It is the first document in this county that is not a count and not a name, and the only two instruments you have ever brought to this place are a piece of paper you could not read and a piece of paper you could not produce, and you have been here twenty-eight days and you have got four sheets cut out of a bill.*”

“That is the whole of what I have got,” said the man of thirty-one.

“It is more than you had on your first morning,” said the clerk of forty-four, “and it is not yours, and I did not come four hundred miles to hand you a county, and I have not got one, and I have not got a good name to spend on it either, and if you turn up in a room in a valley in a year with a document that says these people are an authority, I will be the man who reads it out and I will have a bad afternoon and I will have earned it.”

And then he went down the lane, and nobody stopped him, and about nine people in that yard stood in it, and nobody thanked anybody, and the man of thirty-one was not thanked and was not on anything and had not been asked to be on anything and had not offered and was not going to be asked again about it, and his name is not on the four sheets and there is no space for it and there is no dash either, and a man of fifty-two cut them out of the back of a bill this morning and there was no name at the foot of that bill either.

---

And then a carter of thirty-five who lives at the end of a bad road called Stennick put his hand on the fourth sheet and said the thing that turned the eighth day of that week sideways, and he had come up nine miles to be in a yard he had heard about from a woman of fifty-one and he said it before anybody had asked him anything at all.

“I have got a lane,” he said. “It is not on the list. It is not on your eleven and it is not on my one. It goes out of the sixth house and it goes past nine farms and it comes in at a place where there is a well and a roof that fell in, and I have walked it about forty times in nine years and I could not tell you where it goes to and I have never met anybody who has.”

He put his finger on the sheet.

“That one is not the only one of those in this county and we have all three of us known it for a week. *And a road that has a day on it is a thing that can be produced, and two lanes with no day on them are two things that can be produced together, and if you put them together then the two men at the end of them find out about each other, and I know that, because a man of thirty-one has said it out loud in a yard in this county and I have been in every yard he has been in for a week.*”

Nobody said anything.

“So do not,” said Ferrow Hask.
