# Chapter 286: Contested From Inside It, And She Is Not The Villain Of It

It was a market on the first day of the sixty-third week, and four of the eight on the plank at Coldharrow were the clock and four of them were the market, and the woman of about fifty had nine loads through her bar between the second hour and the noon bell at threepence a load, and she called the count off her own board with the word at the top of it, and the figure at the head of the wall had been sixty-three thousand seven hundred and fifty-one when the cloth came off it.

The yard filled up about the fourth hour. There were about nine of them at the cart's side and about eleven inside the gate and the rest of two hundred and ten people were at a market, and the market is the one day in seven when anything at all can be said in Coldharrow to more than nine people, which is why about nine people in a yard is the biggest room in this county and everybody knows it.

The man of about thirty-one who owns the tower at Nettlebed and makes hurdles and gates had come down the lane with a hurdle frame on his shoulder to sell it, which he does four times a year, and had put it down against the wall about nine feet from the cart, and he had not come to say anything and he said something.

---

He said it to the woman with the board and not to the man of thirty-one, and about four people in that yard understood the whole of it in about nine seconds and about nine understood about half of it.

“I have been in this county all my life,” he said, “and I have watched you go up lanes with papers in a bag for about eleven weeks, and I have watched about thirty people do what you tell them, and I am not saying a word against any of them, and I am a person of a village and you are a person of this country, and I want to say one thing and then I am going to sell my hurdle.

“A hundred and forty-one people said no about four hundred years ago in eleven villages and kept a list of themselves, on the inside of a door, and one of those eleven villages is mine and the door is four miles from my gate. *And what they did not do was find somebody to carry it. What they did was each of them stand in their own yard and say it themselves, one at a time, over about four hundred years, and the list is the list of the people who did the saying and not of the people who organised it.*

“You are not doing that. You are carrying it. And about thirty people carrying one woman’s sentence is the same shape as a room with one person in it deciding what the rest of them are for, and it has got a different name on it, and I have read the name on it and I do not think anybody in this yard would use a different one.”

---

He put his hand on the hurdle frame.

“And I am not asking you to stop,” he said. “I have watched you for eleven weeks and you have not stopped and I do not think you are going to, and I am a man who sells four hurdles a year and I am not going to tell a woman with nine years of a bag what to do with it. And I am not asking for it to be led by somebody else either, because I have been in this country all my life and I have watched one thing get led by somebody else and it did not cost anybody a bell and it did not cost anybody anything that anybody could point at.”

“I am the person of a village and I have said my piece in a yard on a market day, which is the only kind of yard there is. *That is all of it. Now take my hurdle.*”

---

Nobody in that yard had asked him and about nine of them had heard him and about four had already worked out that the sentence he had said was going to take something off a woman who had already had one thing taken off her in a yard at Trope about a week ago by a woman of fifty-one.

The woman with the board had not said anything for about four seconds, and then she said nine words, and she said them before anybody had put the question to her, and that is the fact and it is the only fact about the whole of that morning that anybody in that yard will still have in a year.

“That is right,” said Sable Venn, “and I have thirty people, and here is what I will do with them.”

---

And then she said the rest of it, and it took about four minutes, and it is the whole of the thirty-sixth day, and she was not looking at the man of thirty-one at any point in it and he was nine feet away with a bit of lead in his hand that he did not use.

“I am not going to be relieved and I am not going to be thanked, and I have said the first of those once already in this county and I am not going to say it twice because it does not improve. A hundred and forty-one people each said no in their own yard. I have been in eleven villages in eleven weeks and I have said no in about four of them, and I said it in a kitchen and in a lane and not once in a yard, and nobody noticed that until a man with a hurdle said it just now, and he is right.

“*He is right and I have known it since a woman of fifty-one said it to me in a yard at Trope about a week ago in about eleven words, and I agreed with her before she had finished, and I have been carrying that agreement for a week without doing one single thing about it, and that is the whole of what I have done in this county since the third day of that week.*”

She shifted the bag on her hip.

“Now. Thirty people. Nine of them would go up a lane with me tomorrow and about nine of them would not and I have never once counted which, and a week ago I would have said thirty, and this morning I have said thirty in front of nine people and I have had to stop and think about it and the thinking took about four seconds and about four people in this yard watched me do it.

“What I am going to do with them is the thing I have done for nine years, and I am going to go on doing it, and it is this. *I am going to go up lanes on my own and put a paper in front of a person who has never had one, and I am not going to ask them what they want, because asking is not in me any more and it would take a year to get it back and I have four hundred places to go to.* And that is not a thing I am going to stop, and it is not a thing that man has asked me to stop, and it is the same shape as the door in that tower and he is right about that, and I am going to keep doing it.

“And what has changed is not the carrying. *What has changed is that I know what it is.* For nine years I did not know whether a thing was being carried or a person was being told, and I could not find out and I did not want to, and a woman of fifty-one found it out in about eleven words in a yard in front of nine people and a man with a hurdle has now said it out loud in front of about nine more, and about four hundred places are going to get a paper from me in the next year and every one of them is going to get it from a woman who now knows what she is doing, and that is the whole of what has been taken off me and nothing has been taken off my bag.”

---

Nobody thanked anybody, and about four people in that yard understood the whole of what had been said and about nine understood about half of it, and the man of thirty-one was one of the four and said nothing at all for about an hour, and a man of fifty-two noticed that and said so afterwards in about nine words to nobody in particular, which is a thing he does about nine times a fortnight.

He did not take anything off her. He was not asked to and he did not offer and there was no form in eleven villages that says a man may take anything off anybody, and he has been in this country thirty-six days and has not found one and is not going to.

---

And two things happened after that which are not part of it and are part of it.

The first was that two crosses came off the first sheet.

The man of fifty-two had a rule on a string round his neck and four sheets cut out of the back of a bill, and thirty-five of the fifty-one places on them had a cross against them meaning that a person of that place had said the word once to a carter or a miller and had not been asked twice, and on the first day of the sixty-third week two of those crosses came off, and both of them came off because a person of that place was in that yard on a market day and said the thing out loud.

One of them was a place at the end of a bad road called Stennick with nine farms on it and a well and a roof that fell in, and the person of it was a carter of thirty-five who had put his own lane on the fourth sheet about a week ago and had asked for the two lanes to be put side by side, and he came down that lane on the morning of the market and said his piece and it was about nine words long and it was this: *I would rather not be on a list than not be asked, and both of those are true of me, and the second one is truer, and you can take the cross off and I would be obliged.*

The other one was a mill. Nobody asked the miller's boy about it; the miller himself was in that yard and he said, in about four seconds, that he had said the word once to a carter about ten years ago and had not thought about it since, and that he would rather it was off, and the man of fifty-two took the lead out of his pocket and did not put it on anything and said that was the correct and that it was the rule and that a cross is not an entry.

“A cross is not an entry,” he said, to about nine people. “*A place is in this book when a person of that place says a sentence in that village, and a sentence is a thing said in a yard, and a word said once to a carter in a lane is not a sentence and I put it there because forty places are not villages and I did not want the sheet to look like nothing. And a cross comes off the moment anybody in that place says anything at all, and two of them have, and neither of them was asked first.*”

So the book on the shelf in the press at Coldharrow-edge was one place smaller than it had been the day before, and about thirty people are still in the movement, and one of them is a woman who has said out loud in a yard on a market day what she is for the first time in nine years and has said it into a market yard and not into a village yard and there is no rule that says she had to do it in a village and there is no rule that says she did not.

---

And the second thing was that the man of fifty-two said he was going to walk the whole length of the lane in the morning with a cart and a barrow and report what was at the end of it, and about nine people in that yard understood that nobody had ever done it, and about four of them understood that it was the gate and that the gate had been the first of the four things on a piece of paper out of the back of a bill for three days, and that a bar which is not shut stands at the end of a lane and not at the end of a road, and that if there is nothing at the end of the lane then the bar is the gate of a lane, and that a lane does not have a gate, and that the whole of a carter’s nineteen years is standing at the end of that sentence.

He said it at about the ninth hour and he said it the way he says a thing he has already done the sum on twice.

The last bell went and the day stood at eight, which is four of the clock and a market, and the woman of forty-one put the eight up and did not know that four of the eight were a market and there is no column on that plank and there never was one and nobody in two hundred and ten people has ever asked her.
