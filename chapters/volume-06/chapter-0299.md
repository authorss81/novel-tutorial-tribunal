# Chapter 299: The Resolution, And It Is Worse For Him Than The Corridor Would Have Been

There was a debt called in at the bar at Coldharrow on the seventh day of the sixty-fourth week, and there was a fire at the fourth house that was out before anybody got to it, and the two of them were four, and the head of the plank at Coldharrow read sixty-three thousand eight hundred and thirty-three at the second light.

The fire is worth a paragraph of its own and nobody in two hundred and ten people will ever know about it again, and it is the last and best thing the whole fifty days has found out about a bell.

A bell is rung twice for a fire. One for the alarm and one for the putting out, and the second one is rung whether or not it is out. So a fourth house with a thatch and a spark got two rings at about the fourth hour, and by the time nine people had crossed the yard with buckets the thatch was cold and had been cold for about a minute, and the second ring went anyway, because that is what the second ring is for, and about four people in that yard stood there with a wet bucket and understood what had just happened and about five did not.

*And the second of those two was a stroke on a plank for a thing that did not happen.*

A man of fifty-two said the whole of it out loud in that yard in about nine words, and then said that he was not going to say any more about it in a yard, and then said one more thing, which was that the woman of forty-one at the board had rung both of them and knew, and would know for the rest of her life, and would not be able to prove one stroke of it to anybody in a room, because there is no room.

She has said in about nine yards in seven weeks that the day the figure leaves the board is a figure somebody can use, and she has never once said a figure about a thing that did not happen, and she is the only person in eleven villages who could.

---

She read it out in a yard, standing up, in about four minutes, and about nine people heard the whole of it and about four of them understood the whole of it, which is the same ratio as every other reading anybody has done in this county in seven weeks.

A recordwright of twenty-eight read a status out loud in a yard. That is the sentence and nobody in eleven villages has ever done that before, and the two of them together are the whole of what fifty days of this produced.

And when it was finished, about nine people in that yard waited for somebody to say where the day was.

Nobody said it. About four of them wanted to. Nobody can say where the day is, and the day is not on the sheet, and there is no box on the sheet for it, and there is no form in eleven villages that has a place for it, and nobody in that yard could have named a day if they had been asked and about four of them understood why they could not, and the reason is the whole of seven weeks and it is nine words long in a mouth.

---

And the man of thirty-one said the thing nobody in that yard could say, and he said it standing up, in about nine seconds, because he says things out loud now and because a week ago a woman of forty-one in a valley four hundred miles off told him that a word is not a person and he has not put anything behind that.

“A corridor has a day in it and this has not,” he said. “And I want the second half of that said out loud in a yard in front of about nine people, because everybody in this yard is about to think the one without the day is the smaller thing, and it is not, and I have been working on it for seven weeks and this is what I have got.

“*A status with no day in it is not a lesser thing than a corridor with a day in it. It is the same paper with the day left out on purpose.* And a corridor with a day in it can be produced in a room in about nine minutes, and so can that, and the difference between the two of them is not the strength of the paper. The difference is that a corridor had one name at the foot of it and this has got nobody's.

“*And that is the whole of what four hundred years of a list on the inside of a door was for.* A hundred and forty-one names, four hundred years old, in a hand somebody can date, and no date on it, and no signature, and a column with a figure against every name and nothing whatever over the column, and nobody ever added it up, twice refused out loud on purpose, because a column with nothing over it is not a total. *That is not a charter. That is a piece of paper that cannot be produced to anybody, about a hundred and forty-one people who each said no in their own yard, and it has outlasted every room I have ever been in.* And the whole of what it was for is the sentence I have just said: the day is left out on purpose.”

About four people in that yard understood all of it, and nobody thanked him, and one of the four was a recordwright of twenty-eight who had kept her eyes off him for a week and who turned them on him and off him again inside two seconds.

---

It is worse for him than the corridor would have been, and there is no way of putting that in a yard, and the yard did not try.

A corridor is a thing with a shape. You can point at it. It has one name at the foot of it, and the name belongs to a man of forty who is alive in a valley four hundred miles off and who can be asked about every stroke of it for the rest of his life, and that is a horrible thing and it is at least a thing you can hold up.

A status is a page of things nobody can do. It does not have a gate in it and it does not have a toll in it and it does not have a bench in it and it does not have a date in it and it does not have a vote in it and it does not have a meeting in it and it does not have an officer in it and there is no money in it anywhere, and it cost a man of fifty-two a knife and a sheet out of the back of a bill.

It does not stop anything. It cannot stop anything. It can be produced in a room in about nine minutes, exactly like the corridor can, and a room four hundred miles off that has never heard of it will read it out in the ordinary way and enter a line about it in a book kept in a building with a yard in it, and nobody will ever be asked to look at what that line does afterwards, because a clerk of forty-four said so in a letter and he is four hundred miles off and is not coming back, and nobody in a building with about thirty clerks in it has ever once been asked what a form does after it is entered.

A man of fifty-two said that out loud in about nine words in a yard, standing beside a cart, with a bit of lead in his pocket, and he was not defending anything and he was not asking anybody to give him a better one, and about four people in that yard understood that he was describing the sheet he had cut out of the back of a bill with his own knife three weeks ago.

“That is the whole of what a corridor costs and this is the whole of what a sheet costs, and they are not the same size, and neither of them is worth what anybody paid for it,” he said. “And I have been printing working in yards in this county since the third day of the fifty-eighth week and I have never once said that about a thing I did with my own hands, and I am not going to improve on it, and I would like somebody to be annoyed about it and not me.”

---

Nobody was relieved. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was redeemed. Nobody was thanked.

The bargain is still standing. It was not torn up, and nobody in two hundred and ten people or in a valley of a hundred and forty has tried, and there is no seam in it and nothing written on the back of it and nothing to appeal to, and a bell is owed and unpaid, and the price of a bell, hung, with rope, is six pounds fifteen against a keeper's year at five pounds five, which leaves one pound ten standing between them year on year, and nobody in this county has three pounds and sixpence let alone six pounds and fifteen.

And the right of refusal is not restored and nobody has one.

That is worth saying out loud in a yard in about nine words, because somebody will say it in a lane within a month and it will be wrong, and a woman of fifty-one from Trope said it first and in about nine words and she was not asked.

“A permission with no second half, four hundred years old, on a door, is a permission with no second half, four hundred years old, on a door,” said Isel Cardew. “*Nobody in this lane can refuse one single thing today that they could not refuse seven weeks ago. Nobody in that valley could refuse the corridor either. I have been in two counties in eight days and I have found about nine people who thought they had got something back and every one of the nine is wrong, and I have not corrected one of them, because the correcting would be the same as the having.*”

And she said the second half of it, and it is the finding and not an opinion.

“*What a sheet can do and a door cannot, and what a corridor can do and a sheet cannot, and nobody has written either of those down anywhere, and I have got a gate bar at home that has hung open my whole life and it is going to hang open next week as well.*”

---

And at about the last of the light a woman of about forty who carries other people's paper between eleven villages, and has carried it for nineteen years and has never once opened any of it, put a barrow in the lane with one bundle on it, and would not say where it had come from, and would not say what was in it, and nobody asked her either of those things and about four people in a yard noticed that nobody had.

Thirty-one days ago a woman with a board had put a piece of paper in a satchel in a kitchen in a village nine miles up the lane and had it carried out of this county at a gate that is not shut, and it had come back down the lane with a figure at the foot of it in a hand that is not any hand in eleven villages.

Eleven days ago a clerk of forty-four four hundred miles off had written four lines and told the yard not to write back, and it had come up the lane on a barrow by a carrier who would not say where it had come from, and about nine people in a yard had heard it read out by two people who had not been in the room where it was written.

And on the seventh day of the sixty-fourth week, a third thing went out of this county, and the woman of forty who carried it did not open it, and she has never opened anything, and she did not ask what was in it, and nothing of the kind exists in this county to be done anything about, and nobody in this county is going to do anything about any of it.

Nobody in that yard knew what was on it and about four of them had a fair idea and one of the four was a recordwright of twenty-eight who is not in the register and keeps it and has not been thanked.

And the man of thirty-one is on none of those three routes and put no name to any of them and is not going to be, and he has been in this country forty-nine days, and he has a good name of nobody's to spend, and a leg that is the leg, and a thing in a bag that is off nothing, and a figure in chalk on a wall he is not going to wipe off, and a figure in pencil on a sheet he is not going to take back, and a barrow went out of the county with three days of somebody's life on it and he was not asked and did not offer.
