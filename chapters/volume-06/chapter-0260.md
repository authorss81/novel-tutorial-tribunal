# Chapter 260: The Copy Arrives

The third day of the fifty-ninth week added five to a board in a country where every day adds four, and the fifth was a burial, and the man of thirty-one was in a yard at the second light for the express purpose of not being the person who says anything.

The satchel was under the seat of a timber cart two hundred yards down the lane and the man who had brought it was asleep in a cart-shed with his boots off, and he was about fifty-one and he was a sawyer and he came from about nine days on this road, and he had been in Coldharrow four hours.

They did not frighten him, and they did not take the satchel off him, and they did not ask him anything at all, and it took about a minute and a half of standing in a shed door to get him up and get him to the yard with the bag still on his own arm, and a woman of about forty with a handcart, who had come in behind the timber, stood at the back of the yard the whole time and did not come forward, and it was not that she was hiding.

“You're Coldharrow-edge,” he said, to Ottily Roke, and then stopped, and worked backwards through the yard, and said: “No. You're the board. You're the one that was in the market town.”

“Yes.”

“There's a seat in the market town,” he said, “with a river and a gate on it, and a man came in with a satchel and a dog and he wanted a bed and there was a boy of about seven in the yard who ran off with the dog and got the mud on the girl.”

“That was eight weeks ago,” said Ottily Roke. “And I have not thought about it once since the following morning, which is a thing you should not tell anybody, and I have thought about it about four times since the second of this week.”

He put the satchel on a cart's side, because everybody in this country puts things on a cart's side, and did not open it, and looked at it.

“It's paper,” he said. “It's been paper since I had it. There's four lines on it and it's sealed, and I was given five shillings for it and four days, and I did not know what it was, and I was told it was a thing to carry and not a thing to read, and I have carried it four hundred miles and I have not read it and I am not going to read it now.”

He looked at Sable Venn for about four seconds.

“I do not know you,” he said. “And you will not tell me I do not know you, and I am not asking you to.”

“All right,” said Sable Venn. “You do not know me. That is correct and I have known it for about eight weeks and I did not know it until this morning and I am not going to argue about it in a yard.”

---

“It is a copy of an order,” said the man of thirty-one, “and it is sealed, and it is four lines, and it is about four hundred miles from here. It is not about this county and it does not name this county. *Nobody in this yard has to decide anything about it today and nobody is going to be asked to.*

“Who will read it out?”

He asked it in the way he asks for things, which is the way he has asked for everything for about a month, in every yard and every room and every lane, and it was read out, and nobody in the yard said one word about the asking.

It was the woman of forty-one who read it, because she was the only one of the four in that yard who had the day in her hand, and a boy of seventeen stood about nine feet off and could have read it too and did not offer and was not asked, and a woman of twenty-nine at the back of the yard with a handcart did not move and was not asked, and it is worth saying that both of those were choices and not absences and that neither of them made a sound.

> ONE. THE ORDER OF THE WATER-SEAT OF SALLYBY OF THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH DAY STANDS, AND NO PERSON IN THAT BASIN IS TO DISPUTE IT.
>
> TWO. THE SCHEDULE NAMED IN IT IS THE SCHEDULE NAMED IN IT, AND A SCHEDULE IS NOT A RIVER, AND NO PERSON IS TO TREAT THE SCHEDULE NAMED IN IT AS A RIVER.
>
> THREE. THE HEIGHT NAMED IN IT IS ELEVEN FEET, AND NO SILL IS NAMED IN IT, AND A HEIGHT WITH NO SILL NAMED IN IT IS A HEIGHT ABOVE WHATEVER THE READER IS STANDING ON.
>
> FOUR. THE PARTY NAMED IN IT SHALL PAY THE COST NAMED IN IT AT THE CLOSING OF EVERY YEAR UNTIL THIS MATTER IS REVIEWED, AND THE REVIEW IS NOT APPOINTED.

She read it once and then she read it again, and on the second reading she gave the eleven feet its own beat, the way somebody reads a number out to a person who is going to be asked about it later and has not been told yet that they are; and she had been given that habit in a market town four hundred miles off eight weeks ago by a man who had nothing in his hands, and she had been told about it since, and she had never worked out why he had wanted it.

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes to cross a yard, and it is worth being exact about what happened next, because a great deal rides on it and this is the only part of this that four hundred miles from here anybody will ever be told about.

Nobody said no.

Nobody said yes. Nobody put it down. Nobody carried it to a room. Nobody rang a bell about it, and there was no bell in this country that anybody could have rung about it, and that is the entire reason nothing happened on the third day of the fifty-ninth week in Coldharrow, and it is not a decision anybody took.

The woman of forty-one folded it once along the seal and put it back in the satchel and gave the satchel back to the man of fifty-one, and he put it under the seat of his own cart, where it had been for four hundred miles, and where it will be on the day it goes.

---

“It is not about us,” said Ferrow Hask.

“No,” said the man of thirty-one. “It is not. I want to be very clear about that, because in about a week there is going to be a person in this county who says it is about us, and they will be right in a way that makes it useless to say it is not.

“Here is the whole of it, and it is not a thing I have thought of, it is a thing I have known since a market town eight weeks ago, and the woman of twenty-nine told me the price of it on the sixth day of this week without being asked.

“The second line of that paper says a schedule is not a river. And that is the whole of what the paper is for. A schedule is when something happens, and a river is where it goes. And a bell is a schedule. *The four bells of a Coldharrow day are four bells and they happen and they go nowhere.* The first bell is a height above nothing, and the last is a height above the first, and nobody in eleven villages has ever said what either one is above, and there is not a thing in this country that would tell you, and there is no river in this county that anybody in a market town eight days of this road has ever seen.

“And the third line says a height with no sill named in it is a height above whatever the reader is standing on. And that is a true sentence. It is a completely true sentence. It is a printed rule and it is about nine years old and it is the rule a bench of four works under, and they have it in four hundred and sixty lines of a printed index under a heading, and it is the single most correct sentence any of us have read in nine days.

“And it is a sentence that means that a thing four hundred miles away can be true about a man in this yard, and there is no way on this earth to hand it back.”

The recordwright of twenty-eight said the thing that the man of thirty-one had not said and could not have said, because she had spent nine days on it and he had spent nine minutes on it.

“It is load-bearing here,” she said, “and that is the whole of it, and you have had it backwards and I have had it backwards and the woman with the board has had it backwards since the day she copied it.

“In that valley it was a curiosity. Four lines about a dam, in a valley of about four hundred and ten people, with a good name already spent by a man of thirty-one. Nobody was hurt by it here.

“Here it is a sentence about a height, and the only public thing anybody in this county has ever had is a height that is a height above nothing, and a height above nothing is a height above *whatever is standing nearest*. And what is standing nearest to a bell in a village of two hundred and ten people is two hundred and ten people. It is not a curious paper in this county. It is a lever with our names nowhere on it.”

And a woman of forty-one said, out loud, in a yard, to about nine people, the thing nobody had asked her for:

“No,” she said. “It is not. I am not going to have it in this village and I am not going to have it on my board and I have not written one word of it in chalk and I am not going to, and you can go and ask the man of thirty-one whether I have, and he will tell you that the last thing he wrote in chalk on anything is a number.”

“He did not write it,” said the man of thirty-one, “and he did not want to, and he had already worked out about four hours ago that he was not going to, and he had not said so out loud, and that is the only thing he has done in nine days that he would call work.”

---

So it ended, and nothing was decided, and here is the whole of what Coldharrow had at the last bell on the third day of the fifty-ninth week: a board with three hands in it, thirty-one years old, a figure of fifty thousand four hundred and thirty-four at the second light that morning and fifty thousand four hundred and thirty-nine going on for tomorrow, four bells, a woman of forty-one, a boy of seventeen with a book, a woman of twenty-nine at a back gate who had not moved, a woman with a board, a carter of fifty-one with a satchel under a seat that was going to be under a seat on the day it goes, and a piece of paper about a river that had come into a country where there is no river at a cost of fourpence, and which nobody in two hundred and ten people had done anything about.

And a man of thirty-eight stood at the end of a timber cart and asked the last question of the first ten days of this country, and he asked it in a yard at the last bell, and it was twenty-five words and he had had them ready for nineteen years, and nobody in the yard answered him and he did not appear to want one.

“It does not run north to south, it runs from village to village, and I have never found out who laid it out that way.”
