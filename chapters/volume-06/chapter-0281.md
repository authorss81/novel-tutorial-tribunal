# Chapter 281: The Four Disputes, And None Of Them Is A Border

A man of fifty-two came into the yard at Coldharrow with a fifth sheet of paper under his arm, and there were nine sheets cut out of the back of a bill in this county and he had given four of them away on the sixth day of the sixty-first week and had never given anybody one since, and the fifth was the last of them and he was holding it the way a man holds a thing he has already decided he is going to be wrong about.

The day was going to be seven, and the figure at the head of the board on the tower wall said sixty-three thousand seven hundred and fourteen when the woman of forty-one wiped the cloth over it, and it was the thirty-first morning of a man of thirty-one in this country. A clerk of forty-four had gone down the lane on a cart two days before to a town that keeps weeks he has never learned, and he would be four hundred miles off in about eight more days, and nobody had sent him back and nobody was going to.

---

The yard was empty at that hour except for the two of them and a woman of about fifty who was going round eleven bars twice a week and had a board under her left arm with four letters and a stroke at the top of it, and she was not asked anything and said nothing, which is the way that yard works.

“Four things,” said Tarin Keel.

He put the sheet on the cart's side and weighted it with a piece of the old paving, and there were four lines on it, one under the other, and no number against any of them and nothing at the top of it and nothing at the foot.

“A bar across the end of this lane that is not shut and takes threepence a load off anybody who comes through it, and there is no road on the other side of it for a gate to be the gate of, and nobody in two hundred and ten people has ever asked what it is for.”

“Second.” He put a finger under the second line. “A lane that runs from village to village and does not go by the ground, and no man's cart would choose it, and nobody in this county can say who laid it out.”

“Third.” “A hundred and forty-one names on the inside of a door in a tower in a village that has had no bell in it for about two hundred years, with a figure against every one of them and no heading over the column, and nobody in two hundred and ten people knows what any of the figures counts.”

“Fourth.” “Ninety acres between this bar and a bar nine miles up the lane that eleven families have been grazing for nine years and paying nothing for, and there is a walk on a board at Coldharrow from a morning in the twenty-second year of that board that says a man went along it, and the man is four years dead.”

He took his hand off the paper.

“Now say the thing about them that is true and that I have not said, because if I say it you will all go off and do something and I do not want anybody to do anything.”

---

“Not one of them is a border,” he said.

He said it the way he says a thing he has been carrying a long way, and about nine people were in the yard by then and four of them understood it immediately and the rest did not.

“A border has two sides. A border is the only kind of line in the world that somebody on each side can point at. *Every one of these four has eleven villages on one side of it and nobody at all on the other, and the one that looks most like a border is the ninety acres, and it is the one nobody can draw, because the man who drew it is dead and the board he would have told you about has got nothing in it that says what any stroke of it was for.*”

He picked the paving up off the cart's side and turned it over in his hand.

“I have put four things on a piece of paper in the order they came up and not in the order they matter, and I have not numbered them, and I have not written one word at the top, and about four of you are going to ask me why, so I will tell you: because the first time anybody in this county has put a word over a column, a woman of forty-one worked out inside about a minute that the word is the reason nobody can ask a question about anything else in the column, and she is right, and I have not got a better answer than she has got and I have been counting carts for nineteen years.”

---

The man of thirty-one had been in that yard for about a quarter of an hour and had not said anything, which is now what he does in a yard, and what he had been doing for about that long was arithmetic he was not going to say out loud.

Two of the four things on that sheet were about about a hundred and forty people. One of them was about a hundred and forty-one people who are dead, and one of them was about ninety acres and eleven families who are alive. The other two were about nobody in this county at all, because a bar that is not shut and a lane that goes where it likes are the two things in eleven villages that everybody walks on and no person is standing on.

*There is a bar in this county that takes threepence off every load that comes through it, about nine loads a day on the days the loads come, and hands five pounds five shillings of a year of it to a woman at ninepence a day, and a lane that a man has walked for nineteen years at one shilling and sixpence a day on a hundred and forty days, which is ten pounds ten shillings a year, and neither of them has a name on it and neither of them has anybody in it, and the other two have got about a hundred and forty people in them and one of those hundred and forty is the only person in this county that can be asked anything at all.*

He did not say any of that, and there was a reason and the reason was about nine seconds long and it was this: saying it would make a person of forty-one the middle of a list of four, and she had told a yard on the sixth day of the sixtieth week that the day she became a person in a record was a morning she did not ask for, and he had heard her say it twice since and he was not going to be the reason it happened a third time over a piece of paper cut out of a bill.

Instead he said the thing he had come out here to say, and he said it standing up, in about nine seconds, in front of about nine people, because a month in this county has taught him that the only honest act left is the one announced first.

“I am going to put these four next to each other,” he said. “I am not going to join them. And I want it said out loud in a yard before I do it that I know what that costs, because I have paid it twice in a month with two different pairs of people and it cost a woman of forty-one nine years of a room she had not known she was standing in.

“The price this time is not that the four of you find out about each other. The price this time is that three of the four people in the middle of these have not yet decided what they are for, and one of them decided about four hundred years ago in a village she has never been inside, and I am going to make her stand next to a thing that is hers, in front of people, and she is a woman of fifty-one who has rung a handbell at a lane end for about forty years and has been asked three times who taught her and has never answered, and *she has told me in a yard that she is not going to be asked now either, and I am going to do it anyway.*”

---

She was in the yard. She had come down the lane about an hour before the two of them and had been standing at the back of it with a handbell under her arm, and nobody had told her anything and she had not asked.

“That is four,” said Isel Cardew. “I counted them from the cart's side and I have not read a word of that paper and I am not going to.

“You said I have decided what I am for,” she said, to him, and not to the yard. “I have not. What I have is a handbell and a gate bar and about forty years, and about thirty years ago I stopped going up a tower four miles from my own gate and I have not been to blame for it and a man of fifty-two said so out loud at the bottom of it and I have been living on that for a week. *That is not a decision. It is a hole in a woman and I have had it thirty years and you cannot stand me next to a thing in a yard and call it a decision, and if you write me on that paper I will take it off, and if you do not write me on it I will still not be next to it because I am not going to be asked.*”

“Nobody is writing anything,” said Tarin Keel.

“Four things on a sheet is a list,” said Isel Cardew. “A list is nearly a register and I do not know a thing in this country that has got from a list to not being a list. *Ask me something I have an answer to and I will give it to you in a yard this morning. Ask me what I am for and there is nothing there and you know there is nothing there, because I have been in this county all my life and I have never once been able to answer it and I stopped expecting to about the time my eldest was married.*”

---

And then the man of thirty-four came up the lane, and he did not come into the yard, and nobody asked him to.

He had gone home on the second day of the sixty-second week on a cart going down the lane and out of the county, four hundred miles, to a house with a yard he had not been in for eleven days, and he had come back up the lane that morning with a cart behind him and two men with it, and about eleven people in Coldharrow had watched a man of thirty-four get off a cart at the bar that is not shut and look up the lane as if he were counting houses.

He stood about nine yards off with a barrow's shaft in his hand and he did not come in and he did not go away, and the woman at the bar counted him because she counts everybody, and a stranger is three, and the day had already got four on it, and the three were his, and the day had four of the clock and those three on it, and about nine people in that yard had watched him get off the cart before he had said a word to anybody.

“He came up this lane five days ago,” said the woman of about fifty, to nobody, in the voice of a woman reporting a thing she has decided to be accurate about. “He was in this county for four of them and he went home on a cart the day before yesterday and he has come back up the lane this morning, and I have had that board under my arm the whole of it and I have not asked him one question and I am not going to. “He read out four lines on a wet morning and said a thing about producing, and I have heard that thing from two other people since and both of them were saying it about something else, and I have not asked either of them where they got it. *I have got it as well. I have not said it out loud. I want it noticed that I have got it and have not said it and I do not intend to say it.*”

Nobody in that yard asked her what the thing was, and the man of thirty-one did not ask her either, and he knew what it was because he had been in a field at Trope on the sixth day of the sixty-first week when three men from four hundred miles off were told what a document is, and one of them had said a sentence at the end of it that he has not been able to put down since.

*If it cannot be produced then it has not happened.*

Nine words. It was in about nine heads in this county and it was in about forty more in a county four hundred miles off, and the man of thirty-four was standing nine yards from a yard with a barrow's shaft in his hand and had come back up the lane to say it a third time, and he did not say it while Ilyan was in the yard, and Ilyan has noticed that and does not know what to do with it, and it is the first thing in this country that has happened to him that he cannot put in a list with nothing at the top of it.

---

So that is what the fifth sheet of paper was, and it lay on the side of a cart in a yard in Coldharrow through the rest of the day, and four things were on it, and none of them was a border.

The woman of forty-one came down off the ladder at about the second hour and read it standing up, all four lines, in her own voice, because she is the person in this village who reads things out and nobody had asked her to and she has stopped waiting to be asked about that particular thing some weeks ago.

“A bar,” she said. “A lane. A list. Ninety acres.” She wiped the cloth over her shoulder. “Thirty-one years I have kept a count on a wall and there is no column on it and no heading and nobody has ever asked me what a single stroke of it was for, and I have just read four things off a piece of paper out of the back of a bill and two of them are about eleven families and one of them is about a hundred and forty-one people who are dead, and not one of the four has got a day on it, and I could not have told you in the morning which of the four that was going to be the one that got me.

“Ninepence a day,” she said, “and a hundred and forty days of it in a year, and that is five pounds five shillings, and it is the whole of what a village in this lane is worth, and there are eleven of them, and the four things on that sheet are worth that much between them and I have been the only one anybody could ask about for twenty-five years. *Nobody has ever asked me which of them was going to matter.*”

And she went off up the lane, and the last bell went, and the day came to seven, and it was not the ordinary one.

---

And at about the ninth hour a woman of about sixty came into that yard from up the lane, and she had walked nine miles, and she had a barrow's shaft in her hand that she had not got any more use for and put down against the cart's wheel.

She was not asked why she had come, and she was not asked about anything, and she stood at the side of the cart and read four lines off the side of it with her hand on the wheel, and she took about four minutes over it, and the yard did not fill up for her and about nine people in it kept on doing what they were doing, which is the way a yard of about two hundred and ten people handles a woman who has walked nine miles.

“They have written four things down,” said Wenna Skell. “That is all I came to see and I have seen it.

“And I want to say one thing and I am going to say it to the four of you and not to the yard, and then I am going to go home, and it is the only thing I have got and I am about sixty. *Two of these are about a thing I can say where it started, and one of them is about a thing I can say who is in it, and there are two things only I can say and I have never once been asked about either of them, and I have not come up nine miles to be asked, and if anybody in this yard asks me now I am going to say no and go home.*”

The man of thirty-one looked at her for about four seconds and did not say a word, and it is worth saying exactly why, and the reason took him about nine seconds and it is this: there was a sentence in him, and the sentence would have moved her, and he had put it together out loud, alone, on a stretch of hedge about a mile outside Coldharrow on the fourth day of the fifty-eighth week, and he had never offered it to a living soul since, and both times in the last ten days he had got as far as the shape of it and had stopped, and both times the shape of it had turned out to be a person becoming a room, and there are about eleven people in this county who are rooms and he made one of them himself.

So he said nothing at all, and she went home, and about nine people in that yard had watched him not do it and about four of them had seen him decide not to, and the fifth sheet of paper stayed on the side of a cart until the rain came and Tarin Keel took it inside and put it in a book on a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off, on a shelf that a woman of twenty-nine is not letting anybody put anything else on.
