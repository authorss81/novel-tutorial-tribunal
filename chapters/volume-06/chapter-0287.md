# Chapter 287: A Village That Says Its Sentence, And A Village That Will Not

Four bells and nothing on top of them on the second day of the sixty-third week, and the cloth came off a number on a wall in Coldharrow that was sixty-three thousand seven hundred and fifty-nine, and the woman of forty-one wiped it and left it, and the woman of forty-one put four strokes up and left it, and it is the ordinary number and it is the number that board says about most of the days in a year.

There were about nine people in the yard at Nettlebed at about the fourth hour and there were not about nine people at the bar at Trope at about the ninth hour and there were not about nine people in the yard at Coldharrow at any hour at all, and all three of those facts had been arranged by two people in a lane ninety yards long who had said nothing to each other about it, and neither of them had been asked by anybody.

---

The sentence was said in Nettlebed, in Nettlebed, by a person of Nettlebed, and it is four words long.

“We walk this lane.”

He said it standing in his own doorway with the door on the hasp behind him and a hurdle frame against the wall, and there were about nine people in that yard and about four of them came from other villages, and he had walked up the lane that morning to say it and had said it in about four seconds.

“That is it,” he said. “That is the whole sentence. It is four words and it is about a lane. *It is not a rule and it is not a claim and I have been two days trying to write something that sounded like a rule and I have thrown both of them away, and the reason is that a rule says what people may do and a claim says what is mine, and I have not got either of those and I am not going to invent one in a yard.*

“There is no room in this county and there is not going to be one and a thing that gets said in a yard is the only kind of thing there is, so I have said the truest thing I know about my own village and it is four words long. *We walk this lane. It goes from village to village and it does not go by the ground and no cart in this county chooses it, and we walk it, and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to dress it up because you have all been very serious for three days.*”

---

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him a second question and about four people in that yard said, later, in about four different words, that it was the right sentence, and one of those four was a man of fifty-two with a rule on a string, and he wrote it down.

He wrote it on the fourth sheet, in the book that stands on a shelf in a press in a village eleven miles off, and he wrote the four words and he wrote nothing else, because one of the eleven lines says that a recordwright writes down what was said and nothing else, and because he has never once written a name at the foot of anything in this county and is not going to start.

“Nothing goes into this book except a thing said out loud, in a yard, by somebody who belongs to the place it is about,” he said, reading the line out loud from the cart's side, because about nine people in that yard wanted to hear it read out. “Nobody goes in who has not said one. Nobody comes out. There is no twelfth line. *I have had a book on a shelf since the seventh day of the sixty-first week and not one village has ever been in it, and this morning one of them is, on four words about a lane, and I have been waiting for it since that day, and I have not said that to anybody, and nobody in this yard has noticed until this morning.*”

---

And Trope would not, and the woman of fifty-one said so in her own yard before anybody had asked her, and she had a handbell under her arm and a gate bar behind her that has not been shut since before she was born.

“No,” said Isel Cardew.

She said it in about a second and then she said the rest of it and it took about two minutes, and about nine people in that yard heard all of it and about four understood all of it.

“He has a door and a plank and a key he has never used and he is a person of that village and he said a sentence in that village and it went on a sheet. *That is the whole of the rule and he kept it exactly and he is right and I am not going to say a word against it, because if I say a word against it then what he did this morning means nothing and he did it this morning on purpose and he knew exactly what he was doing.*

“And I have got a hand in that door as well. The last nine figures in that column are my grandmother’s and her mother’s, and the fourth name from the bottom is the grandmother of a woman who stood in that stair, and this village has got more of itself in that plank than any village on this lane has, and I am not going to have a man in a doorway four miles down the road say my village’s sentence for it in a yard nine miles from here.

“That is not a grievance. *I want to be exact, because I have been exact my whole life and it is the only thing I have got.* A village that has said a sentence has joined a thing, and a village that has not joined a thing cannot be asked to leave it, and I have known both of those for about thirty years without ever once having said them out loud, and I have said them out loud this morning in my own yard, and the man who owns the tower has said them in his own yard about four miles away, and neither of us has any power over the other one and neither of us wanted any.

“You have come up nine miles to find out whether a village will say a thing,” said the man of thirty-one, “and one has, and one has not, and you are going to tell me the reason and it is going to be about a handbell.”

“It is going to be about a handbell,” said Isel Cardew. “I have rung one at this lane end for about forty years and nobody has ever asked me who taught me and I have answered once and I have never been asked again, and I am not going to start this month. *Ask me in a year and I will tell you that there is no why and that there has not been one since before I could ask one, and I have said that to a man of thirty-one twice now and I would say it a third time and it is the only answer I have got and it is worth nothing to anybody.*”

---

And Coldharrow would not, and the woman of forty-one said that out loud in a yard at about the ninth hour to about nine people who had not asked her, and it is the finding of the thirty-seventh day and it took her about two minutes.

She is a woman of forty-one who keeps a bell and a count, and there is a bar at the end of this lane that is not shut, and eleven families grazing ninety acres that they have never been told about, and a bar that has not been shut since before she was born at a house four miles up this lane, and a wall with a figure on it and a third hand in chalk at the foot of it that a man of thirty-one put there on the first morning of his being in this country and that nobody has told him to wipe off and that he has not offered to.

“A village that has said a sentence has joined a thing,” she said, “and a village that has not joined a thing cannot be asked to leave it. That is both halves and they go together and I have known them for twenty-five years and I have said them out loud in a yard about four minutes ago at a house nine miles from here and I would like it noticed that I said them in a village that has not said a sentence.

“And I am not going to say ours.

“I am not going to say it because *this village is the one with the head in the chalk.* That is a silly sentence and I have thought about it for two days and it is the only one I have got. There is a man in this county who has had a figure in his hand on a wall in this village for thirty-seven days and about nine people in a yard know it at a glance and not one of them has said a word, and I have made an arrangement about it in ninety seconds and I have not called it a favour, and if a sentence goes in a book from this village then somebody is going to work out in about four years who is the hand that is in it, and there is no column on my board and there is not going to be one.

“I am not being brave. I am being a woman of forty-one who has been the only one anybody could ask about for twenty-five years and who has found out this month that being that is a thing that can be spent, and *I am not going to hand in the only job I have ever had because somebody wrote four words on a sheet and asked me to.*”

---

And the man of fifty-two did the arithmetic, because the market was yesterday and a market is the only day anybody in this county can be said anything to more than nine people, and he had brought the lead and there was a cart's side.

“What is one of them worth,” he said. “That is the question and nobody has asked it and all three of you have been in a yard for three days and not one of you has asked what a sentence is worth, so I am going to ask it out loud and answer it out loud and there are three answers and all three of them are correct, and a man of fifty-two has never in nineteen years had three answers to anything, and I do not know which of the three a person in a yard is meant to believe.”

He put the lead on the cart's side.

“Ninepence a day for a hundred and forty days is one thousand two hundred and sixty pence. *That is a keeper’s year and it is the wage of the woman who keeps the count in this village and it is to the penny.* One shilling and sixpence a day on the same hundred and forty days is two thousand five hundred and twenty pence, and that is a carter’s year, and it is also, to the penny, what the two villages that have never had a bell have been worth to this lane every year for about two hundred years.

“Eleven villages at one thousand two hundred and sixty is thirteen thousand eight hundred and sixty pence, and that is fifty-seven pounds fifteen shillings, and thirteen thousand eight hundred and sixty goes into eleven exactly one thousand two hundred and sixty times. *So one village’s share of the whole of what this lane is worth is one thousand two hundred and sixty pence, and that is a keeper’s year, and if there are eleven villages then every one of them has been worth a woman’s wage a year to the penny and nine of them have been paying it out of a bar and two of them have not been paying it at all.*

“And the third answer. *The register has no money in it. There is no gate on it and no toll and no day and no officer and no vote and no meeting, and it is four sheets cut out of the back of a bill on a shelf in a press.* So the four words that a man said in a doorway this morning are worth nothing whatever, in coin, to anybody, in any county, and they are worth a lane.

“*A keeper’s year, a carter’s year, and nothing. All three are right. I have been printing working in yards in this county for a month and I have never once had three answers before and I do not know which one to give anybody and I am not going to pretend that I do.*”

---

About four people in that yard understood the whole of the three of them and about nine understood the sum, and about two of the nine were surprised, and one of the two was a boy of seventeen who had been at the wall of the last house in Ellerby for an hour and a half that afternoon and had come down the lane and had not said anything to anybody about anything, and who was asked by nobody about the four words and who has still got an empty page in a book he has kept for five years and who said, in the sixth day of the sixtieth week, that he could be asked about it in about six weeks and would rather not be asked about anything else in the meantime.

And a man of fifty-two counted the book at about the last hour and read out what was in it, and there is one place in it.

“Nettlebed,” he said. “Four words about a lane, said in Nettlebed, in a yard, by a person of Nettlebed. *One. Out of fifty-one, in about a week, and it is the first thing that has ever gone into it, and the second line on that sheet is about ninety acres and eleven families and I cannot tell you what it is and neither can she who said it, and that is not a fault in a record, it is a fact about a rule.*”

He put the lead in his pocket.

“And there is a cross against the eleventh name on that sheet and there has been a cross against it since the day I cut it, and the eleventh name is a place on this lane that nobody in two hundred and ten people can name, and a cross comes off the moment a person of that place says anything at all, and I cannot take it off because I do not know where it is. *It has been the eleventh since the seventh day of the sixty-first week and it will be the eleventh in a year and nobody in this county can get it off and I have stopped trying. Put those two facts side by side and there is the whole of the register: the first thing that has ever gone into it is a lane, and the one thing that cannot come out of it is a place that none of us can find.*”

---

And at about the last of the light on the second day of the sixty-third week a carrier came through the bar at Coldharrow on foot with a barrow and a bundle, which is what a carrier does, and put a letter on the side of a cart, and would not say where it had come from, and it was not a form.

It was about the size of a hand and it had a seal on it and it was not a form, and about nine people in a yard looked at it and none of them had ever seen one before, and the man of thirty-one picked it up and could not tell what any of it was.
