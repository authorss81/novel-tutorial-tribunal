# Chapter 264: A Thing From A Building With A Yard

The seventh day of the fifty-ninth week was a market day, and the board had read sixty-three thousand five hundred and ninety-nine at the second light, and a market is the only day anything at all comes up the eleven-village lane. That is a fact about this county that nobody in it had ever had cause to notice before, and the thing that came up on this one came in a barrow with a list of eggs under it and had been in a drawer for four days.

The gate did nine loads between the second light and the noon bell, threepence a load, and the woman of about fifty who keeps that post had her stick and her own board out in the lane and called the count aloud as each one came past, and none of that has anything to do with the rest of this and is here because the three of them were standing in the lane at the time and could not go anywhere.

He asked for the sheet to be read out in the way he asks for things.

That is the second time in ten days, and it had become a thing in Coldharrow without anybody deciding it: the man of thirty-one asks, and the sheet gets read out, and nobody in the yard says one word about the asking, and about four people in that yard had noticed the asking and had stopped noticing it, which is a different thing and is the only kind of difference a person of thirty-one gets in this country.

The woman of forty-one read it, because she is the only one in the yard who reads anything, and she read it twice and gave the fourth line its own beat.

> WATER-SEAT OF SALLOWBY. FORM 6. ACKNOWLEDGMENT OF A COPY CARRIED OUT OF THE DISTRICT.
>
> A copy is not evidence. A copy is paper. An acknowledgment is evidence of one thing only, which is that a person was in a place and read a copy and is willing to say so afterwards.
>
> Enter the day of the reading, in the grain year of the seat.
>
> Enter the name of one person present at that reading, and the words by which that person is known in the place where the reading was done.
>
> The acknowledgment will be entered in the district named in the copy.
>
> Where no day can be shown, the acknowledgment may be entered without one, and the entry will be marked as made without a day.

There is a printed head at the top of it and a printed foot at the bottom of it and the foot has a date on it in a form of reckoning this county has never used and cannot produce, and the man of thirty-one did not look at the date for longer than it takes to know what it is and had a piece of paper read out to him about it two days ago and it took a pound three shillings and sixpence and fifteen days of a man's road and there is nothing in any building nine days from this field that will take a number off a wall.

Nobody said no and nobody said yes, and it is worth saying that about the fourth of them, because about nine people were in that lane and about nine of them understood the whole of what had just been read and not one of them said a word, and the reason is that there is no form anywhere in eleven villages that asks a person to answer one.

---

Then the man of thirty-one said the thing that the sheet did not know, and it is worth saying how small it was, because it is a fact about a form and not about a county.

“It is correct,” he said. “Every line of it, and it is the most correct thing anybody has read in this country in fourteen days, and I have been in rooms where a thing like that was worth four hundred pounds to somebody. A copy is paper. An acknowledgment is a person saying they saw it. That is not a rule anybody made up. That is the only thing that makes a piece of paper worth carrying four hundred miles.”

“Then what is wrong with it,” said Tarin Keel, from the end of a cart, in a voice that has counted carts on roads for nineteen years and does not like being out of a sum.

“It is wrong about the place,” said the man of thirty-one, “in three ways and it is wrong in the same three ways in every country it has ever been in, and not one of the three is anybody being a villain.

“*It asks for a room.* There was no room. There was a yard and a tower and about nine people standing in it and a woman of forty-one on the foot of a ladder with a sheet in her hand. A room is not a technical word. It is a place with a roof and people in it being officially in it. And that is what it means, and there is nothing in this county that is a room in that sense and there never has been.

“*It asks for a day in the grain year of the seat.* The grain year is a thing a bench uses. This village has a year and it is the day the ewes come down off the fell and it moves about three weeks in any year, and the woman who could give you the fell year cannot give you a day, and has never been able to give anybody a day in her life, and says the same four sentences about it that she has said to four men in a county with a road in it over twenty-five years.

“*And it says the acknowledgment will be entered in the district named in the copy.* The copy names a district four hundred miles off. It is a basin with a village of about a hundred and forty people in it and a mine and a market town eight weeks back from here. *So if this county answers this, then this county puts the only name it has got into a record in a valley that has never heard of it, in a room with nobody in it who can be asked about the woman who read it out.* And the form does not know that. The form is not wrong about anything. The form is a bench's form and it has been used about four hundred times and it has never once been used in a place where the reading happened in a yard.”

“That is the third thing in ten days you have said to me about a room,” said the recordwright of twenty-eight. “I have written down two of them and I have thrown both away.”

“I know you have,” he said. “I saw you do it.”

---

And then the woman with the board said the thing that had been underneath it since the first morning of that week and had been coming up under everything since the second day of the fifty-ninth week, and she said it standing at the foot of her own ladder in front of about eleven people, and it took her about four seconds and she had clearly had it since the noon bell.

“You sent for this,” she said, not unkindly.

“Yes,” said Sable Venn. “I sent for it eight weeks ago, in the same week I had a copy made, and I did not tell the man of thirty-one and I did not tell the man of fifty-one and I did not tell the woman of forty-one and I have not been in this county ten days and I have not put one thing on one board. I sent for it because I have carried about four hundred pieces of paper into several hundred people who could not lay down a pound three shillings and sixpence for a day out of a village, and I have known for nine years what the trouble with all four hundred of them is. *A copy is paper.* And I have never once been able to fix that, and I sent for a form in a city I was standing in, and it has come four hundred miles on a barrow under a cloth with a list of eggs on top of it, and I do not know yet whether it fixes it or whether it is the worst of the four hundred.”

Nobody thanked her. Nobody in that lane had thanked her for anything since the second day of that week and there was not going to be a reason to start, and she had not asked, and she was on no paper, and there was no name in that yard that belonged to her.

---

“Now read it again,” said the man of fifty-two, “and tell me what it wants, because I have been listening to a form and I have not understood a form since I was in a room in a market town four hundred miles off, and I have understood everything else said in this lane for fourteen days.”

“It wants three things,” said Ottily Roke. “It wants a day. It wants a name. And then it says where the name goes.

“The name it wants is a person who was in a room when a copy was read out. And there is one, and there is only one, and I do not have to be told that, I have known it since the second day of that week when I read four lines out loud in a yard in front of about nine people and said I was not going to have it in this village in the same breath. *A person who was in a room.* It is a yard. It is a yard and a ladder and a wall and nine people and about half of them were not listening. And I am the one and there is nobody else.”

She did not say no. She did not say yes. She wiped the foot of a board which did not need it, which is what she does, and he had learned by the fourteenth day that it is not a thing she does instead of a thing she is about to say.

“Read the fourth line again,” she said, and Delya Craven handed her the sheet without a word and without having read it, which is worth about nine lines on its own, and the woman of forty-one read the fourth line again and gave it the same beat she had given it the first time.

> Where no day can be shown, the acknowledgment may be entered without one, and the entry will be marked as made without a day.

“That is the whole of it,” she said. “That is the door, and it is not a door anybody has to open. It is a door that is built into the form, by somebody who was being fair, and who did not know that in this county every day is the same four rings, and that I can tell you the fell year and I can tell you the count and I cannot tell you the day, and that is not a thing I have failed at. *That is a thing there is no instrument in this country for.*”

And then she said the other thing, which she had said to herself since the noon bell and which she had not said to anybody, and the man of thirty-one heard it arrive and knew what it was and knew also that it was the first half of what the next ten days were going to cost and that nobody else in that lane knew it and that he was not going to tell them.

“I have been the only one who can be asked in this county for twenty-five years,” said Ottily Roke, “and it has been a comfort and I have never once thought of it as anything else. And I did not know until about a minute ago that a thing like that can be spent. And now I know. *And I have not told anybody, and I would like it written down somewhere that is not a wall.*”

She handed the sheet back to the woman of about forty with the barrow.

“Now put that in a drawer again,” she said, “and bring it out on a day when there is somebody in this village worth being the only one who can be asked for, because there is not one, and I am the only one I have got, and I have been it for twenty-five years and I am not going to be handed over to a form that asks for a room.”

---

And that was the last of the market day, and the last bell went, and the three thousand seven hundred and eighty pence a year went into a woman's mouth as nine strokes on a board that is not the one on the wall, and the nine loads went back down the lane, and about four people stood in a yard with a form in it.

It was not decided, and it was not going to be decided, and what had actually happened in the seventh day of that week in Coldharrow was that three people in a lane had each said the sentence that stands behind the other two, and each of them had said it before being asked, and none of them knew what the other two were, and none of them had been asked.

The carter wanted a road, and the reason was a gate, and the gate wanted a day.

The woman of forty-one wanted the whole of it said out loud where it could be got at, and the reason was that she had been holding a fact about ninety acres for nine years and nobody had ever asked her for it, and eleven families were grazing a strip and paying nothing and had never been told.

And the woman of about sixty, nine miles off, who was twenty-one the day it was walked and had a name that had been said out loud in a lane for the first time that morning, wanted it gone, and nobody in Coldharrow knew that yet, and she had not told anybody, and she was not going to be asked.
