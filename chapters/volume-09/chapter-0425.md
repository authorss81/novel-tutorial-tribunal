# Chapter 425: A Person Of This Place Goes Past The Office's Own Timber On Foot And Comes Back Up The Same Four Yards Of Mud, And The Finding Is That It Works

The seventh day of the eighty-second week was his hundred and seventy-fifth morning and the twenty-fifth day after the settlement, and the fever was twenty weeks and two days old, and the rail had been down fifteen days, and a cart stood in a yard at the low end of Tallowgate with a man of fifty-four on the wheel who had brought four hurdles into this city on the third day of this week and taken them out again on the same afternoon, and there was nobody in that yard to see a woman of twenty-eight get up onto it and there is nothing in four hundred miles that has a form in it saying a person is to be told.

She got up onto it at the fourth hour with a slate under her arm and nothing else, and about nine people at that end of Tallowgate were in the road and had not been sent for and had not been told, and the boy of sixteen was at the fourth door up the low row and had said nothing about it in four doorways and had not been asked to.

The man on the wheel looked at her for a while.

“*You are going out on that,*” he said.

“*I have been entered as removed from this city,*” she said. “*So I have not applied and I have not been asked, and I am going out on it, and I am not going to say a third thing about it because I have said two already in a room and a clerk wrote one of them down and a piece of paper took the other one off me.*”

The cart went, and the man of fifty-two was not in that yard and the man with the bad leg was not in that yard and there were nine people at the end of Tallowgate who watched a cart go east on the Low Road and one of them was the boy of sixteen and he did not go with it and does not go to everything.

---

The road is two cart widths and a ledge on the north side that is a ledge the way a shelf is a shelf, and it runs along the head of the ninth weir for about four hundred yards and then goes east across the flat, and a cart goes on the north side of it because the north side is the side with the ledge, and the woman of twenty-eight said that out loud in the cart without being asked, twice, once at the weir head and once a mile along, and the man on the wheel said *I know that, my father took a load of water up the other side every morning of his life* and neither of them said anything else for a mile.

The cart stopped two miles out.

The four hurdles were across the north side at knee height and the length of chain was between the two outer ones and the plate was on the post above them, and the man of fifty-four got down and walked round on the south side, which is four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half with the water under it, and got back on the other side and came round to the tail-board.

“*That is where I stopped on the third day of this week with the plate in my hand,*” he said. “*And I went round then, and it took me a minute, and I have thought about that minute every day since and I have not got anywhere with it, and I have got thirty-one sheets behind me and not one of them has ever come to a road.*”

“*Then go round,*” she said.

“*I am not going to take this cart round. A cart on the south side is a cart on a ledge a foot and a half over the water and I have got a load that is not mine and I have been asked by nobody to break it. I will wait here. And I would like you to notice that I have said that in a road to a person who is on a cart and not to a person who is on the ground, because you are going to get off, and I have worked that out in the last quarter of a mile and I have not told you.*”

She got off.

“*Where are you going.*”

“*East. Past the post. Over the rise, where the flat stops being flat.*”

“*How far.*”

“*Until it comes back down and there is a road on the other side of it, and I have been told there is and I have not seen it, and I am not going to tell you how many hours, and I am not going to tell you what is on the other side, and if you ask me on the way back I am not going to answer and you are not going to be the first person in four hundred miles to be refused by me about that and you will not be the last.*”

She went up the road on foot on the north side, past the hurdles, and past the post, and over the sill three courses high without stepping on it, and up the rise, and the man of fifty-four stood at the tail-board and watched her go and did not shout anything after her, and about a mile and a half further east the road was not there to be seen from where he was standing, and he had not been asked to go and did not go, and he sat on the tail-board of a cart in a road two miles out and waited.

There was nobody there, and no form in four hundred miles lets anybody ask a man on a wheel what he is looking at, and he did not look at anything else for about half of the light.

---

She came back up the road on the north side at the last of the light and got onto the cart and put her slate down on the boards and did not sit on it, and her boots were wet to about the middle and there was mud on the back of one of them that had not been on it at the fourth hour, and the man of fifty-four looked at the mud and then at her face and did not say a word about either.

“*Well,*” he said.

“*Well,*” she said.

“*I am fifty-four and I have carried thirty-one things for an office in nineteen years and I have slept in a stable in this city twice in four days and I have never been asked what was in one of them and I have not asked about any of them, and this afternoon I watched a person go up a road past that office's own timber and come back down it, and I was the only one who saw it, and I have been sitting on this tail-board for about eight hours with a load that is not mine, and I am going to say the one thing I have got and then I am not going to say it again.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I have been in this country forty years and there is a word people use for a road that a person can come and go on, and there is a word people use for a road that a person cannot, and I have carried the second kind up and down this road for nineteen years and I have never once seen the first kind, and this afternoon I saw one, and it was a woman in boots with mud on them, and there was nothing on it.*”

The cart came back into this city at the dark and the woman of twenty-eight got down off it in a yard at the low end of Tallowgate and walked up the low row to her own step and did not stop at any of the doors, and the man of fifty-four unhitched the cart and did not go up the row and there is no form in four hundred miles that says a man on a wheel is to be thanked.

---

“*Say the finding in your own mouth,*” said the man with the bad ear, at the end of the low row, at about the last of the light, “*because six of us are standing here and five of them did not go out and I have been in this city twenty-two days and I have not seen a road do a thing in my life and I would like somebody who was on it to say what happened.*”

“*It works,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

Nobody said anything.

“*That is the whole of the finding and I am not going to make it bigger, and I have got it in my own feet and I have not got it in anything else, and I am going to say the working in the sentence because a man in this city asks for that and because it is the only way I know of saying a thing I have actually seen. I got onto a cart at the fourth hour and the cart stopped two miles out at four hurdles of its own office, and a cart does not go round four hurdles, so I went round them on foot, and I went east, and I came back west, and it took from the fourth hour to the last of the light and most of what was in between, and there was nothing on the road, and I am not going to tell anybody what was at the other end of it and I am not going to be asked and there is no form in four hundred miles that lets anybody ask me.*”

“*And the second half of that,*” said the man of fifty-two, who had come up the row and had not been sent for. “*Say what a road is for, because a man of fifty-four has just stood in a row and called that a road that a person can come and go on, and I have counted halls and doors for twenty years and I am going to say the thing nobody in this row is going to say and say it once.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*A person may go on that and a person may not, and neither of those is a victory, and neither of those is a finding about the city they went out of, and the sheet does not say this city. It says a city. And there are four hundred miles of country and there are towns in it and there is a woman of sixty-eight in a bed at the top of this one who is not going to be in a room on any day this year, and if a person in this city sits down and writes an account of what happened on the Low Road this afternoon and asks the ward whether it agrees, then the answer on the paper will be whatever the people in this row say, and she is counted in it anyway. That has been the shape of it since the summer and it has not moved and a woman in boots with mud on them has not moved it.*”

About six people at the end of a low row stood in the last of the light, and the boy of sixteen was one of them and had not said anything in four doorways and was not asked to.

“*And the last of it and it is mine,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*A government that bars a road has admitted the road goes somewhere. That is the finding and it is the only one there is and a man of fifty-four saw it happen from a tail-board and he has carried sealed paper for nineteen years and has never been asked what was in one. And I was removed from a city I have never left and put back into it and nobody asked me, and there is a line in a book four hundred miles off that says I was removed and I was not, and I cannot say no and I have not said no. And I am going to say a thing and I would like it written down in a room and not said in a row, and I would like it said by somebody who is not me.*”

The man of fifty-two said, “*Then say it in the morning and I will stand in the room with you and the man of twenty-nine will put it in a book and it will be the fourth thing of mine in a book in a city four hundred miles off and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

And at about the fifth hour, in the public room at the foot of the hill, with the schedule lying flat on the long table in front of him and about thirty people in it, four lines came into his sight and hung there clean and useless, and it was the sixth time in his life and it was two days since the last one, and he read it twice and did not choose anything out of it and he has never chosen anything out of one of them and is not going to start.

**CLAIM: A route is not a road. A road has a bar on it.**
**EVIDENCE: The bar is on the north side and has been since the third day of this week, and the south side of that road has had nothing on it since the rail came down, and a barrow has gone up it every morning of this month, and on the seventh day of the eighty-second week a person of this place went past a plate with no seal on it on foot and came back up the same four yards of mud, and it worked.**
**BURDEN: Somebody must be the person the office can ask whether a person who goes out on that road comes back the same, and there is one person in this city whose whole work is a count of doors, and she is forty-four, and she is paid four shillings a year, and she has not been paid for eleven years, and if she is named she cannot be un-named.**
**CHOICE: Put her name at the top of a line, or leave the road with a bar on it and no person who can say what crosses it.**

It did not say how many doors are on the Low Road. It did not say that a person who came back the same is a thing and a person who did not is a different thing and the column does not have room for the different one. It hung in his sight and asked him for a thing and supplied nothing in place of it, and there were about thirty people in that room and not one of them could see it.

He did not say anything about it to anybody, then or afterwards, and nobody asked him, and the clerk of forty copied the finding of the road fair into a book and put the book on a shelf, and the fever was twenty weeks and two days old, and it did not stop for a route, and nobody in that room was thanked.
