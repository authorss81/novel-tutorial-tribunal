# Chapter 425: A Woman Goes Past The Office's Own Timber On Foot And Comes Back Up The Same Four Yards Of Mud

Seventh day of the eighty-second week. Shelf day three hundred, his hundred and seventy-fifth morning, twenty-five days after the settlement, the fever twenty weeks and two days old, the rail down fifteen days.

Ilyan slept until the fourth hour and did not see any of it, which he thought about afterwards more than he expected to.

The cart was standing in a yard at the low end of Tallowgate with a man of about fifty-four on the wheel, and the cart had brought four hurdles into this city on the third day of the week and taken them out again the same afternoon. There was nobody in that yard. About nine people at that end of Tallowgate were in the road and had not been told anything, and the boy of sixteen was at the fourth door up the low row and said nothing about it in four doorways and was not asked to.

Hessa Marl got up onto the cart with a slate under her arm and nothing else.

The driver looked at her for a while.

“*You are going out on that,*” he said.

“*I have been entered as removed from this city. So I have not applied, and nobody has asked me, and I am going out on it.*” She got her weight settled against the side of the cart. “*I have said two things about it in a room and I am not going to say a third.*”

“*Ott Dray,*” he said. “*Fifty-four. I know that is not an answer to the question.*”

The cart went, and nine people at the end of Tallowgate watched it go east, and one of them was a boy of sixteen who did not go with it, and does not go to everything.

The road is two cart widths and a ledge on the north side, the way a shelf is a shelf. It runs four hundred yards along the head of the ninth weir and then goes east across the flat. Hessa Marl said out loud that a cart goes on the north side, twice, once at the weir head and once a mile along, and Dray said he knew that, his father took water up the other side every morning of his life, and neither of them said anything else for a mile.

The cart stopped two miles out.

The hurdles were across the north side at knee height, the chain between the two outer ones, the plate on the post above them. Dray got down and walked round on the south side, which is four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half with the water underneath, and got back on the other side and came round to the tail-board.

“*That is where I stopped on the third day of this week with the plate in my hand,*” he said. “*I went round then, and it took me a minute, and I have had a week to think about that minute and I have got nowhere with it.*”

“*Go round,*” she said.

“*I am not taking this cart round.*” He said it without any weight in it at all. “*A cart on that side is a cart on a ledge a foot and a half over the water, and I have got a load that is not mine, and I have been asked by nobody to break it. I will wait here.*”

He looked at her, and then at the hurdles, and then at her again, and worked something out in about a quarter of a mile.

“*I have said that in a road to a person who is on a cart and not to a person who is on the ground,*” he said, “*because you are going to get off, and I worked that out back at the weir head and I did not tell you.*”

She got off.

“*Where are you going.*”

“*East. Past the post. Over the rise, where the flat stops being flat.*”

“*How far.*”

“*Until it comes back down and there is a road on the other side of it. I have been told there is and I have not seen it, and I am not going to tell you how many hours, and I am not going to tell you what is on the other side, and if you ask me on the way back I am not going to answer, and you will not be the first person to be refused by me about that, and you will not be the last.*”

She went up the road on foot on the north side, past the hurdles, past the post, and over the sill without stepping on it, and up the rise, and Dray stood at the tail-board and watched her go and did not shout anything after her.

About a mile and a half further east the road was not there to be seen from where he was standing. Nobody had asked him to go and he did not go. He sat on the tail-board with a load that was not his, in a road two miles from a city of ninety thousand people, and watched the weather come over the flat, and did not look at anything else for about half of the light.

She came back up the road at the last of it, on the north side, and got onto the cart and put the slate down on the boards and did not sit on it. Her boots were wet to the middle. There was mud on the back of one of them that had not been there at the fourth hour.

Dray looked at the mud, and then at her face, and said nothing about either.

“*Well,*” he said.

“*Well,*” she said.

“*I have been in this country forty years,*” he said, “*and there is a word people use for a road that a person can come and go on, and there is a word people use for a road that a person cannot, and I have carried the second kind up and down this road for nineteen years and I have never once seen the first kind.*”

He turned the reins over in his hands.

“*This afternoon I saw one. It was a woman in boots with mud on them, and there was nothing on it.*”

They came into the city at the dark. Hessa Marl got down in the yard at the low end of Tallowgate and walked up the low row to her own step and did not stop at any of the doors. Dray unhitched the cart and did not go up the row, and nothing that has been printed anywhere says a man on a wheel is to be thanked.

---

Ilyan was at the end of the low row with six other people, and he had been there since the light went, waiting, and he had not gone out to the marker at all.

“*Say the finding in your own mouth,*” he said. “*Six of us are standing here and five of them did not go out, and I have been in this city twenty-two days and I have not seen a road do a thing in my life.*”

Hessa Marl looked at him for a moment.

“*It works,*” she said.

The row let that sit where it was for a second.

“*That is the whole of it, and I am not going to make it bigger, and I have got it in my own feet and I have not got it in anything else.*” She shifted the slate under her arm. “*I got onto a cart at the fourth hour and the cart stopped two miles out at four hurdles of its own office, and a cart does not go round four hurdles, so I went round them on foot, and I went east, and I came back west, and it took from the fourth hour to the last of the light and most of what was in between, and there was nothing on the road.*”

“*And the second half of that,*” said Darrin Skell, who had come up the row and had not been sent for. “*Say what a road is for, because a man of fifty-four has just called that a road a person can come and go on, and I have counted halls and doors for twenty years, and I am going to say the thing nobody in this row is going to say, and say it once.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*A person may go on that, and a person may not. Neither of those is a victory, and neither of those is a finding about the city they went out of, and the sheet does not say this city. It says a city.*”

About six people at the end of the low row stood in the last of the light and waited for the rest of it.

“*There are four hundred miles of country and there are towns in it, and there is a woman of sixty-eight in a bed at the top of this one who is not going to be in a room on any day this year.*” He had his hands in his pockets. “*If a person in this city sits down and writes an account of what happened on the Low Road this afternoon and asks the ward whether it agrees, then the answer on the paper is whatever the people in this row say, and she is counted in it anyway.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of sixteen was one of the six, and he had not said anything in four doorways, and he was not asked to.

“*And the last of it, and it is mine,*” said Hessa Marl.

“*Then say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*A government that bars a road has admitted the road goes somewhere.*” She said it to the row and not to him. “*That is the finding and it is the only one there is. I was removed from a city I have never left and put back into it, and nobody asked me, and there is a line in a book four hundred miles off that says I was removed and I was not.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Skell.

“*I cannot say no, and I have not said no.*” She took the slate out from under her arm and held it against her coat. “*And I would like this written down in a room and not said in a row, and I would like it said by somebody who is not me.*”

Skell looked at Ilyan, and Ilyan understood that he had been handed something, and that the whole row could see it being handed over.

“*Then say it in the morning,*” he said, “*and I will stand in the room with you, and the man of twenty-nine will put it in a book, and it will be the fourth thing of yours in a book in a city four hundred miles off, and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

---

At about the fifth hour, in the public room at the foot of the hill, the schedule lay flat on the long table in front of Ilyan with about thirty people in the room, and four lines came into his sight and hung there clean and useless.

It was the sixth time in his life, and two days since the last one. He read it twice and chose nothing out of it, and he has never chosen anything out of one of them and is not going to start.

**CLAIM: A route is not a road. A road has a bar on it.**
**EVIDENCE: The bar is on the north side and has been since the third day of this week, and the south side of that road has had nothing on it since the rail came down, and a barrow has gone up it every morning of this month, and on the seventh day of the eighty-second week a person of this place went past a plate with no seal on it on foot and came back up the same four yards of mud, and it worked.**
**BURDEN: Somebody must be the person the office can ask whether a person who goes out on that road comes back the same, and there is one person in this city whose whole work is a count of doors, and she is forty-four, and she is paid four shillings a year, and she has not been paid for eleven years, and if she is named she cannot be un-named.**
**CHOICE: Put her name at the top of a line, or leave the road with a bar on it and no person who can say what crosses it.**

It did not say how many doors are on the Low Road. It did not say that a person who comes back the same is a thing and a person who does not is a different thing, and the column has no room for the different one. It asked him for a thing and supplied nothing in place of it, and about thirty people were in that room and not one of them could see it.

He did not tell anybody, then or afterwards, and nobody asked him, and the clerk copied the finding of the road fair into a book and put the book on a shelf, and the fever was twenty weeks and two days old, and it did not stop for a route, and nobody in that room was thanked.
