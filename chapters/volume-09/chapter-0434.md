# Chapter 434: The Office Answers The Only Question Anyone Here Has Sent It, And The Answer Is A Description Of Him With A Place And A Date On It

Second day of the eighty-fourth week, shelf day three hundred and nine. His hundred and eighty-fourth morning, thirty-four days after the settlement, the fever twenty-one weeks and four days, the rail down twenty-four days.

Ferrow had sent up a sheet with one sentence on it on the fourth day of the eighty-third week, and it had cost a penny a page, and nobody in this city had stopped him.

The sentence was: *In the return of the fourth day of the eighty-third week there are two names in one line, and the office is asked which of them is a person and which of them is a thing.*

The answer came down in the tray at the second hour on the second day of this week, and it had wax on it, and it was four lines, and all four of them were correct.

“*Say the first,*” said Skell.

“*The return of the fourth day of the eighty-third week is received, and is entered in one column under the head of persons and things reported to the office in respect of the Low Road, this city, and the bar lately upon it, and the column has room for a person and for a thing, and there are two names in that line and both of them are in the column.*”

“*Say the second one and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*The office does not require the distinction between a person and a thing in that column and does not pay for it, and the office is asked to say which of the two names in that line is the person and which of the two is the thing, and the office declines to say, and does not require either of the two persons concerned to say, and will not enter either of them separately.*”

Somebody near the door said *both of the persons concerned*.

“*Say the third line,*” said Ilyan.

“*A person not of this city cannot be entered in a register of this city and cannot be struck out of one. The office has entered neither of the two names in that line. The line was filled in at a crossing of the Low Road at the ninth marker in this city, by a person of this place, in the ordinary way, in about eight persons' hearing, and the office has it as it was so filled in and does not require it to be filled in again.*”

“*Say the last one,*” said Skell, and his voice had gone somewhere it does not usually go.

Ferrow read the fourth line in the same voice he had read the other three, which is a thing he does and which he has told this room he does.

“*The office notes that the person not of this city, in that line, said the cost of the entry out loud, in the hearing of the eight persons, before the entry was made, and that the entry was made afterwards, and that this note is made because a clerk at the seat has read it and because it is the kind of thing a register ought to hold.*”

The room was very quiet, and about thirty people were in it, and the rain came off the flat against the north side of the hill.

“*Say the other half of that,*” Ilyan said.

“*There is no other half,*” said Ferrow. “*That is the whole of the fourth line and I have read it four times in my own head before I read it to this room and it does not have a second side to it. I would like the room to know that I have read it four times and that I am going to read it a fifth time if anybody asks me to and not one word less.*”

“*Then read it a fifth time,*” said Fenna Rusk.

He read it a fifth time.

---

Ilyan sat on the window seat with his leg out in front of him and did the arithmetic, because it was the only thing he had that did not require anybody else's permission.

Thirty-four days. A sheet with one sentence on it. A penny a page. A form with a head on it that has room for a person and a thing. And an answer that came down nine days later with a place in it and a date in it and a number of persons in it and the words *said the cost out loud before the entry was made*, in a fair hand, in a register at the seat, and no paper about him carried into a building by anybody.

He had spent four weeks learning that the way to find out what an office will do is to send it one question, and to be wrong in the question.

He had been wrong in the question. He had asked which of the two names was a person. The office had not been asked about a person at all. It had been asked to classify two things, and it had declined, and in declining it had written down where he had stood when he did it, and how many people had watched him do it, and what he had said immediately before he did it, and it had put the whole of that into a book because a register ought to hold that kind of thing.

“*Say what you have found out,*” said Skell, “*because there is a man in this room who is going to have to do something about it and everybody in here has watched him not do anything for a month.*”

“*That I have found out what a question costs when the person asking it is a person the office cannot enter,*” Ilyan said. “*It costs a place and a date and a number of persons and four lines, and the four lines are the truest thing anybody in this city has read about me, and they are true because I wrote all of it down myself and a man of twenty-nine copied it fair and put it in an envelope with no seal on it because there is no seal in this city to put on anything.*”

“*Say the second half of that,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And I am not going to be able to stand in a room in this city for the rest of my life and describe it as a thing I did in a room with a lamp on it. There is a plate two miles out on a road with a sill of three courses and about eight people stood in four inches of mud on the south side of it, and I said the price out loud to a woman who had not asked me for it, and I did it anyway, and the office has got the mud, and it has got the day, and it has got the eight, and it has got me.*”

About thirty people sat with it and one of them shifted a chair.

“*Then say what the room has,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and do not tell me what you have, because I want to know what the room has.*”

“*The room has a sheet of the office's with the ninth marker on it and the second day of the eighty-third week on it,*” Ilyan said, “*and everybody in this room heard the same four lines I did, and about nine of them have heard me describe it four times in four weeks as a thing I did in a room, and every one of those four times was wrong, and none of the four was a lie, and that is worse.*”

---

They went out to the ninth marker in the afternoon, and three of them went, and Renn Calloway said so out loud at the end of the row before anybody had asked.

“*Three,*” he said. “*Not eight. There were eight in the mud on the second day of this month and I have been asked twice since how many people stood in it, and I have said eight both times, and it was eight. Today it is three and I am one and I am saying the number before we go and not after.*”

Ilyan wanted the eight. He did not say so. He said: “*Then say the hour and say the route, and I will walk behind you.*”

They were in four inches of mud on the south side in about fifty minutes. The four hurdles were across the north side at knee height with nine links of new chain between the outer two, and the plate was on the post above them with two nail holes in it and one of them bent and nothing cut in the bottom corner, and the south side had four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half with the water underneath it.

There was a barrow at the top of the mud with a man pushing it who was about forty yards off and had not stopped.

“*Say what you are looking at,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*I am looking for a mark,*” Ilyan said.

“*Say the half of that which is not the whole.*”

“*I am looking for a mark and there is not going to be one, and I have known that since the flat and I came anyway.*”

He put his hand flat on the concrete drum of the marker. The concrete was cold through his glove and it had been cold through his glove the last time, and nothing had changed on the north side of it in a fortnight, and there was still nothing at all on the south side, and eight people had stood in the mud and said a price out loud and the mud has not kept one word of it.

“*Then say what has,*” said Calloway.

“*A clerk at the seat has,*” said Ilyan. “*And I want it understood by the three of us that the reason it has is that a man of twenty-nine in this city wrote down what I said in a room off the low ground and put it in an envelope and sent it up, and that I asked him to.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And I said the price out loud on purpose, and I knew when I said it that it was the price, and I did it anyway, and I have been telling this city for four weeks that I said it before I did it as though that were a thing that had been done for me. It was not done for me. It was done so that a book would have it.*”

She was standing on the ledge with her face towards the water and she said the next part to the water.

“*Then say what you are going to do with a register that has the mud in it.*”

“*I do not know.*

“*Say the other half of that.*”

“*I know what I am not going to do. I am not going to ask for the name to be taken out, and I have said in three rooms that I cannot ask, and it is not because I would be embarrassed.*”

“*Then say why.*”

“*Because a person of this place in a book is asked about the person outside it who put her there, and that is the price, and I said it in the mud on the second day of this month in front of eight people, and the price has come due on schedule and it has come due in a fair hand four hundred miles off and I would like it entered that I am not going to pretend the schedule is a surprise.*”

“*Then it is a thing with a day on it,*” said Calloway, “*and the day was the second day of this month, and I was there, and I have said so twice, and I am not going to say it a third time for a man in a book.*”

“*You are not going to be asked a third time,*” Ilyan said, “*and I want that said out loud in a room with about thirty people in it, and not by me.*”

Fenna Rusk turned round on the ledge.

“*I will not say it,*” she said. “*I have been asked nine times in four weeks by men with books and I have said yes to seven of them and I have not been thanked for one of the seven, and I am not going to be the voice in this city that a man in a book is safe. I have been in a room four hundred miles off in a column with a head on it and the head has got my name under it and nobody asked me whether I would be in it, and I am not going to be used to make a road out of.*”

She got down off the ledge into the mud and started to go west, and stopped after about ten steps.

“*The other half of it,*” she said, without turning round. “*I am on that line. And the office has now written down that a man said the cost out loud before he put his name beside mine, and it has put that in a register, and it says the register ought to hold it.*”

She went on west up the road in four inches of mud with her boots going into it, and she did not say the rest and nobody asked her to.

The fever was twenty-one weeks and four days old, and a plate stood on a post two miles out with nothing cut in the corner of it, and there was a barrow on the south side, and the eight of the second day of the month were not there and could not be got back and were not written anywhere except in a line in a book that said a man said a price out loud.
