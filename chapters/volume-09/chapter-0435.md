# Chapter 435: A Box Goes Up On The North Side Of The Ninth Marker, And The Person Who Cannot Say No Is The One It Is For

A box came up the Low Road on the back of a cart on the third day of the eighty-fourth week: four uprights of sawn ash, a roof of tarred board, a shutter on a hinge, a bench, and a form under a weight of stone. Shelf day three hundred and ten, his hundred and eighty-fifth morning, thirty-five days after the settlement, the fever twenty-one weeks and five days, the rail down twenty-five days.

It was set up on the north side of the ninth marker, three feet from the fourth hurdle, with the shutter facing west, so that a person sitting on the bench in it could look east up the road and would have the hurdles at their right hand and the plate over their head.

The south side still had nothing on it at all.

Bevin Tarr was there with a die in his coat. He had cut the head of the form and he could not leave it, and he had walked two miles to be within reach of a thing he had cut, and he said *huh* once and did not apologise for it.

“*Say what you cut,*” Ilyan said.

“*A head,*” said Tarr. “*Four thousand lines and four thousand dies and they paid me for two of them. This is one more of the four thousand and nobody has paid me for it and I am not going to say that again today.*”

“*Read the head out.*”

“*Keeping of a place at the crossing at the ninth marker on the Low Road in this city under the fifth row of the schedule of the first day of the eighty-first week. One line for the name of the person of this place keeping it.*”

Ott Dray was on the cart and had brought it up on the north side, which is the side a cart is on, and he had said nothing about that and nobody asked him.

“*And the three lines with it,*” he said, and read off a sheet that was under the stone.

“*Say them standing up, because the wind on this flat is not helping anybody.*”

“*One. A place is kept at the crossing at the ninth marker on the north side of the Low Road, at the rate of the first row, which is a farthing a visit.*”

“*Say the second one and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Two. The person keeping it is a person of this place and is entered on a form with one line. The office keeps a book of the visits and does not enter names in it.*”

“*Say the last one.*”

“*Three. Nothing on the form and nothing in the book is a finding about a road.*”

Dray got the sheet out from under the stone, folded it, and put it in his coat.

“*Thirty-one sheets in nineteen years,*” he said, “*and I have carried thirty-two, and I have never once been told what was in one before somebody read it out on a flat in the wind.*”

---

It was the second hour when Ilyan saw the shape of it, in the public room at the foot of the hill, with about thirty people in it and the form under a weight of stone on the long table because the room is warm and the man with the pencil did not trust paper.

It was the sixth time in his life and four of them have been in this city, and ten days since the last one, and he read it twice and chose nothing out of it and has never chosen anything out of one of them.

**CLAIM: A place that is kept is a place somebody can be asked about, and is the cheapest anybody in four hundred miles to ask.**

**EVIDENCE: The box is on the north side, beside four hurdles and nine links of chain, with a roof, a shutter and a bench. The rate is the first row, a farthing a visit. The office keeps a book of the visits and does not enter names in it. A person who crosses the road is never asked anything at all.**

**BURDEN: Whoever sits in it is a person of this place on a form with one line, and the only way out of a line is a person of the place applying at the seat. The only person in this city anybody can be asked about a door is the one who cannot say no.**

**CHOICE: Put her in the box, or leave a bar with a person in it that the office has paid for.**

It did not say how many carts a day come past that crossing. It did not say that a person who sits in a box is asked about the road and never about the carts, and it did not say that a person who cannot say no is the cheapest keeper there is, because that is a sentence the office would not print.

It asked him for a thing and supplied nothing in place of it, and about thirty people were in that room and not one of them could see it.

He did not tell anybody, then or afterwards, and nobody asked him.

---

“*I want her to refuse it,*” Ilyan said, at the end of the low row, at about the fifth hour, to Darrin Skell and to Renn Calloway and to nobody else. “*I have been sitting on that for nine hours and I am going to say it out loud before I do anything, because the last time I had a want in this city I said the price of it out loud and it did not stop me, and I am not going to go into a room with a want again without saying so.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Skell.

“*I want her to refuse it because the box is on the north side, which is the side the bar is on, and a person in a box beside a bar is a person at a bar, and the office does not have to ask a single person on that road anything. It only has to ask the one in the box.*”

“*Say the price of that in your own mouth,*” said Calloway, from the end of the row, with his hand on his bad leg. “*Out loud, and here, and before either of us moves, and then go and do whatever you are going to do.*”

“*The price is that a person of this place in a line is asked about everything that goes past the line, and it is asked by an office that does not check a number, and it is asked for as long as there is a road and a form. And I cannot name one person who could stop that, and I have said in five rooms this month that I cannot name one, and I am not going to stop saying it now.*”

“*Go on,*” said Skell.

“*And the second half of the price, which is mine. She has said out loud in three rooms this month that she cannot say no, and every one of those rooms believed her, and if I go to her now with a sentence she can repeat, then four of those rooms will be right about her in the only way that matters, and she will be doing what a person with a pen tells her to do at the hour a person with a pen is in the room.*”

Calloway looked at him for a while.

“*That is the first sensible thing I have heard a man from four hundred miles off say in this city,*” he said, “*and I am going to say the half of it that is not, and then I am going to go home, and I am not going to say either half again.*”

“*Say the half of it that is not.*”

“*A box with a roof on it and a bench in it and a shutter that comes down, two miles out on a road, at a crossing, in the wind, on the side of the road a cart is on, is the first thing anybody in this city has put on that flat for the people who use it. It is warm. You are talking to me about the office. She is going to be in that box at the hour a cart comes in.*”

---

He got it wrong.

He waited for the row to be empty, which it never was, and he asked her the question in the middle of nine people at the end of the low row at about the sixth hour, and he asked it well. He had it in four sentences and he had said them out loud to himself twice on the way down the row.

And Hessa Marl stood at the fourth door up with her slate under her arm and listened to all four of them, and about nine people stood and listened with her, and she was the only person on that row who could not leave without the whole of that end of the row knowing she had left.

“*Say the last of it,*” she said.

“*You do not have to take it,*” Ilyan said. “*And you can say no in whatever words you want, and if you say no I will write it down and Ferrow will copy it fair and it will go up a hill and it will be a line with your no in it, and you will have said no and it will be written down, and that is what I have wanted for a person in this city for a month.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*And if you take it, then the office has a person at its own bar, and the rate is a farthing a visit, and the rate is money, and you have not been paid for anything in this city in a year, and the box is warm and it has a roof, and the shutter comes down on the west side because the wind comes off the flat in the afternoon.*”

“*Say the half of that which is about me and not about the box.*”

“*You are the person the office can ask about every door in this ward and you have been the person the office can ask about every door in this ward since before I came here, and the only difference is that a box gives the office a place to send the question to.*”

The row waited. About nine people were in it and there was nowhere in it for her to stand that was not in front of them.

“*Say the question,*” she said.

“*I am asking you to refuse it and I have said that I want you to refuse it, and I said it out loud on the row before I came down here, so that it is on a record that is not in a book, and Skell heard it and Calloway heard it, and that is the price of it, and it did not stop me asking you.*”

“*You have said the price out loud three times in a month,*” she said. “*On the second day of this month in four inches of mud. And on Friday to a room of about thirty people. And now on this row.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*And every time you have said it to somebody who could not do the thing you wanted them not to do.*” She turned the slate over once and did not open it. “*I am going to say what is in my head and then I am going to decide, and I am telling you which one I am doing so that you know it is not the other one.*”

The row was extremely quiet.

“*In my head is a box with a roof on it, two miles out, at a crossing, and it comes on the north side because the north side is where a cart can stop. It is going to be there in the rain and I am going to be at the hour a cart comes in.*”

She put her hand flat on her own step.

“*I said in a room on the sixth day of the eighty-second week that I would be at the hour a cart comes in. I said it about a table in a room behind a chandler's shop, and I did not think about which cart.*”

“*I have been standing on this step since I was fourteen. It is worn into a curve and I have never once been able to sit down at the hour a cart comes in.*”

“*That is the half of it and I am not going to tell you the other half because you will put it in a book and the other half is mine.*”

“*Then say the decision,*” said Skell, because that is what he does.

“*I am going to take the box.*”

Ilyan stood on a step worn into a curve in a row of about nine people and had a want that had cost him a mile and a half and three rooms and it did not happen.

“*Go on,*” he said.

“*I am going to take it, and I am going to sit in it at the hour a cart comes in, and I am going to do nine doors in this ward in the morning. I am not going to stop doing nine doors. And I am going to count what goes past that crossing on this slate, in my own hand.*”

“*And what is the count going to be of.*”

“*It is going to be a count of what goes past and it is not going to be a count of anything else.*”

“*And the form has one line in it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The form has one line in it and I will put my name in it because I cannot say no, and I am going to say that out loud in a room on Saturday in about thirty people's hearing so that the office has it in a book and not only out of my mouth, and it is going to be true, and nobody is going to be thanked.*”

---

Skell did the working on the step, out loud, with the wind off the flat, and nobody had asked him.

“*Nine doors a day. Nine times three hundred and sixty-four days is three thousand two hundred and seventy-six visits. A farthing a visit is a quarter of a penny, and three thousand two hundred and seventy-six quarters of a penny is eight hundred and nineteen pence. That is sixty-eight shillings and threepence, and I said that figure in a room on the sixth day of the eighty-second week in a different voice about a different door, and it is the same figure.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The rest of it is that the office will pay her that figure if it counts the visits, and the office does not check a number. The pay is a thing the office decides about, once a year, out of a book it keeps itself, and what she does in that box is not in the book.*” He put his hands in his coat. “*Eight hundred and nineteen pence out of one door, and eight hundred and nineteen pence out of another, in the same year, and there is not one form in this county that will put the two of them in the same line.*”

“*And the rest of that,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the only thing coming out of that box that is not in the office's book is her slate, and I have been looking nine days for a form for a slate.*”

Calloway said *that is the figure* out loud on the step, which was the second time in three days that anybody in this city had counted anything, and it was the first time it had been about a person.

“*Say the half of it that is not about money,*” Ilyan said.

“*I have kept that road twenty-nine years and I have not been on it this morning because a box went up on it and I was not asked to be there and there is no form that says a man may be sent for, and I am not going to pretend to you that I did not hear about it at the fourth hour.*”

He shifted his weight off the bad leg.

“*There is a bar on the north side and there is a box on the north side and the box is the office's and the bar is the office's, and there is a woman in the box, and she is the only one of the three of them that says what she sees. I have been saying since the seventy-ninth week that a road with one keeper and one decider in the same body is held and not kept. I am not going to say the rest of it and I am going home.*”

He went home, and nobody thanked him, and there is no form in this county that says a man may be thanked.

The box stood on the north side of the ninth marker with a roof on it and a shutter and a bench and a form under a weight of stone with one line in it, and the fever was twenty-one weeks and five days old, and it did not stop for a shed, and the south side of that crossing had nothing on it at all.
