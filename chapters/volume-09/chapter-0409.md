# Chapter 409: A True Thing Said In A Room In Her Own Mouth, And A Line At The Top Of A Sheet For The Name Of The Person Asking

The fifth day of the eightieth week was his hundred and fifty-ninth morning and the ninth day after the settlement, and the fever was eighteen weeks and no days old, and the rail had been up twelve days, and there were nineteen people in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour, and the hole in the near side of the weir road had been packed and rolled nine days ago and the road was fit and every person in that room knew it and not one of them had asked the man in the hut.

The clerk of thirty had the office's sheet on the long table, squared to the edge, and the clerk of forty was at the end of the table with the fair hand ready, because there were three things on the sheet and one of them wanted a name in a register and the register is not the fair hand's book but a fair hand is a fair hand.

“*The third clause of that sheet has been up on that wall for three days and nobody has done anything with it,*” said Tarin Keel, from the wall, “*and I am going to say the thing that is going to happen about it, and I say things to the one person who can act, and there is one person in this room who can act and she is a magistrate and she has not been in this room since the second return went up, and the one person who can act about a register at the seat is four hundred miles off and is not going to be in this room in the time it takes to be polite about.*”

“*Say the thing that is going to happen.*”

“*Somebody is going to be named,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and he will be a person of this country and not of this city, and the first thing that office does with a road is hand it to one man, and the second thing that office does with a man is ask him the questions that four hundred people could have been asked, and he will answer for all of them, and they will be satisfied, and there will be a road in this city with a man on it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*That is my method and it has not worked and I have said so and I am not going to pretend it has,*” he said. “*And I am not going to say his method is the other one, because it is, and I have watched it put a woman's name in a book four times in this city and it works, and I have watched it also stop a rail in a hut for eleven days because nine people were in a room and none of them could.*”

---

So Ilyan asked a question in a room in front of nineteen people, because that is the only thing he has and it has never once stopped him, and he asked it plainly and he asked it late, and this is the whole of what happened.

“*What is a bar.*”

Nobody had asked her. That is the fact and it was said out loud in a later room by a clerk and not by the man who did it, and the room at the foot of the hill had nineteen people in it and every one of them was looking at the sheet.

“*A bar is not a road that is shut,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

She said it from the wall, with her slate under her arm, and nobody had asked her, and she did not stop, and it came out of her in one piece and it took about four seconds and the room had it before she had finished it.

“*A bar is a road that is a rule. And the rule is on a sheet, and the sheet is in a press in a building at the middle of the nine bridges, and every morning it is read out in this room to whoever comes and does not ask who they are, and there has been a morning for eleven days on which somebody has read it, and not one of those eleven was me, because I am on the wrong side of it and have been for eleven days, and there is no form in this city that a person goes into a room to say the thing she said.*”

She stopped. She had not raised her voice at any point in it.

---

And then Ilyan said the price out loud, in that room, in front of nineteen people, to the clerk of thirty, who could act, about a person who had not asked to be in any of it, and he said it fast because he had learnt that a slow price is a speech.

“*There is a form in that building that a person of this ward can fill in on their own and it is the oldest one there is and it has three lines, and two of them are the thing to be read and the room it is to be read in, and the third line is at the top and it is for the name of the person asking. That thing she just said is a true thing about a road, said by a person of this ward, in a room, in front of nineteen people, and there is a form for a road and there is no form for her. If it goes into that book, the name at the top of it is hers. And after today she is not only the person anybody in this city can be asked about the doors, she is the person anybody can be asked about that rail, and the two of those were kept apart and there is no form in four hundred miles that can keep them apart again, and she is twenty-eight years old and she cannot say no, and I have not asked her and I am not going to be able to give it back. That is the price and I am saying it in front of you because you are the one who can do it and I cannot, and it is mine and not hers and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be relieved.*”

Nobody stopped him.

The clerk of thirty put his hand flat on the sheet.

“*Who asked.*”

“*I did not.*”

“*You are the only man in this room who has not said a name out loud in it, and the thing that was said in it was said by a person of this ward, and the form wants the name of the person asking.*”

“*Then leave it empty.*”

“*There is no form that says a person may leave a line empty,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and I left one empty at the end of last week and there is no form for that either, and I will be asked about it in nine years and I will not be able to tell anybody. I am telling you now so that you know I know.*”

---

And the line was not filled by him and it was not filled by the man with the leg, and it was not filled in that room at all.

The boy of sixteen was in the doorway. He said it in four doorways before the change and nobody stopped him, and the woman of sixty with a shawl said it at a gate, and the man with the bad ear said it in four words in a row, and the woman on the stool at the low hall said it once to anybody who came through the door, which is what she has done every morning since the eighth morning of the fever, and the woman of twenty-eight said it herself at a door in her own row at the last of the light without being asked to, because she had said it in a room and it was in her now and she was not going to pretend it had gone out of her.

And on the second day a man came into this building with a sheet in his hand and said he was of that ward and had heard a thing said in the public room at the foot of the hill, and Bern Raye filled a line off the paper in front of him, in the ordinary way, at the ordinary hour, in the flat voice, and the clerk of forty wrote it fair beneath the third clause, and a line went in at the top of a page with a name at the top of it.

Nobody thanked anybody.

---

And it was not a line in a book on a shelf. It was copied the same afternoon, fair, into a sheet that went into the press on the ground floor of that building, at a penny a page, and the thing she said was a true thing about a road and it was legible, and it stood in his sight on the wall of that building in the same letters as the rule, and anybody in this city could be shown it for a penny.

“*Say what it is now,*” said the man of fifty-two, from the end of the table, to the room, because he is a man who asks for the second half and who has been asking for it in four rooms in this city and was not let into two of them.

“*Say it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

“*I am going to, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to go and do my work, because that is the arrangement and there is nobody in this room who has the standing to change it.*”

She turned round to the wall where the order was and the bill was under it, crooked, with a blank line in the middle of it.

“*There is a name at the top of a page in this city and it is mine, and the only other one I know of in this ward belongs to a man of thirty-one with a bad ear who asked for it himself in a room with nine people in it, and he paid the price of it out loud before he said yes, and I am not going to ask him what his price was because it is not mine. And the difference between his and mine is the whole of what has happened to me this week, and I would like somebody in this room to be able to tell me what has become of the difference, and there is nobody in this room who can, and there is nobody four hundred miles off who can, and the one person who could have asked me is the one person who cannot be asked because I did not agree to it and there is no form that asks me whether I agreed.*”

“*You did not have to say it in here.*”

“*No,*” she said. “*I did not. I could have gone down my row and said it at four doors and it would have been in four mouths by the last of the light and in a book in a fortnight, because that is how a thing gets from a person of a place into a record in this city, and I have been doing it for forty-eight days and I have never once been asked whether I wanted to and I have said in three rooms that I cannot say no and I have never once been asked.*”

“*Then why.*”

“*Because he asked a question in a room in front of nineteen people and I have never in my life been able to hear a question asked badly and not answer it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*And I have said in this room in the last ten minutes the whole of what it is, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be forgiven, and I am not going to be relieved, and I am not going to be told in a month that I was brave about it. I am a person of a ward and I answered a question in a room and it went in and I cannot get it out, and that is all, and it is the same thing that has happened to a woman of forty-four and to a man of thirty-one and to a clerk in this building, and none of us asked.*”

Nobody thanked her, and she was not thanked, and the man with the bad leg did not say a word to her, and he has not said a word to her since, and there was nothing in the room he could have said that would not have made it cost her more.

The four bells went over Tallowgate. The rail was up on the twelfth day and the road was fit and the man in the hut was still in the hut. The fever was eighteen weeks and no days old, and it did not stop for a name at the top of a sheet, and there is no form in four hundred miles that takes a name off a page, and nobody in that room was thanked.
