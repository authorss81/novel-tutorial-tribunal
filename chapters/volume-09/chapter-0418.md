# Chapter 418: A Register That Is No Longer Empty, And The Third Time In A Doorway That Neither Of Them Has A Price For

The eighth day of the eighty-first week was his hundred and sixty-eighth morning and the eighteenth day after the settlement, and the fever was nineteen weeks and two days old, and the rail had been down eight days, and a sheet came down the hill at the second hour with a seal on it, and it was four lines long, and the clerk of forty read it out in the public room at the foot of the hill to nine people because the boy of sixteen had said nothing about it and had said nothing in four doorways and had not been asked to.

“*One. The register of disputes concerning roads, kept at the seat, is not to be left empty. Two. A person is appointed in it and the name is entered. Three. A dispute concerning a road, or the passage over a road, or the keeping of a road, may be given to that person in the same terms as before. Four. The person appointed is of the seat and is not of this city, and disputes given to that person are not disputes that are lost.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Say that last line again,*” said the man of fifty-two.

The clerk of forty read it again and said that he was sorry and that he was a copyist and not a reader and that he had been the second person in three weeks to have a sentence in a room read out twice, and nobody told him he was not to, and the man of fifty-two said it once and no more.

“*Say what that is,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*It is the third clause of the sheet of the second day of the eightieth week with the register that was empty in it,*” said Tarin Keel, from the wall. “*Word for word, with the words at the front of it taken off. Which means that a person has been put in it, and the person is of the seat, and a dispute about a road in this city can now be handed to a man four hundred miles off, and the sheet at the foot of it says it is not a dispute that is lost, and I would like somebody in this room to say what is worse than losing one.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Say the name.*”

“*It is cut into the die at the foot of it and it is a person and it is not a person of this place,*” said the clerk of forty, “*and I have copied it fair and I have not read it and I am not going to read it and I am not going to be asked.*”

Nobody asked him.

And at the change, at the end of the low row, in the doorway of the house with the step that the woman of twenty-eight has been going to for forty-nine days, two men who are not of this city stood and said things to each other for about eleven minutes, and the boy of sixteen came up the row at the last of the light and stood at the end of it and stayed there.

“*Say the thing you have come to say,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and I have not got a tray and I have not got a room and I have not got a person who can act, and I am going to stand in this doorway and say the thing to you, which is my own method used backwards, and I know that it is, and I would rather do it in a doorway than not do it.*”

“*Then here it is, and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it well.*” Tarin Keel put his hand flat on the door frame. “*I say a thing to the one person who can act. I have said it forty times to a magistrate and a bench of four and a man on a cart and a clerk with a tray in this city in nine days. And the only person in this city who can act about a road is a man of thirty-eight with a book, and he is of the seat, and he has four days left, and on the fourth day of the eighty-second week he goes out of this city on a cart and I will not be here.*”

“*And I say a thing in front of other people,*” Ilyan said. “*That is the other half and I have never once been wrong about it. Nine days and four names in books in two cities, and the whole of the road has not moved.*”

“*You have never once been wrong,*” said Tarin Keel, “*and you have put a woman of twenty-eight at the top of a page and a clerk of thirty on a line and a carter of nineteen in a column and a roadkeeper of fifty-eight in a book, and every one of those was a true thing said in a room in front of people, and the rail came down on the sixth day of the eightieth week from a piece of paper with wax on it and none of the four of them had anything to do with it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Then what is left.*”

“*Walking there,*” said Tarin Keel.

Nobody said anything.

“*Say that again,*” said Ilyan.

“*The office is a person at the end of a road and a book. That is what a clerk of thirty said to a woman at her own step on the seventh day of last week at the last of the light and it is the only sentence anybody in this city has produced in a fortnight that I have not been able to argue with. So there is a person at the end of the road. I am going to go and stand at the other end of it.*”

The boy of sixteen was at the end of the row and neither of them had looked at him.

“*Four hundred miles,*” said Ilyan.

“*On foot, mostly, and on carts where a man will take me and be paid for it, which I have not arranged and am not going to arrange and am not going to ask anybody in this city to arrange, because a man of the seat with a book is in this city for four more days and I have thought about that.*”

“*Then say the price of it out loud,*” said Ilyan, “*before either of us moves, as I have done four times in this city, and to you, because you are a person who can act and you are the only one in this doorway.*”

“*I know what you are doing,*” said Tarin Keel.

“*I know you know. I am doing it because I cannot not, and I have got nothing to put in it, and I am going to say that out loud in a doorway where a boy of sixteen can hear it because he is going to hear it anyway.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*I have been in this city eighteen days and I have named a price in a room four times and every time there was a person in it whose price it was. I do not have one this time. I have got a clerk who is on a cart, and a woman of twenty-eight who is at the top of a page and cannot say no, and a woman of forty-four who has a lamp on and a roll under it and is in a record, and a boy of nineteen who has been given a figure of his own work and did not ask for one, and a man of fifty-eight at a crossing who signed his own name this morning in front of me, and a boy of sixteen who has been asked one question about himself in ten days and has put four doorways into a record by saying a thing in them. That is every person in this ward I could name a price about and I have spent all of them, and there is nobody left in this city for me to put a person into, and I have not got a road's price and I have never in my life been able to name one, and I have stood in four rooms in eighteen days trying.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of sixteen at the end of the row said, “*Then you have run out of people and not out of anything else.*”

“*That is the sentence,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have been in a doorway four hundred yards from that man for two days and I have been trying to get him to say a true thing in a room and he will not, because he says things to the one person who can act and the one person who can act is four hundred miles off, and that is his method and it is correct and it does not get anybody anywhere, and I have been saying things in front of other people for eighteen days and I have got four books out of it.*” Tarin Keel took his hand off the door frame. “*Those are the two things this city has and neither of them is a road. That is the whole of what I came to say in a doorway and I have said it in about nine minutes because I have had it in my head since the first day of this week.*”

“*Then go,*” Ilyan said, “*and I am not going to stop you and I am not going to put it in the row, because if I put it in the row then four people in this ward know that a man is walking out of this city, and a man of the seat with a book is in this city for four more days, and there is no form in this city that says a person may be sent for, and you are the only man in it that can be sent for and is not.*”

Nobody stopped him. He said the price out loud in a doorway, in his own mouth, in advance, to a man who could act, and the price did not stop him, and that is the first time in eighteen days that a price has not stopped something, and neither of them said so.

“*You have got about half an hour of light,*” said Tarin Keel.

“*Then I will not use it.*”

Tarin Keel went down the row and did not look back, and the boy of sixteen watched him go and did not ask where, and the man of thirty-eight with the book was at the end of the ninth row at the change and nobody told him anything about it.

And the boy of sixteen said it in four doorways before the light went, and nobody stopped him, because there is no form in this city that says a person may be stopped in a doorway, and Tarin Keel heard the second one of the four from a gate and did not turn round, and the four bells went over Tallowgate, and a clerk of thirty was on his own two legs somewhere north of here, and a roadkeeper was in a hut with a fire in it, and the fever was nineteen weeks and two days old, and it did not stop for a doorway, and nobody in that row was thanked.
