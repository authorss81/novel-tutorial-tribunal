# Chapter 423: The First Dispute Out Of This City To Reach A Register Four Hundred Miles Off, And It Is Not A Person, And The Form That Comes Back Wants A Number Of Persons

The fifth day of the eighty-second week was his hundred and seventy-third morning and the twenty-third day after the settlement, and the fever was twenty weeks old, and the rail had been down thirteen days, and a return came up the hill to the black building at the second hour with wax on it, and the clerk of forty read it out standing up in the public room at the foot of the hill in about thirty people's hearing, and it was the second thing he had ever read out in his life and he has been on the ground floor three years.

“*The office has received a dispute concerning the passage over the Low Road at the ninth marker in this city, given by a person of this place, and the dispute is entered in the register at the seat, and the person to whom it is given is the person appointed in that register, and the person appointed is of the seat and is not of this city.*”

“*Go over that once more,*” said the man of fifty-two, from the end of the long table, where he had come up from the end of the plate yard's and had not been sent for.

The clerk of forty read it again. He read it in the same voice and he did not put his hand flat on the table the way the man of thirty-eight does, and he has said in this room that he is not going to.

“*And the office has read the dispute and has had it read to it twice, and the office's answer is in four lines, and this is the four lines.*”

“*Say them.*”

“*One. The bar upon the Low Road at the ninth marker is put up and is to remain up. Two. The persons and the premises on the north side of the crossing have been given notice. Three. Nothing in this return is a finding about the road. Four. A dispute given to the person appointed in the register is a dispute given, and is not a dispute that is lost.*”

Nobody said anything, and the room sat with it, and a boy of eight was at the back of it with a barrow between his knees, and he is eight and he is better than he was and he cannot be discharged and nobody in that room said his name and nobody has said it in a room in his life.

“*Say the second half of that,*” said the man with the bad ear.

“*There is a second half and I have not been asked for it and I am going to say it because I have been on this floor three years and I copy them fair and I have never read one out, and a man said a thing in this room on the first day of this month that I have not been able to argue with since, and I am going to say it out loud because he is not here.*” He turned a page over. “*A man stood against that wall on the first day of this month and said a government which reads what you write to it is a government which has not heard you, and he said it meant it, and he said it in a room, and about thirty people heard it, and a man from the seat read a magistrate's objection in that same room on the same day and answered it in four lines and the answer was correct. And that man is four hundred miles up a road on his own two feet at this moment and he is not going to be in this room again, and the four lines on that sheet upstairs are the answer to a magistrate and they are the answer to that sentence too, and they are correct, and the office has read both of them and has not changed its mind by one line.*”

Nobody thanked him for it, and he had not asked to be thanked, and he put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the long table, and squaring a thing to the edge of a table is what a clerk does.

“*And the finding,*” said the man with the bad leg, who had walked a mile and a half and back yesterday and had been out on the flat twice and had not been sent for.

“*The first dispute that has gone out of this city to a register at the seat since the settlement was read out is four hurdles and a length of chain at the ninth marker,*” said the clerk of forty. “*Not a person. Not a clerk. Not a woman. Not a boy. A bar, given by a person of this place, and the person who gave it is not named on the sheet because the sheet has no line for it, and the person it is entered against is a name cut into a die in a room four hundred miles off and I have never seen it and I am not going to read it out and I am not going to be asked.*”

Nobody asked him.

---

“*And there is a second thing in the tray and it is not waxed,*” said the clerk of forty, “*and it is a form, and I have put it in the press on the ground floor and it is a penny a page and there is a window in that room, and anybody in this city can be shown it for a penny, and I have read it out twice this morning in a corridor on my own before I came down, and I am twenty-nine and I write them fair and I have never read one out to anybody, and I have stood in this room for four minutes and read two and I am not going to apologise for either of them.*”

“*Read the third thing,*” said the man of fifty-two.

The clerk of forty read it out.

“*Return of the number of the persons of this place, by ward, and of the doors by street, for the keeping of a place under the fifth row of the schedule of the first day of the eighty-first week. The return is to be made by a person of the place. The office does not provide the number and the office will not check it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Say the last of it again,*” said the man with the bad leg, and it was read again, and about thirty people heard the second reading of a sentence about the office not checking a number of persons.

“*Then here is the whole of what I have in front of me,*” said the man of fifty-two, “*and I am going to say it in a room and not in a row, and I am going to say it standing up, because I have counted halls and doors for twenty years and a man has asked me for a number and I am going to give him the shape of it before I give him anything.*”

He put his hands flat on the table.

“*That form wants two numbers off one sheet and it has room for them and it wants them from a person of this place, and it is a good form and it is legible and it is a penny a page, and there is nothing wrong with it, and there is nothing in this city that can fill in half of it.*”

“*Which half.*”

“*The doors I have got. Nine hundred doors in this ward on the day the settlement was read out and nine hundred doors in it this morning, and I have counted them myself, and I have four halls to their names and I could give you all five numbers by the change and I have given them out loud in four rooms in a fortnight. And the number of persons in this ward I do not have, and neither does the office, and neither does anybody in this room, and the reason we do not have it is that a count of persons in a ward is not a count. It is a division. You take the number of doors and you divide it by something, and the only thing in this city to divide it by is a number of people to a door, and there is no such number, because a door with nobody behind it and a door with two people behind it are the same thing on a roll and a different thing in a street, and every person in this city knows that and about nine hundred of them have just been counted.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And I am not going to give the office a number of persons in this city, because the day that number exists this city has decided what a person is, and it will not be this room that decided it, and I would like it said in here that I have said that out loud and that I have said it in front of a man from four hundred miles off.*”

“*I am not from four hundred miles off,*” said the clerk of forty. “*I am twenty-nine and I am from this city and I copy them fair and I have been on that floor three years, and I have stood here and read out two things this morning and I have said the name at the foot of a sheet with a seal on it is not a person of this place, and I would like the room to have both of those.*”

“*Then I have said it in front of a man of this city who prints the sheets,*” said the man of fifty-two, “*which is worse, and I have said it, and I am not taking it back and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

---

“*And I am going to say the second half of the whole of it, and it is a sentence about a bell and about a porch, and I am saying it because a form has come down this hill that wants a number of people and because I have been in three returns in nine weeks and not one of them had my number in it.*” The woman of sixty with a shawl had not been in the room and was at the back of it and had not come further in, and nobody had sent for her and there is no form in four hundred miles that says a person may be sent for. “*A bell in a county four hundred miles off of here is not hung. A porch in a valley of sixty people is gone. There is a corridor standing in it with a bell unpaid on it. That is three things about three places and not one of them is a number of persons, and I have said them out loud in this room before and I have never been asked to say them again and I am not being asked now, and the reason I am saying them is that a sheet has come down this hill that counts people, and I have been in this city nine years and I have never once been counted, and I would like it said that I have noticed the difference between the two, and I have noticed it, and I am going back to the wall.*”

She went back to the wall. She was not thanked and she did not expect to be and she had said in a room on the first day of this month that she was not going to be relieved about it either, and about thirty people in a room at the foot of a hill did not look at her, and nothing in this city has a form in it that says a person has to be looked at.

“*Then there is nothing anybody in this room can put in that form,*” said the man of fifty-two, “*and I have said so out loud and nobody has sent for anybody, and the office has said it will not provide the number and will not check it, and that is a form with two lines in it that no person in this city can fill in, and it is a good form, and it goes up the hill on the fourth day of the eighty-second week with a man of thirty-eight and no name in it, and I would like somebody in this room to say what happens to it in a room four hundred miles off.*”

The clerk of forty said it, because he was the only person in the room who had a pencil and a book and had been told twice this week to say whether he had written a thing down.

“*Nothing happens to it,*” said the clerk of forty. “*That is the honest answer and I have had it since the second hour. A clerk at the seat will have a line to fill and nothing to fill it off, and there is a man in this city who has already watched that happen once this month with a register and a gap in it, and he told me about it in a hut on the sixth day of this week and said the clerk would either leave it empty or the office would appoint somebody itself, and he said he thought the clerk was a good man. And I have never met the clerk at the seat. I copy fair for a man who is not here. And there is no line on this form for a person who could not fill it in.*”

The room waited.

“*Say that last part again,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*There is no line on this form for a person who could not fill it in.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of eight was at the back of the room with his barrow between his knees and had been there for an hour and a half and nobody had said his name, and the woman of twenty-eight came in at the change and stood at the end of the long table with her slate under her arm and had not been asked to and had not come for anything, and there were six people in the room and about thirty in it a quarter of an hour ago.

“*Then say who can fill it in,*” said the man of fifty-two, to the clerk of forty, “*because you have said the shape of it in front of thirty people and I have been in this room three days and the answer is a mile and a half from this hill and it has been in it all along.*”

“*I am not going to ask the room,*” said the clerk of forty, “*because I have been the one who filled a line off a paper in front of me twice this month and I am not going to be the one who points at a person.*”

“*Then I will,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, from the end of the table, and she had not been asked and nobody in that room was going to be able to say afterwards that she had been asked. “*There is one woman in this city anybody can be asked about a roll and she is a mile and a half down the low ground in a room behind a chandler's shop with a lamp on it, and she is forty-four, and she has kept it eleven years in a room off the low ground, and the office pays her four shillings a year and has not paid her the eleven years, and she is the only person in this city that a number of doors can be asked about, and I have not been asked whether I may say that and I have said it in a room, and I am not going to say her name in here because her name is at the top of a page in a city four hundred miles off and it got there off a paper that one of us carried in the ordinary way, and I am not going to be the one who puts it in a second book on the same day.*”

The clerk of forty wrote nothing down, and about six people who had been in that room all morning knew where the only number in this city was, and the boy of eight went out with his barrow and nobody said his name, and the fever was twenty weeks old, and it did not stop for a count, and nobody in that room was thanked.
