# Chapter 444: Four Wards Answer, And Every One Of Them Fills In Two Lines, And Two Of Them Name The Same Person Twice, And An Answer To One Sentence Comes Down The Hill On The Fifth Day

Fifth day of the eighty-fifth week. Shelf day three hundred and nineteen, his hundred and ninety-fourth morning, forty-four days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and no days, the rail down thirty-four days.

Two carts came up the hill on the day before and one came up it this morning, and they brought four sheets, and one of them was not from a ward.

Ott Dray was on the wheel of the second one and he put it down at the top of the low ground and did not come up, and a carter who is paid by the mile does not carry paper up a hill for nothing, and nobody in this city had asked him to and nobody in this city told him not to.

He waited about forty minutes with the horse's head at the wall and then he took the four sheets up in two loads on his own back, and he did that for no reason anybody could name, and he has never put a name on it, and there is no line for it in any book in this county.

“*Say the working on that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Skell.

“*It is four sheets and two loads and about a hundred and ten steps,*” said Dray. “*I have carried thirty-one sheets for the office in nineteen years and thirty-two of them in all, and I have never been asked what was in one of them, and yesterday I carried four up a hill to a room and I did not open one of them, and I have not been asked about that either, and I have noticed that nobody has asked me about it.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*The other half is that I have been on a wheel for nineteen years and the only thing I am paid on is a mile, and a mile does not go up a hill twice, and I did it twice.*"

“*Say the wards first,*” said Skell. “*Say them in the order they came. I have asked for the order four times in six weeks and I am asking for it now, because three of them are going to have a person's name at the foot of one of them, and the man who wrote the form is going to be on the other line.*”

“*One,*” said Ferrow. “*The ward at the end of the plate yard. One person named for the first line: Darrin Skell, counting hall and doors. One person named for the second line, to stand beside the name at the foot of the form: Renn Calloway, keeping a road.*”

“*Say the second.*”

“*The ward at the end of the low row. First line: Fenna Rusk, keeping a roll of doors. Second line: Fenna Rusk, keeping a roll of doors.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Skell.

“*The second line names the same person as the first line. The sheet says so in a line of its own at the foot, which says the ward has named the same person twice and has not been asked whether it may.*”

---

“*Say the third and the fourth.*”

“*Three. A ward at the middle of the nine bridges. First line: Nell Fisk, who reads out. Second line: Darrin Skell, counting hall and doors.*”

“*And four.*”

“*Four. A ward by the rail, where the rail is still against the north wall with its own sheet nailed to it. First line: Bess Low, sixty, who reads what arrives. Second line: Bess Low, sixty, who reads what arrives.*”

“*Say the working on that,*” said Skell, “*because I am the only man in this room who is good at it, and it is a small piece of it, and I am going to do it standing up.*”

He put his hands flat on the table.

“*Four wards out of thirty-one. Four first lines and four second lines. Four people named in the first lines and four in the second, and two of the four wards have put the same name in both of theirs.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And one of the second lines is the man who is in the first line of a different ward, and the office has not been asked which. And the two wards that put the same name in both of their lines have between them not been asked whether they may.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not do a division on it.*”

“*Four wards have produced eight names, and eight names are five people, and it is five because the ward at the end of the low row has the same name in both of its lines, and the ward by the rail has the same name in both of its lines, and I am the third of those three, and I read four sheets out loud in this room this morning and I did not see my own name on two of them. And about twenty-seven wards have not answered at all.*”

He took his hands off the table.

“*And the office said in the third line of its sheet that a ward which sends none has not declined and is not entered as having declined. That is the best sentence the office has sent this city in six weeks, and it is a best sentence that nobody can do anything with.*”

“*Say why nobody can do anything with it,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because a sentence that protects a silence is not a protection. It says that nothing happened. And in four days a ward in this city can be asked about a road by an office, and the answer to that is that nothing happened, and nothing is not a thing a person can be asked about.*”

---

The fifth sheet was not from a ward.

It came down in the tray on the fifth day of the eighty-fifth week with wax on it, and it had four lines under a head, and it was an answer.

“*Say how long it took,*” said Skell, before the man with the pencil had it open.

“*It went up on the first day of this week at the end of the day, in an envelope with no seal on it,*” said Ferrow. “*It came down on the fifth day, and that is four days.*”

“*Say the sentence that went up.*”

“*The office is asked whether a person of this place named beside a name in a form or a return may be asked by the office concerning the name so placed beside them.*”

“*And the answer.*”

“*One. The office may ask a person of this place concerning anything in the form or return in which they are named.*”

“*Say the second one.*”

“*Two. The office has a return of this city in which one column carries two names, and no form of the office carries two names in one column, and the office does not require a city to use the forms of the office, and does not check a second name in a column, and does not require one.*”

“*Say the third one and go slowly,*” said Ilyan.

“*Three. The office has asked the person named beside the name of the person not of this city, at the seat, in a room there, who placed that name in the line beside their own, and requires an answer within five days.*”

“*Say the last one.*”

“*Four. Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road.*”

The room at the foot of the hill had about thirty people in it, and it had gone very quiet in the way a room goes quiet when a thing that happened in a room off the low ground at the fourth hour turns up in a tray.

---

Ilyan wanted something out of that room and it was a bad thing to want, and he had wanted it before anybody said a word about it, and he said it out loud, which he does.

“*Say what I want,*” he said. “*Say it before the room does it for me. And then I am going to sit down and not say anything else about it for a week.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I want the second foot line filled in by somebody who is not in this room. I want it filled in by a ward that does not know me. And I want the office to have a person to ask about me who is a person of this place and whom I have never spoken to about it.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The other half is that I do not want to see it happen. I have wanted that since the second hour this morning, and the reason I wanted it is that I do not want to see it happen.*”

“*That is not a want,*” said Skell. “*That is a thing a man says in a room so that the room does not look at what he did.*”

“*Then say what I did,*” Ilyan said.

“*You wrote a line on the fifth of six pages, and you left the other one empty, and you put your own name under it, and you did it in a room with nine people in it, and then you set it in a window in the rain where anybody could read it.*”

He put his hands flat on the table.

“*And a clerk four hundred miles off did not read it out of a window either,*” said Skell. “*He read a column of a return that has been in a book since the second day of the eighty-third week with two names in it, and he asked a woman at a town four hundred miles off who put you there, and the answer went up a hill, and it is going to come down in five days with her name in it.*”

---

Fenna Rusk had been sitting at the second chair along since the sheets were read out and she had not moved, and she put both hands flat on the wood.

“*Do not go out of this room,*” she said. “*You have been going out of rooms every time I have said something, and I have watched you do it four times, and every one of the four times was for me.*”

“*I had said I would.*”

“*I know what you had said. Stay in it.*”

He stayed in it. He had a leg that had been on a chair since the second hour and about thirty people in front of him, and he stayed in it, and that was the whole of what he did.

She said four things and it took her about two minutes.

“*That I was entered in a column of a return on the second day of the eighty-third week by a man who said the price out loud in four inches of mud in front of eight people. And that the office then wrote down that he had said the price before he entered me, because a register ought to hold that kind of thing.*”

“*The second thing, and do not make it smaller,*” said Skell.

“*That I answered the question the office put to me at the seat in the eighty-third week. I said yes seven times. I have never once been thanked for one of the seven, and I told this room in the eighty-fourth week that I would not be the voice in this city that a man in a book is safe.*”

“*The third thing.*”

“*That the office has now asked me a second time, four hundred miles off, in a room with a window in it, at about the fourth hour on the day before. And that the person who put the question to me was not of this city, and read the sentence out of a book, and did not ask me one question of his own.*”

Then she said the fourth thing, in her own mouth, to about thirty people, and she did not turn round.

“*He put himself there. He wrote the line on the fifth of six pages and he wrote his own name under it and he left the second line empty in a window of a press on the Friday, and he has not asked me, and he is not going to ask me.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it,*” said Skell.

“*And the office has asked me at a town four hundred miles off at the fourth hour. And I am going to answer the office, and I am going to answer it in this room, and I am going to say the name of the man, and he is standing in it.*”

She said it in her own mouth.

“*Ilyan Vester,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*put me in that line on the fourth day of the eighty-fifth week in his own hand.*”

“*The whole of it and not the first half,*” said Skell.

“*And I am not going to say one word about whether that was a good thing to do, and neither is he, and there is no form in this county that lets either of us say a word about it.*”

About thirty people in that room watched a man with a bad leg not say the thing he had come into the room with, in front of a woman who had been entered in a book in the eighty-third week, and he did not say it, and it was the first time in five weeks he had been in a room with a want in his mouth and had kept it shut.

Then Ferrow read out what he had written, and it was four sentences, and one of them had a name in it, and the man with the pencil said out loud that he had not been asked to leave it out and would not have left it.

---

The answer went up the hill on the evening of the fifth day of the eighty-fifth week in an envelope with no seal on it, and it was one line, and the line was a name.

And the form stood in the window of the press with a hand in the second foot line of the fifth page that was not the compositor's, and a name in it, and about nine people a day read that page in the rain outside a shop and did not have anybody to say it to.

The fever was twenty-three weeks and no days old, and it did not stop for four answers or for a fifth sheet in a tray, and nobody in that room thanked anybody, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be thanked.
