# Chapter 448: The Terms Come Down In The Ordinary Way On Six Short Clauses, And Two Of Them Are About A Man Who Has Refused, And Nobody Is Relieved

Second day of the eighty-sixth week. Shelf day three hundred and twenty-three, his hundred and ninety-eighth morning, forty-eight days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and four days, the rail down thirty-eight days.

There were about thirty people in the long room at the foot of the hill and a man of sixty-one was at the back with his coat still wet from the road, and at the second hour Ferrow had six clauses under a head and wax on the sheet, and he had it open before anybody had sat down, because it had come in the tray at half past the hour and the tray had gone round.

“*Say the head,*” said Skell.

“*In the matter of the office of Regent of the Shelf, and of a form of the city of this place concerning the naming of a person of a ward, and of the dispute concerning the passage over the Low Road at the ninth marker, given to the person appointed on the fifth day of the eighty-second week.*”

“*Say the first clause and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*One. The instrument of succession lodged with the office on the sixth day of the eighty-fifth week is received, and is entered, and is not withdrawn.*”

“*Say the rest of that clause and do not make it smaller than it is.*”

“*And the person named in it has declined the office in a room of about thirty persons in this city on the seventh day of the eighty-fifth week, and the declining is entered, and the declining is not a finding about any road.*”

---

“*Say the second one and do not stop half way through it,*” said Ilyan.

“*Two. The writ of succession provides for the passing of the office upon the ceasing of the holder of it, and names the person in an instrument, and makes no provision whatever for a person who is named in an instrument and declines.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*And the office notes that it has no such provision and does not supply one, and the office is not held.*”

Nobody in the room said anything at all for about five seconds, and a woman of twenty-eight at the back said the half of it before anybody else did.

“*Say the rest of that in your own mouth,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The office is not held,*” Hessa Marl said. “*And I have been at that box fourteen days counting what goes past a crossing on a slate, and the office is not held, and there is nobody to hold it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Skell.

“*And my form has one line in it with my name in it, and the office keeps a book of the visits, and there is no office to keep the book, and I do not know what happens to a book when there is no office, and neither does anybody in this room, and I have looked.*”

“*Say the third clause,*” said Ilyan.

“*Three. The office is to be filled in the ordinary way by the council of the Shelf at its next sitting. And until the office is filled, no bar upon any road in this city is to be taken down, and no plate concerning any road in this city is to be put up.*”

“*Say the rest of that clause and do not leave it in the middle.*”

“*And the office does not require this city to be represented in the council and is not able to require it.*”

“*Then the bar stays up,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*Then the bar stays up,*” said Ferrow, “*and so does the plate, and so does the chain, and so does everything else that is on the north side of the ninth marker.*”

He squared the sheet to the edge of the table.

“*And it stays up because there is nobody left who is able to take it down, and about nine people in this room have spent six weeks asking for that, and it has arrived by accident.*”

---

The other three clauses were shorter, and Ferrow read them out at the same pace, and the fourth and the sixth of them were read twice.

“*Four. The twenty-two wards that have named persons have done so under a form of this city and not under a form of the office, and the office notes the naming, does not check it, and may ask a person so named concerning the roads, the keeping of them and any bar upon them.*”

“*Say the fifth one again and then say what it is,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*It is the fourth clause of the sheet of the fifth day of the eighty-third week, word for word, and it has been in two documents in eighteen days.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it larger than it is.*”

“*And it is the only sentence anybody has sent this city about money in six weeks, and it does not pay anything.*”

“*Say the last one.*”

“*Six. Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road, and nothing in it is a finding about any person in any city, and it takes effect according to its terms and not otherwise.*”

“*Say the working on the whole of it,*” said Skell. “*Is there a line in six clauses that pays a person anything.*”

“*No,*” said Ferrow.

“*Say the list out loud, and say the whole of it, because there is a man with a pencil and I would rather he wrote it than that I said it.*”

“*The twelve shillings, unpaid, noted.*”

He went down the list in the same voice, and he did not raise it or lower it for any item in it.

“*Two pounds and four shillings, refused by the person it is owed to in front of nine people on the fourth day of the eighty-fourth week, unpaid, noted. Four pounds for a bell in a county four hundred miles off, eleven pence against eleven names, one name without a penny, one penny without a name, the difference one, not hung.*”

“*Say the rest of the list and do not stop in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*Four shillings the hundred for a bill on a wall with three hundred names blank on it, and the blank line is the correct figure and is not filled in. One farthing a visit for a place kept at a crossing, payable to a person entered on a form with one line, and the visits are in a book and the crossings are on a slate, and a crossing is not a visit.*”

He put the sheet down.

“*That is the list, and there is not one line in six clauses that pays any of it, and I have read them twice.*”

---

Marrow had not asked to speak and he was not asked, and he stood up at about the third hour and asked for the room to hear one thing, and the room heard it.

“*Say it in your own words and not in the office's,*” said Ilyan.

“*In my own words. Nineteen years ago there was one instrument, and it was a piece of paper, and it said who held the roads, and I have spent nineteen years being that piece of paper.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on, and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*And on the sixth day of that week, in the night, I put my own name on a second piece of paper and stepped out of the chair. Because a succession you arrange yourself is the only kind there is that never has a vacancy in it, and I have watched three men do it in my time, and two of them were worse than me.*”

He looked at the long table.

“*And a man of about forty came down to the ninth marker in the night and would not take the chair, and I have lost, and I lost it to a refusal. And I have never in nineteen years been refused by a person, and I have never been refused by a piece of paper either.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and say it whole,*” said Skell.

“*And I am not going to stand in this room and blame this city for it, because every person in it has done what I would have done, and I have done two of the four myself this week.*”

“*Say why not,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because the third clause suspends my road power in this city until a council four hundred miles off sits, and the council will sit, and there are people in it who will fill the office, and the roads will go back to an office.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not be certain of it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*I have no doubt at all that they will. Because a road with no office on it is a road that gets decided by whoever is standing nearest it, and I have seen what that costs in a fever winter, and I have written the number down more than once.*”

“*Then say the last of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the last of it is that a man who cannot be sent for has kept that road for twenty-nine years, and there is no form in this county that says a man may be sent for, and I would like somebody in this room to write that down as the fault it is and not as a virtue it has.*”

He sat down.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Renn Calloway, from the wall.

“*It is both,*” said Marrow. “*I have been saying so in four rooms for forty years and nobody in any of them has written it down, and it is the reason I came.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle,*” said Skell.

“*And I am going down the road in the rain in the morning, and round the four hurdles on the south side, because I have been on that mud once and I am not going to be a man who has been on it once.*”

---

Skell made him get it into one sentence, and it took most of an hour, and about four people in the room tried first and made it longer.

Before he began, Ilyan said the one thing he had come into the room with, out loud, because he always says it out loud and because about thirty people had heard him say it on Monday in a different room and had not believed a word of it.

“*I wanted the bar down,*” he said. “*I have wanted that bar down for five weeks and eleven days and I have said so in four rooms and in a market of two hundred people, and I have not got it down, and this morning it is still up, and the only reason it is still up is the third clause of a sheet about an office that is not held, written by a man who has never been to this city, and that is not a thing I can put in a sentence and call a result.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop half way through it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And the last three words of my sentence are the only part of it I wanted to write. A road is kept. I have wanted those three words since a man said them to me in a hut in the seventy-ninth week, and I have been writing a form about persons for two days, and I put them in because Skell told me not to put anything in that this room would want, and I broke that in the last three words and I want it entered that I broke it.*”

“*Say it in one sentence and say it as a fact,*” he said, “*and I do not want a settlement in it, and I do not want anything in it that anybody here would want, because a thing everybody in this room wants is a thing a room has wanted before and we have been here before.*”

He stood up with a bad leg and had it in about nine attempts and said the tenth one.

“*The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held by anybody. And until the council of the Shelf fills it, no bar upon any road in this city is to be taken down, and no plate concerning any road in this city is to be put up.*”

“*Say the rest of the sentence and do not stop half way through it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And twenty-two of thirty-one wards have named twenty-four persons who may be asked about the roads, and one line has a box in it and no person, and nine wards have named nobody.*”

“*And the last three words,*” said Skell.

“*And a road is kept.*”

He stopped there, and the room had it.

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The other half is the last three words, and I put them in there because Skell told me not to put anything in that this room would want.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*A road is kept is the only part of it that is true, and it is true by a man in a hut, a woman in a box, and a man with a barrow, and none of the three of them has been paid, and none of the three of them is in any of the six clauses.*”

---

His standing in this city was settled in the same room, at about the fifth hour, by a man with a book and a man with a pencil, and it was not what he had wanted, and it took about four minutes and it was not a speech.

“*Say what is entered,*” said Cope, reading his own page.

“*That a person not of this city was named in an instrument of succession lodged with the office on the sixth day of the eighty-fifth week, and declined the office in a room of about thirty persons on the seventh day of that week, in the words of an exception dated the first day of that week, and that the declining is entered, and that the writ is silent.*”

“*Say the rest of it and there is a rest of it.*”

“*And that the person so named is entered in a form of this city at the foot of the fifth page, in the first of two lines, and that twenty-two wards have put a person of this place beside him who may be asked concerning him.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*”

“*And that one of those persons is a woman of forty-four who keeps a roll of doors at the low end of Tallowgate, and that a person cannot be struck out of a line in this county, and that a person not of this city cannot be entered in a register of this city, and that both of those are correct, and that both of them are in the same book.*”

“*And the rest of it.*” said Skell.

“*And that there is no line in any of the six pages, or in the six clauses, or in the writ, for the person not of this city to be taken off the foot of a form. And that he is not able to take himself off it, and that he did not ask to be put on it, and that he wrote the line himself, in a room off the low ground, on the fourth day of the eighty-fifth week, in his own hand.*”

Nobody in that room said one word to him, and there is no form in this county that a man may be thanked on, and about nine people in that room had not said one word to him in five weeks.

The fever was twenty-three weeks and four days old, and the office of Regent of the Shelf was not held, and the four hurdles, the nine links, the plate and the box were still on the north side of the ninth marker because nobody had the power to take them down. The south side of that crossing still had no name in any document anywhere, and about two hundred crossings a day went up four yards of mud in the weather.
