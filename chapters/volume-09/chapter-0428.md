# Chapter 428: A Form With Two Questions In One Line In It, And A Boy Of Sixteen Who Is The Only Person In This City Who Can Put A Person Of A Place Into A Book By Saying A Thing Out Loud

The third day of the eighty-third week was his hundred and seventy-eighth morning and the twenty-eighth day after the settlement, and the fever was twenty weeks and five days old, and the rail had been down eighteen days, and a form came down the hill in the tray at the second hour, and it was not waxed, and the clerk of forty read it out on his feet in the long room at the foot of the hill in about thirty people's hearing, and it was the seventh thing he had read out in thirteen days and he had said on the first day of this week that he was not going to apologise for any of them.

“*Enter a person under age, named in a return as having carried a statement concerning a road from four doorways of the low row into a building at the middle of the nine bridges on the seventh day of the eightieth week, at the ordinary hour and in the ordinary way.*”

“*Go over the second line,*” said the man of fifty-two.

“*Whether the person so named reported the road unsafe, and if not, the name of the person who did.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*That is the whole of it,*” said the clerk of forty. “*It is one line and there is a line for yes and there is a line for no and there is a line for a name, and the line for a name has a head on it that says name of the person who did, and there is nothing else on the paper, and it is a decent form and it can be read standing up and it is a penny a page and there is a window in that room.*”

“*Then say what a person under age does with it,*” said the man with the bad ear, who had come a mile and a half and had not been sent for, “*because there is a line at the top of that form for the name of the person who signs, and I have watched a man of the seat come four hundred miles to be told by a boy on a step that he could not sign it, and I would like to know whether anybody has thought about that this week.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of sixteen was in the doorway of the room, and he had been at a door at that hour since the eighth morning of a fever, and there is no form in this city that says a person may be at a door instead of in it, and about thirty people in that room did not turn round, and he came in.

He came in and stood at the end of the long table and did not ask anybody to read it to him, and about nine people at that end of the room noticed that and nobody said anything about it.

“*Say the line again,*” said the boy of sixteen.

The clerk of forty read the second line again, in the same voice, and about thirty people heard the second reading and the man with the bad ear did not remark on the asking and nobody in that room did.

“*It is one line and it is asking two things,*” said the boy of sixteen. “*Did you carry a statement about a road. And did you report the road unsafe. And the answer to the first one is yes and the answer to the second one is no, and there is one line on that form and the line takes one, and I am not going to choose between them in a line, and I would like somebody in this room to say what happens to a person who answers half of a question.*”

The clerk of forty said it, because he had the form in his hand and had been asked for the second half all week.

“*Nothing happens to him,*” said the clerk of forty. “*That is the honest answer. A line takes one answer and a form with a line in it does not have a place for half of an answer, and the office does not require a half, and I am not going to be the man who works out which half was meant and writes that half, because that is making a thing up and I write down things that are in front of me and I have been on that floor three years and that is the whole of what I do and I have not got better at it in a fortnight.*”

“*Then say what you are going to write,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*I am going to write that a person under age was offered the form and read the second line and said the second line is two questions and he would not choose between them, and that he did not report the road unsafe, and that he carried a statement about a road on the seventh day of the eightieth week from four doorways in the ordinary way, which is in the return already and which I have not entered twice.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And the line for the name of the person who did.*” The boy of sixteen had not looked at the form. “*I am not going to put a name in it.*”

“*Then the form is not filled in,*” he said, “*and a form that is not filled in goes up the hill on the fourth day of this week with a man of thirty-eight or on a cart, and the office has a line and nothing to fill it off, and I have watched that happen once this month in a register and a man in a room told me the clerk there would either leave it empty or the office would appoint somebody itself. And I have not met that clerk and I copy fair for a man who is not here.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and say it going down the row and not in a room, because the rest of it is the part that matters, which is what happens to the person whose name is not in a line.*”

The boy of sixteen stood at the end of the long table with his hands behind him and he did not say it for a while, and about thirty people who had come up that hill waited for a boy of sixteen, which is a thing that happens in this city and is not a thing anybody has written down.

“*Nothing happens to him either,*” said the boy of sixteen. “*And that is the part I have been trying to get at since the second hour and I have got it now and I am going to say it once and then I am going to go and do something else. The man of this ward who heard one of the four of what I said and wrote it down and carried a paper about it into a building at the middle of the nine bridges in the ordinary way at the ordinary hour, and a clerk of thirty who filled a line off that paper, are not in that form. Neither of them is in it and neither of them is named on it and neither of them has been asked anything, and I am the only person in this city who could put either of them in it, and there is no form in four hundred miles that asks a person of sixteen whether he may.*”

About thirty people in that room had been asked nothing all morning and had watched it happen to a boy of sixteen instead.

“*And I have been asked one question about myself since the first day of this month and that was fifteen days ago and a man came down this row with a sheet and asked me whether I wished to be struck out of a register, and I said no, and he wrote it down in a book, and that was the first one. And this is the second one and it is a different one and it is worse, because the first one was about whether I was in a thing and this one is about whether I put somebody else in a thing, and I did not know on the first day of this month that I could do that. I have been in four doorways in a fortnight and I have got a man's name into a book and a clerk's name into a book and a woman's name into a book four hundred miles off, and every one of them was me standing in a doorway saying a thing out loud, and none of them were because anybody thought I knew anything.*”

The man of fifty-two was at the end of the long table and had not said a word and had not been asked one, and the man at the wheel was not in that room.

“*So I am going to say why I am not going to name him, and then I am going to leave this room and I am not going to say it in a row either, because a row is how a thing goes into four mouths and a room is how a thing is heard once.*”

“*Say it,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and I have been asked for the second half four times this week and I have got three of them and this is the fourth and it is going to be a boy of sixteen in a room and I would like the room to notice that as well.*”

“*If I say his name then the whole of what I have been in for a fortnight was because of who I know and not because of what I said,*” said the boy of sixteen. “*And what I said was that a rail was up on a road. I said it about a road four times and I did not say a word about a person, and I have said it since to a man in a doorway and to a woman of thirty with a head on her knee and to about four hundred people who came and stood where I was standing, and every one of those people came because of what I said and not because of who carried it. And I am not going to be the one standing in a doorway who says a person's name about a road.*”

The clerk of forty wrote in a book with a pencil and did not look down at it, and the boy of sixteen watched him do it and did not ask what he wrote, and the man with the bad leg watched him do it and did not ask either.

“*What are you writing.*”

“*That a person under age was offered the form, read the second line, and declined to name, and that the form has no line for a person who will not name, and that the person so named is not entered.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of sixteen said the thing that was in the room, and he said it in his own mouth, and nobody had asked him for it, and about thirty people were in the room and two of them could have said no and neither of them did.

“*Put down that I was asked and I did not,*” he said, “*and if you have a line for a person who was asked and did not then put me in it, and if you do not then put down nothing, and I do not mind which, and I am not going to be thanked for either, because there is nobody in this room who can do that and I have not asked.*”

He went out of the room and did nine doors, because that is what he does, and he did not say anything at one of them.

And at about the fourth hour a woman of sixty with a shawl read out, in the same room, in about nine people's hearing, a paper that had come in the tray with the form and had not been waxed and had an address at the top of it in a hand nobody in this city knows.

“*It is four paragraphs and it is tired, and I am going to say so before I read it and not after,*” she said. “*It is from a person who has been doing this work longer than the man with the bad leg and who is not in this city. It is not a sheet of the office and it does not have a seal on it and it is in the tray because somebody put it in the tray, and there is no form in four hundred miles about a tray.*”

“*Read it,*” said the man of fifty-two.

“*It is correct about the work. It says that a count of doors and a count of people are two different things and that a person who cannot say no is not a witness and that a witness who is in a record is a person the office can ask, and it says that a bar is a rule with a room missing out of it, and it says all four of those in about six lines and every one of them is right, and I have heard all four of them said out loud in this city in the last fortnight by four different people and one of them was a boy of sixteen and one of them was a woman of forty-four who cannot see a slate.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And it is wrong about the plan. It says that the thing to do is to ask the office to do it properly, once, in the right terms, and that the office holds the roads and is the only hand that can take a bar down, and that a thing a government can be asked to do is a thing a hundred other cities can be asked to do the same way, and that is the whole of the plan and it is a good plan and it is wrong, and it is wrong because a count got put in a column on a form this month in this city and went up a hill in a coat on the afternoon of the second day of this week, and the woman who kept it cannot tell anybody whether it is right any more, and that is what happens to the count the moment the office is asked to hold it.*”

About nine people who had stayed in that room had heard a sheet read out that said four true things and one wrong one and nobody in the room had said one kind word to anybody, and the clerk of forty did not copy it into a book because it was not in front of him, and the woman of twenty-eight was at a door at the end of the low row and was not in the room and was not told, and the paper went in a pocket of a man of fifty-two who took it home and put it on a table, and nobody thanked the woman four hundred miles off who had written it, and there is a claim of six years in her family's name that has not been filed and nobody in this city has asked her about it and nobody in this city is going to, and the fever was twenty weeks and five days old, and it did not stop for a form with two questions in one line in it, and nobody in that room was thanked.
