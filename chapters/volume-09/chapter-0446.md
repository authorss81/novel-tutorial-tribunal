# Chapter 446: The Man Who Wrote The Third Line Of The Writ Comes Up The Road On Foot And Goes Round On The Mud Side, And Is Refused Line By Line In The Terms Of The Offer

Seventh day of the eighty-fifth week. Shelf day three hundred and twenty-one, his hundred and ninety-sixth morning, forty-six days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and two days, the rail down thirty-six days.

He was at the back of the long room at the foot of the hill at the second hour. He had come in the ordinary way on the ordinary road, and he had gone round the four hurdles on the south side, in four yards of mud, in the rain, at about the fourth hour in the morning. He was wet to the knee when he came in, and he had not asked anybody to stop him.

He was sixty-one or about it and grey, and he had the look of a man who has been in rooms that are not this one for forty years. About thirty people in that room had never seen him. Nine of them found out inside of a minute.

“*Say the name that is cut into the foot of the fifth sheet of the second day of the week,*” said Skell, “*and then say the half of the fourth line out loud, because there is a woman of twenty-eight at the back of this room who has had a form in a book since the eighty-first week and has never been told whose office it is.*”

“*They are the same name,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and I have known it since Tuesday, and so has the man of the seat, and so has the man with the pencil. About nine people in this room have not known it until this second.*”

“*Say it out loud anyway,*” said Skell, “*because there is a woman of twenty-eight at the back of this room who has been putting a name on a form for the office since the eighty-second week and has never once been told whose office it is.*”

“*Iven Marrow,*” said the man at the back. “*Regent of the Shelf until the fourth hour of last night, and not since. I have come to be told what a room says about it, and not to have it said to me twice.*”

---

Wray Cope had the book open before Marrow had his coat off, and he did not stand up.

“*Say the one thing I am going to say,*” said Cope, “*and then we will go on and I will not say it again.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*You came round on the mud. There is a line in my book for what I have asked and a line for what I have been told, and there is no line for a man of the seat of the Shelf walking round a bar in the mud at the fourth hour in the morning. I am going to enter the walking, and I am not going to enter the mud.*”

“*Enter the mud,*” said Marrow. “*I went through it. It is a foot and a half of ledge and four yards of it, and I am sixty-one with a bad knee on the other side, and I went through it.*”

“*Then say the rest of it and do not make it a speech,*” said Skell.

“*And the man who keeps that road has kept it for twenty-nine years, and does not come into this room, and I would like somebody to say why.*”

“*Say the question you have actually got,*” said Ilyan.

“*My question is short and I will put it once.*”

Marrow took a folded paper out of his coat and did not look at it.

“*I wrote the third line of that writ. The roads, and the keeping of them, and the bar upon any of them, and the taking down of any bar. I wrote it because I have watched a road be held by whoever was standing nearest it at the time, twice, and both times people died on it.*”

“*Again, and all of it.*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The first time was in the winter of my first year in the chair, at the weir head, and there were four dead off one cart in a week. That is a fact and it is in a book at the seat and it is the reason the third line is in the writ.*”

He put the paper away.

“*And I have got a man in this city who keeps the Low Road for twenty-nine years, unpaid, at four in the morning. And he has said twice in this room that there is no form in this county that says a man may be sent for.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Skell.

“*And I have got a city of ninety thousand people, and nine hundred doors in a ward, and a fever at twenty-three weeks. And the whole of my difficulty is that I have exactly one instrument, and it is a piece of paper that names one person, and it named him at the fourth hour in the night in a town four hundred miles off, and he has never seen this city.*”

---

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*And the man it names is standing in this room, and he has been in my country for one hundred and ninety-six mornings, and he has spent six weeks saying out loud that a thing written down is a thing somebody can be asked about.*”

Marrow did not raise his voice. He had not raised it once in nine minutes.

“*And that has been true every time he has said it. And it is the truest thing I have heard in forty years of rooms. And I have come four hundred miles to tell him that it has killed the one instrument I have.*”

Nobody in that room had an answer to that, and about four people said so out loud, and one of them was a man with a book.

---

Ilyan stood up with a bad leg and had the offer on the long table in front of him, five lines, six days old. He went through it in the order it was printed, in the terms it used, and about thirty people heard every word.

“*The first line says a person not of the Shelf may be named in an instrument of succession, and that the office does not require a person able to hold that office to be of a city upon a road that runs in it.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*I accept that line. I have said out loud in four rooms that it is the truest sentence anybody in this country has put in writing to me in one hundred and ninety-six mornings, and I am going to say it again here, and I am not going to be thanked for it.*”

“*Say the second one,*” said Skell.

“*I decline the second. It is a seat at a council and four pounds a year payable quarterly. And there is a woman in this room who has kept a roll of doors for eleven years at four shillings a year, and came to two pounds and four shillings, and refused it in front of nine people.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it.*”

“*And I will not take four pounds a year out of an office while a woman in this room is owed two pounds and four shillings for eleven years of a roll and has refused it.*”

“*Say the third.*”

“*I decline the third, and I am going to say the whole of it in one sentence. The third line says the roads pass with the office. And there is a bar of four hurdles, nine links and a plate on the north side of a crossing two miles out, and a box with a shutter on it, and four yards of mud with nothing written on them anywhere.*”

He looked up.

“*And I have stood in the mud twice this week, and I am not going to hold a piece of paper that says all of that passes with a chair.*”

“*Say the fourth one,*” said Fenna Rusk.

He stopped in the middle of it, and about nine people noticed, and he went on.

“*The fourth line makes a crossing out of this country, and makes the keeper of the box its keeper. I decline it, and I decline it out loud and in public, and I am going to say why.*”

“*Say it,*” said Marrow.

“*Because I have said the price of a want out loud four times in six weeks and it has not stopped me once. This is the fifth time. And this time it is going to cost somebody, and I would like it entered that I said it first.*”

“*Then say the price.*”

“*The keeper of that box is a woman of twenty-eight who cannot say no. She has said so out loud in this room and in a room off the low ground, and it is in a book four hundred miles off.*”

He turned round and looked at the back of the room.

“*And she was conveyed through that crossing in the eighty-second week and came back at the last of the light. About nine people in this room have been at that crossing in the last two days. The number of them who have asked her one question about it is none.*”

“*Say the fifth line,*” said Skell.

“*I decline the fifth, and the fifth is the one that is about me, and I am going to say it slowly.*”

He put his hand flat on the long table.

“*Nothing in that sheet is a finding about any person in any city. And I have been a finding about a person in a book in four hundred miles since the second day of the eighty-third week, on a line with a woman who kept a roll, because I said the price out loud in a room before I put my name in it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it.*”

“*And a man in a book four hundred miles off wrote down that I had. And I cannot be a person who is not to be asked about this city afterwards, while I am the person the office of Regent of the Shelf is, in a writ that says the roads go with the office.*”

“*Then say it as a refusal,*” said Marrow.

“*I decline the exception. I do not take the second seat. I do not accept the instrument of succession lodged at the fourth hour of last night.*”

He looked round the room and let about thirty people get it.

“*And I am saying that in a room of about thirty persons, and there is a man of the seat with a book and a man with a pencil, and I would like all three of you to have it in the words of the offer and not in mine.*”

“*Say the whole of it and not the first half.*” said Skell, and his voice had gone somewhere it does not usually go.

“*I have been in this country one hundred and ninety-six mornings and I have wanted the fourth line every one of them, and I said so out loud in this city on Wednesday, in a room at the foot of this hill, in front of about thirty people, and about nine of them are in this room. And I would like it entered that I said it in public and refused it in public, and I am not going to be relieved about that, and neither is anybody else.*”

---

The room did what a room does. It took about four seconds, and then about nine people said, in four different voices, the same thing, and it was not the thing anybody had expected, and it was not cruel, and it was the most reasonable thing anybody had said in six weeks.

“*He has shut the crossing.*”

“*Out loud, and all of it.*” said Fenna Rusk, and nobody in the room could tell whether she was saying it to the nine people or to him.

“*He has shut the crossing. There is a fever at twenty-three weeks in this city, and about a hundred and forty people are dead of it, and the nearest doctor any of us can name is four hundred miles off.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not leave it in the middle,*” said Skell.

“*And on Wednesday there was a piece of paper that said that crossing was a way out of this country, and a man read it out to about thirty people in a room. And this morning that same man has said no to it in the same room. And if I am ill in the next four weeks, or my mother is, or my child is, that is what has happened.*”

“*Say the working on the bed,*” said Skell. “*I have said no to this in a market of two hundred people, and I would like somebody to do it now, and I would like it done properly.*”

“*The rest of that.*” said Ilyan.

“*I have no working,*” said the man from the door, who was about sixty and had not said anything until then. “*I have a mother in the third house of this row. She has had the fever since the eighty-third week and she is better and she is not well, and there is no form in this county that gets anybody four hundred miles, and there never has been.*”

He turned round and looked at the middle of the room.

“*And I do not blame the man, and I am not going to not blame him, and I would like him to stand there and be told it by somebody whose name is in a room.*”

Ilyan stood there and was told it, and about nine people watched him not answer, and he did not answer, and after about four seconds he said the only thing he had.

“*I am not going to say that the crossing was never going to be open,*” he said. “*It was open in writing on Wednesday, and I read it out in that room myself, and it is closed because I said so, and I am not going to be relieved about that in this room or out of it.*”

---

Marrow had not sat down once, and he said one more thing before he was asked anything else, and he said it to the room and not to the man beside him.

“*There is a word for the thing that happened in a room in the eighty-third week,*” he said. “*The right of refusal. It was taken out of the law of this country in an emergency about a hundred and forty years ago, by men who had a better reason than I have, and it has not been put back.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say the whole of it,*” said Skell.

“*There is no form anywhere in four hundred miles that gives one person in ninety thousand the right to stand in front of one decision and stop it. I have looked for one for forty years and there is not one. That is why I am standing in a wet room at the foot of a hill instead of being able to be told no.*”

“*Then say the last of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*And I will say the last of it and then I will stop. I am the instrument. Somebody has to hold the roads.*”

He put his hand out, flat, at nothing in particular.

“*A road is not held by a bar. It is not held by a paper. It is not held by a man who is good at a sum, or by a woman who cannot say no. Every one of those four things has been tried in the last two days in this city, and one of them is a man who has been a finding in a book since the eighty-third week.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Skell.

“*And I am not going to stand in this room and be the reason a road gets decided by whoever is standing nearest it. That is how it always happens, and the man who does it is never the man who meant to.*”

He looked at Ilyan, and he waited, and about thirty people waited with him.

“*Then somebody has to hold the roads,*” Marrow said. “*And I am asking you the sixth question, which is not in the sheet. I have asked it in about nine rooms in forty years and I have had an answer in two of them. I am not going to be a chair. Say the answer.*”

Ilyan had about four seconds, and about four hundred miles of road in his head, and a leg that had been on a table since the fourth hour of the night. He said the true thing, out loud, in a room, to about thirty people. It was the thing he had been saying out loud in rooms for six weeks, and it had never once stopped him.

“*I do not know,*” he said. “*And somebody has to hold the roads, and I cannot answer that, and I have been asked for a second half in five rooms in six weeks and I have given one five times, and this is the sixth and I have got nothing.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Skell.

“*I do not know who holds the roads, and I have not got a remedy for that. And the price of the price is that a man of sixty-one has walked four hundred miles and gone through four yards of mud at the fourth hour of the morning to stand in this room, and I have made him wait a day for it, and a day is about a day.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*And I would like the clerk to enter that I could not do it in two days and not in two years.*”

Marrow nodded at him once and sat down for the first time. The room did not thank him, and nobody was relieved.

At about the fifth hour Ferrow read out what he had written, and it was six sentences, and one of them had a name in it, and one of them had the words *and I do not know* in it, and he said out loud that he had not been asked to leave either of them out and would not have left either of them.

And Cope said the half of the law of it, quietly, to about nine people who could hear him. The writ of succession was silent. It had no line at all for a person who is named in an instrument and declines the office, and it had been silent on that for as long as it had been in a tray, and he had entered the declining and the silence together, and nobody in this city could fill in the gap, and neither could he.

The fever was twenty-three weeks and two days old, and on the long table at the foot of a hill there was a form with six pages and two lines in it, and thirty-one wards had until the first day of the eighty-sixth week.
