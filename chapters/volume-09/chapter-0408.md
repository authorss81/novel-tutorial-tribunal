# Chapter 408: A Visit That Is Not A Shilling, And The Difference Between A Door With A Man At It And No Door At All

The fourth day of the eightieth week was his hundred and fifty-eighth morning and the eighth day after the settlement, and the fever was seventeen weeks and six days old, and the rail had been up eleven days, and a sheet with a seal on it was nailed to the post at the end of the row below the bill, and there was a line on the sheet about going to doors on the account of the office, and the rate on it was at the figure paid to a keeper of a roll, and the man at the wheel had said the working out loud in a room at the foot of a hill the day before and the working was four shillings a year over three hundred and sixty-four days, which is about a farthing.

It is forty-seven days since she began.

A woman of sixty with a shawl asked her at a door at the first hour, and asked her properly, which is to say she stood on the step and said what the sheet said and then said what the rate was and then stood there and waited, and she had been in three rooms in this city in ten days and had not been in this one and had come on her own account.

“*Will you go on the book.*”

“*What is the book.*”

“*It is a book of the visits. Somebody is to go from house to house on this row and the four rows off it and say to anybody who comes to the door that the office will hear a dispute about a road, and say a name out loud at a door, and that is the whole of the employment. And it is paid at the figure the sheet gives, and the figure is the figure a keeper of a roll is paid, and the man at the wheel worked it out loud in a room and said it is about a farthing a day.*”

“*Say the rest of the sheet.*”

“*It says the names are not entered in the office's book. It says the office keeps a book of the visits and the names go in the ward's own book and not in the office's. I had the man with the tray read it out to me twice at the end of the row last night because I have been in this city nine years and I do not take a thing on a post on somebody's word.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Then there is nothing in it for me,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and I want to say why in a room and not at a door, and I am going to go and get changed and come down, and you are welcome to come with me and you are welcome to tell anybody in this ward that I asked you to come.*”

---

There were fourteen people in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour and the sheet with the seal on it was on the long table, and the woman of twenty-eight put the slate down and did not turn it round.

“*I have been offered a farthing a day to go to doors,*” she said, “*and I am going to say no in a room in front of people and I am going to say it as a person of this ward and not as a person who has been asked a question about her own work, and I want it entered in nobody's book, and I know that I am the only person in this ward anybody can be asked about the doors and that a thing I say in this room is a thing that gets kept, and I am doing it anyway, and I would like it said that I knew that when I stood on the step and I came down here rather than say it at a door.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*First, and I am saying it fast because I have been up since the first hour: a visit that is paid for is a visit that can be sent from somewhere else.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*That is the whole of my reason and it has been my reason since before the seal came and it is not new. If a person goes to a door on their own account, then the person who is at the door is the person who chose to be, and I can be asked about it, and so can they, and so can their house. If a person goes to a door because a farthing a day is on a sheet, then anybody can be sent to my door instead of me, and come back and put a mark against it, and the mark will be a mark. And the woman of twenty-eight is not a name, she is a person of this ward, and a person of this ward cannot be sent to a door by an office four hundred miles off. That is not in the sheet. That is the thing the sheet has not got, and it is the only thing on the post that is worth saying out loud.*”

“*Say the second half of it.*”

“*The second half is worse and I have thought about it for two days and I am going to say it anyway. The sheet says the office keeps a book of the visits and the names are not entered in it. So the office is going to pay a farthing a day for somebody to go to nine hundred doors and is not going to write down whose name was said at any of them.*”

She stopped and the room waited.

“*So it does not buy a roll. It buys a person standing in a woman's shoes for a day. The roll is still the roll and it is still in a room a mile and a half down a row with a lamp on, and there is still one person in this city who can be asked whether it is right, and she is on the other side of that rail, and if a farthing a day walks my doors for a month then at the end of it there will be a second person in this ward who can be asked about a month of walking, and she will be a person in a record about a month of walking, and the woman of forty-four will still be a person in a record about a roll, and neither of those two things makes the roll any truer, and the rail will still be up.*”

Nobody said anything, and Bevin Tarr said *huh* out loud at the end of the long table, which is not a word he has said in a room in four years, and he did not apologise for it, and nobody stopped him.

“*And there is a third thing and it is the price and I am saying it because I have watched it done twice in this city and both times it was said out loud before the doing and neither time it stopped anybody.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I am going to say no in a room, and from the second hour this afternoon there will not be one person in this ward who can say that nobody offered me anything. For ten days the row has said that there is no money in this ward for anything and that is true and that is on a bill on a wall, and after this afternoon that is not true, and it will not be true again. An offer I refused is still an offer that was made. And the man of sixty has said four fevers in forty years and that is the only thing in this ward anybody has ever been able to lean on, and in an hour it will be a thing that a woman said no to, and he did not get asked and he will be leaned on less and not more, and he will say so, and he will be right.*”

---

And the boy of sixteen asked the question, and he asked it in the doorway, and he is sixteen and has been in this city his whole life and nobody stops him.

“*Then what is a person supposed to do,*” he said, “*if somebody offers them a way out of a thing that is being done to them.*”

And the woman of twenty-eight turned round, and she looked at him for a while, and she answered him in this room and she answered him once.

“*There is no word for it in this city and I am not going to invent one for a boy of sixteen at ten in the morning.*”

“*Say it without a word.*”

“*Then here it is without a word, and I am going to say it once and you may carry it out of this room in your mouth and I will not stop you, because that is what happens to everything said in here.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*If somebody gives you a way out, and the giving is in their hand, then it is not a way out. It is a door with a man standing at it, and he can take it back on any wet morning like this one, and while he is deciding whether to take it back you are still in the room. And if nobody gives you a way out at all, then you are not free either. You are just further off. There is no door and there is no man and there is nobody to be angry at and nobody who has to be answerable, and that is the other half and nobody ever tells you the other half.*”

“*So which one is it.*”

“*It is not a right and it is not a refusal and it is not one of the two bad things, and I am not going to give it a name in a room because I have a row to go down and four doors I have not been to and nobody has asked me to make a speech.*”

The boy of sixteen said it four times in four doorways before the change and nobody stopped him and by the last of the light the whole of the low row had it and it was in a dozen mouths and no two of them were saying it the same way, and the woman of twenty-eight went down her row and did her work and did not say it to anybody herself.

The man of sixty was on his bench at the end of the ninth row at the change, and a boy of sixteen said it to him in the doorway, and he listened to the whole of it and did not say it back and was not asked to and has not been asked for a fourth time.

“*It is not a word I have,*” he said, to the boy, and to nobody. “*I have got nine and I have said them once and I am not going to add to them in a wet week because a boy of sixteen has been in my doorway four times. A fever goes down. That is the one I have. You go and tell whoever it was that said it to you that a man of sixty on a bench at the end of this row heard all of it and has nothing to add to it and is not going to be asked again.*”

The boy of sixteen said that in four doorways, and the man of sixty was not thanked and did not expect to be.

And the four bells went over Tallowgate and the rail was up on the eleventh day and a farthing a day was on a post at the end of the row, and the fever was seventeen weeks and six days old, and it did not stop for a visit, and nobody in that room was thanked.
