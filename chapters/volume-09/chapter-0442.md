# Chapter 442: The Office Asks Thirty-One Wards For A Person, And The First Answer Is A Man Who Counts Doors, And Who Has A Figure About Four Halls That He Does Not Use

Third day of the eighty-fifth week. Shelf day three hundred and seventeen, his hundred and ninety-second morning, forty-two days after the settlement, the fever twenty-two weeks and five days, the rail down thirty-two days.

The sheet that came up the hill that morning had a head on it and four lines under the head, and the head said it was addressed to every ward upon a road of the Shelf, and Nell Fisk had it in her hands before Ferrow got to the table.

She read the head out twice, in the open, in the doorway, the way she reads everything, and about nine people on the stair behind her heard it twice.

“*Say the first line and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Skell.

“*One. The person so named may be asked concerning the roads, the keeping of them, and any bar upon them.*”

“*Say the rest of that line.*”

“*And the office asks for the name of a person, and does not ask for the name of a function, and does not require a person to be of any calling, and does not check what the person named does.*”

“*Say the second one.*”

“*Two. The person named may decline to answer any question put to them.*”

Somebody near the door made a sound. About nine people said the next part out loud together, before he had read it.

“*The office notes a declining, and does not press for an answer, and does not enter the declining in any register of this city. And a person named who declines to answer is not to be asked again about the same matter by the office.*”

“*Say the last two and do not make them small,*” said Ilyan.

“*Three. The name is to be sent within six days of the receipt of this sheet. And the office does not provide a name, and does not require a ward to have one, and a ward that sends none has not declined and is not entered as having declined.*”

“*And the last line.*”

“*Four. Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road, and nothing in it is a finding about any person in any city, and it takes effect according to its terms and not otherwise.*”

---

About nine people in that room said, within about a minute of each other, that line two was the right of refusal. Four of them said it as a question.

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*It is a permission and not a right,*” said Skell. “*A person may say no to a question. There is no line anywhere in this county in which a person may say no to a decision, and there has not been one in this country for about a hundred and forty years, and I have looked for one in nine days and there is not one in the press or in the tray.*”

“*Then say the other half of it, and say it in your own mouth and not in mine,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The other half is that a person who may decline to answer is a person the office has already decided does not have to be told anything,*” she said. “*And a right to hear nothing is not a way of stopping a thing. It is a way of being governed without ever being spoken to, and I have been on the other end of that for eleven years, and it is called a roll of doors.*”

“*Again, and all of it.*” said Cope, from the end of the table, without opening his book.

“*And I am the person appointed in the register of disputes, and a register is not a remedy, and I have said in this room twice that I am not going to say what my office is for. I am saying it now because a man has asked me to and because the second line is correct and it is twelve words long.*”

---

“*Say how many wards,*” said Fenna Rusk.

Ferrow turned the sheet round and looked at the foot of it, which was a column of numbers and not names, because thirty-one wards do not fit in a column of names without a fold.

“*Thirty-one,*” he said, “*set out by number and not by name, running one to thirty-one, and there is no column for the name of a ward against the number, and I have looked for one and there is not one.*”

“*Say what that is,*” said Skell.

“*That is the correct figure of the number of wards of this city, in the office's own hand, and I do not know how they got it. And I have said nine times that I write down what is in front of me and do not tell anybody how it got there.*”

“*Thirty-one is right,*” said Skell. “*I have counted halls and doors for twenty years and there are thirty-one halls in this city, and I have a figure about four of them that I do not use, and I am not going to say the figure in a room.*”

“*Why not,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because I did the division out loud in the eighty-second week and I did not enjoy it, and it is in a book four hundred miles off, and I have not got another one in me.*”

---

The first answer came up the hill on the evening of the third day of the eighty-fifth week. It was one line on one side of a sheet, and it had a name in it, and the whole of this city heard about it within a day because thirty-one halls are not far apart and about nine of them share a corridor.

“*Say the name,*” said Skell.

“*Darrin Skell,*” said Ferrow. “*Of the ward at the end of the plate yard, counting hall and doors. Sent by the ward at the end of the plate yard, which is a ward and not a man, and the sheet is signed for the ward.*”

“*Say the rest of the foot of it.*”

“*Nothing else. One name, the name of the ward, and the day, which is the third day of this week.*”

Nobody said anything for a moment, and then Ilyan said the thing that was in his mouth, and it was the wrong thing, and he knew it about a second and a half after the end of it.

“*Then the first ward in this city to answer the office has sent it a man who counts halls,*” he said. “*And it has sent him because he counts.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it a speech,*” said Skell.

“*A representative chosen because he can count is a representative chosen for his use. The office asked for a person and a ward has sent it a function. That is the shape of what is being built in thirty-one wards this week, and I am saying it in a room before I have said it to myself, which is what I do.*”

---

Skell had a slate of his own under his arm and he put it down on the wood before he answered.

“*Say the half of that which is mine,*” he said, “*and then say the other half, and I have not got a speech and I have had six weeks to make one and I have not.*”

“*Say yours first.*”

“*I am not a counter. I am a man who is good at a sum. I have counted doors in this city for twenty years because there was nothing else to do with a Tuesday.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*And because a man who is good at a sum in this city is a man who is asked about sums. That is the whole of what I am. It is not nothing and it is not a function.*”

“*Say the other half,*” said Ilyan.

“*And a function is a thing a person is useful for. I heard you say that, and you said it in a room with about thirty people in it.*”

He did not raise his voice. He did not have to.

“*I have said no twice in six weeks, and one of the two times I said it in a market of two hundred people. I would like you to say the word again, and I would like to stand here while you say it.*”

“*I said a function,*” Ilyan said. “*And I say it again. I said a function, and I am sorry for it, and I am not going to take it back.*”

“*Then say the rest of it.*”

“*The rest of it is that I am not sorry for the other half. Thirty-one wards have been asked for a person in six days, and thirty of them have not answered, and the one that has answered has answered with you.*”

He waited, and Skell waited, and about nine people waited.

“*And the reason it answered with you is that you are the man the ward knows. That is not a fault of yours. It is a fault of a form with one line in it.*”

---

Skell looked at the slate he had put down.

“*Then the form has one line in it, and the office has thirty-one of them going out today, and one has come back already,*” he said. “*And about twenty-nine of the other thirty are going to a hall with a counter in it, and a hall with a counter in it is a hall with a man in it who does sums.*”

“*Say the rest of that arithmetic and do not do a division,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am not doing a division. I am saying that the office is going to end up with thirty-one men who do sums, and it is going to be correct, and it is going to be the most useful body of men in four hundred miles of country. And nobody in this city is going to be able to ask it anything at all.*”

They got as far as the second hour and the room had not moved, and then a woman near the door asked the question the room had been turning over for an hour without getting out of.

“*Say what I am going to ask,*” she said. “*I am going to ask how a form asks for a person. I have asked it twice this week in a different room and got nothing, and I would like somebody here to try.*”

“*Say the question out loud,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and say it to the room and not to him.*”

The woman said it out loud.

“*How does a form ask for a person?*”

About thirty people waited, and Ilyan had an answer in about four seconds, and it was wrong, and then he had a better one in about another four seconds and it was also wrong, and he said both of them out loud, because a room had taught him in six weeks that a man who stops in the middle of a sentence is a man managing somebody.

“*A form asks for a name,*” he said. “*And a name is a line of ink, and any form in this county can ask for a name in a line. The office has a form for everything it does and every one of them I have seen asks for a name. Not one of them asks for a person, and that is not a fault of the forms. That is what a form is.*”

“*Say the second one,*” said Skell.

“*And the second one is that a name in a line is a thing anybody can enter. A person can be entered and a function can be entered and a hall with a counter in it can be entered. The office does not check, and does not require, and does not press.*”

He put his hands in his coat.

“*So a form with one line in it cannot tell a person from a use, and a ward cannot be blamed for putting a use in it, because a ward does not know the difference, and neither does the form.*”

“*Say the half of that which is not the answer,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The half of it is that a form cannot ask whether somebody is a person at all. Only a person can, and the only person in this city who can be asked about a door is a woman of twenty-eight who cannot say no, and I am not going to put her in a form about this.*”

He stopped there. About nine people in the room agreed with him out loud, and one of them was not a person he had expected to hear it from.

“*Then I am going to say the part I have,*” said Fenna Rusk. “*And I have had it since the third hour, and I have not said it because there is a man in this room who answers faster than I do and I have been letting him.*”

“*Say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*A form asks for a person by asking for two. Not the person and the office. The person, and a second person who will stand beside that person and be asked about them.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go slowly,*” said Skell.

“*Because a person who is a person has somebody who will answer for them, and a use has nobody. A man who does sums in a hall has nobody in this city who would stand up in a room and say that man is a person and not a thing he is useful for.*”

“*And the second person,*” said Ilyan.

“*The second person is a person of this place, and is not the person named. The office can ask the second person about the first one. The first one does not get to choose the second one.*”

“*Say the last of it and then say what you want out of the room,*” said Skell.

“*And I do not know a single ward in this city that has ever chosen anything, and I have looked for one. I would like somebody to say whether a ward can.*”

Skell put his hands flat on the table.

“*That is not how a form asks for a person,*” he said. “*That is how a form asks for two people. And nobody in this room has said how a form asks for a person, and you have been in this room since the second hour, and you have not said it either.*”

“*Then say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I do not know,*” he said. “*I have a way of asking a room what a thing is four times a day and I have never once been able to ask one what a thing is for, and I have been in this country one hundred and ninety-two mornings and I have not got it.*”

About thirty people were in a room at the foot of a hill, and eleven of them, roughly, knew what a ward was, and the answer to her question was that a ward was nine hundred doors and a hall and a counter, and a ward had never chosen anything in this city, and there was no form anywhere in the county that said a ward could. And the way of asking a ward for a person was on the table in nine mouths and in nobody's hand.

And a man not of this place sat on the window seat with a bad leg, and he had wanted something out of that room since the second hour and had not said the price of it. There was an exception on the table at the foot of the room with a way out of this country in the fourth of its five lines, and he had still not answered it, and the office would not check a number and would not require a person to be asked.

The fever was twenty-two weeks and five days old, and a sheet had gone out to thirty-one halls with one line in it, and a man who counts doors was in the first answer, and a person was on it before anybody had asked for one. The first of thirty-one answers to a question about the roads was a man of fifty-two who had been called a function in a room, twice, and had not been struck out of anything.
