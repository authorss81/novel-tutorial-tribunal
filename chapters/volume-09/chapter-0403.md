# Chapter 403: The Woman Who Goes To The Ends Of Roads, And The Arithmetic Of A Shut Door

The sixth day of the seventy-ninth week was his hundred and fifty-third morning and the third day after the settlement, and the fever was seventeen weeks and one day old, and the rail had been up six days, and the woman of twenty-eight went out to the end of it at the first hour and came back at the change and went out again in the afternoon, which is not what she had been doing since the eighth morning of the fever, when she had been going to doors.

She had been going to the ends of roads. There is not a person of this ward over the age of nine who has not noticed that in the last five days the woman of twenty-eight has been walking to the far end of the row and standing at the rail for a while and coming back, and that she has been doing it at the first hour and at the change and at the last of the light, and that she has never once gone past the rail, and that when she comes back she goes straight to the end of the row and starts on a door, as if the walk had been a thing she had to get out of the way of before she could do her work.

She is not the only person in this ward who can be asked about the doors. She is the only person anybody can be asked about the doors, and she cannot say no, and she has said so in three rooms and she is not going to say it again in this one.

It is forty-two days since she began.

---

There were eleven people in the public room at the foot of the hill at the second hour and the order was on the wall behind them with the bill under it, and the woman of twenty-eight put her slate on the long table and turned it round, which takes a while, because the slate is small and the table is long and there are eleven people and not all of them want to see it.

Nobody asked her to. That is the thing about it. She is the one who put it down.

“*I am not going to give you a number of my own doors,*” she said, “*and I have been asked for one twice and I have said no twice and I am going to say the reason again in a room because there are going to be a great many more days of this. A number of mine is a number about how many doors I have been to, and a door I have been to is a visit, and a visit is the only thing in this city that is not a shilling, and a visit is not a thing a person can be held to. Ask me how many doors are on the far side of that rail and I will tell you I do not know and I will tell you the reason in a moment and the reason is not a number.*”

“*Say the arithmetic you can do.*”

“*The arithmetic I can do is not mine. It is the keeper's and she gave it out loud in her own room six days ago in front of me and a magistrate, and I am giving it back to her in a room, and she is at the end of this table and she has not asked me to.*”

The woman with the book was at the end of the long table and had come a mile and a half and had not opened the door for it, and the room let her, and nobody thanked her.

“*Say it,*” said the woman with the book.

“*Nine hundred doors,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*Thirty of them a fortnight. Thirty lots of fourteen days is four hundred and twenty days, and a year in this city is three hundred and sixty-four days, so one round is a year and fifty-six days, and that is the keeper's figure and it is hers and she has said it eleven times in thirty-one years.*”

“*And the other half.*”

“*The other half is the correction, and the correction is the round done again over the doors that were shut the first time, because a shut door can be counted and not opened, and there is no mark in this city for a house that has emptied and no form that says a door was shut. Four hundred and twenty twice is eight hundred and forty. Eight hundred and forty less two years of three hundred and sixty-four, which is seven hundred and twenty-eight, is a hundred and twelve. That is two years and a hundred and twelve days, and that is the keeper's figure too and she said it with the working in it so that a person could check it and not believe her.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And how much of it has been done.*”

“*Eleven years,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and a year in this city is three hundred and sixty-four days, and eleven of them is four thousand and four days. Four thousand and four over four hundred and twenty is nine, and nine times four hundred and twenty is three thousand seven hundred and eighty, and four thousand and four less three thousand seven hundred and eighty is two hundred and twenty-four. So there have been nine rounds and two hundred and twenty-four days of a tenth in that book, and that is what has been done, and I have it in my head because she said it in front of me and I have been carrying it since.*”

“*And the rest.*”

“*And the rest is two years and a hundred and twelve days from wherever she has got to, and it was not going to be done in anybody's lifetime in this ward and there is nobody going to be standing in that room at the end of it to be asked whether it was done properly, and she said that in her own room six days ago and she said it before anybody asked her, and I am not going to say it again for her.*”

---

“*Now say the part about the rail,*” said the man with the bad ear, from the back, because he asks for the second half and he is not proud of asking for it and he does it anyway.

“*I have got it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and it is not a number and I have been saying it in doorways all week and I am going to say it in a room because a doorway does not keep anything and I want this one kept and I have not asked anybody to keep it.*”

She put two fingers on the rail's chalk line, which she had not drawn, and which was on the bench in the hut, and she was talking about a rail four hundred yards off, and nobody in the room corrected her.

“*A door behind that rail is a door I cannot go to. And a door I cannot go to is a door that is shut. And a shut door is not on a list and not off a list, it is a door I went to. That is the keeper's argument, given to me in her own room six days ago, and I am handing it back to her in a room because it is hers and because it is the only arithmetic anybody has got on five days of that rail.*”

The woman with the book did not say the words and did not look up.

“*So the correction round is not two years and a hundred and twelve days any more,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*It is one round, and then the doors behind the rail, and then the round again over those, and there is no figure for it and I am not going to make one up at this table. I have been out to that rail three times today and I have not been past it and I am not going to be past it, because I have not been asked to and because nobody in this room would be standing here if I had been.*”

---

And then the woman with the book said the thing, and she said it standing up, and it was the first time in four days she had stood up in a room with other people in it.

“*I am going out to the far side of it in the morning.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*I am not going to be asked to and there is no form that says a person may be asked, and I am going to say the price of it out loud in a room because that is how it is done and I have watched it be done and I am not going to be the one person in this city who does a thing and does not say what it costs.*”

“*Say the price.*”

“*The price is that after tomorrow there is no person in this ward anybody can be asked about the roll of this ward,*” said the woman with the book. “*Not because I shall be ill and not because I shall be dead, and not because that is going to happen, and not because I have done anything to cause it. Because I shall be on the other side of a rail, and a person on the other side of a rail is a person about whom there is no form, and a person about whom there is no form is a person who does not exist as far as this ward is concerned. In two years and a hundred and twelve days, if that rail is ever down, there will be a person in this city who was not born who can be asked whether the roll is right, and she will ask me, and I will be dead or I will be in a row above the plate yard's with somebody's bread and I will say I did it, and she will have to take it.*”

“*You could count it from here.*”

“*I could count the rail and I could count nothing else,*” said the woman with the book, “*and there are four miles of road on the other side of that rail with houses on it and I have been going to those doors for eleven years and I do not carry a list in my head, because the book is the list, and the book is in this room, and if I put four miles in my head then the book and my head are two records and the four records of this ward have to be kept in agreement and I am not adding a fifth one for a rail.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*I am going because a round that has not counted the doors behind the rail is not a round, and I have got nine rounds and two hundred and twenty-four days of a tenth in that book and I am not going to add a lie to it.*”

---

Nobody said the price out loud, and he noticed that, and it is worth saying what he noticed and what he did about it, because it is the whole of the difference between this morning and a yard at the low end of Tallowgate four months ago.

He had a thing he says before he does things, and it has a name at the top of a form on the other side of it, and it has cost a woman of thirty-four at a bar eleven miles off a lane four hundred miles from here and a woman of forty-four in a book four floors up and a man of thirty-one with a bad ear, and it has never once stopped him, and it is the only method he has ever had.

He did not say it. There was no instrument in the room and there was nothing to price. A woman of forty-four was going to walk past a rail in the morning because a round was not a round, and if he had stood up and named the price of it out loud he would have been putting a person of a place into a record about a road, in a room, in front of eleven people, for the ninth time, and there was nobody in that room who could act on it except the clerk of thirty, and the clerk of thirty fills lines off a paper in front of him and there was no paper.

So he said a true thing instead, which is what he has instead now.

“*You have been in that room since before I came into this ward,*” he said, “*and you have been keeping that roll for eleven years and you are going over a rail in the morning on your own account, and you are the only person in this city anybody can be asked about that book, and I am a man with a leg who came on foot and I cannot go where you are going and I am not going to pretend that saying a thing in this room is a way of being on the other side of that rail with you.*”

“*No,*” said the woman with the book. “*And you have not said that before, and I have been in this room nine weeks and I have watched you say a great many things, and the man of fifty-two has a number about halls and the man of sixty has nine words he will not say again and the clerk of thirty has a form, and every one of you can do one thing and not the other, and it is the first time I have heard a person of that leg admit to the second half.*”

“*Say the second half.*”

“*The second half is that the second half is no good to me either,*” said the woman with the book, “*and I am not being kind. A man who admits he cannot help is worth more to a ward than a man who says he can, and that is all, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be relieved and I have told a room and a magistrate and a bench of four that I will not be thanked and I am not going to stop saying it.*”

Nobody thanked her.

She went out into the row at the change and the road was four hundred yards off and she walked it, and the boy of sixteen was at the rail in the afternoon and said in a doorway at the end of the row that the woman with the book was going over in the morning, and nobody stopped him and nobody asked her whether she had said so, and the fever was seventeen weeks and one day old and it did not stop for a round, and nobody in that room was thanked.
