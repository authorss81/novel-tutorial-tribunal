# Chapter 414: The Same Door And The Same Question, And The Only Difference Between The Two Times Is A Book

The fourth day of the eighty-first week was his hundred and sixty-fourth morning and the fourteenth day after the settlement, and the fever was eighteen weeks and five days old, and the rail had been down four days, and the sheet with the rate on it was still nailed to the post at the end of the low row below the bill, and the bill was crooked, and there was a man of thirty-eight standing under the bill with a case at the second hour when the woman of twenty-eight came down her row with her slate, and there were about thirty people at that end of the row by the time he had finished asking.

The boy of sixteen had said it in four doorways at the change the night before and what he had said was that the man with the case was going to ask her, and he had said it four times without being asked to, because that is what he does.

Nobody had sent for anybody.

“*Will you go on the book.*”

He did not read the sheet off the post. He said it in his own words, which is not what a man with a case does, and the woman of twenty-eight stood on the step and looked at him and did not answer, and the woman of sixty with a shawl was four doors along and had come out of her own door to stand at the end of the row and did not come closer.

“*Say it as it is on the sheet,*” said the man with the bad ear, from the back, “*because you are a person of the seat and it is not your sheet and you have read five rows of it in a room already.*”

Kerwin Dace opened the case and put his finger on the row.

“*One. A person of the place who goes to a door on the account of the office is paid at the figure paid to a keeper of a roll, and the payment is for the visit and not for the name, and the office keeps its own book of the visits and the names are not entered in it.*”

“*The farthing,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, to nobody.

“*The farthing,*” said Kerwin Dace, “*and the man at the wheel worked it out loud in this room nine days ago and said it twice, and I have not got a different figure and I am not going to give you one.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*So it is the same question,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and the same rate, and the same door, and you are the third person to ask me it.*”

“*I am the first person to ask you it who can be sent for,*” said Kerwin Dace, “*and I am not going to pretend that is the same thing and I am not going to pretend it is a different thing either, and I would rather you had both of those from me now than one of them in a fortnight.*”

---

“*No,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

He wrote it in the book with the pencil and he did not look down at it, and there were thirty people at that end of the row and every one of them saw him do it, and that was the whole of the difference and everybody there knew what it was except the person who was being asked.

Nobody said anything.

“*Say the second half,*” said the man with the bad ear.

“*There is no second half,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*I said it on a step six days ago and I said it again in a room the same day and both of those were the same words, and the same words are still the same words, and I am not going to find a new way of saying a thing because a man has a pencil.*”

“*There is a second half and I have it in front of me,*” said Kerwin Dace, “*and I am going to read it out, because you are entitled to know what you have just signed and you have not signed anything, which is a thing I have learnt about this form since the day I came down the hill.*”

He read from the row and not from the book.

“*And a person who declines is noted as declining, and a person who has declined is required to give a reason, and the reason is entered with the declination, and a person who has given a reason may be asked again in the same terms and the answer and the reason are entered again.*”

Nobody said anything at all.

“*Say that again,*” said the man with the bad leg.

Kerwin Dace read it again, and he read it in the same voice, and about thirty people at the end of a low row heard the second time and the man with the bad ear did not remark on the asking and nobody in that row did.

“*That is on the sheet you printed,*” said the man of fifty-two, who had come up the row from the end of the plate yard's and had not been sent for. “*A reason is required and the reason is entered and then you may ask again in the same terms.*”

“*That is on the sheet I printed,*” said Kerwin Dace. “*I did not write it. I have been a person of the seat for eleven years and I have printed what I am given and I do not know who gave that to us and I have not asked, because that is not the work, and I am telling this row that I have not asked and that I am telling them because I read it out and I would like somebody from this city to be standing here when I say it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Then it is not a book of the visits,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*It is a book of reasons, and there is no line in it for the words I use.*”

“*There is a line for yes and there is a line for no,*” said Kerwin Dace, “*and there is no line for anything else, and I have been in this city three days and I have had four answers and two of them had a reason attached and both of the reasons went in, and I have not been able to find a person who thinks that is strange, and I have stopped saying so this morning because I think it may only be strange to me.*”

---

“*Then here is my reason,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and I am giving it to this row and not to you, and you can write it down or you can put your pencil away, and I have had a fortnight of people putting things I say into books and I am not going to pretend to a man with a case that there is a different way to do this.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*A visit that is paid for is a visit that can be sent from somewhere else,*” she said. “*That is the first of it and I said it six days ago and it is in four doorways and it is in a dozen mouths and it was there before your schedule and it will be there after it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And the second of it is worse and it is the one I gave in a room and you have not got it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and the second of it is that the office keeps a book of the visits and does not enter the names, so a farthing a day is not a roll. It is a person standing in my shoes for a day, and the roll is still in a room a mile and a half down this row with a lamp on it, and there is still one woman in this city who can be asked whether it is right.*”

She stopped and the row waited.

“*And that is the whole of the reason and I have given it four times in ten days and the fourth time is now, and it is in four doorways and a room and a book four floors up and it is now in a book in a city four hundred miles off with a date on it, and I did not choose that and I could not have stopped it, and I have said the price of it out loud every single time and the price has not stopped anything one single time.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*So write it down,*” she said. “*Write all of it. And when it is in there, go and ask the woman of sixty with a shawl what she thinks of it, because she read your first row out in a room on the first day of this week and said a true thing about a settlement and a woman in a bed at the top of this city, and you wrote that down in a book with a pencil, and she is standing at the end of this row and she has not come over here and she is not going to.*”

The woman with the shawl did not move and did not say anything and was not thanked and did not expect to be, and about thirty people at the end of a low row were looking at her and she was the one person in that row who could not leave, because leaving is a thing a person of a place can do in a room and cannot do in a doorway.

Kerwin Dace wrote for a while, and it took longer than the first two lines had taken.

“*That is all of it,*” he said. “*I have it. And I am going to tell this row what the office is going to do with it, because you have all been told what things are for by people who could not act, and I can act, and I have got four days and a case and a pencil, and the honest answer is that I do not know.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*I do not know what it is for,*” said Kerwin Dace. “*I have not asked. I have been asked nothing about it and I have not asked, and I have been in this city three days and I have had no word at all for what any of it is, and I have not found one and I am not going to look for one, and I have watched a woman say a true thing into a pencil and I have watched a boy of sixteen say no on a step and I have watched a clerk carry three sheets out of a press on his own two legs, and I could not tell you the shape of any of it and I have not been told and I have not asked, and that is the whole of what I have got.*”

Nobody said anything, and the man with the bad ear said, “*Then you are the first honest man who has come down that hill and you are the first one who has said he does not know, and I am not going to say that I believe you, because you print the sheets.*”

“*I print the sheets,*” said Kerwin Dace, “*and I have never been thanked for one and I am not going to be thanked for this one, and I am going down this row to the next four doors and I am going to ask the same question, and the next person is going to say no, and I would like this row to understand that it is not going to get better by the end of the row.*”

The four bells went over Tallowgate, and a woman of twenty-eight went down her row and did nine doors and did not say a word at any of them, and a man of thirty-eight went down a row with a book and a case and four days, and a boy of sixteen was at a door and had been told the night before and had not been asked to be anywhere, and the fever was eighteen weeks and five days old, and it did not stop for a second asking, and nobody at the end of that row was thanked.
