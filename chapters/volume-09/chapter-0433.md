# Chapter 433: A Sheet Comes Back Down The Hill With A Stamp On It From A Town Nobody Here Has Been To, And There Is A Name In The Fifth Row

The eighty-fourth week began that morning. Shelf day three hundred and eight; his hundred and eighty-third morning; thirty-three days after the settlement; the fever twenty-one weeks and three days; the rail down twenty-three days.

The sheet came down in the tray at the second hour and it had wax on it, and there was a second stamp on the face of it that was not the office's seal.

“*Say the stamp first,*” said Skell.

Ferrow had the sheet up at the long table in the public room at the foot of the hill, standing, because he reads things out standing up, and about thirty people were in the room because it is the sixth day of a market week and the rain has kept them in.

“*The stamp is not a seal,*” he said. “*It is ink on the face of the sheet, pressed with a die, and it says the name of a town. Cray Crossing. And under the town there is a day of the month on it, and the day of the month is one day after the day this city sent the thing up the hill.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*There is nothing else on the stamp. It does not say what happened at Cray Crossing. It does not say who pressed it. It is a town with a name and a day on it, and Cray Crossing is four days up the road and there is nobody in this city who has ever been to it.*”

---

“*Say what the sheet is,*” Ilyan said.

“*It is a schedule of payments in five rows, and it went out of this city on the fourth day of the eighty-second week inside a case, in the hand of a man of the seat of thirty-eight, together with a return of persons and things in one column with no name in it.*”

Nobody in the room said the man's name, and about nine of them had said it out loud in that room three weeks before.

“*Say the half of that which is not on the sheet,*” said Skell.

“*The man of thirty-eight is not on the sheet, and he is not in this city, and he is not in the room, and I have looked at the top of it four times in the last minute to be certain there is no name on it anywhere.*”

“*Say where he is.*”

“*I do not know. He is four hundred miles up a road on his own two feet or he is not, and I am not going to guess at it in a room, and the office has not said, and if the office had said I would read it out and you would all hear it.*”

Skell put his hands flat on the table.

“*Then read the fifth row,*” he said, “*because that is where the office put something, and I can see where the ink is, and there is more ink on that row than there is on any of the others.*”

---

Ferrow read the fifth row out twice.

“*One. A place is kept, and the keeping of it is at the rate of the first row, which is a farthing a visit. Two. A person of the place entered under this row is conveyed at the charge of the office beyond the Shelf. Three. The office will pay that person one year's wage at the rate of the second row, in one sum, at the seat, on the giving of a name.*”

He stopped, and read the fourth line on its own.

“*Four. A person so conveyed is not to be asked about the city afterwards.*”

Then he read the fifth.

“*Five. A person of the place who is upon a road the office keeps, at their own charge, and not conveyed at the charge of the office, has made use of the road, and is not to be asked about that road afterwards.*”

He stopped, and then went on, because he had decided to.

“*And there is a name in the fifth line. It is written in a fair hand and it is not the office's hand. It is the name of a man of forty-six who walked out of this city on his own two legs on the second day of this month, having refused the charge of the office, and it is the first name that this office has entered against a road in this city without a paper about the person having been carried into a building by one of us in the ordinary way.*”

“*Say the last of that again,*” said Ilyan.

“*There is a name in a line about a road, and the office put it there itself, from a road it keeps, and it did not need any of us.*”

“*Is that a fault,*” said Skell.

“*It is not a fault,*” said Ferrow. “*I have been in this room every day for three weeks waiting for one of the lines to be wrong and it has not been wrong once, and this morning it has not been wrong twice.*”

“*Say the reading of it,*” Ilyan said, “*the part where it is the office's hand and not yours.*”

“*Every word of lines one to five is the office's and has been since the first day of the month. The name is a name and a name is a name, and a clerk wrote it off a thing the office already had, and I am not going to say the clerk did more than write, because I do not know what the clerk had.*”

---

Ilyan had been in this city thirty-three days and he had a want, and the want had a bad shape, and he worked the shape out in about four seconds and said it out loud, which he should not have done.

“*I want a line in this city's book saying that he refused conveyance,*” he said. “*I want it in a book four hundred miles off, with a date on it, saying that a man of forty-six walked out of this city at his own charge and said no to the office's cart. I want it there before the office reads what it has read today.*”

“*Why,*” said Skell.

“*Because the fifth line says a person upon a road at their own charge has made use of the road, and that is true, and it is true of him, and it is not true that anybody paid him to be on it.*”

“*Say the second half of that.*”

“*The second half is that I want a line in a book in a city four hundred miles off that says a man said no to the office, because nobody else in this city is going to say it and I am the only person here he has ever argued with.*”

“*Then say the price of it,*” said Fenna Rusk, from the second chair along, where she had been sitting since the second hour with a bare table behind her and a lamp off.

“*I know the price of it,*” Ilyan said, and he did know, and that was the difficulty and it did not stop him.

Skell wrote it. He wrote it in a book with a pencil in it, standing up, in about thirty people's hearing, and it was six words and a date, and it went into the tray on the evening of the same day in an envelope with no seal on it because there was no seal in this city to put on anything.

“*Say what you have written,*” said Ilyan, because he asked everybody what they had written and it was the only habit he had left that was worth anything.

“*That a man of forty-six of this city went out of it on the second day of this month on his own two legs, at his own charge, and refused the conveyance of the office, and said so to a person in a room in about nine people's hearing.*”

“*And the rest of it.*”

“*And that I have written it because a man with a bad leg asked me to in a room, and that I have not been sent for.*”

---

The envelope went up the hill on the second day of the week and it came back down on the fourth, and the office had written four lines on the back of it in the hand it uses for everything.

“*Say them,*” said Skell.

“*One. The office has received a statement that a person of this place went out of it at their own charge and is satisfied that the road is the office's, which was not in dispute.*”

“*Say the second one and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Two. The office notes that a person who goes out of a city upon a road the office keeps, at their own charge, is not conveyed, and is not paid the year's wage at the second row's rate, and is not entered as a person removed.*”

“*Then say the third one, because that is the one I have been waiting two days for.*”

“*Three. The office has entered the name of that person in the fifth row, and the entry is made on the office's own motion and from the office's own knowledge of a road, and no paper concerning the person was required from this city, and none was sent.*”

“*Say the fourth.*”

“*Four. A person entered in the fifth row is not to be asked about the city afterwards, and is not to be asked about the road, and the office will not put a question to that person, and does not require this city to put one.*”

The room sat with that for a long time, and the fire in the corner, which had been out for a fortnight, had somebody put a log on at some point in the last hour and was making a small noise.

“*Say the other half of that,*” said Ilyan.

Ferrow looked at the sheet for a while.

“*The other half is that the sentence in the fourth line is a protection,*” he said. “*I have read it out four times this month in two rooms and it is a protection. A person so conveyed is not to be asked about the city afterwards. It is a promise. It is the only promise the office has ever printed at this table and it has printed it twice, once for a woman of twenty-eight who never left and once for a man of forty-six who walked.*”

He squared the sheet to the edge of the table.

“*And the promise is being kept. That is what I cannot get round. If the office does not ask him about the city, and if this city is not to ask him about the city, and if he is not to be asked about the road either, then there is no road in this volume of the country with a man walking on it that anybody is permitted to speak to.*”

It went on being quiet for a long time.

“*And the man who is walking on it is the one who said a sentence in a room on the first day of this month.*”

Skell was standing very still.

“*Say that sentence,*” Ilyan said.

“*I have it in a book,*” said Ferrow, “*because a clerk of twenty-nine read it out in this room on the fifth day of the eightieth week and I wrote it, and the clerk is the man who is in the return in one column, and I did not know that when I wrote it.*”

“*Read it,*” said Skell.

“*A government which reads what you write to it is a government which has not heard you.*”

The room was a room of about thirty people and a rain came off the flat against the north side of the hill and a bell in the top of it had not been rung since the fever.

“*Say the last of it,*” said Ilyan, “*and say it as a finding and not as a thing anybody is to feel.*”

“*I have got a sheet in a book that a man wrote out of his own mouth on the first day of this month, and I have got four lines on the back of an envelope that came back down a hill. The four lines are the office agreeing with him and acting on it, and he is fourteen days up a road on his own two legs. There is nobody in this city who is permitted to ask him about any of it.*”

He put the pencil down.

“*And I am not going to be relieved about that, and I am not going to say it again.*”

---

The stamp on the sheet was from a town four days up the road, and the day on it was one day after the day this city sent the thing up the hill, and the office entered a man's name in a row about roads out of its own knowledge of a road, and the first thing it did with that name was to promise that nobody would ever ask him about any of it.

There is no form in this county that says a person may be told where a man who is walking is, and Ilyan had said out loud in a room on the fifth day of the week that he was not going to stop a man walking north, and nobody had asked him whether he was going to send anybody after him either.

The fever was twenty-one weeks and three days old, and the man of forty-six was on the far side of a stamp, and about thirty people in a room at the foot of a hill had just found out that a promise can be made about a person without that person being in the room.
