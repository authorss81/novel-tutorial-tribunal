# Chapter 437: For The First Time In Four Weeks Anything In This County Has Let Somebody Be Sent For, And It Is A Boy Of Sixteen, And The Paper Has No Head On It

The summons had no head on it. It came down in the tray at the second hour on the fifth day of the eighty-fourth week with wax on it and a seal on it, and at the top of it, where a sheet has the name of the thing it is, there was nothing at all. Shelf day three hundred and twelve, his hundred and eighty-seventh morning, thirty-seven days after the settlement, the fever twenty-two weeks old to the day, the rail down twenty-seven days.

“*Say the first line of it,*” said Skell.

Ferrow held it up.

“*There is no first line of it. It begins in the middle of a sentence.*”

“*Say the middle of the sentence.*”

“* — is summoned to appear at the second hour on this day at the room at the foot of the hill in this city, and is to answer concerning a road, and is to come without an adult if there is no adult to come with him.*”

“*Say what is at the foot of it.*”

“*A name,*” said Ferrow, and he did not read it, and about thirty people in that room who had been in it four weeks knew whose name it was.

“*Say the rest of the foot of it.*”

“*Nothing below the name. That is the whole of the foot of it.*”

“*Then say the thing I have been waiting five weeks to hear somebody say in this room,*” said Skell, and he put his hands flat on the long table. “*For five weeks, nothing that has been printed in this county has let anybody be sent for. We have all four of us said so out loud, in rooms, more than once, and it has been true, and I have counted it as one of the few good things about this month.*”

He turned the sheet round so the room could see the wax.

“*It has just let one.*”

---

The man with the summons was standing at the back of the room and had been there before anybody came in, and about nine people had walked past him on the stair.

He said who he was before anybody asked him.

“*Wray Cope. Thirty-four. Of the seat. The person appointed in the register of disputes concerning roads, and the dispute concerning the passage over the Low Road at the ninth marker was given to me in this city in a tray on the fifth day of the eighty-second week, and I have come in the ordinary way on the ordinary road and I am not asking anybody to stop me.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am the fourth person the office has sent to this city in a month and three of them were on a cart and one of them walked, and I have come on the road because I did not want to arrive the way a plate arrives.*”

Skell looked at him for a while.

“*You have come out on a road with four hurdles on it,*” he said.

“*I have,*” said Cope, “*and I have been round them on the south side, which is four yards of mud, and I did not have to, and nobody asked me to and I want that said in this room before anybody else says anything.*”

“*Say the paper,*” said Ilyan.

“*The paper has a seal on it and it has no head. I have carried sheets for eleven years and every one of them has a head, and the head says what the sheet is, and this one begins in the middle of a sentence, and I have been in this room four minutes and I have asked a clerk of twenty-nine to tell me what it is and he has read it out twice and neither reading has a head on it.*”

---

The boy was not in the room for the first two minutes and then he was in the doorway, and he had his hands behind his back, and he was doing what he had done at a door at that hour since the eighth morning of a fever.

“*Say that to the man of the seat,*” Ilyan said, quietly. “*Say that you are here and that nobody sent for you, and then say what you want, and then do not say anything else for four minutes whatever anybody in this room asks you.*”

The boy looked at him.

“*Say the last part of that again.*”

“*Do not say anything for four minutes. I have watched two men in this city get a person to talk by waiting, and the waiting is the whole of what they do, and I am telling you about it because you are sixteen and I have been in this country five weeks and I would like one thing I know to be of use to somebody.*”

“*And then what do I do?*” said the boy.

And Ilyan answered, because he was asked, and because he had a true answer ready, and because being right about the immediate mechanics is what he does and there is not one of those answers in this city that has not come back with a price on it.

“*Say only what is in front of you, and nothing else. That is what the man with the book has said in this room twice and it is correct and it is the whole of what a clerk can do, and if a question on a paper is not answered, the paper goes up a hill with a gap in it, and a clerk at the seat will either leave the gap or the office will appoint somebody itself, and the second of those is worse for you than anything on the paper.*”

The boy stood in the doorway with his hands behind his back and did not move for about four seconds.

“*Say that again,*” he said.

“*Say only what is in front of you.*”

“*Then that is what I am going to do,*” said the boy, “*and I want it written down that I worked out the way of doing it and not that I was told the way of doing it, and I want it written down before I say anything in this room, and there is a man with a pencil.*”

---

Ferrow wrote two lines and read them out, and they said that a person under age had asked for the manner of his answering to be entered before he answered anything, and that no adult was present with him, and that nobody had sent for him.

Then Cope read the summons out again from the middle, and asked the boy three questions, and every one of the three was about a road.

Did he carry a statement about a road from four doorways of the low row into a building at the middle of the nine bridges on the seventh day of the eightieth week, in the ordinary way, at the ordinary hour. That one is in the return and he said yes to it in about eleven words and did not add anything.

Whether he reported the road unsafe. That one he did not answer, and he did not say no, and he said the word *and* twice and then stopped.

And then Cope asked the third one, which was not on the summons and which he said he was asking on his own account and not out of the paper.

“*Will you tell this room which of the four doorways you were at when you said the thing that was written down?*”

Nobody in that room breathed for about two seconds.

“*No,*” said the boy.

“*I have to write that down.*”

“*You can write that down.*”

“*I will also have to write that a person under age declined to answer a question put by the person appointed, and I want you to know that I am going to do it and I am not going to be sorry for it, and I am not going to be thanked for it either, because there is nobody here who can do that and I have not asked.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Skell.

Cope looked at the room.

“*The half of it is that I put the third question on my own account. There is no line on that paper for the third question. I asked it because I am the person appointed in a register and a person appointed who does not ask a question does not do the office, and I asked it in front of thirty people so that if he said no there would be thirty people who heard it.*”

He put the pen down.

“*I did not ask it to trap anybody. I have not got anything in this room to trap anybody with and I am not from four hundred miles off in a way that lets me take a plate back off a post.*”

---

“*And now the rest of it,*” said the boy, “*and I have been working at it since the second hour and I am going to say it and then I am not going to say anything else today.*”

The room waited, which it had learned to do in four weeks.

“*I am not going to name him. I said that on the third day of this month in this room and I have not changed and I am not going to change it because there is a man of the seat standing in here with a book.*”

He came two steps further into the room.

“*And I am not going to say the road was safe and I am not going to say it was unsafe, and the reason is that I was told about the rail by four people in four doorways and one of the four wrote it down, and the other three did not, and I have not asked any of the four why.*”

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am going to say the order of the four doorways and the hour at each of them, because I have not said that to anybody, and because it is the only part of it that is mine, and because there is a man with a pencil who writes down what is in front of him and if I say it here it is in a book and it is in a book where I cannot get at it.*”

“*Do not,*” said Ilyan.

“*It is too late,*” said the boy, “*I have said that I am going to say it. I want it entered that I worked out the way of doing it and not that I was told the way of doing it, and you wrote that down, and there is a man in this room who is going to write this down as well because it is in front of him.*”

Ferrow wrote. He wrote for about a minute and a half, and he did not look down while he wrote it, and he did not say what he had written, and when Ilyan asked him he said it was the order of four doorways and the hour at each and it was four sentences and there was no name in any of them.

“*That is not the same thing as no name in it,*” said Ilyan.

“*No,*” said Ferrow.

---

The room at the foot of the hill had a boy of sixteen in it who had been the only person anybody could ask, and who had decided the terms of his own answering, and about thirty people had watched it happen.

Afterwards, in the corridor, the boy stood with his back against the wall for a while with his hands behind him and worked something out, and it took him about ninety seconds, and then he said it out loud to nobody, and Ilyan was the only person within thirty feet of him.

“*They have got the order of four doors and the hour at each of them,*” said the boy. “*And the man who wrote it down was at one of them. Not one of the other three. The one I said it at, and I have never said which one, and I have not had to, because I have just told them which one it was.*”

Ilyan had the sentence ready. It was the sentence from ten days before, the good one, the one that would have cost the boy nothing, and he had not said it then and he did not say it now.

“*Say it,*” said the boy, without turning his head. “*I know you have got one. You have had one since the second hour. Say it out loud so that I have heard it and I can decide about it, and then I will go and do nine doors.*”

He had it. It was a sentence that would have worked on a form and it would not have worked on a person. It would have cost the boy the thing he had built for himself in ninety seconds in front of thirty people, and Ilyan had learned in four weeks that being right about a form is not being right about a boy.

He had said that out loud to a room on the second day of this month, and it had not stopped him once.

He did not say it.

He stood in a corridor with a bad leg and let a boy of sixteen walk out of a building and go and do nine doors.

The boy's name was on a sheet with a seal on it that had come from four hundred miles off with no head on it at all. He had given that name to a man in a room ten days ago and asked that it not be written down, and the office had it anyway, and had sent for him on a piece of paper that does not say what for.

At the change of the light Hessa Marl stood up in the same room with about thirty people in it and said out loud, at the top of her voice, that she had put her name in a form with one line in it because she could not say no.

She said that the office now had a person at its own bar, and that she would be in that box at the hour a cart came in, and that nine doors in this ward would be done as well.

Nobody thanked her, and the boy had gone by then, and the fever was twenty-two weeks old to the day and it did not stop for a summons or for a box or for four doorways in order.
