# Chapter 407: The Same Answer, And It Is Worse This Time, And A Line Left Empty

The third day of the eightieth week was his hundred and fifty-seventh morning and the seventh day after the settlement, and the fever was seventeen weeks and five days old, and the rail had been up ten days, and the man with the bad ear asked him a question at the end of the row at the second hour, in the same place, in the same flat voice, and the clerk of thirty came down the hill with a tray and was four feet away and stood there.

Nobody had sent for anybody.

“*Go on,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*I have come a mile and a half for it and I have not got anything else to do until you do.*”

“*How many people are on the other side of that rail.*”

---

He had a true answer and it was shorter than the last one and it was worse, and the reason it was worse was that eight mornings ago the answer had been a shape — there is nobody, there is no post, there is no form — and this one had a person in it.

“*I do not know,*” he said. “*And I am going to tell you why, and the why has a name in it, and I have not said the name and I am not going to say the name, and here it is anyway.*”

“*The person who could tell you is a mile and a half down this row with a lamp on and a roll under it, and she is the only person in this city anybody can be asked about the roll of this ward, and she is forty-four years old and she has been going to those doors for eleven years and she is on the other side of that rail, and she has been since the light on the day before yesterday, on her own account, and there is no form that says a person may be sent for and no person in this city who can be sent for.*”

“*Say the number of the doors anyway.*”

“*I cannot and the woman who could not tell me either, and I will not guess at a number of people in a room.*”

The man with the bad ear stood in the road a while.

“*Then say the thing under it,*” he said, “*because you have said the top of it twice and you are a man who says things out loud and you have never once in my hearing stopped at the top of one.*”

So Ilyan said the thing under it, and he said it in front of nine people at the end of a row, which is the only method he has, and which has never once stopped him, and which is the reason there is a name at the top of a line in a book four floors up in this city and the reason a woman of forty-four is in a record and a man of thirty-one with a bad ear is in a record and why the clerk of thirty's name went on a sheet yesterday out loud in a room.

“*Nobody is on the other side of that rail at the end of it except the people who are behind it, and the only way to count them is to walk it, and the only person who can walk it is the man in the hut, and he will not say the road is fit because if he does then there is nobody left at that crossing to say he has not seen it, and I asked him to and he said no in a hut with a fire in it and I had said the price before I asked and it did not stop me and it was not supposed to.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And I am going to say the other half and then I am going to stop talking about it for a while, and the other half is this. That is the second time this week that I have said a true thing in a room and watched it not be the end of anything, and both times it was me saying it, and neither time it was me who could do the next thing. I can ask. That is all I can do. I can put a thing in a room and the room will keep it, and there is no form in this city that a room keeping a thing does anything with.*”

---

“*Then write it on a board,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*and I will carry it up the row and the boy of sixteen will say it in four doorways and by the last of the light it will be in this ward.*”

“*Do not.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because that is how it gets into a person,*” Ilyan said, “*and I have watched it do that twice in this city and both times it was the person who said the thing who was the one it went into, and neither of them was me, and one of them is forty-four and the other is twenty-eight and neither of them asked for it, and if I say that in nine people again the nine people are not the ones who will carry it. It will be the woman at the bar and the woman at the ends of the row and the clerk with the tray. I know that. I have known it for a month and I have not had a use for it.*”

The man with the bad ear looked at him.

“*Then say that in nine people as well,*” he said, “*since that is the thing you have just said and you are standing in a row.*”

And Ilyan stood in a row at the end of a ward and said out loud, in front of nine people, that a true thing said in a room goes into whoever is nearest the door, and nobody in that row wrote it down, and the boy of sixteen was not in that row and has never once been where a thing was said first, and that is the whole of the mechanism and nobody in it can stop it and it is not hidden and nobody is to blame.

---

And the clerk of thirty stood there with the tray the whole of that and put the tray down on a wall at the end of the row, and then he asked a question, and he had never asked one, and he asked it in the flat voice and everybody in the row turned round.

“*Will you put a name at the top of that line.*”

“*No.*”

“*Say why, in one line, because I have a tray.*”

“*Because a name at the top of a line is the only person anybody can be asked about that line, and because you read yours out loud in a room yesterday and said the price of it and filled it in anyway, and I have had no part in that and I am not going to have a part in yours.*”

“*That is not why,*” said the clerk of thirty. “*You do not refuse things. You have said a price and done a thing four times in this city and stopped once, and I was not in the room for the stopping and I have thought about it since. Say the real one.*”

He stood in a row with nine people in it and had to say the real one, and it took him longer than the other one.

“*Because I have been asked to say the road is fit and there is only one person in this city who can be asked that and he is in a hut, and I asked him and he said no, and I have no second road to put in front of anybody, and if I put my name at the top of that line the office will ask me what I know about a road and I will have to tell them the whole of this row, and then they will have a written account of this ward in a room four hundred miles off, and the rail will come down and the account will still be there and there will be people in this ward who are named in it and did not know they were going to be.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*There. That is the real one. It is not a better reason. It is the same reason four times running with a different road on it.*”

“*Then I am going to leave the line empty,*” said the clerk of thirty.

“*You cannot.*”

“*There is no form that says a person may leave a line empty,*” said the clerk of thirty, “*and I have been on this floor nine years and I have never left one empty, and I am going to leave this one empty, and in nine years somebody in this building is going to come back and ask me why there is a gap in a register on a shelf and I am not going to be able to tell them, and I am not going to tell you why I did it, and you are not going to ask me, and if you ask me I will have to think about it and I have not got the hour.*”

He picked the tray up off the wall.

Nobody asked him why.

---

And the man with the bad ear went and asked the carter's boy, because that is the one thing he is able to do and he has said so and it is not a job and it is not a threat, and the carter's boy was at the end of the row with his good hand on the rail and he said no again, and he said it the same way, and it was the eleventh time he had said it and he had not been asked eleven times, he had been asked three times and had said no once, and Ilyan was in the row and did not know the difference and the carter's boy told him the difference.

“*You have asked me three times and I have said no once,*” said the carter's boy. “*Eleven people have asked me eleven times and I have said no nine and said nothing twice. There is a difference between a man asking and a ward asking and it is not in the arm and it is not in the rail. I do not know what it is. I have thought about it for eight days and I have not got it and I am nineteen and I am not going to be told it by either of you.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Say it to the room,*” said the man with the bad ear.

“*I have not been in a room today and I am not going in one,*” said the carter's boy, and he took his hand off the rail and went home, and nobody stopped him, and it was the ninth time anybody had not stopped somebody in this row in ten days.

The rider left the city on the second morning with his fold delivered and thirty-six pence and a loaf, and he had slept the first night in a stable in this city and had been put through the rail by the man at the crossing, who knew by then what he was carrying and waved him on without asking, and the rider said in a doorway at the end of the low row that a man on a cart does not have to be asked what he is carrying and that it is the one good thing about a seal and the worst thing that has ever happened to him, and the boy of sixteen said it four times in four doorways, and the four bells went, and the rail was up, and the fever was seventeen weeks and five days old, and it did not stop for a line left empty, and nobody in that row was thanked.
