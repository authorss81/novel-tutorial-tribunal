# Chapter 410: A Road Open At The Fourth Hour By An Office Four Hundred Miles Off, And A Person Outside It

The sixth day of the eightieth week was his hundred and sixtieth morning and the tenth day after the settlement, and the fever was eighteen weeks and one day old, and a sheet came down the hill in the tray at the second hour with a seal on it, and it was one line long, and Bern Raye read it out standing up in the flat voice in a room that was full because a boy of sixteen had said it in a doorway at the change, and the line was this.

“*A bar upon the Low Road at the weir head in this city is taken down, the report being made by this office in respect of that road, and the persons and the premises on the north side of the crossing are to be given notice, and nothing in this sheet is a finding about the road.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Say the second half of that again,*” said the man with the bad ear.

“*Nothing in this sheet is a finding about the road,*” said the clerk of thirty, and he read it again because a man in the room had asked him to, which is a thing a clerk of thirty does four hundred times a day and has done twice in ten days, and then he put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the table and said: “*And that is the report. The first clause of the sheet that came down four days ago says the office may take down a bar upon the report of a person of the place, and the office has made the report, and I am not going to say in this room whether an office is a person of the place, and I have been asked that question twice this week by two people and I have not got an answer and I do not intend to find one before the fourth hour.*”

---

The rail came down at the fourth hour on the two days, with two men and a barrow of rubble and a lever, and nobody sent for the two men and nobody paid them, and about forty people from the low row and the four rows off it stood on the south side of the crossing in the light and watched a rail that had been up thirteen days come out of two sockets in a road.

There was no form for it. There was a return for the Mercy Trial and a bill on a wall with a blank line in the middle of it and a hearing in a road nine days ago with no return and no second hand and nothing at the end of it that anybody in four hundred miles can be shown, and there was a rail, and the rail came down, and there is no form in this city that puts a bar up and there is no form in this city that takes a bar down, and the thing that took it down was a sheet that was legible.

Nobody thanked the two men with the barrow. Nobody knew what to thank and nobody said.

And the man at the crossing was in his hut.

---

He was fifty-eight and he had kept the Low Road for twenty-nine years and he had a fire going in a hut that has no chimney and eleven flat things on a sill and a line of eleven chalk strokes on a bench that is not a record. He came out of the hut when the light went off the flat and made the road white, which is when a road is a road and not a place people are standing in, and he stood on the north side of the crossing eleven feet from a man with a lever, and he was not asked.

Nobody asked him.

There were forty people on that road and a clerk's flat voice had been read at the foot of a hill in the morning and a carter's boy of nineteen was there with his arm out of the splint by half and a woman of sixty with a shawl and a man of fifty-two and a man of sixty who had come four miles for it and had not been asked to and had not been told, and the woman of twenty-eight was not on that road at the fourth hour because she was on the far side of it at a door.

He watched it come out of the sockets. He watched the two men lever the sockets and knock the packing out and tip the rail onto the cart, and then he went back into his hut, and he shut the door, and he did not come out again that day.

Nobody went to the door. There was a man with a bad leg forty feet away who could have walked to it and did not, and he has not given a reason for that and there was nobody in the road to ask him and he did not go and tell anybody, and the boy of sixteen was not on that road either and does not go to everything.

Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked and he had said in a hut nine days ago that he was not going to be and he did not have to say it a second time to anybody, and he did not.

---

And a mile and a half down the low ground, in a room behind a chandler's shop with a lamp on and a roll under it, there was a woman of forty-four who had been on the far side of a rail for seven days and had come over the crossing at the fourth hour with about thirty other people, and the first thing she did in this city was to go to her own table and put her hand flat on the roll, which is what she does before she says the last of anything.

The man with the bad leg was not in the room. He was at the crossing, and he had walked down the row afterwards, and it was near the last of the light.

“*Say the figure,*” said a man who had come down the row and had not been sent for.

“*No,*” said the woman with the book.

“*You are the only person anybody can be asked about that book and you have been over there for seven days and there is not one person on this side of a rail who can be asked about it and I am not asking you about the book, I am asking you what it is like to be the only person who can be asked about a thing and not be asked.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*It is like every other day since a man with a bad leg put my name in a chamber four floors up,*” said the woman with the book. “*I have been the only person anybody can be asked about that roll for eleven years and about a person for nine weeks and the difference is that for eleven years I could walk to it.*”

She turned the chair round to the table, which is what she does when she is going to do something with her hands, and she put two fingers on the roll.

“*And I am not going to be thanked,*” she said. “*I have said that in three rooms and a bench of four and a magistrate and I am going to say it in this one and then I am going to stop saying it, because I have worked out in the last seven days that saying it does not stop anybody and I have never once been relieved by saying it and I am forty-four and I have nine rounds and two hundred and twenty-four days of a tenth in that book and the door I went to on the third day of this week is a door I have still got to go to, and there are about four miles of road on the other side of a rail that was up until an hour ago and there is nobody going to be in a room at the end of two years and a hundred and twelve days to be asked whether it was done properly.*”

“*Then say the part about the sheet.*”

“*The part about the sheet is that it says nothing in it is a finding about the road,*” said the woman with the book, “*and the rail came down anyway, and I am going to be careful here because I have been a person in a record for nine weeks and everything I say gets kept and I do not want it kept wrongly.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*A road in this city is now a thing that can be shut and a thing that can be opened, and neither of those is a finding about the road, and both of them are a decision about the road, and both of them were made by a sheet that a man on a cart carried here, and not one person who lives on that road was asked about either of them. It is not a bar any more and it is not a way to leave. It is a road that a place does not have a say in. And in two years and a hundred and twelve days there is going to be a person in this city who was not born who can be asked what happened to the Low Road, and she is going to ask the clerk, and the clerk will read her the sheet, and the sheet says nothing in it is a finding about the road.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*That is a worse thing than a bar and I have been over the rail seven days and I have had nothing else to think about,*” she said. “*A bar is a rule with a room missing out of it and you can go and stand at it. That is a rule with a person missing out of it, and there are four hundred people on that road and not one of them is in it.*”

---

And the woman of twenty-eight was on the far side of that road at the last of the light with her slate under her arm, in the four miles where the doors are, and she had gone over at the fourth hour with about thirty other people and had not come back, and that was not a decision anybody made and it was not a decision she announced.

There are about four miles of houses and a road and a row above a plate yard's and the far side of the ninth weir, and the doors in it are doors she has been going to since the eighth morning of a fever, and there were nine hundred doors in this ward on the day the settlement was read out and there are nine hundred doors in it this evening, and the rail was up thirteen days, and a door behind a rail is a door that is shut, and a shut door is not on a list and not off a list, it is a door somebody went to.

She is the only person in this ward anybody can be asked about the doors and she cannot say no, and she is the only person anybody can be asked about the road as well now, because of a line at the top of a sheet, and there is no form in four hundred miles that takes a name off a page and none that asks her whether she agreed, and there is a clerk four floors up who filled it off a paper in front of him in the ordinary way, and a man with a bad leg who said the price out loud in a room before it happened and it did not stop him, and a boy of sixteen who said it in four doorways and is sixteen.

The road was open. The rail was in a cart in a yard at the low end. The water at the low end had gone down another inch in the week and the hole in the near side was packed and rolled and had held nine days of carts, and about a hundred and forty people in this city were dead before the first morning a man with a bad leg came down off a road into it, and they are dead, and the fever was eighteen weeks and one day old and it had gone down a fortnight ago because a fever goes down, and the order had an end and a date and a review and a second hand on it and the second hand was four men and not a person, and about nine hundred doors were still being corrected at thirty a fortnight over a round of four hundred and twenty days and then the same four hundred and twenty days over the doors that were shut, and the line on the bill for the three hundred names was blank and the blank was the correct figure, and a bell in a county four hundred miles off was still not hung, and a porch in a valley of sixty people was still gone.

The four bells went over Tallowgate. A man of fifty-eight was in a hut at a crossing with the door shut, and a woman of forty-four had a lamp on and a roll under it and had said she was going to stop saying she would not be thanked, and a woman of twenty-eight was on the far side of an open road with a slate under her arm and forty-nine days of doors behind her and no form in four hundred miles that puts her in the room where the answer is, and nobody in this city thanked anybody, and the road was open.

*And there is still a name for what he has been doing for ten days, and nobody gave it to him and nobody in this city is going to use it about him. A Remedy Drafter is a person who proposes a real thing and takes the blame of it in front of the people it lands on, and he proposed one at a rail in a road, and it was refused by a man in a hut, and it cost a woman of twenty-eight her name, and the road came down anyway from a room four hundred miles off, and there is no power in any of it. It is a piece of work and not a rank, and it is not a step up to anything, and the only thing it has done to him this week is that a clerk's flat voice said a name off the foot of a sheet and did not say where it went.*
