# Chapter 436: An Answer About Four Pounds And Four Shillings, And The Only Way To Be Paid Is To Be A Register

The answer about the sum came down the hill in the tray at the second hour on the fourth day of the eighty-fourth week, unwaxed, on one side of a sheet, with the other side blank. Shelf day three hundred and eleven, his hundred and eighty-sixth morning, thirty-six days after the settlement, the fever twenty-one weeks and six days, the rail down twenty-six days.

About nine people were in the room behind a chandler's shop and Fenna Rusk was at the bare table with the lamp on.

“*Say the head of it,*” said Ilyan, “*and then all four lines, and I am not going to interrupt you and I am not going to ask you for the second half of anything, because I have asked for a second half nine times in a month and I have got one nine times and I would like a morning off.*”

“*One. The office has received from this city a claim of four pounds and four shillings in respect of the keeping of a roll of doors, and the sum is in a book at the seat, and it has been in that book since it was sent up on the fourth day of the eighty-third week.*”

“*Say the second one.*”

“*Two. The sum is payable to a person of this place entered in a register of keepers of rolls.*”

“*Say the third one and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Three. A roll of doors sent to the office in the column headed persons and things reported to the office in respect of the Low Road, this city, and the bar lately upon it, has been entered in that column, and the person in whose keeping it was is not entered in a register of keepers of rolls, and the office does not enter a person in that register upon the sending of a roll, and does not require one to be entered.*”

“*Say the last one.*”

“*Four. Where a person of this place declines a sum, the office notes the declining, does not press for the sum, and does not pay it, and the declining is entered in the book in which the sum is kept. And nothing in this sheet is a finding about a roll.*”

Fenna Rusk had her hands flat on the table and she had not moved while any of it was read.

“*Say the working,*” said Darrin Skell.

Nobody had asked him. He had come up the low ground from the end of the plate yard at about an hour before the light and had not been sent for.

“*Say the working on the sum,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and say it out loud, and do it with the working and not the answer, and there is a man with a pencil in this room and about nine of us.*”

---

He did it standing up, with his hands flat on the table, the way he does, and it took him about ninety seconds and nobody said a word in it.

“*Four thousand and four days. A keeper of a roll is paid four shillings a year. A year in this city is three hundred and sixty-four days, the way the office counts it, and eleven times three hundred and sixty-four is four thousand and four. So it is eleven years and no days, and I have been saying about eleven years since the first day of this week because I did not want to be the man who stopped a woman from a figure in her own room.*”

“*Say the next part.*”

“*Eleven years at four shillings a year is forty-four shillings.*”

He stopped.

“*Say the rest of it,*” Ilyan said.

“*There is no rest of it. Forty-four shillings is two pounds and four shillings, and I have been saying four pounds and four shillings in this city since the first day of this week, and I have said it in this room, and I have said it on a step, and a man of twenty-nine put it in a book, and it has gone up a hill, and it is in a book four hundred miles off at this moment.*”

Nobody moved.

“*Say where the other one came from,*” said Fenna Rusk, in her own mouth, and she had not said a word for four minutes.

“*From me, in this room, out loud, with the working, on the first day of this week.*” He did not look up. “*I did not double it. I never wrote a two. I have sat down with a pencil twice since and gone through it the way I am going through it now, and I get the same figure, and it is half of what I said, and I have had nine days of knowing that and I have not said it in a room, and I would like the room to know that I knew.*”

“*Say what you want done about it,*” said Ilyan.

“*Nothing at all,*” said Skell, “*and that is the first time in a month I have been asked that question and given that answer.*”

---

“*Then say the second half of the sheet,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and I will tell you whether the office has got it right.*”

Skell looked at the sheet for a while and then at the man with the pencil.

“*Line two. The sum is payable to a person of this place entered in a register of keepers of rolls. That is correct, and there is no register of keepers of rolls in this county, or in this city, and the office has not created one, and it does not require one.*”

“*Say the next part of it.*”

“*Line three says that a roll sent in the column with that head is not in a register of keepers of rolls, and that the office does not enter a person in one upon the sending of a roll, and does not require one to be entered.*”

She put her hand flat on the table.

“*So the only way the four pounds and four shillings gets paid is that somebody in this city asks to be entered in a register that does not exist.*”

“*Yes,*” said Skell.

“*And asking is a form.*”

“*It would have to be. I have looked for one for nine days. There is no form for it and there is no line on any of the four sheets that have come down this hill in a month for anybody to enter themselves in anything.*”

“*And if there is no form,*” Ilyan said, and then stopped, because he had a sentence in his mouth and it was the right one.

The room waited, because a room of about nine people in a city of ninety thousand waits when a man with a bad leg stops in the middle of a sentence.

---

The sentence was this, and he did not say it: *that a person of this place can be entered at the seat, and that a person who asks to be entered is a person who has asked, and that asking is a thing a person can do in a room four hundred miles off with a form, and that the office does not have to be asked twice.*

He did not say it, and he knew inside about two seconds why.

The reason was that being entered in a register of keepers of rolls in a city four hundred miles off is the exact thing that happened to her on the fourth day of this week. The reason it happened is that a person carried a paper about her into a building in the ordinary way. If he said the sentence in this room this morning then he would be telling a woman who cannot see a roll that the way to get her money is to become a person in a book again.

He had spent four weeks learning what that does. He had a page in his own hand with a fold in it and a knot on it that was not the knot it went up with.

So he kept the sentence, and he put his hand on the back of a chair instead of on the table, and Fenna Rusk turned her head about a quarter of an inch in the direction he was standing in, because she heard better when she was not being looked at and she was not being looked at.

“*You have stopped in the middle of a sentence,*” she said.

“*I have.*”

“*Say so.*”

“*I have had a sentence and I have not said it and I am not going to say it, and I am going to say why in one line, and you are not going to like it, and then I am going to go and stand outside.*”

“*Say the line.*”

“*The sentence is a way for you to be entered in a book in order to be paid, and I have watched what being in that book did to you on Friday, and I am not going to hand you the key to it at ten in the morning without asking you first.*”

“*Then ask me first,*” said Fenna Rusk.

He did not ask her first. That was the second thing, and it was worse than the first, and he did it in about half a second, and he was aware of the whole of it happening and he let it happen, which is the thing he has been doing all month and which he named out loud in a room a fortnight ago and which has not stopped him once.

“*I am going outside,*” he said.

---

“*I am not going to be relieved about that,*” said Fenna Rusk, to the room, and not to him. “*A man has a sentence and does not say it, and I have sat at this table for eleven years and been asked for my own work by people with books, and I know exactly what it looks like when somebody is managing me for my own good, and I have been managed before, and I have been paid in nothing every time.*”

Skell shifted his weight.

“*Say the other half of it,*” he said, “*because it is no worse now than it was, and because the clerk is going to write it down and I would rather he wrote it than that I said it.*”

“*The other half is that he is right about the sentence, and I would have said no to it, and I am not going to be thanked for a thing I was never asked.*” She put both hands flat on the wood. “*And the half of that which is the whole of it: he did not ask me, and I said ask me first, and he went out of the room, and he has now said out loud that he went out of the room, and there is a man with a pencil and I do not know whether he wrote that down.*”

“*I did not write that down,*” said Ferrow, “*and I am going to say what I did write down and it is four sentences, and there is a name in one of them and I have said nine times in four weeks that a name in a sentence is a name in a book.*”

“*Say it,*” said Skell.

“*That a person of this place has been sent a sheet about a sum of four pounds and four shillings, and that the sheet is correct, and that a person of this place has read it in about nine persons' hearing in her own room, and that she has not been asked whether she wishes to be entered in anything.*”

---

Then she refused the money, and she refused it in front of all nine of them, and she did it in about forty words.

“*I am not going to be entered in a register of keepers of rolls in order to be paid four pounds and four shillings for eleven years of a roll I kept in a room behind a chandler's shop. The entering is the thing that happened to me on Friday. There is no form for the entering. And a man with a bad leg stood in this room ten minutes ago with a way of doing it and would not give it to me.*”

“*Say the last part of it,*” said Skell, “*because you have not said what happens to the money.*”

“*The money stays where it is. I am not going to chase it to a town four hundred miles off with a form in my hand and my name at the top of it, and I have not been paid in eleven years and I am not going to spend the eleventh asking.*”

Ferrow wrote it. He wrote for about a minute and then he stopped and looked at what he had written and did not read it out, which is not what he does.

“*Say what is in it,*” said Skell.

“*Four sentences and a date,*” said Ferrow, “*and the last of them is that the declining of the person is to be entered in the book at the seat in which the sum is kept, which is on the sheet, and which the office printed before she said it.*”

The room went on for about four seconds without a sound in it.

“*Say what that is,*” Ilyan said from the door, and he had said he was going to stand outside and he had not got as far as outside.

“*It is a line in a sheet that came down the hill in the tray this morning,*” said Ferrow, “*and line four of it says that where a person of this place declines a sum, the office notes the declining and does not press for the sum. And it is dated the sixth day of the eighty-third week, and she has said her declining today, which is the fourth day of the eighty-fourth week, and the office had it five days before she said it.*”

“*Say the half of that which is not the office's fault,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The half that is not the office's fault is that the office is right,*” he said. “*It has been right about everything it has printed in this city for a month and it has printed a shape for you before you stood in the shape, and it did that because you are a person of this place in a book and a person of this place in a book declines, and it is not going to be embarrassed about having been right.*”

“*Then I am going to be a person of this place in a book that declines,*” she said. “*Write that. That is the whole of what I have got and I would like it in a book four hundred miles off with the day on it.*”

He wrote it.

---

The four pounds and four shillings is in a book at the seat, and four pounds and four shillings is the wage of twenty-one years, and the work is eleven years of it, and the man who said the figure said it in a room with the working and nobody checked the working. The office was right to hold what it was sent, and the office is holding ten years of a wage that was never done, and the woman it is owed to has declined it in front of nine people.

The declining went up the hill on the evening of the same day in an envelope with no seal on it, and a clerk at the seat will enter it in the book in which the sum is kept, on the day after it arrives, and the woman it is owed to will not see that page, and no form in this county will send it to her.

And outside a room behind a chandler's shop a man with a bad leg stood in the rain with a sentence in his mouth that would have been the right one, and the rightness of it had a price in it, and he had named that price out loud in a room four times this month and had not once named this one.

The fever was twenty-one weeks and six days old, and two pounds and four shillings of it is correct, and about nine people in a room had just watched the correct figure get said out loud by the man who had got it wrong.
