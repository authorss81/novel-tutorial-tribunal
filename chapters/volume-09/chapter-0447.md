# Chapter 447: Twenty-Two Of Thirty-One Wards Answer, And Twenty-Five Names Go In The First Lines And Twenty-Two Go In The Second, And One Of The First Lines Has A Box In It And No Person

First day of the eighty-sixth week. Shelf day three hundred and twenty-two, his hundred and ninety-seventh morning, forty-seven days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and three days, the rail down thirty-seven days.

The last day of the six was a Tuesday, and about forty people were in the long room at the foot of the hill at the second hour, and there were twenty-two sheets on the long table, and one of them had a name of a thing in it.

“*Say the number,*” said Skell, standing up with his hands flat. “*Say it before anybody says anything else. And then I am going to do the working, and I would like the room to let me get to it.*”

“*Twenty-two wards of thirty-one have sent in the form,*” said Ferrow. “*Nine have sent nothing.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*There is a foot to the form that says the number of sheets received and the day. It is in the office's hand, and it is twenty-two, and the day is this one.*”

“*Say the nine,*” said Ilyan.

“*The nine are not on anything. There is no list of the nine. The form has a column for a ward's number, and the sheets that came in have a number in that column, and the nine that sent nothing have no number on any paper in this city.*”

“*Say the office's line about them again,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*A ward that sends none has not declined and is not entered as having declined. I have read that line out three times since Wednesday and I am going to read it out again this morning, because nobody in this room has done anything with it.*”

---

“*Say the rest of the working and do not make it small,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*Twenty-two sheets. Twenty-two first lines.*”

Skell put one hand down and kept the other on the table.

“*Three of the twenty-two have two names in the first line, because three wards sent two. So that is twenty-five names.*”

“*Say the next part of it and say it slowly.*”

“*There are twenty-two names in the second lines as well, and two of those are the same man in two different wards. Darrin Skell, counting hall and doors, once for the ward at the end of the plate yard, and once for the ward at the middle of the nine bridges. So that is twenty-one persons in the second lines.*”

“*And now take the first lines back,*” said Nell Fisk, who had read the office's request aloud on the flat on the Thursday it came down, and had read four ward sheets aloud in a street on the Saturday, “*Say how many of the twenty-five are people.*”

“*Twenty-four,*” said Skell. “*One of the twenty-five is not a person. It is the keeper of the box at the ninth marker, which is a thing with a roof on it and a shutter and a bench in it, and a woman in it at the hour a cart comes in. That ward has sent the keeping of it and not the woman, and there is no name on the sheet, only a number.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not let anybody make it smaller,*” said Ilyan.

“*I do not know which ward it is. There are thirty-one numbers on the column and I have been in about nine of the thirty-one halls in this city and I am not going to guess in a room.*”

“*Then say the whole of the count in one line and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Thirty-one wards asked, twenty-two answered, nine sent nothing. Twenty-five names in the first lines and twenty-four persons. Twenty-two names in the second lines and twenty-one persons. One line with nobody in it at all.*”

He took his hand off the table.

“*And the office has written the figure twenty-two on the foot of a form in its own hand, and will go on using twenty-two.*”

---

The sheet from the office was in the middle of the twenty-two. It was the same sheet that had gone out on the third day of the week, with a line added at the foot of it.

“*Say the added line,*” said Skell.

“*Where wards have named persons under a form of this office or under a form of the city, the office notes the naming, treats the matter as settled in respect of those wards, does not require the naming by the wards that have not sent one, and does not check what has been named.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the office's fault,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*None of it. Every word of it is correct, and it is the fourth thing in six weeks the office has sent down that is correct and useless, and I have not been able to find the wrong word in it in about four minutes, and I have tried twice.*”

“*Say the other half of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The other half is that the office has settled twenty-two of thirty-one, and that the other nine are settled too. Because the same line says the office does not require the naming by the wards that have not sent one.*”

He looked at the twenty-two sheets on the table.

“*And so there is a settlement of the matter of the roads in the whole of this city, and it was made on one day by twenty-two wards, and nobody in this city agreed to it, and in nine wards nobody was asked.*”

Marrow had come in at the back at the second hour and had not sat down, and he said one sentence from there, and it was heard.

“*Twenty-two of thirty-one is a road administration,*” he said. “*I have governed this country for nineteen years on nine of thirty-one, and I never once put the figure in a room, and I am not going to say a word against the other nine, because I know exactly what the other nine are. I have been one of them.*”

Nobody answered him, and about four people agreed with him out loud, and he wrote down nothing because there is nobody in this room whose book he is in.

---

The second foot line had been filled in.

It said, in a fair hand that was not Ilyan's and not a clerk's, on the fifth of six pages, in a room off the low ground on the evening of Saturday. The word *placed* had been struck through and the word *put* had been written over it, and under the word *put* the clerk had drawn a line and left it.

“*Say the name in it,*” said Skell, and about nine people in the room had already looked at it and had not said it.

“*Fenna Rusk,*” said Ferrow. “*Keeping a roll of doors. And the ward that sent it is the ward at the end of the low row, which is her ward.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it.*”

“*And that ward has now named her three times in two days. Once in the first line. Once in the second line on Saturday. And once in the second line at the foot of the form on Saturday evening.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The half of it is that the ward was not asked which of the three to leave out, and the office is not asked, and there is no form in this county that asks a ward whether it may name the same person for two different things.*”

She put her hands flat on the wood.

“*And I am now named in three places in three days, and I have not been asked about any of them, and I am not going to be.*”

“*Go on to the end of that.*” said Ilyan.

“*And the rest of it is that he did it.*”

She did not turn her head, and Ilyan was eight feet from her at the second chair along.

“*He wrote that line and left it empty in a window, and he has not filled it in, and he is not going to. And the office asked at the top of a hill, and I have answered at the top of a hill and in this room, and there is a fair hand in a form with a name in it and it is mine.*”

“*Say the last of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Skell.

“*And I have not said one word about whether I would do it, and he has not either, and there is nobody in this room who is going to ask me, and that is correct, and it is the whole of what I have got, and I would like it in a book four hundred miles off with the day on it.*”

Ferrow wrote it down in the ordinary way, and read it out, and it was four sentences, and one of them had a day in it and another had a name.

---

The question came from the back of the room, and it came from Bess Low, who was sixty and had been standing against a wall since the second hour.

“*Say it out loud,*” said Ilyan, “*and say it to the room and not to me. I have asked for that nine times in six weeks and I am going to keep asking for it until somebody stops me.*”

“*There is a form in the window with two lines in it,*” Bess Low said, “*and thirty-one wards have been asked to fill them in, and twenty-two have, and nine have not. And the office says that a ward that sends none has not declined.*”

“*Go on.*” said Skell.

“*I have read that three times this week and I want to say the thing it does, and I want to say it in a room and not in a street, because I have been in this city nine years and I have never once been counted and I am not going to do this outside a door.*”

She looked at the twenty-two sheets.

“*It means that a ward can be silent and be protected by nothing. Not by the form, because the form has no line for silence. Not by the office, because the office says it does not require a naming and does not enter a declining.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not leave it in the middle,*” said Skell, and about nine people in the room had gone completely still.

“*And not by him, because he wrote the second line and left the first foot line empty, and there is no line in six pages anywhere for a ward to say no in.*”

“*Say what is at the bottom of that,*” said Fenna Rusk, and she said it to the second chair along.

Ilyan had the answer in about two seconds, and it was the right one, and he said it.

“*Because if there is a line for a ward to say no in, then this city has thirty-one wards and about twenty-two of them have said no, and the office has nine, and the office is right, and I would have handed the office nine wards on Monday.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop in the middle,*” said Skell.

“*And I have not handed the office nine wards on Monday, and I am not going to hand the office nine wards today.*”

“*Then say why not,*” said Ilyan, and he said it before about nine people in the room could, and the room turned over onto his side of it inside of a second, the way a room does.

“*I wrote a form that asks a ward for a person. I did not write a form that asks a ward for a refusal, and I did not write it because I am afraid of what a refusal would be asked to carry.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*I have said it now in a room and it is going in a book, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be relieved about it either. And a great many people have read the third line of it standing in the rain this week and did not know what it cost, and that is the ordinary way of these things, and I have stopped expecting it to be otherwise.*”

---

The result stood, and it was read out in a room at the foot of a hill at about the fourth hour on the first day of the eighty-sixth week. It stood the way a thing stands when nobody in the room can make it stand any other way.

“*Say the result,*” said Skell. “*In one sentence, and say it as a fact and not as a settlement, because the office has settled it and we have not.*”

Ilyan read it off the foot of the form, in a fair hand, with his own name at the top of the fifth page and Fenna Rusk's under it.

“*Twenty-two of thirty-one wards have answered, and twenty-four persons have been named in the first lines as those who may be asked concerning the roads, the keeping of them, and any bar upon them.*”

“*Say the rest of it and say the whole of it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And twenty-one persons have been named in the second lines to stand beside the first, and one line has the keeping of a box in it and no person, and nine wards have named nobody.*”

“*And the last of it,*” said Skell.

“*And the person who drew this form up is a person not of this place, and cannot be entered and cannot be struck out, and the person named beside him is a person of this place, and can be asked about him for as long as the form stands.*”

He put the sheet down on the long table.

“*That is the result. It is not a settlement. It is twenty-two wards that found a person and nine wards that found nobody, and a man who cannot be removed from the bottom of a page, and a woman who can be asked about him until somebody stops it.*”

He put his hand flat on the long table.

“*And the office will go on using the figure twenty-two, and the figure twenty-two is not the number of people. And neither of those two things is going to stop on its own, and I have not got a remedy for either.*”

And nobody in the room thanked him for it, and there is no form in this county that says a man may be thanked. The fever was twenty-three weeks and three days old, and on the low road two miles out four hurdles and nine links of chain and a plate were still up on the north side of a crossing. The second seat at the council of the Shelf was vacant, and the office of Regent of the Shelf was not held by anybody at all, and the roads were therefore nobody's, and the bar was therefore still up.
