# Chapter 426: A Roll Copied Fair Into A Return And Carried Up The Hill Inside A Coat, And What It Cost A Woman Of Forty-Four Who Said All Of It In Her Own Mouth

The first day of the eighty-third week was his hundred and seventy-sixth morning and the twenty-sixth day after the settlement, and the fever was twenty weeks and three days old, and the rail had been down sixteen days, and a mile and a half down the low ground, in a room behind a chandler's shop, the lamp was on at the second hour and the roll was on the table and not under it, and there were about nine people in that room and none of them had been sent for and no form in four hundred miles lets a person be sent for.

The woman of twenty-eight was not in the room. She was at the end of the low row at a door with a slate under her arm, doing the ninth of nine, and nobody had told her that anything was happening in a room a mile and a half from her step and nobody was going to.

“*Say the question,*” said the man with the bad leg, “*and say it in the form of a question and not in the form of a request, because I have asked four rooms in this city for things in ten days and every one of them was a request and I would like one that is a question.*”

“*Can the roll say whether the person who went out of this city on the seventh day of the eighty-second week at the fourth hour is the person who came back into it at the last of the light.*”

The man of fifty-two was at the end of the table and had come up from the end of the plate yard's and had not been sent for, and the clerk of forty had a book and a pencil and was standing up because he reads things out standing up and he is a man who copies fair and has been on that floor three years.

“*No,*” said Fenna Rusk. “*And I am going to say why and I am going to say it once and I have said on the sixth day of this week that I have stopped saying that I will not be thanked, and I am not going to start again, and I am not going to be relieved about this either, and I have got a lamp on and about nine people in my own room and I am going to say the whole of it in my own mouth and I would like it all written down because it is going to be in a book and it is not going to be in one otherwise.*”

“*Say it,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*A roll counts doors. A door opened at the second hour and a door shut at the last of the light and a roll has both of those in it and can say both of those out loud with the hour against them, and that is what a roll is and it is what I have kept for eleven years. And the person is not in it. The person went out of a door and came in at a door and the door is the same door and the roll can say the door and cannot say the person, and that is not a fault in the roll and I have never once said it was a fault in the roll, and if a man in this room has come a mile and a half to find a fault in it then he has come for the wrong thing and I would rather he had come for nothing.*”

“*Then say how a person gets into it,*” said the clerk of forty, “*because I have the form in front of me and I am going to read the head of it out, and I would like to read it out before the man with the bad leg asks for it a second time and before I am asked again whether I have written a thing down, and I am twenty-nine and I write them fair and I have never read one out to anybody and this is the fifth thing I have read out in twelve days.*”

He read it out.

“*Return of the number of the persons of this place, by ward, and of the doors by street, for the keeping of a place under the fifth row. The return is to be made by a person of the place. The office does not provide the number and the office will not check it.*”

Nobody said anything, and he turned the form round on the table, which is a thing he does.

“*And the head of the other one, which is not a form but a return with a column in it, and which came up this hill with the schedule on the first day of the eighty-first week, and which is in the press on the ground floor at a penny a page and can be read standing up and anybody in this city can be shown it for a penny.*”

“*Say the head of it,*” said the man of fifty-two.

“*The head of it reads: persons and things reported to the office in respect of the Low Road, this city, and the bar lately upon it.*”

About nine people in a room behind a chandler's shop sat with that, and the man of fifty-two put his hands flat on the table and the clerk of forty did not look down at his book.

“*Then that is what it is,*” said the man of fifty-two, “*and I am going to say the whole of it in about four sentences and I have had it since the second hour of the first day of this week and I have been waiting for a room with the form on the table in it. If that roll goes up that hill in that column, then the head of it stops being a roll and becomes the head of a return, and everything under a head is a thing. And there is one woman in this city that a thing can be asked about and she is sitting at this table, and from today she is not a person in a book. She is a keeper. And a person can be struck out of a book of the office and there is a form for it at the seat and the striking out is done by the office and not by the person. And a thing cannot be struck out, because the form begins with a person who does not wish to remain entered, and a thing has no wish and there is nothing in four hundred miles that takes one.*”

Nobody said anything, and the woman of forty-four put her hand flat on the table where the roll was, and did not look at it.

“*And the working on what that costs her,*” said the man of fifty-two. “*Four thousand and four days. That is nine rounds of four hundred and twenty days and two hundred and twenty-four days of a tenth, and nine times four hundred and twenty is three thousand seven hundred and eighty, and three thousand seven hundred and eighty and two hundred and twenty-four is four thousand and four. And four thousand and four days is about eleven years. And a keeper of a roll is paid four shillings a year, and eleven years of that is four pounds and four shillings, and I am not going to say the next part in this room and I am going to say it because there is a man with a pencil and there are about nine of us and a man of twenty-nine said in a room at the foot of a hill two days ago that he is the one they are going to ask.*”

“*Say the next part,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*She is the one they are going to ask. Not about a roll. About a person who went out of a road and came back into it, once, on the seventh day of this week, and about every one that goes after her, for as long as there is a road and a form and an office that does not check a number. And there is no line on that form for a person who says no, and there is no line on it for a person who says yes, and there is no line on it for her name at the top, because the head of the form is a head of persons and things and not a head of names.*”

The boy of sixteen was at the door of that room and had said nothing and had not been asked to, and he was sixteen and there are about nine people in that room and nobody had looked at him.

“*Then I am going to say the part that is mine and nobody has to write it and I am saying it because the two men in this room have both asked for a second half and one of them is going to have to be the one who pays and it is not going to be him.*”

She took her hand off the table.

“*A roll cannot be asked whether a person is the same. That is not a fault in a roll. That is what a roll is for, and I have said that since the beginning and I have said it in four rooms and a bench of four and a magistrate and nobody has printed it. To make a roll say whether a person is the same, you have to stop it being a roll, and you have to put it in a column, and a roll in a column is a register, and a register goes up the hill, and there is no line on any form in four hundred miles for the keeping of a roll, and the office does not pay for a copy of one, and does not require a copy of one, and will return an original so conveyed in the ordinary way. And the ordinary way is a man on a cart, paid by whoever sent it, and whoever sends it is a man of twenty-nine who writes them fair.*”

“*Say the last of it,*” said the man with the bad leg, “*and say the working on the four pounds and four shillings, because you have said the rest of it and I am asking for that.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*I am not going to say it,*” said Fenna Rusk. “*And I am going to say why I am not going to say it, and it is because a keeper of a roll is paid four shillings a year and there is one of me and I have not been paid one of them in eleven years, and the only number in this city that anybody has ever asked me for is what a roll is worth, and there is no such number, and the reason there is no such number is that a roll is worth a person and I am the person, and I am not going to stand in my own room with about nine people in it and put a figure on myself. That is the whole of it. That is what I have been keeping for eleven years instead of a figure.*”

The clerk of forty had the pencil out and had not looked down at it for about a minute and a half.

“*Say whether you have written that down,*” said the man with the bad leg.

“*I have written that a person of this place has said, in a room, in nine people's hearing, that a roll cannot be asked whether a person is the same, and that the office's return has a column with the head of persons and things on it, and that a roll sent up that hill in that column leaves this city without the only thing anybody can be asked about a roll in it, and that a person cannot be struck out of a book and a thing cannot either and there is no form for either.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*I have not written her name, because the head of my book has no line for the name of a person who says a thing in a room like this one, and I filled a line off a paper in front of me in the third week of last month that put a name at the top of a page in a city four hundred miles off, and I said the cost of that out loud in a room and I did it anyway, and I have been on this floor three years and I have not got a better way of writing a thing down and I am not going to invent one at a table in a room behind a chandler's shop.*”

The roll went up the hill that afternoon inside the coat of a man of twenty-nine, and before it went he copied eleven pages of it fair into a second one, in his own hand, and put the second one in a press on the ground floor of a building at the middle of the nine bridges, where it is a penny a page and there is a window in the room and anybody in this city can be shown it for a penny.

He read the first page of the copy out to the woman who could not see it, standing up, in her own room, with about nine people in it.

“*That is not my hand,*” she said.

“*No,*” he said. “*It is fair and it is legible and it is what goes up instead of the page, and the page goes up as well, and the copy is the one that stays in this city, and I have read the first page of it back to you and I have read all eleven and I will read any of them again in this room or in the room at the foot of the hill in front of anybody who comes, and nobody has thanked me for one thing in three years and nobody is going to start.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Then I cannot tell you whether it is right,*” said Fenna Rusk, “*and that is what it cost, and I am saying it in my own mouth in my own room with the lamp on, and I was the only person in this city anybody could be asked whether a roll was right and I was that person at the second hour this morning and I am not that person at the fourth, and it happened in a room with the door open and nobody sent for anybody, and there is a copy of it in a press in a building at the middle of the nine bridges and it is in a stranger's letters and I can read it and I cannot tell you whether it is right, and I am not going to be relieved about that and I am not going to be told at the end of the month that I was right to send it, and I have kept that roll for eleven years and I have not been paid one hour of it and I am not going to be thanked.*”

She put the lamp out.

“*And I am going to say the last of it and it is short and it is for the man with the bad leg, because he asked for the working four times this morning and he is the only one in this room who came for a question and got one.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I have been asked what a roll costs. I have never been asked what a year of a roll costs, and there is one of me, and the answer is four pounds and four shillings, and I have had none of it, and the office will say in a room four hundred miles off that it notes the sum and does not press for it, and it has been saying that since the third row of a schedule came down this hill, and it is a true sentence and I want it noticed that it is true.*”

The lamp was out in a room behind a chandler's shop and the table was bare, and the fever was twenty weeks and three days old, and it did not stop for a roll, and not one of them in that room was thanked.
