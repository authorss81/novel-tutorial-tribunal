# Chapter 449: A Sheet Comes Down In The Tray At The Second Hour On The Morning The Regent Goes Down The Road, And The Mark At The Foot Of It Is Two Words In A Die, And One Of Its Clauses Governs Everybody In A Register Whether They Are Alive Or Not

Third day of the eighty-sixth week. Shelf day three hundred and twenty-four, his hundred and ninety-ninth morning, forty-nine days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and five days, the rail down thirty-nine days.

The man of sixty-one went down the Low Road in the rain before the light, on foot, round the four hurdles on the south side through four yards of mud. There was nobody at the second hour who had been in the room the day before and who knew what an office was for, and about nine people in that room had noticed his going and had not said anything about it.

Then the tray came up at the second hour with two things on it. One of them was an envelope with no seal on it. The other was a sheet that had not come from the office of the Regent of the Shelf, and the man of the seat was at the end of the long table with his book open when it was put down, and had been there since the second hour, and nobody had sent for him.

“*Say the envelope first and get it out of the way,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*One line,*” said Ferrow. “*That the office notes the answer of the person named in the second line, given at this city on the fifth day of the eighty-fifth week, and enters it in the book in which the form is kept, and does not press for it.*”

“*Read the rest of it out.*”

“*There is no rest of it. It is four words longer than the answer was, and it came down five days after the answer went up.*”

“*Say what that is and do not make it larger than it is,*” said Skell.

“*It is the office being correct, and it is the office writing a woman's name into a book a second time for saying a man's name out loud in a room.*”

Nobody said anything about that. About nine people in the room had said nothing about it in public, and it was entered.

---

The other sheet had a head on it and four clauses under the head, and a seal on it that was not the office's seal, and a date in a reckoning that nobody in that room used and could not check.

“*Say the head and do not translate it,*” said Skell.

“*Declaration of continuity, in respect of the Shelf, and of the office of Regent of the Shelf, and of the roads of the Shelf, and of the registers of the Shelf.*”

“*Say the first clause and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*One. The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held. The ceasing of the holder of it, and the lodging of an instrument of succession, and the declining of the person named in that instrument, are entered.*”

“*Say the rest of that clause and do not make it smaller than it is,*” said Ilyan.

“*The office is to be filled. This declaration does not fill it and does not supply a person and does not name one.*”

“*Say the second one and read it twice.*”

“*Two. This declaration is made by the First Witness. The First Witness is not of this city, does not hold the office, has not been asked by this city about anything, and is not a person.*”

“*Say the last few words of that clause again,*” said Skell, “*and I want the room to have them twice before we go on.*”

Ferrow read it twice. Nobody in the room asked him what a witness was, and about four people in it had understood the whole of the other clauses in the first reading.

“*Say the third clause and go slowly,*” said Ilyan, “*because I have read it four times and I do not understand it and I am not going to pretend that I do.*”

“*Three. This declaration governs every person entered in a register of the Shelf, in every city and town upon a road of the Shelf.*”

“*Say the rest of it and do not stop half way through it.*”

“*And it governs them whether or not they are in the city in which the register is kept, and whether or not they are alive, and it governs them after they have died in the same way that it governs them before.*”

---

The room at the foot of a hill was quiet for a length of time that Ilyan has thought about since.

Fenna Rusk was the one who worked out what it was about, and she did it in the middle of the room, out loud, and she did not need a light for it.

“*Say the rest of the fourth clause,*” she said, “*and then say the question, because there is one and everybody in this room is standing in it.*”

Ferrow read the last clause.

“*Four. Nothing in this declaration is a finding about a road, and nothing in it is a finding about any person in any city, and it takes effect according to its terms and not otherwise.*”

Then Fenna Rusk said it.

“*Every person entered in a register of the Shelf, alive or not. And a person is entered in a register of the Shelf when they are entered in one.*”

She put her hand flat on the table.

“*And this city has been sending returns up a hill for six weeks, and has had a roll of doors entered, and a schedule of payments entered, and a return with one column entered, and a line with two names in it entered.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on to the end of it,*” said Skell.

“*And a person of this place who was conveyed out of this city, entered as removed, and entered as returned the same. And about a hundred and forty people in this city are dead of a fever, and this city has been putting names into registers up a hill for six weeks, and I am not going to stand in a room and say what a person who is dead is doing in a book four hundred miles off.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not make it larger than it is.*”

“*And there is not one line in this city, on any document, that says a name of one of them. And the third clause of that sheet says they are governed by it anyway.*”

“*Say the count out loud and do not divide it,*” said Skell.

“*About a hundred and forty. That is the figure, and it is not a sum, and I am not going to add it up or split it. It is the same figure it has been since the eighty-third week, and there has never been a line for one of them.*”

“*And there is one now,*” said Ilyan.

“*Say what you mean by that, and say it as a fact.*”

“*I mean that there is a document in the tray four feet from my hand which says it governs them, and that is the first line that has ever existed anywhere in the world that has those people on it.*”

He put his hand flat on the long table.

“*And it is on it as a category and not as names, and they are governed as a class, and I do not know whether that is better or worse.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not leave it in the middle,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And I have been awake since the fourth hour and I have not got to the bottom of it, and I am not going to get to the bottom of it in a room, and I would rather say that out loud than say a thing in here that turns out to be a relief.*”

---

At the foot of the sheet there was a mark, and it was not written.

It had been pressed into the paper in a die, and it stood up off the sheet in the way a cut name does, and it was two words long.

Bevin Tarr was in the room, and he had been in it four times in fifteen days, and he had a die in his coat, and he took it out and put it on the long table beside the sheet and did not say anything for about a minute.

“*Say it,*” said Skell.

“*That is a die mark and I did not cut it,*” said Tarr. “*I have cut four thousand lines and I am paid for two dies, and I have said that in this room three times, and it is not a thing I am going to stop saying because a man with a bad leg is in the room.*”

“*Say the rest of it and say the whole of it,*” said Ilyan.

“*The mark says two words. The first of them is a word and not a name, and the second of them is Witness. And a name with a word in front of it is not a person, and there is no person in this country called that.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*And I have cut that word about nine hundred times for a seal of the seat, and I have never once cut it with anything in front of it.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Skell.

“*And whoever pressed it did it from a die, and a die has to be cut, and a person in this country has to be paid to cut one. And either this is a fourth mark I do not know about, or somebody in four hundred miles has cut a thing they have not paid me to cut, and I am the only man in this country who would know.*”

He picked his die up and put it in his coat.

“*Huh,*” he said, and did not apologise for it.

---

Cope had the book open and he did not write anything for about two minutes, and then he wrote, and read it out, and it was three sentences.

“*That a declaration of continuity in respect of the Shelf, and of the office of Regent of the Shelf, and of the roads of the Shelf, and of the registers of the Shelf, has been received in this city.*”

“*Say the rest of it and go on, and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Skell.

“*Bearing a date in a reckoning not used in this city, and not signed by a person, and sealed with a mark that a die-cutter of this city says he did not cut. And that it is entered in my book whole, and that it is not a finding about a road.*”

“*Say the rest of that and there is a rest of it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And that the person appointed has not been asked by the office, or by any person in this city, to say anything about it, and has asked for nothing, and has no line in my book for a declaration of continuity, and has entered it in the ordinary way.*”

Ilyan stood up. He had said the price of every want out loud in that room five times in six weeks, and he had never once said it after doing it, and he was not going to start.

“*I want to ask one question, and I want it in a book. The price of that is four feet of table between my hand and a sheet, and a man with a book, and an answer I do not have, and I am going to say the price before I ask it, because I have said it five times in this room and it has not stopped me once.*”

“*And the other half of it is the mistake,*” he said, before anybody had asked him. “*I have told this city for six weeks that a thing written down is a thing somebody can be asked about. And I am about to ask a man with a pencil to write down the one question in this city that nobody can be asked about, and I would like that entered as the second half of the sentence.*”

“*Ask it,*” said Skell.

“*What did the First Witness sign, and which people are still governed by that signature.*”

Ferrow wrote it down. Then he read it out, and then he said the thing he has said in four rooms in six weeks, and this time it was not a boast.

“*I do not have the answer,*” he said. “*And I have been the man they ask for six weeks, and there is a question in this book, and I do not have the answer.*”

“*Say the rest of that and say it whole,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And I am going to write that down underneath it. And if anybody comes up the hill in a year and asks me what I knew about the first witness on the third day of the eighty-sixth week, they will find out that I copied four clauses and asked for the working.*”

The fever was twenty-three weeks and five days old, and about thirty people were in a room at the foot of a hill with a sheet on the long table that governed ninety thousand living people and about a hundred and forty dead ones, and had a category for them and no names. There was no form in this county that a person in that room could fill in about it, and the right of refusal was still unrestored, and nobody in this city had one.
