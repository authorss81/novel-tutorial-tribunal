# Chapter 441: A Fifth Sheet Comes Down Under The Writ, And The Fourth Of Its Five Lines Makes The Woman Who Cannot Say No The Keeper Of A Way Out Of This Country

Second day of the eighty-fifth week. Shelf day three hundred and sixteen, his hundred and ninety-first morning, forty-one days after the settlement, the fever twenty-two weeks and four days, the rail down thirty-one days.

The tray came up at the second hour with the writ on top of it, and underneath the writ there was a fifth sheet, and the fifth sheet had no seal on it and was thin enough to read the day through.

There were about thirty people in the long room at the foot of the hill. They had stood up off a wall and then sat down again, which they did every morning, and the writ was still on the long table where it had been put the day before, squared to the edge.

Skell came up the hill from the end of the plate yard and had not been sent for. He put his slate down on the table and looked at the tray and did not say anything for about ten seconds, which is not what he does.

“*Say what is under it,*” he said at last.

“*A sheet, and it has no seal,*” said Ferrow. “*And it is dated the first day of this week, which is the day the writ came down in this tray, and I would like the room to have the two of those facts next to each other before anybody reads the sheet.*”

“*Say the head of it.*”

“*Exception, by the Regent of the Shelf, in respect of one person.*”

---

He read the five lines out standing up, one at a time, and did not stop in the middle of any of them. The room had learned in six weeks that when a man of twenty-nine does not stop in the middle of a thing, it is because the thing is one sentence.

“*One. A person not of the Shelf may be named in an instrument of succession lodged with the office of Regent of the Shelf, and may be entered in the register of that succession. And the office does not require a person able to hold that office to be of a city upon a road that runs in it.*”

Somebody near the door said *that is what he said yesterday*. About nine people said it at the same time, because a man of the seat had said it in this room on the first day of the week, and had said it about a register and not about an exception.

“*Say the second one and do not stop in the middle of it.*”

“*Two. The person so named is appointed to the second seat at the council of the Shelf, and is paid at the rate of four pounds a year. The sum is payable quarterly and does not depend upon the keeping of a road or upon the number of doors in a ward.*”

“*Say the third one.*”

“*Three. The office will lodge an instrument of succession naming the person so named, upon the ceasing of the holder of the office. And the roads of the Shelf, and the keeping of them, and the bar upon any of them, and the taking down of any bar, pass with the office as they pass under the writ of succession.*”

The room made a sound that was not a word.

“*Say the fourth one.*”

Ferrow read the fourth one twice, and the second time he read it slower, and about nine people understood why.

“*Four. The crossing at the ninth marker on the Low Road in this city is, by the exception of the Regent, a place of passage out of this country. And the keeper of the box upon the north side of that crossing is the keeper of it. And a person so passing is not to be asked about the city afterwards.*”

He put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the long table, which is what a clerk does.

Then he read the last line, in the same voice, which is the voice he reads a last line in.

“*Five. Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road, and nothing in it is a finding about any person in any city, and it takes effect according to its terms and not otherwise.*”

---

Nobody in that room could say a word for about four seconds. Then Fenna Rusk said one, from the second chair along, and she had not been in the room for the whole of it.

“*Say the working on the second line,*” she said. “*The sum, and not the rest of it. I have a figure in this city and I would like a man who counts to put the two of them on the same table.*”

Skell put his hands flat on the long table and did it in about thirty seconds.

“*Four pounds a year. A pound is twenty shillings, so four pounds is eighty shillings.*”

He did not look up.

“*And a keeper of a roll of doors in this city is paid four shillings a year, and has been for eleven years without being paid, and eleven years came to forty-four shillings. That is two pounds and four shillings.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it,*” said Ilyan.

“*So the seat pays eighty shillings in a year. That woman has been paid nothing for eleven years, and the work she did in the eleven came to forty-four shillings, and forty-four is in a book at the seat, and it is not in her hands.*”

He took his hands off the table.

“*That is the working and I have not done a division and I am not going to do one.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The other half is that it is a wage. Payable quarterly. It does not depend on a road and it does not depend on a number of doors, and I have wanted for nine days to say that a wage is not the interesting part of it. I have not said it. I am not going to say it now.*”

---

At the foot of the fifth sheet there was a name, and it was not written.

It had been cut into a die and pressed, and it stood up off the paper in the way a cut name does.

Bevin Tarr was at the end of the long table. He had a die in his coat that he had not brought out, and he took it out now and put it on the wood.

“*That is a die,*” he said, “*and it is not mine, and I am going to say the rest of it in one go, because I have been in this room four times in seven days saying one sentence at a time and it takes all day.*”

“*And the rest of it.*”

“*I cut four thousand lines and I am paid for two dies. The office has the two, and the two are different heads, and that is one of the two. I cut it in the eighty-first week at a bench in a room over a yard that is not in this city, and nobody in this city asked me to, and I am not going to say in a room with thirty people in it what the other one is.*”

He put his hand flat on the table.

“*And the name on that sheet is cut with the same head as the name at the foot of the sealed sheet of the seventh day of the eighty-first week, and the office has been cutting that name into brass for two years, and it paid me for that head once and not again.*”

“*Say the name,*” said Ilyan.

“*Iven Marrow. Regent of the Shelf.*”

---

The room said nothing for a moment, and then about nine people said something at once, and Skell let them finish, and then put one hand up flat, and the room stopped, because he had done that four times in six weeks and everybody in it knew what it meant.

“*Say what everybody said,*” he said, and it was fair, and about four people in the room said it again in lower voices.

“*He is the office,*” said Ilyan. “*The writ came down yesterday and it is his writ. The third line is his third line. The exception is his. The office has been correct about everything it has printed in this city for six weeks, and I have said out loud in four rooms that the difficulty is that it is correct, and the difficulty is still that it is correct.*”

“*Then say what the five lines are for,*” said Fenna Rusk.

---

Ilyan had been in this country one hundred and ninety-one mornings, and the fourth line of the fifth sheet was about a way out of it, and he had wanted a way out of this country every morning of the whole of it.

He said so. He said it in a room at the foot of a hill with about thirty people in it, at about the third hour, on the second day of the eighty-fifth week. He wanted it, and he said the wanting out loud in a room instead of saying it to a man in a corridor, and he had done that four times in six weeks, and it had not stopped him once.

He had resolved at about the second hour not to do it. He had resolved it on the way up the hill with his hand on the rail, and there is no form in this county for a resolution, and he has never once in this city kept one.

“*I want the fourth line,*” he said. “*I have wanted it for one hundred and ninety-one mornings. That is the whole of it. I am not going to stand in this room and pretend that I have been drafting remedies for six weeks and thinking about anything else.*”

“*Go on.*” said Skell.

“*The rest of it is that I have a price for it, and I am going to say the price before anything else happens, because the last time I had a want in this city I said the price on a step and it did not stop me. I have not got better at it in nine days.*”

“*Say the price.*”

“*The price is in the fourth line, in the office's own words. A person so passing is not to be asked about the city afterwards. And the fourth line says the keeper of the box is the keeper of that passage, and there is one keeper of that box, and she is at the back of this room with a slate under her arm, and she has not said a word in twenty minutes.*”

About thirty people turned round and looked at a woman of twenty-eight with a slate under her arm.

“*Say the whole of it,*” said Hessa Marl.

---

“*I will say the parts of it I can say,*” she said, “*and I am going to be a long time about it. I have been in this room twenty minutes and I have not said anything, and everybody in it has been waiting for me, and I would like to say it the way I do nine doors, which is one at a time.*”

She turned the slate over once and did not open it.

“*One. I was conveyed to the ninth marker on the seventh day of the eighty-second week at the fourth hour, and I came back at the last of the light.*”

“*Say the rest of that one and go on to the end of it.*”

“*And there is a line in a book four hundred miles off that says I came back the same. And the last word of that line is the office's and not mine, and nobody in this city has ever asked me about any part of it.*”

“*Say the second one.*”

“*Two. Nobody in this room has put to me the question of whether that crossing takes anybody out of this country. I have not asked it either. I did not ask it in the eighty-second week because I was in it at the fourth hour.*”

“*And the other half of that,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*And I have not asked it in six weeks because I do not know how to ask it in a room without about nine people hearing me ask it. And I have not been able to find a room.*”

“*Say the third one and then say what you will do,*” said Skell.

“*Three. The fourth line says I am to be the keeper of it. And I cannot say no. I have said that out loud in this room and in a room off the low ground, and a man with a pencil has it in a book. And there is no version of this in which I get to be asked whether I will.*”

“*Say what you will do.*”

“*One part of it, and it is the part I can do. The office keeps a book of the visits to that box, and the book has visits in it and no names. So I will enter the visits in the book.*”

She put the slate down on the table in front of her.

“*And a crossing is not a visit. And I have been counting crossings on this slate in my own hand since the fourth day of the eighty-fourth week. And there is no book in four hundred miles that a crossing goes in. That is the whole of what I have, and I am not going to say the rest of it in this room.*”

“*Why not,*” said Ilyan.

“*Because the rest of it is whether I will count a person going out. And I do not know the answer. I have had a want in this city for six weeks and I have never once been able to hold a want, and I am not going to find out in front of thirty people whether I can.*”

---

Fenna Rusk had her hand flat on the table since the fourth line was read and she had not moved it.

“*Read the rest of it out.*” she said, “*and then say whether he has answered.*”

“*I have not answered,*” Ilyan said. “*I have said what I want and I have said the price of it, and I am not going to answer before the second hour tomorrow, and I would like this room to have that from me now rather than at the second hour tomorrow.*”

“*Say why the second hour tomorrow,*” said Skell.

“*Because if I answer it in the next hour, I will answer it in a room where thirty people have just found out what I want. I will answer it as the man who read a fifth sheet out to them.*”

He put his hand flat on the table beside the sheet.

“*And in six weeks in this city I have watched four people answer questions about themselves in rooms. Every one of the four answers cost somebody else something. I have been the one who asked in three of the four.*”

“*Say the four,*” said Skell.

“*They are not yours to have,*” said Fenna Rusk.

And a man of the seat, who had been at the end of the long table for four days with a book, said one thing about the fifth sheet before anybody had asked him, and it was that he had entered it whole, and that a sheet of an office of a country that is not held is still a sheet, and that he had read all four of its lines and would not leave one of them out of his book.

---

And a man with a bad leg sat in a room over a saddler's at the middle of the nine bridges with the fifth sheet on the table in front of him, and he did not answer it that day, and the fourth of its five lines was a way out of this country with a woman who could not say no at the keeping of it.

The fever was twenty-two weeks and four days old and it did not stop for an exception. The crossing at the ninth marker had been a place of passage out of this country in writing since the day before, and about two hundred crossings a day went up the other side of it in the mud, and there was no name anywhere in this country for the side they went up.
