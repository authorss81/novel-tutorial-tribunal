# Chapter 402: The Man Who Keeps A Road And Is Not Allowed To Say It Is Fit

The fifth day of the seventy-ninth week was his hundred and fifty-second morning and the second day after the settlement, and the fever was seventeen weeks and no days old, and the rail was still across the Low Road at the weir head on the fifth day, and the man who kept that road had been in his hut at the crossing since the light and had a fire going in a hut that has no chimney, and he was fifty-eight and his name was Renn Callow and he had kept the Low Road for twenty-nine years and had not been asked in any of the twenty-nine to say anything about it.

The hut is four posts and a roof and a bench and a lamp and a barrow-wheel leaned against the north post, and there is a line of chalk on the bench, which is not a record, and eleven small flat things on the sill, which is not a tally either, and he is very firm about both of those things.

He came down the ledge with his leg bad and without hurrying about it, and the roadkeeper watched him do it and did not offer an arm, and the man with the bad ear was already at the hut, standing with his back to the water and his hat in his hands, having come down the row at the fourth hour and walked out four hundred yards of road to do it, and he was not asked to and he was not thanked and he had told the boy of sixteen on the way so that the boy could say it in a doorway.

---

“*You have come to ask me how many doors are on my road.*”

“*I have come to ask you what the road is for.*”

“*Then ask me the doors,*” said Renn Callow, “*because everybody who has ever come out here has wanted the doors, and you are the first one to say the other thing, and I would rather be asked the one I can answer than the one I cannot, and I have never been able to answer the doors and I have never been asked.*”

“*How many doors are on your road.*”

“*I do not know,*” said Renn Callow, “*and I have kept it twenty-nine years, and I will tell you what I know and what I do not know and you may put it where you like. I know where the road is. I know it from the ninth weir as far as the ninth marker and I know it past the ninth marker for two miles and no further, and I have not walked the two miles in four years and nobody has asked me to and I do not go on it alone. I know which side the winter is on. I know that a cart will go round the north side of the third marker if the water is under the sill of it and not if the water is over the sill, and I have never once written that down and I can say it in the dark. I know that eleven doors on this road are shut and I do not know how many of the shut ones have people in them.*”

“*That is not the same as knowing how many doors.*”

“*No,*” said Renn Callow. “*And I have known for eleven years that it is not the same, and I have never said so out loud to anybody, because there is nobody to say it to, and because a roadkeeper says what he sees and a man who has kept a road for twenty-nine years and cannot say how many doors are on it has not got anything anybody could do with.*”

---

He put the lamp down on the bench and put his hand flat on the chalk line, which is eleven chalk strokes, none of them a name and none of them a figure.

“*This is what a road is for,*” he said, “*and I have never had to say it to a person who wanted writing and I am going to say it now because you asked me for it and not for the doors, and I will say it the way I say everything, which is once, and I am not going to be asked to say it twice by anybody in this ward or above it.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*A road is four things and the four are in a different order in every month of the year and the order is the whole of what a road is.*”

He put one finger on the bench.

“*First: it is how a thing gets to a place. Not a person. A thing. A cart of water, a coffin, a woman in the third day of a fever going to a hall, a plate for a roll, a boy of eight in a barrow. That is first and it is first all year and no roadkeeper in the world has ever had it any other way.*”

Second finger.

“*Second: it is how a person gets away from a place. Not out of the country. Out of the ward. Nine hundred doors in this ward and a road at each end of the row, and the two of those are the same road and they are not the same thing, and the day they are the same thing is the day this ward has one door and I will not be its keeper.*”

Third finger.

“*Third: it is what a person stands on to be heard from. A man on a road is a man in a street and not in a room, and a street is a place where a thing gets said and not a place where a thing gets signed.*”

Fourth finger.

“*Fourth, and it is last because it is the one everybody gets wrong: it is the only thing here that nobody owns. Not the council, not the bench of four, not the man at the wheel, not me. I keep it. I do not hold it. There is a difference and it is the only difference I have got, and if you want it in a form, the form does not exist and it never has.*”

---

“*Then the form for the bar.*”

“*The form for the bar is a form for a road reported unsafe and a report is made by a person of the place and the line at the top of the form is for the name of that person.*” Renn Callow turned the lamp round so the wick was out of the wind. “*And the line is empty, and the rail is up, and I am going to tell you what has happened in four days in the order it happened, and I have not said any of it to anybody and I am saying it to you because you are a stranger with a leg and a stranger is the only person in this city who cannot be sent for.*”

“*Go on.*”

“*On the sixth morning the near side came out of the water and it was not a bank. Eleven of us saw it inside a quarter of an hour. I said so to the carter and the carter said so to a carter and a carter said so at a standpipe, and by the second hour of that day there were about forty people at the standpipe saying it, and by the fourth hour the boy of eleven had a mark on a slate and was going along the row with it, and he is eleven and he is the head of his own house and he was doing it because his mother is in that queue and not because anybody asked him.*”

“*And then.*”

“*And then a woman of about forty came in from the row with a sheet and she wanted to know how to fill in a line at the top of it, and she had it in her hand and she could not see it, and she asked me whether the line was for the person who said the road had gone or for the person who was standing nearest the hole when it went, and I told her it was for the person who said the road had gone, and she said that she did not know who that was, because there were forty of them and she was one and she would not put forty names in a line, and I said that a line is a line and a line takes one, and she said that it is not the same thing, and she was right and I have not been able to stop hearing it.*”

“*What was her name.*”

“*She did not give it and I did not ask and it is the only name in this week I have wanted and have not had,*” said Renn Callow. “*And the rail went up on the seventh morning at the light and a man came with a barrow of rubble and two other men and they cut the sockets and set it in, and nobody sent for anybody, and nobody paid them, and there was a form nailed to it and the line at the top of the form is empty and the rail is up.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*That is it,*” he said. “*That is the whole of what happened on this road in four days and I have been in this hut for all four of them. I did not put the rail up and I cannot take it down and both of those are because of the same line on the same sheet and neither of them is my doing.*”

---

“*Then say the road is fit.*”

The man with the bad ear put his head up. He had not said anything for a quarter of an hour, which is three times as long as he has ever gone in a room, and he came a mile and a half down the row for this and he was not going to hear the answer in a hut.

“*Say what.*”

“*I am going to ask the man who keeps this road to walk four hundred yards of it and say out loud that it is fit for a cart, and to say it to the woman of twenty-eight, and to say it in the public room at the foot of the hill, in front of anybody who comes, which is the way a bar comes down in this city.*”

“*And if he says no.*”

“*Then the rail stays up,*” Ilyan said, “*and I will have asked a person of a place to do the ordinary thing, and he will have said no in front of nine people, and it will be in a room, and that will be on me and not on him, and that is the whole of what I have come out here for and I said the price before I asked and I am not going to say it twice.*”

Renn Callow looked at him for a while.

“*No,*” he said.

“*Say why, and say it in your own mouth, and I will write it down on the back of a candle bill and I will not put my name on it and there is no form for it and nobody is going to read it out in a room.*”

“*You are not going to write it down,*” said Renn Callow, “*and I know that and I would still say it.*”

“*A roadkeeper says what he sees,*” he said. “*That is the whole of what I am and it is the only thing I have ever been. In twenty-nine years I have said to this road about nine hundred things and every one of them was something I saw with my own eyes and every one of them was said out loud and not written down, because a thing that is written down is a thing somebody can be asked about and the day I begin to be a thing somebody can be asked about I stop being a person who can be asked what the road is doing.*”

“*That is a method and not a reason.*”

“*It is both and I am not going to separate them for you,*” said Renn Callow. “*And the second half is this, and I have never said it and I am saying it once. If I say the road is fit, then I am the man who keeps the road and the man who says the road is fit, and there is nobody left to stand at this crossing and say I have not seen it, because the only person who can say that is me. A road with one keeper and one decider in the same body is not kept. It is held. And I have been keeping it for twenty-nine years and I would rather it were held badly by four hundred people than kept by one of me.*”

Nobody said anything at all, and the water moved under the weir head, and the eleven flat things on the sill did not move because they were not going to.

“*Say the other part.*”

“*There is no other part,*” said Renn Callow, “*and you are going to go away and be right in a room and I am going to be here tomorrow and the day after and the day after that, and there is nobody in this city who can be sent for me, and there is no form that says a person may be sent for the keeper of a road, and I have been here twenty-nine years and I have never wanted to be sent for and I am not going to start.*”

He picked the lamp up and turned it round again so the wick was out of the wind, which is what he does with a lamp.

And the man with the bad ear walked back up the four hundred yards with the man who had the bad leg, and neither of them said anything for the whole of it, and the man with the bad ear said one thing at the ledge, and it was not a question.

“*I have been at that crossing before,*” he said. “*I was at that crossing the day the water came the first time, in the first week, and I stood on that ledge and watched that water come over the sill of the third marker, and I did not know then that a roadkeeper was a person and I have known since and I have not come back, and I came back this morning and I am going to say that out loud to a room, because a man who has come to a place four hundred yards and there is nobody in this ward who has come and that is about a ward and not about me.*”

Nobody thanked him and he did not wait to be thanked, and the four bells went over Tallowgate at the change, and the rail was still up, and the fever was seventeen weeks and no days old, and it did not stop for a roadkeeper.
