# Chapter 450: What The Road Looks Like In The Rain, With Four Hurdles Still Up, A Slate In A Box, A Roll Under A Stone Two Miles Off, And Two People Who Cannot Take Each Other Off A Line

Fourth day of the eighty-sixth week. Shelf day three hundred and twenty-five, his two hundredth morning, fifty days after the settlement, the fever twenty-three weeks and six days, the rail down forty days.

He walked a mile and a half in the rain at the second hour on a Friday, and there was a bar of four hurdles of sawn ash across the north side of the ninth marker at knee height, with nine links of new chain between the two outer ones. There was a plate wired to the post above them, with two nail holes in it and one of them bent, and nothing cut in the bottom corner of it, and a name at the head of the plate and no name at the foot of it.

The bar was up because there was nobody left who was able to take it down, and that had been the position of that road since the night the instrument was lodged, and it was going to be the position of that road for as long as it took a council four hundred miles off to sit.

Hessa Marl was in the box on the north side with the shutter up against the wind off the flat, and she had a slate under her arm and a lamp on the bench beside her that she was not using.

“*Say the count and say how many days you have been counting,*” Ilyan said, “*and say it standing up. There is a woman in this city who cannot see that slate, and she is a mile and a half off at this hour with a lamp in her hand, and I have known for three weeks that a person can say a thing out loud for somebody else, and I have not done it until now.*”

She turned the slate round.

“*Fifteen days, from the fourth day of the eighty-fourth week to this morning.*”

“*Say the figure and then say the two days before it.*”

“*Three thousand. And it was two hundred and six yesterday, and a hundred and ninety-nine on the day before that, and a hundred and ninety-nine is not two hundred, and I have had a bad morning of it, and I have gone back and got it twice.*”

“*Say why you have got it twice and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Fenna Rusk, who had come out along the north side at about the fourth hour with a lamp in her hand.

“*Because a number I have said once out loud is a number I have said in a room, and a number I have said in a room is a number somebody can ask me about, and there is nobody in this city that I would rather do that to than to.*”

---

Skell was not there.

Skell had come out to the end of the plate yard at about an hour before the light on most mornings since the day the writ came down in the tray, and had not come on this one. A man who has gone to a room at the foot of a hill is not a man who comes out to a road.

So it was Ilyan who did the working, standing on a ledge with the water going under it, and he had a bad leg, and he had done a great many divisions in six weeks, and he got it right the first time and said so out loud.

“*Fifteen days at two hundred a day is three thousand,*” he said. “*A farthing is a quarter of a penny, and three thousand quarters of a penny is seven hundred and fifty pence.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not do it twice,*” said Renn Calloway from the north side, where he had a lamp and eleven chalk strokes and no figures, as he has had for twenty-nine years.

“*Seven hundred and fifty pence is sixty-two shillings and sixpence. That is five pounds two shillings and sixpence.*”

He put his hand flat on the concrete drum of the marker.

“*And the second seat at the council of the Shelf is paid four pounds a year, and it is not held, and a woman of twenty-eight has made five pounds two shillings and sixpence in fifteen days in her own hand on a slate.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it,*” said Calloway.

“*And the office keeps a book of the visits to that box, and the book has nine visits in it in fifteen days, because nine people have gone into it and sat down, and a visit is a farthing at the first row, and nine of them is two pence and a quarter of a penny. And a crossing is not a visit, and there is no column in that book for two hundred and six.*”

---

“*Again, and all of it.*” said Calloway, “*and go on to the end of it.*”

“*There is no book in four hundred miles that a crossing goes into, and there is no form that turns a crossing into a visit. The office pays at the first row, and the first row is a farthing a visit, and she is not being paid anything at all.*”

He took his hand off the drum.

“*And the five pounds two shillings and sixpence is a figure on a slate in the rain, and it is hers, and it is not a claim, and there is nobody it is owed to.*”

Fenna Rusk had not put her lamp down. She had carried it a mile and a half and she had not put it down once, and she stopped at the fourth hurdle and put her hand on the top rail of it.

“*Say the whole of it out loud,*” she said. “*There are about nine people on this road, and one of them cannot see the slate, and one of them has just done a division in the rain. This is the fiftieth day, and I would like the last thing said on this road in this week to be a figure and not an opinion.*”

He said it. He said the number, the days, the rate, the division, the five pounds two shillings and sixpence, and the nine visits, and the difference. About nine people stood on two sides of a road in the rain and did not make a sound.

Then she said the other half, and she said it to him and not to the road.

“*Now say the other figure, and say what I have done with it,*” she said. “*And I am not asking for it. I have not asked for it in six weeks, and I have asked for a division of everything else in this city in front of about thirty people at least nine times.*”

“*I have not sent it,*” Ilyan said.

“*Say where it is.*”

“*In the inside pocket of this coat,*” he said, “*and it has been there since the sixth day of the eighty-fourth week.*”

“*Say what it is and do not leave it in the middle.*”

“*It is a return on the office's own form, with my name at the head of it, and four lines in the margin, and a figure in the fourth. It is dated the morning I wrote it. And it is the only number in this city that the office has not got.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*The other half is that I told thirty people in a room that there was a number, and then I took it out of the room, and I have been in this city two hundred mornings, and it has been in my coat for twelve days, and I have not sent it and I have not said it and I have not torn it up.*”

He looked down the south side of the road.

“*And there is a form in the window of the press with my name at the foot of the fifth page and a woman's name under mine. And there is a declaration on a long table at the foot of that hill, a mile and a half off, that governs everybody in a register whether they are alive or not.*”

“*Say the last of that and say the whole of it,*” said Calloway.

“*And the number is not on the form, and it is not on the declaration, and it is not in the office's book of the visits, and it is the only figure in this city that is in no book at all except the inside pocket of a coat.*”

---

They stood on the south side, in the mud, at about the fifth hour, because the south side was the side they stood on.

It was the same barrow, the same two feet of mud in the wheels, and the same man, and he came on up past the two of them the way he came up past four hurdles that had not been in his road for six weeks.

He had gone up four yards of mud every morning for nine years, and for the forty days before the rail came down, and on the night of the sixth day of the eighty-fifth week he had gone past nine men with a bar of ash and a written authority while about forty people and two carts came up behind him in the dark.

He did not know that anybody had ever written any of it down, and nobody in this city had ever asked him, and he had not been in a room in this city since the rail came down, and there is no form in this county that says a person going to work in the morning has to be in a book.

“*Say his name,*” said Ilyan.

“*I do not have it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*Then say the half of that which is the other half.*”

“*That is the whole of the position of this crossing on the north side as well. There is a plate with a name at the head of it and no name at the foot of it, and a box with one line in it and a woman in it, and a bar of four hurdles with nothing behind them, and about two hundred crossings a day going up four yards of mud on a side that has no name anywhere at all.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop in the middle of a sentence,*” said Renn Calloway, from the north side, without his lamp, which was in his hand behind him where he had not been able to get it up.

“*And every one of those is the office's, and the office is not held, and the road is kept.*"

“*Say the thing that is on the line,*” Ilyan said. “*Say it out loud on a road and not in a room, because you told me in a room in the eighty-fourth week that you would not say one word about whether I was safe, and I have not asked you for it since.*”

“*Say the rest of that and go on, and do not stop in the middle of it,*” said Fenna Rusk.

“*It is a line in a book four hundred miles off, and neither of us can take the other off it, and it is going to be there after both of us.*”

“*And what is on the line,*” he said.

“*Your name beside mine, in a column with a head on it,*” she said. “*And the office wrote down that I said the price out loud before you put it there, because a register ought to hold that kind of thing.*”

“*Say the rest of it and say it whole,*” said Ilyan.

“*And it is in a book at the seat, and about eight people were in the mud on the second day of the eighty-third week, and I could not tell you which eight, and neither could you, and I have not asked in fifty days, and you have not asked me either.*”

“*Say the last of that,*” said Calloway.

“*And neither of us can take the other off the line. And I have not asked you for anything since the eighty-fourth week, and you have not given me anything.*”

“*Say the rest of that and do not stop half way through it,*” said Ilyan.

“*And there is no arrangement in this country by which one of us pays the other back, and I am not going to look for one in the rain, and you are not going to offer me one, and if you do offer me one I am going to say no, and you know that before you open your mouth.*”

“*Then say the other thing out loud, and do not stop at the first thing,*” she said. “*It is fifty days, and there are about nine people on this road, and I have a roll in a room two miles off that nobody has asked me about once.*”

“*Go on,*” Ilyan said.

“*It is eleven pages,*” said Fenna Rusk. “*It came down a hill on a cart on the sixth day of the eighty-third week with a knot on it that was not the knot it went up with, and a fold in the eleventh page that I did not make, and it has been under a weight of stone in a room behind a chandler's shop for nineteen days.*”

“*The rest of that, and do not stop half way through it,*” said Calloway.

“*And I have not been able to say whether it is right once in nineteen days. The pages are in order. I said the pages were in order in a room on the sixth day of that week and I have been able to say it nineteen times since. And the figures on the eleventh page are under a fold I did not put there, and I cannot see it, and there is nobody in this city who can see it and tell me, and there is no form in this county that gets a person four hundred miles to look at a page.*”

“*Say the half of that which is the other half,*” said Ilyan.

“*The other half is that the office has it too, in a register, folded across, and that the office does not check a figure. There are two copies of eleven pages in four hundred miles and one of them is wrong, and I made the one that is right, and I cannot prove it, and that is the whole of me.*”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds, and about nine people went up the four yards of mud past the two of them.

“*I know it,*” he said. “*And I am not going to offer you one, and I have stopped trying to, and I want that said out loud on this road at the fifth hour of a Friday morning by both of us. In fifty days I have said the price of five things out loud in rooms, and the price of the sixth one is that I cannot pay back the fifth, and I have stopped saying it, and I would like somebody to hear me stop.*”

The rain came off the flat.

The four hurdles and the nine links and the plate with nothing cut in the corner of it stood on the north side of the ninth marker, and a box with a shutter and a bench stood three feet from the fourth hurdle with a slate in it, and a man who had gone to a room at the foot of a hill had not come out to the end of the plate yard.

About two hundred crossings a day went up four yards of mud on a side of that road that had no name in any office document in the world, and two people who could not take each other off a line in a book four hundred miles off stood in it in the rain and went home up it separately, and the fever was twenty-three weeks and six days old.

It had not stopped for a writ, or an exception, or a refusal, or a form with two lines in it, or for a hundred and forty people, and it was not going to stop now.
