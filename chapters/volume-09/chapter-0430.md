# Chapter 430: Six Clauses, All Of Them Correct, And A Boy In A Doorway Who Is Not On A List And Is Not Off A List

Fifth day of the eighty-third week. Shelf day three hundred and five, his hundred and eightieth morning, thirty days after the settlement. The fever was twenty-one weeks old. The rail had been down twenty days, and a sheet came down the hill in the tray at the second hour with wax on it.

Ferrow read it out on his feet in the long room at the foot of the hill, in about thirty people's hearing, and it was the eighth thing he had read out of that press in fourteen days, and he had not apologised for one of them and said so before he started.

“*One. A dispute concerning the passage over the Low Road at the ninth marker has been given to the person appointed in the register of disputes concerning roads, and the person appointed is of the seat, and a dispute so given is a dispute given and is not a dispute that is lost.*”

“*And now say the rest of it,*” said Skell.

“*Two. A bar upon the Low Road at the ninth marker is put up, and the persons and the premises on the north side of the crossing have been given notice, and the bar is to remain up.*”

Nobody said anything. The sheet was lying on the long table with the wax still on it and the room could smell the wax.

“*Three. The office has received from this city a return of persons and things and has entered it in one column, and does not pay for the distinction between a person and a thing, and does not require it.*”

Hessa Marl was at the end of the long table with her slate under her arm, and had not been asked, and had come for nothing.

“*Four. The office requires from any person of this place a count of the persons of that place, by ward, and of the doors by street, for the keeping of a place under the fifth row of the schedule of the first day of the eighty-first week, and the office does not provide the number and does not check it, and the office notes the sums due to the persons named in returns of this city and does not press for them.*”

The room sat with that for about four seconds longer than it had sat with the rest of it. About nine of the thirty people in the room had been in that room when the first of these things was read out.

“*And the fifth clause,*” said Ferrow, “*and I am going to read the fifth clause myself, in the same voice, and I would like the room to know that I read it in the same voice.*”

“*Five. A person of the place who is not of a ward in which the road runs is not a person of the place, and a person who is not of this city cannot be entered in a register of this city and cannot be struck out of one.*”

“*Again,*” said Ilyan.

Ferrow read it again in the same voice, and about thirty people heard the second reading of it, and nobody in that room remarked on the asking.

“*And six,*” he said.

“*Say the sixth.*”

“*Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road.*”

He put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the long table, and squared it again, which is what a clerk does. There was a bill crooked on the wall behind them with a line of names on it and a blank line in the middle of the bill, and the blank line is for three hundred names, and the blank is the correct figure, and the office has now said in its own words, on a sheet with wax on it, that it notes the sums due to the persons named in returns of this city and does not press for them.

The office has been true about everything it has printed in this city. Neither of those is the same thing, and both of them are true.

“*Say the finding of it in your own mouth,*” said Ilyan, “*because there are about thirty people in this room and they are all going out of that door in a quarter of an hour and taking it with them, and I have been in this city twenty-three days asking rooms for second halves, and I have not had one that was the whole of it.*”

“*I am not going to give you one,*” said Ferrow. “*I am twenty-nine and I copy them fair and I have been on that floor three years and I have read eight things out of it in fourteen days, and I am not going to be the man who says what the office means. But I will say what the office has done, because it is in front of me and I have read it out twice and anybody can check me on every word of it.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*It has answered everything.*” He put one finger on the sheet. “*In fourteen days this office has read a magistrate's objection and answered it in four lines. It has read a count that a person of this place could not give and has written that it does not provide the number and does not check it.*”

“*It has read a return with one column in it and has written that it does not pay for the distinction. It has read a road with a bar on it at the ninth marker and has written that the dispute is not a dispute that is lost.*”

He stopped, and then went on, because it was the last of it.

“*And it has read four pounds and four shillings that is owed to a woman in this city and has written that it notes the sum and does not press for it. Every one of those lines is correct, and not one of them is wrong, and there is no line in any of them that says anything about a road.*”

Bevin Tarr said *huh* out loud at the end of the table, the second time in four days, and did not apologise for it, and still had a plate under his arm.

“*And the other half of that,*” said Skell.

“*The other half is that it is all correct, and I have stood in a room in this city every day for fourteen days waiting for one of those six lines to be wrong, and it has not been wrong once.*” He looked at the floor. “*I am not going to pretend to this room that I have been waiting for it.*” He looked at the floor. “*I wrote it down two weeks ago in a book with a pencil, and a man of the seat read it out in this room on the first day of this month and meant it.*”

“*Where is he?*” said someone at the back.

“*Four hundred miles up a road on his own two feet at this moment, and he will not be in this room again.*” He squared the sheet. “*The office has heard him, and has not changed its mind by one line, and there is nothing in this sheet that was written for him and there is nothing in it that was not.*”

Tarin Keel was not in that room and had not been in it for eleven days, and he had walked past a plate and four hurdles two miles out on his way north, and nobody knew that and nobody said so, and there is no form in this city that says a person may be told where a man who is walking is.

---

“*Then say the last of it, and say it in the road and not in a room,*” Ilyan said.

“*There is one person in this city that the fifth clause does not reach, and I have been looking for him for two days. I am going to say his age and not his name, and everybody in this room knows who I mean, and about forty of them know his name, and not one of them is going to say it, including me.*”

Sef was in the doorway of the room, with his hands behind him, doing what he had done at a door at that hour since the eighth morning of a fever. The man with the bad leg did not look at him and did not raise his voice and said the whole of the rest of it to about thirty people and not to one of them.

“*A person of this place who is not of a ward in which the road runs is not a person of the place. That is a true sentence, it is on a sheet with wax on it, and I have had it read out twice this morning and I have not been able to argue with it.*”

He let the room sit with it.

“*And the road runs in the low row, and he is of the low row, so that clause does not touch him. And he is under age, so there is no line at the top of a form for his name. And he was asked on the second day of this month whether he wished to be struck out of a register, and he said no, and a man with a pencil wrote it down.*”

“*Go on,*” said Skell, quietly.

“*The form for being struck out is for a person who does not wish to remain entered. A person who has been asked and said no is a person who does not wish to remain entered, and so he can be struck out, and there is a form for it at the seat, and the striking out is done by the office and not by the person.*”

Nobody in the room was looking at the doorway.

“*And no form anywhere takes a person who was asked and said no out of a book without striking him out, and the striking out would put him in a book, and so he is not on a list and he is not off a list. And a man of the seat said that sentence in this room on the second day of this week about a person who does not apply, and I have been carrying it since, and it is the truest thing anybody has printed in this room this month, and it took nine days to come round to the person it was about.*”

Ferrow had the pencil out and did not look down at it and did not write anything, because there was nothing in front of him.

“*And I have one thing to say about it, and I have been holding it since the second hour,*” said Renn Calloway from the wall, where he had been standing since the second hour, because nothing that has been printed says a person may be sent for, and so nobody sent for him and he was not told about anything and he came.

“*Say it,*” said Ilyan.

“*I have kept the Low Road twenty-nine years and I cannot tell you how many doors are on it, and I have said so three times this week and it is in a book four hundred miles off twice. And there is a roll in that book too, that went up the hill yesterday on a cart with the return, and the woman who kept it for eleven years for four shillings a year has not been paid one hour of it, and the office has written in its own words that it notes the sum and does not press for it.*”

He shifted his weight off the bad leg.

“*And the four hurdles are still on the north side of the ninth marker and there is nothing at all on the south side of it, and a barrow goes up that side every morning, and a person went past it on foot on the seventh day of this week and came back up it. And there are two names on a line in a book in a city four hundred miles off, and neither of them can take the other one off, and I am not one of them and I did not put myself there and I would like that noticed.*”

“*And what does it mean?*” said Bevin Tarr.

“*I am not going to say what it means,*” said Calloway. “*I said in a hut on the fifth day of the seventy-ninth week that a roadkeeper says what he sees, and I have seen a bar, and I have seen a road, and I have seen a woman walk past one and come back, and I have not seen anybody in this city say no to a thing and keep their place in it.*”

He got himself off the wall.

“*There is no form for that and there is not going to be one, and nobody has relieved me and nobody has thanked me and nobody is going to, and the water is coming up under the ninth weir and I would like to go and look at it.*”

He went, and the four bells went over Tallowgate.

The bill on the wall was crooked with a blank line in the middle of it, and the blank is for three hundred names. The people who are dead in this city are on none of the six clauses, and there is no line for them and there is not going to be one.

A man of twenty-nine is at the end of a long table with a book and a pencil, and has read eight things out of a press in a fortnight. A woman of twenty-eight went out of the room and did nine doors and said nothing at one of them. A woman of forty-four is in a room with a lamp on and a bare table. A boy of sixteen is in a doorway and is not on a list and is not off a list.

The fever was twenty-one weeks old, and it did not stop for a sheet, and not one of the thirty of them was thanked.

---

Ilyan got out of the room with the leg and stood in the corridor with his back against the wall until the footsteps had gone down the stairs.

There was a word for what he had done on Wednesday, and nobody had given it to him. Darrin Skell had said it out loud in a room on a day of mud and had not been a co-witness himself, and Fenna Rusk had said she had never heard the word and had asked what it was for before she would have anything to do with it, and he had told her: for the road. That was all. It was a piece of work and not a rank. It was not a step up to anything, there was no form for it, and there was not going to be one.

He had said the price of it before he did it, in his own mouth, to a person who had not asked, with a man in the mud who could write it down, and the price had not stopped him, and in the whole nineteen years of testing other people's rules he could not remember the last time naming a thing had not stopped him.

The price was going to be paid by the woman with the lamp, and it would go on paying itself after he was out of this city, and out of the country, and after there was nobody left who remembered that a man with a bad leg had once stood in four inches of mud on the south side of a road two miles from a city of ninety thousand people and said so.

He was not going to be relieved about that. There was nobody he could name who would stop it, and he had said that out loud in the same breath, and neither of the two people standing in that mud had said it back to him, and he had not asked.

Down the corridor the fire door was open on the stair, and the light off the flat came in and made a long grey shape on the flags, and Ilyan picked up his tea, which had gone cold hours ago, and drank it anyway, because it was cold and bitter and it was the only thing in the building that had not changed, and he went down to the yard at the low end of Tallowgate to see whether a cart had gone east.
