# Chapter 430: A Sheet With Six Clauses On It In The Office's Own Words, And A Boy Of Sixteen Who Is Not On A List And Is Not Off A List

The fifth day of the eighty-third week was his hundred and eightieth morning and the thirtieth day after the settlement, and the fever was twenty-one weeks old, and the rail had been down twenty days, and a sheet came down the hill in the tray at the second hour with wax on it, and the clerk of forty read it out on his feet in the long room at the foot of the hill in about thirty people's hearing, and it was the eighth thing he had read out in fourteen days and he had not apologised for one of them and he said so.

“*One. A dispute concerning the passage over the Low Road at the ninth marker has been given to the person appointed in the register of disputes concerning roads, and the person appointed is of the seat, and a dispute so given is a dispute given and is not a dispute that is lost.*”

“*And now say the rest of it,*” said the man of fifty-two.

“*Two. A bar upon the Low Road at the ninth marker is put up, and the persons and the premises on the north side of the crossing have been given notice, and the bar is to remain up.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Three. The office has received from this city a return of persons and things and has entered it in one column, and does not pay for the distinction between a person and a thing, and does not require it.*”

The woman of twenty-eight was at the end of the long table with her slate under her arm and had not been asked and had come for nothing.

“*Four. The office requires from any person of this place a count of the persons of that place, by ward, and of the doors by street, for the keeping of a place under the fifth row of the schedule of the first day of the eighty-first week, and the office does not provide the number and does not check it, and the office notes the sums due to the persons named in returns of this city and does not press for them.*”

Nobody said anything, and the room sat with that for about four seconds longer than it had sat with the rest of it, and about thirty people who had come up that hill had all four clauses in their ears and about nine of them had been in this room when the first of them was read out.

“*And the fifth clause,*” said the clerk of forty, “*and I am going to read the fifth clause myself and I am going to read it in the same voice and I would like the room to know that I read it in the same voice.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*Five. A person of the place who is not of a ward in which the road runs is not a person of the place, and a person who is not of this city cannot be entered in a register of this city and cannot be struck out of one.*”

The man of fifty-two asked him to say the fifth clause again, and the clerk of forty read it again in the same voice, and about thirty people heard the second reading of it and nobody in that room remarked on the asking.

“*And six.*”

“*Say the sixth.*”

“*Nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road.*”

The clerk of forty put the sheet down and squared it to the edge of the long table and squared it again, which is what a clerk does, and it was thirty-one days ago that a clerk of thirty read a name off the foot of a sheet with a seal on it and the name did not answer and was not asked anything, and there is a bill crooked on the wall behind them with a blank line in the middle of it, and the blank line is the correct figure, and the office has now said in its own words on a sheet with wax on it that it notes the sums due to the persons named in returns of this city and does not press for them, and the office has been true about everything it has printed in this city, and neither of those is the same thing, and both of them are true.

“*Say the finding of it in your own mouth,*” said the man with the bad ear, “*because there are about thirty people in this room and they are all going to go out of that door in a quarter of an hour and take it with them, and I have been in this city twenty-three days and I have been asking rooms for second halves since the first day of this month and I have not had one yet that was the whole of it.*”

“*I am not going to give you one,*” said the clerk of forty. “*I am twenty-nine and I copy them fair and I have been on that floor three years and I have read eight things out of it in fourteen days and I am not going to be the man who says what the office means. But I will say what the office has done, because it is in front of me and I have read it out twice and I can be checked on every word of it.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*It has answered everything. In fourteen days this office has read a magistrate's objection and answered it in four lines, and it has read a count that a person of this place could not give and has written that it does not provide the number and does not check it, and it has read a return with one column in it and has written that it does not pay for the distinction, and it has read a road with a bar on it at the ninth marker and has written that the dispute is not a dispute that is lost, and it has read four pounds and four shillings that is owed to a woman of forty-four in this city and has written that it notes the sum and does not press for it, and every one of those six lines is correct, and not one of them is wrong, and there is no line in any of them that says anything about a road.*”

Bevin Tarr at the end of the long table said *huh* out loud, which is the second time in four days, and he did not apologise for it, and he had a plate under his arm because he could not leave a plate.

“*And the other half of that,*” said the man of fifty-two.

“*The second half is that it is all correct and I have been in a room in this city every day for fourteen days waiting for one of these six lines to be wrong, and it has not been wrong once, and I am not going to pretend to this room that I have been waiting for it. I wrote it down two weeks ago in a book with a pencil and a man of the seat read it out in this room on the first day of this month and meant it, and he is four hundred miles up a road on his own two feet at this moment and he will not be in this room again, and the office has heard him and has not changed its mind by one line, and there is nothing in this sheet that was written for him and there is nothing in it that was not.*”

Tarin Keel was not in that room and had not been in it for eleven days and had walked past a plate and four hurdles two miles out on his way north, and nobody knew that and nobody said so, and there is no form in this city that says a person may be told where a man who is walking is.

“*Then say the last of it and say it in the road and not in a room,*” he said, “*because there is one person in this city that the fifth clause does not reach and I have been looking for him for two days and I am going to say his age and not his name, and everybody in this room knows who I mean and about forty of them know his name and not one of them is going to say it, including me.*”

Nobody said anything, and the boy of sixteen was in the doorway of the room, and he had been at a door at that hour since the eighth morning of a fever, and there is no form in this city that says a person may be at a door instead of in it, and the man with the bad leg did not look at him and did not raise his voice and said the whole of the rest of it to about thirty people and not to one of them.

“*A person of this place who is not of a ward in which the road runs is not a person of the place,*” said the man with the bad leg. “*And that is a true sentence and it is on a sheet with wax on it and I have had it read out twice this morning and I have not been able to argue with it. And the road runs in the low row and he is of the low row, so that clause does not touch him, and he is under age, so there is no line at the top of a form for his name, and he was asked on the second day of this month whether he wished to be struck out of a register and he said no and a man with a pencil wrote it down, and the form for being struck out is for a person who does not wish to remain entered, and a person who has said no is a person who does not wish to remain entered, and so he can be struck out, and there is a form for it at the seat and the striking out is done by the office and not by the person. And there is no form in four hundred miles that takes a person who was asked and said no out of a book without striking him out, and the striking out would put him in a book, and so he is not on a list and he is not off a list, and a man of the seat said that sentence in this room on the second day of this week about a person who does not apply, and I have been carrying it since, and it is the truest thing anybody has printed in this room this month and it took nine days to come round to the person it was about.*”

About thirty people in a room at the foot of a hill did not look round at him, and there is no form in this city that says a person must be looked at, and the clerk of forty had the pencil out and did not look down at it and did not write anything, because there was nothing in front of him, and there was a boy of sixteen in the doorway of that room and nobody in it had said his name.

“*And I have one thing to say about it and I have been holding it since the second hour,*” said the man of fifty-eight, from the wall, where he had been standing since the second hour because there is no form in four hundred miles that says a person may be sent for and so nobody sent for him and he was not told about anything and came.

“*Say it.*”

“*I have kept the Low Road twenty-nine years and I cannot tell you how many doors are on it and I have said so three times this week and it is in a book in a city four hundred miles off twice. And there is a roll in that book too, that went up the hill on a cart on the fourth day of this week with the return, and the woman who kept it for eleven years for four shillings a year has not been paid one hour of it and the office has written in its own words on a sheet with wax on it that it notes the sum and does not press for it. And the four hurdles are still on the north side of the ninth marker and there is nothing at all on the south side of it, and a barrow goes up that side every morning, and a person went past it on foot on the seventh day of this week and came back up it, and there are two names on a line in a book in a city four hundred miles off and one of them is mine and neither of them can take the other one off.*”

Nobody said anything.

“*And that is the whole of it from a man who keeps a road,*” said the man of fifty-eight, “*and I am not going to say what it means, because I said in a hut on the fifth day of the seventy-ninth week that a roadkeeper says what he sees, and I have seen a bar, and I have seen a road, and I have seen a woman walk past one and come back, and I have not seen anybody in this city say no to a thing and keep their place in it, and there is no form for that and there is not going to be one, and nobody has relieved me and nobody has thanked me and nobody is going to, and the water is coming up under the ninth weir and I would like to go and look at it.*”

He went, and the four bells went over Tallowgate, and the bill on the wall was crooked with a blank line in the middle of it, and the people who are dead in this city are not on any of the six clauses and there is no line for them and there is not going to be one, and a man of twenty-nine is at the end of a long table with a book and a pencil and has eight things read out of a press in a fortnight and is not thanked, and a woman of twenty-eight went out of the room and did nine doors and did not say anything at one of them, and a woman of forty-four is in a room with a lamp on and a bare table, and a boy of sixteen is in a doorway and is not on a list and is not off a list, and the fever was twenty-one weeks old, and it did not stop for a sheet, and not one of the thirty of them was thanked.

*And there is still a name for what he has been doing for four days, and nobody gave it to him and nobody in this city is going to use it about him. A Co-Witness is a person whose name is at the top of a line beside the name of a person of a place, in respect of a road, and it is a piece of work and not a rank, and it is not a step up to anything, and there is no form for it and there is not going to be one. He put his own name on a line in four inches of mud on the south side of a road two miles out from a city of ninety thousand people, and he said the price of it out loud before he did it and in his own mouth and to a person who had not asked and with a man who could act standing in the mud with them, and the price was that a person of this place would be asked about him for as long as there is a road and a form and an office, and the price did not stop him, and that is the first time in the history of this instrument that naming it has not stopped him, and a woman of forty-four in a room with a lamp on and nothing under the table is the one it will be done to, and she said in her own mouth that she cannot tell anybody whether a roll is right any more, and neither of them has thanked the other and neither of them is going to be relieved, and the people who are not in the room are counted in the answer anyway.*
