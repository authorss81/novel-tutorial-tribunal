# Chapter 10: The Stay

The preliminary hearing opened at the hour after sunset, when the court’s black lamps had been lit along the ninth bridge and the public road had been divided into three waiting lines: the Ro household, the bridgekeeper’s guild, and the eastern district. The clerk’s translator rendered each answer into English before it reached Ilyan; the local voices remained audible beneath the formal words.

Ilyan stood inside the court safety perimeter with Sera on his left and Tarin behind him. The boundary was marked by pale stones set into the paving. Beyond it, the repaired bridge waited under its white safety order. The warning bell hung motionless above the western approach. The court’s current plate stood on the public table. The old saltglass plate remained sealed in the court locker, visible through a small barred window.

Harrow stood at the center of the field with the recorder. Oren and three guild workers occupied the maintenance line, their tools laid out in the order of the test. Neris and Tamsin sat near the lower edge on two low benches. Tamsin’s ankle rested on a folded blanket. Her mother’s account pouch hung at Neris’s belt, closed.

The crowd made no sound when Harrow raised the court tablet. The field tightened around the bridge.

The System translated the immediate notice into Ilyan’s sight.

**PRELIMINARY HEARING: THE CURRENT CHARTER, THE MAINTENANCE CONDITION, AND THE COPIED PRECEDENT.**
**REMEDY 1: AMEND THE CHARTER. BURDEN: the assembly must maintain the changed condition and name a new review route.**
**REMEDY 2: BREAK THE SPAN. BURDEN: the affected districts must provide another crossing and maintain it without the ninth bridge.**
**REMEDY 3: HOLD THE HEARING OPEN. BURDEN: the bridge and the districts carry the present risk until the court issues a ruling.**
**NO REMEDY IS A FINAL SETTLEMENT. EACH PARTY MAY WITHDRAW OR LIMIT ITS TESTIMONY.**

The lines beneath the three choices were not complete. They named where the burden might fall, not who would actually carry it. Ilyan felt the familiar urge to turn the incomplete form into a solution.

He did not.

Sera stepped forward. “Sera Quill, provisional advocate, limited to the bridge charter, maintenance record, and disclosure. I sponsor the filing of Ilyan Vester’s challenge and the Ro household’s limited request for a hearing. I do not speak for any witness who refuses to answer.”

Harrow’s eyes moved to her. “Your suspension ended with a notation, not a judgment.”

“My representation is limited until the court decides otherwise.”

“Recorded.”

Neris stood. “The household is present. We have not offered the flood account.”

“Recorded as disputed,” Harrow said.

Maren Kett stood from the eastern district line. “The district invokes the petition as precedent and objects to an unlisted household making a claim over a shared route.”

“Recorded,” Harrow said.

Oren lifted a piece of dark stone from his tool tray. “The bridgekeeper’s guild offers the bridge as a witness. We will show the load marks, the brace, and the repair sequence. We do not accept a ruling that makes the guild liable for every crossing not named in the charter.”

“Recorded,” Harrow said. “The guild has named the limit of its offer.”

The court’s formal field opened with a low vibration. Ilyan felt it in the soles of his boots, then in the cracked pen in his pocket. The bridge’s shadow moved across the paving, although the lamps had not changed.

Harrow called the first witness.

“Tamsin Ro.”

Tamsin stood. She kept one hand on Neris’s sleeve and looked at the bridge rather than the recorder.

“What do you offer?” Harrow asked.

“The bell rang before the bridge moved.”

The System translated only the words she had spoken. No flood account appeared. No description of the household entered the field.

“Did you see the bell?” Harrow asked.

“Yes.”

“Did you see the span move before the bell?”

“No.”

“Did you know whether the rope was a warning or an instruction?”

“No.”

“Will you say more?”

Tamsin looked at Neris. Her mother gave her a small nod.

“No,” Tamsin said. “That is the fact I chose.”

Harrow made a mark on the tablet. “The witness has limited her testimony.”

Ilyan’s first impulse was to ask what the bell had looked like, how many times it had rung, and whether the sound had changed under the crowd. Those questions might have strengthened the case. They would also have turned Tamsin’s choice into a sequence of useful inputs.

He kept his mouth closed.

“Neris Ro.”

Neris stood without touching the account pouch at her belt. “I offer three facts. My daughter’s name was removed from the lawful-traveler register after a local petition. The dye-yard where I work is across the ninth bridge. The household has a room on the lower road, but the landlord requires a recognized crossing record before renewing it.”

The words entered the field as a formal list. Ilyan saw the place where the flood account would have appeared if he had filed it again. It remained absent.

“Will you offer the earlier flood record?” Harrow asked.

“No.”

“Will you allow the court to use it as context?”

“No.”

Harrow looked at Ilyan. The question was not an invitation to object. It was a test of whether the claimant had learned the difference between evidence and permission.

“Recorded,” Harrow said. “The household has refused the account.”

Oren was called next.

He laid three iron rings on the public table. The first held a bright pin. The second was empty. The third had been filed down until the mark was almost gone.

“This is the ninth bridge’s load sequence,” he said. “The support rests on the count, but the count does not tell the whole truth. The bridge changes when the maintenance work is named and performed. The public charter has lost the review that once followed the return of ordinary load.”

“Who removed it?” Ilyan asked.

Harrow’s gaze cut toward him. “The witness answers the question put to her.”

Oren looked at Ilyan. “I do not know who removed the words. I know the workroom used them. I know the current copy does not contain them. I know the petition outside repeats a fragment of the old language and changes its burden.”

“Will you offer the physical plate?”

Oren looked at the court locker. “The original saltglass plate is sealed. I will offer its preservation mark, the bridge’s physical behavior, and my crew’s sequence. I will not ask the court to treat my memory as a copy.”

The recorder approached the locker. Harrow raised a hand.

“Seal the original,” he said.

A clerk brought forward the public table and placed the current court plate on it. The recorder read the bridge charter into the field. Ilyan did not need the System to translate every word. He watched the span.

The current text made the lower support tighten around the registered approach. The eastern petition’s coupled thread pulled the load toward the district. The unlisted condition at Tamsin’s boundary produced a third pressure, weaker but not absent. The bridge shook when the three forces met.

Ilyan stepped toward the field’s center. The boundary tightened around his boots.

“Claimant,” Harrow said, “state the condition you challenge.”

“The bridge’s current charter treats registration as a condition of safe passage, but the physical bridge responds to load, maintenance, and witness. The old maintenance review is missing from the active record. The eastern petition uses the same old language to make an unlisted household’s refusal a shared danger. The court must test whether the missing condition is part of the covenant and decide who can carry the burden of keeping the span open.”

“State the remedy.”

“A public review with named burdens and a temporary access stay for the Ro household until the review. The bridge remains under the current charter, and no party may claim the household’s consent.”

Harrow’s expression remained unreadable. “That is a request, not a remedy.”

“It is the remedy I can propose without assigning the burden to the household.”

“Your access request is still ungranted.”

“I know.”

Sera stepped forward. “The claimant’s request is limited. The household asks only for a named access route until the review. The guild asks that its maintenance burden remain named and finite. The eastern district asks that transport effects be recorded.”

Maren’s representative stood. “The district does not consent to a shared bridge staying open under an unlisted crossing.”

“Then the court must record that disagreement,” Sera said. “It cannot make consent out of the petition.”

Harrow turned to the crowd. “No party has accepted another party’s burden. The hearing will proceed.”

The bridge’s lower joint cracked. A pale line of dust ran from the registered approach to the sealed locker, then stopped at the court’s current plate. Oren’s crew moved at once. Jessa called the count. Mero set the weighted beam. Puck watched the water line.

Oren looked at Ilyan. “Do not touch the brace.”

“I won’t.”

“Call the sequence.”

Ilyan watched the marks rather than the crowd. “First load. Named inspection. Return to ordinary load. The record must recheck drift before the next crossing.”

Oren’s eyes narrowed. “The court did not say ‘recheck.’”

“The bridge did.”

“And if it does not?”

“Then we have evidence that the current charter is incomplete.”

The System translated the active record into a single warning.

**THE FIELD CAN TEST THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN A CONDITION AND ITS BURDEN. IT CANNOT ADD A MISSING TERM OR CHOOSE WHO ACCEPTS IT.**

Ilyan felt the field tighten around his hand. The bridge’s pulse pressed against his palm, and a second beat traveled through the stone. His head filled with pressure. He did not reach for the brace.

The pale line stabilized when Oren’s crew completed the inspection. It did not return completely to the mark at the iron ring. A fraction of drift remained.

Harrow turned to Oren. “Is the span safe for a controlled crossing?”

“Not for a crowd,” Oren said. “For one named household, on the repaired load path, with the crew present, yes. For another public count, no.”

“Can you maintain that condition?”

“For the time the court names.”

“What burden does that place on the guild?”

“The guild must keep the brace, the count, and the inspection visible. The work is not free.”

Harrow looked at the court tablet. “And if the guild refuses the work?”

“The bridge closes to any crossing until another repair is accepted.”

Maren Kett spoke. “That is an unacceptable burden. The district will not close its roads because one household’s status is disputed.”

Neris said, “Do not call us a status.”

Maren looked at her. “You are not registered.”

“No. We are named.”

The distinction entered the field. The bridge did not settle. It waited.

Harrow turned to Ilyan. “First Defendant, name the burden you will accept.”

Ilyan felt the shape of a trap. He could claim the burden of the bridge’s full safety, and every later failure would find him by name. He could claim nothing and let the court call the household the burden. He could ask the guild to carry a burden without limit, then call that a remedy.

He looked at the six lines on his notebook. Subject. Condition. Burden. Witness. Remedy. Sunset or exit.

He wrote a seventh line beneath them.

“I will keep my identity, my report, and my post-hearing testimony attached to this case through the next review,” he said. “I will not conceal the costs of that record, and I will not reassign them to the Ro household, the guild, or the eastern district. If the court uses my name to justify a burden, the court must show the burden to the people who carry it.”

The court tablet accepted the statement. No strength entered him. No invisible weight settled on his shoulders. The change was public and limited. Until the stay, he could ask to open a case through Sera, but he could not ask for a live exception or temporary access right on his own. With Sera named beside him, the court now recognized a narrow request for a provisional stay as a distinct step. He still could not choose the remedy alone or touch another witness.

Harrow looked at the notice. “Your burden is recorded. It does not grant you authority over another witness.”

“I know.”

Harrow turned to Tamsin. “Do you accept the proposed temporary access for you and Neris Ro until the review?”

Tamsin looked at the bridge. “Only if the bell stays on.”

“The court can keep the warning bell active for the temporary stay,” Harrow said. “The guild must maintain it.”

Tamsin considered this. “And I can stop crossing.”

“Yes.”

“Then I accept for the crossing I choose. Not for every future crossing.”

Neris spoke. “I accept access for the route to work and the shelter review, until the next ruling. I do not accept the flood account, and I do not accept permanent registration by this stay.”

“Recorded,” Harrow said. “The household’s access is limited to the named review period.”

Harrow looked at Oren. “Do you accept the maintenance burden for the same period?”

Oren looked at the current court plate. “The guild will maintain the repaired load path, the named crew, the warning bell, and the visible inspection. We will not accept liability for crossings outside the stay or for a ruling that turns every unlisted person into a guild debt.”

“Recorded. The burden is finite and named.”

Maren stood. “The eastern district will not consent.”

“Then the district’s refusal is recorded,” Harrow said. “The court will consider the transport burden in the full hearing.”

Harrow raised the tablet. The field tightened around the bridge.

**PROVISIONAL STAY: THE RO HOUSEHOLD MAY USE THE NINTH BRIDGE FOR THE NAMED REVIEW PERIOD. THE CURRENT CHARTER REMAINS IN FORCE. THE STAY EXPIRES UNLESS THE BENCH ISSUES A NEW RULING AT REVIEW.**
**REVIEW DATE: THE SEVENTH MORNING BELL AFTER THIS RULING.**
**BURDEN: Ilyan Vester’s attached record; the bridgekeeper’s named maintenance; the household’s limited access; the district’s recorded disagreement.**

A second notice attached the standing required for the limited stay.

**PROVISIONAL STANDING: ILYAN VESTER, LIMITED TO THE NINTH-BRIDGE CASE.**
**LIMIT: no citizenship, immunity, or authority outside this dispute.**

The words stayed visible after the court bell sounded. The bridge still showed a pale drift mark, and the white safety order remained nailed to the arch. The ruling gave the household a place to cross without pretending that the work was finished.

Tarin opened the neutral line and led Neris to the repaired path. Oren’s crew took their positions. Tamsin stood beside her mother, one hand on the rail, the other gripping Neris’s sleeve. They crossed together, slowly. The span held. The warning bell gave one clear note after they reached the far side.

Oren watched the load mark and called, “Ordinary load. Inspection clear.”

The stay solved a crossing, not Ilyan’s way home. He touched the spent token through his coat; it remained dull. The Bench had given him a public obligation instead of an exit.

The public crowd remained outside the field. Some people cheered. Others stayed silent. The eastern district representative did not join them.

Sera filed the stay with the clerk. “For the full hearing, I recommend that my provisional advocacy be reviewed for formal licensing. I do not recommend that I be given sole responsibility for the case. I recommend a public advocate for the Ro household’s access and housing appeal, and an independent recordwright for my family’s withdrawal claim.”

Harrow looked at her. “Your recommendation is not a license.”

“It is a record that I believe I can carry the work under review. It is not a promise of a verdict.”

The recorder made the filing visible. Ilyan’s name appeared beside the stay, followed by the words **attached witness record**. His public record had become a resource: the court could not hide the maintenance condition behind an unnamed claimant. It had also become a liability. The eastern district could now point to his name whenever it wanted to argue that the bridge case had already been decided by an outsider.

Neris returned to the bridge edge after helping Tamsin settle near the shelter. “The crossing held,” she said.

“For this review period,” Oren replied.

“For this review period,” she agreed.

Tamsin looked back at the span. “I could hear the bell before.”

“Will you speak again?” Ilyan asked.

She considered him. “If I choose.”

“That is the correct answer.”

Sera touched his arm. “Come to the Quiet Chamber after the clerk closes.”

“Why?”

“Because the duplicate I made is gone, and I have found the original record of its removal.”

The public hearing ended with the bridge open under the stay and the current charter untouched. Tamsin and Neris were safe for the moment. Oren’s crew had work before dawn. Ilyan’s name was attached to a public burden, and the court had not given him the authority to hide its cost.

Sera led him through the service corridor toward the archive. She opened a sealed box containing the original page’s empty binding sleeve. Its thread had been cut cleanly, and the inventory mark beside the slot was written in a newer hand. The mark recorded the original’s removal before Ilyan’s crossing. Her duplicate had disappeared later, during or after the night field. The record did not say whether the two losses belonged to the same hand.

Ilyan looked at the fresh ink while Sera fitted a replacement sleeve around the empty slot. The original had not slipped behind a shelf. Someone had opened the binding, removed it, and left a record of the loss in its place.

The first hearing had not answered the bridge case. It had given them two dates and a new place to begin looking.
