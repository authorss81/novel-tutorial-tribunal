# Chapter 1: The Route That Remembered

The last server window for *The Sovereign’s Wake* opened at 11:47 p.m. and closed in thirteen minutes.

Ilyan had learned not to trust a countdown that appeared before the final build had finished loading. The number was usually a promise made by a machine that had not yet met the thing it was measuring. He set the cracked steel pen beside the keyboard, checked the room behind him, and counted the exits anyway.

One door. One emergency panel that probably unlocked only from the corridor. A ceiling hatch sealed for maintenance, its hinges painted over. The room was a converted soundstage dressed as a tutorial vault: black acoustic foam, a table made from two desks, three monitors, and a locked cabinet marked with a number that had belonged to a different project. At midnight, Morrowlight would archive the build, revoke the testers’ credentials, and turn the room into storage. Ilyan had seen the cancellation notices. He had not yet found a way to care about them less.

On the central screen, a pale-haired woman stood at a locked gate. Rain had blurred the mountains beyond it. A line of silver text waited above her head.

**The road is safe only for those whose names are recognized.**

The game had called that a tutorial warning. It had not told the tester what happened to the woman when she pressed the option to continue.

Ilyan clicked the refusal route.

The world dimmed. The gate opened, and the woman looked back at him—or at the camera behind him—with the exhausted expression of someone who had been waiting too long for someone to notice. The game displayed a result screen: ROUTE COMPLETE. A small bell sounded somewhere behind the interface.

Then her memory came back.

It arrived in fragments, each one sliding over the last. A burning workshop. A list of names crossed out. The player-character’s hand closing around a knife. A child calling from a locked room. The woman said, “You promised we could leave,” and the game replaced the sentence with a soft chime.

The interface tried to return to the gate. It tried twice, then a third time. Each reset restored the woman’s smile but not the memory behind it. On the fourth reset, the road outside became a red field and the player-character’s name vanished from the status bar.

Ilyan opened the defect log.

**Tutorial refusal route returns erased memory. Completion state overwrites prior testimony. Reproduction: deterministic.**

He typed the first line, deleted it, and typed it again. The words were not more precise because he had repeated them. He had learned that at Morrowlight. Precision came from finding the exact place where a system stopped making sense, not from making a louder accusation.

The build manager pinged in the team channel.

**ILYAN: final route is supposed to be a clean completion path. Don’t turn every uncomfortable beat into a bug.**

He looked at the woman on the screen. Her hand was still lifted toward the gate. The rain had stopped, although the sky remained black.

**ILYAN:** The refusal route completes with the character’s memory restored. The tutorial says completion means the cause is forgotten. I’m filing an inconsistency.

**MORROWLIGHT LEAD:** We can add a confirmation line if the emotional beat is too clear.

**ILYAN:** That would hide it.

A pause. The project lead’s name in the channel was a small red mark beside the time.

**MORROWLIGHT LEAD:** The project is cancelled after tonight. The report will sit in review. Finish the route and go home.

Ilyan looked at the timer. Nine minutes.

He restarted the route without touching the character’s inventory. He moved the woman to the edge of the field, tested whether the memory returned if she refused the bridge, then tested the same sequence with the bridge register altered. The memory survived the changes. It did not survive a completed tutorial. At one point he replaced the child’s name with a blank marker. The game refused to advance. At another, he let the player-character accept the road’s protection. The road opened, the gate shut, and the woman’s testimony vanished.

That was the seam.

Not a hidden door. Not a secret ending. A rule disguised as a reward.

He opened the formal report and filled every field the production system required. Environment: final closed test. Subject: refusal tutorial. Expected behavior: the route should explain why the character may refuse and preserve the cause of the refusal. Actual behavior: completion erases the cause while marking the route successful. Consequence: the player is taught that a safe outcome is more important than a person’s account of what happened. Reproduction: four resets; one altered bridge condition; memory restored in each refusal branch until the completion state is entered.

The final field asked whether the defect had been reproduced independently. Ilyan typed YES, then stopped.

He had no independent tester. The project had canceled the rest of the team before midnight. He had four resets and a server timer, not corroboration. He could file the observation as a personal report and accept that the report would be treated as noise, or he could soften it into a recommendation and make it easier to bury.

He chose the exact truth.

**REPRODUCED BY: Ilyan Vester, lead tester. No independent witness available.**

The warning about the sealed session appeared. A project lead’s name was added to the chain of custody. The report was not merged. Its signed sequence was preserved as an external challenge because the word *refusal* appeared in the subject field, the reproduction cited a condition that had no accepted interpretation, and a faint vibration rose from the black shale fragment Morrowlight kept beneath the room. The fragment stayed where it was. The response was coming from the record around it.

Ilyan’s hand stopped above the keyboard.

The vibration was not mechanical. It came up through the desk and into the pen. For half a second, the room’s reflection in the black monitor showed a second doorway behind him. There was no doorway in the room.

He canceled the local upload.

The screen returned to the refusal route.

**Do you wish to report inconsistency?**

The prompt had appeared without a button before. Ilyan clicked it because he had spent six years learning where a broken system hid its true question.

The words **before remedy** appeared in a thinner line beneath the prompt. For a moment, the cadence of the text was wrong. It sounded like someone reading over the game’s shoulder and correcting a word at a time.

Then the room tore.

Not violently at first. The edges of the monitor bent toward the sealed ceiling hatch. The server fans spun down in a descending sequence. Ilyan grabbed the paper notebook with the report sequence and tried to abort the transfer with the emergency command he had memorized years earlier.

The command went nowhere.

A blackglass token appeared in his palm, cold enough to burn. Its center was split by a white line. The paper beneath his other hand began to tear away, but he held on to the corner with the signature and the exact order of his tests. If the system was taking him somewhere, it would not get the rest of the record for free.

“Abort,” he said. “This is a test session. Abort the transfer.”

The light behind him answered in a voice that had no body.

**Continuity preserves the record before remedy.**

The room folded.

He hit the floor hard, one shoulder against stone. The smell came first: wet iron, river silt, and hot oil. Above him, blackglass work lights burned in a row. Each went out as he lifted his head. A great span of stone and metal groaned overhead. Water dripped through a crack in the paving, carrying a red thread of rust.

The token in his hand had become flat, dull, and spent.

Ilyan pushed himself up. The test room was gone. In its place, a lower approach opened beneath a bridge too large for the room he had left. People were shouting on the far side. A woman’s voice cut through theirs, sharp with fear.

Then a child’s hand appeared beneath the failing span, fingers reaching over the edge of a maintenance stair.

The bridge lights went dark one after another.

Ilyan ran toward her.
