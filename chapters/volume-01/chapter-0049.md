# Chapter 49: The Ruling

Harrow took the whole of the last hour and used it to say what he was not going to do, which was the thing Ilyan had begun to expect of him somewhere around the second remedy on the first day and had stopped expecting by the fourth, and had started hoping for during a recess in which a clerk from a rented room wrote down the reason he could not pay a wage.

“The court has three remedies,” Harrow said. “It has had them since the preliminary hearing. It is going to dispose of all three out loud, including the one it adopts, and I am going to do the adopted one last, because the two I am refusing are the reason the third one is shaped the way it is, and a room that hears only the ruling will hear a document and not a decision.

“Remedy three: hold the hearing open.”

He let that sit for a second, the way you let a bell sit.

“Mr Vell has argued for it and he has argued it better than it has ever been argued, and he has argued it by naming nine bundles of roof on a road with eleven one-handed households on it and one boy who cannot be on a ladder, and that is a real cost, and it is being paid, and by the people who can least afford to pay it.

“And I am refusing it. I am refusing it for a reason that has nothing to do with Mr Vell and everything to do with the fact that holding this hearing open is the third local arrangement in three weeks, and each of the three has been written by the bodies that carry it and each of the three has had an end date on it, and the end dates have all passed, and the reason they have all passed is that an interval is a thing a room gives itself when it does not have a ruling.

“This court has a charter in front of it with a hole in it the size of a keeper, and the hole has been the size of a keeper for twenty years, and every interval in this case has been a way of not writing the sentence that closes it. I am not going to give this case a fourth end date. The third remedy is refused, and Mr Vell's objection to the refusal is preserved, and the refusal does not say that holding costs nothing. It says that the cost of holding is not a number and that a cost which is not a number cannot be carried by a woman on the lower road for a fourth time.”

Joren Vell stood at the eastern stake and did not say anything for a while, and then he said, “Entered,” and sat down, which from Joren was the whole of a concession and he made it in public.

“Remedy two: break the span.”

Dain was already standing. He had been standing for a while.

“Mr Marshal. You have argued that breaking the span preserves the charter, removes the need for a power no district voted for, and stops a keeper being able to take a carter's afternoon.”

“I have, and I still do, and I would like it recorded that I still do, and that I am not withdrawing it because I have lost, but because the court has removed the ground from under it, which is a different thing and which I have never had happen to me before and do not fully trust.”

“State the ground,” Harrow said.

“You made it conditional,” Dain said. “On a crossing for every household that loses this one, at a named route and a named cost, before the span comes down, and on the ward's water and the lower road's shelter and the pump staying exactly as they are. And if this court rules that the Ro household and every other household entered in that register has a named crossing, a sworn tenancy, a portable work record, and a protected water route — before anything, and not as a consequence of anything, and payable to the person and not to a place — then breaking the span is no longer the fair thing. It is the thing you do afterwards, to a bridge nobody needs.”

“Entered,” Roke said, and his voice had gone strange, because he was a record hand and he had just been handed a district's own condition being turned into the reason it did not apply.

“Remedy two is refused,” Harrow said, “and the refusal is not a finding that the span is safe. The span is not safe. Mr Sedge's own testimony is that the west seat is empty on the underside to a depth of seven inches and that the water comes up four inches in a day, and the court is not going to pretend that a charter amendment is a repair. The refusal is that the court will not destroy a public crossing in order to avoid writing the sentence that was cut out of it twenty years ago, and the district's objection to the refusal is preserved and is one of the two objections in this case that the court expressly invites to an appeal.”

Then he stopped, and looked at the span, and at the west end, and at the pale line a finger's width from the iron ring, and when he began again his voice was different, and there were about three hundred people on that approach who had never heard him use it.

“Remedy one: amend the charter.”

Roke read for forty minutes. Ilyan followed about a third of it, which was more than on the previous occasion, because the fourth sentence of the read-back was in the guild's words and he had written half of it himself, and he gave up entirely at the enumerated cases and did not pretend otherwise to anybody.

The court amended the ninth-bridge charter. Not the amendment the district had drafted. Something with ten terms in it, of which a keeper's counter had written a sentence and a ward's landlord had sworn a book about and a road man had written on a frame in a cart driver's lettering.

Harrow stopped the clerk before the last term was read and put three sentences in from the bench.

“One difference from the district's own paper, and the clerk is to enter that I have made it. The district offered the guild's hands for the unloaded works at the guild's levy rate. The court buys those hands at the ward rate, which is higher, and the district has not objected, and Mr Vale is to enter that it has not objected, and if a day comes when the district wishes to object it may do so in a day-book line like any other party and the wage will not stop in the meantime.”

“Entered,” Roke said, and Meridith Vale, who was standing at the eastern stake with his case under his arm, said nothing at all, which Vale's own file records as a thing he would like somebody to be annoyed with him about in a month.

“One more. The eighth of a load-fee, collected from nine named cart owners, is not ended by this ruling. It is not the district's money and the court cannot end it, and A. of the eastern yard keeps the book and not the guild. What this ruling does is add to it. The toll pays the whistle and the count. From today it also pays the six hands of a called pause, and it is short on a day when the road is thin, and the clerk is to enter that it is short and that the span's line is the district's and not the toll's, and that a road which cannot pay a pause does not get to refuse one.

“Entered,” Roke said, and Larch said, “That is going in the ward roll as well,” and wrote it.

**THE NINTH-BRIDGE CHARTER, AS AMENDED, SET FOR THE FOURTH MORNING BELL OF A HEARING HELD ON THE SPAN, IN THE PRESENCE OF THE SPAN.**
**ONE. THE MAINTENANCE-REVIEW CONDITION IS RESTORED TO THE ACTIVE CHARTER. A NAMED KEEPER OF THIS SPAN, HAVING READ THE WEST JOINT ON A NAMED INSPECTION, MAY CALL THE DECK CLEAR, AND THE DECK BEING CLEAR IS THE WHOLE OF THE POWER. THE ORIGINAL WORDING OF THE CONDITION IS NOT RECOVERED. IT WAS CUT FROM THE ACTIVE CHARTER BEFORE THIS HEARING AND IT IS NOT SUPPLIED BY THIS RULING.**
**TWO. THE WARNING BELL'S WHEEL AND PIN ARE PART OF THE SPAN'S MAINTENANCE AND ARE INSPECTED ON A NAMED DAY BY TWO HANDS, ONE OF WHOM HAS PULLED THE ROPE. A KEEPER WHO RINGS A BELL THAT DOES NOT SOUND IS A PERSON PULLING A ROPE.**
**THREE. THE BURDEN OF THE SPAN'S MAINTENANCE IS NAMED. THE GUILD PERFORMS THE COUNT, THE INSPECTION, THE UNDER-DECK WATCH AT TWO HANDS, THE BELL, AND THE RELEASE, FROM THE FIRST BELL TO DUSK, AT THE LEVY RATE, OUT OF THE GUILD'S OWN ACCOUNT. THE DISTRICT PAYS THE STOPPED HOUR AND THE UNDER-DECK WATCH AND THE PUMP'S SHORTAGE AND THE GUILD'S HANDS FOR THE UNLOADED WORKS, AT THE WARD RATE, ON NAMED DAY-BOOK LINES, POSTED IN CHALK ON THE FRAME. THE GUILD'S FOUR PROPS ARE TAKEN FROM THE FOURTH BRIDGE, WHOSE KEEPER IS NOTIFIED, OBJECTS, AND WHOSE OBJECTION IS PRESERVED, AND THE PROPS ARE REPLACED OR THE EQUIVALENT PAID OUT OF THE DISTRICT'S ROAD FUNDS BEFORE THE REVIEW DATE. THE NINTH BRIDGE'S SETTLEMENT MAY NOT SPEND THE FOURTH BRIDGE'S SAFETY IN A DOCUMENT THAT DOES NOT NAME IT.**
**FOUR. THE WARD RATE IS PAID AT THE MOMENT OF THE WORK TO THE PERSON PERFORMING IT, BY NAME, ON A DAY-BOOK LINE, IN CHALK, AND NOT TO A BODY AND NOT TO A PLACE AND NOT TO AN ADDRESS. AN EMPTY LINE IS A RECORD. A FULL LINE IS A WAGE.**
**FIVE. A PAUSE LASTS AS LONG AS THE LOADS STANDING ON THE DECK AND NO LONGER. IF THE DECK IS NOT CLEAR WITHIN THAT TIME THE BELL IS A WARNING AND THE SPAN IS RELEASED, AND NO KEEPER HAS HELD ANYTHING. A LOAD THAT DOES NOT COME OFF IS A LOAD THAT HAS NOT BEEN STOPPED BY ANYBODY.**
**SIX. AN APPEAL. ANY PERSON AFFECTED BY A RULING ON THIS SPAN MAY CHALLENGE IT BEFORE THE REVIEW DATE, IN A PUBLIC SITTING, ON NOTICE GIVEN TO EVERY PARTY NAMED IN THIS INSTRUMENT. THE APPEAL DOES NOT SUSPEND THE ACCESS, HOUSING, OR WORK PROTECTION ENTERED UNDER THIS INSTRUMENT, FOR THE REASON GIVEN BY THIS COURT AT THE SEVENTH MORNING BELL OF THIS MATTER, WHICH IS THAT A RELIEF ENTERED UNDER A RECORD DOES NOT LAPSE BECAUSE THE COURT CONTINUED THE MATTER, AND WHICH IS NOW WRITTEN INTO THE CHARTER SO THAT NOBODY HAS TO ASK FOR IT AGAIN.**
**SEVEN. AN EXIT. THE CHARTER AS AMENDED MAY BE REFUSED BY A PERSON, BY A HOUSEHOLD, OR BY A BODY, AS TO A SPECIFIC BURDEN. THE REFUSAL IS ENTERED IN A REGISTER KEPT IN THE WARD AND SWORN BY THE LANDLORD OF THE LOWER ROAD, WHICH RECORDS THE BURDEN REFUSED, THE PERSON WHO CARRIED IT INSTEAD, THE DATE, AND WHAT IT DID TO THE SPAN, AND WHICH RECORDS NO CONSENT AND NO ADDRESS. NO PERSON IS REMOVED FROM ANY REGISTER BY REFUSAL. NO RELIEF ALREADY ENTERED IS WITHDRAWN BY REFUSAL. THE RIGHT TO REFUSE MAY NOT BE EXERCISED SO AS TO CONCEAL A HARM FROM A PERSON WHO DEPENDS ON THE SPAN.**
**EIGHT. ACCESS, HOUSING, AND WORK. A HOUSEHOLD ENTERED AS A PARTICIPATING PARTY IN THE LOWER WARD'S COMMON REGISTER IS RECOGNIZED AS A PARTY TO THIS CHARTER. THE HOUSEHOLD MAY CROSS AT A NAMED HOUR, ON THE REPAIRED LOAD PATH, WITHOUT REGISTRATION, AT THE WARD RATE AND NOT AT A TOLL. THE HOUSEHOLD'S TENANCY ON THE LOWER ROAD IS PROTECTED UNTIL AN APPEAL OR A REVIEW SAYS OTHERWISE, AND IS NOT ENDED BY A REFUSAL ENTERED UNDER TERM SEVEN. THE HOUSEHOLD'S WORK RECORD IS PORTABLE AND IS NOT CONDITIONAL ON THE HOUSEHOLD'S PLACE IN ANY REGISTER. THE LANDLORD OF THE LOWER ROAD IS BOUND BY THAT PROTECTION.**
**THE ACCESS CONDITION OF THIS SPAN CONTINUES FOR A LAWFUL TRAVELER OF THIS SPAN. A PERSON WHO IS NOT ENTERED IN THE LAWFUL-TRAVELER REGISTER IS NOT A TRESPASSER BY THAT FACT ALONE. A KEEPER ADMITS ANY PERSON ON FOOT, OR WITH A HANDCART, OR WITH A WHEELBARROW, WHO OFFERS NO LOAD AND DOES NOT ENTER THE LOAD PATH, AND THE PERSON'S NAME IS ENTERED IN THE COLUMN KEPT IN THE WARD, WHICH IS A RECORD OF CROSSING, AND IS NOT A REGISTER, AND TAKES NOBODY OUT OF ANYTHING.**
**THE PROTECTION IN THIS TERM IS ENTERED UNDER THE NINTH-BRIDGE RECORD AND IS NOT CONDITIONAL ON THAT RECORD. AN APPEAL, A REOPENING, OR A REVIEW MAY END IT ONLY BY A NAMED FINDING AND NOT BY THE LAPSE OF ANY INSTRUMENT.**
**NINE. THE GUILD'S ACKNOWLEDGMENT ENTERED THIS DAY IS ACCEPTED AS NAMED AND IS ENDS WITH ITS KEEPER, AND SHALL NOT BE SERVED UPON THE GUILD OR ANY SUCCESSOR OF THE GUILD IN RESPECT OF THIS SPAN EXCEPT WITH A TERM, A DATE, AND AN EXIT ON THE FACE OF IT.**
**TEN. A REVIEW, ON THE FOURTH MORNING BELL OF THE THIRD MONTH AFTER THIS RULING, AT THIS SPAN, ON PUBLIC NOTICE, WHICH MAY BE CALLED EARLY BY ANY PARTY ON A NAMED READING OUTSIDE THE BAND THE KEEPER HAS KEPT, AND WHICH MAY NOT BE POSTPONED.**

And then, at the bottom, in the flat unarguable grammar of a thing that has the authority of the page it is printed on, in a thinner hand and a cadence Ilyan had now heard four times in a city he had lived in for nineteen days:

**CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD BEFORE ANY REMEDY IS ENTERED.**

The fourth time had said *stated*. This one said *entered*.

Roke read his own tray twice.

“That is not on my tray,” he said.

“Do not take it out of the record,” Sera said, before Dain could, and before anybody else could, and in a voice that shut four conversations at once. “Take it down and it is gone, and it is gone the way a page is gone, and I have spent nineteen days learning what a city looks like after it has decided that a line nobody can account for is a line that should not have been there. It goes in. It goes in as a line the court cannot account for, attached to a ruling it did not make, and it goes in with the word changed, and somebody in a hundred years is going to see that the word changed.”

“I am not ruling on it,” Harrow said, to the field, to the district, to the record hand, to the three hundred people on the approach. “I have not ruled on it before and I am not ruling on it now. I have said once that a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter, and I have said it twice, and I am going to say it a third time in the same words, because the alternative is that this court becomes the sort of place where a thing it cannot explain gets struck out, and there is a district forty miles from here that struck a page out of a binder with cold paste and put a different page in, and I am not going to be asked about that in four years.”

The System put the entry into Ilyan's sight while Harrow was still refusing the line, and it was a record of a ruling and not a rule about the world, and Ilyan read the ten terms and the line under them and the hand it was in and understood that he had just watched a court change a charter in a city where a woman on the lower road had lost a room and a girl had lost the use of a bell and a keeper had lost the ability to stop a cart, and that the tenth line had arrived on its own, and that nobody in the room had asked for it, and that the word had changed.

“Marshal,” Harrow said. “Your objection, to the refusal of remedy two, is preserved. Your district's answer is in the record with the reason on the back of it. Is there anything else.”

“There is one thing,” Dain said, “and it is a formality and I would like it read.”

“Read it.”

“The attached record-holder has no constituency, and has had none since the twenty-eighth day, and the assembly's clerk entered that in the open, and the court is about to enter his name on a charter that four districts will read.” Dain looked at Ilyan, and it was not unkind, which was somehow much harder. “The district does not object. The district would like it recorded that a man with no constituency has been the instrument of this settlement and that the district does not intend to be the only body in four districts that is surprised by it.”

“Entered,” Roke said. “And the attached observation, and the three facts and the guild's book and the costed proposal, are the grounds of this settlement, and the settlement is not theirs, and the court is going to say that in the instrument, because a settlement whose grounds are four people and whose text is a court's is a settlement that a person in another district can be shown and can be told the difference.”

“It is the court’s text,” Harrow said. “Say so in the instrument.”

“In this room it is an amendment,” Sera said, before he could move on, and he did not move on. “That is what was done to a charter and it is what the ten terms say was done to a charter. In a few days it will be called an exception, because that is the word this city uses for a thing one bridge is allowed to do that the charter does not allow, and a word costs a clerk less than an argument costs a district. Nothing in these ten terms will stop a form being printed with that word at the top of it. And when that happens, the paper in the clerk’s hand will still be the paper I verified, and the word will not be on it, and somebody will come to me and ask which of the two documents is the real one.”

“Enter nothing of the kind,” Dain said. “That is a prediction and not an objection.”

“It is a recordwright telling a room what a record does,” Sera said. “You asked for it in daylight. It is in daylight.”

Then, because he had said he would do it out loud and not in a file, he turned to the whole approach.

“A word about a precedent, since nobody has asked for it and everybody has thought about it, and I would rather it were done in daylight.

“This settlement is entered as a precedent witness. It is available to other districts. It may be read, cited, copied, and adopted. The four districts at the weighhouse may each decide whether to adopt it, and two of them cannot produce their own maintenance terms and will be the hardest to persuade, and Mr Prenn's draft eleven will be a harder document to argue for after today than it was yesterday, and that is the cost of a template and the court pays it.

“The attached record-holder's name is on it. His name is on it because his attached observation is one of the four grounds, and a precedent that names nobody is not a precedent anybody can use. He asked for that, in a corridor, earlier tonight, in front of his advocate, and he asked for it knowing that his name on a document in four districts is a thing that cannot be taken back, and he has already put two copies of a private sequence of his own into this city's records and cannot take either of them back either.

“Mr Vester. Do you want to say why, into the record, or do you want it entered that you asked for it and gave no reason, as a man with no constituency has already established is his practice.”

“Enter it that I gave no reason,” Ilyan said. “I would rather the record says a man asked for a thing and gave no reason than that it says a man explained himself and was believed, because a document that carries a reason can be quoted without the man.”

Harrow almost smiled and did not.

“Mr Vester. Your grant at the first hearing ran one review cycle and that cycle ended at the seventh morning bell, and it was not extended and could not be, and you have spent this hearing as a witness to your own observation with no standing and no constituency, as the assembly's clerk entered in the open. I am going to restore it, and I am going to read it out before I do it, because the last time it was read out to you it was read out to you and you accepted it before you had read it, and this court does not do that twice.

“Provisional standing, limited to the ninth-bridge matter. You may file in it and be heard in it with a named advocate. You may not be cited as a permit, a schedule, a levy, an acknowledgment, or a bearer of labor, and the assembly's limited word of the twenty-fourth day is carried into the instrument. And you are to be produced at the public review to answer for the cost of your own record, and you are to come with your report and with the two copies of your own sequence that you entered into this city's record and cannot take back, and you are to say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and you are not to send somebody with your name on it.”

He read the whole of it twice, and then he stopped and let Ilyan answer, which nobody in the field had expected and which a court record hand of nine years' standing wrote down with a note that it was not a formality.

“I accept it,” Ilyan said, “and I am not going to pretend it is a small thing, because a man who took a standing and called it a formality in the same breath as he asked to have his name printed is a man who has not read the instrument. It is a burden and it is a continuing one and it is mine. My identity, my report, my post-hearing testimony, and everything I have put into this city's record stay attached to this case through the review and after it, and I may not conceal what that record cost and I may not hand the cost to the Ro household, to the guild, to the district, to the ward, or to the woman whose refusal column four districts are going to read. If this court uses my name to justify a burden, this court must show the burden to the people who carry it, and I will be the one standing in the room when it does.”

“Entered,” Roke said. “A personal continuing burden, named by the party, and accepted by him, and not the price of the precedent.”

“That last clause is Mr Quill's,” Sera said, from the advocate stool, “and she gave it to me in a corridor an hour ago, and I am going to say so on the record before somebody claims it.”

“Enter that too,” Harrow said.

“Mr Kett. You have an objection.”

“I have an objection to the phrase *lawful traveler*,” Kett said, and he had the draft in his hand, and he was not a lawyer and he had read it twice on the road. “It is the charter's own word. It is two hundred years old. It is in the access condition and it means registered, and it has meant registered since before the west abutment moved, and in four years somebody in a poor district with a sanitation problem is going to read that word and understand it as *proper*, and the proper thing is to change it now while it costs a morning to change it.”

“Mr Quill.”

“It is the charter's word and it is in the charter's condition and I am not going to rewrite the access condition of a bridge on the last day of a hearing on the last day of a week because somebody is frightened of a future reader,” Sera said. “And the future reader will read the whole instrument, and the whole instrument says what a lawful traveler of this span is, and it says it in a keeper's words on the frame, and it will be read with it, because I have just taken a license to make sure that it is.”

“Then the phrase stands,” Harrow said, “and Mr Kett's objection to the phrase is preserved, and the court notes that the phrase is the charter's and not the settlement's, and that it will be read by other districts, and that is what a precedent is.”

Which is the oldest lesson in this case biting a man who knew it, in public, on a bridge, and being told by a recordwright that the instrument would be read with the frame, and being believed by nobody and carried anyway.

The day-book line was opened at the last bell of the night, in chalk, on the frame, in front of everybody, and it was the first wage the district had ever paid on the ninth bridge and it was nine words long.

Larch came up with the roll and Vale's slip and read it back, because a clerk reads back, and because Bevin Larch had decided some weeks ago that a record which does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by, and had been right, twice, in public, and was twenty-four years old.

**NINTH-BRIDGE WARD RATE, DISTRICT ROAD FUNDS, DAY-BOOK LINE OPENED THIS DAY.**
**PAID AT THE WARD RATE, TO THE PERSON, BY NAME, FOR THE WORK OF CLEARING THE DECK AT THE THIRD BELL OF THIS DAY UNDER THE KEEPER'S BELL: I. BRAY. O. SEDGE. COLL. P. (Puck, first name not entered, and the clerk read that out twice). J. VELL. D. RUSK.**
**PAUSE OF TWO BELLS. SIX HANDS. NO OTHER LINE IS OPEN FOR THIS HOUR.**
**THE PUMP'S SHORTAGE OF TWO DAYS, AND HALF A DAY ON THE DAY AFTER THE ARRANGEMENT WAS WRITTEN, IS PAID. THE PUMP IS PAID, NOT THE WARD. D. RUSK IS NOT REQUIRED TO SAY THANK YOU.**

Neris Ro read that line twice, standing at the lower edge, and then said, to nobody, “That is the first time in two years that a piece of paper in this city has had my name in it and my name on it about something other than a refusal.”

“It has Neris Ro in it in three places,” Ilyan said, “and one of them is a signature she did not give and one of them is a sentence she spoke in a lane at two in the morning, and the third is going to be in a precedent that four districts will read, and I have thought about that all afternoon and I have decided that I would rather her name be on a document that says she is owed something than on one that says she refused something, and I would like somebody to stop me if that turns out to be a way of spending her.”

“Stop you,” Sera said.

“I am not going to,” Ilyan said, and Neris said, “No,” and they said it at about the same moment, in two different voices, and about two hundred people heard both, and neither of them looked at the other.

Oren rang the bell for the change, and the bell gave one clear note, and the pin was good, and Puck had looked at it on the seventh morning because a child had told him in public that nobody was.
