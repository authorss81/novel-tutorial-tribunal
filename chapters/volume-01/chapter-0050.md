# Chapter 50: The Line Beside the Line

Ten days on, the ninth bridge stood on four props and carried nothing at all, and it was the safest it had been in six years.

The unloaded period was twenty days and it began at dusk on the day of the ruling, when the span closed on four props and did not open again. The seat had been opened on the drain side that morning and bedded the next, in crushed stone and lime bought by the district at a yard off the fen road and moved, at last, by somebody with an instrument, and it was sitting in its new bed curing and would go on sitting there for another fortnight after the props came out. Puck did the packing and did it with the guild's props holding the deck and four of his own hands and two hired from the fourth bridge, whom Wist Anhalt sent without being asked and about whom Oren said nothing at all for a day and then wrote the two names into the count book and kept them there.

The count still ran. That was the thing that surprised the district and it surprised Ilyan more. Every change, from the setup before the first bell, four hands on a deck propped over a channel, two of them underneath, and the reading taken twice, and the reading written down, because the second hand was on the ward's line and the second hand was a person and a person was in a book.

And the bell was rung at every change, and the pin was looked at on the seventh morning and again on the fourteenth, by two people, one of whom had pulled the rope.

---

The roof went over on the morning of the ruling, which was the last morning the span was open, and it went over on nine bundles and six pairs of hands at the district's expense, and it took the whole of the day, and the day-book line said so in chalk, and the span closed at dusk and there was nobody standing in the road afterwards.

Joren Vell had put the date on the frame on the morning of the ruling, in his own lettering, at the height where a cart driver on the box could read it, and nobody had to ask him twice, and he did not write a roof into a bridge document, because he had said in a public field that he would not and because he had meant it. He wrote the date. He did not write the word roof anywhere on the frame, and a carter could work out for himself what nine bundles on the last open morning meant.

The lower road got all nine. Eleven households and one of them had been refused twice and lost once, and the arithmetic of that is not interesting; what is interesting is that a carrier who had read a frame in chalk on the first morning of a week found a road with a date at the bottom of it and a day named for the roof, and came up the fen road with nine bundles, and asked at the eastern stake whether there were six pairs of hands, because he had been told by somebody on that approach and would not say by whom.

“There are six,” Oren said, “at the district's expense, at the ward rate, and I want it entered that the district is paying, and I want the ward's clerk to enter it, and I want it entered before we start.”

Room nine was done first, because it was first on the list, and because a woman who cannot go up a ladder is not a woman who can wait two days for a crew to get to her.

Besh Rennick, nine, carried bundles up and down the inside stair and put them on the roof flat where they were and was told four times by different people that he was not to go up, and went up the outside at the ninth hour when the crew were on the other side, and came down again having been seen, which the district's own ward roll records as a safety incident and which he has described, in his own account, as *the bit where I did the thing*.

Marda Rennick sat on the bench in the lower road all day with her leg out and did not go up, and did not go up because she could not, and about eleven people came and tried to help her and were sent away by a woman of ten with a bundle on each arm, and one of them was Ilyan and she sent him away by name.

“You have not got a roof to put on,” she said.

“That is not—”

“You have not got a roof to put on,” Tamsin said, and went up the stair with the last bundle, and Ilyan stood in the lane for about two minutes and then went and found something else to carry on the eastern approach, which is the entire repertoire of a man who has been told he is not needed.

---

The rider came up off the fen road on the fifth day of the unloaded period, six days before the day this chapter is set on, and he had a print, and the print was two days old.

It was a single sheet, printed on a district's paper from a form, and it had been printed in Halloway — a poor district on the low water, four miles below the weighhouse, with a sanitation complaint in front of its own bench and a ward that had asked for a rule — and it was an extract of the ninth-bridge settlement, and it had been printed on the second day after the ruling, before the day-book line was opened, before the re-bedding had begun, and before any of the four districts had sent anybody to ask what had been decided.

“Same words,” the rider said, handing it over, and he was fifteen and he had ridden eleven miles and he was not interested in the case. “Same words, in a different column.”

It was the same words. That was the part that landed. Not a copy of a summary, not a clerk's paraphrase, not a version with the awkward parts left off: the *same words*, in the same order, in a district's typeface, on a district's paper, in a district's column.

And in the fourth paragraph, where the ninth bridge's settlement said that the access condition of the span continued for a lawful traveler of this span, Halloway's print said that the gate at the low-water culvert would remain closed to any person who was not a lawful traveler of the district, and the word was the same word and it was doing the opposite work, and it had been in a printed document with a district's name at the top since the second day after a ruling that was nine days old.

Maren Kett read it standing up. It took him a long time and he read it twice.

“This is not a copy of a rule,” he said. “This is a copy of a *shape*. Nine terms and a shape and a phrase. And the phrase is the one Mr Vester told me to be frightened of on the last day of the hearing, and I told him it was the charter's word and I was right and I was wrong in the same breath, and here it is on a printed sheet in a poor district with a sanitation complaint in front of its bench.”

“Can it be stopped?” Ilyan asked.

“No,” Kett said. “Not by us. Not by the guild, not by the district, not by this court, not by me. It is a copy of a public instrument, lawfully printed, by a district that is entitled to print public instruments, and if we go to Halloway and say *you may not use that wording*, then the next thing that happens is that nobody in four districts can use any of it, and the two districts that cannot produce their own maintenance terms will never look at it again, and the fourth bridge's props will be a local favour instead of a clause, and we will have made a rule nobody can quote in order to stop a rule being quoted badly, which is the most expensive sentence anybody has said on this bridge in three weeks and I have wanted to say it for a day.”

“Then somebody has to go and stand in their room,” Sera said.

“Somebody has to go and stand in their room,” Kett agreed, “and it cannot be the attached record-holder, because his name is on it and every person in that room will read the name and stop listening, and it cannot be the recordwright of the instrument, because the recordwright of the instrument will be treated as the party that wanted it, and I will go, and I will go because I argued for one legible rule and this is what a legible rule looks like when it walks into a room I did not think about, and I would like it entered that I am going under protest and that my written instruction says *one legible rule* and does not say *and then a copy of it in a poor district's sanitation dispute*.”

“Entered,” said Larch, who was there, and who wrote it, and who read it back, and who was going to be asked about it in four years and had decided in advance that he would write it again.

Ilyan stood at the eastern stake with the print in his hand and understood that the volume's oldest lesson had arrived on a bicycle from a fifteen-year-old, four days after he had asked for the door to be made citable, and that he had got exactly what he asked for, and that Sera had told him in a corridor to be frightened of the difference between a precedent and a template, and that she had used the word *frightened* and he had not been.

He gave the print to Sera. She read it, and gave it back, and did not say anything about it at all, and he understood that she was going to take it to a bench on the next working morning and that this was the fourth time in nineteen days she had done something with a document he had not asked her to do.

---

The old register was a board, eight feet long, bolted to the wall of the eastern approach above the frame, and it had on it, in four hands and four decades, every lawful traveler of the ninth bridge, and there were eleven hundred and six names on it, and the last of them had been added nine years ago, and under the last name somebody had scratched a line and written *and after this nobody*, which had not been a rule and had been true anyway.

Beside it, on the eleventh day of the unloaded period, at four in the afternoon, a ward clerk and a recordwright and a landlord nailed up a strip of board about a foot and a half long.

It was not a copy of anything. It was not a form. It was a strip of the same board, planed, cut from the edge of a bound volume that was sworn in a room on the lower road and kept there, and it had four lines on it in four different hands, and the first line was Hanne Kolm's, because she had said a book has a column and a column is a thing you have to look at.

**REFUSALS ENTERED AGAINST A BURDEN ON THE NINTH BRIDGE. SWORN BY H. KOLM, LOWER ROAD. THIS COLUMN RECORDS NO CONSENT AND NO ADDRESS AND TAKES NOBODY OUT OF ANYTHING.**

**CARRY ON FROM HERE. THESE ARE NAMES OF PERSONS WHO CROSSED. THEY ARE NOT A REGISTER AND THEY REPLACE NOTHING.**

And under that, in a child's careful hand that Ilsa Bray had taught her in the guild's book over two evenings and had refused to help her with afterwards:

**AND NAMES.**

Twenty-two names in ten days, because the span is propped and unloaded and a handcart and a wheelbarrow and a person on foot may cross it under the keeper's bell, and every one of the twenty-two was somebody with no lawful-traveler entry at all, and the first four of them were the households of the lower road who had spent a fortnight with a date chalked on a frame, and the fifth was Puck, and the sixth was Coll, and the seventh was a carter's daughter of about eleven who had been standing at the eastern stake every day of the hearing waiting for somebody to say she could cross on her own.

The twenty-third was a woman nobody on that approach knew.

She had come up off the fen road on the last of the nine bundles' cart, after the roof was on, and she had a bag and a bundle and a piece of paper, and the paper was a district's notice of some kind from a district three valleys away, and she said, in the flat voice of somebody who has said this sentence in four rooms, that she was not on any register and had not been for two years, and that she had heard at the low-water culvert that the ninth bridge was open to people who are not on a list, and that she had walked eleven miles to see whether it was true.

Harrow was not there. There was no court on the approach. There was Larch and Moot and a district clerk, and Tarin at the eastern stake with his roll over his shoulder, and about four hundred people who had no office of any kind and had come because the span was propped and the bell was rung every change and there was nowhere else to be.

Larch looked at the board, and then at the clause, and then at the strip of board four inches wide, and then at the woman, and Ilyan watched a twenty-four-year-old ward clerk stand on a public approach and have the most important four seconds of his working life.

“Your name,” Larch said.

“Halide Sen,” she said. “And it is with a y at the end and I know, and everybody writes it with an i.”

Larch wrote it on the strip, and spelled it as she said it, and then read it back, and then stepped aside.

She crossed.

The props took her weight and held. Puck was under the west seat with a lamp he did not need and a rule he did not use. The count read one and a half and it came back, and the pale line sat a finger's width from the iron ring where it had been sitting for six years and where it would sit for another nine days until the props came out, and for a fortnight after that before anybody could say a word about it, and the bell was not rung because there was no load, and the whole business of a bridge that has a keeper in it and a rule about what he may do took about forty seconds and was invisible to everybody standing on the approach except four people.

Halide Sen came down the lower stair and stopped, and turned round, and looked at the strip of board, and then at the four hundred people, and then, in a voice nobody had planned for, said, “Then my sister can come too,” and Neris Ro, from the lower edge of the crowd, said, “Bring her. It is there. It is on the board.”

---

The ledger was Sera Quill's, and it was a bound book with a sewn spine, and it had thirty days in it, and she closed it on the step of the workroom at the last bell of light on the day the strip of board went up, and she did it in front of Ilyan because she had a professional reason and a second reason and she was not going to pretend to him which one she was doing it for.

On the inside of the back cover, in her own hand, in the small square letters of a woman who enters things for a living, there were three lines.

**NINTH-BRIDGE SETTLEMENT, FOURTH MORNING BELL, FULL HEARING, ON THE SPAN. CHARTER AMENDED. RELIEF ENTERED UNDER THE RECORD CONTINUED AND DOES NOT LAPSE. THE INSTRUMENT GOVERNS AND THE READ-BACK DOES NOT.**
**REVIEW: THE FOURTH MORNING BELL OF THE THIRD MONTH, AT THE SPAN, ON PUBLIC NOTICE.**
**APPEAL: ANY PERSON AFFECTED MAY CHALLENGE THIS RULING BEFORE THAT DATE, IN A PUBLIC SITTING, ON NOTICE TO EVERY NAMED PARTY. THE APPEAL DOES NOT SUSPEND ACCESS, HOUSING, OR WORK. THAT IS IN THE CHARTER AND IT IS NOT A FAVOUR.**
**AND A FOURTH LINE, WHICH IS THE ONE HE IS TO BE PRODUCED TO ANSWER FOR: THE ATTACHED RECORD-HOLDER'S PROVISIONAL STANDING IS LIMITED TO THIS MATTER, MAY NOT BE CITED AS A PERMIT, AND HE IS TO COME TO THE REVIEW WITH HIS REPORT AND SAY OUT LOUD WHAT THE RECORD COST AND WHO PAID IT. NOT A FAVOUR EITHER.**

“That is the last line on purpose,” Sera said, “because in about four months somebody is going to want a copy of this and there is going to be a clerk who has the ruling and not the charter, and a ruling without the appeal on it is a door that looks like a wall, and I have spent a month learning that a wall that looks like a door is worse than a wall.”

“And the rest of it.”

“The rest of it is yours to read or not to read and I am not going to tell you what is in it and I have not shown it to anybody, and I am going to say the thing about the license that I did not say in front of the court, because you asked me in a corridor once and I did not answer.

“I took it because for nineteen days I could have made a mistake and nothing would have happened to me, and because when I made one on the sixth day I ended somebody's representation and I had a piece of paper that said *provisional* on it, and *provisional* is a word a person hides behind.” She shut the book and put her hand flat on the cover. “I am not going to hide behind it. And I am not going to be yours. I will be the record of a bridge, and if you go wrong in that room in front of a copy, I will correct you in front of a copy, and that is what you are getting and it is not affection and it is not a reward and it is not going to soften, and if it becomes affection later then that will be a separate entry and it will be dated.”

“All right,” Ilyan said.

“All right,” Sera said, and neither of them said anything else, and the drying line on the lower road creaked twice in the wind, and the count came back at mark on a span propped over a channel with two hands under it and a bell on a post, and out one and a half, and back.

Far off, past the fen road, past the weighhouse, past four bridges and a district that had printed a word it did not understand on a piece of paper it was entitled to print, another span took a load at dusk and answered with a single clear note that came across the water flat and thin, the way a bell comes across water, and it sounded, to two people on a step on the lower road who had spent a month learning to listen to machines, like a page turning over in a room that nobody had ever been let into.
