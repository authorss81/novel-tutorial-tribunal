# Chapter 26: Sera Draws a Boundary

Sera’s room above the lock-and-ledger shop had one table, two chairs, and a rule that had never been spoken because it had never been broken until now: nothing on the table belonged to anybody else.

Tonight the table held a schedule book she was not supposed to have taken out of the records room, a rubbing in a paper sleeve, a printed form with three fields already filled, and a cup of tea that had gone cold while Ilyan talked.

He had been talking for forty minutes. She let him finish, which she had learned was the only way to be sure what he was actually asking for.

He wanted to file. Before the seventh morning bell, in the narrow window between now and the bridge review, he wanted to enter a request that the ninth-bridge record be reopened on the ground that the missing maintenance-review condition had been removed through a process that could be shown to have routed through the same office that had handled her family’s withdrawal request and his own pre-arrival paper. A request to reopen. A request that said: the record has been altered, the alteration is traceable to a process, and therefore the record cannot be relied upon as it stands.

“If they reopen it, the Bench can put the missing term back into the active charter,” Ilyan said. “Not a stay. Not an exception. The term itself, with a review route attached, before the seventh bell. Then the bridge review starts from a charter that is not missing its own maintenance condition, and everything the guild says at the review has a place to stand.”

Sera set down her cup.

“No,” she said.

The word sat in the room and did not get better.

“Give me the reason in order,” she said. “And do not give me the reason in the order you would like me to agree with it.”

“The reason is that we have three documents that do not belong together and a routing code that appears on all three, and if I file the request while the assembly’s limited word still binds nobody from citing my name, the request is not a political instrument, it is a custody record, and it goes in as evidence rather than as an argument.”

“That is the reason in the order you would like me to agree with it,” Sera said. “Try the other one.”

Ilyan looked at the printed form.

“The other one is that the ward seats are empty and the schedule book has a cut margin and the lowland conservancy has withdrawn its citation,” he said. “If I file the reopening and it fails, I have spent my one examination, my name is worth nothing on any schedule in the region, and the districts are free to keep the wage credit. If I file it and it succeeds, the maintenance condition goes back into the charter and the guild gets the term it has been carrying unpaid since the field.”

“And the stay?”

“The stay is derived from the record. A reopened record is not a stayed record.”

Sera opened the schedule book, found a page near the back that was folded and not numbered, and turned it toward him without a word.

The court slip on it was four lines long, and the third line was the one that had ended his plan.

**A REQUEST TO REOPEN AN ACTIVE RECORD BEFORE A SCHEDULED REVIEW SUSPENDS EVERY RELIEF ENTERED UNDER THAT RECORD UNTIL THE REOPENED RECORD IS SETTLED.**
**THE SUSPENSION IS NOT A PUNISHMENT. IT IS THE ONLY WAY THE RELIEF CAN BE STATED HONESTLY.**

“The stay is a relief entered under the record,” Sera said. “If the record is reopened, the stay is suspended. Not by a district. Not by a marshal. By the Bench, as a matter of how a relief is stated, because a relief cannot be enforced out of a record that someone is arguing is not a real record.”

“Until the reopened record is settled,” Ilyan said.

“Until the reopened record is settled,” Sera said. “Which nobody can say, because you do not know what a reopened record asks for, and neither do I, and the Bench has said in writing that it does not name a time.”

He felt it go. He had walked into her room with a three-document chain and a routing code and the distinct, terrible clarity of a man who has finally found the hand, and the hand had turned out to be attached to a door that opened onto a bridge where a child with a bad leg would not be allowed to cross.

“How long is a record settled?”

“That is what reopening means,” Sera said. “It is not a question with a small answer. It is why the district does not reopen records. It is also why they should be able to, and why the reason they cannot is a thing worth changing, and I will fight about that on a day when the family with the bad leg is not standing on it.”

He did not argue. He wanted to, and the wanting was the honest thing, and he let it be honest.

“So no,” he said.

“No,” she said. “Not tonight, and not by that route, and not with my family’s page as the engine of it. You can file a custody record. You can enter the fact that three documents carry one routing code. You cannot file a request whose effect is to suspend the only legal crossing a ten-year-old has, and you cannot do it in a way that spends the archive I have spent nine years keeping in order, because I am not the mechanism you reach for when the road is bad.”

The room was very quiet. Below them, the shop’s shutter-crew were putting up the bars for the night, and the sound of it, metal on wood, went through the floor.

“I have done everything you asked for since a courier fell asleep across my threshold,” Sera said. “I found the empty sleeve. I found the routing request. I found the witness index with the name in it, and the levy ledger with the name cut out of it, and the copy that came in through a court slot at midnight, and the dates that do not run in the order the district needs them to run. Every one of those cost something. I want you to hear what I got for them.”

She put her hand flat on the printed form.

“I got a table with somebody else’s problem on it,” she said. “You arrive, and there is a problem, and you leave with a tool, and I am the place the tool comes from. My grandmother’s well is not a lever for your bridge. The day I let it be a lever is the day my family’s claim becomes a technique, and a technique can be reused by anyone who learns it, including the people who printed that form.”

“I have not used your family —”

“You asked me to use my family’s page to reopen a bridge record and suspend a stay in the same sentence,” Sera said, “and you did not notice you had done it, and if I had said yes tonight instead of this, then within a month somebody would come to this table and say *the recordwright’s family opened the bridge, and here is how you open one*.”

She stood up and put the schedule book under her arm, and then stopped, and looked at the table, and did not leave.

“Sit down,” Ilyan said. “I have an actual task and it is smaller than the one I came with.”

That surprised her enough that she sat.

“Ordinary custody,” Ilyan said. “No villain. Tell me what to verify.”

“That is a better question and it is not a favor,” Sera said. “A favor is when you want the answer to be a person. A question is when you want the answer to be a sequence. I will answer questions at a public archive desk until the hour, and I will answer them in writing if you want them in writing, and I will not answer them for my family.”

“Then the terms are: the family record is yours. I do not read the chain, I do not copy the chain, and I do not enter anything from it in the bridge case without your hand on it.”

“That is correct.”

“And the public bridge and the copied-precedent entries are mine to trace. Every document in the open case, every schedule, every form, every ledger that a district clerk has ever entered. I trace custody. I do not infer.”

“You will infer,” Sera said.

“I will not infer,” Ilyan said. “If custody gives me a sequence, I will read the sequence and I will not put a name at the end of it that the sequence does not hold up.”

Sera looked at him for a moment and then nodded, once, which was the entire negotiation and had taken her four days and one ruined evening.

“Then here is the first question,” she said, “and it is the only one I have. A district maintenance update replaces the affected-party column in the working book and retains the old version in the file. The old version is retained. Where does a retained version live?”

“The file.”

“Whose file?”

“The district’s. The maintenance office’s.”

“The office is in a rented room off the weighbridge and it has a strongbox with a district seal and a lock that the district has never once had re-keyed,” Sera said. “So the retained version is a piece of paper in a strongbox in a rented room, entered by a clerk at an hour of his choosing, with a version number, and no copy anywhere else. That is a chain of custody that is nine steps long and every step belongs to the same office.”

“That is not a violation.”

“It is not a violation,” Sera agreed. “It is also a place where a person can be made to disappear without anyone touching them, because the only place that says a household refused is a paper in a room the household cannot reach, and the paper that says what the crews will actually do is a page in a book the household also cannot reach, and the two papers are allowed to be different.”

She opened the schedule book to the update line and turned it so the lamp was over it.

The line was routine. Date, time, version three to version four, the clerk’s name, the signature, and a small notation in the margin that any update carried and that no one had ever read aloud.

**ROUTING: C—2 / BRIDGE PLATE OFFICE. PRINT RUN: 114.**

“The code is the same,” Ilyan said. “And the second half of the code is the office that does not exist under that name.”

“It is the plate office that used to certify bridge working plates and is now a routing desk inside the maintenance office,” Sera said. “It has no clerk of its own. It has a stamp.” She looked at the line. “And the print run. That is the part I want you to have, and I want you to write it down in your own book, not mine.”

“The print run is the form.”

“The print run is a form printed in a batch, by a public print shop, on the court’s own stock, on an authority to print,” Sera said. “Print run one hundred and fourteen. If you go to the print shop with a request for the authorization for run one hundred and fourteen, you will get one of three things. Either the run was authorized by a named person, or it was authorized by an office with no name, or the page in the stock ledger for that run has been cut out and replaced, and the replacement carries the same routing code with the second half missing, which is what happened to the margin on the slate.”

Ilyan wrote it down. Date, run, code, three possible answers. He did not write what he thought the third answer was, because he had promised, and because he had learned what the promise was for.

“And the district office keeps the authority to print,” he said. “The maintenance clerk. Vale.”

“The maintenance clerk holds the standing print authority of the office and applies it to routine forms,” Sera said. “There is no run one hundred and fifteen on that form. The form has been printed once, in a batch, and distributed, and then the office has used the same printed sheet for weeks, which means the form is not a form. It is a copy with a distribution list.”

“Which means somebody wrote the household into a copy of a form before there was a schedule to write it into.”

“Which means somebody wrote a household into a copy of a form, distributed it, and then found a schedule that would take it,” Sera said. “And I cannot tell you whether the household was printed because of the schedule, or the schedule was made to take the household, because the authorization for the print run would tell me, and the authorization is on the page that has been cut.”

She closed the book.

“That is the entire chain, and it is nine steps, and every step is somebody doing a job,” she said. “A form printed on court stock. A run authorized. A sheet distributed to a maintenance office. A standing print authority applied to a weekly schedule. An update entered at an hour with a signature on the seal. A column replaced. A version retained in a strongbox. A half-hand credited against a guild’s wage bill every morning. And a household that has been paying for a bridge with its own footsteps since the night of the stay, and did not know, and would have been told it had consented.”

“Where do we go tomorrow?” Ilyan asked.

“Tomorrow morning I go to the print shop with a public request for run one hundred and fourteen’s authorization,” Sera said. “That is one line, it is a custody question, and nobody has to lose anything by answering it. Tomorrow afternoon you go to the workroom and find out how many pages the district’s working book has lost, and whether any of them were refusals, and whether the ones that are left agree with the ward’s copy. Those are two lines. And neither of them reopens the record and neither of them is my family.”

“And the code?”

“The code goes in your book as an incomplete mark with two locations,” Sera said, “and you do not put it in the bridge case, and you do not put it in my family’s case, and you do not say the words *continuity voice* in either, because we have two instances of an incomplete code and no speaker, and a person who says *voice* before she has an office is guessing with the volume up.”

She put the cup of cold tea in the sink, and stood with her back to him for a moment, and Ilyan understood that he was being given the end of the evening and not an invitation to extend it.

“Sera.”

She turned.

“I was going to spend the review on the chain,” he said. “You stopped me.”

“Yes,” she said. “And tomorrow you will find out how much of a fool that would have been, and it will not be as much as I think, and that is not a compliment to either of us. Sit down. I will read you the print shop’s address and the clerk’s hours, and then you are going to go home and sleep, because you have a bridge review in three days and a household that sat four rows back and refused to look at you, and both of those are easier to survive rested.”

He sat down. She read him the address and the hours. When he was at the door she said one more thing, in the flat voice she used for things she had decided to say exactly once.

“If they had asked you to reopen the record,” she said, “and I had said yes, and Tamsin had lost the crossing for two years over a form printed in a print shop, then I would have done that. And you would have won an argument. And I would have made a technique out of my grandmother’s well, and I would have had to live in the room where I had done it.”

The stair creaked under him. Below, the last shutter went up, and the street went quiet in the way that a street goes quiet when a building that has been keeping other people’s secrets all day finally stops.

Three documents carried one code. The second half of the code was gone from all of them, cut or scraped, by a person or a process that was doing exactly what it had been built to do.

And the household that had been paying for a bridge in footsteps was asleep, eleven streets away, in a room with a provisional lease and a landlord’s book and a girl with a bad leg who had sent nothing at all to the examination, because nothing was the correct answer and she had chosen it before anyone gave it to her.
