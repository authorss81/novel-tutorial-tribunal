# Chapter 7: The Field Around the Bridge

The first pale boundary cut across the lower approach, climbing the bridge’s western support in a crooked stair. A second line crossed the lower channel. Between them, the air looked darker, as if the night had been folded into a sheet and laid flat. The wind still moved the canvas above Tamsin’s head, but the bridge’s warning bell did not ring.

Ilyan watched the boundary instead of the span. He had learned to trust the place where a system changed shape more than the first dramatic symptom.

Tarin stood just outside the line with two guards. “No one enters without a witness duty or a named crew,” he said. His voice carried in the local language, but Ilyan understood the command from its repeated shape and from the way the guards moved.

Sera remained at the public edge with a slate. She had not entered the field. “The court is testing the charter and the maintenance record,” she called. “The Ro household may remain outside. Tamsin may answer from the boundary. Neris may answer for herself. Oren’s crew is named for the load path.”

The clerk read the advocate line aloud: Sera Quill, limited advocate for the Ro household’s access request; Ilyan Vester, external witness under the accepted filing. Sera had not resumed sponsorship of Ilyan’s claim, but she had accepted the household’s limited request.

The court clerk raised a black tablet. “The field records the current charter, the submitted maintenance record, and the behavior of the bridge. An unlisted person inside the boundary may be treated as an unlicensed alteration.”

Tarin immediately moved to the line. “That is not a safety order. It is a conclusion.”

“It is the court’s standing rule,” the clerk said.

“Where is it written?”

“The rule is entered in the field notice.”

The System translated the notice for Ilyan.

**AN UNLISTED PERSON INSIDE THE FIELD MAY VOID THE CHALLENGE.**
**THE FIELD TESTS ACTIVE RECORDS. IT DOES NOT REWRITE AN ARCHIVE PLATE.**
**BENCH RULING FOLLOWS THE RECORDS PRESENTED, NOT THE SYSTEM’S DESCRIPTION OF THEM.**
**PLAYABLE: A RULE SET PERMITS PREDICTABLE ACTION AND MEANINGFUL RESPONSE.**

Harrow explained the last term when Ilyan looked at it. “Playable is the court’s technical word for a rule that can be tested and answered. It is not a judgment that the world belongs to you.”

Before Harrow finished, a thinner line flickered beneath the formal notice: **Continuity preserves the record before remedy.** It arrived in the same cadence Ilyan had heard during the crossing, and for a moment he could not tell whether the court had added it or something inside the field had.

He looked for the familiar shape of a choice. None appeared. The field was not offering him a menu.

Tarin lifted a hand to the guards. “Make a neutral perimeter. Nobody crosses the line except the named crew, the affected witnesses, and the person holding the witness duty. If the crowd pushes, hold them outside. Do not call it a lawful crossing.”

Harrow stood on the far side of the field with the recorder. He had changed into a black court coat, and a pale seal at his throat marked him as a Trialwright rather than an advocate. A second seal marked the recorder’s authority to replace an active working plate.

Ilyan crossed the boundary. The stone beneath his shoes warmed, then cooled. His headache returned, dull this time, as if the field were taking an inventory of the body inside it.

Harrow watched him. “You are within the witness duty.”

“I am.”

“The field does not grant you authority.”

“I know.”

“It also does not protect you from the burden of being present.”

That sounded more like a warning than a ruling. Ilyan kept his hands away from the bridge.

Oren entered with his crew. Jessa, Mero, and Puck followed, each carrying a tool or a marked cord. The eastern runner came next, clutching the petition. Neris and Tamsin stopped at the edge, where the clerk had placed two low stools. Tamsin kept her injured leg extended. She had chosen the edge, not the field.

The bridge seemed to recognize the witnesses. A fine vibration moved through the western support. The pale boundary brightened along its length.

The crowd outside the line grew louder. A man in a brown coat pointed at Tamsin. “She is the reason the span turned.”

Another voice answered, “The register is the reason.”

“The register kept us alive.”

“It kept the span’s count.”

“Your count is not worth my child’s road.”

Tarin moved toward the rope, but the crowd surged. Two guards caught a woman trying to cross. She did not fight them. She simply held up a small bundle of cloth and said, “My son is on the other side. He cannot hear the warning.”

The bell’s absence made the argument louder. People could see a field that tested a bridge, but they could not hear the bridge answer.

Ilyan looked at the active records. The court’s certified current plate hung in a black frame near the public table. Its clean surface carried the bridge charter with no line about reviewing the load after the count returned. The saltglass plate was in Oren’s crew kit, wrapped in oilcloth, ready to be presented as a disputed witness.

The bridge’s actual load moved before either plate was coupled. The lower support drew a thin line of dust toward the unregistered edge. The pale boundary narrowed around Tamsin’s stool.

Harrow stepped forward. “Remove the unlisted witness from the field.”

“She is at the boundary,” Ilyan said.

“She is inside the rule’s test area.”

“Your rule says unlisted person, not unlisted child.”

“Do not turn a technical distinction into permission.”

Harrow pointed to the recorder. “Mark the field observation. The witness is unlisted.”

Tarin’s hand went to the blade at his hip, not drawing it. “You want to remove a child because the rule cannot hold both facts at once.”

“The rule must be tested before it is honored,” Harrow said. “Otherwise every claimant chooses the version that gives them access.”

Neris stood. “I choose the boundary. I choose to keep her there.”

“You are also unlisted.”

“Then mark both of us.”

A murmur went through the crowd. The field brightened at the word both. Ilyan saw the bridge’s response before the System could describe it: the dust-line split into two paths, one toward the stools and one toward the eastern petition. The charter was trying to place responsibility on a person rather than on the maintenance condition.

He turned to Tarin. “Can you make a neutral perimeter without treating the boundary as a crossing?”

“Give me a line I can defend.”

Ilyan pointed to the pale marks already on the paving. “Use the field’s line. No one crosses it except the named crew and the witnesses who choose to enter. If a guard moves a person, record the reason and the person who gave it.”

Tarin looked at Harrow. “Do you object to a neutral observation line?”

Harrow’s jaw tightened. “I object to any arrangement that lets an outsider direct a court field.”

“I am not directing the field. I am asking the route-arbiter to keep people from turning a hearing into a crowd.”

Tarin raised two fingers. The guards moved. One faced the eastern district, one faced the bridgekeepers, and a third stood beside the Ro stools. The line became a place where people could speak without becoming a load.

Ilyan walked toward the western support. The field tightened around him. The dust-line followed his steps, then stopped when he did.

“What happens if I touch the span?” he asked Oren.

“The record may take your body as the disputed condition,” Oren said. “The field could use you to prove the bridge’s behavior.”

“Or it could collapse.”

“Or both.”

Ilyan placed his palm on the lower brace. The metal was cold through the dust. A hard vibration ran through the stone, and a hairline of dust appeared on the far side of the brace where no second load was visible. It slid toward the current plate, stopped, and fell.

He withdrew his hand. His stomach turned.

Sera called from outside, “What did you see?”

“A second load response.”

“Record it as a load response, not a claim about yourself.”

“I can record it without calling it mine.”

“Then do that. You are not licensed to make the record say more than it saw.”

Harrow’s recorder approached the court plate. “The old maintenance plate is a disputed witness,” he said. “The current certified duplicate is the active working record. We must continue with the certified record or suspend the field.”

“Swap the plate,” Ilyan said.

Harrow looked at him. “You are asking the court to remove evidence.”

“I am asking the court to stop treating a disputed record as if it were the only record. If the old plate is wrong, the discrepancy matters. If the current plate is wrong, the bridge may be unsafe.”

“The Bench will test both.”

A formal notice appeared above the field boundary.

**ACTIVE PLATE: CURRENT CERTIFIED DUPLICATE.**
**DISPUTED PLATE: MAINTENANCE SALTGLASS COPY.**
**REMEDY: CONTINUE WITH THE CURRENT RECORD, OR SUSPEND THE FIELD AND RETURN THE RECORDS TO THE CLERK.**

Ilyan could not read the local choice, but the structure was clear. Harrow’s hand moved to the recorder.

“Continue,” he said.

The recorder lifted the current plate from its frame. For a moment the bridge’s vibration became regular. The crowd fell silent. Even the dust stopped moving.

Then the recorder reached for the old saltglass plate.

Ilyan stepped forward. “If you remove the old plate, the field will only know the current text.”

“The old plate is being preserved in the court locker,” Harrow said. “The field cannot test an object held outside its active boundary.”

“Can the court hear that it exists?”

“It can hear the record of its preservation.”

“Not the sentence.”

“No.”

Oren looked at him. “Do not let them put the sentence away before the bridge hears it.”

Harrow’s voice hardened. “The court is not a performance for the guild’s comfort.”

“The court is a performance,” Ilyan said. “That is why people are being hurt inside it.”

The recorder placed the current plate into the frame and sealed the old plate in a black case. The case closed with a heavy click.

The field changed at once.

The bridge lurched upward. The lower brace bent, and the boundary contracted toward the support. Tamsin cried out. Neris pulled her stool back as the pale line snapped across the paving.

“Clear the field!” Tarin shouted.

The guards moved, but not everyone obeyed. The crowd pushed forward. The eastern runner dropped the petition. Oren’s crew caught the lower cord and hauled the weighted beam into place. Ilyan reached for the brace, then stopped himself before touching the span.

The field could use his body. If he became the disputed condition, the court would have a new witness and the bridge a new burden.

Harrow stood at the edge, face pale. “The record is active.”

“Show the missing line,” Ilyan called. “Show what the bridge is carrying.”

“The current record has no such line.”

“Read the active record.”

“It is a certified current charter.”

“Read it.”

Harrow looked at the recorder. The recorder looked at the black case holding the old plate.

“Continue the field,” Harrow said. “We will preserve the discrepancy for review.”

The field held for a moment longer. The bridge stopped rising, but the boundary remained tight around the support. When the final court bell closed the announced one-bell window, the pale lines vanished all at once.

The night did not return to normal. The bridge remained braced and intact, but the court’s working plate now carried only the current charter. The maintenance-review language was absent from the record in front of the court.

The saltglass original was sealed in the locker beyond the boundary.

Tarin lowered his guard’s stance. “Is the field closed?”

Harrow checked the tablet. “Closed.”

“And the maintenance exception?”

“Expired.”

Oren leaned on his tool. “The bridge is safe for now.”

“Until the next count,” Tarin said.

Oren looked at the empty third mark on the iron ring. “Yes.”

Ilyan stood on the public side of the boundary with dust on his hand and a headache behind his eyes. He had asked for a hearing that would test the discrepancy. Instead, the court had removed the discrepancy from the working field and preserved it somewhere he could not reach.

Sera’s voice came through the dark. “The original is preserved,” she said. “That is a fact. It is not the same as a remedy.”

Tamsin sat on the edge of the shelter, her face turned toward the bridge. “Did they hear the old plate?”

Ilyan looked at the black locker, then at the current plate in its frame.

“They heard that it existed,” he said. “I do not know whether they heard what it said.”

The next morning would decide that. For now, the bridge stood, the child was alive, and the court had begun keeping a record that could make the next failure look different from the first.

Ilyan did not sleep. When the sky lightened, he went back to the workroom and found the court’s working plate already waiting there, clean and current.

The old words were gone.
