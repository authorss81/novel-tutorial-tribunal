# Chapter 43: The Three, Said Out Loud Before the Argument

Harrow did not let anybody argue for an hour, which was the first thing he did that surprised the room and the second thing he did that Ilyan understood, and the understanding arrived about a minute after the ruling did.

“Before any of you says a word about which of the three you prefer,” Harrow said, from the middle of the span, with Roke behind him and the tray out, “this court is going to hear the burden of each one, said by the person who objects to it, and not by the person who proposes it. That is not a procedure of this court. I have invented it this morning, as I invented another thing on a bridge a fortnight ago and said so at the time, and the district may object to the innovation and the objection will be preserved.”

He held up three fingers and did not look at them.

“One. Amend the charter. The objection to it is that it puts an undefined burden on districts that are not in this room.”

“Objection,” Dain said, immediately, and it was the district's whole case in four words and he had been carrying it in a coat for a fortnight.

“Two. Break the span. The objection to it is that it removes the only lawful route to housing and work for a household in the register.”

“Objection,” Sera said.

“Three. Hold the hearing open. The objection to it is that it is what has been happening for nineteen days and it has a price that is being paid on a lower road by a woman with a knee and a boy who cannot be on a ladder.”

“Objection,” Joren said, from the road party's stake, and nobody had expected the road party to have a voice in a court and Tarin had put him on the witness list eleven days ago for exactly this and looked like a man who had bet a cart on it.

“Now the arguments,” Harrow said, “and I want each of them made by somebody who believes it, and I do not want a single person in this room to be able to tell me afterwards that a remedy was carried into this room by a man who had a reason to be carrying it.”

---

Maren Kett argued for the amendment, and he argued it with a mandate in his hand that he did not read from, because he had read it four times on the road and could have said it from memory, and the fact that he did not read it was a piece of theatre and Ilyan noted it and did not object.

“Kett, eastern routes, and I am speaking for a written instruction that says *one legible rule*,” he said, “and I will tell you why that instruction exists, because it is not vanity. This city has had two regimes on one bridge for three weeks. It has had a district schedule, a guild arrangement, a court stay, a register entry with a verification column that has no date in it, and a frame with a date chalked on it in letters you can read from a cart seat. Any one of those is a rule. Together they are a situation. And a situation is what a clerk has to survive at four in the morning with nobody to ask, and I have watched a good clerk break this month and I am not going to name her.”

Nobody said anything, which was the response Kett had wanted and had not expected to get.

“Amend it,” he said. “One rule, one condition, one keeper with a power, one review, one page, and every district that touches a bridge of this class adopts the same wording, and then a carter knows the rule on the ninth bridge and the tenth and the eleventh, and a clerk fills in one column instead of seven, and this case becomes something that happened once instead of something that has to be explained to every child on every road in a year.”

“And the burden,” Harrow said.

“The burden is the maintenance, and the maintenance is what it has always been, and the moment you write *a keeper may stop a load* into a common clause then every district in this region has a clause that says a man with a bell can take a carter's afternoon, and no district in this region has voted for that, and two of them cannot even produce their own maintenance terms — which, Mr Prenn, you told me at the weighhouse, and which is the only reason your draft eleven is sitting unadopted on a table in a room with no door.”

“Then who pays the undefined burden,” Harrow said, “and I am asking the question because the record has to answer it and the record is going to be made out of what is said on this span, and not one of you has said it in three weeks.”

“The districts near the bridge pay the maintenance,” Kett said.

“The districts near the bridge,” Oren said, from the maintenance line, without turning round, “have four bridges between them, and one of those four is on a different water, and two of them are on districts that could not produce their own maintenance terms at a conference a week ago because somebody took the page off a shelf and put it in a cold room, and the fourth has a keeper nobody has ever asked to write down an hour. Name the districts. Name the four. Name who in each of the four is going to ring the bell, and where the money for the hour comes from, and I will stop arguing with you in the rest of my life.”

Kett opened his mouth, and closed it, and then said the sentence that made him still a man worth having in a room four months from now.

“I cannot name them,” he said. “And I would like the court to record that I said so, because I have argued for this amendment in two rooms this week on the grounds that a common rule is better than a situation, and I would like it on the record that when you ask me who pays for a common rule, I have to guess, and that I have been arguing for a guess since the weighhouse.”

“Entered,” Roke said.

And Dain, who had been standing at the rope with his arms folded and a face that had not changed for three weeks, said, “Then you have described my second objection and mine is older and cheaper than yours, and I would like to say it in a room where Mr Kett has just said the word *guess* on the record.”

“Entered,” Harrow said, with the faintest possible movement at the corner of his mouth, which Ilyan had not seen before and did not see again.

---

Dain argued for breaking the span, and it was the argument that made the crowd go quiet, because it was the argument of a man who had wanted to win for three weeks and had decided that winning was worth less than a bridge.

“I am going to say the thing nobody wants,” he said, “and Mr Vester has already told the ward the sentence that makes it easier to hear, so I am going to use his sentence and not pretend it was mine. The span's maintenance is the district's decision. It is not mine. The district built the span, the district's council decided what the span was for, the district wrote the charter and the district's own clerk has written in a public book that the office has no power here. There is no mechanism by which this court, or any court, can make a district maintain a span. It can be *asked* to. It has been asked, in a lane, and in an office, and in a room with a forge in it, and the answer has been no every time, and the answer is correct, and the reason the answer is correct is that the district does not have money and cannot be made to want it by a foreign record-holder with a good argument.”

“And the empty seat,” Harrow said.

“The empty seat is the whole of my case and it is why I am not enjoying this,” Dain said. “I have been on this span three weeks telling anybody who would listen that a bridge that cannot be stopped is cheaper to maintain, and I have been right about that, and the west seat has now proved me right in the worst possible way. It is not wearing. It is being emptied by water out of a channel that moved six years ago, and it borrows up to three fingers of itself on a heavy load and gives most of it back, and the fix is three weeks' cure that this case does not have and this district cannot buy, and the stone for it is sitting at a yard off the fen road at the district's expense because there is no instrument to send a cart.”

He took the folded paper out of his coat and this time he did not unfold it, and put it back.

“Break the span,” he said, “and the charter survives, and the missing clause becomes an interesting question for the next four bridges instead of a reason to hold a broken bridge open, and the district is not asked to fund a power it did not create, and no keeper in this city ever has to explain to a carter why an hour of his afternoon went somewhere. That is a good outcome. It is a good outcome for this city. I have thought about it for four days and it is the best case I have and I am going to say it out loud on a public bridge.”

“Now the rest of it,” Sera said. “Say the part where you take it away from a woman who has to cross it at six.”

“I am not going to say the part where I take it away,” Dain said. “I am going to say the part where I am *asked* to, and I am going to say that I will accept it on two conditions, and both of them are the district's conditions and not mine, and I have been carrying them since the fourth day and I have not produced them because the district is not entitled to produce them in a room where the charter is in dispute.”

“Say them.”

“First: a crossing for every household that loses this one, at a named route and a named cost, before the span comes down, and not after.” Dain's voice did not change at all, which was how Ilyan knew it cost him something. “There are four crossings above this one. The fourth is a bridge and it is a toll bridge and it is eleven minutes longer and it has a toll on it, and the toll is not the district's and the district cannot move the toll, and I have been to the man who keeps it and I have told him what this case is about and he has told me what his toll is, and his toll is what his toll is, and I am not going to stand here and pretend the fourth bridge is a gift.

“Second: the ward's water and the lower road's shelter and the pump stay exactly as they are, and none of it is traded for the span.”

“Neris Ro,” Sera said.

“The household is on the fourth bridge at the toll and at the shelter with a sworn book and at the dye-yard with a work record, or the span does not come down,” Dain said. “And I would like the court to hear me say that I have put the toll in my own mouth on purpose, because I am the enforcing officer and I do not get to decide what a toll is, and if the toll is the reason the span stays up then this chamber has a toll in it, and everyone in this room should know whose money it is.”

“It is ours,” Neris said, from the lower edge, in a voice with nothing in it. “It has been ours for two years. You are the first person in three weeks to say it out loud and I would like it read back to me before the end of the day.”

---

Joren argued for holding the hearing open, and he argued it by naming a roof, and the room did not like it, and it was the only argument in the three that was not about law at all.

“Do not let it be closed today,” he said. “That is all I am saying. Do not let it be closed today, because holding it open is what has happened for nineteen days and I have watched what it costs, and I am the one who has watched it, and I am going to be the man who says the thing that everybody in this room thinks is a confession.”

He had the frame's words in his hand, not read from anything, and he had them in the cart driver's lettering because he had copied them off a board with chalk.

“*From dusk to the fourth morning bell and no longer.*” He said it slowly. “That is a sentence about a road and it is also, every single evening, a sentence that a carrier reads before he puts his load on it. Every evening since the frame went up, a man has driven up the fen road with a roof on a cart and read that and turned round, and gone east, and the roof has gone on a house with two pairs of hands, and there are eleven houses on the lower road with one pair of hands and one of those houses has a boy who cannot be on a ladder, and their name is Marda Rennick and I am not going to say it again in a room because the foreign record-holder said it once on the public approach and it was not his to say.”

“Then say the number,” Harrow said. “Do not make this about a person. Make it about an hour.”

“Three days of it with a date on the frame, and a carrier reading it before he loads,” Joren said. “Three deliveries. Nine bundles. Eight roofs, and the ninth on a house that got it from a neighbour at dusk, from a man on a ladder, on the second evening, and I have written all nine in the ward's book because the district's book has no line in it for a roof and the ward is keeping its own now, and nobody has ever asked the district to confirm a single one of them because the district has no copy of anything to confirm against.”

“So the third remedy is the one that has been costing the household its roof,” Harrow said.

“The third remedy is the one that has been costing the lower road its roof since the seventh morning bell,” Joren said, “and the district is going to tell you the district has no obligation, and that is true, and the district is also the reason there is no obligation, and both of those are the same sentence and I have watched a clerk try to write them as two lines so that a court could have one of them without the other.”

He put the paper down.

“Do not close it today,” he said. “Whatever you rule, rule something with a day on it that a carrier can read. A ruling that says *amended* with no date at the bottom is a ruling a carrier reads as *ended*, and a carrier is not a lawyer and neither am I, and I have never once in my life been able to write anything that a man on a cart seat could not read, and that has been the only good thing I have ever done with a pen.”

Nobody argued after that, and the span was quiet for a long moment, and in the quiet the count came back from a light load that had gone over without anybody noticing, and Ilsa said, “Out one and a half, and back, and the half.”

And Harrow turned to Oren.

“Mr Sedge. The court has three remedies and no physical witness. The guild ordered itself to produce a costed pause proposal, and the district has offered a money, and a witness account is in a guild's book under five terms, and I am not going to rule on a bridge from a slate at dusk. I want the span to be shown to this court while it is under load.”

“The span is under load,” Oren said. “There were three light loads promised by the cart book and two have gone over.”

“Then I want four,” Harrow said, “and I want a keeper to read the joint while the fourth is standing on the west end, and I want the count read aloud, and I want the whole of it in a field so that the record is a record of the bridge and not of anybody's memory of it.”

He looked at the frame, at the rope, at the wheel on its worn pin.

“And when the keeper is satisfied, or not satisfied, I want the bell rung,” Harrow said, “in front of this court, so that the court can hear what a warning on this bridge sounds like before it writes a word about one.”

Oren Sedge walked out to the middle of the deck, and stopped, and looked west, at the place where the joint is, which is not where anybody standing on a bridge thinks it is.

“Coll,” he said. “Book. Puck, get under with the rule. Ilsa, the beam. And somebody go and ask the eastern approach whether there is a load with a road behind it, because I am going to want one that cannot turn round.”
