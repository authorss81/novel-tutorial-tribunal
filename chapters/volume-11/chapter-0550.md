# Chapter 550: The Sixth Day Of The Hundredth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which The Whole Of What Has Happened In This Town In A Hundred And Forty-One Days Is Read Out In A Room With The Door Open As A List And Not As A Finding, And A Class Is Named Out Loud, And A Man Of Eighty-One Asks Out Loud Whether A Person Of The Record And Not Of The Place Can Become A Person Of The Place, And Nobody Answers Him, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says The Four Words He Came To This Country Carrying, And A Woman Writes Them Down For The First Time In A Hundred And Forty-One Days, And Reads Them Back To Him In The Doorway Before She Shuts The Cupboard

Sixth day of the hundredth week. His three hundredth morning. One hundred and fifty days after the settlement. The fever thirty-eight weeks and one day. One hundred and four days since the division. Fifty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The room was the chancel, eight feet by eleven, with the door open and the window down, and the book was in the vestry cupboard on the key on a string until about the half hour after the eleventh.

About nine people went in. There was nothing on the table, because there had been nothing on the table on the Sunday of the sixth day of the ninety-ninth week either, and the man of thirty-four from four days off noticed that and the woman of about forty from the wool bench did not.

A man of eighty-one was in the chair by the fire and had not been sent for and had not been told. A woman of sixty-eight of the upper row. A woman of about forty at the wool bench. A woman of about fifty-one of this town. A man of about thirty-four from a town four days off, who has been in this country twenty-one days.

---

The keeper of the book said what it was before she did it, standing at the door with the key in her hand, and she said it to the room and not to the man of thirty-one.

“*That is the whole of what has happened in this town in a hundred and forty-one days. I am going to read it out as a list and not as a finding, and I have said in this room three weeks ago what the difference is, and nobody has asked me since and nobody needed to.*”

“*Say the difference again, because there are about nine people in here and four of them have heard it and about five have not.*”

“*A finding is a thing entered about a person and the person is not asked. A list is a thing said out loud in a room with the door open, and nobody has been asked any of the people it is about, and that is not an accident of this morning, that is what a list is.*”

The woman of about forty from the wool bench understood all of that. About nine people in that room heard a woman say *finding* and thought of a court.

---

Then she read it out, standing in the voice she uses for minutes, and it took about eleven minutes, and it had no heading at the top of it and nothing was ruled.

“*A leaf of forty-three names was struck through in one stroke a hundred and forty-one years back. The ink is heavier than any other in this book. The four words name no order and no day. The household on it was the household of Kiln Row, and it gave the answer it was asked for, and the answer was made use of.*”

“*The leaf was read out once, in this room, in about eleven minutes, in front of about nine people, on the fifth day of the ninety-fifth week, and about a hundred and eighty-one people heard nothing at all. Four people who are descended from four of the forty-three went up a step in a square and struck the names they are kin to out of a copy of it with their own hands, and about a hundred and ninety people watched, and nobody asked them whether they meant it.*”

“*A claim was entered at the back of this book in respect of a half-acre at the bottom of Kiln Lane and of the fold and of the ground behind this building, by a weaver of sixty-two who is the last of a name on that leaf. Nothing is carried. The leaf is not struck back. Two objections stand at the foot of it and both people have been told.*”

“*A finding was entered with the day a woman of sixty-eight was told on it and not the day it was made, and she said no to two thirds of it, and that went in as well.*”

“*Two accounts of the morning of the thirtieth day after the division, which was a Wednesday and was seventy-four days back from today, were read out in this square one after the other before about two hundred people. The square decided nothing. About four of the nine who heard both can say where they stopped fitting, and nobody has made them fit since.*”

“*A man of about seventy-two was asked one question in this room on the Thursday of the ninety-seventh week and said he did not remember, and was asked nothing for twenty days. On Wednesday he was asked the question he had asked to be asked about, and he said no, and it is entered. It is the second thing in four hundred and eleven years that records a person declining something, and the keeper of this book said out loud in a porch that it is not a form, not a practice and not a thing anybody else may do.*”

“*A return was made in an office nine miles off about the disagreement between two towns. It records two answers and the form has no line for two, so it is on the back, and a man of fifty-four signed the back and not the front, and it is in a drawer in a house nine miles from here and there is no form in that county that says it may be asked for.*”

“*Four pages came four hundred miles off into a town two days from here. A woman of about fifty-one of this town was in a square there on the thirty-ninth day after the division and wrote them out of her own memory in about four minutes of sitting.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*She has had them in two rooms over a milliner’s for about a year and nobody knew. On the Friday of the ninety-eighth week she asked in a lane for them to be looked at by about nine people one at a time, and about four of the nine could say how they were made and not one of them could say whether they are true.*”

“*A copy of a copy of those four pages came down the lane on a cart, in a quicker hand, with about nine mistakes in it and none of them corrected. Four lines at the foot of its third page are not on the copy underneath it. A name is at the foot of its fourth page in a third hand.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*That name was entered at the foot of that page on the seventh and last day of the ninety-ninth week, with the day on it, as the name of a person who was asked a question in a square. The person named was told, and was asked nothing, and said nothing, and the keeper of this book entered four words, which is that he said nothing.*”

About four of the nine people in that room had not heard any of that before and about nine had heard all of it.

---

Then she read the last of it and she read it slower, and it was nine lines and they were the same nine lines that went into a square on the Thursday before last.

“*That a person is told what was decided about them. That a person is asked before a thing is entered about them. That a person is asked what they remember. That a person may say no and have it entered. That a thing a person said is not a thing the town said. That a record has a day on it. That a keeper can be asked whether the keeping is enough. That a thing held by one person is held by one person. And that nobody in a county of about two thousand four hundred people can be sent for about anything else.*”

“*Those are nine lines and about nine people in a square can repeat one of them and about a hundred and ninety-one heard a woman say them for the first time and not one of them is in a book.*”

“*That is the list. It is a list. It is not a finding and it is not a decision and nobody has been asked any of the people it is about.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said it three times now in a morning and there are about nine people in this room and one of them has not been asked anything in a hundred and fifty days.*”

“*It is a list and I am not going to convert a list into a finding by saying the word, and I said that in this room three weeks ago and the man who asked me to convert it is in this room and he asked me a different thing this morning and I will come to it.*”

---

Then she named the class out loud, and she did it standing at the door with the key in her hand, and about nine people in that room heard the two words and about four of them knew which two words.

“*And there is one more thing in this county and it is nine miles off and I cannot be sent for about it.*”

About four seconds went past in that room, and the woman of about fifty-one counted the four walls of it and stopped at four.

“*On the fourth day of the ninety-ninth week a man of fifty-four of the county opened a form folded in four in a porch in front of about nine people, and read out an entry out of the book of orders at Aldwick. That entry puts a man of thirty-one into a class by himself, with a name over the class, and the name is an external witness, and the entry says that a person at that office may be asked about the matters on which that person came, and may make a return in respect of him in the ordinary form.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because about four people in this room heard you say a name and about nine heard a man being entered in a book.*”

“*An external witness is a person of the record and not of the place. That is what the word means. He is in a book in Aldwick and he can be asked about by a clerk, and no person in this county can ask him anything, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what a man has to do to stop being one.*”

She put the key on the table.

“*And a heading is a promise about what goes in it, and I have said that in a porch in front of about nine people and about four of them understood it and about nine did not, and it is now the whole of what this county has learned in five weeks.*”

The woman of about fifty-one understood all of that and did not ask one question about it, which is what she has done for about a year. About nine people in that room heard a woman say *record*.

---

Then Barnaby Rill said the thing from the chair, and nobody had sent for him, and it was a question and it was asked out loud in a room with the door open.

“*Ask him.*”

Nobody moved in that room for about four seconds and somebody took his weight on a chair at the back of it.

“*I am asking whether a person of the record and not of the place can become a person of the place. That is the whole of it. I am eighty-one and I have been that person twice in this town and said nothing both times. There is no form in this county that lets anybody ask it, and so I am asking it here.*”

Nobody in that room answered him.

The man of thirty-four from four days off had understood that it had been asked. About nine people in that room had heard a man of eighty-one ask a question out loud and had not worked out what the question was.

“*Say it out loud, then, so that about nine people in this room know what has just been asked and has not been answered.*”

She said it out loud. She said it in the voice she uses for minutes, standing at the door with the key on the table in front of her.

“*Can an external person become a citizen of a county without being taken into the shape of the thing the county tells about itself. That is the question. It was asked in this room by a man of eighty-one who was not sent for, and it has not been answered.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I am not going to answer it. I am the only person in this county anybody can be sent for, and I have been asked one question this morning, and it was not that one.*”

Nobody in that room answered it then and nobody answered it afterwards. About four of the nine understood that nobody was going to. About nine noticed that nobody had.

---

Then the man of thirty-one said the sentence he had come to this country carrying. He said it out loud in that room in front of about nine people. About four of them knew which sentence it was, where he got it and who first said it to him, and about nine did not, and nobody stopped him.

“*A book is a door.*”

He said it standing at the end of the room with his leg out at the angle he stands it at.

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said the recordwright.

“*It is not the same sentence. A woman in a room over a taproom in a town two days off said it to me on the thirty-eighth day after the division, and you struck it out of a sheet with a line through the middle of it and stopped me before the second word.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I have said it in about nine rooms since. It has cost me a bed, a leg that is worse than it was, and six refusals in ten days.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds, and about nine people in it heard a man say the sentence about a door and did not know that they had heard it before.

“*I know what it is for,*” she said. “*I struck it out on a Tuesday in a porch in about four seconds. I said in that porch that the half of it about nine of us heard was a man from another county talking about a town he is not of, and I am not going to argue you out of the half you said this morning.*”

Then she did the thing that had not happened in a hundred and forty-one days, and about nine people in that room watched her do it.

She took the pen off the table and put the book on it, and she did not open it at the front.

She wrote at the back of that book, on a fresh leaf, in the ordinary hand, with the day on it, ruling nothing and heading nothing. She wrote nine words, and about four of the nine people in that room counted them.

Then she read them back to him in the doorway, standing, with the book in her hand and not shut, and about nine people heard all nine.

“*Entered on the sixth day of the hundredth week, in a room with the door open, in front of about nine people, at the back of this book and not in any of its four columns: that a person not of this county said out loud in this room the four words *a book is a door*, which he has said in about nine rooms since he came here, and that he came here not knowing what a citizen was, and that he has been refused six times in ten days and has understood every one of them.*”

“*That is nine lines and not eight,*” said the man of thirty-four from the back of the room, “*and I have counted them twice.*”

“*I read them back to him and not to the square. I have not ruled a column and I have not headed it.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*That is five things at the back of this book in five weeks, and every one of them has been written with nothing at the top of it, and this is the fifth. I have told this room twice that an unheaded thing and a heading are not the same, and they are not the same, and he is standing in the middle of both of them.*”

Barnaby Rill understood the whole of that from the chair. About nine people in that room heard a woman say that the fifth entry in five weeks was a sentence a man had brought with him.

---

Then she shut the cupboard, and it took about four seconds, and about nine people in that room heard the lock.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody in that room was sorry for anybody.

A weaver of sixty-two had a name written at the back of that book on Friday with the day on it and was not thanked for saying it out loud. A man of about seventy-two had said no in a porch on Wednesday and was not thanked. A woman of about seventy had said no a second time in a porch on Saturday and was not thanked. A woman of about fifty-one had had nine people look at four pages in her two rooms and was not thanked.

A man of about thirty-four from a town four days off had said out loud three times in five weeks that he does not want a person in this county who answers for other people’s papers. He was not thanked and he was not believed.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody in that room was sorry for anybody. A man of thirty-one who had been refused six times in ten days had said four words out loud in a room, and they were written down at the back of that book for the first time in a hundred and forty-one days.

The fever was thirty-eight weeks and one day old. A list of the whole of what has happened in this town in a hundred and forty-one days was read out in a room eight feet by eleven with the door open as a list and not as a finding, and a finding is not a decision, and nobody has been asked any of the people it is about.

A man of thirty-one is entered in a book nine miles off in a class by himself with a name over it, and the name means that he is a person of the record and not of the place. A man of eighty-one asked out loud in that room whether a person of the record and not of the place can become a person of the place. Nobody answered him.

And at the back of a book with four columns and no fifth, on a leaf with nothing at the top of it, with the day on it, in the ordinary hand, there are four words a man of thirty-one carried out of a town two days off and into this one. The woman who keeps that book read them back to him in the doorway before she shut the cupboard, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody was sorry for anybody.
