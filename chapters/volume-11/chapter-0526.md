# Chapter 526: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Seventh Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market, And On Which A Man Of About Seventy-Two Who Walks Two Miles Every Wednesday To Hear One Line Read About Four Acres At The Bottom Of The Lane Is Asked, In A Room With The Door Open, In Front Of About Nine People, Whether He Was Asked A Question In A Square In A Town Two Days Off On The Morning Of The Thirtieth Day After The Division, And Says Out Loud That He Does Not Remember, And About Four People In That Room Understand That This Is The Finding And About Nine Understand That It Is A Failure

Third day of the ninety-seventh week. His two hundred and seventy-sixth morning. One hundred and twenty-six days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and five days. Eighty days since the division. Twenty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

It is a market on a Thursday. The square fills between about the eighth hour and the tenth, and it is about two hundred people and it is not two thousand. There are about nine stalls and a great many eggs and a wool bench and a man who comes down off the downs once a quarter with about forty ewes.

Nothing happened in that square about any of this until about the third hour of the afternoon. The thing that did happen there is at the end of this.

---

The asking was arranged by a woman of sixty-one on the Wednesday night, and she told the whole of it in a barn at Kiln Row in about nine sentences. There were about four people in that barn and about nine of the nine heard all of it.

“*A man of about seventy-two was named on a piece of paper on Tuesday. The paper is a copy of a copy of four pages that came from a building four hundred miles off. It is in a quicker hand with nine mistakes in it, and about four people in this town can read the hand underneath it and about four cannot read the hand it is in.*”

“*I have had it in my hands for four days and I have not been able to make either of those two things better.*”

“*He was not in that square,*” said Marda Rill.

“*I do not know that. That is the whole of it, and it has taken me two days to get to that sentence and I have said nine other sentences first.*”

Nobody in that barn said anything for about four seconds.

“*There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked what they remember. I have been in nine towns in four years and I have been told no four times, and one time I was not told anything at all.*”

“*I have not asked this man a question in three weeks and I am not going to. I have said everything I have got to say in this barn and I am going to bed.*”

And the keeper of the book said the thing that made the Thursday, in about four sentences, to nobody in particular.

“*Then I will ask him, and I will ask him in a room with the door open, and I will ask him one question. It will be whether he was asked a question in a square in that town on the morning of the thirtieth day after the division.*”

“*I will not ask him what the question was and I will not ask him what he said. If I ask him what he said then a man of about seventy-two is being asked to remember a thing in front of about nine people, and that is a different room from the one we have been working in for a fortnight.*”

“*You are going to ask a man whether a page is true,*” said the woman of sixty-one.

“*I am going to ask a man a question about a morning, and about nine people are going to hear the answer, and he cannot be sent for, and I am the only person in this county who can be.*”

“*And I have had nineteen years to work out what I would do on a day like this, and this is what I would do.*”

---

He was asked at about the second hour of the afternoon on the Thursday, in the chancel, with the door open and the window down.

He had come up the lane at about the half hour after the first, on a Tuesday-shaped errand on a Thursday. Nobody had sent for him and nobody had told him what it was about. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may be told what a room has been opened for.

About nine people were in that room. The man of eighty-one was in the chair by the fire and had not been sent for and had not been told. The man of forty-four was at the back with his coat on. A woman of sixty-eight of the upper row was at the side of the window.

The woman of about fifty-one who had gone down a lane on Sunday and asked a question of a neighbour was at the wall. A woman of sixty-one stood by the door. A weaver of sixty-two stood with his hands behind him. A man of thirty-one stood at the end and had been in that room twice in twenty-five days and was not a person of the county.

He was given a chair. He had not asked for one, and the chair was in the room because the room had three chairs and about nine people in it, which is the arithmetic of this building.

She said the terms out loud first, and she said them to the room and not to him, and about nine people heard all of them.

“*He has not been asked anything about the leaf in three weeks. He has not been asked anything about himself in three weeks.*”

“*He is here because he came up a lane and nobody told him why, and that is wrong, and I am not going to let a Thursday go past without saying so in a room.*”

“*Say the question,*” said the man of thirty-one, “*and then ask it, and do not put anything else in the room. Everything I have said in a room in a hundred and twenty-six days has been about what a room is for and I have been wrong in most of them.*”

She did not look at him.

“*Mr Kettle. On the morning of the thirtieth day after the division there was a square in a town two days from here, and about two hundred people were in it, and a woman read out a letter. I am asking you one question. Were you asked a question in that square on that morning.*”

“*I am not asking you what the question was. I am not asking you what you said. If you were not there, say you were not there. If you were there and cannot remember, say you cannot remember.*”

“*There is no form in this county that says you must answer me, and there is no form that says a person may be sent for afterwards about it, and I am the only person anybody can be sent for about. I will write down exactly what you say and not one word more than you say.*”

He was quiet for about nine seconds, and the room was quiet for about nine seconds, and nobody in that room moved.

“*I was not in that square,*” said Wynd Kettle.

“*Say where you were.*”

“*I was in a porch here. I stood at the end of the bench. She read me a line about four acres at the bottom of the lane and I said that was right, and she said it had been right the whole time.*”

He turned his head about four inches and it cost him something.

“*I have stood in that porch ninety-four times and I have never once been asked where I was on a Wednesday. If you had asked me a week ago I would have told you the same, and I would have told you the hour as well. I was in that porch at the ninth hour and I was in it until about half past ten, and then I went home and had my dinner.*”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“*But I am not going to say the other half yet,*” he said. “*Because you said you would not ask me two things and you have asked me one. I am a man of seventy-two and I have been asked about nine things in my life and six of them were about money.*”

“*Mr Kettle—*”

“*You said the question, and you said the question was one question, and you are not going to ask me a second one. So I am going to say a thing and it is not an answer, and if it is not an answer then you write down that it is not an answer and not what it is.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I do not remember.*”

And then he said the rest of it, in about four sentences, and it was not slow.

“*I do not remember whether I have stood in that square or not. I do not remember being asked a question by anybody. I have been asked about a wall by a man of forty-four twice in my life and both times in a lane.*”

“*I have not been in that town since my wife died, and that is about nine years back, and I do not know the season of it. So a page has my name on it against a question I cannot remember being asked.*”

“*I am telling you in a room with about nine people in it that I do not remember, and I want it written down that I said that and not that I said I was not asked.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

Then a man near the door said, “*Then the page is wrong.*”

And the man of about seventy-two said, without turning round, “*Say that again and say it quieter.*”

“*The page is wrong—*”

“*You have been in this room for about four minutes and you have not once asked me where I was on that morning, and you have said twice that a page is wrong.*”

“*A page is a thing in a hand, and a name on a page is a thing somebody wrote, and it may be right and it may be wrong, and I have told you the only thing I have got, which is that I do not remember.*”

“*And if you turn that into a page being wrong, then in a hundred years there will be a person in this town who was told that his name was taken off a thing and never was.*”

Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody in that room was relieved.

Then he said the other thing, and he said it to the man of thirty-one, and it is the only time in a hundred and twenty-six days that a person in that building has answered him before he had got the question out.

“*You have wanted a thing back since Tuesday.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*You wanted my name back off that page.*”

“*I wanted to know whether it was true, and I wanted to be the one who found out, and those are two things and I have been pretending for four days that they are one.*”

“*You wanted it back for the record,*” said Wynd Kettle. “*And I am not a thing that is got back. I am a man of seventy-two who has come up a lane on a Thursday nobody asked me to come up, and stood in a room with about nine people in it, and been asked a question, and said that I do not remember.*”

“*That is in a book four hundred and eleven years old in about a minute now, in the hand of a woman who can be sent for, with a day on it, and I cannot get it out again.*”

About four people in that room heard the whole of that.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said the man of thirty-one.

“*You have been in this country a hundred and twenty-six days and you have asked about nine rooms to be the one who says a thing. Today you wanted a man of seventy-two to remember a square so that a book would have a name in it that a person could be sent for about.*”

“*I have been coming to that porch for nine years for one line about four acres and I have never once asked whether I was there on a Wednesday, and nobody has ever asked me, and you have spent four days wanting to make a page into an instrument and you have made it into a man in a chair.*”

He put his hand on the arm of the chair.

“*That is the difference and you may write it down if there is a column for it, and there is not one. I am seventy-two and I have had one question in a room in twenty-five days and I have said no to nothing and I have said yes to nothing, and I have said that I do not remember, and that is the whole of what I have.*”

About nine people in that room understood that a man had been made into a record. About four of them understood that a person had been asked a question. The two groups were not the same four, and nobody in that room can be asked which was which.

---

The keeper of the book wrote it at the back of that book on a fresh leaf, and she did not read it out, and then she read out the last four lines of it. Those are the only four lines in four hundred and eleven years of that book that are hers alone.

“*Entered on the twenty-sixth day of a count begun in another county, which is not the counting of this town. A person of this town, of about seventy-two years, being asked in a room with the door open whether he was asked a question in a square in another town on the thirtieth day after the division, said that he did not remember whether he had been in that square.*”

“*He said that he does not remember being asked a question by anybody, and that he was in the porch of this building on the morning in question at about the ninth hour, and that he was asked nothing.*”

“*The name at the foot of the fourth page of a copy of a copy of four pages is not withdrawn and is not confirmed. It stands. There is no column in this book for a person who does not remember, and I have ruled none.*”

Then she said the thing that is the whole of this band, and she said it standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, in front of about nine people. About nine of them heard it and about four of them heard all of it.

“*There is a page in a town two days from here that says a man of this town was asked a question in a square, and there is a man in this town who says he does not remember being asked a question. Those are not the same shape of thing, and this town cannot make them into the same shape of thing.*”

“*This town remembers a Tuesday a hundred and forty-one years back with four words on it and no order. That town remembers a Wednesday fifty days back with a woman’s letter in it and about two hundred people who did not sleep.*”

“*Both of those are correct and neither of them is about the other one, and there is no instrument in the county of Aldwick that can hold two correct accounts of the same morning. And that book has four columns and there is no fifth, and I have had two days to look for one and there is not one.*”

“*So what is the finding,*” said the man of forty-four from the back with his coat on.

“*That is not a finding. That is a Tuesday, and I have not had a Tuesday since the leaf.*”

Nobody in that room said anything at all for about four seconds, and then the market began to go in the square. It is a Thursday, and the square fills between about the eighth hour and the tenth, and it had been filling for four hours with about two hundred people who had not heard one word of any of it.

---

And the man of thirty-one’s want that day was the plainest one he has had in this country, and it was not a want about a page at all.

It took him about nine seconds in a room to work out that it was not.

He had wanted the memory back. He had wanted a man of about seventy-two to stand in a room and say a thing that made a sheet in two towns into one thing, so that a book four hundred and eleven years old could carry a name and a morning and be sent for, so that about nine people in this town could be asked about either of them in a year.

And a man of seventy-two said that he is not a thing that is got back. About four people in that room heard the difference between a person who has been made a record and a person who has been asked a question, and the man of thirty-one was one of the four.

It cost him about nine seconds, and nothing in that room was any use to him whatever.

The fever was thirty-four weeks and five days old. A man of about seventy-two who walks two miles every Wednesday in a wet coat was asked a question in a room with the door open in front of about nine people, and said that he did not remember.

The name at the foot of the fourth page of a copy of a copy of four pages stands, and is neither withdrawn nor confirmed. A book four hundred and eleven years old has four columns and no fifth.

A town of about two thousand four hundred people and a town of about four thousand people are both holding a true account of a morning that is not the same morning, and there is no instrument in the county of Aldwick that can hold two.
