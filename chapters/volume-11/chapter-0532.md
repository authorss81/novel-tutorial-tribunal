# Chapter 532: The Second Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Wednesday Which Is The Day A Man Of About Seventy-Two Walks Two Miles To Hear One Line Read Out About Four Acres, And On Which He Says Out Loud In A Porch The One Thing He Has Not Said In Nine Years, Which Is That He Would Like To Be Asked About It Again, And About Four People In That Porch Understand What That Costs Him And About Nine Hear A Man Of Seventy-Two Ask For A Question, And A Woman Of About Fifty-One Says It In About Ten Words At About The Fourth Hour

Second day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-second morning. One hundred and thirty-two days after the settlement. The fever thirty-five weeks and four days. Eighty-six days since the division. Thirty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

A Wednesday is the day a man of about seventy-two walks two miles to hear one line read out of a book four hundred and eleven years old about four acres at the bottom of the lane. It is not a public day and it is not written anywhere. It is done because he does it and because his father did it.

He came at the ninth hour and he came in a wet coat, and it has not rained in this country for about eleven days, and everybody in that porch worked that out in about nine seconds and had worked it out for nine years and had never once said so.

He stood at the end of the bench with his hands behind his back, in the place about two-thirds along it where the stone is worn.

---

It took about eleven minutes and that is the whole of a Wednesday.

She read him one line. The line says that a holding of four acres at the bottom of the lane stands in his name in the second column and that the number of the holding is cut on a beech stake.

“*That is right,*” he said.

“*It has been right the whole time and it will be right after you.*”

“*Aye.*”

Then she asked whether he wanted to go and look at the stake, and he said no, and she said *same as ever*, and that was what he had said the last four times.

About four people in that porch had come and gone in the eleven minutes and about nine had come and stayed. The man of thirty-four from four days off was at the far end with his back to the wall and did not come closer than about nine feet, and she had told him on the Tuesday morning that he could stand in the porch and not in the room and that the reason was the same reason as everything else.

The porch bench is a thing that is not in the room and not out of it. The mark in the stone is about two-thirds along it. A man of about seventy-two stands at the mark. A man of eighty-one has sat at the far end of it. A man of thirty-one had been on it every day for a fortnight and nobody had ever said any of that out loud.

He had asked, on the Tuesday, whether he might come on a Wednesday.

“*The porch,*” she had said. “*Not the room. There is no form in this county that says a person not of it may stand in a porch, and there is no form that says they may not.*”

“*That is the whole reason and it is a good one.*”

“*It is the whole reason and I have not got a better one at the fourth hour on a Tuesday, and I have had nineteen years to get one and I have not.*”

---

And then he said the thing.

He said it after the eleven minutes were over and after the woman of thirty-four had shut the book and put the key back inside her dress, and he said it standing, and he did not preface it.

“*I would like to be asked about it again.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say what you mean by it, and I am asking because I am the only person who can be asked and not because I am going to do it.*”

“*The Thursday.*”

The keeper of the book did not move.

“*Twenty-six days on the Thursday, in a room with the door open, in front of about nine people. And six days since, which is thirty-two. And it is in a book four hundred and eleven years old with a day on it and my name on it, and it says that I said I did not remember.*”

“*That is what it says.*”

“*And nobody has asked me anything since. Not you, not a woman of sixty-eight, not a man of forty-four, not a woman of thirty-four who read two accounts of a morning out in a square.*”

“*I have not asked you because there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked what they remember, and I have had nineteen years to find one and I have not found one, and that is the whole of my reason and I am not going to give you a better one.*”

He was quiet for about four seconds and then he said the rest of it, and he said it to her and not to the porch.

“*I would like to be asked about it again. Once. And I do not want to be told what I said last time, and I do not want anybody told what I say, and I do not want it read out in the square on a Saturday.*”

About four people in that porch understood what that cost him, and what it was going to cost anybody who said it after him.

About nine heard a man of about seventy-two ask for a question.

She did not say no. She said the other thing, and she said it standing in a doorway with a book under her arm.

“*I have not said yes and I have not said no, and that is the whole of what I can give you in a porch on a Wednesday morning, and I have thought about it for two days and that is what I have got.*”

He said *aye*, and he said it the way he had said everything that morning, and he went home, and it took him a while.

Nobody thanked anybody. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may be thanked for asking to be asked.

---

Then the square did what a square does on a Wednesday, which is nothing at all.

The man of thirty-four stood at the end of it and worked out that the man of about seventy-two had come two miles for eleven minutes. He had got one line out of the eleven minutes, and the line had been the same line for about nine years.

He caught the keeper of the book at the corner of the meeting-house wall at about the eleventh hour and asked her one question, and it was a plain one and it was about his own trade, which is the first time in two days that anybody in that town had heard him ask about anything except himself.

“*On a shelf, a person may come and look. Once a week, on a set day, and not the keeper, and somebody else stands in the room with them so that the keeper is not alone.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*How long does that take?*”

“*A man of about seventy-two has just done it in about eleven minutes.*”

“*And what did he look at?*”

“*One line about four acres at the bottom of the lane and a number cut on a beech stake he declined to go and see.*”

The man of thirty-four was quiet for about four seconds, which is a thing he had not done in the two days he had been in that town.

“*We let a person look once a week on a set day and it costs the keeper about four minutes and the person a walk of two miles, and in exchange the person knows that a thing they gave us is still in the county. That is the whole of what a shelf is.*”

“*That is the whole of what a shelf is, and I have just told you there is no form in this county that lets anybody stand in a room in that building with me in it.*”

“*Then who stands in the room with him?*”

“*Nobody. He stands in a porch and I read out of a doorway and the window is down, and about four people are in the porch and about nine hear it, and the square has about two hundred people in it on a Thursday and not one of them hears a word.*”

“*So your version has one keeper and no witness.*”

“*My version has one keeper and a witness on a Wednesday, and a room with the door open on a Saturday, and a lane on a Sunday when a woman of about fifty-one asks her neighbour a question, and about four people who understand which is which.*”

“*And that is a shelf with the name of the keeper on it and the number scratched out.*”

“*And that is exactly what it is, and I have known it since about the fourth hour yesterday and I have not told him, and you may tell him if you like, and there is no form in this county that says you may not.*”

Nobody standing at that corner told him. About four of the people there had worked out that she had said the sentence on purpose, and about nine had heard a woman agree with a stranger about a thing she has been doing for nineteen years.

---

And then the man of thirty-one did the thing he has done in about nine rooms in a hundred and thirty-two days, which is to want a sentence to come out of him in front of about nine people.

He had been carrying it since the Sunday of the ninety-fourth week, which is the sixth day of that week and is twenty-four days back, and the sentence is the one the whole of this town has been circling since a leaf was read out of a book on a Saturday.

A man who is asked a question and says he does not remember has now been asked. That is in the back of that book with a day on it. And there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked what they remember.

He stood on that step at about the half hour after the tenth with the mark in the stone under his boot and about nine people in the porch and did not say it.

He said, instead, that the Wednesday had been an ordinary Wednesday, and he said it out loud, and about four people in that porch heard a man make a day into nothing on purpose and about nine heard a man say a day had gone the way it always goes.

He had wanted the ordinary Wednesday to be ordinary. He had wanted it badly enough that he stood in a porch and said it was ordinary, and it was, and it cost about four minutes of his own wanting.

And then a woman of about fifty-one said it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, in a lane behind the bottom of the town, in about ten words, to about nine people.

She had come up from the milliner’s with a bundle under her arm that was not the four pages and was bread.

“*A man who says he does not remember has been asked.*”

It was about four seconds before anybody in that lane spoke. About four of the nine people standing in it understood the whole of what had been carried in that sentence and about nine heard a woman say a thing about a man of seventy-two.

The man of thirty-one was at the corner of the garden wall and he had been carrying the sentence for twenty-four days.

“*How long have you had that.*”

“*Since the Sunday a woman of sixty-eight said no in a room with the door shut, and I was in that room, and nobody has asked me a word about it in twenty-four days,*” she said.

“*And I had it out in a lane this week for the first time, and a woman who sells eggs told me this morning that a man from four days off says there is a shelf.*”

“*You have not been asked for it.*”

“*I have been asked nothing in about a year. That is the difference between you and me and it is the only difference, and one of us can be sent for and the other cannot.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I have never once in this country got the whole of a thing from you.*”

“*I have four pages in two rooms over a milliner’s and I made them out of my own memory in about four minutes of sitting in a square in a town two days off, and a man from four days off says there is a shelf where other people’s papers go.*”

She shifted the bread.

“*And the woman of thirty-four who keeps your book is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for, and she stood in a porch this morning and let that be true out loud, and I was not in the porch and I heard it from a woman who sells eggs.*”

“*So you have heard about the shelf and not from him.*”

“*I have heard about the shelf twice today and neither time from him, and he is a stranger and he has been in this town two days, and you have been in it thirty days, and neither of those is a reason to trust a man with a trade name.*”

Nobody in that lane asked her about the four pages. About four of the nine people in it wanted to and did not, and about nine had not occurred to them.

---

The man of thirty-four said one more thing in the lane, at about the fifth hour, to the man of thirty-one and not to the nine, and it was about a woman and not about a shelf.

“*I have been in this town two days and I have asked one person one question and she answered it. Do you know what the question was?*”

“*No.*”

“*I asked her whether there was a room in this county that a person can be sent for out of. She said no, and then she said the other half, which was that there is a room, and that she is in it every day of the week, and that the room has a lock and a key on a string and about nine people who have never been asked whether they would rather be outside it.*”

“*That is three facts and none of them is a question.*”

“*That is three facts and every one of them was the answer to my question, and I have been in this country about nine years and I have never had an answer like that.*”

He put his hands behind his back, again, in the way of a man of about seventy-two who was by then walking back up the lane.

The fever was thirty-five weeks and four days old. A man of about seventy-two who walks two miles every Wednesday in a wet coat in a dry week stood in a porch and said out loud that he would like to be asked about it again.

A woman of thirty-four said that she had not said yes and had not said no, and a woman of about fifty-one said the sentence a man of thirty-one had been carrying for twenty-four days in about ten words, in a lane, in front of about nine people.
