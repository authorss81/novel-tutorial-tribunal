# Chapter 524: The First Day Of The Ninety-Seventh Week, And The Day Before Was The Last Of The Ninety-Sixth, And A Tuesday On Which A Thing Comes Down The Lane That Is Not A Page And Is Not A Return, And It Is A Copy Of A Copy Of A Thing A Person In A Town Two Days Off Saw In A Square, And It Says A Name, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In A Room In This Town The Sentence He Has Been Carrying Since He Came To This Country About A Door, In Front Of About Nine People, About A Town That Is Not His

First day of the ninety-seventh week, and the day before was the last day of the ninety-sixth. His two hundred and seventy-fourth morning. One hundred and twenty-four days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and three days. Seventy-eight days since the division. Twenty-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The second town is two days from here. It is a town of about four thousand people on the same river further down, with no register and no counter and no Bench of any kind. The cart road to it goes through Coldcombe and stops at a toll bar four miles short of this town, and the last four miles are on foot.

It has the four pages. That is the thing it has that this town has never had. The four pages came into it folded in four, with nothing on the outside, out of a building of four hundred people on a road four hundred miles off. They were addressed to a water board and not to a man, and signed with no name at all.

What this town has is a copy of them. The copy is in two rooms over a milliner’s at the bottom of this town. It is in a round hand with forty-one mistakes in it and thirty-eight of them corrected, and there is a thumbprint on the third page where a woman of about fifty-one of this town put her thumb down on wet ink.

It was made in about four minutes of sitting in a square in that town on the thirty-ninth day after the division by that woman, who was in the square and has never said so in this town until the Thursday of last week.

And the entry at the back of the book that says that nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original has been in that book for four days.

---

It came down the lane on the Monday night and was in the porch of the meeting-house by the seventh hour of the Tuesday morning. Nobody had sent for it and nobody could be sent for about it.

A man who keeps a cart between Coldcombe and the toll bar came down the four miles of lane at about the ninth hour on the Monday with a bundle on the tail-board. He gave it to the woman of sixty-one at the corner by the meeting-house door, because she is the only person in this town anybody has ever heard of who writes to other towns.

He said that a woman of about thirty at a stall in the town two days off had put it into his hands. She had said it was for the woman in the barn at Kiln Row, and she had not said anything else about it.

The woman of sixty-one had it for about eleven hours before she opened it, and about nine people knew she had it, and about four knew what was in it.

It is four pages and it is a copy of a copy.

The four pages came from four hundred miles off. A copy of them was made out of memory in a square in the town two days off, in about four minutes of sitting, by a woman of about fifty-one of this town. It came home inside the bundle of a copyist and was lent out about a year ago, and it has not come back.

And this is a copy of that copy, made in the same town by a person who was in that square. It is a generation further on again, and it is in a quicker hand than the hand it was copied from, and it has about nine mistakes in it and none of them corrected.

And at the foot of the third page, where the first copy has nothing at all, there are four lines that are not on the copy in the room over the milliner’s.

*That on the thirtieth day after the division, at about the eleventh hour, in the square at this end of the town, a woman of thirty-four read out the letter of a woman of this place, and about two hundred people were in the square.*

*That a person of Bramblefold who is named at the foot of this was asked a question in the open in that square before about two hundred people, and gave an answer.*

*That the answer was that the common above the town was not used, and that the answer was made use of.*

*That the person was asked by a man of thirty-four of Bramblefold and not by the woman, and about four people heard the question and about nine heard the answer.*

And at the foot of the fourth page, in a hand that is a third hand, and is the hand of the woman of about thirty who put it into a cart, there is a name.

The name is Wynd Kettle.

---

Nobody in that room had expected a name.

About four people in that porch understood what the four lines were. About nine understood that there was a name on a piece of paper.

The man of thirty-one was one of the nine, and got to the name inside about four seconds, and then had to stand in a porch for about nine minutes with a leg and a sentence he had been carrying for a hundred and twenty-four days.

He wanted the name. That is the whole of the wanting, and it is a small one, and it is the fourth time in twenty days that he has wanted a thing to be on a piece of paper in front of about nine people.

“*Read it out,*” he said. “*In the porch, now, with the door open, and about nine people are in this porch and about four of them have never heard a word of any of it. If those four lines are read out in the open then they are a thing that about nine people in this town can be asked about instead of a thing four of them carry.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill.

“*It is not a page and it is not a return. It is a copy of a copy, and there is a name at the foot of it in a hand you cannot read, and if it is read out in a porch then it is read out by the only person in this county anybody can send for. That is a different thing from carrying it in a coat for a week.*”

“*It is a different thing and I am not going to do it on a Tuesday morning. I will give you the whole of the reason and then you will not ask me again this week.*”

She had her hand flat on the bundle and not on the pages.

“*That copy was made in about four minutes of sitting by a woman who was in a square and could not sleep, out of four pages that came four hundred miles off into a town I have never been to. It has forty-one mistakes in it and thirty-eight of them corrected, and the corrections are in the hand of the woman who made them and the mistakes are in the hand of whoever wrote the wrong thing.*”

“*That is the only evidence anybody in this country has got about how those four pages were made. And this is a copy of that copy, made in about the same town by a person who was in the same square, and it has nine mistakes in it and none of them corrected.*”

“*And the four lines on the third page are not on the copy underneath them, and I cannot tell you from a bundle on a Monday night whether they are true, and I will not have a name read out in a porch in this county on the strength of a thing I cannot read and cannot check and cannot send for.*”

“*You can read the first copy. You are one of four people in this country who can read the hand on the front of that book, and there is not one of the four of you who is under seventy except you. You have had that copy in a bundle in your house for about a year.*”

“*I have, and I have never taken it out in a room, and that is the arrangement. It is the reason nobody in this county may be asked about the original, which is an entry in the back of that book with my name at the foot of it in my own hand. I wrote it on the Thursday of last week and I did not soften it.*”

And then the man of thirty-one did the thing that has got him shut out of a roll in a town two days off. It has cost him about nine rooms in six weeks and four rooms in three weeks in this one. It is to make a thing legible so that it can be held, and it costs about nine seconds and a leg.

“*A book is a door,*” he said.

He said it out loud, standing in a porch, in front of about nine people, about a town two days off and about nine miles of lane and four miles of foot. He said it the way he has said it in about nine rooms since the second day of his being in this country. It came out of him as a thing he had carried and not as a thing he had thought about.

There was a bundle on a bench in that porch with the four pages beside it, and a woman of thirty-four, and a woman of sixty-one, and about nine people at the door. About nine people in the square heard a man say four words and did not know that they were four words anybody had heard before in a town they had never been to.

About four people in that porch understood the whole of it, and what the sentence was, and where it came from, and who had first said it to him and who had struck it out of a sheet. About nine of the nine understood that it was a man from another county who could not be sent for, talking about a town he was not of, in a town where everybody could be sent for.

“*Say that again,*” said the woman of thirty-four, in the voice she uses for minutes.

“*A book is a door.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because I have heard the four words four times in this country in three weeks and I have never once heard the rest of them until a Tuesday morning in a porch.*”

“*It is not the same sentence, and you know it is not the same sentence, because the last time you said it in this town you said it to a woman holding a copy with a smudge on it in a lane behind the bottom of this town, and she told you she had never heard it and did not know who you were.*”

“*A woman in a room over a taproom in a town two days off said it to me on the thirty-eighth day after the division. I have said it to about nine rooms since, and you struck it out of a sheet with a line through the middle of it and stopped me before the second word.*”

“*And I have understood it about four times since and it has cost me about nine rooms and a bed and a leg that is worse than it was.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything about that.

“*Then it is a sentence you own and not one this town can use,*” she said. “*You have just said, in front of about nine people, that the thing four of them in this porch have been working on for three weeks is a sentence a man brought with him, and that the town it came from is two days off and this is not it.*”

“*And that nobody in this porch can be sent for to the place it came from, and neither can you, because you are not of either county.*”

“*That is not what I said.*”

“*It is the half of what you said that about four of us heard. The half that about nine of us heard is that a man from another county is talking about a town he is not of, and I am not going to stand in a porch and argue a man out of the half he said.*”

Then the man of eighty-one said the thing, from the step. He had come down on a stick and nobody had sent for him.

“*Say the whole of it, and say it into that room and not into the porch. A porch is not a room, and I have been in this town eighty-one years, and a thing said in a porch is a thing anybody on the step can walk off with.*”

“*And I have watched four words walk round this town from a pump in three weeks and I am not going to watch a sentence do it from my own doorstep.*”

They went in. The door was open and the window was down, because a book has to be read with the window down or people fall asleep. About nine people went into a room eight feet by eleven.

The man of thirty-one was in it and was not asked and was not refused, because it is not a room where a book is read this morning. There is no book out. The book is in the vestry cupboard with the door shut on the key on a string, and the four pages are on a table.

The keeper read the four lines out standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and she read the name at the foot of the fourth page.

About nine people in that room heard a name of a man of about seventy-two who walks two miles every Wednesday in a wet coat in a dry week to hear one line read about four acres at the bottom of the lane.

Nobody in that room had thought about that man since the Saturday before last. The reason was that he is a man of about seventy-two who has never once asked a question about a leaf and has never once been offered one.

The fever was thirty-four weeks and three days old. Four pages came down the lane in a bundle on a cart and were a copy of a copy, and at the foot of the third page there were four lines that are not on the copy underneath them in a room over a milliner’s, and at the foot of the fourth page there was a name in a third hand.

The name was of a man of this town who holds four acres at the bottom of the lane, and about four people in that room had understood a sentence about a door and about nine had heard a man from another county say four words about a town he is not of.
