# Chapter 535: The Fifth Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Saturday Which Is The First Week Since A Third Column Was Ruled On A Board In A Smithy With Nothing At The Top Of It, And On Which A Boy Of Fourteen Is Asked To Write A Week In Which A Person Of This Town Is Asking To Have Four Pages Kept In This Town, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Rules Nothing And Heads Nothing And Writes One Line About It, And The Boy’s Name Is At The Foot Of It Because He Wrote It, And She Says Out Loud That There Are Now Four Ruled Things In This County With No Heading On Them And That Two Of Them Are On A Board That Any Person In This Town May Rub Out

Fifth day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-fifth morning. One hundred and thirty-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and no days. Eighty-nine days since the division. Thirty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The week of a country town is made on the Friday night and it is read on the Saturday, and this is the Saturday, and the board was still up from the Friday with the week on it, and a board is a board and not a page.

It was read at about the tenth hour in a chancel eight feet by eleven, with the door open and the window down, and it took about eleven minutes, and about nine people went in.

A holding passed on the Friday. Four sheep went out of the fold on the Tuesday and two came in on the Thursday. A child was born on the Wednesday at about the fourth hour on the upper row, and it is a girl, and the ground behind the meeting-house came to her mother from a grandmother.

The dead man of about eighty-four was in the ground column with the day against him, and the reading of him took about four seconds, and nobody in that room said anything about him, and about four of the nine had never once read his name on that board.

Then, at the end, and not out of the book, the keeper of the book read one line from a board in a smithy, standing in the doorway, in the voice she uses for minutes.

“*About nine feet of the bottom of the common wall, open since the day it came down. Two accounts of one morning, in two towns, fifty-nine days back, both read out in the square, and neither of them in the book. A person of this town is asking to have four pages kept in this town.*”

Nobody in that square said anything about it, and about nine of the about two hundred people in it heard a woman reading three things out of a doorway, and about four of those nine understood what the three things were.

The dead man of about eighty-four was in it, in the ground column, in the middle of the column about the ground behind the meeting-house, with the day against him, where he has been since the Friday of the ninety-fourth week.

About nine people read that board every week and about four of them have never once read his name in it. One of the four asked about it in the week after and was told it was the recordwright’s doing, which was true.

And there were the two columns with nothing at the top of them. The first was about a hand wide and about a foot and a half long, which is the length of a gap, with about nine feet of chalk in it and a day of a week against it. The second was about a hand wide and about two feet long, with one line in it about two accounts of one morning in two towns, and the boy’s name at the foot of it because he wrote it.

The boy came in at about the seventh hour with the chalk and the rag and stood in front of the board and did not start.

---

“*There is a dead man in the ground column,*” he said.

“*And a gap in a wall in the next one with nothing at the top of it.*”

“*And two towns remembering one morning differently in the one after that.*”

Nobody in that smithy had asked him for any of it. About nine people in it went on with what they were doing.

“*And now there is a person of this town asking to have four pages kept in this town.*”

“*Say the last one again, because that is a different kind of thing from the other three and I want to know which it is.*”

“*It is a person who is asking for a thing to be held somewhere, and the other three are things that happened.*”

He was right about that and about four of the nine men in that smithy had heard a boy of fourteen make the distinction in about nine seconds.

“*I do not know what to do with it. That is what I said about the gap, and it went in the column with nothing at the top of it, and I have not slept a whole night about any of them since. There are three of them now and one of them is a request.*”

One of the men at the fire said *put it in the fold*, and the boy said no, because the fold is a hundred and forty acres whichever way a wall stands, and a request is not an acre.

One of them said *put it in the holdings*, and the boy said a holding is a number and a quantity, and the quantity is right, and a request is not a number.

One of them said *put it in the ground behind the meeting-house*, and the boy did not answer that one out loud, which is what he did the week before, and about four of the nine noticed that he did not say no to it this time either.

---

The man of thirty-one had been in that smithy since the fourth hour and had been asked nothing and had not offered.

His want on that Saturday evening was a heading, and it was the third time in fifteen days that he had asked for one, and he had never once been given it.

He had asked on the Friday of the fourth day of the ninety-sixth week. He had asked at the end of the seventh day of the ninety-seventh. And he asked again now, and he asked in about four sentences and in the voice of a man who has an arithmetic behind him.

“*Three. A space with a line down each side of it on a leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old, a column on the end wall of this smithy, and a third one next to it. Three ruled things in this town with nothing at the top of them.*”

“*There is a fourth and it is nine miles off in a leather wallet,*” said the recordwright, from the door.

“*Head the third one. You have had a fortnight to find out what goes in it and you have had a week since Saturday, and a person asking to have four pages kept in this town is a thing a heading can hold.*”

“*No.*”

“*You have given me the same answer twice in fifteen days.*”

“*I have.*”

She put the chalk down on the bench and stood with her hand flat beside the second column.

“*No, and I am going to give you the other half of it, and then I am going to stop giving it to you, and I would like the first of those two things said out loud in a smithy in front of about nine people.*”

About nine people in that smithy stopped.

“*If the other two were headed, then a person in a hundred years would fill them in according to the heading, and they would fill them in wrongly. Reasonably, and out of the best intentions, and with a good hand, and there would be nine lines in it and eight of them would be sensible and one would not. The one that would not is the one somebody in about a hundred and forty years would go and act on.*”

“*Then what is a heading for, if it is not for telling somebody what goes in it?*” said the boy of fourteen, from in front of the board.

Nobody in that smithy had expected the boy to ask it, and about four of the nine had already been thinking it.

“*That is a better question than the one he asked, and I am going to answer it in a room and not in a smithy, and I will tell him in about four years if I have got an answer.*”

“*And if you have not got one?*”

“*Then the answer is that there was not one, and I will have said so out loud in a room in this building, and that is a thing a person can check against a day of a week.*”

She picked the chalk up and put it in the boy’s hand, and she did not take it back, which is the thing she has done three times in nineteen years.

“*A not-knowing in a column is better than a knowing that is not true. A column with a name over it is a promise, and a promise is worth more than a guess, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked about a promise she does not remember making.*”

---

And then the man of thirty-one said the thing that ended it, and he said it out loud, in a smithy, in front of about nine people, and it was not clever and it was not fair.

“*You gave it to me the other two times.*”

Nobody in that smithy said anything for about four seconds.

“*The whole of the reason. The hundred years and the eight sensible lines and the one a person would act on. You gave me that on a Friday in this smithy and you gave it to me on the Monday of the ninety-seventh week standing in front of that board, and you have not given it to me this evening.*”

“*No,*” she said.

“*Why not.*”

“*Because I have given it twice and I am not going to give it three times, and about four of the nine people in this smithy have worked out why and about nine have not, and I am going to say the reason out loud because you have asked me a plain question.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*A reason given once is a reason. A reason given once is a reason. A reason given twice is a habit, and a habit is a thing a person does without deciding to.

“*And in about eleven years there will be a person in this town who was told that a column with nothing at the top of it is a good thing, and they will not be able to tell you that I thought it in a room on a Wednesday in my nineteenth year.*”*”

She said that standing in front of a board, in a smithy, in front of about nine people, and about four of them understood the whole of it inside about four seconds.

About nine people in that smithy heard a man of thirty-one be told that a reason he had been given twice would not be given a third time.

He said nothing for about nine seconds and then he said *aye*, and he meant it, and it did not get him anything.

---

She ruled nothing.

That is worth saying exactly. A board is the one place in this town where a person could rule something in four seconds with the edge of a hand.

There is no lock in that smithy and no key and no string round anybody’s neck, and any person in this town may add to that board or rub it out.

She wrote one line. The boy wrote it. His name went at the foot of it because he wrote it, and there was nothing at the top of it.

*That a person of this town has asked to have four pages kept in this town. The fifth day of the ninety-eighth week.*

Then she said the thing that was the last thing she said in that smithy that evening, and she said it standing in front of the board.

“*There are now four ruled things in this county with no heading on them. A space with a line down each side of it on a leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old. Two columns on the end wall of this smithy. And the back of a form in a leather wallet nine miles off, with six lines on it and a man’s signature at the foot of them and nothing at the top of that either.*”

“*Two of the four are on a board that any person in this town may rub out with a rag, and I have ruled none of them tonight.*”

“*About nine people read that board going in and out and about two hundred read it on a Thursday, and not one of them can check a word of it.*”

Nobody in that smithy thanked anybody. The boy was not thanked for writing a line about a person asking to have four pages kept in a column with nothing at the top of it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a boy may be paid anything else for a column he ruled himself.

The penny he is paid comes out of the twenty-eight pounds a year that a hundred and forty acres of sheep bring in at four shillings the acre, and a penny out of twenty-eight pounds is not a sum he can do.

The board went up on the end wall at about the ninth hour.

The fever was thirty-six weeks and no days old. On the end wall of a smithy there were two chalk lines with nothing at the top of them, one about a foot and a half long and one about two feet.

A third piece of chalk said that a person of this town is asking to have four pages kept in this town, and a boy of fourteen’s name was at the foot of all three because he wrote them.
