# Chapter 535: The Fifth Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Saturday Which Is The First Week Since A Third Piece Of Chalk Went On The End Wall Of A Smithy With Nothing At The Top Of It, And On Which A Boy Of Fourteen Is Asked To Write Down A Week In Which A Person Of This Town Is Asking To Have Four Pages Kept In This Town, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Rules Nothing And Heads Nothing And Writes One Line About It, And The Boy’s Name Is At The Foot Of It Because He Wrote It, And She Says Out Loud That There Are Now Four Ruled Things In This County With No Heading On Them And That Two Of Them Are On A Board That Any Person In This Town May Rub Out

Fifth day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-fifth morning. One hundred and thirty-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and no days. Eighty-nine days since the division. Thirty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The week of a country town is made on the Friday night and it is read on the Saturday, and this is the Saturday, and the board had been standing on the end wall since the Friday night with the week on it, and a board is a board and not a page.

It took about eleven minutes in a chancel eight feet by eleven with the door open and the window down, and about nine people went in, and about four of them had gone in before the reading started.

A holding passed on the Friday. Four sheep went out of the fold on the Tuesday and two came in on the Thursday. A child was born on the Wednesday at about the fourth hour on the upper row, and it is a girl, and the ground behind the meeting-house came to her mother from a grandmother.

And the dead man of about eighty-four was in the ground column for the ninth week running, with the day against him, and the reading of him took about four seconds, and about four of the nine people in that room had never once read his name on that board.

At the end of the reading, and not out of the book, she read one line off the end wall of a smithy at the bottom of the town, standing in the doorway, in the voice she uses for minutes.

“*About nine feet of the bottom of the common wall, open since the day it came down. Two accounts of one morning, in two towns, fifty-nine days back, both read out in the square, and neither of them in the book. A person of this town is asking to have four pages kept in this town.*”

About nine people were at that smithy door and about four of them understood what the three things were, and about two hundred people will walk past that board on a Thursday and not one of them can check a word of them.

---

The boy was already at the board when she got to it, with the chalk in one hand and the rag in the other, and he had not started, and the fire was in and about nine people were in the smithy.

The two columns were still on the board where she had left them on the Monday. The first was about a hand wide and about a foot and a half long, which is the length of a gap, and it had about nine feet of chalk in it and a day of a week against it. The second was about a hand wide and about two feet long, which is longer than the length of a gap, and it had one line in it about two accounts of one morning in two towns, and a boy of fourteen’s name at the foot of it because he had written that one too.

About nine people in that lane walk past that board every day, and about four of them have ever once put a hand flat on a column with nothing at the top of it to see whether the chalk would come off.

“*There is a dead man in the ground column,*” he said.

“*And a gap in a wall in the next one with nothing at the top of it.*”

“*And two towns remembering one morning differently in the one after that.*”

“*And now there is a person asking to have four pages kept in this town.*”

“*Say the last one again, because that is a different kind of thing from the other three and I want to know which it is.*”

“*It is a person who is asking for a thing to be held somewhere, and the other three are things that happened.*”

He was right about that, and about four of the nine men at that fire worked out that he was right inside about nine seconds.

One of the men at the fire said *put it in the fold*, and the boy said no, because the fold is a hundred and forty acres whichever way a wall stands, and a person asking is not an acre.

One of them said *put it in the holdings*, and the boy said a holding is a number and a quantity, and the quantity is right, and a person asking is not a number.

One of them said *put it in the ground behind the meeting-house*, and the boy did not answer that one out loud, which is what he did the week before, and about four of the nine noticed that he had not said no to it this time either.

The fire got on with it, which is what about nine people in a smithy do with a thing they have not been asked about.

---

The man of thirty-one had been at the end of that fire since about the fourth hour and had been asked nothing and had not offered.

What he wanted on that Saturday evening was a name at the top of one of the two columns, and it was the third time in fifteen days that he had asked for one, and he had never once been given it.

“*Three in this county,*” he said. “*A space ruled on a fresh leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old, and these two on this wall. Three things with nothing at the top of them. And there is a fourth nine miles off in a leather wallet that I have not seen and am not going to argue about.*”

“*Three that I have seen,*” he said. “*A space ruled on a fresh leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old, and these two on this wall. Three things with nothing at the top of them. And there is a fourth nine miles off in a leather wallet that I have not seen and am not going to argue about.*”

“*No.*”

“*You have given me the same answer twice in fifteen days.*”

“*I have.*”

She put the chalk down on the bench and stood with her hand flat on the board beside the two columns.

“*No, and I am going to tell you now that this is the last time I give you the reason, and I would like the first of those two things said out loud in a smithy in front of about nine people.*”

About nine people in that smithy stopped what they were doing.

“*Why is it the last time.*”

“*Because I have given it twice. On the Friday of the fourth day of the ninety-sixth week in this smithy, and on the Monday of this week standing in front of that board with a hundred and forty years in it.*”

“*And you have not given it to me this evening.*”

“*I have not, and you noticed it, and I would like somebody in this smithy to notice it with you, because it is the difference between a reason and a habit and nobody is going to be able to tell which one this was in about a hundred years.*”

She gave him the rest of it standing, in front of the board, in about nine words, and she did not raise her voice.

She gave him the rest of it standing, in front of the board, in four sentences, and she did not raise her voice.

“*Say the rest of that, because that is a rule about you and not about a column.*”

“*And in about eleven years there will be a person in this town who was told that a column with nothing at the top of it is a good thing, and they will not be able to tell you that I thought it in a room on a Wednesday in my nineteenth year. A rule about a person is the only kind there is that anybody in a hundred years can check.*”

About four of the nine people in that smithy understood the whole of that inside about four seconds.

He stood at the end of the fire for about nine seconds and then he said *aye*, and he meant it, and it got him nothing.

---

She ruled nothing.

That is worth saying exactly. A board is the one place in this town where a person could rule something in four seconds with the edge of a hand.

There is no lock in that smithy and no key and no string round anybody’s neck, and any person in this town may add to that board or rub it out with a rag.

She wrote one line. The boy wrote it. His name went at the foot of it because he wrote it, and there was nothing at the top of it.

*That a person of this town has asked to have four pages kept in this town. The fifth day of the ninety-eighth week.*

Then she said the last thing she said in that smithy, standing in front of the board with the chalk still in the boy’s hand.

“*There are four ruled things in this county with no heading on them and there were three a fortnight ago. A space with a line down each side of it on a leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old. Two columns on the end wall of this smithy. And the back of a form in a leather wallet nine miles off, with six lines on it and a man’s signature at the foot of them and nothing at the top of that either.*”

“*There are four ruled things in this county with no heading on them. A space with a line down each side of it on a leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old. Two columns on the end wall of this smithy. And the back of a form in a leather wallet nine miles off, with six lines on it and a man’s signature at the foot of them and nothing at the top of that either.*”

Nobody in that smithy thanked anybody. The boy was not thanked for writing a line about a person asking to have four pages kept in a column with nothing at the top of it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a boy may be paid anything else for a column he ruled himself.

He is paid a penny for the week. It comes out of the same twenty-eight pounds a year that a hundred and forty acres of sheep bring in at four shillings the acre, and one penny out of that is not a sum a boy of fourteen can do in his head, and that is not a figure of the boy, it is a figure of the money.

The board went back up on the end wall at about the ninth hour, with the two columns still on it and nothing at the top of either, and one of the men at the fire said out loud that it was only a board, and about four of the nine agreed with him and about nine did not.

The fever was thirty-six weeks and no days old. On the end wall of a smithy there were two chalk lines with nothing at the top of them, one about a foot and a half long and one about two feet.

A third piece of chalk said that a person of this town is asking to have four pages kept in this town, and a boy of fourteen’s name was at the foot of all three because he wrote them. A fourth ruled thing with no heading on it is nine miles off in a leather wallet that nobody in this town can open.
