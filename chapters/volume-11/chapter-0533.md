# Chapter 533: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Four From A Town Four Days Off Says Out Loud In A Square With About Two Hundred People In It That A Shelf Is A Good Thing And That He Is Not Here To Take Anybody’s, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud That A Shelf In A Room In Bramblefold Is The Only Thing In This County That Would Make The Copy Of The Four Pages Safe, And A Woman Of About Forty At The Wool Bench Says The Other Half Of That In About Four Seconds, And The Square Does Not Resolve It

Third day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-third morning. One hundred and thirty-three days after the settlement. The fever thirty-five weeks and five days. Eighty-seven days since the division. Thirty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

It is a market on a Thursday. The square fills between about the eighth hour and the tenth, and it is about two hundred people and it is not two thousand. There are about nine stalls and a great many eggs and a wool bench and a man who comes down off the downs once a quarter with about forty ewes.

Nothing happened in that square about any of this until about the eleventh hour, and about nine people in it had known about the shelf since Tuesday and the rest had not.

---

He got the word into it the way a thing gets into a market, which is by standing where the eggs are.

“*Shelf.*”

Nobody in that square stopped what they were doing. About nine of the about two hundred had heard the word in that sense before and about nine of the rest had not.

“*There is a trade and it has a name and the name is the Archive Lords, and I am one of nine of us in four counties, and we hold about four hundred shelves, which is the number of shelves and not the number of people.*”

A woman at the ninth stall asked him whether he wanted eggs and he said no, and that is how about two hundred people found out that there was a man in the middle of the square with a word and no eggs.

Then he said the two things, and he said them in the order a man says them when he has thought about the order for four days.

“*A shelf is a good thing. I have watched two of them stop being kept in about eleven years and both times it was ordinary.*”

“*And I am not here to take anybody’s.*”

About nine people in that square heard the first of those and about nine heard the second, and about four of the two hundred understood that the second one was said because the first one was going to be heard by people who would be frightened.

---

He tried to explain the difference and about two hundred people could not hold the two halves of it in their heads at once.

“*A copy is a thing made from a thing that is in front of the person making it, by a person who can see it. A held paper is not that. A held paper is a thing a person is responsible for, and a person who is responsible for it can be asked whether they have it.*”

“*How do I know which one I have got,*” said a man at the wool bench.

“*You do not, and that is the whole of the difficulty, and it is the reason the trade exists, and it costs about four minutes of sitting to find out and I have watched people take four hours.*”

“*Four hours of what?*”

“*Of being careful in front of about nine people who are waiting to be told which it is.*”

Then a man of about thirty-five said the thing that about nine of the two hundred had come to the square to hear, which was that a shelf in a town four days off is a long way to keep paper, and about four people in that square agreed with him and about nine did not.

And then the man of thirty-one made his mistake, and he made it in the open air in front of about two hundred people, which is the worst place in this county to make it and the only place where anybody could hear the whole of it.

“*A shelf in a room in Bramblefold,*” he said, “*is the only thing in this county that would make the copy of the four pages safe.*”

About nine people in that square understood what he had said. About four of the two hundred heard the word *safe*. The rest heard a man talking in a market.

Nobody answered him for about four seconds.

Then a woman of about forty at the wool bench said the other half of it, and she said it in about four seconds, and she did not raise her voice, and she had been in that square every Thursday for about nine years.

“*A shelf is a thing a person is responsible for.*”

“*That is what the man from four days off has been saying for a quarter of an hour and he has been saying it well.*”

“*There is no form in this county that releases a person from being the one.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because that is two sentences and they have a third in them somewhere and I can hear where it is going.*”

“*The copyist of that book has been the one for nineteen years. Two shillings a year from each of about sixty households, which is six pounds a year, and the households hold the fold, and she writes the book.*”

“*That is the whole of a woman’s year out of this square and I am not going to work out what it is a figure of in a market on a Thursday.*”

“*You will not, and neither will she, and neither will he from four days off, and the reason is that nobody has asked her.*”

Nobody in that square had asked her. About four of the two hundred had worked that out in about four seconds, and about nine had not, and one of the nine was the man of thirty-four from four days off.

“*Nobody in this county has asked her whether it is enough,*” said the woman of about forty, “*and she has wanted somebody to ask her that for nineteen years, and I know that because I have sat on that bench for nine years and there is not one Thursday on which she has come to this square at all.*”

---

And the recordwright was at the edge of it, and she had not come into the square and had not sent for anybody, and about four of the two hundred had noticed where she was standing.

She said one thing, standing, and she said it into the middle of that square and not to the man of thirty-one.

“*I am the one. That is true, and I have said it out loud in a smithy in this town and I am not going to unsay it in a market because a stranger has said the word *shelf*.*”

“*Then take it,*” said a man of about fifty at the ninth stall.

“*No, and here is the half of it, and about two hundred of you are going to hear it and about nine are going to hear the rest.*”

She put her hand out flat on the end of the wool bench, the way she does for a figure.

“*A man from another county has stood in this square and said that a shelf in a room in Bramblefold is the only thing that would make a copy safe. About two hundred of you heard that. About four of you heard that he said he did not want a person in this county who answers for other people’s papers, which he said on Tuesday in a porch, and he meant it when he said it.*”

“*Then there is no harm done.*”

“*There is no harm done by him this morning, and there is harm done by the sentence, and the harm is not that it is false. A shelf would make a copy safer than a room over a milliner’s.*”

She took her hand off the bench.

“*The harm is that a shelf is a place a person stands, and about two hundred people in this square have just been told in the open air that the place a person stands has been found.*”

“*In about eleven years?*”

“*In about eleven years there will be a person in this town who was told which room it is, and does not remember being told by anybody, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for to ask them about it.*”

About nine people in that square understood the whole of that. About nine heard a woman say the word *room*.

“*And the other half, and I am saying it because about nine of you are about to say it for me.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*About nine people in this town could hold that room today. About nine people could be asked about it in a year. Those are the same nine and that is the whole of the trouble with a shelf, and it is not a trouble with the man from four days off. It is a trouble with a county of about two thousand four hundred people in which about nine is the largest number that can be got into a room.*”

Nobody in that square thanked anybody. The man of thirty-four had not been thanked for saying out loud that a shelf is a good thing, and the woman of about forty had not been thanked for the other half, and the recordwright was not thanked for a thing she said in the open air that she had already said in a smithy.

---

The woman of about fifty-one was in that square for about an hour and nobody asked her anything, and that was arranged by nobody.

She had come up from the milliner’s and she was in her ordinary clothes and she is the only person in this county anybody could send for who has four pages made out of her own memory in a hand with forty-one mistakes in it.

About nine people in that square had worked out who she was by the Thursday, because the four pages have been in this town for about a year and there are only about nine people in it who know that.

A man of about forty came up to her at about the twelfth hour and asked her, in front of about nine people, whether the paper he had heard about was in her rooms, and she said she did not know what paper he meant.

Nobody in that square called her on it. About four of them had worked out in about four seconds that she had said the true thing. There is a copy of four pages in this town and there is also a copy of a copy of those four pages in a room with the door open, and a woman is not obliged to say which one is hers.

Then the man of thirty-four came over and asked her one question, and it was a different question, and it was asked from about four feet away and not loudly.

“*Have you ever had a thing of yours put on somebody else’s shelf?*”

“*No. I have had a thing of mine in two rooms over a milliner’s for about a year and I have not put it on anybody’s shelf and I am not going to.*”

“*Why not?*”

“*Because a man from four days off has said in this square that he does not want a person in this county who answers for other people’s papers. I read a sentence of his in a lane on Tuesday and I have not said it out loud in this square.*”

“*And?*”

“*And I am not going to hand him my four pages to find out whether I was right.*”

About four people standing near them understood the whole of that and about nine heard a woman say that a man was not going to have her pages.

Then the man of thirty-one came over, and she looked at him, and he had nothing to say to her, and she said that first.

“*You offered your room on Tuesday and you were refused, and I heard about it from a woman who sells eggs, and I have thought about it for two days.*”

“*I am sorry about it.*”

“*Do not be. It was the right refusal and it was given to you in about four seconds and it was the fastest anybody has said anything to you in this country.*”

---

The man of thirty-four said two more things that afternoon and both of them were about himself and neither of them got him anything.

He said that he had watched two shelves stop being kept in about eleven years and that in the first one the keeper died and the papers went into a house and the house could not read the hand, and in the second one two people in one house each thought the other had it.

He said that in both of those there was a person who could have been sent for and there was no form that said so, and that he had gone back to both of them afterwards and written it down, and that neither of the two was anybody’s fault.

And then he said the other one.

“*I do not want a person in this county who can be asked about a shelf and who answers for other people’s papers. I have said that in four counties and I have said it in this square and about two hundred of you have heard it and about nine of you have believed it.*”

“*Why should we believe that,*” said a man of about thirty at the ninth stall.

“*You should not. I said it because I would say it, and because a man who has come four days to ask a county for a shelf has to say it out loud before he asks, and that is the whole of the honesty I have got.*”

“*And what do you want.*”

“*I want to be asked what I want in a room. I have not been in one in this country, and the woman who keeps your book will not let me, and she is right and she told me why on Tuesday in about four sentences.*”

About nine people in that square heard a man of thirty-four say that he wanted a room. About four of them heard what was underneath it.

The band did not resolve it. A square in a country district is the only instrument a county of this size has for putting a thing in front of about two hundred people who did not ask, and about two hundred people heard the word *shelf* on a Thursday afternoon and about nine of them understood the whole of the offer.

The fever was thirty-five weeks and five days old. A shelf was offered in the open air in front of about two hundred people in a market square on a Thursday.

A man of thirty-one said it was the only thing in this county that would make a copy of four pages safe. A woman of about forty at the wool bench said the other half of that in about four seconds.

About nine people in that square understood the whole of the offer, and the square did not resolve it.
