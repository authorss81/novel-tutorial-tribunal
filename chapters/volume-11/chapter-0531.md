# Chapter 531: The First Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Tuesday On Which A Man Of Thirty-Four Who Came Down The Lane On Sunday From A Town Four Days Off Says Out Loud In A Porch That There Is A Shelf, And Says Out Loud What His Name For It Is, And About Nine People In This Town Find Out That There Is A Shelf, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers A Room And Is Refused In About Nine Seconds

First day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-first morning. One hundred and thirty-one days after the settlement. The fever thirty-five weeks and three days. Eighty-five days since the division. Thirty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The man came down the lane on the Sunday and he has been in this town two days and he has not said why he came.

He is about thirty-four. His coat has been on a cart for four days and it is not clean at the cuffs, and there is a copy of the map on the back of it that is made by a different hand from the one that makes the maps in this county.

The map in his town has a road on it that does not come near this town.

He came to the porch of the meeting-house at about the ninth hour on the Tuesday morning with his hands empty.

That is the first thing about him. About nine people in that porch worked it out inside about nine seconds. He came four days with nothing in his hands and a person who has come to take something says so in the first four minutes.

He stood at the end of the bench. The stone bench is about six feet long and there is a mark worn into it about two-thirds along, and a man of about seventy-two stands at that mark on a Wednesday.

---

He said the word first, and he said it into the middle of the porch and not at anybody.

“*Shelf.*”

About four of the nine people in that porch had heard the word before in a different sense and about nine had not.

“*There is a word for what I do and it is not a good one, and I have not got a better one, and I am not going to stand here for an hour deciding on one.*”

He put his hands behind his back. It is what the man of about seventy-two does, and about four people in that porch noticed the likeness and about nine did not.

“*The Archive Lords. That is the name. It is ours, we chose it about nine years ago, and it sounds like a boast, and I chose it, and I would rather have chosen a duller one and I have not found a duller one.*”

Marda Rill was on the step with her key in her hand. She is thirty-four and she has kept the book in that building for nineteen years and she is the only person in the county of Aldwick who can be sent for about anything in it.

“*Say the rest of it,*” she said, “*and say it in the porch and not in a room, and I will tell you why after.*

“*I keep other people’s papers. That is all of it. There are nine of us in four counties and between us we have about eleven shelves, and a shelf is a board with a number on it and a keeper’s name against the number.*”

“*Nine of you.*”

“*Nine of us and about four hundred shelves between us, which is the number of shelves and not the number of people, and I want that said before anybody says the number out loud in a porch and somebody writes it on the back of a hand.*”

Nobody in that porch wrote it on the back of a hand. About nine of them had heard the number and about four had understood why he had said it that way.

“*A person brings four pages to a shelf,*” he said. “*And the keeper writes the number of the shelf on the front of them, and the day, and the name of the person who brought them if there is one. And the keeper is not told who else is on the shelves, and the person is not told, and that is on purpose and I will come back to it in about four minutes.*”

“*You said the word *keeper*.*”

“*I said the word *keeper* and I have been in this porch for nine minutes and I have not yet said the word *ruling*, and about four of you are waiting for me to.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything for about four seconds.

“*I do not want a person in this county who can be asked about a shelf and who answers for other people’s papers,*” he said. “*I have said that in four counties and I will say it in this one and nobody has believed me in any of them, and I would like it said in a porch on a Tuesday that I said it.*”

---

She said the half of it, and she said it standing on the step with her hand not on the key.

“*I have been the keeper of a book for nineteen years and there is one word for it in this county and you have just used it, and I did not know until this morning that it was a word anybody used.*”

“*It is a word anybody uses.*”

“*It is a word I have not had to think about in nineteen years, which is the same as a word that has not been said out loud. I have been the one for nineteen years and nobody has ever asked me whether the one was enough, and I did not know that was a thing anybody wanted to ask.*”

He looked at her for about four seconds and then he said the thing he had come down the lane to get to.

“*That is what I came about. Not the four pages. I have not come about the four pages.*”

“*Then what have you come about, and I am asking it in a porch in front of about nine people and not in a room, and I would like the answer before the tenth hour.*”

“*I have been in this town two days and I have not got it. I have got a piece of it.*

“*I have got that a woman of thirty-four in this town is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for. That came off a smithy, and out of a man who cannot be sent for, and I had it four days before I came down your lane.*”

“*And you have come four days on that.*”

“*I have come four days on the strength of a thing a stranger said at a fire, and I would do it again, and I would rather tell you that than have you find it out in about four weeks.*”

About four of the nine people in that porch understood where that sentence was going to stop. About nine heard a man say he had come a long way on a thin thing.

He said the other half then, and he said it faster, and it was the part he had come with.

“*A shelf is a good thing. I have watched two of them stop being kept in about eleven years and both times it was ordinary and neither time anybody did anything wrong.*”

“*Say one of them.*”

“*A woman of about sixty died in the eleventh year. Her papers went into a house and the house did not know what they were, and the family were people who could not read the hand, and the hand was a hand, and there was nothing anybody could have done about it in about a fortnight.*”

“*And the other one.*”

“*Two people in one house. Each of them thought the other one had it. They were neither of them lying. That is the ordinary way a shelf stops and there is no form anywhere that stops that one.*”

Nobody in that porch thanked him for telling them about two dead shelves. He had not come to be thanked and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may be thanked for saying out loud that two shelves stopped.

---

She asked him two questions then, and she asked them standing, and she did not ask them to him so much as to the porch, which is a thing she has been able to do in that building for nineteen years.

“*You said a person brings four pages to a shelf. Who brings them?*”

“*Whoever has them. A man off a cart. A woman out of a bundle. A clerk nine miles off who is copying something old and has run out of room in his own office, and that is four in nine.*”

“*And do they get told where it has gone?*”

“*They get told the number of the shelf and they get told the day. They do not get told the name of the keeper, and that is on purpose, because a person who knows the name of the keeper will go to the keeper.*”

“*Why should they not go to the keeper.*”

“*Because in about eleven years a person will go to a keeper about a thing that is not theirs, and the keeper cannot say no twice in a day to the same person, and after a year of that the keeper has stopped saying no at all. That is not a fault in the keeper. I have watched it happen twice and both times the keeper was a good woman doing the work properly.*”

About four of the nine people in that porch had understood that she was agreeing with him and about nine had heard a woman argue about a shelf.

“*And you have not been in a room in this town,*” he said. “*You have been on a step of it. I have watched you for two days and you stand on the step and you read things out of a doorway and the door is open and there are about nine people in a square behind you.*”

“*That is what I have done for nineteen years, and the reason I do it is the same reason you do not give out names, and I did not know that until this morning.*”

“*Say it out loud, because there are about nine people standing here and about four of them have not worked it out.*”

“*There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be sent for to hear what I read out in a doorway. So I have never had to be afraid of it, and it has cost me the one thing I have wanted for nineteen years, which is somebody to ask me whether it is enough.*”

She said that at about the half hour after the tenth, in a porch, standing, in front of about nine people, and she did not look at the man of thirty-four while she said it, and she did not say it twice.

---

And then the man of thirty-one made the mistake, and it was the first one of this week and it was the same one he has been making for a hundred and thirty-one days.

He has a bed at the top of a joiner’s shop at the bottom of the town and he pays for it, and there is a room in the front of it that nobody sleeps in after the ninth hour, and he has been in this porch at about the ninth hour for a fortnight.

“*Then I will keep them,*” he said.

About nine people in that porch did not understand what *them* was.

“*The four pages. The copy in a room over a milliner’s. I have a room and nobody in it, and I could put a sheet of paper on a board and keep it, and you would know where it was on a Wednesday.*”

She refused him in about nine seconds. She did not raise her voice and she did not stop him at the beginning of the sentence, which she has done three times in three weeks.

“*No.*”

“*You have not asked me a question about the room.*”

“*I have not, and I am going to give you the whole of the reason and I am going to say it in a porch on a Tuesday morning and not in a room, and the reason is about four sentences and it is the reason the whole of this is about.*”

She put her hand on the end of the bench.

“*You cannot be sent for. Which means you cannot be asked whether you still have it.*”

“*Nobody is going to come and take it.*”

“*Nobody is going to come and take it, and that is the same thing said the other way round and you have had a hundred and thirty-one days to notice that the two are the same.*”

“*Say the other half.*”

“*A thing nobody can be asked about is a thing nobody can check. A sheet of paper on a board in a room over a joiner’s shop is safe because nobody may take it, and not because somebody is holding it.*”

“*And those two look the same from the outside.*”

“*They look the same from the outside and they are not the same, and I am not going to have it said in this porch that they are.*”

“*And there is a woman of about fifty-one at the bottom of this town who has had a copy of four pages for about a year. She has never mentioned it to anybody, and the woman she pays rent to does not know.*”

“*That is four facts and none of them is about me.*”

“*It is not about you, and the fifth one is. She is a person this county could send for, and you have spoken to her once in a lane, and you do not know her name.*”

He had about four seconds and he used all of them and none of it was any use to him.

“*That is the reason and I have not got another one,*” she said. “*The man from four days off has said in that porch that a shelf is a good thing. About nine of you heard him say it and about four of you heard the rest of it.*”

“*I heard both halves and they did not go together the way you are saying.*”

“*They went together the way a thing goes together when it is being set down for the first time, and I would like it said in this porch that nobody in it has said the word *ruling*. He said he did not want one. About four of us heard the half of that and about nine heard the other half.*”

About four people in that porch had understood the whole of what had just been said to a man of thirty-one and about nine had heard a woman refuse a room.

He was in a porch on a Tuesday morning of the hundred and thirty-first day of his being in this country. He had offered the one thing he had, and the reason it was refused was the reason he could not get past.

It took four sentences and it was said out loud in front of about nine people.

---

And then a woman of about fifty-one said one thing to him in a lane behind the bottom of the town at about the fourth hour of the afternoon.

She had come up from the milliner’s and she had heard the whole of it from a woman who sells eggs. She waited for him at the corner of the garden wall, which is four feet of the white the kiln makes, and about nine of those stones carry four letters and a stroke cut into the face of them.

He cannot read them. He has never had them read to him.

“*You offered your room,*” she said.

“*I did.*”

“*Do not do it again. Not this week and not in a market, and not to a man from four days off, and I am saying it to you in a lane and not in a porch because I have never been in a porch in this town where a thing was said that was mine.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because there is a copy of a copy of four pages in a room eight feet by eleven in this town with the door open. At the foot of its fourth page there is a name in a third hand.*”

“*I know the name.*”

“*You know the name and not where the page is, and neither do nine people of the nine who have that name in their mouths.*”

“*And the name is of a man of about seventy-two who cannot be sent for, and the woman who wrote it cannot be sent for either, and between those two facts there is a gap. There is nobody in this county who can close it.*”

“*There is a man from four days off who keeps about four hundred shelves.*”

“*There is a man from four days off who said out loud in a porch this morning that he does not want a person in this county who answers for other people’s papers. About four people heard him say it.*”

“*And I have been in this town all my life, and I would like to know what the word is for before anybody uses it about my two rooms.*”

She did not ask him for anything. Nobody in that lane asked him for anything, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what a woman of about fifty-one may ask of a man of thirty-one in a lane on a Tuesday.

The fever was thirty-five weeks and three days old. A man of thirty-four from a town four days off stood in a porch and said that there was a shelf, and gave the name of the trade that keeps shelves, and about nine people in this town found out that there was a shelf.

And a man of thirty-one offered a room and was refused in about nine seconds, and the reason was that a thing nobody can be asked about is a thing nobody can check, and that is not a protection, it is the opposite of one.
