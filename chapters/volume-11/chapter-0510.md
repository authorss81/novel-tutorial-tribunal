# Chapter 510: The First Day Of The Ninety-Fifth Week, And The Day Before Was The Last Of The Ninety-Fourth, And A Tuesday On Which A Claim Is Entered At The Back Of A Four-Hundred-Year-Old Book And The Leaf Is Not Struck Back And An Objection Is Entered At The Foot With A Man Of Forty-Four’s Name Against It, And A Column Is Ruled On A Fresh Leaf With No Heading On It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks For A Heading And Is Told That A Heading Is A Promise About What Goes In It

First day of the ninety-fifth week, and the day before was the last day of the ninety-fourth. His two hundred and sixtieth morning. One hundred and ten days after the settlement. The fever thirty-two weeks and three days. Sixty-four days since the division. Ten days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The reading of the struck leaf is on the Saturday after this one, which is four days off. About nine people will be in a room eight feet by eleven, and about a hundred and ninety will be in the square outside. That is the first time in a hundred and forty-one years that anybody in this country has arranged to read that page.

Nothing was decided on the Tuesday. That is worth putting at the top of this and it is worth putting at the bottom of this as well, because four people in this town had spent a week waiting for a Tuesday to be a day something settled on, and the rest of the town had not.

---

The thing that happened at about the ninth hour, in the chancel, with the door open, was an entry.

A book four hundred and eleven years old, in calf, with four hands in it and four columns and no fifth. At the back of it, on the leaf next to the one that has a man of thirty-one’s name on it, a claim was entered.

“*I am entering it exactly as it is said,*” Marda Rill said, “*and I am going to read it out in the porch first, because the door is open, and anybody in the porch may object, and the objection goes in at the foot with the name of the objector against it. I have done that once in nineteen years and I did it in a case about a wall, and I am doing it again today twice over, and the second one was done on the Sunday and I did not read it out.*”

“*Read it.*”

“*Entered on the tenth day of a count that was begun in another county, which is not the counting of this town and is put here because it is the only day anybody can be given. A claim is made in respect of the holding numbered six, of a half-acre at the bottom of Kiln Lane, and of the fold, and of the ground behind the meeting-house, by Abel Tarn, weaver, of this town, who is of the descent of a name upon a leaf of this book struck through in a hand heavier than any other in it, of which the only account is four words. The leaf is not struck back. The claim is entered. Nothing is carried.*”

She read it out in the porch standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and it took about ninety seconds.

About nine people were in that porch and about nine were in the square, the square on a Tuesday being a place with the bars of last Thursday’s stalls still lying in the mud. About three hundred people in a town of about two thousand four hundred had found out by Tuesday that something was going to happen in a room on Saturday, and about a hundred and ninety of them would be in that square on the market day, and the other hundred and ten would not, and a place of that size finds out a thing like that in a day and a half.

“*Nothing is carried,*” she said, when she had finished. “*Say that back to me, please, because it is the load-bearing three words and I want to hear somebody else say it in the open air.*”

A man in the second row of the porch said that nothing was carried.

“*Good.*”

---

The objection was entered at the foot, with a name against it, and it took about four sentences.

He said it before she had the pen out of the ink, which is a thing a man does who has had it in his mouth since Saturday, and she let him.

“*I object to the claim and not to the page. I want the page read on Saturday, and I want the page read to everybody in the square and not to nine people in a room, and that is a separate thing and I am not confusing them.*”

“*Then say the objection.*”

“*I hold the next one along. My grandfather took it off a family that was leaving and paid them four pounds, and I have the bill of sale, and I will show it to anybody in this square or in that room. I say the claim is not a claim on my ground, and I say it is a claim that will cost me the next one along the day somebody works out what a claim is worth. I want my name at the foot of it, and I do not want to be told to take it back, and I am not a man anybody is going to be able to find.*”

Nobody told him to take it back. Two people in that porch put their heads forward as though they were going to and did not, and a woman of thirty-four read the objection out standing with his name against it, and put it at the foot of the entry, and set her own hand under his and not beside it, and the day on it.

Then she said the other half of it, and about nine people in that porch were still there and about nine in the square.

“*Mrs Crow’s went in at the foot of a leaf of her own on Sunday night, and she asked for the day on it and I gave her the day. Hers is against the leaf going back in at all. His is against a claim. I have written in the margin that I think they are against the same three words, and if I am wrong the day is on it and somebody can put me right in a year.*”

---

And then she ruled a column.

She did it at about the eleventh hour, and she did not do it in the book. She did it on a fresh leaf at the back, and the leaf is not the same paper as the book and it is slightly whiter, and it is the third leaf from the back, and it is the one that has a man of thirty-one’s name and the day on it.

She turned it over and ruled a column down the middle of the other side with a straight edge she brought for the purpose, and that had been in her drawer since before he came.

It is about two inches wide and about nine inches long, and it is ruled in the same hand as the rest of the book, and it is not in the book.

Then she put the pen in and wrote one name at the top of it, and the name was Abel Tarn, and the name is the same name that is at the head of the entry, and the name is not struck through and there is nothing against it.

And she did not head the column.

“*You are not going to head it,*” Ilyan Vester said.

“*No.*”

“*It is a column. It has a name in it. It has a day on the leaf and two objections at the foot of the entry, and it is going to be about four days from now that nine people are going to read a page out in that room. A column with no heading in a book that has had four columns for four hundred years will be read as a fifth column, and the fifth column is a thing people put things in.*”

“*It is not a column. It is a space with a line on each side of it.*”

“*You have written four hundred and eleven years of other people’s book.*”

“*I have, and I have never once headed a thing that nobody in the room had agreed what went in it, and I am not going to start on a Tuesday on a page with two objections under it.*”

She set the pen down.

“*A heading is a promise about what goes in it. A heading says of the death, or of refusals, or of people who came to see the book, and everybody who reads it for four hundred years knows what it means, and they know it because somebody agreed it in a room. There is no agreement in this room. There are two objections and one claim and a man who cannot be sent for.*”

“*So a man who needs a heading to be in a book has misunderstood what a book is.*”

“*Yes,*” said Marda Rill.

“*And I have watched you work that out in about four seconds, and I would like it noticed that it took four seconds this time. It took about nine weeks in a market town, where a copyist of thirty-four had to strike a sentence of yours out of a sheet with a line through the middle of it in front of nine people.*”

---

And then she did the other thing, and it was at the foot of the leaf and not on the entry, and it was in her hand and in a different ink.

Four sentences, in a different ink, and the longest of them is about thirty words. They say that the name at the head of the space was entered at the asking of a person not of this town whose record is not findable; that he is entered at the back of that book on an unruled leaf with no column upon it, and that he came into this town on a cart road that ends at the Four Gates; that no body in this county can be asked anything about him; and that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says he may be asked and none that says he may not.

She read them out in the porch standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and about nine people in that porch heard the whole of them. About nine in the square heard a woman reading something out of a doorway on a Tuesday morning and not one word of it.

“*That is the ninth of the nine,*” she said afterwards, when a man of about nineteen asked her what the nine was. “*It is a man who came to see the book and is not a descent and holds nothing and has no line but a space with a name in it. It is the only one of the nine that anybody in this town can read out and understand in about four seconds, and I expect it will be the one of the nine that is read out in a hundred years.*”

---

At about the second hour he was sent for a second time, by a boy of about eleven who came up the lane.

The boy said that the man at the smith’s wanted the money for the bed, which was eight days at a penny and had not been asked for once. He was handed a penny by the man of thirty-one and gave it back, and said that the man had told him to say the room was his till Thursday, and that the man was not to be asked anything else about it.

There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what a lodging-housekeeper may be asked, and none that says he may not.

A man of thirty-one, eight days into a town that has four instruments and no officer, worked that out standing in a porch in about four seconds and did not say it out loud, because a copyist of thirty-four had told him in another town in another county that the arrangement in this country is that nobody is asked.

At about the third hour a woman of about forty came and stood at the far end of the bench. She said that her grandmother had told her about a page being struck, and that her grandmother had been told about it by her grandmother.

She had come to say that nobody had ever said what came of the ones that were struck, and she wanted to know whether the four rooms and a yard at the upper row were going to be taken off anybody.

“*Nobody has said that,*” Ilyan Vester said, “*and nobody in this room has any power to say it, and I would tell you not to believe a person who told you that the book could do it, because a book cannot do anything at all until a person picks it up.*”

“*That is what I wanted,*” she said, and went away, and the two people beside her on the bench were looking at the open leaf at the back of the book and had stopped talking.

He was in the porch until about the fourth hour, and about nine people came and went, and four of them stopped and said something.

One of them was a boy of about nine from the Four Gates, who had run down the lane on a Tuesday because it is the day nothing happens on the cart road. He asked the man of thirty-one whether he was the one at the meeting-house, and then asked whether the gates were going to be written down as well.

Nobody answered him, and about four people in that porch thought about it for the rest of the afternoon.

The fever was thirty-two weeks and three days old. A claim was entered at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old, and the page it was claimed on was not struck back. Two objections were entered at the foot of the entry, with the name of a man of forty-four against one of them and the name of a woman of sixty-eight against the other.

A space with a line down each side of it had been ruled on the back of a leaf, with no heading at the top of it, and one name was in the space.

Four sentences in a different ink were at the foot of the leaf, about a man of thirty-one who was not of this town and could not be sent for, and whose whole of what was written about him anywhere in that country was that.
