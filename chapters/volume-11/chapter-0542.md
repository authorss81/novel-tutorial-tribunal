# Chapter 542: The Fifth Day Of The Ninety-Ninth Week, And A Saturday Which Is The Ordinary Reading Of The Week, And On Which A Boy Of Fourteen Has A Fourth Thing To Write, And The Fourth Is That A Person Of Another County Has Been Entered In The Book Of Orders At Nine Miles Off In A Class With A Name Over It And The Class Is Not A Person, And A Recordwright Rules Nothing And Heads Nothing And Writes One Line, And The Boy’s Name Is At The Foot Of It Because He Wrote It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Write It For Him In A Smithy In Front Of About Nine People And Is Refused In About Four Seconds

Fifth day of the ninety-ninth week. His two hundred and ninety-second morning. One hundred and forty-two days after the settlement. The fever thirty-seven weeks and no days. Ninety-six days since the division. Forty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The week of a country town is made on the Friday night and read on the Saturday, and it has been read in that building for four hundred years, and it takes about eleven minutes, and nobody in that room has ever been asked whether they would rather it did not happen.

It was read out this morning with the door open and the window down.

A holding of five acres passed on the Wednesday. Two sheep went out of the fold on the Thursday and four came in. A woman of about seventy was buried on the Friday behind the meeting-house and the ground behind it is seven feet, and the seven feet is now hers in the fourth column and the man who had it is in the book in the first column and alive.

And the dead man of about eighty-four was in the ground column on the end wall of the smithy for the eleventh week running, with the day against him, and the reading of him took about four seconds, and one of the about nine people who read that board every week has never once read his name on it.

The man of thirty-one was on the step. A person not of this town is not in a room where a book is read, and it has been settled since the third week of his being in this country, and he has understood it in about four seconds every time and it has never once got him anything.

---

She read the last line out from the end wall of a smithy at the bottom of the town, standing in the doorway, in the voice she uses for minutes, and it was not out of the book.

Then the boy was already at the board with the chalk in one hand and the rag in the other, and he had not started, and the fire was in and about nine people were in that smithy.

The two ruled columns were still on the board. The first was about a hand wide and about a foot and a half long, which is the length of a gap in a wall, and it had about nine feet of chalk in it and a day of a week against it. The second was about a hand wide and about two feet long and it had one line in it about two accounts of one morning in two towns. His name was at the foot of both, because he had written both.

And there was a third piece of chalk, un-ruled, saying that a person of this town is asking to have four pages kept in this town, and it was still on the board, and nobody had rubbed it out.

“*There is a dead man in the ground column,*” he said.

“*And a gap in a wall in the next one, with nothing at the top of it.*”

“*And two towns remembering one morning differently in the one after that.*”

“*And a person asking to have four pages kept.*”

“*And that is four things and I have never been asked about any of them.*”

Then he put the chalk against the board and stopped, and he did it for about nine seconds in front of about nine people, and about four of them had seen him do it four times.

“*Say what the fifth one is,*” said the recordwright.

“*A person of another county has been entered in a book nine miles off in a class with a name over it, and I do not know what a class is.*”

---

Nobody in that smithy had said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of that, and say it in the order you have got it in, because a boy who cannot add can still count and I want to know where it stops.*”

He looked at the rag in his other hand and then at the board.

“*There are four things in this county with nothing at the top of them and there were three a fortnight ago.*”

“*Keep going.*”

“*A space with a line down each side of it on a leaf at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old, with one name in it. A column on this wall about the length of a gap in a wall. A column on this wall about two feet long with two towns in it. And the back of a form in a leather wallet nine miles off with six lines on it.*”

“*That is four and it is right, and I said it in this smithy a fortnight ago and you were not here.*”

“*And now there is a fifth thing nine miles off with a name over it, and what is under the name is a person, and it is not a thing like the other four because the other four have nothing at the top of them and that one has a name.*”

He put the chalk down on the bench.

“*And I do not know which one of the five I am supposed to write next to.*”

---

The man of thirty-one had been at the end of that fire since about the fourth hour and had been asked nothing and had offered nothing.

What he wanted on that Saturday morning was a name at the top of one of those two columns, and it was the fifth time in about five weeks that he had wanted one, and he had never once been given it, and on this morning he wanted something else more than that, and he wanted it because a boy of fourteen had just said in front of about nine people that he did not know what to do.

He said it in about four sentences and in the voice he uses, which is the voice of a man who has an arithmetic behind him.

“*I will write your line for you.*”

“*No,*” said the recordwright.

It was about four seconds and she did not raise her voice and about four of the nine people in that smithy had understood the whole of it before she had finished the word.

“*Say the reason, because a boy of fourteen has asked a question in front of nine people and he is owed one.*”

“*A line you write has nobody behind it. You cannot be sent for. I cannot ask you on a Wednesday whether that line is still what you said it was, and in about four years nobody in this county will be able to ask anybody about it at all.*”

“*I have never once lied about a thing I wrote.*”

“*I know. That is not the half of it and you have had that half for about four weeks. Here is the other one, and it is about the boy and not about you.*”

She put her hand on the board beside the two columns.

“*A line a boy of fourteen writes has a boy behind it. He holds a beast or he does not. He has a father and a house and he is in this fold and he goes to that smithy every day of his life, and in about nine years there will be a person in this town who can go to his door and say, what did you write on that wall.*”

Joe Drury understood that inside about four seconds and put the hammer down on the bench, and about nine of the men at that fire heard a woman refuse a stranger.

“*And I cannot go to his door about it either, because a boy of fourteen is not sent for. He can be asked and I cannot. That is the difference and it is about him and not about you and it is why I said no.*”

Nobody in that smithy said anything for about four seconds.

“*Then what I can do is nothing,*” he said.

“*Then what you can do is stand at the end of that fire and not put your hand on the board, and I would like you to do that in front of about nine people so that it is a thing that has been said and not a thing that has been thought.*”

He said *aye*, and he did not put his hand on the board, and it got him nothing whatever, which is the shape of most of what happens to him in this county.

---

She ruled nothing.

That is worth saying exactly. A board is the one place in this town where a person could rule something in four seconds with the edge of a hand, and there is no lock in that smithy and no key and no string round anybody’s neck, and any person in this town may add to that board or rub it out with a rag.

She took the chalk out of the boy’s hand and gave it back, and she wrote nothing, and the boy wrote.

*That a person of another county has been entered in the book of orders at nine miles off in a class with a name over it, and that the class is a person and not a thing. The fifth day of the ninety-ninth week.*

And his own name went at the foot of it because he wrote it, and there was nothing at the top of it, and he was paid a penny for the week out of the same twenty-eight pounds a year that a hundred and forty acres of sheep bring in at four shillings the acre.

Then she said the thing that made the afternoon, standing in front of the board with her back to it, in front of about nine people.

“*Four ruled things in this county with no heading on them. A space at the back of the book. Two columns on this wall. And the back of a form in a leather wallet nine miles off that I have not seen and have not asked to see.*”

“*You said four a fortnight ago and there were three before that,*” said the man of thirty-one.

“*I said four a fortnight ago and there are four now, and I have not ruled one this morning and neither has anybody else. We are at four. We have been at four since the Saturday before last and we are going to be at four on Thursday.*”

“*And the one with a name over it.*”

“*And there is a fifth place with a name over it, nine miles off, and it is not one of the four, and here is why and I would like it said in a smithy and not in a market.*”

She put her hand flat on the board.

“*This book has had four columns with names over them for four hundred years and every one of them is about a thing. The descent is a thing. The holding is a thing. The fold is a quantity. The ground behind this building is a depth. Four hundred years of this town have gone under four words that all of them mean a thing to do.*”

“*And the fifth one is over a man.*”

“*The fifth one is over a man, and that is not the same kind of hole in a book as the other four. A thing with nothing at the top of it is a thing nobody has thought hard enough about to name. A thing with a name over it is a promise, and the promise was made by a person whose name is not on the page, and I have not met him and neither has the man of fifty-four who filled the line in.*”

A woman of about fifty at the fire understood all of that, and the rest of them were looking at a board.

---

Then the boy asked a question, and it was the best question anybody asked in that smithy in five weeks, and about four of the nine people in it had been expecting it since the Thursday.

“*Which of the five do I write it under.*”

“*Say the whole of it, and say it slower, because a boy who cannot add can still ask.*”

“*There is a space at the back of the book and two columns on this wall and a form in a leather wallet, and none of them has anything at the top of it, and I do not know what goes in any of them, and she has told me twice that she does not know either and that that is why they can hold anything.*”

He looked at the rag.

“*And now there is a thing with a name over it and I know what goes in that one because you read it out in a porch yesterday, and it is a man, and the man is standing at the end of this fire.*”

Nobody in that smithy moved.

“*So I know what goes in the fifth one and not what goes in the other four, and if I knew what went in the other four I would not be able to write in them, and you have told me that is how they work.*”

“*What do you want me to say.*”

“*I want you to say which of the five I put my line under.*”

The recordwright stood in front of that board for about four seconds and did not answer him, and about nine people in that smithy watched a woman not answer a boy of fourteen.

“*I am not going to,*” she said. “*And I am not going to give you a reason, and you may put that down as the second time in five weeks that I have not given a reason to anybody in this smithy.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because you asked me which of the five, and there is no which, and if I point at one of them then in about four years a person in this town will read that board and see that a woman of thirty-four decided in a smithy what a class is, and there is no form in this county that lets anybody ask me whether I was right.*”

Nobody in that smithy thanked anybody. The boy was not thanked for writing a line with nothing at the top of it, and the recordwright was not thanked for not answering a question, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for either.

The board went back up on the end wall at about the ninth hour with three ruled lines on it and a piece of chalk, and about nine people in that lane walk past it every day and about two hundred will read it on Thursday and not one of them can check a word of it.

The fever was thirty-seven weeks and no days old. A boy of fourteen wrote a fourth thing on the end wall of a smithy, and his own name was at the foot of it because he wrote it, and nothing was at the top of it.

And there are four ruled things in this county with no heading on them, and there were three a fortnight ago and there were four a week ago, and a fifth place with a name over it nine miles off, and what is under the name is a man who was standing at the end of that fire and was not thanked and was not sorry for anybody.
