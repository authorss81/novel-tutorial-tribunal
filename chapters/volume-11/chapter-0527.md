# Chapter 527: The Fourth Day Of The Ninety-Seventh Week, And A Friday On Which About Nine People In This Town Are Saying The Second Account Out Loud In The Same Words They Have Been Saying The First One In, And A Sentence A Weaver Of Sixty-Two Said To A Man Of Thirty-One On The Fifty-Ninth Day After The Division In Two Rooms Over A Joiner’s Shop Is Said To Him Again By A Woman Of Sixty-One Out Of A Bundle Of Thirty-Two Letters She Has Had For Four Years, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Says Out Loud That She Is The Only Person Anybody Can Ask About Either Of Them And That Nobody Has Ever Asked Her What She Remembers

Fourth day of the ninety-seventh week. His two hundred and seventy-seventh morning. One hundred and twenty-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and six days. Eighty-one days since the division. Twenty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

Two accounts of one morning are going round this town in the same week, in the same mouths, and about nine people in this town can now give you both and about nine can give you only the one they heard first.

A thing said in a room and repeated in a square is a thing about nine people, and the nine are not the same nine, and the year after that it will be a different nine.

---

The two of them are the four words and the four sentences, and this is the shape of them.

The four words are *struck by order.* They have been going round Bramblefold since a Saturday nine days ago, at a pump, in a doorway, in a lane, in a kitchen at Kiln Row. About nine people in this town could have said them to a stranger about a wall, and about a frost pocket, and about a dead man of about eighty-four who was not struck.

The four sentences are about a square in a town two days off, and a woman of thirty-four reading a letter out, and a person of Bramblefold being asked a question in the open before about two hundred people and giving an answer that the common was not used.

And from the Thursday afternoon they have been going round this town in the same mouths, in about the same words, from the same people. About nine people in this town have both of them and can give you either.

That is what happened on the Friday, and it is worth saying what it is, because it is not a thing anybody arranged.

A sentence a person has been carrying for four years and does not know they are carrying is the most portable thing in a country district. A woman of sixty-one has been saying the same sentence, or a version of it, thirty-one times in four years in nine towns to about nine people at a time.

She said on the Wednesday that the sentence about a thing said in a room being a thing about nine people is hers, or the shape of it is hers. She said it in about four words in a room with the door open. About four people in that room believed her and about nine did not, and the not-believing is a thing she said she expected.

So she said it to a man of thirty-one in a porch at about the fifth hour on the Friday. He had heard the same sentence from a weaver of sixty-two in two rooms over a joiner’s shop at the bottom of the town on the fifty-ninth day after the division, at about the ninth hour, with the looms still.

“*You told me a thing in a porch on the Wednesday of a Wednesday, and I had it nine days before you said it, and I did not say it, and I said so. You said that was the correct use of nine seconds and that it was no use to me whatever.*”

“*I have thought about that sentence every day since, and I would like it noticed that I have been carrying a thing that a weaver said to me and a woman said to him.*”

“*It is the same sentence and it has been the same sentence for about nine years in two counties,*” said Odalie Fenn, “*and I have never once heard his, and I would not expect to, and I have a third one in my bundle and it is in a town four hundred miles off in a hand I cannot read and I have never read it out.*”

---

The third thing she has is a letter, and it is one of thirty-two, and the bundle has been in a barn at Kiln Row for four years and it is the reason she is in this town.

She named three of them out loud in a porch on the Tuesday of last week and would not read them, and nobody in that porch asked her, and about four of the nine noticed that nobody asked her.

The third one is a letter of four lines from a town in another county, of about four hundred people, on a road, out of a building.

*That a thing said in a room and said again in a square is a thing about nine people, and that the nine are not the same nine, and that the year after that it will be a different nine, and that a person who is a stranger in a town can carry a sentence out of it in a day and it can be a thing about this town in nine days, and we think the people here ought to be asked whether they want it.*

And the last line of it, which is the only reason she has kept it for four years.

*And we do not know what to do with a person who says no, and we have not found out in about nine years, and there has not been a week of those nine years without one, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked what they remember about it.*

Nobody in that porch said anything for about four seconds.

“*You have had that for four years,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*I have had it for four years and I have not read it out in this county, and I am reading it out now because about nine people in this town are carrying a sentence they got from a weaver and about nine are carrying a sentence they got from me.*”

“*And if a person four hundred miles off wrote those four lines first, then I am the third person to say it in this town, and I have been saying it in nine towns for four years and I did not know it was anybody else’s.*”

“*That is not what I asked.*”

“*No. You asked whether I have had it for four years, and I have, and I have carried it out of nine towns and put it in a bundle and taken it out of the bundle at a pump.*”

“*Nobody in this county has ever asked me where a sentence came from, because there is no form that says a person may be asked that. The reason I read it out is that two accounts of one morning are going round this town and a person who has been asked nothing is the one person in it who knows what it is like.*”

About four people in that porch understood the whole of that. About nine understood that a woman of sixty-one had read out a letter from a town four hundred miles off in a porch in a chalk town nine miles from an office.

She put the letter away and said the other half of it before she went.

“*And we do not know what to do with a person who says no. That is the line, and I have read it about nine times in four years, and I have never once read it in a room to a person who had said no.*”

“*Last Sunday a woman of about fifty-one asked a neighbour a question in a lane in this town, and the neighbour said no, and the keeper of the book has written it at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old.*”

“*And that letter has been in my bundle since before you came to this country, and it says they have one of those and they do not know what to do with it.*”

Nobody in that porch thanked her.

---

And then the keeper of the book said the thing that made the Friday into what it was, at about the sixth hour in the smithy with about nine people in it, and she said it about herself.

“*There are about nine people in this town who can give you both accounts, and about nine who can give you only the one they heard first. Those two nines are the whole of the finding of this week and neither of them is a decision.*”

“*And the number of people who can give you both is the number of people who can be asked about the other one, and there are about nine, and I am one of them.*”

“*You are the only one anybody can send for,*” said a man at the fire.

“*I am the only person in this county who can be sent for about either of them, and I have been that for nineteen years, and I have never once been asked what I remember.*”

About nine people in that smithy were at that sentence and about four of them were at a different one.

“*Say the rest of it,*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked what they remember.*”

“*I have been in nine rooms in this county in a hundred and twenty-seven days where nine people were in a room and a book was on a table. Every one of those nine could be asked what they remembered, and about four of them would have said so and about five would have made something up.*”

“*I have not been asked in any of them. In about nine of them I would have said something, and I have not said it, and I am not going to say it in a smithy on a Friday evening to about nine people. There is a difference between a thing you will say when somebody asks and a thing you say because you have not been asked in nineteen years.*”

“*I am asking.*”

“*You are a man from another county, and I have told you on the second day you were in this town that a person not of this town is not in a room where a book is read, and you have understood that in about four seconds every time, and I have watched you understand it eleven times.*”

“*And I am not saying it in a smithy on a Friday. And if you want to stand here for another minute then stand there and be quiet, and that is a thing you can do, and it is the only thing I am asking anybody for.*”

He stood at the end of that smithy for about a minute and was quiet. About nine people in it went on with what they were doing and about four looked at him, and about four of those four looked away again inside about four seconds. That is the whole of what happened to him that evening.

Then the man of eighty-one said it, from the chair, and he had not been sent for.

“*A thing a person remembers is a thing that is not a copy and is not a copy of a copy,*” said Barnaby Rill, “*and there is no column for it, and I have been that person twice in this town and I have said nothing both times.*”

Nobody in that smithy asked him which twice. About four of them had worked it out in about four seconds and about nine had not, and the not-working-out is the whole of what a town of two thousand four hundred people is.

---

And the man of thirty-one’s want was that the two accounts would settle, and he said it out loud in a porch at about the eighth hour. It was the first time in a hundred and twenty-seven days that he had said a sentence about a year in a room with about nine people in it.

“*They will settle in about eleven years. That is how this works. A thing said in a room is a thing about nine people, and the year after that it is a different nine, and about eleven years after that there is nobody left who was in the room, and what is left is the shape, and the shape is what a town says a hundred years afterwards about a Tuesday it cannot name.*”

“*That is what a book does,*” said Marda Rill, “*and not what a town does.*”

“*It is the same thing.*”

“*It is not, and the difference is the whole of my nineteen years, and you have been in this country a hundred and twenty-seven days and you have worked it out in about four seconds. It is the same four seconds it takes every time.*”

She was standing at the step with her key in her hand and she had not put it away.

“*A book settles in about eleven years because a book has one hand in it after about thirty years, and then it is the same hand forever, and nobody can ask it anything, and it can be read and not be argued with. That is what Mr Wenlock has been saying in a market square for nine years and he is right.*”

“*And a town.*”

“*A town does not settle. A town goes on carrying both.*”

“*In about eleven years there will be about nine people in this town who remember a Wednesday, and about nine who will tell you about it, and about nine of those nine will be telling it to somebody who is about nineteen and has never been asked anything. There is no form in this county that says a person may be sent for to be asked what they were told at nineteen by a woman of sixty-one.*”

She put the key inside her dress.

“*And I am the only person anybody can ask about either of the two, and nobody has ever asked me what I remember, and I have not been asked because there is no form that says a person may be asked what they remember.*”

“*I have had two days to find one and I have not found one, and I have had nineteen years, and there is not one.*”

The fever was thirty-four weeks and six days old. Two accounts of one morning were going round a town of about two thousand four hundred people in the same mouths in the same week.

About nine people in that town could give you both and about nine could give you only the one they heard first, and the number who could give you both was the number who could be asked about the other one.

And a woman of thirty-four said out loud in a porch that she was the only one, and that nobody had ever asked her what she remembered.
