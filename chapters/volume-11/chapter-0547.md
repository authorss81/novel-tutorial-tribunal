# Chapter 547: The Third Day Of The Hundredth Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market, And On Which Nine Things That Nine People Of This Town Have Said Out Loud In Four Weeks Are Read Out In A Square In Front Of About Two Hundred People In About Eleven Minutes, One Line Each By About Nine People, And About Nine Of The About Two Hundred Can Repeat Them Afterwards And About A Hundred And Ninety-One Heard A Woman Say Nine Things They Had Never Been Told, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks For The List To Be Entered In The Book Before It Is Read Out And Is Refused

Third day of the hundredth week. His two hundred and ninety-seventh morning. One hundred and forty-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty-seven weeks and five days. One hundred and one days since the division. Forty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

It is a market on a Thursday. The square fills between about the eighth hour and the tenth, and it is about two hundred people and it is not two thousand. There are about nine stalls and a great many eggs and a wool bench and a man who comes down off the downs once a quarter with about forty ewes.

Nothing happened in that square about any of this until about the eleventh hour.

About two hundred people were in it. About nine of them had heard the word *shelf*. About nine more had been in a room with the door open in the last four weeks. The rest had been to a market and had bought something and had gone home.

---

The keeper of the book stood at the end of the wool bench and said what it was, and she said it in about nine sentences and she did not raise her voice, and about two hundred people heard her say that nine things were going to be read out and that nobody was going to be asked about them.

“*Nine lines. About eleven minutes. Each one of them is a thing a person of this town has said out loud in a room, a porch, a lane, a smithy or a square in the last four weeks, and each person who reads one will say where it came from and in how many words.*”

“*Are you going to say it into a book.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill.

Nobody in that square had expected the answer to come that fast and about four of them noticed.

“*Say why not.*”

“*Because I said the reason in a room ten days ago and it has not changed. A finding goes into a book without anybody asking the people it is about. That is what a finding is. If I enter this list before it is read out then it is a finding, and there is nobody in this county that anybody could send for to hear me say that a finding is a finding.*”

Then she said the other half of it, standing at the end of the wool bench with her hand out flat on it, the way she does for a figure.

“*A list in a book about nine things a man said in a smithy is a list with a keeper standing behind it. And I have said out loud in a market square in this county twice that I am the one. I am not going to be the one for this.*”

“*That was a smithy twenty days ago and a lane before it and a square before that, and I have heard it in all three, and it has not changed in one of them.*”

“*It has been four rooms and a porch and a lane and a square, and there is a keeper standing behind all of it, and the fact that she is the same woman in all of those places is the whole of what she is.*”

Nobody in that square thanked anybody and about nine people in it understood that a list of nine things was about to be read out in the open air and was not going into anything.

---

Then they read it. About nine people. One line each. About eleven minutes, and it took eleven, and it was the third time in five weeks that a list had gone into that square in the open air. The first of those had been forty names with four gaps in them on a Saturday and had taken about nine minutes. The second had been two accounts of one morning and had taken about eleven. This one took about eleven as well.

Nobody arranged the order. The keeper had written nine lines out on a sheet in the ordinary hand on the Friday night and had not numbered them and had not put a heading over them, and anybody who wanted to read one read the next one on the sheet, and there were about nine people who wanted to.

---

The first reader was a boy of fourteen with chalk on his fingers, standing on the end of the wool bench.

“*That a person is asked what they remember.*”

“*Say where it came from.*”

“*I worked out on the Saturday of the ninety-fourth week that a man who had died belongs in the ground column, and I told one person about it in a porch and he could not be sent for, and I have not told anybody else in four weeks. That is the whole of it and there is no column in that book for a thing a boy worked out on his own.*”

About four people in that square had heard that before and about nine had not.

---

The second was a woman of sixty-eight of the upper row, at the same bench, with her sleeves down.

“*That a person is told what was decided about them.*”

“*Where it came from.*”

“*Off my own door, on the seventh and last day of the ninety-sixth week, at about half past nine in the morning, with about nine people in the road. And I asked for the day on it to be the day I was told on and not the day it was made, and it went in with my day on it and the day it was made stayed on the same leaf in her hand.*”

She put her hands together in front of her and did not say anything else.

---

The third was a man of about thirty-four who mends ploughs and has worked in that smithy eleven years, and he is not a person anybody in that lane has ever thought about.

“*That a keeper can be asked whether the keeping is enough.*”

“*Say where it came from.*”

“*Out of my own mouth in that smithy on the first day of the ninety-ninth week, in front of about nine people, and I had been a man who mends ploughs for eleven years and it had not occurred to me in one of them. And she gave me four sentences and told me not to thank her.*”

About nine people in that square heard a man say he had worked in a smithy for eleven years and about four of them had known it.

---

The fourth was the keeper of the book herself, standing where she had been standing. She did not move off the end of the bench.

“*That a person is asked before a thing is entered about them.*”

“*You are reading your own.*”

“*Nobody else here said it as plainly as I did and about four of you would have said it was mine. It came out of a room eight feet by eleven on the seventh and last day of the ninety-eighth week. I said that a finding goes into a book without anybody asking the people it is about, and I said it in nineteen years of keeping one, and about nine people in that room heard it and about four of them understood the place where it stopped.*”

---

The fifth was a man of eighty-one in a chair that somebody had carried out of the building and put in the square. Nobody had sent for him and nobody had told him.

“*That a record has a day on it.*”

“*Where it came from, Barnaby.*”

“*There are five records in this town that anybody can be checked against and one of them has a day of a week cut into the top of it in a shelf inside a four-foot garden wall, and the day of a week is the only one of the five that anybody can go and look at.*”

He did not get up from the chair and he did not say anything else, and about four people in that square had heard that he had watched nine things stop being kept in this town and had not been asked about one of them.

---

The sixth was a weaver of sixty-two, in the cloth he had come in.

“*That a thing a person said is not a thing the town said.*”

“*Where it came from.*”

“*The Saturday of the ninety-fifth week, in this square, when four words were read out and about nine people heard them and about a hundred and eighty-one heard a woman talking. And about nine of the hundred and eighty-one would have told you afterwards that a hundred and ninety people had been struck off something, and not one of them was struck off anything, and every one of them would have said it out loud in about four seconds if you had asked them.*”

He had not raised his voice once.

“*That is a page about a Tuesday and a page about a hundred and ninety people, and they are not the same and I am tired of being a man who says things about four words.*”

---

The seventh was a woman of about fifty-one of this town, who lives in two rooms over a milliner’s. She made four pages out of her own memory in about four minutes of sitting in a square in another town, and she had never read anything out loud in her life.

“*That a person may say no and have it entered.*”

“*Where it came from.*”

“*The Sunday of the ninety-sixth week, in a lane behind this town, when I asked a neighbour one question and she said no, and a woman of thirty-four wrote it at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old with the day on it. And on Wednesday this week, in a porch, a man of about seventy-two said no to a thing about his own name and it went in the same place with the same day on it, and she read it back to him and not to us.*”

About four of the about two hundred in that square understood the whole of that and about nine heard a woman read out the fact that two people in her county had declined something.

---

The eighth was a man of about thirty-four from a town four days off. He is not of this county and cannot be sent for from it.

“*That a thing held by one person is held by one person.*”

“*Where it came from.*”

“*A room eight feet by eleven on the seventh and last day of the ninety-eighth week, in about nine words. A thing is safe because somebody holds it and a thing is safe because nobody may take it, and those look the same from the outside, and I have been in this county eighteen days and about nine people in it can say that difference out loud and about nine cannot.*”

“*And you have said that you do not want a person in this county who can be asked about a shelf.*”

“*I have said it in four counties and in this one three times and nobody has believed me in one of them and I am not going to be helped by saying it a fourth time in a market.*”

---

The ninth was a woman of about forty who has sat on that bench for about nine years and sells wool there on a Thursday.

“*That nobody in a county of about two thousand four hundred people can be sent for about anything else.*”

“*Where it came from.*”

“*This bench, on the third day of the ninety-eighth week, in about four seconds. A shelf is a thing a person is responsible for, there is no form in this county that releases a person from being the one, and the woman who keeps our book has been the one for nineteen years and nobody has asked her whether it is enough and she has wanted somebody to ask her that for nineteen years.*”

“*And a man who mends ploughs asked her on a Tuesday.*”

“*And a man who mends ploughs asked her on a Tuesday and she gave him four sentences and told him not to thank her, and I have said out loud since that none of it came to anything and that none of it is in a book, and here we are in a square and it is not in a book.*”

---

Then the keeper spoke again, and she counted out loud, because she counts out loud.

“*About eleven minutes. Nine lines. Nine people. And I am going to say the number about this square and I have said every number I have said in this county in the open air.*”

Nobody in that square stopped what they were doing.

“*About nine of the about two hundred of you can repeat one of those nine lines this afternoon. Not all nine. One.*”

“*And the other about a hundred and ninety-one heard nine things that they had never been told, and about four of the nine of them said so out loud to the person next to them, and about five of them did not.*”

“*Say the other half of it, because a count is a finding and you have told this square twice that a finding is not a decision.*”

“*The other half is that a square is the only instrument this county has for putting a thing in front of about two hundred people who did not ask, and about a hundred and ninety-one of them now have nine sentences they did not have this morning, and not one of them is in a book, and about nine of them will have nine of them inside a year and about four will have one.*”

“*And is that worth anything.*”

“*It is worth exactly what a person can be asked about it for, and I have said that out loud in this square before.*”

Nobody in that square thanked anybody. Nine people had read nine lines out loud in the open air on a Thursday morning and none of them was thanked, and the man from four days off was not thanked for saying it three times in sixteen days, and the boy of fourteen was not thanked for standing on the end of a wool bench with chalk on his fingers.

---

Then the man of thirty-one did the thing he has done in about nine rooms in a hundred and forty-seven days, and he made it in the open air in front of about two hundred people, which is the worst place in this county to make it and the only place where anybody can hear the whole of it.

“*Enter it.*”

“*No,*” said the recordwright.

“*Say the reason, because nine lines have just been read out in a square in about eleven minutes and about a hundred and ninety-one people have heard them and there is nothing whatever to say against that.*”

“*I gave you the reason at the top of this square and you did not wait for it. A list entered before it is read out is a finding, and this is not a finding, and there is nobody in this county anybody could send for to ask me whether I had made a finding out of nine things nine people said in four weeks.*”

“*And a hundred and ninety-one people have just heard nine sentences they had never heard, and there is a boy of fourteen on that bench who said out loud that he worked out a thing about a dead man on his own and told one person about it and nobody else, and you have just read that out in a market.*”

“*I have. And about a hundred and ninety-one people heard it, and not one of them can be sent for about whether they heard it, and about nine of the hundred and ninety-one will have it inside a year, and that is what a square is and you told me so in this square nineteen days ago when I said a room was better.*”

Nobody in that square answered him for about four seconds.

“*And the cost of it is yours and not mine,*” he said, “*because I cannot be asked about this afternoon in a year and neither can the four of you, and about a hundred and ninety-one people heard it and there is nothing anywhere that says they heard it.*”

“*There is a boy of fourteen with chalk on his fingers who said where it came from and a woman of sixty-eight who said it off her own door and a man of eighty-one in a chair, and that is nine people who can be asked about it in about four years and not one of them can be sent for tomorrow. That is the whole of what nine lines in a square buys and I have just told you the price of it in front of two hundred people, which I have not done before and am not going to do again.*”

The fever was thirty-seven weeks and five days old. Nine things that nine people of this town had said out loud in the last four weeks were read out in a market square in front of about two hundred people in about eleven minutes, one line each, by about nine people, and about nine of the about two hundred can repeat one of them this afternoon.

About a hundred and ninety-one of them heard a woman read out nine things they had never been told. Not one of the nine lines is in a book, and a man of thirty-one asked for the list to be entered before it was read out and was refused in a market square in about four seconds.
