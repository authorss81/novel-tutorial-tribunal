# Chapter 522: The Sixth Day Of The Ninety-Sixth Week, And A Sunday On Which About Four People In The Bottom Of This Town Find Out That The Woman Who Went Up A Step In A Square On Saturday And Struck A Name Out Of A List With Her Own Hand Is Not A Person A Stranger Can Go And Ask A Question Of, And A Woman Of About Fifty-One Who Has Been Asked Nothing In A Year Goes Down A Lane And Asks Her Instead, And What Goes In The Book That Afternoon Is The First Thing In Four Hundred And Eleven Years That Records A Person Declining Something, And There Is No Column For That Either

Sixth day of the ninety-sixth week. His two hundred and seventy-second morning. One hundred and twenty-two days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and one day. Seventy-six days since the division. Twenty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

Four people went up the step of that meeting-house on Saturday morning in front of about a hundred and ninety people and struck a name out of a list with a piece of chalk out of a slate with a rim.

Each of them struck out the name of the person they are descended from. A woman of about seventy, whose grandmother is at the thirty-first name. A man of about thirty-five, whose grandmother is at the seventeenth. A woman of about twenty-eight. A boy of about nineteen.

Not one of them is on that list. Every one of them is kin to somebody who is, and not one of them was asked anything before they went up.

She is a woman of about seventy and she keeps a house on the lower row. She came to this town with her grandmother about a hundred and fifty years back from about ninety miles off, and she has never had anything in writing about the house, and she said in a square that she would not be said in a square to come out of that woman.

The sheet went back to the woman of thirty-four at about the twelfth hour with forty names on it and four gaps in it. It is in a book at the back of that book, because the person who keeps that book put it there.

---

Nothing had been done about it in a day and a half, and the reason was not anybody being busy.

The thing a person does when they have been said to have done something in a square is to wait to find out whether it was a thing. There is a shape to that in a town of about two thousand four hundred people. About nine people find it out in about a day and a half and about nine more find it out in a week.

Nobody in that town wrote the shape of it down, because there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked whether they meant a thing they did in the open air in front of about a hundred and ninety people.

The recordwright had said out loud in a porch on Saturday that the striking will be the part that is left in about eleven years. About nine people in that porch had heard it and about four had understood where it was going.

And on the Sunday the thing came back the other way.

---

He got as far as the lower row and about four people told him in about four seconds that he could not go and ask her, and all four of them were right.

One of them was a woman of about thirty-five with a child on her hip at the corner of a wall. The wall was four feet of garden wall built out of the white the kiln makes. About nine of those stones carried four letters and a stroke cut into the face of them, and he could not read them and had never had them read to him.

“*You cannot go and ask her that,*” she said.

“*I am going to ask her whether she wants a thing put in a book about her own hand on a Saturday morning, and I am going to ask it as a question and not as anything else.*”

“*You are going to ask her whether she wanted to be named in a book this week. She was named in a book this week whether she wanted it or not, and that is done and it is over and there is nobody left in that arrangement to ask.*” She shifted the child. “*She went up a step. Everybody saw. There is no asking whether a person wanted to go up a step. A person is a person who went up a step or a person who did not, and you cannot go and turn a person into a person who was asked.*”

“*And you cannot be the one who asks her anyway, because you are a man from another county. There is no form in this county that says a stranger may stand on a step in this town and put a question to a householder.*”

“*If she says no to you then she has said no to a man she cannot send for and she will not know she has done it. And if she says aye then you are a man who has been told something about a house on the lower row and you cannot be asked about it and neither can she.*”

About four people in that lane had stopped, and about two of them had worked out why a stranger could not do a thing a neighbour could do. It is not because the stranger is worse. It is because a neighbour is somebody the person can tell to go away.

“*Then who asks her,*” he said.

“*Somebody who lives here,*” the woman said, “*and not you, and not her with the key, and not anybody who wants it written down.*”

He stood in that lane for about four minutes after she had gone and worked out that he had wanted to be the one who asks, and that he had wanted it in about nine rooms in a hundred and twenty-two days, and that every one of the nine had been refused.

All four of the refusals had been about the same thing. A refusal about the same thing four times is not a run of luck.

---

And then a person who had been asked nothing in about a year did it.

She is a woman of about fifty-one of this town and she has been of it all her life. She lives in two rooms over a milliner’s at the bottom of the town. She has had a copy of four pages for about a year and has never mentioned it to anybody, including the woman she rents the rooms from.

She was entered in the record on Thursday as a fact. There is a copy of four pages in this town. It was made from memory in about four minutes of sitting by a person who was in a square in another town on the thirty-ninth day after the division. It has a thumbprint on the third page and no name on it, and nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original.

Four things, with the day on them, read back in a lane behind the bottom of the town in the voice the keeper uses for minutes. About four people were standing in that lane. About nine heard nothing.

Nobody had asked her a question. She had said one thing, and it was that a person with a pen and no copier does the only thing available, and that the only reason she had not said it out loud in this town is that she has never once been asked her anything, and that nobody asking is not the same as nobody listening.

Nobody replied to it, because there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what a person may say in reply to a thing like that in a lane.

So she went down the lane herself, on the Sunday morning, at about the eleventh hour, and she was gone about forty minutes.

---

The woman of about seventy was at the door of her own house on the lower row and she came out to the step, and about four people in that lane saw it happen.

“*You are the one with the four pages over the milliner’s. I have known that since the Thursday and I have not said anything about it and I am not going to now.*”

“*I am not here about the four pages.*”

“*Then what are you here about?*”

And here is the whole of the question. It took the woman of about fifty-one about four seconds to get it out, and she had never asked anybody anything before in that town and it shows in the sentence.

“*On Saturday you went up the step and you struck your grandmother’s name out of a list with your own hand. I am not asking you whether you meant it and I am not going to.*”

“*The sheet it is on is in a book at the back of a book, because the woman who keeps it put it there, and she will have to put it somewhere on Friday or let it go. There is nowhere on that page for a person to say which of the two she wanted.*”

“*I am asking you one thing. Do you want it in the book, or do you want it left on a sheet of paper in a room?*”

That is the first question anybody in that lane had asked anybody in about four hundred years, and the woman of about seventy did not answer it for about nine seconds.

“*No,*” she said.

“*No to which?*”

“*No to the book. Leave it on the sheet. I want it on the sheet in the room and I do not want it in the book.*”

“*Say the other half, because I am going to have to write it down and I would rather write down what you said than what I thought you said.*”

“*Because if it is in the book then in a hundred years there will be a person in this town who was told that a woman of about seventy went up a step and struck her own grandmother’s name out of a descent. That is a thing a person is told, and I have watched what this town does with a thing it is told in about four days.*”

She had her hand on the door frame and she was not looking at the woman with the four pages.

“*That is all. I am not saying I did it for the page and I am not saying I did it against the page. I am saying I do not want to be in the book about it, and there is no book in this town I can say that to, and you have come down a lane on a Sunday morning and asked me and I have said it.*”

Nobody in that lane said anything for about four seconds. About four of the people in it understood the whole of it. About nine heard a woman say no to a thing in a book.

---

The keeper of the book was told at about the second hour of the afternoon and she came down to the bottom of the town herself, which she has done four times in nineteen years.

She wrote it at the back of that book, on a leaf of the same paper as the leaf the copy of the four pages is entered on. She put the day on it, and she read it back to the woman of about fifty-one and not to the lane.

“*Entered on the sixth day of the ninety-sixth week. A person of this town, being asked whether the striking of a name she is descended from, made in a square in the open air with her own hand, is to be entered in this book with the day on it, said no, and asked that it be left on a sheet of paper in a room.*”

“*The striking is entered on that sheet. The sheet is in this book at the back. The name is not struck again in this book and the name is not restored in this book, and the leaf of a hundred and forty-one years ago is not touched, and the four gaps are four gaps.*”

Then she read out the next four sentences, and she read them to the lane.

“*That is the first thing in four hundred and eleven years that records a person declining something. I have gone back through that book this afternoon and that is the first of them.*”

“*Four hundred years of it carry what a person did and what a person held and how deep a piece of ground went to how many people. There is nothing in any of the four columns that says a person did not want a thing done.*”

About four people in that lane understood what she had said. About nine heard a woman say that a book was four hundred and eleven years old.

“*And I am going to say the other half of it before anybody in this lane says it for me,*” she said, “*because somebody will, and it will be in a market within a fortnight. This is one woman and one name and one afternoon. It is not a form. It is not a practice. It is not a thing that anybody else may do, because there is no column for it and I have not made one and I am not going to.*”

“*If a second person in this county stands in a lane on a Tuesday and says no to a thing about her own name, then what happens to that is a question. It will have to be worked out by whoever is here at the time, and it will not be by me at somebody else’s asking.*”

“*And I will not head it, and I will not rule it, and I will not put it in the space with the name in it at the back of that book, because that space is two inches by nine and it is not a place for a person who has said no.*”

Nobody in that lane thanked anybody. The woman of about fifty-one had not come down the lane to be thanked, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a woman may be thanked for asking a question of a neighbour.

The man of thirty-one was at the far end of that lane for the whole of it and had asked nobody anything. About four people in it had watched him not ask.

He said out loud at the end that he had come down that lane twenty minutes before the woman of about fifty-one had, and had got as far as a corner. He said that a man of thirty-four from another county had got a great deal further in a fortnight than he had in a day and a half. He said that this was the second time in twenty-one days that the town had done in a morning what he had been trying to do for a fortnight, and the first time had been a woman of sixty-one in a porch.

The fever was thirty-four weeks and one day old. A sheet of paper with forty names on it and four gaps in it was in a book four hundred and eleven years old.

The woman of about seventy who put the four gaps there with her own hand had asked, for the first time anybody in that town knew of, that it not be put anywhere else. It was entered at the back of that book with the day on it, and there was no column in that book for a person declining something, and the keeper of it said out loud, in a lane, to about nine people, that she was not going to make one.
