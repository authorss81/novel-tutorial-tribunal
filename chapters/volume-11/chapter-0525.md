# Chapter 525: The Second Day Of The Ninety-Seventh Week, And A Wednesday Which Is The Day A Man Of About Seventy-Two Walks Two Miles To Hear One Line Read, And On Which Two Accounts Of One Morning Are Put Next To Each Other On A Table In A Room Eight Feet By Eleven With The Door Open In Front Of About Nine People And Do Not Fit, And About Four Of The Nine Can Say Exactly Where They Stop Fitting, And A Man Of Thirty-One Reads The One That Is Less Useful As Though It Were An Error Of Copying And Is Told By A Woman Of Thirty-Four That A Thing Written Out From Memory By A Person Who Could Not Sleep Is Not A Copy And Is Not A Copy Of A Copy Either

Second day of the ninety-seventh week. His two hundred and seventy-fifth morning. One hundred and twenty-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and four days. Seventy-nine days since the division. Twenty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

A Wednesday is the day a man of about seventy-two walks two miles to hear one line read out of a book four hundred and eleven years old about four acres at the bottom of the lane. It is not a public day and it is not written anywhere. It is done because he does it and because his father did it, and it takes about eleven minutes.

He came at the ninth hour and he came in a wet coat, and it was not raining, and it has not rained in this country for about four days.

Everybody in that porch understood in about nine seconds that the coat is the coat he came in, and that it is not about the weather, and has not been for about nine years.

He stood at the end of the bench with his hands behind his back, in the place about two-thirds along it where the stone is worn. He was told *same as ever* and said *aye*, and she read him the line, and he said that was right, and she said it had been right the whole time and would be right after him, and he said *aye*.

Then she asked whether he wanted to go and look at the stake and he said no, and that was what he had said the last three times.

That took about eleven minutes and that is the whole of a Wednesday.

---

Then the room was opened up again, and it was not opened up for a book.

The four pages had been on a table in that room since the Tuesday morning, and the copy of a copy had been on a table beside them. The door had been open and the window down all day on the Tuesday. About nine people had looked in and about four had come in, and nothing had been read out and nothing had been entered.

What happened on the Wednesday was that a woman of thirty-four put two sheets of paper side by side on a table in a room eight feet by eleven and asked about nine people to look at them. Then she said out loud that the meeting was not a meeting and produced nothing, and that anybody could go out of that door and come back in, and that nobody in the room would be asked anything at the end of it.

The first sheet was four pages in a round hand with forty-one mistakes in it and thirty-eight of them corrected, and a thumbprint on the third page, and no name on it. It is a copy of four pages that came four hundred miles off into a town two days from here, and it was made out of memory in about four minutes of sitting by a woman of about fifty-one of this town. She was in a square at about the eighth hour on the thirtieth day after the division, and it was read out in that square that morning by a woman of thirty-four, and about two hundred people heard it and did not sleep that night.

The second sheet was four pages in a quicker hand with about nine mistakes in it and none of them corrected. It is a copy of the first sheet, and it was made in the same town, and it came down the lane in a bundle on a cart on the Monday.

About nine people in this town have the four words. The four words are *struck by order*, and they are four words and no order, and a person can say them about anything. There are about nine people in this town who could have said them to a stranger on the thirty-ninth day after the division, and about nine who could not.

About nine people in the other town have four sentences. A woman of thirty-four read out the letter of a woman of this place, and a person of Bramblefold who is named at the foot of it was asked a question in the open in that square before about two hundred people and gave an answer, and the answer was that the common above the town was not used, and the answer was made use of, and the person was asked by a man of thirty-four of Bramblefold and not by the woman.

Both sets are correct and neither of them can be checked.

---

This is where the wanting started, and it is worth putting down exactly what it was, because it was four minutes long and it was about a leg.

What they have are two accounts of one morning. The morning is the thirtieth day after the division, and that is a Wednesday, and the keeper of the book read the ordinary Wednesday leaf out in that porch on it, and a man of about seventy-two stood at the end of the bench and heard it.

In the town two days off, that morning is the morning a letter was read out in a square and about two hundred people did not sleep. In this town, that morning is the morning one leaf was read out to one man in a porch in about eleven minutes, and about four people understood it.

And a page says that on that morning, in that square, in the open, a man of this town was asked a question and gave an answer, and the answer was that the common above the town was not used, and the answer was made use of.

Nobody in that room said it out loud for about four minutes. Then the man of thirty-one said it, because he had been the only person in that room who could do arithmetic on two things at once for twenty-four days and he could not leave it alone.

“*The thirty-ninth day after the division,*” he said, “*is in the entry of the copyist of this town, and the thirtieth day after the division is in the four lines. Two different mornings, or one morning and a woman who could not sleep.*”

“*The answer in both of them is that the common was not used. The return at Aldwick says in a hundred and forty-one years that the answer was made use of, and the four words in the margin of a leaf in this building say struck by order and name no order and no day.*”

“*So the sentence that is less useful is the one that says the leaf is a hundred and forty-one years old and there is no order. And I am saying the other one is an error of copying.*”

Nobody in that room moved.

“*A man of about seventy-two is named at the foot of it. He is in that porch and he has been in that porch about ninety-four times, and he has never once asked me a question about a leaf.*”

“*If the four lines were read out in that square on the thirtieth day after the division then somebody in that town put a name against an answer, and this county cannot send for anybody, and the most that anybody in this town can do about a name in a hand somebody can read is to say that the name is wrong.*”

“*You have just said that about a page you have not been able to read for four days,*” said Marda Rill, “*in a room with the door open, in front of about nine people, and about four of them understood the whole of it.*”

“*I have said that a name against an answer on a copy of a copy is not a document of this county and cannot be asked about. I have not said his name is wrong.*”

“*You have said the sentence that is less useful is the one that is the error of copying. That is the same sentence with the other end of it turned round, and about nine people in this room cannot tell which end is which.*”

“*I have been keeping that book for nineteen years and I have watched about nine people make that turn in a market square in this county, and I am not going to watch it in my own room.*”

Then she put the sheet down.

She put it down flat on the table, on the copy of the four pages, with her hand beside it. It took about four seconds, and it was the first time in a hundred and twenty-five days that anybody in that county had put a document down in front of about nine people rather than read it out.

“*Now I am going to say the thing this room has not got, and I am going to say it once. I am not going to rule anything or head anything or enter anything today, and if it goes in a week from now I will have done it without a form and I will say so on the day.*”

About four people in that room understood that they were about to be told something. About nine thought she was going to read something.

“*That sheet is not a copy, and that sheet is not a copy of a copy either. The reason is that it was written out from memory in about four minutes of sitting by a person who had been in a square all afternoon and had gone home and could not sleep and had a pen.*”

“*It is the only thing anybody in this country has got about how those four pages were made, and it is a third thing, and there is no column for a third thing in any book in this building.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say what a copy is,*” said a man near the door.

“*A copy is a thing made from a thing that is in front of the person making it, by a person who can see it. That sheet is not that.*”

“*That sheet is a thing made from a thing a person remembered, in about four minutes, in a hand with forty-one mistakes in it of which thirty-eight are corrected. The corrections are the only part of it anybody can check, because they are the part where a person went back and said, no, that is not what it said.*”

“*Then it is not worth anything.*”

“*It is worth a great deal and it is worth a different thing from a copy. I have not got a word for the different thing. I have had a day to look for one and I have not found it, and I am not going to invent one at the fourth hour of a Wednesday in front of nine people.*”

---

And the place where they stop fitting is a place four people in that room could point at and five could not.

It is not the answer. Both of them say the common was not used, and the return at Aldwick says the answer was made use of, and that is three of the same fact and none of the three is in dispute.

It is the name.

A man of about seventy-two was asked a question in a square in a town two days off, in the open, before about two hundred people, on the morning of the thirtieth day after the division, and gave an answer.

On the morning of the thirtieth day after the division he stood at the end of a stone bench in a porch in this town with his hands behind his back, in a wet coat in a dry week, and heard one line read out about four acres at the bottom of the lane.

A hundred and forty-one years ago a household of this town was asked, by an office nine miles off at Aldwick, in respect of the matter of the common above the town, and gave answer that the said common was not used, and the answer was made use of, and the line of that household was struck from the roll of descent in a single stroke from an inch below the top rule to an inch above the bottom one.

Four words went into the inner margin, and no order was named on it.

A man of about seventy-two is not a hundred and forty-one years old. He was not born. There is nobody in that room who is on that leaf, and every one of the forty-three names on it belongs to somebody who is dead.

And a return in a book nine miles off says that an answer was given and that the answer was made use of. It does not say what the question was. About nine matters a person could have been asked about in those days, and one of them was whether the common was used, and one of them was how many sheep the common carried.

“*There it is,*” said the man of thirty-one. “*That is where it stops fitting, and I have had it for about four minutes and I have not said it out loud because I have been trying to make one of them be a copy of the other one and it will not go in that shape.*”

“*Say where it stops fitting,*” said Marda Rill, “*and say it to the room and not to me, and say it slowly. In about eleven years there will be a person in this town who has read about the day a man of seventy-two was asked a question in a square, and I would like there to be a sentence of yours near it.*”

“*It stops fitting at a hundred and forty-one years,*” he said.

“*Everything on the leaf is a hundred and forty-one years old and there is no order and no day, and the answer was given to an office and the answer was made use of, and that is a fact about a Tuesday.*”

“*And a man of this town was asked a question in a square in another town on a Wednesday forty-nine days ago in front of about two hundred people, and gave an answer about the same common, and it was made use of, and a person wrote his name against it in a hand I cannot read.*”

“*Forty-nine days is the seventy-ninth day after the division less the thirtieth, and the thirtieth was a Wednesday, and the day I am standing in is a Wednesday, and I have had to work that out twice in a porch this week, and there is nobody in this county who can check it but me.*”

“*Those cannot both be the same morning and neither of them is wrong. And there is no column in that book for a morning, and there never has been one, and this town has never once had a dated thing to count from. A man of eighty-one has told me that the last thing it counted from was a year of the long frost, which is a season and not a day.*”

About four people in that room were looking at the two sheets. About five were looking at the man of thirty-one. About nine of the nine understood that two things said by a person who cannot be sent for did not agree. About four of them understood why.

Then the man of thirty-one wanted the two to fit, and he tried, and it is worth writing down exactly how, because he has done it in about nine rooms in a hundred and twenty-five days and it is the whole of what he is.

He had spent twenty-four days with a name at the foot of the fourth page, and he wanted it to be a document, and a document is a thing that is either right or wrong. He wanted it to be wrong, so that this town could go on with a leaf that is a hundred and forty-one years old and four words that name no order.

“*Then the four lines are the error,*” he said. “*And I will say so in a porch in about four minutes, in front of about nine people. I will say that a copy of a copy is a copy of a thing somebody remembered and not a thing anybody can be sent for, and that is the end of it, and this county cannot do anything else with a name.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill.

“*You have just spent a quarter of an hour telling this room that the four lines are a third thing and not an error, and I am saying out loud, in a room with the door open, which of the two is worth less. I am doing it about a man of seventy-two who cannot be in this room and has not been asked anything.*”

“*You are doing it about a man of seventy-two who cannot be in this room and has not been asked anything. I have been in this building nineteen years and that is the shape of about nine of the ten things that have gone wrong in it.*”

“*And I have watched a man of sixty-two stand in a market square in this town and be told he had not said the name of a man of forty-four, and I have watched him be right.*”

She put her hand on the copy of the four pages and did not pick it up.

“*Write this down and put it on the board if you like. A thing written out from memory by a person who could not sleep is not a copy and is not a copy of a copy either. It is a third thing.*”

“*And there is no column for a third thing in that book, or in the book of returns at Aldwick, or in any instrument in this county, and I have had a day to look and there is not one. And I am not going to rule one on a Wednesday about a man who is standing in a porch outside and has not been asked.*”

Outside, at the end of a stone bench about two-thirds along, in a wet coat, a man of about seventy-two was standing with his hands behind his back, waiting to be told that a Wednesday was over.

The fever was thirty-four weeks and four days old. Two accounts of one morning were on a table in a room eight feet by eleven with the door open and about nine people were in the room, and about four of them could say exactly where the two stopped fitting.

It was at a hundred and forty-one years, and neither of them was wrong.
