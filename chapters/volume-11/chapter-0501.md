# Chapter 501: The First Day After The Hundredth Day After The Settlement, And A Sunday On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Asks A Copyist To Enter A Line In A Minute Saying He Was Here For Forty-Eight Days, And Is Told There Is No Form In The County Of Ostrey That Says A Man May Be Entered As Having Been In A Place, And Begins A Sentence That Would Be A Thank You And Is Stopped Before The Second Word

Sixth day of the ninety-third week, and the day before was the hundredth day after the settlement. His two hundred and fifty-first morning. One hundred and one days after the settlement. The fever thirty-one weeks and one day. Fifty-five days since the division. One day since a page of a book two days off was read out in a room with the door shut.

The bar four feet long at the head of the sluice had not been turned at the sixth hour or at the twelfth hour for twenty-five days. The wheel at the mill had been slow twenty-four days completed. The two of those are different counts, and the difference between them is a day on which the sixth hour came and was gone past, and the difference between them is now in a minute of a water board. A man leaving this town on a Sunday does not need to know either figure and carries both.

The Stone House had the door open from the ninth hour, as it has every day for eleven years. The press against the west wall was unlocked and the door beside it was open, and there were nine things in that press and a copy of eight of them lying on the sill. Any person of the town might read all of it, and not one of the nine binds anybody.

Ilyan Vester took the copies at about the tenth hour. The copy of the roll. The copy of the instrument of the fortieth day. The copy of the compact of six lines. The copy of the request with the open witness line on it. And the copy of the page, which was the fifth thing on the sill and was folded in four.

He put it in the inside pocket of his coat, against the paper the unsent return was wrapped in, which he had carried since the twenty-fourth day after the division and had never opened for anybody.

The copyist was at her own table four feet from the long one, with a fire that had not been let out properly since the first week he came to this town.

She is thirty-four, and she is paid two pounds a year, and the two pounds have not moved in eleven years. She is the only person in this town who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square, and she said no on the fifty-first day after the division and has not changed her mind in four days and is not going to.

“*I want a line,*” he said. “*In anything. In a minute, in a sheet, on the back of anything. That says a man of thirty-one was in this town for forty-eight days and went out on the side of it. Anything with my name on it and a day on it that is not an objection.*”

She put her pen down flat, which she does before she says a thing she means to be able to be asked about later.

“*Say the sentence you want written.*”

“*That I was here.*”

“*I have heard you. I am not refusing you. I am telling you what there is.*”

She turned her hand over on the table, palm up, the way a person does when they are counting a small inventory.

“*There is a minute of a water board and there is a minute of a House. There is a sheet in a press and there is a book in a miller’s counter that is shut to a hand’s width. Not one form in the county of Ostrey says a man may be entered as having been in a place. I have looked. Not this morning. I have been looking since the fifteenth day after the division.*”

“*A person is here. Nine people have seen me here.*”

“*Nine people have seen you here and every one of them can be asked about anything except where you slept. There is no form that makes that difference for you, and there is no form that makes it for anybody.*”

He had come in with it ready. He had known the answer before he came up the steps, because he had asked her a version of the same question on the fortieth day and been told the shape of it. Asking anyway was the want, and the want was that a thing he had spent forty-eight days in be a thing there was a line about.

Then he said, “*Thank you*,” and got as far as the second syllable.

She put two fingers on the paper.

“*No.*”

“*I only*”

“*There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be thanked.*”

She said it in the voice she uses for minutes, which is not loud and is not slow either. About four people in that room heard the word come out of him and stop. About nine people were in that room and the rest of them were in the square. “*I have not thanked you. Nobody in this town is going to thank you. I have a sheet in that press with a line through the middle of a sentence of yours and your name at the foot of it, and that is the only record of you in this town, and it is a record of a refusal. I made it on the fifty-first day after the division and I would make it again.*”

“*I know you would.*”

“*Then you have got what you came for and it is not a line.*”

---

A woman of about thirty-four was standing in the square at the north side with a bundle on her back and a basket at her feet and a folded sheet inside her coat. She had been standing there since about the eighth hour on the Saturday, and about four people in that town had asked her what she wanted and been told that she was going home today.

She was not of the county. She had come to the square on the Saturday and stood at the back of it through the whole of a charter being read out in a stone room with the door open. About two hundred people were in that square, and a man of thirty-one had asked for a line of a witness line to be read again more slowly in front of all of them.

She had a copy of four pages in a round hand, unsigned, with a smudge on the third page where a thumb had been put down on wet ink.

“*You are the man who read the square out,*” she said. “*No. You are not. You are the man who stood at the side of it and asked a question, and nobody answered it all the way.*”

“*That is fair.*”

“*It is not fair. It is accurate, and I have not got time for fair.*” She shifted the basket to the other foot. “*I keep a book. It is four hundred years old and it is in a chancel that is the only locked room in our meeting-house, and about nine people in this country can read the hand on the front of it and four of those are over seventy and one of them is me.*”

“*What does it do?*”

“*It says who descends from whom.*” She said it flatly, the way a person says a thing that has been said to her nine thousand times. “*The fold, and a holding, and a place in the ground behind the meeting-house go with a descent. Three things. I write the book. I have written it nineteen years and I have never been asked whether it is right by anybody who could answer.*”

Ilyan Vester looked at her for a moment longer than a person does a stranger.

“*A book is a door,*” he said.

“*Say that again.*”

“*A woman in this town said it in a room over a taproom on the thirty-eighth day after the division. I have said it to about nine rooms since, and a copyist of thirty-four has not let me say it in a room where it counted.*”

The woman of thirty-four put the four pages out of her coat and unfolded them against the side of the basket. The four pages were the same four pages that had come into this town on the forty-third day after the division, folded in four, with nothing on the outside, out of a building of four hundred people on a road four hundred miles off, addressed to a water board and not to a man, and signed with no name at all.

They were not the same four pages. They were a copy of them, and they were a generation further on, and the hand was not the hand of the woman four hundred miles off. It was a rounder hand and a quicker one, and it had made forty-one mistakes and corrected thirty-eight of them. There was a thumbprint on the third page, and at the foot of the fourth page it said, in the copy, who had written it out.

A woman of this town, on the thirty-ninth day after the division, from memory, that night, because she had been in the square and had not been able to sleep and had a pen.

“*Page two,*” he said. “*A lane works.*”

“*I have read that four hundred times and I have read the copy best of all.*” She folded the four pages away again. “*There is a lane in this book. Not a lane. There is a fold of a hundred and forty acres and a wall about eleven feet high and a lane that goes round the top of it about a quarter of a mile. It is the only lane in this part of the country that a man can walk along and not be on anybody’s ground, and there is no form in this county that says he may.*”

“*That is not the same thing.*”

“*It is the same shape.*” She got the basket up onto her hip. “*I am going home. I have a man of eighty-one in my house who kept the book before me and cannot read it and has not been asked about it in nine years. I would like somebody in this square who is not a stranger to have said the sentence about the door where I could hear it, and you have said it. That is the whole of what I came for, and I did not know that until you said it.*”

He got his coat off the back of a stool and his leg out from under the table and stood, and it took longer than it had taken a fortnight ago.

“*Then I will walk with you as far as the Four Gates.*”

“*You will walk with me as far as wherever you are going. I do not know where that is and neither do you, and there is a cart from Coldcombe at the seventh hour and it goes to the Four Gates and stops.*”

---

They went out through the square and up the lane. The lane is four hundred yards of mud to the ankle and it is the worst four hundred yards in the county, and a man of thirty-one with a bad leg goes up it at the speed of a man carrying a basket.

At the sluice a woman of thirty-eight was sitting on the frame with her feet clear of the water, and the gates stood open on two hinges behind her. The bar was in the mud about a chain’s length off where it had been put down on the Wednesday of the thirtieth day.

She looked at the two of them go by and lifted one hand off the frame and put it back. Neither of them stopped and neither of them said anything, and about four people further up the lane saw the hand go up and did not know what to make of it.

Behind them the mill wheel stood in the head of the race with the water lying black over the boards above it, and it was twenty-four days slow. It is a different count from the bar’s and it is one day behind it, and the difference between them is a day on which an hour came and went. There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be required to find out which of two numbers is the right one.

At the head of the lane where the four hundred yards end, the ground goes up to the downs, and there is a place with four rooms over a smithy where a woman takes a bed at a penny a night when the smith is away. He took one. The woman gave him a bowl of something and did not ask him a question and did not ask him to leave a name, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a traveller must leave a name at a lodging.

The two of them ate bread at the end of the table, and the woman of thirty-four read the copy of the four pages from end to end, out loud, in a low voice, to a man and a woman and a boy of about eleven who were eating.

None of the three said anything at all during it. When she came to the third page she turned it round and showed the smudge, and said that the woman who wrote it had put her thumb down on it, and had been in the square all afternoon, and had gone home, and had written it out from memory, and had told nobody she was doing it.

She said that the woman had told the man of thirty-four the next day in a market, and that the man of thirty-four had said, *well, that is the record then*, and had gone off to a kiln.

“*That is the whole of how that reached my house,*” she said. “*Not the woman four hundred miles off. A woman in a square who could not sleep.*”

He got up early enough to be at Coldcombe for the first hour, with one pound and fourpence. That is eighty-one days of a bed at a penny and bread at a penny, with a penny over, and nothing at all for a day of work.

He was in a town of two thousand four hundred people on a chalk slope by the time the light was on the kiln, and the kiln was burning its ninth day out of ten. The smell of it got into a coat in about a minute and stayed for a week.

The fever was thirty-one weeks and one day old. A man of thirty-one had asked a copyist for a line and had been given the shape of the answer, and had begun a sentence and been stopped before the second word, and had walked out of a market town of four thousand people where the only record of him was a line drawn through the middle of his own sentence with his name at the foot of it.

There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be thanked, and nobody in that town thanked him, and nobody is going to.
