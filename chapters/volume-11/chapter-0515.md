# Chapter 515: The Sixth Day Of The Ninety-Fifth Week, And A Sunday On Which About Nine People In That Town Have Begun Saying The Four Words Out Loud In Ordinary Talk, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In A Porch That A Weaver Of Sixty-Two Said A Sentence To Him On Thursday Night Which A Man Of Eighty-One Said To Him In The Same Shape In The First Week Of His Being In This Town, And A Man Of Fifty-Four Of The County Comes Down Nine Miles With The Fact That The Order Was Never Written On Anything

Sixth day of the ninety-fifth week. His two hundred and sixty-fifth morning. One hundred and fifteen days after the settlement. The fever thirty-three weeks and one day. Sixty-nine days since the division. Fifteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

By the Sunday morning about nine people in that town were saying the four words in ordinary talk.

Not in a room and not at a door. At the top of the street by the pump. In a doorway on the upper row. In the lane at the bottom of the town where the white wall is, and in a kitchen at Kiln Row where a woman of sixty-one was doing nothing at all and had done nothing at all for four years except write to towns.

The four words are *struck by order.* They are four words and no order, and a person can say them about anything.

By the Sunday they were being said about a wall that had been a course and a half higher since the Saturday, and about a frost pocket in an orchard, and about a man of about eighty-four who had died on the Wednesday of the fifty-eighth day after the division.

That last one was not true and it went round for about two days.

It went round because nobody can be asked about a thing they heard at a pump. That is the third term of Wednesday, and the third term of Wednesday was read out in a porch on the Wednesday before last in about a minute, and about nine people in that porch heard it and about nine people in the square heard a woman reading out of a doorway, and neither of those nines was the pump.

A thing said in a room and said in a square and said at a pump is three different things by the Wednesday. None of the three can be asked about by anybody who was in it. The man who said it at a pump does not know that he is a person in a record, because no form in the county of Aldwick has ever told him so.

Barnaby Rill came down to the porch at about the eleventh hour, on a stick, and about nine people were in it, and he listened to two women at the top of the street say the four words over a pail.

“*Four words going round a town is four words going round a town,*” he said. “*I am not going to tell the two of you to stop, because a man of eighty-one telling nine people not to say a thing is the same thing with my name on the end of it, and I have seen that done twice in my life and it was done by better men than me.*”

He looked at the man of thirty-one, who was in that porch and who had been in it every day for ten days.

“*You will want to say something about that. I can see the shape of it on you.*”

“*I was going to say the same thing you said on the Thursday in the market.*”

“*Say it anyway. I have been in this town eighty-one years and it is the first time anybody has said it back to me.*”

---

So the man of thirty-one said it, at about the eleventh hour on a Sunday, in a porch, in front of about nine people, and it took about a minute and it was not a speech.

A thing said in a room and repeated in a square is a thing about nine people. The nine are not the same nine. The year after that it will be a different nine, and the ones in it will be saying it about a different thing, and in about four years there will be nobody left in this town who can say who was in the room.

“*Abel Tarn said that to me on Thursday night,*” he said. “*In his own rooms over a joiner’s shop at the bottom of the town, at about the ninth hour, with his looms still, and it was the fifty-ninth day after the division.*”

“*And I have heard the same sentence in another shape from a different person in a different room in a different place. I have been carrying it about since the Wednesday of my first week in this town. I would like the two of them put next to each other in a room, because I do not think either man knew about the other.*”

The man of eighty-one in the chair by the fire said it in about four words.

“*A thing that stops being his and becomes the town’s,*” he said. “*That is the other half of it. Mine was the same shape and I have never once heard his and I should not have expected to, because I said mine to a stranger on a step and he has said his to a stranger in his own rooms and that is the whole of the traffic either of them ever had.*”

“*And I had a third,*” said the woman of sixty-one.

She was in that porch and she had been in it since the tenth hour, and nobody had sent for her and nobody had asked her, which is how she was in about four rooms in this town in a week.

“*A town wrote it to me four years ago. A person in it, not a keeper, not an office. Four lines about nine people hearing a thing in a room and passing it on. I have it in a bundle with thirty-one other letters and I am not going to say which town and I am not going to read it out, and I would like it noticed that I have never told anybody in this county that I have got it.*”

Nobody in that porch asked her, and about four of them noticed that nobody asked her. The man of thirty-one did not ask either, which is the whole of what he had learned in a hundred and fifteen days, and it cost him about four seconds and nobody in that porch could see it cost him anything.

---

Simon Verge came down at about the second hour of the afternoon and he did not stay the night.

He is fifty-four and of the county of Aldwick, which is nine miles off, and the county offices are there, and the office keeps one book of returns and one book of orders and can make a return and nothing else.

He had come down on the Wednesday of the week before and gone back on the Thursday. He had twenty-one other returns to make and he has made four of them. Nobody has asked him for any of them, and he said that in a porch in about nine words. About four people in that porch understood that it was the reason he was in the book of orders again, and about nine understood that it was the reason he was in the town.

“*I have been back in both books,*” he said, “*and I have opened the book of orders at the year of your leaf, which I had not done in about eleven years, because I had twenty-one returns to make and I did not need to.*”

“*And the order is there,*” Ilyan Vester said.

That was the mistake, and it took about two seconds to make and it was not a stupid mistake, and it was the one he made.

“*Somebody took it out of that book. Or somebody has it. Whoever struck the line had to write something and put it somewhere. A thing written a hundred and forty-one years ago in a county is still in a chest somewhere between here and nine miles of country. And if it is not in the book, then it was carried out of the book by somebody who had a reason.*”

Simon Verge put his hand inside his coat and took out a folded sheet and did not open it, and held it the way a man holds a thing he has read a great many times.

“*Nothing was taken out of that book,*” he said. “*Nothing has ever been on that page. That is the whole of what I have come down for.*”

“*Then somebody has it.*”

“*Nobody has it, because there is no it.*”

He said that without any weight on it at all.

“*The book of orders at that year is one page. On the page there is a heading in the same hand as the returns, and under the heading there is a half-sheet pasted in, and the half-sheet is blank, and it has been blank for a hundred and forty-one years, and the paste is as old as the binding.*”

About nine people in that porch were still at the sentence they had come in with, and about nine of those nine had come in with a man hiding something, because that is what a boy of about nine and two grown men come in with when a man of the county opens a book in a porch.

“*Why is there a half-sheet in it?*”

Nobody in that porch had thought to ask that, and about four of them had been looking at a book for about a minute without seeing a half-sheet in it.

“*Because the heading is a heading,*” he said. “*A heading goes on the page whether or not there is anything under it. A man in a county office in a week nobody can name wrote the heading first and the half-sheet second. Then nothing happened for a hundred and forty-one years, and nobody has ever turned to that page, because there is no reason to turn to it.*”

He put the folded sheet back inside his coat.

“*That office did not know a thing had been done. That office made a return, in the ordinary way, out of an answer that came in, and it went in a book, and that is the end of that office’s part in it. I have been in that book four times in nineteen days and I could not find an order if there were one. There is not one. There never was one. I am not going to sit here and be blamed for a page I did not take anything off.*”

Nobody in that porch blamed him for anything. About four people in it had stopped being angry and about nine had not, and the difference between those two is not a thing anybody can be asked about.

A county office is nine miles off and it is correct, and a man of fifty-four rode nine miles on a Sunday to put a folded sheet in his coat and take it out again and say that a page in a book is blank. He would not stay the night. He had seventeen returns left and nobody had asked him for them, and he said that in a porch in front of about nine people and did not ask for anything.

Then he said the other half of it, and he said it to the man of thirty-one and not to the porch, and he said it because he had carried it nine miles.

“*You are asking me for a thing somebody took.*” He said it without heat. “*There is a difference between a thing that was hidden and a thing that was never written, and it is the whole of your week, and you have had it the other way round since Wednesday. A thing that was hidden is a thing somebody can be found about. A thing that was never written is not about anybody. There is nobody to be found, and the man of the county has a horse and there is no road to ride him on.*”

“*That is fair,*” Ilyan Vester said.

“*It is not fair. It is correct. I am going back to Aldwick tonight and I have seventeen returns to make and nobody has asked me for any of them.*”

He put the folded sheet away, and then he stopped with his hand in his coat, and took out a second thing, which was a sheet of paper with about nine lines on it and a seal on the corner of it.

“*This is the other half of why I came and I had it in the other pocket. That is a copy of the return, made in my own hand in my own office, and the office will give a copy of a return to anybody who asks in writing. I have never done it before because nobody has ever asked, and I have brought it down in a coat nine miles on a Sunday because I would like it to be in more than one county.*”

He put it on the step of the porch and did not put his name on it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a copy of a return may not be given away by the man who keeps the book of returns, and none that says it may.

The fever was thirty-three weeks and one day old. Nine miles off there is an office that keeps a book of returns and a book of orders. At the year of the leaf the second of them has a heading on it and a half-sheet pasted in under the heading. The half-sheet is blank and has been for a hundred and forty-one years. Nobody took anything out of it, because there was never anything on it.
