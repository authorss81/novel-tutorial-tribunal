# Chapter 537: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And The Morrow Is The First Of The Ninety-Ninth, And A Monday On Which A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Reads Out In A Room With The Door Open In Front Of About Nine People The Whole Of What Four Columns Of A Book Four Hundred And Eleven Years Old Carry And Do Not Carry, And A Fifth Thing Said From A Chair By A Fire, And Three Things Said Out Loud In A Lane In One Week, And Six Lines On The Back Of A Form Nine Miles Off, And Says Out Loud That The Whole Of It Is A List And A List Is Not A Finding And A Finding Is Not A Decision And Nobody Has Asked Any Of The People It Is About, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks Her To Enter It As A Finding And Is Refused

Seventh and last day of the ninety-eighth week, and the morrow is the first day of the ninety-ninth. His two hundred and eighty-seventh morning. One hundred and thirty-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and two days. Ninety-one days since the division. Thirty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The room was the chancel, eight feet by eleven, with the door open and the window down. There was no book out of the vestry cupboard. The book was in the cupboard with the door shut on the key on a string.

About nine people were in that room, and the man of thirty-four from four days off was in it, and he had asked on the Friday whether he might be in it and had been told yes and told the reason.

---

She read it out standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and she read it as a list and not as a speech, and she said at the top that it was a list.

“*The first column is the descent, who comes from whom. It carries a name, a father’s name where there is one, and a hand, and about two hundred years of it are in four hands and the fourth hand is a man who rebound the book ninety years back and his name is on the back of the last leaf in a small firm hand.*”

“*It does not carry a day,*” said a man near the door.

“*It does not carry a day and it never has, and that is the first thing about the whole of it. A column that carries a descent and not a day is a column that cannot be asked when.*”

“*The next one.*”

“*The second column is the holdings, with the number of the holding and what it is. It carries a number, and the number is cut on a stake, and the number and the thing in the book have been the same number for four hundred years.*”

“*The next one.*”

“*The third column is of the fold, and it carries the number of acres of a hundred and forty that a household may put sheep on. A hundred and forty acres at four shillings the acre is twenty-eight pounds a year, and it is divided among about sixty households, and that is the whole of the money this town has and it is not in this book once.*”

The third column took about four seconds to sit in a room with the door open, and it is the only one of the four that is money.

“*The fourth column is of the ground behind the meeting-house, in feet and inches, going back about two hundred and twenty years. It carries a depth, and it can carry a name, and it can carry a day, and it does not carry the fact that the man is dead, and that is the whole of why a name is in it.*”

“*Say the four again, in the other order, because I have written them down in the other order.*”

“*A name, a number, a quantity and a depth. Those are the four things. And the four things that are not on it are a day, a year, a death and a person who does not want to be in it.*”

About four people in that room understood all four of the four and about nine heard a woman read four things a book does not carry.

---

“*The fifth thing was said from a chair by a fire in this building ten days ago,*” she said, “*and I am putting it in the list because it is in this building and nobody has entered it.*”

Barnaby Rill was in the chair. He had not been sent for and had not been told.

“*A thing a person remembers is a thing that is not a copy and is not a copy of a copy, and there is no column for it, and I have been that person twice in this town and I have said nothing both times.*”

“*That is two lines in a list and I am not entering either of them,*” said the recordwright.

“*I know you are not. I said it ten days ago in a smithy in front of about nine people and about four of them heard the place where it stopped and about nine did not, and the difference is not a thing I can put in a list.*”

---

“*Three things have been said out loud in a lane in this one week,*” she said, “*and I am going to name them and then I am going to say why they are in a list, and neither of those is a document.*”

“*The first was on the Friday in a lane behind the bottom of this town. A woman of about fifty-one asked for her two rooms to be looked at by about nine people one at a time.*”

“*She gave the reason and the reason was that there are only about nine people in this county who can be sent for, and she did not want all nine of them in a room at once.*”

“*The second was on the Sunday in a lane at Kiln Row, where a woman of sixty-one said that she was going to stop, and gave the reason, and a man of thirty-one asked her in a porch not to.*”

“*And the third was in a lane on the Tuesday of this week, where the same woman of about fifty-one told a man who had offered her his room that he was the fastest anybody had ever said anything to him in this country.*”

About four people in that room had been in one of those three lanes and about nine had not.

“*And the fourth thing is nine miles off. It is six lines on the back of a form, and it is the first return anybody in that county has made that does not record an answer to a question.*”

“*The form has no line for two, so it is on the back, and a man of fifty-four signed the back and did not sign the front.*”

She did not read the six lines out. About four people in that room had not heard them and about nine had, and she said why.

“*I am not reading them because they are in a coat in a drawer nine miles off, and there is no form in this county that says they may be asked for.*”

“*I have been told about them in about nine sentences, and I am not going to be the person who puts six lines about a disagreement between two towns into a room with the door open.*”

---

Then a woman of about fifty at the door said that she wanted to write it down, and about four people in that room had the same thought inside about four seconds and about nine had not.

“*You can write it down and it will be wrong in about a year,*” said the recordwright.

“*Then I will write it down anyway.*”

“*You will get the four things right, because I have said them four times in about nine minutes. You will get the three lanes wrong, because I named them in an order and you will write them in a different one.*”

“*And in about a year there will be a person in this town who reads your version and says the lanes happened in a different order.*”

“*And whose is the version then?*”

“*Yours. And you will be at the bottom of it with your name, and there is no form in this county that says I can be sent for to ask you about it, and there is no form that says I cannot.*”

About four people in that room had worked out what a list that is read out once in a room with the door open actually is. It is a thing in about nine mouths. There is one woman of about fifty at the door with a slate and a stub of chalk and no second copy.

About nine people in that room heard a recordwright say out loud that a thing written down in a room is a thing that has an owner by the second day.

Then the man of thirty-four said that he had done this for about eleven years in four counties and had never once watched it fail in the way she had just described.

The recordwright said that he had keepers and that she did not, and that a list with nine keepers behind it and a list with one are not the same instrument, and about nine people in that room had not thought about that at all.


Then she stopped reading, and she said the thing that the whole week had been getting to, and she said it standing with the slate under her arm.

“*That is the whole of it. A name, a number, a quantity, a depth, four things a book does not carry, a fifth thing a man said out of a chair, three things said in a lane, and six lines on the back of a form in a drawer.*”

“*It is a list.*”

“*It is a list. And a list is not a finding, and a finding is not a decision, and I entered two findings in three weeks and I have not carried a single thing in four weeks.*”

“*You have not carried a single thing and I would like to know what that is worth.*”

“*Nothing. It is worth nothing and I have said so out loud in a square twice and I will say it in a room a third time, and a finding that carries nothing is worth exactly what a person can be asked about it for.*”

And then the man of thirty-four said it, and he said it in about nine words, and about nine people in that room heard all of it and about four of them understood the whole of it in about four seconds.

“*There are two kinds of safety and they look the same from the outside.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*A thing is safe because somebody holds it. A thing is safe because nobody may take it. The first is a room with a person in it who can be sent for and asked. The second is a room with a rule in it that says nobody may go in, and there is nobody in it.*”

“*And which one is better.*”

“*I do not know, and neither do the four counties. I will say the two things I have seen instead. The first is a room with a person in it, and when the person cannot come in on a Wednesday the room is shut and nobody notices for about a year.*”

“*And the second.*”

“*The second is a room with a rule in it, and the rule holds, and in about four years there is a person who says nobody is allowed in and nobody remembers why, and there is no form that says they may be asked.*”

About nine people in that room could say that difference out loud by the time he had finished. About nine could not, and the two groups were not the same two groups and nobody in that room can be asked which was which.

---

And then the man of thirty-one asked her to enter it as a finding, and that was his want and it was the plainest one he has had in this country, and it cost about four minutes and it got him nothing.

“*Enter it as a finding.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill.

“*Then it is a list that has been read out in a room with the door open in front of about nine people.*”

“*There is a man of eighty-one in a chair who cannot read the third hand. There is a man of about seventy-two who is not in this town. There is a woman of sixty-one in a barn who cannot be sent for either, and a woman of about fifty-one in two rooms who can be sent for and has not been asked about any of it.*”

“*All of that is true and none of it is a finding.*”

“*Then what is it.*”

“*It is a list. And I am not going to convert a list into a finding by saying the word, and here is the reason in about four sentences and then I am going to sit down because I have been standing since the seventh hour.*”

She did not sit down.

“*A finding goes into a book without anybody asking the people it is about. That is what a finding is. It is a thing entered about a person and the person is not asked, and that is why it needs a keeper who can be sent for, and it is why a country that has one has a court of some kind.*”

“*And this county has neither.*”

“*And this county has neither, and I have been the one who has not asked for about nine cases out of a hundred in nineteen years.*”

“*The tenth is a widow of sixty-eight who came because a man in a chair asked her to come. The eleventh is a boy of fourteen who writes the week. The twelfth is a man of forty-four on a Monday morning. Not one of the three of them is a person I asked.*”

She looked at him and she said the rest of it without any weight on it at all.

“*And the thirteenth is going to be a man of thirty-one who is not a person of this county, and I have had four days to think about whether it has to be a finding at all, and it does not.*”

Nobody in that room thanked anybody. A man of forty-four was not thanked on the twelfth of those, and a man of thirty-one was not thanked on the thirteenth, and about four of the nine people in that room understood that he had not been refused the finding for anything about the finding.

The fever was thirty-six weeks and two days old. Four columns and no fifth were read out in a room eight feet by eleven in front of about nine people.

Then a fifth thing said from a chair by a fire, and three things said out loud in a lane in one week, and six lines on the back of a form nine miles off that she would not read out.

And then the difference between two kinds of safety, which about nine people in that room could say out loud afterwards and about nine could not.
