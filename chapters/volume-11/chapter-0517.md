# Chapter 517: The First Day Of The Ninety-Sixth Week, And The Day Before Was The Last Of The Ninety-Fifth, And A Tuesday On Which It Is Said Out Loud In A Porch By A Woman Of Sixty-One That The Two Objections At The Foot Of That Entry Are Both Against The Same Three Words, Which Are Nothing Is Carried, And The Finding Goes In At The Back Of The Book With The Day On It

First day of the ninety-sixth week, and the day before was the last day of the ninety-fifth. His two hundred and sixty-seventh morning. One hundred and seventeen days after the settlement. The fever thirty-three weeks and three days. Seventy-one days since the division. Seventeen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The morrow is the first day of the ninety-sixth and the day before was the last of the ninety-fifth, and there is one week boundary in these ten days and both sides of it say so.

There has never been two objections on anything in that book. There are two now, at the foot of one entry, and they went in on two different days by two different hands, and neither of them knew the other had done it.

About four people in this town had worked out since the Saturday that the two of them are against the same three words, and all four of them had worked it out alone, and none of them had said it out loud, because a person who says a thing like that in a porch is a person who has been wrong in front of about nine people and would have to find out about it by Wednesday.

She had the book on the step, open at the back, on the third leaf from the back and not the last, and she did not read the two objections out of it and neither did anybody else. Everybody in that porch had heard both of them read in the last twelve days and neither of them is short.

Harl Wenlock’s is four sentences. It is against the claim and not against the page, and it asks for the page to be read on the Saturday, and it asks for the page to be read to everybody in the square and not to nine people in a room, and it says that that is a separate thing and he is not confusing them. It says that a claim is not a claim on his ground. And it says that he is not a man anybody is going to be able to find.

Edith Crow’s is on a loose leaf out of the vestry cupboard, and it is four sentences. It says that if the page goes back in she is descended from somebody on it and is kin to whoever is kin, and that a boy of nine at Kiln Lane will be able to come and say he is her cousin. It says that she has paid for four rooms and a yard for twenty-two years and has never had a title to them in her hand and never wanted one, because everybody on that street knows, and that is what a street is, and a book is not a street.

It asks that it be written down that she said no before anybody argued with her and not after.

Nobody had said so. The recordwright had written it in the margin on the Tuesday, that she thought they were, and had put the day on it, and had said out loud in a porch that if she was wrong somebody could put her right in a year.

Five days went. Nobody put her right in a year, and about four people in that town went about their work in the meantime not knowing that the woman who keeps the book had written down that she thought so.

---

He had been carrying it since the Sunday, which was the sixth day of the ninety-fourth week and the ninth day back from this one, in a vestry room nine feet by seven with the door shut on purpose for the first time in nineteen years.

A person who is written back into a record is owed the thing the record carries. A person who is written back into a record is also owed the burden of the record, and the second of those is the one nobody is designing for.

He had read that standing against a wall a foot above the level of a sill. He had understood the whole of it in about nine seconds. He had not said a word of it out loud in the nine days since that Sunday. He had said it to himself in a bed, on a stone bench, on a wall, and at a pump.

At about the fourth hour on the Tuesday Ilyan Vester was in the porch, and about nine people were in it, and he got as far as the first four words of it.

And a woman of sixty-one said it first, in about eleven seconds, and it was not his.

“*They are both against the same three words,*” Odalie Fenn said.

Nobody in that porch had said a word for about four seconds, and she did not wait for it to pass.

“*Harl Wenlock objects to a claim. He has a bill of sale and the holding numbered five, and he said in a market square that a claim will cost him the next one along the day somebody works out what a claim is worth. Edith Crow objects to being put back into that book at the cost of a claim on four rooms and a yard she has paid for twenty-two years.*”

She put two fingers on the step.

“*And the three words at the foot of that entry are the three words that neither of them is objecting to. The three words are nothing is carried.*”

“*You have had five days for that,*” said Marda Rill.

“*I have had five days, and a bundle of thirty-two letters, and four years of nothing else to do with either of them. I have been in nine towns in those four years and two of them had this exact thing in it, and in both of them somebody had called it something else so that it could go on being done.*”

Nobody in that porch knew what the other thing was called and about four of them wanted to ask.

“*A man said it to me in a kitchen nine years ago and he called it a burden, and he was right, and I have thought about that word for nine years.*” She was not loud and she was not performing it. “*I have not been in a room in this county that had two people in it who were objecting to the same thing and did not know it. I have not been in one anywhere.*”

“*Then you have said it in a porch,*” said the recordwright. “*And there is no form that makes a porch a room where a book is read, and that is the only reason you are standing on that step, and I would like it entered while you are in front of me.*”

“*Enter it,*” said Odalie Fenn.

So Marda Rill entered it. She entered it in about four minutes, at the back of that book, on a fresh leaf, and she put the day on it.

The day on it is the seventeenth day of a count that was begun in another county, in a town two days and four miles of lane from here, and it is not the counting of this town, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a count begun in another county may not be used to date an entry in this one, and none that says it may.

The finding is that the two objections are not a quarrel between two people about a half-acre. They are one sentence, said twice, about what an entry does to a person who is already in one.

Nobody thanked the woman of sixty-one for saying it. She had not asked to be thanked and there is no form in this county that says a person may thank another in a porch.

What she did ask for was this. She asked to be told if the two people the finding is about were ever put in a room about it.

The two of them were not in that porch. Neither of them was sent for. The recordwright said the reason in about two sentences: a finding is a finding and not a notice, and a notice has to go to two people who have to be asked whether they agree, and if she asked them she would be asking them in about four minutes whether they still objected, and four of the nine people in that porch thought that was not the same question and about nine did not.

The recordwright said she did not know of a thing that was going to happen, and that there was no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be sent for to be asked whether they still object to a thing they objected to a fortnight ago.

The woman of sixty-one said that was the answer she expected and had now had four times in four years, and that she had stopped minding about two years back, and that a person who minds four times is a person who will say something stupid on the fifth.

---

And the man of thirty-one had about nine seconds.

They are the nine seconds a man gets in a room when somebody has said the thing he came into the room with. There are not many of them left in him. The whole of his habit in a hundred and seventeen days is to get to the front of a thing and make it legible so that it can be held.

He wanted to have said it first. He wanted it the way a man wants a chair.

He used the nine seconds on not saying it, and he has done that twice now, in two counties, and both times it was the correct use and neither time it was any use to him at all.

He had a finding. He had a thing he was good at. Somebody else had spent eleven seconds in front of about nine people, and there was nothing left for him in that porch except to agree, and agreeing loudly would have been the fourth time in a hundred and seventeen days that he had made a thing about himself in a room where a person was already saying it.

“*It is right,*” he said. “*It has been right since the Sunday and I did not say it in the nine days since that Sunday, and that is not a virtue and I am not going to stand on this step and be praised for it.*”

He stopped there, and about nine people in that porch waited, and he had to put the second half out because he had promised himself in about ninety-four rooms that he would not leave half a sentence standing.

“*I would like it noticed that I had it first. I would like it noticed that I saw the other person get to it, and that noticing took about nine seconds, and that all I did with the nine seconds was not talk.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything about that. About four of them understood the whole of it. About four understood only that he was a man of thirty-one who was not of this town and could not be sent for.

Those two groups were not the same two groups, and nobody in that porch can be asked which was which.

Then the recordwright read the finding out again, standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, with the day on it and nothing else added to it.

“*Entered on the seventeenth day of a count begun in another county, which is not the counting of this town. A finding, and it is a finding and not a decision, and nothing is carried by it. The two objections at the foot of the entry of the fifteenth day of the ninety-third week are both against the three words nothing is carried. Those three words are not about a half-acre. And a person who is already in an entry does not get a second thing out of an entry, and does not get a second thing put on him either.*”

Nobody in that porch had heard it put that way. About nine people in that town had been arguing about the second half of it for a fortnight, in porches, at pumps, and in a market square. About four of them had known all along. About five had not, and were not going to be told by a book, and the recordwright said so out loud, because she has said every other hard thing about that book out loud in the last three weeks and there was no reason to start making an exception for this.

What was still not done at about the sixth hour of that Tuesday was the only thing that mattered about the finding, and the recordwright wrote it at the foot of the leaf in her own hand and did not read it out, because it was not hers to read out.

*Neither of the two people this finding is about has been told of it.*

The fever was thirty-three weeks and three days old. Three words went in at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old on the seventeenth day of a count that is not the counting of this town, and the three words were that nothing is carried, and a woman of sixty-one said them in about eleven seconds in a porch, and a man of thirty-one had had them for nine days and did not say them, and nobody was thanked for either.
