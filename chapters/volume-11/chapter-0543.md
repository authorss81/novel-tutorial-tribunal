# Chapter 543: The Sixth Day Of The Ninety-Ninth Week, And A Sunday On Which The One Entry In The Back Of A Book That Makes The Woman Who Keeps It The Only Person Anybody Can Be Sent For Is Asked About Out Loud In A Room With The Door Open In Front Of About Nine People, And She Says Yes, And The Reason Is Not That Anybody Has Convinced Her Of Anything, And It Is That She Wrote A County And Not A Person, And A Man Of Thirty-One Is The Reason It Is Broken And Is Told So Out Loud In The Same Room

Sixth day of the ninety-ninth week. His two hundred and ninety-third morning. One hundred and forty-three days after the settlement. The fever thirty-seven weeks and one day. Ninety-seven days since the division. Forty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The room was the chancel, eight feet by eleven, with the door open and the window down, and there was no book out of the vestry cupboard. The book was in the cupboard with the door shut on the key on a string.

About nine people went in at about the eleventh hour. That room has held about nine people at a time since the seventh and last day of the ninety-eighth week, and the reason anybody is in it is that the woman who keeps the book opened it and did not ask who wanted to come.

Nobody wanted to come. There was nothing on the table. That is worth saying exactly, because a room with about nine people in it and nothing on a table is the first thing of its kind in this county in nineteen years.

---

The man of thirty-four from a town four days off had been in that room once, on the Monday, and had stood at the window and not come further in, and he asked at the door for a thing about four seconds before he came in.

“*Is this the Monday that was arranged.*”

“*The Monday that was arranged is the morrow,*” said the recordwright. “*I said in a square on Thursday that I have a man in a room on Monday who has to ask me a question first. That is still true and I have not moved it.*”

“*Then why am I here on the Sunday.*”

“*Because I opened the room and you were in it and there was nothing on, and if I had said that on Thursday in a square there would have been about two hundred people in front of it by now.*”

About four of the nine people in that room worked out what that meant. Barnaby Rill was in the chair by the fire and had not been sent for and had not been told. The woman of about fifty-one was against the wall. A woman of about forty from the wool bench. The man of about thirty-four. The man of thirty-one, at the end, standing, with his leg out at the angle.

Nobody thanked anybody for being in a room with nothing in it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for standing in one.

---

Then Ilyan Vester said the thing that opened the afternoon, and he said it standing at the end of the room, and about four of the nine people there heard the shape of it before he had got to the end of the sentence.

“*I have been a person of this country for a hundred and forty-three days and I have not been asked one question by anybody in it.*”

The woman of about fifty-one put her hand flat on the back of a chair and left it there, and about four seconds went by.

“*And I have found out in the last four days why. There is an entry in the back of that book in her hand that says nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original. It has been there twenty-four days. I have been counting it as a protection since the Thursday I found out it existed, and I have been wrong about that in about nine rooms and I did not know I was counting.*”

“*Say the rest of it, and say it to the room and not to me, because a man of thirty-one who has been refused in about nine rooms gets a shape that is wrong and you have had one in the lane behind this town.*”

“*There is nothing behind me to ask, so nobody has asked me anything, and I have been calling that a kind of safety. It is the same word a man from another county used on Monday about two different things, and I have had his sentence for four days and I have not put the two halves of it together until this morning.*”

The man of thirty-four put his hand on the back of a chair and did not let go of it.

“*Say the other half of that again, because you have just used a word I gave this room on Monday and I want to know whether you have got my half or the whole of it.*”

“*A thing is safe because somebody holds it and a thing is safe because nobody may take it. You said that. And I have been the second one for a hundred and forty-three days and I did not know which I was.*”

Barnaby Rill understood that inside about four seconds from the chair, and about nine people in that room heard a man describe himself as a room with a rule in it.

---

“*Then say what you want,*” said the man of thirty-four, “*and say it in about nine seconds, because I have been in this county for fourteen days and I am going to ask one question this afternoon and I am not going to be the instrument of a thing you wanted instead.*”

“*I want the entry broken.*”

“*That is not a question. That is a want, and I have been told what to do with a want once this week and it was by a man who could not be sent for.*”

“*Then ask her.*” Ilyan Vester turned round. “*Ask her whether it may be broken. In this room, in front of these nine people, and it is not notice and it is not me asking. It has to be a person of this county or it is worth nothing.*”

About four seconds went past in that room. A chair at the back of it took the weight of somebody standing up and then did not.

And then the man of thirty-four from the town four days off said the question, and he said it standing where he had been standing by the window, and he said it to her and not to the room.

“*Mrs Rill. Is the entry in the back of that book, the one that says nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original, allowed to be broken. I am asking because I have been in this county for fourteen days and you told me on Friday in a lane that the answer would be no, and I have been carrying that no for nine days and I would like it to be asked properly.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

“*Yes,*” said Marda Rill.

---

About nine people in that room heard her say it and about four of them had not understood what they had heard, and both of those groups were right about their own ears.

“*Say the reason,*” said the man of thirty-four, “*and say it before the room because the room is here and I did not ask in a lane.*”

She put the key on the table.

“*I am not going to say that anybody has persuaded me of anything, because nobody has and I should know. I wrote that entry twenty-four days ago. The Thursday of the ninety-sixth week, and this is the sixth day of the ninety-ninth week, and that is twenty-four days, and I have said the subtraction out loud because I am the only person in this county anybody can be sent for and I do not do a figure in my head in front of nine people.*”

“*That is the day and not the reason.*”

“*The reason is that I wrote a place and not a person.*”

She said the rest of it standing, in the voice she uses for minutes, and it took her about four sentences.

“*It says that nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original. It does not say that nobody may be asked. It says a county. And the person who has just asked me is not of this county, so there was never anything in that entry standing between him and the question, and I wrote it in a room with the door shut in about nine minutes without anybody in the room to tell me that.*”

“*And that is the whole of it.*”

“*That is the whole of it and I have had twenty-four days of it and I did not have it on the first day, and a rule a person can break because they came from somewhere else is not a rule about people. It is a rule about a place. And I have spent nineteen years being the only person anybody in this county can be sent for, and the whole of what I have to show for it is a line in my own hand that stops at a boundary.*”

The man of thirty-four from four days off understood the whole of that inside about four seconds, and about nine people in that room heard a woman say that she wrote a place and not a person.

---

Then she said the other half of it, and she said it to a man of thirty-one who had been standing at the end of that room for about eleven minutes saying nothing.

“*And I am going to say this next part to you and not to the room, because you are the reason it happened and about four people in here have worked that out in the last two minutes and about nine have not.*”

He did not turn round.

“*A rule broken because a stranger wanted to know something is a rule a stranger has broken. I said on Friday in a lane that the answer would be no so that nobody could ask me privately in a fortnight and get something else. I said it to be safe. And this morning a man said out loud in a room with the door open that he has been a rule instead of a person for a hundred and forty-three days, and a man from a town four days off turned round and asked me the question I had made sure could not be asked.*”

About four of the nine people in that room heard the whole of that and about nine heard a woman give a man an account of his own want.

“*I am not angry with you and I am not going to say that in a room. I want you to hear that you did it, and that it was the right thing to want and the wrong way to want it, and those are two different sentences and I am saying both.*”

---

And then she did the part that is the arrangement, standing by the table with her key in her hand.

“*I am not striking the entry out. A strike is a thing this county has done once in a hundred and forty-one years and it was done with a heavier ink than anything in that book and no order named, and I am not going to do a small one of those.*”

She took a leaf of the same paper as the leaf the copy of the four pages is entered on, and she wrote on it in the ordinary hand, with the day on it, and she ruled nothing and headed nothing.

*Entered on the sixth day of the ninety-ninth week, in a room with the door open, in front of about nine people, that the entry at the back of this book which says that nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original was asked about by a person, in that room, in front of those people, and the keeper said that it may be broken.*

*That the asking happened first and was a question and not a notice.*

*That the reason given was that the entry says a county and not a person, and that the person who asked is not of this county.*

*That nothing about the original has been entered here, because nobody in this county has the original and nobody in this county has seen it, and this town has four pages in it and not one of them is the original.*

Then she read it out to the room and not to the lane, and about nine people heard all four of those sentences.

“*Say the last one again,*” said Barnaby Rill from the chair.

“*That nothing about the original has been entered here, because nobody in this county has the original and nobody in this county has seen it.*”

“*Then what has that entry been keeping out for twenty-four days.*”

“*A question,*” she said. “*That is what it has been keeping out. Not a person. Nobody in this county has anything to be asked about, and the only woman in this county who has seen a square with about two hundred people in it wrote four pages out of her own memory in about four minutes and has never told anybody, and she is standing against that wall and she is the one person this county could send for.*”

The woman of about fifty-one said nothing at all for about four seconds, and then she said one sentence.

“*I have not been asked about the original either, and I have not been asked about anything.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill. “*And I have not asked you and I am not going to ask you today, and there is no form in this county that says I may, and if that changes this afternoon then it changes on a day somebody asks me in a room with the door open and not in a lane.*”

---

Nobody in that room thanked anybody and nobody in that room was sorry for anybody.

A man of thirty-four had asked a question and been told yes. A woman of thirty-four had said yes about twenty-four days after she had promised in a lane that the answer would be no, and she had given the reason and the reason was not that she had been persuaded. A man of thirty-one had wanted a thing and had been told in a room, in about nine words, that he was the reason it happened and that wanting it was right and wanting it that way was wrong.

Nobody in that room was thanked for any of it and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for having an entry broken, or for having one broken against them.

The fever was thirty-seven weeks and one day old. An entry twenty-four days old in the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old was asked about in a room with the door open in front of about nine people, and it was allowed to be broken, and the reason it was broken was that the woman who wrote it had written a county and not a person.

A man of thirty-one was standing at the end of that room when it happened, and the only protection he had had for a hundred and forty-three days was that nobody could ask him anything, and about four people in that room knew he had just spent it and about nine did not.
