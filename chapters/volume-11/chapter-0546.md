# Chapter 546: The Second Day Of The Hundredth Week, And A Wednesday, Which Is The Day A Man Of About Seventy-Two Walks Two Miles To Hear One Line Read Out About Four Acres At The Bottom Of The Lane, And On Which The Woman Who Keeps The Book Asks Him At Last The Question He Asked To Be Asked About Fourteen Days Ago, And He Says No, And It Is Entered At The Back Of A Book Four Hundred And Eleven Years Old, And It Is The Second Thing In Four Hundred And Eleven Years That Records A Person Declining Something, And She Says Out Loud In Front Of About Nine People That It Is Two People And One Afternoon And Not A Form And Not A Practice And Not A Thing Anybody Else May Do

Second day of the hundredth week. His two hundred and ninety-sixth morning. One hundred and forty-six days after the settlement. The fever thirty-seven weeks and four days. One hundred days since the division. Forty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

A Wednesday is the day a man of about seventy-two walks two miles to hear one line read out of a book four hundred and eleven years old about four acres at the bottom of the lane. It is not a public day and it is not written anywhere. It is done because he does it and because his father did it.

He came at the ninth hour, in a dry coat, in a dry week, and he stood at the end of the bench in the place about two-thirds along it where the stone is worn.

It took about eleven minutes and that is the whole of an ordinary Wednesday. She read him one line. He said that was right. She said it had been right the whole time and it would be right after him. Then she asked him whether he wanted to go and look at the stake, and he said no, which is what he has said the last six times.

About four people in that porch had come and gone in the eleven minutes and about nine had come and stayed.

---

Then she asked him, and she asked him in the porch with the door of the room behind her open and the window down, and about nine people were in it and about four of them had been in the porch for the eleven minutes and had not come away.

“*Mr Kettle.*”

“*Mrs Rill.*”

“*I said on the Wednesday of the ninety-eighth week that I had not said yes and had not said no, and that I had thought about it for two days, and that is what I had. It has been fourteen days since you asked me that in this porch and twenty days since I asked you a question in that room, and I have had a fortnight to decide and I have decided this morning and I am going to say what I have decided before anybody asks me a question about it.*”

Barnaby Rill, not being in that porch, understood it on Wednesday morning and had told nobody. About nine people in that porch heard a woman say a number out loud.

“*Say it slower, and say the numbers again, because a keeper who has been wrong about a figure in this county has been wrong about it once and everybody in that room heard it.*”

“*Fourteen days since you asked me. Twenty days since I asked you anything at all. I have said both out loud because I am the only person in this county anybody can be sent for and I do not do a figure in my head in front of nine people.*”

Then she said it.

“*You asked to be asked about it again, once, and you said three things with it, and I have kept all three and I am going to keep them this morning.*”

“*Say them, because I am seventy-two and I want to know that this room has them.*”

“*You said you did not want to be told what you said last time. You said you did not want anybody told what you say. And you said you did not want it read out in the square on a Saturday.*”

“*Ay.*”

“*Then here is the question and I am going to ask it once. On the morning of the thirtieth day after the division, in the square in the town two days from here, before about two hundred people: were you asked a question.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything for about four seconds, and about nine people were in it.

And the man of about seventy-two said no.

---

He said it in about four seconds and he said it the way he says *aye*, and then he said the rest of it, and he said it to her and not to the porch.

“*No to the question and no to the answer, and no to whether I gave one, and no to the four lines at the foot of the third page, and no to the name at the foot of the fourth one.*”

“*Say the reason, because I am going to write this down in a moment and I am going to write what you say and not what I think you mean.*”

“*I asked you to ask me, so that I would know what a room with about nine people in it does with a man who says he does not remember. I have found out. It does nothing at all.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*I am sixty-eight next year. I have walked two miles on a Wednesday for about nine years and I have got about nine things to say at the end of it. I am not going to give the tenth to a piece of paper.*”

The man of thirty-four from four days off understood the whole of that inside about four seconds and looked at the door of the room behind her.

“*Then what I have written down at the back of that book is what you have just said.*”

---

She wrote it at the back of that book, on a fresh leaf of the same paper as the leaf the copy of the four pages is entered on, in the ordinary hand, with the day on it. She ruled nothing and she headed nothing. She did not read it out to the porch.

Then she read the last four lines of it back to him, standing at the door of her own room with her back to about nine people, and about nine of them heard her say she was going to and about four heard what she read.

“*Entered on the second day of the hundredth week. A person of this town, of about seventy-two years, being asked in a porch, before about nine people, the question he had asked to be asked about, and declining it.*”

“*That he said he was asked no question and gave no answer on the morning of the thirtieth day after the division, and that this is his account and not a correction of the four lines at the foot of the third page of a copy of a copy, which stand.*”

“*That he has been told that he is named at the foot of the fourth page of that copy and he has not been asked whether it is right.*”

“*That no finding is entered and that nothing is carried and that there is no column in this book for a person declining something and none has been ruled.*”

“*That is four lines and I have read them back to you and not to that porch, because you asked me for that on the Wednesday of the ninety-eighth week and I have kept it for fourteen days.*”

---

Then she turned round and said the thing to the porch, and it took her about nine sentences, and about nine people were in it and about four of them understood all of it.

“*That is two people in four hundred and eleven years.*”

“*Two?*” said a man of about thirty at the end of the bench.

“*Two people and one afternoon each. A woman of about seventy on the sixth day of the ninety-sixth week, in a lane behind this town, who asked that the striking of a name she is descended from should be left on a sheet of paper in a room. And this man, this morning, in this porch.*”

“*And what about the woman of about twenty-eight on Tuesday, who said she did not know and would not guess?*”

“*She declined nothing. She was asked what a household of a hundred and forty-one years back is owed and she said she did not know and would not guess, and that is an answer and it is not a no to a thing about her own name. There are two and not three, and I have counted them twice this morning because I am the only person who can count them.*”

About four of the nine people in that porch had worked out that she had been awake counting them. About nine heard a woman say the number two.

“*Now I am going to say the other half of it, and I said the other half of it in a lane twenty-four days ago and I am going to say it again here because this is the porch and it is a Wednesday and about nine people are in it.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*It is not a form. It is not a practice. It is not a thing anybody else may do. Two people, one name each, two afternoons, and a keeper who is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for and who is not paid anything for either afternoon.*”

---

And then she said the sentence that she had said in a lane a fortnight ago about the first one, and the whole of that porch heard her say that it was now true of her and not of somebody else.

“*On the Sunday of the ninety-sixth week I stood in a lane behind this town and I said some of that. If a second person in this county says no to a thing about her own name, then what happens to that is a question. It will have to be worked out by whoever is there at the time, and it will not be by me at somebody else’s asking.*”

About four seconds went past in that porch and a woman of about thirty-five with a child on her hip looked down at the child.

“*It has come to the time. It is a Wednesday and not a Tuesday and I am in a porch and not a lane, and the second person came to it in the room on Monday and not in a porch, and neither of those is the arrangement I said and I am not going to pretend that it is.*”

“*And the question has to be worked out by whoever is there at the time, and this morning whoever is there at the time is me, and I have had a fortnight to find somebody else to work it out and there is nobody in this county to send for about it.*”

“*So here is what I am going to say and I am saying it to nine people and not to a form.*”

She turned round from the door.

“*If a third person in this county says no to a thing about their own name, then what happens to that is a question, and it will have to be worked out by whoever is there at the time. And I am not going to be the one who says what it is. I will enter what the person says and I will not rule a column and I will not head one and I will not tell them in advance what it does, because I could not tell myself and I have tried for fourteen days.*”

Nobody in that porch thanked anybody. A man of about seventy-two was not thanked for saying no in four seconds and a woman of thirty-four was not thanked for entering it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for either.

---

The man of thirty-one was at the end of that porch for the whole of it and had been asked nothing, and before the reading of the four lines he had asked the keeper one question in about four seconds, standing at the door, and about nine people in that porch heard both the question and the answer.

“*Would he mind me being in the porch.*”

She had not made him ask. She had looked at him once and he had asked, and about four of the nine people in that porch understood in about four seconds what a man who cannot be sent for standing in a porch is, and about nine did not.

“*He would not mind you being in the porch. He has not said so and I have not asked him, because I am not going to ask a man of seventy-two a second question in one morning.*”

“*I will stand at the end of it and not say anything.*”

“*You will stand at the end of it and not say anything. There is no form in this county that says I can make you stand there. There is no form that says I can make you go away.*”

“*That is the same thing I said to a man from a town four days off in a porch three weeks ago, and you were in the room.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*You are right to stand at the end of it. And a man who cannot be sent for standing in a place is a man who cannot be told to leave it.*”

“*I would like somebody in this porch to notice that about you, because in about four years there will be a person in this town who has been told that strangers are not kept out of porches.*”

The woman of about fifty-one understood all of that. About nine people in that porch heard a woman say that a man might stand at the end of it.

The fever was thirty-seven weeks and four days old. The question a man of about seventy-two asked to be asked about, on the Wednesday of the ninety-eighth week, was asked of him at last on the second day of the hundredth week, in a porch, before about nine people, and he said no.

It was entered at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old with the day on it. Nothing was ruled and nothing was headed. It is the second thing in four hundred and eleven years that records a person declining something, and the keeper of that book said out loud in front of about nine people that it is two people and two afternoons and not a form and not a practice and not a thing anybody else may do.

And she said that if a third person in this county says no to a thing about their own name, then what happens to that is a question that will have to be worked out by whoever is there at the time, and she would not be the one who says what it is.
