# Chapter 536: The Sixth Day Of The Ninety-Eighth Week, And A Sunday On Which A Woman Of Sixty-One Who Has Written To Nine Towns In Four Years Asks For Pages Says Out Loud At The Door Of A Barn At Kiln Row, And Then In A Lane, That She Is Going To Stop, And Says Why, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks Her In A Porch In Front Of About Nine People To Keep Going, And She Tells Him That A Person Who Is The Reason A Thing Keeps Going Is A Person Who Cannot Stop

Sixth day of the ninety-eighth week. His two hundred and eighty-sixth morning. One hundred and thirty-six days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and one day. Ninety days since the division. Thirty-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

She has a bundle of thirty-two letters in it and she has had the bundle for four years, and the bundle is the reason she is in this town.

She is Odalie Fenn, sixty-one, a Keeper of returned names. She sleeps in a barn at Kiln Row. She is not of this county and there is no house here that she rents and no form in the county of Aldwick that lets a person be sent for out of a barn.

She has said the same sentence, or a version of it, thirty-one times in four years in nine towns, to about nine people at a time, and she has said it in a market square once with about two hundred people in it.

On the Saturday of the ninety-seventh week she read out one of her thirty-two letters, in a porch, four lines long, from a town four hundred miles off, and about nine people heard it and about four understood where it was going.

She had read one out of thirty-two in this county and had named four of them and nobody had asked her about the other twenty-eight.

---

She said it at the door of the barn at about the second hour of the afternoon and about four people were in the yard and about nine of the nine heard it.

“*I am going to stop.*”

Nobody in that yard said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it, because you have said the first four words of it in a yard and about four of us have known you for about five weeks and about nine have not.*”

“*I am going to stop asking nine towns for pages, and I am going to stop saying the sentence, and I have four years and nine towns of it and I have got it right, and I am stopping.*”

“*Why.*”

“*Because a thing said in nine towns for four years is a thing said in nine towns. That is the whole of what I have and it is not a small thing, and it has taken four years to be worth saying and it takes about nine words to stop.*”

Then she said the why, and she said it standing in a yard in front of about nine people, and it took her about four sentences.

“*The first of the nine towns to be asked a question about a person is going to be a town where somebody has heard me say a sentence that I was told.*”

“*You do not know that.*”

“*I do not know that and I cannot know it, and neither can they. That is the whole of it. I go into a town and I say a sentence, and the sentence is in me the way a coat is in me, and I cannot tell which parts of it are mine.*”

“*And you would not know the difference, and neither would they.*”

“*No. And that is the day I stop, and I have known it was coming since about the fourth hour of last Thursday when a man of about thirty-four said the word *shelf* in a market square in front of about two hundred people.*”

---

A man of thirty-one was at the wall of that yard and had been in it since the first hour, and his want that afternoon was the plainest one he has had in this country since the leaf was read.

He wanted her to keep going. He wanted nine towns to go on being asked, and he wanted a sentence about nine people to go on being said in a town of about two thousand four hundred people.

The two towns were still remembering one morning differently, and about nine people here could now give you both accounts, and that was the whole of what four weeks had produced.

He asked her in a porch at about the fourth hour, in front of about nine people, and he asked her in the wrong way and he asked her on the same grounds he had used in about nine rooms in a hundred and thirty-six days.

“*Keep going.*”

“*No,*” said Odalie Fenn.

“*The two towns that remember that morning differently are still remembering it differently. That is nine days old and it has not settled, and it is not going to settle, and the only reason anybody in this town knows that is because you and a woman of sixty-eight and a weaver of sixty-two have been going round it in the same mouths for a fortnight.*”

“*That is the reason and you have said it well.*”

“*Then keep going for that reason.*”

She looked at him and it took her about four seconds to work it out, and about four people in that porch had already worked it out and about nine had not.

“*So I am the reason a thing keeps going.*”

“*You are the reason about nine people in this town can give you both accounts. I have heard that said out loud in a smithy and I have written it down.*”

“*Then I am the reason, and a person who is the reason a thing keeps going is a person who cannot stop, and I cannot stop, and that is why I have to stop.*”

“*That is not a contradiction, that is just a person in a difficulty.*”

“*It is not a difficulty. It is the arrangement. I have had four years of it and about nine towns of it, and in every one of the nine towns I was the reason, and in every one of the nine towns the thing I was the reason for had my face on it.*”

She said the last of it standing in a porch, in front of about nine people, and it was the shape of the whole of the band and she had it ready in about nine seconds.

“*The last of the nine towns was a market town with a bar in the mud that had been down about three weeks. A woman of thirty-four there would not let a man read a line out of a room, and she gave him the reason in about four sentences.*”

“*And I sat on a bench for about an hour afterwards and worked out that I would not have given him those four sentences in the first year I went round.*”

“*Why not.*”

“*Because in the first year I would have wanted him to read the line. That is the difference between the first year and the fourth and I have just heard myself say it out loud in a porch in front of about nine people.*”

Nobody in that porch thanked anybody and nobody in that porch was sorry for anybody, and about four of the nine people in it understood the whole of what had just been said to a man of thirty-one and about nine heard a woman of sixty-one say that she was going to stop.

---

A man of eighty-one came down to that porch at about the fifth hour on a stick and nobody had sent for him and nobody had told him.

He had been in this town eighty-one years and he had been that person twice and had said nothing both times, and about four people in that porch had worked that out in about four seconds and about nine had not.

“*You have said that sentence in nine towns.*”

“*I have.*”

“*Who said it to you first.*”

Nobody on that step said anything for about four seconds, and about nine people in that porch heard a man of eighty-one ask a woman of sixty-one a question that four counties of people have never asked her, and about four of them understood that it was the first one.

“*I do not know,*” said Odalie Fenn.

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*I have got three answers and they are all true and I have never put them next to each other because putting them next to each other is the thing I do not do.*”

“*Say the three.*”

“*A woman of sixty-eight wrote it to me in a town two days off eleven years back and it was four lines and it was hers. A man of thirty-four in a market square wrote it on the back of a sheet and did not sign it and I have never seen him since. And a letter of four lines from a town four hundred miles off says it as a conclusion and does not say who wrote the letter.*”

“*And you have said it thirty-one times.*”

“*Thirty-one times in four years and about nine people at a time, and I have never once been in a room with the woman of sixty-eight, and the second one is in the county that the rest of us cannot be sent for, and the third one is four hundred miles off.*”

Barnaby Rill stood on that step for about four seconds and then he said the thing he had said in a smithy a fortnight ago in a different shape.

“*A page cannot be read backwards and a person can, and that is the only thing in that building that is not a page. I have watched four words walk round this town from a pump and I would not tell two women at a pump to stop, because a man of eighty-one telling nine people not to say a thing is the same thing with his name on the end of it.*”

“*And this is the same,*” she said.

“*This is the same and it has a worse end on it, and the end on it is a town two days off where a woman of sixty-eight has eleven years of it and has never been asked by anybody.*”

Nobody in that porch asked her to go and find out. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked to go to another county, and about four of the nine people on that step had worked out that the man of eighty-one had just made a thing that could not be done in about four days and about nine had not.

---

Then she walked down the lane at Kiln Row at about the fifth hour with about nine people, because about nine people walked with her, and she said one more thing in the lane and it was about the stopping and not about the years.

“*I am not going to hand the bundle to anybody.*”

“*Why not. There are thirty-two letters in it and about nine of them are about people in this county.*”

“*Because a bundle of thirty-two letters handed to a woman of thirty-four in a lane on a Sunday evening is a bundle of thirty-two letters that one person answers for, and I have been the answer for thirty-one of them for four years and I am telling you in a lane that I have had it.*”

“*Then it stays in a barn.*”

“*It stays in a barn with a woman of sixty-one in it, and no form in this county says a person may be sent for about a bundle, and in about four years there will be a person in this town who is told that the letters were given away and it will not be true.*”

“*You could write down what is in them.*”

“*I could, and I have not. There is a woman of thirty-four in this town who has just told me in a porch that she has a rule about when a thing may be broken and who asks first.*”

“*I have been in this country five weeks and I am not going to break anything before I have understood the rule.*”

About four of the nine people in that lane understood that. About nine heard a woman of sixty-one say that she was not going to give a bundle of thirty-two letters to a woman of thirty-four, and about nine of them heard the other half of it and did not.

The man of thirty-one asked her the question he had wanted to ask since the Tuesday of the ninety-seventh week, and he asked it in the lane, and about four of the nine people in it had already worked out that he was going to.

“*You read out one of your thirty-two letters on the Saturday of the ninety-seventh week. The fourth line of it says that a town four hundred miles off does not know what to do with a person who says no.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*This town has had one. A woman of about seventy said no in a lane in this town and the keeper of the book entered it, and the keeper has said out loud in a lane that it is not a form and not a practice and not a thing anybody else may do.*”

“*Then they have one and they do not know what to do with it either. I have nine towns that do not know what to do with it and about nine letters a year coming back about it.*”

“*And you are asking me to go on being the person who tells nine towns that this county has one as well.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*And you cannot go on doing it.*”

“*No. Because in about four years one of those nine towns is going to send a person here to find out how a woman of about seventy in Bramblefold said no, and that person is going to be told my sentence, because I have been saying it in nine towns for four years, and I will not know that I said it and neither will they, and I have not got a way of stopping that other than stopping.*”

“*Then say it to the person when they come.*”

“*And how do I know which of the two of me said it. That is the question I have been carrying since about the fourth hour of last Thursday, and I have got no answer to it.*”

“*I am stopping before I have to find out in a room with a stranger in it.*”

The fever was thirty-six weeks and one day old. A woman of sixty-one who has written to nine towns in four years asking for pages said out loud at the door of a barn at Kiln Row, and then in a lane at Kiln Row, that she was going to stop.

She gave the reason, which was that a person who is the reason a thing keeps going is a person who cannot stop, and a man of thirty-one heard it and got nothing out of it.
