# Chapter 540: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Ninth Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Four From A Town Four Days Off Says Out Loud In A Square With About Two Hundred People In It What He Came For, And It Is Not The Four Pages, And A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In Front Of About Two Hundred People That The Man Came For The Four Pages And Is Corrected In About Four Seconds By The Man Himself, And Nobody In That Square Believed A Word Of It

Third day of the ninety-ninth week. His two hundred and ninetieth morning. One hundred and forty days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and five days. Ninety-four days since the division. Forty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

It is a market on a Thursday. The square fills between about the eighth hour and the tenth, and it is about two hundred people and it is not two thousand.

The bars of the last Thursday are lying in the mud, and the sweep is done on the Wednesday night by two men who are paid a penny each out of the twenty-eight pounds a year that a hundred and forty acres of sheep bring in at four shillings the acre.

He has been in this town eleven days. He came down the lane on the Sunday of the ninety-seventh week and he said the word *shelf* in a porch on the Tuesday of this week and he has not said what he came for since, and about nine people in this town have worked that out and about nine have not.

---

He got to the middle of it at about the eleventh hour and he was not carrying anything, which was the second time in eleven days that he had stood in a public place in that county with his hands empty.

“*About two hundred people,*” he said. “*I have been in this square twice. On the Thursday of last week I said the word *shelf*. A man of thirty-one said a shelf in a room in this town was the only thing that would make a copy of four pages safe, and a woman at that bench said the other half of that in about four seconds.

“*And I have thought about it for seven days.*”*”

Nobody in that square stopped what they were doing. About nine of the about two hundred had heard all three parts of that sentence before and about nine had heard none of them.

“*I did not come for the four pages.*”

Then the man of thirty-one made his mistake, and it was his last one in this country for a while, and he made it in the open air in front of about two hundred people.

“*He came for the four pages.*”

He said it out loud, in a market square, at about the eleventh hour on a Thursday, in a town of about two thousand four hundred people, and about nine people in that square heard it and about nine of the about two hundred did not.

The man of thirty-four corrected him in about four seconds and it was not an insult and it was delivered in the voice of a man who has said the same thing to about nine people in four counties.

“*No.*”

“*You said the word *shelf* on the Tuesday and on the Thursday and you have looked at four pages and you have asked for a shelf in a square. Say that you did not come for them.*”

“*I did not come for the four pages, and I have said so in four counties to about nine people at a time, and I am not going to say it a fifth time in a louder voice because a man of thirty-one has said it wrong.*”

About four of the two hundred had worked out that the man from four days off was not angry and about nine had not.

“*Then say what you came for, in a square, in about nine sentences, and I will stand here and not say anything until you have finished.*”

---

He said it standing in the middle of that square at about the half hour after the eleventh, and it took him about nine sentences, and about two hundred people heard the word *shelf* and about four of them heard the whole of it.

“*I keep a shelf. I have kept one for about eleven years and I am one of nine of us in four counties and between us we have about four hundred shelves, which is the number of shelves and not the number of people.*”

“*The first thing I have come about is not a paper. It is a woman of thirty-four in this town who is the only person in this county who can be sent for about anything, and she has said so out loud in a smithy, and she is right.*”

“*In about eleven years she will be sixty. And there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what happens to a book when the person who keeps it cannot come in on a Wednesday.*”

About four people in that square understood all three of those sentences. About two hundred heard the number *eleven years* and about nine of them heard the word *Wednesday* and understood it.

“*I have watched two shelves stop being kept in about eleven years. One was a woman of sixty who died and the papers went into a house and the house could not read the hand. One was two people in one house who each thought the other had it.*”

“*In both of them there was a person who could have been sent for and no form that said so. I went back to both of them afterwards and wrote it down, and neither was anybody’s fault, and I have not told anybody a story about either of them since, because I would be telling it wrong.*”

Then he said the last of it, and it was the sentence he had come down the lane with.

“*I am not here to take anybody’s. I have said that in a porch and in a square and I am saying it a third time in front of about two hundred people, and nobody in this county has believed me, and I am not going to be helped by saying it again.*”

Nobody in that square believed him. About four people in it had understood the whole of what he came for and about nine had heard him say that he was not here to take anybody’s and had not believed a word of it, and both of those groups were right about their own ears.

---

The recordwright was at the edge of the square again, where she had been on the Thursday of last week, and she had not come into it and had not sent for anybody, and about four of the two hundred had noticed where she was standing.

She said three things and she said them into the middle of that square and not to the man of thirty-four.

“*There are about two hundred people in this square. About nine of them have heard the whole of what that man has just said, and about nine of them heard the word *shelf*, and about a hundred and eighty-two heard a man talking about a trade he does not live in.*”

“*And I can do the first of those. A square is the only instrument this county has for putting a thing in front of about two hundred people who did not ask, and I have said that out loud in this square twice.*”

“*And I cannot do the other one, and I have not pretended otherwise today.*”

“*Then what do you want from him,*” said a man of about thirty at the ninth stall.

“*Nothing this morning. I have a man in a room on Monday who has to ask me a question first. I said in a lane on Friday that it may not be broken in a lane.

“*And I am not going to be the second person this week who hears a thing in the open air and then carries it into a room about nine days later and says it in a different shape.*”*”

About four people in that square understood the whole of that. About nine heard a recordwright say that she was not going to be the second person that week who did something in a square.

---

And then she said the fourth thing, and it was the only thing she said that was about herself, and it was said in the open air in front of about two hundred people, and it was not a thank you and it was not a complaint.

“*He came down a lane to say that there is no form in this county that says what happens to a book when I cannot come in on a Wednesday, and he is right, and I have known it since about the fourth hour of last Thursday.*”

“*And I am not going to tell about two hundred people what I am going to do about it, and I have not told nine people, and I will not tell a room until a room has asked me first.*”

Nobody in that square thanked anybody. The man of thirty-four was not thanked for saying out loud in the open air what he had come four days for. The recordwright was not thanked for standing at the edge of a square and saying three things about a square. A man of thirty-one was not thanked for having been corrected in about four seconds by the man himself.

Nobody in that square believed a word of it.

---

And then a man of about thirty at the ninth stall asked the man of thirty-four, in the open air, whether it was true that four people in this town were each holding one of four pages, and about nine people in that square heard the question.

It was true, and the man of thirty-four said so, and then he said the whole of what it was and it took him about nine sentences.

“*Four people, in four houses, asked yesterday by a woman of about fifty-one in a room with the door open, in about four seconds each. She asked eight people. Four said yes and about four said no. She is not one of the four and neither is the woman who keeps the book.*”

“*And what happens if one of the four loses it?*”

“*Nothing that anybody in this county can find out, and I have been eleven days in this country and that is the first sentence I have said in it that I am certain of.*”

“*Then it is not a shelf.*”

“*No. A shelf has a keeper and a number on a board and a person who is paid, and this has four people and no keeper and no number and nobody paid, and it will not survive a death or a removal or a woman getting tired of it.*”

About four of the about two hundred understood all of that. About nine heard the word *shelf* used against itself for the first time in a market square.

Then the woman of about forty at the wool bench said one thing, and she had said the other half of something in this square six days ago, and about nine of the two hundred heard her say it.

“*I said on the Thursday of last week that a shelf is a thing a person is responsible for, and that nobody had asked the woman who keeps our book whether it was enough.*”

“*You did.*”

“*And on Tuesday a man who mends ploughs asked her, in a smithy, in front of about nine people, and she answered him, and about four of this square have heard about it from somebody and about nine have not.*”

“*And what has come of it.*”

“*Nothing. It came to nothing. She answered a question about money in a room, and a man of thirty-four answered a question about a Wednesday in this square, and a woman of about fifty-one asked nine people in a room.*”

“*And none of it is in a book, and that is the whole of what a week of work in a town of about two thousand four hundred people has produced.*”

About nine people in that square heard a woman at a wool bench say the number of things a week had produced. About four of them could not say afterwards what the things were.

---

And then the man of thirty-four said the last thing of the morning, and he said it to about nine people and not to about two hundred, and about four of the nine understood the difference.

“*About four of you have understood what I came for. About nine of you have heard the word *shelf*. About a hundred and eighty-two of you have heard a man talk.*”

“*And that is not a failure. A square is not a room and I have spent eleven days in this county learning the difference from a woman of thirty-four, and what I came for is a room-shaped thing and I have been putting it into a square-shaped place, and about four of you heard it, and that is about four more than I had.*”

The fever was thirty-six weeks and five days old. A man of thirty-four from a town four days off said out loud in a market square in front of about two hundred people what he came for, and it was not the four pages.

It was that a woman of thirty-four in this town is the only person in this county who can be sent for about anything, and that there is no form here that says what happens to a book when the person who keeps it cannot come in on a Wednesday.

About four people in that square understood the whole of what he came for, and about two hundred heard the word *shelf*, and a man of thirty-one said out loud that he had come for the four pages and was corrected in about four seconds, and nobody in that square believed a word of it.
