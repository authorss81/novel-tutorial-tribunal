# Chapter 545: The First Day Of The Hundredth Week, And The Day Before Was The Last Of The Ninety-Ninth, And A Tuesday On Which Four People Who Struck Their Own Names Out Of A List In A Square Thirty-One Days Ago Are Asked Separately In Four Different Rooms What The Household Of Kiln Row Is Owed, And They Are Four Different Answers, And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks A Weaver Of Sixty-Two In A Lane In Front Of About Nine People For The Four Of Them To Agree And Is Refused In About Four Seconds

First day of the hundredth week, and the day before was the last day of the ninety-ninth. His two hundred and ninety-fifth morning. One hundred and forty-five days after the settlement. The fever thirty-seven weeks and three days. Ninety-nine days since the division. Forty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The week of a country town is made on the Friday night and it is read on the Saturday, and there were four rooms in that town with somebody in them at about the tenth hour of this Tuesday morning, and none of the four rooms was the meeting-house.

The keeper of the book went to all four of them herself. She has said out loud in this building that she is the only person in this county anybody can be sent for, and a thing that is going to be asked four times in four rooms is a thing that has to be asked by the person who can be sent for afterwards.

She said the terms at the door of each one and she said the same terms four times.

“*You have been on a sheet with forty names and four gaps in it since the Saturday of the ninety-fifth week. You are not on it and you are descended from somebody who is. Nobody is going to write anything down this morning and I will not rule anything and I will not head anything.*”

“*And the question.*”

“*One question. What the household of Kiln Row is owed. That is the whole of it and it is the question a man of thirty-one has been carrying about this county since the Thursday before last and he is not the one who is going to ask you.*”

---

The first was a woman of about seventy who keeps a house on the lower row, and she came out to her own step, and about nine people were in the road because nine people were in the road on that row every day at that hour and nobody had told them to be.

“*A hundred and forty-one years is a long time to owe a person anything,*” she said. “*And it is four names and every one of them is under the ground. I have paid for four rooms and a yard in twenty-two years out of a mangle and a drying ground and eleven weeks a year teaching children to read over a chandler’s, and nobody has put a penny of that against this.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because a keeper has to write down what a person says and not what a keeper thinks a person means.*”

“*The rest of it is that you cannot be owed a thing by somebody who is dead. If you could, it would come to me, and I would have to be forty in a year, and I am sixty-eight and I have not got nine years in me for anything that is not the roof.*”

The woman of about fifty-one who had asked her understood the whole of that from about nine feet away. About nine people in that road heard a woman say that a household of a hundred and forty-one years back was owed nothing.

---

The second was a man of about thirty-five who had written the figures on the back of his hand on a Saturday and got them wrong and said he would rather be the one holding the chalk, and he was asked in his own front room with a fire in it and about nine people in it who had come to hear whether he was right about the figures.

“*They are owed the paper,*” he said.

“*Say the rest of that, because there is no paper in this question. There is a leaf with forty-three names on it and a return in a book nine miles off.*”

“*Then they are owed the leaf with forty names on it and four gaps in it, and not the leaf with forty-three. Because the leaf with forty-three says forty-three and a stroke and four words with no order, and it is a page about a Tuesday. The sheet says forty names and four gaps and four people struck their own names out of it with their own hands on a step in front of a hundred and ninety people, and nobody asked them and I have been one of them.*”

He put his thumb on the edge of his own table.

“*A person in about a hundred and forty years should be able to open that book and see both. Not a page about a Tuesday. A page about a Tuesday and a page about four people in a square, and neither of them lying.*”

---

The third was a woman of about twenty-eight and she was asked in a room over the chandler’s shop. She gave the shortest of the four answers and she gave it before the question had finished.

“*I do not know.*”

“*Say the rest of it, and then I will go, and I am not going to stand in this room and wait for you to find one.*”

“*The rest of it is that I will not guess, and I am not going to be asked the same question in two rooms this week and be told the first one was not the real one.*”

A man of about fifty who keeps a beast understood that she was not being difficult. About nine people in that room heard a woman say she did not know and stop there.

“*Write it down as not known.*”

“*I have never once been told you write things down and I have not asked whether you do, and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.*”

“*I am going to write down that you said you did not know and that you would not guess, and I am going to write down the day, and there will be no ruling and no heading and no column, and if that is a place in a book four hundred and eleven years old then it is a place with nothing at the top of it.*”

---

The fourth was a boy of about nineteen and he was asked in a kitchen at Kiln Row, and about four people were in that kitchen who had not come to be asked anything and had stayed.

“*A thing said out loud,*” he said. “*Once, in a square, by somebody who was not asked to say it, about what happened to them.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because the other three in this county have said what they are owed and two of them said nothing and one of them said paper, and you have said nine words and I do not yet know what they are for.*”

“*They are for a person in a hundred and forty years to know that the household of Kiln Row was here and was asked and answered, and not only that it was struck. Because there is no order named and there is no day and there is nobody’s name against the stroke, and a person in a hundred and forty years opens that book and finds a line through forty-three names and no way at all of working out what was done to them or by whom.*”

He looked at the door.

“*A page says what was done. A person in a square says what happened. They are not the same and one of them has somebody behind it.*”

---

Nobody thanked anybody in any of those four rooms. The woman of about seventy was not thanked for a kitchen step, and the man of about thirty-five was not thanked for a front room with a fire in it, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for an answer they did not agree with.

Nothing was entered. Nothing was ruled. Nothing was headed. There were four answers in four rooms in one morning and none of them was in a book by the fourth hour of the afternoon, and about four people in this county now knew all four answers and could not hold them at once.

---

And then the man of thirty-one did the thing he has done in about nine rooms in a hundred and forty-five days, and he did it in a lane at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with about nine people in it.

He wanted the four of them to agree. That is the whole of it. He had wanted it since the second room that morning and he had worked out in the fourth that if four people were asked the same question separately and came out of it with the same sentence then a person in about a hundred years would have a thing that could be entered and sent for.

He put it to the weaver at the corner of the garden wall, which is four feet of the white the kiln makes, and about nine of the stones in one stretch of it carry four letters and a stroke cut into the face of them that nobody in this county can say whose they are.

“*They have all said no,*” he said. “*Three of the four have said no in about nine words each and a man of thirty-five has said paper. If you ask them again on Friday, in the same words, one at a time, and I do not say anything at all while you do it, then either they will come out with the same sentence or they will not, and either way I will know what this town thinks.*”

The weaver refused him in about four seconds and he did not raise his voice and he had not raised it once in a hundred and forty-five days.

“*No.*

“*Four people who have each been asked a question by a stranger and answered it differently are the only evidence this town has. That is what they are. A sheet with forty names and four gaps in it is not evidence. A return in a book nine miles off is not evidence. A man who cannot be sent for saying *they ought to agree* in a lane is not evidence.*”

“*That is four sentences and I have been refused once since Saturday and I would like the whole of it.*”

“*The whole of it is the fifth sentence, and it is this: a room in which four people agree is a room in which one of them has been persuaded. And you cannot get that room in this county, because the only person anybody can send for in this county is me, and I am not going to send for four people to agree about a household that is dead.*”

The woman of about fifty-one understood the whole of what had just been said to a man of thirty-one. About nine people in that lane heard a weaver of sixty-two refuse a man a room.

Nobody in that lane thanked anybody and nobody in that lane was sorry for anybody, and about nine of the four letters and a stroke on the wall at the end of it were cut into the white the kiln makes by somebody who cannot be read by a man of thirty-one and never has been.

The fever was thirty-seven weeks and three days old. Four people who struck their own names out of a list in a square thirty-one days ago were asked separately in four rooms what the household of Kiln Row is owed, and the four answers were nothing, a page with four gaps in it, not known and not guessed, and a thing said out loud once in a square.

And a man of thirty-one asked a weaver of sixty-two in a lane in front of about nine people for the four of them to agree, and was refused in about four seconds, and the reason was that a room in which four people agree is a room in which one of them has been persuaded.
