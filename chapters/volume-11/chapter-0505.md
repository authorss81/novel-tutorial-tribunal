# Chapter 505: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Fourth Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market Of Bramblefold, On Which About Two Hundred People Are In The Square And A Weaver Of Sixty-Two Stands Up And Says That A Name Is Not A Word And Has His Mother’s Holding In It, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Reads Four Lines Of A Return Nine Miles Off That Do Not Say What The Household Was Asked Or By Whom Or When

Third day of the ninety-fourth week. His two hundred and fifty-fifth morning. One hundred and five days after the settlement. The fever thirty-one weeks and five days. Fifty-nine days since the division. Five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

Bramblefold market is on a Thursday. It is about two hundred people, and it is not two thousand, and there is no cattle and no cloth and no iron. It is a market of provisions and seed and a great many eggs, and about nine stalls, and a wool bench, and a man who comes down from the downs once a quarter with about forty ewes.

It filled the square between about the eighth hour and the tenth. The man who sweeps it swept the middle on the Wednesday night for a penny each, and swept it again at noon on the Thursday for nothing, out of the custom, and the second sweeping is not in any minute, and about nine people every Thursday do it.

The man of thirty-one had been in this town since Monday night, and a woman of about sixty-one had told him, in a porch, that questions in this town are asked in a room. He went into the market at about the half hour after the tenth and stood at the end of the wool bench.

A man of about fifty asked him whether he was the one at the meeting-house, and then, without waiting for the answer, told him that he would be at the sheep tomorrow.

“*Will I?*” said Ilyan Vester.

“*Nine of them at the top wall, off the down, and the wall wants doing before the ground goes.*”

And that is how he learned the thing he came for in the second market he ever stood in, which is that in a country of about two thousand four hundred people the making-up of a wall and a claim about a hundred-and-forty-year-old page have the same weather, and that a stranger who has come two days about a book is on the wall by Friday.

---

It was the woman of sixty-one who got it started, and she did it in the middle of the market with a voice that could be heard at the wool bench. About two hundred people heard the word *struck* and about nine of them understood the rest of the sentence.

She is Odalie Fenn and she has been in Bramblefold eleven days, and she is a Keeper of returned names, which is not an office and not a body and is a thing a person does. She has written to nine towns in four years asking for pages. She is not of this county and has no house here and sleeps in a barn at Kiln Row.

“*You have told nine people in four days,*” she said. “*You have told my business to a stranger at a gate on Monday, to a girl on Tuesday about a leaf, to a girl in a porch on Wednesday, and to the recordwright. I am not angry about it. I would have done the same. I have done the same.*”

“*You are going to say it in the square.*”

“*I am going to say it in the square on Saturday, and there will be nine people in the room and a hundred and ninety in this, and the hundred and ninety are the ones I have to say it to. A name is not put back by nine people.*”

“*Say it, then.*”

She said it. It took her about forty seconds and it was not a speech. She has said a version of it thirty-one times in four years, and it comes out the same shape every time, and she was not performing it.

“*There is a page in a book in the meeting-house behind me with forty-three names on it and a pen through the lot of them. It was done a hundred and forty-one years back under a heading that says struck by order, and there is no order. Nine towns have told me no this year and one town has not answered. I would like the page put back, and I would like it put back in a way that is written down. I am not asking anybody to be hanged about it and I am not asking anybody to be sorry. I am asking for a line to be taken out of a book.*”

Then a man put his hand on her arm and told her there would be no more of that, and she said that there had been no more of it for a hundred and forty-one years and that was what she had come about.

---

The claim came in from the end of the market and not from anybody who had arranged it.

He is Abel Tarn and he is sixty-two and he weaves, and his looms are in two rooms over a joiner’s shop at the bottom of the town. He came into the market at about the half hour after the eleven with about a hundred and forty yards of unbleached linen over his arm, because he has been asked to bring it to a woman at Kiln Row who has a daughter getting married.

He was about forty feet away when he heard the word *struck* and stopped where he was.

The woman of sixty-one said his name. He said *aye*, and then asked what this was about Kiln Row.

“*It is not about Kiln Row. It is about a page.*”

“*It is about Kiln Row because my mother is on it.*”

That is the whole of how it came out. There was no drumroll and nobody had told him, and about two hundred people in that square turned round at the same moment, which is a thing that happens twice in a market in a life.

He came about forty feet and put the linen on a stall and came up through the crowd. A man of about forty-four came with him and stayed six feet back. That man is Harl Wenlock, and he holds Lower Halfpenny, and he has held it twenty years, and his grandfather bought it off a family that was leaving.

“*Say your name to her,*” said Odalie Fenn, “*and say it loud, because there are two hundred people here and nine of them will be in that room on Saturday, and everything that is said in this square is going to be said again in that room by people who heard it here.*”

Abel Tarn said his name. He said it the way a man says a thing he has said to a weaver’s guild and a clerk of a court and his own mother, not loudly.

“*Abel Tarn. That is the name on it. That is my mother’s name and my grandfather’s, and it is the name I was christened with at Kiln Row in a room over the kiln, and my father had it, and it is on that page, and there is no page for me.*”

She asked how he knew it was on it, and he said that his father had told him it was struck when he was about eleven, that his father had told him it was struck on a Tuesday, and that his father had told him Kiln Row was asked something and answered it, and that that was the end of them.

He stopped there and the square did not stop with him.

“*I have been in this square eleven times over forty years, and there is nobody on that page, and there is nobody in this town of two thousand four hundred people who can say my name is not in the book. There is nobody in this town who has read the book.*”

The woman of sixty-one said that four people could read the hand on the front of it.

“*Let the four read it,*” he said. “*I have not come to the square to argue with a woman about a hand. I have come because the woman in the porch said they would read it on Saturday, and I have been in this town fifty-one years, and I have never once been asked to be in that room, and I am not going to be asked twice.*”

---

A man of thirty-one was thirty feet away with a piece of paper nowhere in sight, and his want that morning was the plainest want he has had in ten volumes, which was that the answer to this turn out to be the right one. He got about nine seconds of it before it turned into a sentence he had not meant to say.

“*Then put it back,*” he said. “*Put the names back. A name restored is a name restored, and if there is no order named then there is nothing to weigh against it, and forty-three families get their page and this is finished.*”

It was, by the standards of a market square in a country district, a reasonable sentence. About four people heard the gap in it.

Abel Tarn turned round.

“*Say the last part of that again.*”

“*I said a name restored is a name restored.*”

“*Say the part after it.*”

“*I said if there is no order named then there is nothing to weigh*”

“*My mother’s holding is in it.*”

There was a noise from about nine stalls away where somebody dropped a basket. It was an egg basket, and it went about fourteen eggs onto the ground, and the market went back to itself inside about four seconds.

Abel Tarn did not stop and did not lower his voice.

“*That page has got a holding in it, and it has got a figure in the fold, and it has got seven feet in the ground behind the meeting-house. That is what the four columns are, and that is why a name is not a word.*”

“*My mother’s name is the name of a half-acre at the bottom of Kiln Lane with a wall round it that my father built out of the kiln’s own white. It is the only warm thing in that row, and there are four of us in it now, and we are not on that page. We have not been on that page for a hundred and forty-one years, and the wall is still standing.*”

He was six feet off by then and had not raised his voice once.

“*You can put forty-three names back on a page,*” he said. “*I have not asked you for forty-three names. I have asked, and I am asking now, in a market, in front of two hundred people, and I will ask again on Saturday in the room: is that half-acre mine. That is the whole of it. Not my mother’s name. The half-acre.*”

A man of about forty-four six feet back said his name.

“*Tarn.*”

“*Don’t.*”

“*I only want to say that I have held the next one along for twenty years and I did not take it off anybody. My grandfather took it off a family that was leaving and he paid them four pounds, and it was a fair price, and I have the bill of sale, and I will show it to anybody in this square.*”

“*I know what you did,*” said Abel Tarn, and did not turn round, and that was the end of it. About two hundred people in that square had watched two men find out that they had both been talking about the same ground for forty years without ever once putting them in a room together.

---

And then the old man came.

He came down the lane on a stick at about the ninth hour and stopped at the end of the wool bench, and the square made about four feet of room for him and took it back again, which is the whole of what a market square does for anybody.

He is Barnaby Rill. He kept the book before his daughter and he cannot read it now and says so, and he has come out on a Thursday for the first time in about nine years, and he did not come for the eggs.

Nobody in that square had asked him anything. About two hundred of them heard the word *struck* and about nine of them heard the rest of it.

“*You want to know what the question was,*” he said. “*I am eighty-one. I have never known, and there is nobody left in this county who was told.*”

“*You were not there,*” said Abel Tarn.

“*I was born sixty years after it was struck,*” said Barnaby Rill, “*which I will say again if it is the only thing I have.*”

He put both hands on the end of the bench, and the linen on the stall beside it was the only thing near him that was not grey.

“*What I was told, in a kitchen, by my father, is that it was done on a Tuesday, and that the people of Kiln Row were asked a question and gave an answer, and that the answer was the wrong one, and that I was not to ask which question.*”

“*Your father may have been told that and been wrong,*” said a man of about fifty at the end of the bench.

“*He may have been, and I have had sixty years to think about it and I have not, and I am not going to now.*” Barnaby Rill did not look at Abel Tarn. “*I have not once said out loud in this town what my father called that answer, and I am not going to do it in a market, because a thing a man repeats for sixty years stops being his opinion and starts being the town’s, and then one day somebody asks me whether I was there and I have to say no twice.*”

A woman at a stall said that that was the most he had ever heard him say about it, and Barnaby Rill said that she was welcome, which was not a thank and was not taken for one. The square had gone back to the eggs by then.

---

The other thing that happened on that Thursday was in a lane behind the pond: four lines on a sheet with a county seal on it, out of a book at Aldwick, which is nine miles off, in the hands of a man of about fifty-four of the county who came down on the Wednesday and went back on the Thursday and would not stay the night.

It is a return. It is dated, by a day and a year, in a hand nobody at Aldwick could now read, and it is a hundred and forty-one years old, and the county keeps a book of returns and a book of orders, and the office makes a return and puts it in a book.

*That the household of Kiln Row in Bramblefold, being asked by this office in respect of the matter of the common above the town, gave answer that the said common was not used.*

*That the said answer was made use of.*

*That the line of the said household is struck from the roll of descent of Bramblefold, in respect of the said answer.*

*That this office has no further particular in the matter.*

The man of the county read it out in a lane in about ninety seconds and then said about four things, and one of them was the one that mattered.

“*The first line does not say what the question was. It says *being asked in respect of the matter of the common*. There were about nine matters a person could be asked about in those days, and one of them was whether the common was used, and one of them was how many sheep it carried. If you had been asked the second and had answered the first, you would be in this lane today for exactly the same reason and with exactly the same four lines.*”

Ilyan Vester said that they were asked wrongly.

“*They were asked something,*” said Simon Verge, “*and they answered it, and it was made use of, and this office did not know and did not ask, because this office does not know and has never asked.*”

He said that he was not here to defend it and not here to say it was wrong either, because he had read the four lines, and the four lines were what that office was told to make, and that office made them correctly.

Ilyan Vester asked who had ordered the leaf struck.

The return does not say, said Simon Verge. It says the line is struck. Who struck it, who ordered it struck, and on what day is not in it, and he had been in both books of that office this morning and the order was in neither.

He put the sheet back inside his coat.

“*And that is not because anybody hid it. It is because a return is made, and a return goes in a book, and in those days a thing that was done to a person was not an order, it was a consequence. This office made a return. Somebody struck a page. Those are two facts about the same morning and about nine miles of country between the two.*”

“*You will not find it.*”

“*I will not find it in this county,*” Simon Verge said, “*and I would not tell you to go and look in a stranger’s chest. I am going back to Aldwick on Friday, and I have twenty-one other returns to make, and nobody has asked me for any of them.*”

---

And on the Thursday night, at about the ninth hour, in a room over a joiner’s shop at the bottom of the town where there are two looms and a smell of sizing, Abel Tarn said the other half of the whole of it, to a man of thirty-one who had come to sit in the corner and had not been asked in.

“*You are from a town two days off where a man read a woman’s letter out in a square to about two hundred people. I heard about that before I came here. A woman in a barn at Kiln Row heard about it from her sister who was at that market and could not get home.*”

He said yes. Then he said that he knew what that was, and that it was a thing read out where about two hundred people could hear it and none of them could be asked about it afterwards.

The looms were still.

“*And on Saturday there will be about nine people in a room and about a hundred and ninety in a square. A hundred and ninety who did not hear a word of it in that room will have heard it, and they will have heard it from nine people, and the nine will be different from the nine, and the year after that it will be a different nine.*”

“*My father was told that in a room with about nine people in it, and I am sixty-two, and that is the only time anybody has ever told me anything about it.*”

He did not ask the man of thirty-one for anything at all. That was the thing about it. He had said the whole of what he wanted, and it was in a market square at eleven o’clock in the morning, and it was a half-acre. About nine people heard the words and about two hundred heard the word *struck*, and neither of those numbers is a number anybody can be sent for.

The fever was thirty-one weeks and five days old. About two hundred people were in a market square in a town of about two thousand four hundred people, and a hundred and ninety of them now knew a thing that nine people in a room knew in a different shape, and a man of sixty-two had stood in a market square and said that a name is not a word, and had been answered by a man holding a bill of sale in a coat who had not touched it.
