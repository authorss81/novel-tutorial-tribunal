# Chapter 539: The Second Day Of The Ninety-Ninth Week, And A Wednesday Which Is The Day A Man Of About Seventy-Two Walks Two Miles To Hear One Line Read, And On Which Eight People In A Room With The Door Open Are Asked One At A Time By A Woman Of About Fifty-One In About Four Seconds Each Whether They Will Hold One Of Four Pages, And Four Say Yes And About Four Say No, And A Man Of About Seventy-Two Is Read His Line At The Ninth Hour And Is Not Asked, And A Fifth Sheet Is Written Out With Four Names On It And Offered To A Man Of Thirty-One, And He Says Yes In About Four Seconds, And Is Refused On His Behalf In About Nine Words, And Nobody In That Room Is Holding It

Second day of the ninety-ninth week. His two hundred and eighty-ninth morning. One hundred and thirty-nine days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and four days. Ninety-three days since the division. Thirty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

A Wednesday is the day a man of about seventy-two walks two miles to hear one line read out about four acres at the bottom of the lane.

He came at the ninth hour in a wet coat in a week in which it has not rained for about eighteen days, and he stood at the end of the bench in the place about two-thirds along it where the stone is worn, and about nine people were in that porch.

He was read his one line and he said that was right, and she said it had been right the whole time, and she asked him whether he wanted to go and look at the stake and he said no, which is what he has said the last five times.

Nobody in that porch asked him anything else.

That is worth saying exactly.

About nine people in that porch knew that a man of about thirty-one had been carrying a sentence since a Sunday in the ninety-fourth week and had not said it once. They knew that a woman of about fifty-one had said it out loud in a lane six days ago in about ten words, and that the sentence is that a man who is asked a question and says he does not remember has now been asked.

About four of the nine in that porch understood that the not-asking this morning was an arrangement, and about nine had not.

Nobody in that porch thanked anybody for the eleven minutes, and he was not thanked, and nobody was sorry for him.

---

Then the room was opened at about the eleventh hour and about nine people went in and the door stayed open and the window went down.

The four pages were on a table. They are four pages in a round hand with forty-one mistakes in them and thirty-eight of them corrected, made out of memory in about four minutes of sitting in a square in a town two days off on the thirty-ninth day after the division.

The woman of about fifty-one stood at the head of the table. She is the only person in that room who cannot be asked to produce them again if she does not want to.

She said one thing to the room before she asked anybody anything, and she said it in about nine words.

“*He told you in here on Monday that there are two kinds of safe. I have had it for two days and I am not going to argue with him about it.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because nine words is a thing you say when you have thought about it and not a thing you say when you have just said it.*”

“*I am going to use it. That is the rest of it, and I would like it said in this room before I ask anybody anything, because I am about to ask eight of you the same question in about four seconds each and one of you will get it from the woman next to you and I would rather you did not.*”

Then she asked them.

She asked eight people, one at a time, and she asked them in about four seconds each, and she said the same four sentences to every one of them and did not change a word.

“*Will you hold one page of these four in your own house, and be a person who can be sent for about it.*”

That is the whole question and it was four seconds and it was asked eight times, and nobody was asked whether they wanted to hear it, and about nine people in that room heard all eight askings.

Four said yes.

They were a woman of about forty at the wool bench, a man of about fifty who keeps a beast, a woman of about thirty-five with a child on her hip, and a man of about twenty-eight who works at the kiln. About four of the nine people in that room knew one or two of them and about nine knew all four.

About four said no.

They were Joe Drury the smith, a man of about fifty at the fire, a woman of about sixty-eight of the upper row, and a man of about twenty-two. Nobody in that room said anything at all for about four seconds after the last of them and about nine people in it were waiting for a reason.

Edith Crow gave one, out loud, standing, without being asked, and it was the reason and not a defence.

“*I have four rooms and a yard, and sixteen days ago I was told that there was a finding entered about my own objection, and I said no to two thirds of it.*”

“*And that went into a book four hundred and eleven years old with the day I said it on and not the day it was made.*”

“*And the half you have not said.*”

“*The half I have not said is that I have had a thing put in a book about me once in four weeks and I did not know it was going to happen.*”

“*And I am not putting four pages over my bed in about four years to find out what has been done with them.*”

About four of the nine people in that room understood the whole of that and about nine heard a widow of sixty-eight give a reason.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody. The four who said yes were not thanked for saying yes, and the four who said no were not thanked for saying no, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be thanked for holding or for refusing to hold a sheet of paper.

---

The recordwright was at the window and she was not one of the eight, and nobody in that room had asked her whether she would take one.

“*You are keeping a book with a lock on it and a key on a string round your neck,*” said the man of thirty-four, “*and you are the only person in this county who can be sent for about a sheet of paper as well as about a book.*”

“*I am.*”

“*And you are not one of the eight, and you have not offered, and about four people in this room noticed and about nine did not.*”

“*I noticed at the fourth asking and I said nothing at the fourth asking, and about four of you noticed me say nothing, and that is one more of about nine cases out of a hundred in nineteen years, and I am not going to rule a line about it.*”

“*That is the fourteenth and you said on Monday that the thirteenth was a man of thirty-one.*”

“*The thirteenth was a man of thirty-one. This is the fourteenth, and it is a Wednesday, and I am not going to rule a line about it.*”

Then Barnaby Rill said one thing from the chair, and he had not been sent for, and about four of the nine people in that room had been expecting it since the eighth hour and about nine had not.

“*Four pages and four houses, and about four weeks between now and the first frost.*”

“*And I have watched a thing get carried out of a room in this town in a cart once, and the room was empty afterwards, and the cart went four miles, and nobody asked me where the cart went.*”

“*What was in it.*”

“*I do not know. And that is the half I have not told you, and I have been that person twice in this town and I have said nothing both times.*”

“*You have told us that before.*”

“*I have told you that before, and I am telling you the half of it I have not told you.*”

“*If one of those four houses is emptied, or the woman at the wool bench dies, or the man at the kiln goes to a town four days off, then in about four weeks this arrangement is three houses. In about a year it is a sentence in a smithy.*”

About four seconds went past in that room and a man of eighty-one was not the only one counting them.

“*And I am eighty-one and I am not asking anybody to do anything about it. There is no form in this county that says a keeper of a book may be asked to plan for a year.*”

“*I have watched nine things stop being kept in this town in nineteen years, and I have not been asked about one of them.*”

---

Then there was one thing left on that table and one person she had not asked. The thing was a fifth sheet and it was not one of the four. It was a list she had written out that morning with four names on it and four houses under the names and nothing else on it at all.

He was standing at the end of the room with a leg he stands on at an angle and a hundred and thirty-nine days in a country that is not his, and she turned to him and offered him the list.

“*You.*”

“*Yes,*” he said.

It took him about four seconds and it was the fifth time in a hundred and thirty-nine days that he had said yes in about four seconds to a thing he had not thought about, and he heard himself do it and could not stop it.

“*No,*” said the woman of about fifty-one, and she said it before he had finished, and it was about nine words.

“*A thing a man who cannot be sent for says yes to is a thing that has a man who cannot be sent for in it.*”

It was about four seconds before anyone in that room said a word, and a man of eighty-one in the chair was not the only one counting them.

“*Say the rest of it, and say it slower, because about four of the nine people in this room have understood that and about nine have not.*”

“*The other half is that a page you cannot be sent for about is a page that cannot be checked, and I said that in a porch on Tuesday to about nine people, and I am not going to take it back because I am tired.*”

“*I was not going to take it.*”

“*You were going to say that, and you were going to mean it. And then about four years from now there will be a page of four that nobody in this county can be sent for about, and there will be a rule in a book or on a board that says nobody may take it.*”

“*That is the other kind of safe. It was explained to about nine people in this room on Monday, and about nine of them understood it and about nine did not.*”

“*Then who holds the list.*”

“*Nobody. I do not. I have the four and I wrote that sheet out this morning. I am the woman who made the four out of my own memory in about four minutes of sitting in a square in a town two days off, and there is no reason on earth why I should also be the one who holds the list of them.*”

She put the list down on the table beside the four and did not pick it up again.

“*And I have not been asked whether I am enough, and I am not the keeper of a book in this county, and the woman at the wool bench who said in a square on the Thursday that a shelf is a thing a person is responsible for was talking about a person who gets paid for it. I do not get paid for it. Nobody in this county pays anybody for holding a sheet of paper.*”

About four of the nine people in that room understood the whole of that. About nine heard a woman of about fifty-one say that she was not going to hold the list, and that she was not being paid for it, and that the man of thirty-four’s rule did not apply to her.

---

Then the man of thirty-four said the last thing, and he said it to the man of thirty-one and not to the room, and it was about four sentences and it was the answer to a question nobody had asked.

“*There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked whether they still have it.*”

“*No.*”

“*So you have just been told that four people in this room will hold one page each, and not one of them has been told what happens if they lose it, and not one of them can be told, because there is nothing to tell them.*”

“*So four people in a town of about two thousand four hundred people can be asked about a sheet of paper in about four years.*”

“*And if it goes the way a thing goes, then in about nine years that could be a third of this town, and none of the third would have chosen it, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether they want to be in it.*”

“*And I have just watched a man who could be one of those four say yes in four seconds to a thing he had not thought about, and he is right that it cannot be him.*”

“*So no single person holds it, and I did not expect to say that on a Wednesday, and I have been in this town ten days.*”

“*I have come ten days down a lane to find out whether that is a thing that can be done in a county of about two thousand four hundred people. I have found out that it can be started. I have not found out that it works, and those are two different things.*”

“*And I am not going to say the first one in a market square on Thursday, because about two hundred people will hear the first one.*”

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

The fever was thirty-six weeks and four days old. Nine people were in a room with the door open, and a woman of about fifty-one asked eight of them in about four seconds each whether they would hold one of four pages. Four said yes and about four said no.

A man of thirty-one was offered a list of four names and said yes in about four seconds, and was refused on his behalf in about nine words by a woman who said that a thing a man who cannot be sent for says yes to is a thing that has a man who cannot be sent for in it.
