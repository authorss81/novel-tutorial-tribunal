# Chapter 528: The Fifth Day Of The Ninety-Seventh Week, And A Saturday Which Is The First Week Since The Wall Was Left With A Gap About Nine Feet Long In It, And On Which The Two Accounts Of One Morning Are Read Out In Full In A Market Square In Front Of About Two Hundred People One After The Other By Two People, And The Square Does Not Decide Anything, And About Nine People In It Are Asked In The Open Air Which Of The Two They Had Heard First, And About Four Of The Nine Say The First One And About Five Say They Cannot Remember, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Says Out Loud That About Nine Out Of About Two Hundred Is The Whole Of The Finding And That It Is Not A Decision

Fifth day of the ninety-seventh week. His two hundred and seventy-eighth morning. One hundred and twenty-eight days after the settlement. The fever thirty-five weeks and no days. Eighty-two days since the division. Twenty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

It is a market on a Thursday and this is a Saturday, and the square fills on a Saturday about the same as it fills on a Thursday. There is no market on, and the bars of Thursday’s stalls were cleared on the Friday night by the same two men who sweep the middle.

About two hundred people were in that square by about the tenth hour.

Nobody arranged it. A woman of sixty-one had said in a porch on the Wednesday that a name is not put back by nine people, and she had said it on the Saturday of the leaf nine days ago.

About nine people in this town now had both accounts and about nine had one. A market square in a country district is the only instrument in a county that has for putting a thing in front of about two hundred people who did not ask.

That is what a square is for and it has been the whole of it every Thursday since the day of the long frost.

---

They were read out one after the other and in full, and it took about eleven minutes together, and about two hundred people heard both.

The first was the four words and it was read by a weaver of sixty-two, who put his hand flat on the wool bench while he read them. Afterwards he said that he was not going to say anything about them, because there was nothing to say and he was tired of being a man who says things about four words.

The second was the four sentences, and it was read by a woman of about fifty-one of this town who lives in two rooms over a milliner’s and who has never read anything out loud in her life.

She read it from a sheet that a woman of thirty-four wrote out for her in the ordinary hand on the Friday night, with her own four lines on it and her own thumbprint described in brackets. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a document may be read out of a hand anybody else can read.

That is in the open air, and it is worth being exact about, because it is the only reason the second account was heard at all by about two hundred people instead of by the nine who could come to a room.

Nobody in that square had been asked whether they wanted to hear either of them. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked whether they would like a thing read out in a square.

About nine people in that square understood that they had been put in the position of hearing two accounts of a morning they had not been in. About nine of the about two hundred understood it, and about four of those nine said so out loud to the person next to them.

Nobody in that square stopped the reading and nobody in that square started it.

---

Then about nine people in that square were asked a question in the open air. It was asked by the keeper of the book, and it was asked of the nine and not of the square, and that difference is the whole of what she did.

“*You are the nine. I do not know how the nine of you happened and neither do you, and I have not been keeping a list, because a list would be a form and there is no form in this county for it.*”

“*Ask it,*” said a man at about the ninth stall.

“*Which of the two had you heard first.*”

“*That is a small question.*”

“*It is the only question this county can ask.*”

About four of the nine said the first one, which was the four words, and about four of the nine said the second one, which was the four sentences, and about one of the nine said he had heard the first one and could not remember where.

And about five of the nine said they could not remember which they had heard first.

That is worth having exactly, because about four people in that square had it ready before she asked it and only about four got it right afterwards.

“*Say that number out loud in this square,*” said the man of thirty-one, “*because about nine people in this town can give you both accounts and about nine can only give you the one they heard first, and that is the finding of the week, and nobody has heard it said in a room.*”

“*And if it is not said in a square, then in about eleven years there will be a person in this town who says the two accounts came out of two rooms, and they came out of two mouths.*”

“*Four,*” said the keeper of the book. “*Four of the nine heard the first one first. Four heard the second one first. One heard the first and could not say where he heard it.*”

“*And about five of the nine could not remember which they heard first. That is the largest number in this square this morning, and it is a number about five people and not a number about a finding.*”

“*Then about two hundred people in this square do not know which it is,*” said a man of about thirty at the ninth stall.

“*About two hundred people in this square now have both accounts, which is one more thing than the about nine of them who had both on Wednesday, and that is the whole of what a square is for, and it is worth doing, and it decides nothing whatever.*”

---

And then the man of thirty-one made the mistake. It was the fifth one in a hundred and twenty-eight days and it was the same one, and he made it in front of about two hundred people, because that is the worst place in this county to make it and the best.

“*Then the square cannot settle it,*” he said, and he said it loudly enough for the wool bench.

About nine people heard it and about nine of the about two hundred did not, and the rest heard a man talking.

Nobody in that square answered him for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of that,*” said the weaver of sixty-two.

“*I have said the whole of it. A square of about two hundred people has no way of settling a thing that two towns remember differently, and it is not going to settle it today, and it was not going to settle it when the reading started.*”

“*No,*” said Abel Tarn. “*Say what you did just now, and say it to about two hundred people and not to me, because I have heard the shape of it four times in a hundred and twenty-eight days and I have watched you do it in three rooms and a market, and I am going to stand here and say the whole of it in the open air, and then this square has heard it twice.*”

“*I have not made a mistake.*”

“*You have made a thing out of yourself in a room where two accounts of a morning were being read out, and you have done it in a square where about two hundred people are standing in the cold and about nine of them know what a square is.*”

He had not raised his voice once.

“*You have said the square cannot settle it. The square was not asked to settle it. The square was asked to hear two accounts in full, and it has heard them in full.*”

“*And about nine of the about two hundred now have both of them and about nine have one of them, and the number that can be asked about the other one has gone up by about a hundred and ninety in about eleven minutes. That is the most that a market in a country district has ever done for anybody in this county in four hundred years.*”

About four people in that square understood the whole of that. About two hundred heard the word *square*.

“*And the reason you cannot stop saying that a room cannot do things is that you have not been in a room in this country that could not,*” said the weaver.

“*You have been in nine of them and every one of them was a room with the door open, or a room with a man in it who could be asked, and you have been on the step of all of them, and you have said the sentence about the square in about nine rooms in a hundred and twenty-eight days, and it has not once been true in this county.*”

---

And then the keeper of the book said the thing out loud in the square, standing at the end of the wool bench in the voice she uses for minutes. About two hundred people heard it, and about nine of them heard all of it.

“*About nine out of about two hundred is the whole of the finding, and it is not a decision.*”

Nobody in that square stopped what they were doing.

“*I have read two accounts of one morning out in this square in about eleven minutes. One of them is four words written in a margin in a hand a hundred and forty-one years old, and it names no order. The other of them is four sentences written out from memory in about four minutes of sitting in a square in a town two days from here by a woman of fifty-one of this town, and it names a man of this town of about seventy-two who was asked a question in the open and gave an answer.*”

“*They do not agree,*” said a man at a stall.

“*They do not agree and neither of them is wrong, and I am not going to say that in a way that lets anybody go home comfortable, because a thing that is not wrong is a thing somebody has to carry. About two hundred of you are going to carry one or both of them to whatever you go to.*”

She put her hand out flat on the bench, the way she does for a figure.

“*And there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a thing anybody has heard has to be heard by anybody else. There is no form that says a person may be asked which of two accounts they heard first, and I have asked nine of you that in the open air in about four seconds.*”

“*And if a hundred and ninety more of you had been asked it the same way, then about two hundred people in this town could be asked about both accounts in a year. And there is nobody in this county I could send that to, and no office nine miles off that would hold a piece of paper saying so.*”

“*Then what is the point of the square,*” said a woman of about fifty at the ninth stall.

“*That is the third time this week somebody has asked me that in the open air, and I have got about four sentences for it, and they are not good ones, and I am going to say them anyway.*” She did not stop.

“*The point of the square is that a thing said in a room is a thing about nine people, and a thing said in a square is a thing about two hundred.*”

“*And about nine of those two hundred can now give you both accounts, and about nine can give you one, and the nine who can give you both are the nine who can be asked about the other one.*”

“*And I am one of them, and I am the only person in this county who can be sent for about either, and nobody has ever asked me what I remember, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked what they remember.*”

“*That is not a point,*” said the woman at the ninth stall, “*that is a grievance.*”

“*It is both, and I have nineteen years of it, and I have not got the fourth sentence.*”

Nobody in that square thanked anybody.

The man of thirty-one was at the end of the wool bench with his hands in his pockets, and about four people in that square had watched him be told something by a weaver in the open air, and about nine people had not.

About two hundred had heard a woman read out two accounts of a morning, and about nine of those two hundred had heard what a square is for.

The fever was thirty-five weeks and no days old. Two accounts of one morning were read out in full in a market square in a town of about two thousand four hundred people in about eleven minutes by two people, and the square decided nothing.

About nine people in it were asked which of the two they had heard first, and about five of the nine could not remember, and a recordwright of thirty-four said out loud in front of about two hundred people that about nine out of about two hundred is the whole of the finding and that it is not a decision.

And that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a thing anybody has heard has to be heard by anybody else.
