# Chapter 523: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Ninety-Sixth Week, And The Morrow Is The First Of The Ninety-Seventh, And A Monday On Which A Finding Entered At The Back Of A Book Four Hundred And Eleven Years Old On The Seventeenth Day Of A Count Begun In Another County Is Put In Front Of The Two People It Is About, And Edith Crow Of Sixty-Eight Is Told First And Says That She Knew And That She Has Been Waiting About Four Days To See Whether Anybody Would Do It, And Asks For The Finding To Go In With The Day She Was Told On It And Not The Day It Was Made

Seventh and last day of the ninety-sixth week, and the morrow is the first day of the ninety-seventh. His two hundred and seventy-third morning. One hundred and twenty-three days after the settlement. The fever thirty-four weeks and two days. Seventy-seven days since the division. Twenty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The finding went in at the back of that book on the seventeenth day of a count begun in another county, in a town two days and four miles of lane from here, which is not the counting of this town.

The finding is that the two objections at the foot of the entry of the fifteenth day of the ninety-fourth week are both against the three words nothing is carried, and that those three words are not about a half-acre, and that a person who is already in an entry does not get a second thing out of an entry and does not get a second thing put on him either.

A finding is a finding and not a decision, and nothing is carried by it.

At the foot of the leaf, in her own hand, and not read out, there is a line that says that neither of the two people this finding is about has been told of it.

That line had been there for four days. About nine people in this town had read the finding, and not one of the two people it is about knew it existed.

---

The keeper of the book did it herself and she did not do it in a room.

She sent for the two of them on the Sunday night, separately, by two different people. She did it in about nine words to each, and the nine words were the same. A thing has been entered at the back of the book on the Tuesday of this week that concerns your objection. I am going to read it out to you in a porch at about the tenth hour tomorrow, and you may bring anybody with you, and I will not ask who it is.

About four people in that town found out about that on the Monday morning and about nine found out on the Tuesday.

Harl Wenlock is forty-four and he holds the holding numbered five, which is the next one along. He has held it twenty years. His grandfather bought it off a family that was leaving for four pounds, and he has the bill of sale in a coat. He is right about the book and right about the wall and wrong about one thing, and no man in this town is that.

He came at about the tenth hour with a man of about fifty and did not say why the man was with him.

She went to the upper row first, at about the half hour after the ninth, and Edith Crow was at her own door with her sleeves rolled. She had been up since the fifth hour.

---

“*Mrs Crow. I am going to read you four sentences out of a book, and then I am going to ask you a question about the day on them, and the question is not whether you agree.*”

“*Read them.*”

The porch on the upper row is not the porch of the meeting-house. It is about four feet deep, and it is a step and a roof, and there is no bench in it. About nine people who had come up the row to find out what was happening stood in the road outside and heard about four of the five sentences.

“*Entered on the seventeenth day of a count begun in another county, which is not the counting of this town. A finding, and it is a finding and not a decision, and nothing is carried by it.*”

“*The two objections at the foot of the entry of the fifteenth day of the ninety-fourth week are both against the three words nothing is carried. Those three words are not about a half-acre.*”

“*And a person who is already in an entry does not get a second thing out of an entry, and does not get a second thing put on him either.*”

Edith Crow did not move and did not sit down and did not ask what the three words were.

“*I knew,*” she said.

“*Say how you knew, because I have had four days of it and I have not slept on two of them and I would like to be told that I was the only one carrying it.*”

“*You wrote it in the margin on the Tuesday of the ninety-fourth week that you thought the two of us were against the same three words. You put the day on it. You said out loud in this porch that if you were wrong somebody could put you right in a year.*”

She had her hands on the frame of her own door. She was sixty-eight years old and had paid for four rooms and a yard for twenty-two years out of a mangle and a drying ground and eleven weeks a year teaching children to read in a room over a chandler’s.

“*A woman of sixty-one came to this town and went round about nine rooms in a fortnight saying a thing she had said in nine towns in four years. I heard it at the pump and at the wool bench and in here. About four days ago I worked out that the thing she was saying and the thing in your margin were the same sentence, and I sat down on this step.*”

“*And you did not come to me.*”

“*No. And you did not come to me either, and you have had a key on a string round your neck for nineteen years and you could have come up this row in four minutes.*”

Nobody in that porch said anything about that, and about four people standing in the road understood the whole of it.

Then she said the thing she had come out on the step for, and she said it before anybody asked her a question.

“*There is one thing and it is the only thing I am going to ask for. I would like it before I am asked anything else, because I have been thinking about it for four days and I am not going to think about it in front of nine people.*”

“*Ask for it.*”

“*Put the day on it that you read it to me, and not the day you made it.*”

The keeper of the book did not answer for about four seconds, and that is the longest she has stood in a porch in nineteen years.

“*Why.*”

“*Because a day of a week comes round and a count begun in another county does not. And because if that finding says the seventeenth day of a count I have never kept, then in a hundred years a person will read it and think it was made in a room I was standing in.*”

“*It was made in a porch on a Tuesday by a woman of sixty-one who has been in nine towns in four years and cannot be sent for, and I was at home.*”

“*Then I would rather it said the twenty-third day of the ninety-sixth week, which is a Monday, and I would rather it said that I was told on it. And I would rather it kept the day it was made somewhere on the same leaf, because four days of a man not knowing is a thing worth having on a page and not worth losing.*”

Nobody thanked anybody. About four people in that road understood that a widow of sixty-eight on the upper row had asked a keeper of a book to change a day on a finding. About nine heard a woman ask for a day.

Then the keeper asked the question, standing on the step of a house, with about nine people in the road.

“*Mrs Crow. The three words at the foot of your objection are that nothing is carried. I have entered a finding that says your objection and Mr Wenlock’s objection are both against those three words, and that the three words are not about a half-acre, and that a person already in an entry does not get a second thing out of an entry and does not get a second thing put on him either. Do you agree with the finding.*”

“*No,*” said Edith Crow.

“*Say the rest of it, because you have never once in this town said a bare word to me and I am not going to write a bare word down.*”

“*I agree with two thirds of it. I have not read the finding before this morning, and I am not going to say I agree with a sentence I have had four days to think about and did not hear until about two minutes ago.*”

She let go of the door frame.

“*I have been twenty-two years in four rooms and a yard with nothing in writing. I said no to my own family about it in a room nine feet by seven before anybody argued with me, and I asked then that it be written down that I said it before and not after. I am not going to say the other thing now and have it be the second time.*”

Nobody in that road said anything at all for about four seconds.

“*Then that goes in as well. It goes in with the day you said it and not the day I asked it, and there is no column in that book for a person who has been asked whether they agree and has had four days to think about it and has not heard the question until two minutes ago.*”

“*And I am writing it at the foot of the leaf in the ordinary hand, and I am not ruling anything, and I am not heading anything.*”

---

Then the man of forty-four was at the porch of the meeting-house at about the half hour after the tenth, with a man of about fifty, and the four sentences were read out to him standing. About nine people were in the square and heard about four of them.

He did not say he knew.

“*That is right,*” said Harl Wenlock, and then he said the other half of it, which is the thing he does. About four people in that square had begun to expect it inside a week.

“*It is right about my objection and it is right about hers. I am going to say the other half, and I have had four days to get to it and I did not get to it until about two minutes ago. That is the same thing that woman just said about herself, and I noticed her say it.*”

Nobody in that square had expected a man of forty-four to notice what a widow had said.

“*Say the half,*” said the keeper.

“*A person who is already in an entry does not get a second thing out of an entry. That is right, and I have been saying it in a market square in a different shape for nine years, and I did not know it was the same sentence, and neither did he.*”

He had his hands in his coat and the bill of sale is in that coat and he did not take it out.

“*And a person who is already in an entry does not get a second thing put on him either. That is right, and it is right about me, and I am the second person in this town that finding is about.*”

“*I have had four days in which I did not know a book had said that a man of sixty-two is not going to have my next one along off me. I have spent about nine seconds of those four days being glad of it, and I am telling you that in about nine people. You may enter it or you may not, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may be asked what he felt on a Tuesday about a thing he was not told of.*”

About nine people in that square heard the first part of that and about four heard the place where it stopped.

He was not thanked and he did not ask to be. The keeper entered the nine seconds or did not enter it, and she said out loud in the porch that she was not entering it, and that she had been the one who has not asked for about nine cases out of a hundred for nineteen years, and that this would make it the twelfth, and that the twelfth is a man of forty-four on a Monday morning and not a widow.

---

And the man of thirty-one was in that square and had been in it since the seventh hour. He had not been in the room and had not been asked to be.

His want that morning was the plainest one he has had in this country. He wanted the finding to be right.

He had carried it for eleven days, since the Sunday of the ninety-fifth week. It had been said out loud in a porch on the Tuesday by a woman of sixty-one. He had had it first, and he had used about nine seconds on not saying it, and it had gone into a book four hundred and eleven years old with a line at the foot of it saying that neither of the two people it was about had been told.

He wanted it to be right, and it was right, and it was right in a way he could not do anything with at all, and both of the people it was about had been told by somebody else in about nine words on a Sunday night.

That is what happened to him in a square on a Monday morning. About four people in it understood it and about nine did not, and the man of thirty-four from another county stood at the end of a wool bench and said out loud, to about nine people, that he had said the finding was right in a porch on the Tuesday, and that he had said it in about two words.

He said that a woman of sixty-eight had just told him in front of about nine people that she had not agreed with it, and that he had understood the whole of what that was in about four seconds. It took about four seconds because he had said a thing was right before he had heard whether the people it was about had been asked.

The keeper of the book heard it and looked at him for about four seconds longer than she looked at anybody.

“*You were in the porch on the Tuesday,*” she said.

“*At about the fourth hour, and I had it since the Sunday before that, and I did not say it. A woman of sixty-one said it in about eleven seconds and I had nine seconds of wanting to have said it first, and I used them on not talking.*”

“*And this morning you have said the finding is right in a square full of people who cannot be sent for, about a finding that two people have been told of twenty minutes. And one of them has said no to two thirds of it.*”

“*Yes.*”

“*Then say the other half of that as well, because you have been in my porch for a fortnight and I would rather hear the rest of it from you than work it out.*”

“*The other half is that I would like it to be right, and that wanting a thing to be right is the whole of what I have been doing for a hundred and twenty-three days, and that it has not once in a hundred and twenty-three days made a person safer.*”

About four people in that square understood the whole of that. About nine heard a man say that he wanted a thing to be right.

The man of thirty-four was not of this county and could not be sent for, and about nine people in that square understood the second of those things better than the first.

The fever was thirty-four weeks and two days old. A finding went in at the back of a book four hundred and eleven years old with the day a widow of sixty-eight was told on it and not the day it was made, and the day it was made stayed on the same leaf in the keeper’s own hand.

There was no column in that book for a person who has been asked whether she agrees and has had four days to think about it, and a man of forty-four said out loud in a square that he had spent about nine seconds being glad of a thing he had not been told, and a man of thirty-one said out loud that he wanted a thing to be right.
