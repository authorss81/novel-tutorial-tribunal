# Chapter 538: The First Day Of The Ninety-Ninth Week, And The Day Before Was The Last Of The Ninety-Eighth, And A Tuesday On Which A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Is Paid Two Shillings A Year By Each Of About Sixty Households, Which Is Six Pounds A Year And Has Not Moved In Nineteen Years, Is Asked In A Smithy In Front Of About Nine People By A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Has Worked In That Smithy For Eleven Years Whether That Is Enough, And Answers In About Four Sentences, And A Man Of Thirty-One Stands In That Smithy For About Four Minutes Waiting To Ask It And Does Not

First day of the ninety-ninth week, and the day before was the last day of the ninety-eighth. His two hundred and eighty-eighth morning. One hundred and thirty-eight days after the settlement. The fever thirty-six weeks and three days. Ninety-two days since the division. Thirty-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The week of this town starts on a Tuesday and not one person in it can say why. A town counts from a dated thing, and this one has not had one since a year of the long frost, which is a season and not a day.

The last thing that happened in a smithy on the Saturday was a piece of chalk about a person asking to have four pages kept in this town, and a boy of fourteen’s name at the foot of it.

---

She is paid two shillings a year by each of about sixty households, which is six pounds a year. Sixty households at two shillings is one hundred and twenty shillings, and one hundred and twenty shillings is six pounds, and that is the whole of what a woman of thirty-four takes for keeping the book of a town of about two thousand four hundred people for nineteen years.

The households hold the fold. A hundred and forty acres at four shillings the acre is twenty-eight pounds a year, and the recordwright’s six pounds is a fifth of what the down brings in, and neither of those figures has moved in nineteen years.

A man of eighty-one said it in a smithy in this town eleven days ago, out of a chair by the fire, and he had not been sent for. What he said was that he had been that person twice in his life and had said nothing both times, and then he said that nobody in this county had ever once asked her whether six pounds a year was enough, and that he would like it entered that he had said so. Nobody entered it. She did not press it.

The man of thirty-one had had that for a hundred and thirty-eight days. He had wanted to ask it in about nine rooms and he had asked it in none of them.

---

He got to the smithy at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and there was a fire in it and about nine people in it, and the fire is the only fire in that part of the bottom of the town that a man may sit at without giving a reason for it.

He stood at the end of it for about four minutes with a question ready in his mouth, and he did not ask it, and he has not been able to say why since, except that he worked it out at about the fifth hour of that Tuesday and it took him about four seconds.

The reason is that she could not have told him to go away.

That is the whole of it. A man of about thirty-four who has worked in that smithy for eleven years can be told to go away by a woman of thirty-four, and a man of thirty-one cannot, and the difference between the two is the whole of what a question costs the person who is asked it.

About four of the nine people in that smithy noticed that the man of thirty-one had been standing at the end of it for about four minutes and had not said anything. About nine noticed that a man of about thirty-four had asked a question about money. About four of those nine worked out why, and the two groups were not the same four.

---

The man of thirty-four was called Joe Drury and he mends ploughs and he has worked in that smithy eleven years and he is not a person anybody in that lane has ever thought about.

“*Mrs Rill.*”

She was at the board with the rag in her hand, because the Saturday reading had three unheaded things on it and one of them had been rubbed out by somebody in the week.

“*Joe.*”

“*I have been in this smithy eleven years and you have been in this town nineteen, and I have a question and it is about money, and I have thought about it for about four days and I am going to ask it in front of about nine people and not in a kitchen.*”

Nobody in that smithy stopped what they were doing. About four of them had heard what was coming and about nine had not.

“*Ask it.*”

“*Is six pounds a year enough.*”

The rag stopped.

About four of the nine people in that smithy had understood the shape of the question before he asked it and about nine understood it after. Nobody in that smithy said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it, because that is a bare question and you have had four days to make it a longer one, and I am not going to answer a bare question with a bare answer in a room with about nine people in it.*”

“*I do not know the rest of it. I have got a question and I have not got the second half of it.*”

She said *aye* to that and then she said the answer, and it was four sentences, and it was not a complaint, and it was not a thank you.

“*Two shillings a year from each of about sixty households is six pounds a year, and it has been six pounds for nineteen years, and the households hold the fold, and I write the book. That is the arrangement and I have never once tried to change it.*”

“*And is it enough.*”

“*It is enough because I have a house and I keep no beast, and a woman who keeps a beast is paid out of the other half of that twenty-eight pounds and I am not, and I would not do it for nothing and I would not do it for nine pounds either.*”

She put the rag down on the bench.

“*And a thing nobody has asked me about for nineteen years is not a thing I have been underpaid for. It is a thing I have not been asked about. Those are two different things and about nine people in this smithy have been treating them as one since the Saturday a leaf was read out.*”

About four of the nine people in that smithy understood the whole of that in about four seconds.

“*And I am going to say the last of it and then I am going to go and do the week.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*I have not said out loud in nineteen years that nobody has asked me, and I said it in a smithy eleven days ago to about nine people, and about four of them heard it and about five of them heard a woman being brave. You have just asked me a question about money in a room where nothing has been decided, and that is a different thing and it is the first one anybody has done in nineteen years.*”

“*Then thank you—*”

“*Do not. I have not thanked you and you are not going to thank me, and if you thank me then this becomes a thing that happened to me instead of a thing I answered.*”

Nobody in that smithy thanked anybody. Joe Drury had asked a question about six pounds a year in front of about nine people and had not been thanked for it, and a recordwright of thirty-four had answered it in about four sentences and had not been thanked for it, and a man of thirty-one was at the end of the smithy having not asked it.

---

The woman of sixty-one came in at about the fifth hour and she had not been sent for and nobody had told her there was a fire lit.

She has said in four sentences and in nine towns in four years that the only person anybody in this county can be sent for about either of the two accounts of the thirtieth day after the division is a woman of thirty-four, and that she is the only one, and that nobody has asked her what she remembers.

She said it again in that smithy at about the fifth hour, in about four sentences, to about nine people, and this time four of the nine were in the room when she said it and about five were not and about nine heard all of it.

“*Mrs Rill. You have been asked a question about money. I heard about it from a man who mends ploughs, in a lane, and he said it out loud in the lane because he is the sort of man who says things out loud in lanes.*”

“*He is.*”

“*Then I am going to say the four sentences and then I am going to go, because I said on Sunday in a lane that I was going to stop, and this is the last time I say them in this county.*”

She said them. She said that the only person anybody can be sent for about either of the two accounts is a woman of thirty-four, and that she is the only one, and that nobody has ever asked her what she remembers, and that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked what they remember.

Nobody moved in that smithy for about four seconds.

“*That is the fourth time anybody has said it and the first time it has been said in a room with about nine people in it, and I would like it noticed which of the two of those it is.*”

“*It is a room with about nine people in it,*” said the recordwright.

“*Then it is a room, and rooms are where people are held to, and I have just used one to do the last thing I am going to do in this county, and I would like somebody in about eleven years to be able to tell which of the two of us arranged that.*”

About four of the nine people in that smithy understood the whole of that. About nine heard a woman of sixty-one say a four-sentence thing for the last time in a county she had been in for about five weeks.

She went out again at about the fifth hour and she did not say anything else in this town, and nobody asked her to, and there is no form in this county that says a person may be asked whether they would say something else.

---

And then Joe Drury said the thing that made the afternoon, without being asked, and he said it to the room.

“*There is a man from four days off in this town saying that a shelf is a good thing, and a woman of about fifty-one has four pages in two rooms over a milliner’s, and about four people in this county can be sent for.*”

“*That is five facts and you have not said what you want done with them.*”

“*No. I wanted to say the number out loud in a room where it could be checked, and about four of the nine of us can be sent for and about five of us cannot, and that is the whole of what I had.*”

Then he looked at the end of the smithy, at the man of thirty-one, and said the other half of it, and it was not unkind and it was not slow.

“*You have been standing there for about four minutes with it and you have not asked it, and I have watched you not ask it, and I would like to say why I could and you could not and I do not think it is a thing you would like hearing.*”

“*Say it anyway.*”

“*Because she can send me away. If I ask her a question about money and she does not want to answer it in front of nine people, she says *not now, Joe*, and I go and mow a corner, and in about four days she has decided whether she wants to answer it. You have never once in a hundred and thirty-eight days been able to leave a room by being told to.*”

There was about four seconds in that smithy after he said it, and about four of the nine people in it understood the whole of a thing and about nine heard a man say that he was sorry for another man.

“*That is the whole of it and I am sorry for you and I am not going to say that again.*”

“*Do not be sorry for me. It is the fifth time in a hundred and thirty-eight days that I have wanted to be the one who asks and you have given me the reason in about nine seconds and about four of the people in here heard the whole of it.*”

“*Then I have not been doing it for you. I have been doing it because I wanted to know and I am a man who mends ploughs.*”

That is the whole of what happened in that smithy on that Tuesday, and about four of the nine people in it understood all of it and about nine heard a man of thirty-four ask a question about six pounds a year and a man of thirty-one not ask it.

---

And the man of thirty-four from four days off said one thing at about the sixth hour, and he said it into the middle of that smithy, and it was the last thing anybody said in it that day.

“*On a shelf, the keeper is paid. That is the whole of a shelf that is not a room with a rule on it. Somebody is responsible and somebody is paid, and the two are the same person, and if they are not the same person then it is not a shelf, it is a building with rooms in it.*”

“*And who pays the six pounds.*”

“*Sixty households, two shillings each, and it has not moved in nineteen years, and I have never in about eleven years seen a keeper paid out of a thing that could be stopped by one household.*”

“*Why is that the good arrangement.*”

“*Because sixty households can stop it in about four days if they want to and one person cannot stop it at all. That is not a fair arrangement. It is a checkable one, and I have found that I would rather have a checkable one.*”

About four of the nine people in that smithy understood what that was. About nine heard a man from another county say that a woman’s six pounds a year was worth having because about sixty people could take it away from her.

The fever was thirty-six weeks and three days old. Two shillings a year from each of about sixty households was asked about in a smithy by a man who mends ploughs, and a recordwright of thirty-four answered in about four sentences, and the answer was that being underpaid and not being asked are two different things.
