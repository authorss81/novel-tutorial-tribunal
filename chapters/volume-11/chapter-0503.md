# Chapter 503: The First Day Of The Ninety-Fourth Week, And A Tuesday On Which The Market Is Not And A Man Of Thirty-One Is Put In The Porch Of A Meeting-House Because A Person Not Of This Town May Not Be In A Room Where A Book Is Read, And Is Entered On A Separate Leaf In The Back Of It In A Hand That Is Not The Book’s, With His Name And The Day And The Words A Person Not Of This Town Whose Record Is Not Findable, And There Is No Column On That Leaf For Anything Else

First day of the ninety-fourth week. His two hundred and fifty-third morning. One hundred and three days after the settlement. The fever thirty-one weeks and three days. Fifty-seven days since the division. Three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The market of Bramblefold is on a Thursday. That is written on a slate outside the chandler’s and it has been written there about forty years, and the chalk is always a week ahead of the day. This was a Tuesday, and the square was empty, with the bars of last Thursday’s stalls still lying in the mud and the sweep in the middle not done yet, because the sweep is done on a Wednesday night by two men who are paid a penny each out of the twenty-eight pounds a year that a hundred and forty acres of sheep bring in at four shillings the acre.

A woman of thirty-four came down the lane at about the seventh hour with a basket on her hip, slept two hours, and came into the town again at about the tenth with her sleeves rolled and no basket. She went straight to the meeting-house and unlocked the chancel door with a key that is on a string round her neck.

About nine people on the square saw a stranger go into a building she had a key to, which in Bramblefold is a thing that four or five of them will have an opinion of by the second hour of the afternoon.

The meeting-house is a long low building of flint and chalk with a square tower at the west end that has never had a bell in it. It was built about two hundred and thirty years ago by people who had left a town about ninety miles off. It has a gallery at the north side, and the ground floor is not unusual.

The chancel is off the south-east corner. It is about eight feet by eleven and it has one window and a door with a lock on it, and the lock is the only lock in the building, and it is on the chancel because the chancel is where the vestry cupboard is, and the vestry cupboard is where the book is.

Ilyan Vester was on the step at about the eleventh hour with a square of paper in his coat and a leg he was standing on at an angle. He wanted to be in the room where the book was read, and the whole of what was between him and the room was a key on a string and a sentence.

“*Are you the keeper?*” he said.

“*I am.*”

“*I have come two days to ask about the book.*”

“*I know you have. I came back from four days of walking myself, and there is a stranger in a room at the smith’s who says the same three sentences to anybody who will stand still for them.*” She had the key in her hand and had not put it away. “*You are not of this town.*”

“*No.*”

“*Then you are not in that room while it is open, and I am going to tell you why, and it is not because I do not know you. I have met you twice on a road and once in a square.*”

She pointed with the key, not at the door, at the wall beside it, at about the height of her own shoulder.

“*A room where a book is read is a room where people say things they are going to be held to. That is what the room is. If a person is in that room they have said something, and next week somebody can come and say, you said that, on that day, in front of these. And that is worth something. It is worth a great deal. It is worth a holding, an orchard, a yard, and about seven feet of ground behind this building.*”

“*And I have nobody here to hold.*”

“*You have nobody here to hold because you are not of this town, and there is no form in this county that will make you a neighbour. If I put you in that room and a man asks me in a year why I did it, I have got nothing to say to him that is not, I liked the look of him.*”

About four people had come across the square by then, not to listen, because a man talking to the recordwright on a step is not a thing a town in a country district crosses a square for. They came to be near it, which is different, and is about four ninths of what crossing a square in a market town is.

“*Then where can I be?*” he said.

“*In the porch. You can be in the porch all Wednesday if you like, and nobody will turn you out of the porch.*”

He said that it was a room with a door that did not shut.

“*It is a porch,*” she said, “*and it is a porch in this country, which is a thing you will find out about in a fortnight and which is not a step and a roof the way it is in your county. There is no such thing as a porch in a chalk town. There is a thing that is not in the room and is not out of it.*”

---

She went in and the door shut, and he sat on the bench in the porch, which is a stone bench about six feet long. It has been sat on by about four hundred years of people waiting to hear whether a thing they were waiting about had happened.

At about the half hour after the eleventh she came out with a leaf.

It was a single leaf and it was not ruled, and it was not the same paper as the book, and it was slightly whiter, and the hand on it was not the hand of the book.

“*Stand up.*”

He stood up.

“*I am going to write what you are, and I am going to read it back to you before I put it away. If I write it wrong you will get one chance to say so and I will change it if you are right. If you are not right I will say so and keep it.*”

She wrote for about ninety seconds. He could not see the page and he did not try.

Then she turned it round and held it out at the level of his own chest and read it, standing, in the voice she uses for minutes.

“*Entered on the seventh day of the ninety-fourth week, being the fifty-seventh day after the division. A person not of this town, name Ilyan Vester, whose record is not findable.*”

He read it himself. There was nothing else on it. There was no age on it. There was no household on it. There was no *by whom* and there was no *for what*, and there was no line against which anybody could be asked what it was for.

“*That is all of it?*”

“*That is all of it, and it is on a separate leaf at the back, and it is not ruled, and it is not in the book, because you are not a descent and you do not hold anything.*”

She took it back and did not fold it.

“*And I am going to say the other half of it, because you are going to sit on that bench and work out what I have done, and I would rather you had the whole of it.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*In four hundred years there are nine things entered at the back of that book on separate leaves. Four of them are a person who came to see the book and could not read it. Two are a person who came to see the book and would not give a name. One is a person who came to see the book and was told the hand and did not believe it and came back and believed it.*”

“*And the ninth?*”

“*A person who came to see the book in a wet coat. That is a man of about seventy who came in the year of the long frost and stood where you are standing. He will tell you about it if you ask him, and I have not put his name in either, because he did not give it.*”

“*And you put mine in.*”

“*You gave it to me twice on a road and once over a letter about a lane, so it was not a gift.*”

---

He had it in his hand for about eleven seconds, and then he did the thing that was in him, which is that a man who has been a stranger everywhere will produce a paper if you give him a piece of paper and eleven seconds.

He put his hand into the inside pocket of his coat. It went past the copy of the roll and the copy of the six lines and the copy of the request with the open witness line on it, and came out with the page.

It was a page from a register in a building of four hundred people on a road four hundred miles off. It carried the printed head of that register and the ruling of the columns and one entry, and the entry was a refusal given in another county six years ago by a household that is dead except one person, and at the foot of the entry there was a witness line with two names in it.

He held it out to her without unfolding it any further than it already was. About four people in that porch and about four people in the square could see that there was a paper and could not see what was on it.

“*That is why I am a person who can be asked about,*” he said. “*That is a page off a register. There is a name on it in a hand that a clerk four hundred miles off can read, and it came to my town, and I was in the room when it came, and nine people in that room and about two hundred in a square can be asked about it.*”

She did not take it.

That is the part he has thought about since. She did not take it, and she did not refuse it, and she looked at the folded page in his hand for about four seconds, and then she looked at his face.

“*Pull it back in.*”

“*It has a witness line on it with two names in it, and one of them is a name of my town, and*”

“*Pull it back in.*”

She had not raised her voice. There was a woman of about sixty at the other end of the porch who had come in out of the sun and was sitting down on the bench and had clearly not come for any of this. The woman of sixty looked up at the sound of the second time it was said, and then went back to her own business, and that is the whole of what a porch is.

“*You have just shown a page off a register in a porch in a town where the record of a name is the only thing anybody owns. You have shown it to a woman you have met three times, and you have said that the reason you are a person is on it. If I take it I become the person who can be asked about it. That is not your business. It is not even your mistake. It is the only mistake anybody makes in a porch.*”

He put it back in the coat.

“*You do not have to*”

“*I have not said anything about it and I am not going to, and that is the arrangement, and you are about to find out that in this country that is the arrangement in about ninety cases out of a hundred. The case that is different is a man with a case and a woman carrying a key.*”

---

She put the leaf in at the back, between two ruled pages, and it went in flat, and it went in the third leaf from the back and not the last, which he noticed and did not understand.

Then she came out and shut the chancel door and put the key back inside her dress. She said the book was read on a Saturday, and the entries for that week were made on the Friday night, and the book was not read on a Tuesday, and nobody in that town was going to be told anything about the book until a Saturday.

“*Saturday,*” he said.

“*Saturday. And on Wednesday I read the leaf of the man who came in a wet coat, because he is the only living thing in that book that anybody might want to see. On Thursday it is market and there are about two hundred people in that square. On Friday night I write the week.*”

“*And what is on the week?*”

“*Who is descended from whom, and who died, and who was born, and what came into the fold and what went out of it, and whose ground it is under the wall.*” She was walking away down the step. “*That is the week. That has been the week for four hundred years. And on the Saturday after next, which is eleven days off, I am going to read out a leaf that has not been read since the year it was struck. About nine people in this town will be in that room, and you will be in the porch.*”

She got four paces away and turned round.

“*You will be able to hear it. I am going to leave the inner door open, because a book has to be read with the window down or people fall asleep, and there is a door between the porch and the chancel and it is not locked. A door that is locked every day is a door that people are afraid of.*”

“*That is generous.*”

“*That is Thursday, Wednesday and Friday,*” she said. “*It is a working day. Come at nine.*”

The fever was thirty-one weeks and three days old. A man of thirty-one had been entered at the back of a book four hundred years old, in a hand that is not the book’s hand, on an unruled leaf, in the third leaf from the back and not the last. The whole of what was on it was his name, the day, and the words *whose record is not findable*, and there was no column on that leaf for anything else and nobody in that porch was going to add one.

In the inside pocket of his coat there was a page folded in four that he had offered to a woman with a key and had been told to pull back in, and she had not looked at it, and had said, in a porch, that the case that is different is a man with a case.
