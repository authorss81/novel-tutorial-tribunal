# Chapter 519: The Third Day Of The Ninety-Sixth Week, And A Thursday Which Is The Market And The First Working Day Since The Wall, On Which About Two Hundred People In A Square Are Given The Number Of A Gap In A Wall They Cannot See, And A Recordwright Of Thirty-Four Finds The Woman Of This Town Who Wrote Four Pages Out From Memory In A Square Four Hundred Miles Off, And Does Not Ask Her To Say Anything In The Square, And Enters Four Things About The Copy With The Day On Them

Third day of the ninety-sixth week. His two hundred and sixty-ninth morning. One hundred and nineteen days after the settlement. The fever thirty-three weeks and five days. Seventy-three days since the division. Nineteen days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days from where he is standing.

The wall stopped on the Wednesday night, and it is the first working day since the ground went on Monday.

Three days of work went into the top of a section about eleven feet high, on a barrow for the first day and by hand for the two after, and about nine men and a woman were on it, and one of the nine did the footing on the last morning because he is the only man of the nine who could lift a barrow of chalk up a bank and not go down on his knees in it.

The top of that section is good. The sightline from it is the whole of a hundred and forty acres and about nine hundred apple trees in a square.

The bottom of it is nine feet of nothing, with a boundary stone in a shelf in the middle of it, and it is still nine feet of nothing, and about nine men in this county are not going to shut it before the weather turns.

About two hundred people in a market square had never heard a word about any of that until the Thursday morning.

It filled between about the eighth hour and the tenth. About two hundred people, about nine stalls, a great many eggs, a wool bench, and a man who comes down off the downs once a quarter with about forty ewes. About nine men of the wall were in it with their coats over their shoulders and one of them went back up the lane at about the twelfth hour, because a beast wants milking and there is nobody in that house but him and a boy of eleven.

The number of the gap got into the square at about the eleventh hour, and about two hundred people heard it, and not one of them could check it.

---

Harl Wenlock said it first, and he said it badly, on purpose.

“*Nine feet,*” he said. “*That is the length of it. You can see it from the top lane if you go to the end and look over. You cannot see it from this square. I know the number because I put my hand in it at about four feet on Monday afternoon.*”

A man of about thirty at the ninth stall said that nine feet of a wall eleven foot high is a hole a man can go through, and Harl Wenlock said that was the first sensible thing anybody in that square had said since Thursday morning.

He stopped there. What was behind that was about a dog and about four seconds, and it had been said on the wall on Monday, and it did not need saying again in a market.

Then the recordwright said it, and she said it in pence, because she says everything in pence now and it is about a fortnight old as a habit.

“*On the sixty-first day after the division, which is a Saturday, in the second column of a book four hundred and eleven years old, in the same hand as everything else in it, there is a section of the common wall made up in a fortnight.*” She put her hand out flat, the way she does for a figure. “*There is a coping on the top of it a foot wide, set on edge, and the sightline is good. At the bottom of it there is a gap of about nine feet, because the three days went to the top. There is no column in that book for a gap.*”

She looked at the square and not at anybody in it.

“*I am telling you this in a square because about nine people were on that wall and there is nobody in this square who was on it. That is the whole of what a square is for and it has been the whole of it every Thursday since the day of the long frost.*”

About two hundred people in that square had a figure they could not check. That is a thing that happens in a country market about four times in a generation, and nobody in that square wrote the number on the back of a hand, and about nine of them said it out loud to the person next to them and about four got it wrong.

A man of about fifty wanted to go up the top lane and look over the end. About four people told him it was a mile and a half, and that the coping is nine inches proud, and that a man standing on the down side at the top of that wall would see the wall and not the gap.

He went anyway, at about the twelfth hour, and he was back at about the second hour of the afternoon.

He said that you could see the wall and not the gap, and that the figure was still a figure, and that he had walked three miles to be told a thing that four people had told him for nothing at a stall.

That is about two hundred people in a town of about two thousand four hundred knowing a number they cannot check, and the number is right, and the fact that it is right does not make it checkable, and there is no form in this county that turns a correct figure into a thing somebody can be asked about.

---

And then the recordwright saw a woman in that square who was not doing what about a hundred and eighty people in it were doing.

She is about fifty-one and she is of this town and has been of it all her life. She lives at the bottom of the town in two rooms over a milliner’s. She has had a bundle of papers for about a year and has never mentioned it to anybody, including the woman she rents the two rooms from.

She was in that square on the Thursday morning of the market of the thirty-ninth day after the division, in a town of about four thousand people two days and four miles of lane from here.

A woman of thirty-four in that square read a woman’s letter out loud. A man of thirty-one stood at the side of it and asked a question that nobody answered all the way. About two hundred people heard it and did not sleep that night.

She wrote four pages out from memory. It took her about four minutes of sitting. It is in a round hand with forty-one mistakes in it and thirty-eight of them corrected. There is a thumbprint on the third page where she put her thumb down on wet ink. At the foot of the fourth page it says where the four pages were, and it says nothing whatever about who wrote them out.

The woman of thirty-four was told about it the next day in a market, by the woman who had written it, and did not give her name.

The copy came home inside the bundle of a copyist who had come two days to a press, and it was lent out about a year ago to a woman of fifty-one at the bottom of the town, because it is the only four pages anybody in this town has ever had a copy of. It has not come back. Nobody has asked for it. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person must ask.

She was in that square on that Thursday, with a copy of the page in a bundle under her arm. She is the only person in about two hundred who has read the page itself.

---

The recordwright found her at about the twelfth hour in a lane behind the bottom of the town, and Ilyan Vester was in the lane, and he had been in it since the eleventh.

He had about four minutes of wanting something, and it was the wrong something, and it was the thing he has wanted in about nine rooms in a hundred and nineteen days.

“*Read it out,*” he said. “*In the square. There are about two hundred people in it and about nine of them have the four words by heart and none of them have the page, and if the page were read out in that square this afternoon then the four words and the page would be the same thing in the same square and about two hundred people would have both of them at once and not one of them nine days off.*”

“*No,*” said Marda Rill.

He had got as far as the whole of the sentence and she stopped him at the end of it, which is what she has always done.

“*Because the woman who wrote that copy is standing in that square with a bundle under her arm, and she has not said a word to anybody in it, and you want me to put a thing about her into about two hundred people’s mouths on a Thursday afternoon. She has not been asked. You have not asked her. I have not asked her. I am not going to do it in a square and I am not going to do it in a room either, and that is the whole of the answer and it is about a person and not about a page.*”

“*Then what do I do with it?*”

“*You do nothing with it, and that is the first useful thing anybody in this county has been told to do since the leaf was read out.*”

---

The recordwright did not ask her to say anything in the square. She said so in about four words, and the woman of fifty-one said aye, and neither of them said anything else for about nine seconds.

Then she wrote it on the back of a leaf out of her own book, in the ordinary hand, and read it back in the voice she uses for minutes, and this is the whole of it.

*That there is a copy of four pages in this town. That it was made from memory, in about four minutes of sitting, by a person who was in a square in another town, on the thirty-ninth day after the division of a common in a county nine days off. That it has a thumbprint on the third page and no name on it. And that nobody in the county of Aldwick may be asked about the original.*

Nobody in that lane asked about the original.

The woman of fifty-one said one more thing and then she did not say anything else, and what she said was that she had told herself for about a year that a person with a pen and no copier does the only thing available, and that the only reason she had not said so out loud in this town is that she has never once been asked her anything, and that nobody asking is not the same as nobody listening.

About four people in that lane were still standing there when she said that. Nobody replied to it, because there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says what a person may say in reply to a thing like that in a lane.

The recordwright said out loud why she had entered it, and the reason was that a thing which cannot be asked about is a thing somebody will invent a reason about inside a fortnight. The four words in the margin of that leaf have been invented about four times in three weeks, and twice about a man who died of a fever in a year the household of Kiln Row never mentioned, and about four people in that town believed the second of those.

And the man of thirty-one was in that lane the whole of the time, and about four people in it heard him say the sentence about the door.

“*A book is a door,*” he said, to a woman of fifty-one holding a copy with a smudge on it.

“*I have never heard that,*” she said. “*And I do not know who you are.*”

That was true, and it was the whole of it, and he had about nine seconds.

He had said it in a square in a town two days off on the thirty-ninth day after a division, in front of about two hundred people. He had said it to about nine rooms since then. A copyist of thirty-four had not let him say it in a room where it counted.

He had never once in a hundred and nineteen days worked out what a sentence does to a person who has never heard it.

About four people in that lane found out inside about nine seconds that the man who has been on a step in that porch for a fortnight says things he said in a square in another town. Nobody asked him to account for it, and nobody was going to.

The recordwright was the only person in that country who could have said whether he might be asked about it, and she is two days off with a key on a string round her neck, and there is no form in the county of Aldwick that sends anybody to a person two days off for a sentence said in the open air.

The fever was thirty-three weeks and five days old. In a lane at the bottom of a town of about two thousand four hundred people there was a smudge on the third page of a copy of four pages, and a name on nothing, and an entry with a day on it saying that there is a copy of those pages in this town and that nobody in this county may be asked about the original.
