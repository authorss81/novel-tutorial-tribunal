# Chapter 576: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Fourth Week, And A Friday, And Which Is The Market Day And The Second Market Held In That Town Since The Rising, And On Which About Three Hundred People Come Onto A Green From Which The Standing Water Has Gone Off In Seven Days, And On Which The Back Of A Remedy Of About Three Hundred Words Is Read Out Loud In The Open Air And Is Worth Less Than It Was Seven Days Ago Because About Nine Of The Three Hundred Have Been Somewhere And Seen Something, And On Which A Man With Sixteen Acres Puts Twelve Shillings And Sixpence On An Oak Bar In The Open For The Season On Six Acres His Brother Gave To A Room Eleven Days Ago And Is Refused By A Man With A Board Outside His Own Door, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Who Could Have Put That Money There Says Out Loud Why He Is Not Going To

Fourth day of the hundred and fourth week. His three hundred and twenty-sixth morning. One hundred and seventy-six days after the settlement. The fever forty-one weeks and six days. One hundred and thirty days since the division. Seventy-six days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

A market in Withermere is on a Friday and it has not moved in about two hundred years. The first one since the rising was called off by a man with no right to call it off. This one was on, and about three hundred people came onto a green between the eighth hour and the tenth.

The standing water had gone off the low side of it in seven days. Nine stones that had been moved up out of the wet the first time were still up. The other nine had not been touched and were not showing, and about nine people walked round them and about nine more did not.

There were also about nine men on that green who had come in off the drove, which is impassable for most of the year, and who had not been in that town since the fire was in the sallow four days east. They had not heard the figure of twenty-one acres. They had not been in a weigh-house. Four of them had come for a cart and five of them had come to look at the low side, and about nine of the three hundred people on that green had no way of reaching any of them before Tuesday.

---

Nobody on that green thanked anybody for the seven days either.

Then the back of the remedy was read out loud, and it was not read by the man who wrote the front of it.

A boy of fourteen read it. He had been asked to write a thing five times in his life and had said he did not know what to do with it four times, and on this Friday he read five lines in a woman’s hand off the back of a sheet of paper to about thirty people, in the open air, and he read them as slowly as he wrote them.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman at the step with the slate and the oak bar, who was not the clerk and had said so. “*A boy of fourteen has read five lines off the back of your paper in front of about thirty people. Your four sentences were read off the front of it seven days ago by a man of thirty-one. About nine of us can say both sets and about two hundred and ninety-one can say neither. I would like to know what is different about a market seven days on.*”

“*The standing water has gone off the low side of the green in seven days. About nine stones that were moved up are still up, and about nine were never moved. The first of the hay in this district is a fortnight late. A hay that is a fortnight late goes to seed, and takes a man four passes instead of one.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because that is a thing about a green and not about a remedy, and about nine people in this market have been somewhere since Wednesday and have seen a thing, and a remedy is not worth what it was on Friday when nobody in this market had been anywhere.*”

“*Then it is worth what it is worth. It is five lines and a day, and the day is the day a woman of thirty-eight came up a lane twice. A market is not a bar at a green, and a bar at a green is not a room over a stable. I have said out loud in three of those places that I cannot make one of them into the other two, and I still cannot.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

Then the sally bed went on the step. It went on the step because it is the only parcel in that district whose season is not taken up, and about thirty people were at the step at about the tenth hour to see it.

Twelve shillings and sixpence a year. Six acres. A figure in a square at the right-hand end of a line with nothing in it, for the first time in about two hundred and thirty years.

The clerk read it out the way he reads the rest of them, in the order of the three columns, and the third column was where he stopped.

Nobody put money on the bar. About nine people at that step had about twelve shillings and sixpence between them and about nine had not, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may not put what he has on a bar, and none that says a man may.

Then a man who had sixteen acres on that water came and put money on the bar, and it was a different thing, and it was done to him in the open.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, from behind the oak bar, with the slate under his arm. “*There is about twelve shillings and sixpence in small silver on that bar. There is no form in this hundred, in this county, or in this water that says a man may not put it there. About thirty people are standing at this step, and about nine of them can be checked against each other afterwards.*”

“*I am standing in front of a bar with my brother’s ditch on it. My brother is thirty-three. He has six acres of the sally bed and he gave that season to a room on the Monday before the rising and he gave it to a room on purpose, before he had time to think about it, and I have not spoken to him since the Thursday, when he was standing in a foot of water on his own ground.*”

Nobody at that step thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and say what you want, because about nine people at this step have worked out what you want and about nine have not, and I would rather all of them heard it from you.*”

“*I want the rate on those six acres paid for a year, so that the ditch between the sally bed and the low meadow is cut. I have put twelve shillings and sixpence on this bar for it, which is one hundred and fifty pence, which is a barrow of sallow and a plank and a man holding the other end, once a year. I have sixteen acres of my own. Sixteen acres at twenty-five pence the acre is four hundred pence, which is one pound thirteen shillings and fourpence, and I have worked that out twice.*”

“*Then say the rest of it, and understand that I have to answer it at a stone step in the open, and that I have a board outside my own door that says any person in the hundred may ask me anything and I will answer it, and that there is no form in the county of Kell that lets me take twelve shillings and sixpence off a man and hold it, and no form that lets me give it to anybody.*”

“*Then take it off the bar and pay the ditch.*”

Nobody at that step thanked anybody and nobody at that step was sorry for anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and say it in four sentences, because about nine people at this step have just heard me refuse a man who has offered to pay a rate on a ditch and I have never in nineteen years refused a man who offered me money, and I would like about nine of them to be able to say why in a form that fits in a head.*”

“*I cannot take it and I cannot give it. I can read it out. A man may say a number at my step and I may say the number back to him and the wall may carry it, and that is the whole of what a step is for, and it has been the whole of what a step is for in about two hundred years.*”

“*Then say the rest of that.*”

“*And about nine of the three hundred people standing behind that man cannot be asked about what he said after the hour of eleven, and about nine of them can, and I do not know which nine, and neither does he, and that is why a step is a step and not a room.*”

“*I cannot. There is no form that lets me hold it and no form that lets me give it away. If I put it in my own purse then the water-clerk of Withermere has a hundred and fifty pence of another man’s ground in his pocket, and in about four years a man will say to a room that I took his money, and there is nothing in this county that would let anybody say otherwise to me. So the money goes back in your pocket. The six acres stay with nobody. The ditch is not cut, and in about two years the low meadow is gone and not in a wet year.*”

“*Then I am going to say a thing at this step and I would like the clerk to write it down if he can, and I do not think he can.*”

Nobody at that step said anything.

“*Say it, and about nine of us will have it tonight and about two hundred and ninety-one will not be able to be asked about it, and that is not my complaint. That is the shape of a market.*”

---

Then the silver went back in the man’s pocket. About nine people in that market watched it go, and about nine more looked away, and the two nines did not ask each other anything.

It turned out that the only person in Withermere who could have put that money where it was wanted was a man of thirty-one with a bad leg. He had been standing about nine feet off the step with his mouth shut since the fourth hour, and about nine people in that market had worked that out in the four seconds it took the silver to go back into a pocket.

And then a woman of about thirty-eight came through the middle of that market with a barrow and a boy of about four. She said nine words in the middle of a green where about three hundred people were standing, and a man of thirty-one heard her say them and heard himself answer out loud before he had worked out that he was going to.

“*He cannot be stopped and he can be asked.*”

About four people in that market turned round.

Nobody in that market thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman at the step with the slate. “*About four of us have turned round and about nine have not. About nine of us have heard a man of thirty-one say a thing out loud in a market that he has not said in a week, and I would like to know what the four of us have turned round for.*”

“*Those are the words of the woman with the barrow. She said them at a gate on a towpath twenty days ago, before any of you had heard a number of feet. I wrote them down in my own head, which is the only place I have ever been able to keep anything, and I have not said out loud to one person in this county that they were hers.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and say it to her and not to me. I have a slate, and about nine people who can be checked against each other, and she is standing about nine feet behind you and has heard it, and there is no form in this county that says a man may not take nine words off a woman in a market.*”

He turned round and said it to her, in the open, with about thirty people within about nine feet of him.

“*Those are your words. You said them at a gate twenty days ago and I have carried them since, and I have not said so, and about nine people in this market have just heard me say them twice. I am not going to ask you whether I may. That would be the fourth time in twenty-two days that I have made a person the worse for something I did in the open, and I have not asked to be the one who says a number in this county and I am not going to start asking to be the one who says this.*”

Nobody in that market thanked anybody and nobody in that market was sorry for anybody.

Then a man of thirty-one stood about nine feet off the step and said out loud why he was not going to put that money on that bar, and about thirty people heard him.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman with the slate. “*Because there is a man of thirty-one standing nine feet off my step in a market of about three hundred people and he has said nothing in a week, and about nine of us noticed that and about nine did not, and I would like to know what he is doing there.*”

“*I am standing in front of a bar with about twelve shillings and sixpence on it that I could put there, and I have under two pounds, and I am not going to put it there, and I am going to say why in front of about thirty people, and I have not worked out how to say it so that about nine of them repeat it and about nine of them do not.*”

Nobody in that market thanked anybody.

“*Then say it, and say it slowly, and understand that about nine people will have it at the pump tonight in a different order and that is not a thing you can do anything about.*”

“*A man who pays a rate on a parcel holds the season on it for a year. There is no form in this county that says a man may not. There is none that says a man may. If I put that money on that bar then in about four seconds I am one of about thirty men. Everything I have said in that room for twenty-two days would then be said by a man who holds eleven of the seats, or one of them, or a twelfth. About nine people in that room would be right about that and about nine would not.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because about nine of us have not worked out what the rest of it is, and I have a slate and I would like it on the slate if it can go on the slate.*”

“*The rest of that is that a man who holds a season in that hundred cannot stand in that room and say a true thing about a man in it. The only thing I have ever been in this county is that. I would be giving it up in front of about three hundred people to buy a barrow of sallow and a plank.*”

Nobody in that market thanked anybody and nobody in that market was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-one weeks and six days old. A market of about three hundred people was held on a green from which the standing water had gone off in seven days, and about nine stones that had been moved up out of the wet were still up and about nine had never been moved.

A boy of fourteen read five lines off the back of a remedy of about three hundred words in the open air, in a woman’s hand, and the remedy was worth less at that market than it had been seven days before, because about nine of about three hundred people had been somewhere and seen something and could not be argued with.

A man with sixteen acres put twelve shillings and sixpence on an oak bar at a stone step, in the open, for the season on six acres of the sally bed that his brother had given to a room eleven days ago. A man with a board outside his own door refused him in the open and gave the reason in nine sentences, and the money went back in his pocket.

And a man of thirty-one with under two pounds said out loud in front of about thirty people that he was not going to put that money on that bar, and gave the reason. The reason was that a man who holds a season in that hundred cannot stand in that room and say a true thing about a man in it. Nobody in that market thanked him for it. About nine of about three hundred people can repeat every word of it, and about two hundred and ninety-one cannot be asked about it.
