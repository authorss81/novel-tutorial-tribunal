# Chapter 594: The First Day Of The Hundred And Seventh Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which About Nine People Are In A Room Over A Stable With A Sheet About Four Feet Wide On A Board And Nothing At The Top Of It, And On Which A Water-Clerk Says Out Loud That There Is No Form In The County Of Kell That Gives Anything To Anybody And Has Never Been One, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In About Nine People’s Hearing That A Settlement Cannot Give Fifty-Eight People A Place And Is Told In The Same Hour That He Has Said Half Of It Before Anybody Has Found Out What There Is Instead Of It, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Says Out Loud That There Is No Licence And Never Has Been One In Twenty-Eight Days And That The Sack Is Not On That Ground And Not In That Town, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-One Puts Six Acres Down With No Name At The Top Of It And No Name At The Bottom Of It, And On Which Somebody Puts An Arm Out Along Two Hundred And Twenty Feet Of A Rise And There Is Nothing At The End Of It

First day of the hundred and seventh week. His three hundred and forty-fourth morning. One hundred and ninety-four days after the settlement. The fever forty-four weeks and three days. One hundred and forty-eight days since the division. Ninety-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixth week.

---

About nine people were in a room over a stable on the Tuesday afternoon and the sheet about four feet wide was on a board and there was nothing at the top of it.

The river had been at the cut in the tally stone for seven days. It was a thumbnail’s width under the cut and it had not gone over. A water-clerk of fifty-eight had put a rod in that channel twice that morning and had said out loud that he did not know what it meant, and would not be asked what he remembered it meant.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody.

---

Then the second thing on that sheet took about two hours and it went wrong twice.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane, who had a holding, a seat in the hundred and a skep behind his own gate. “*Because about nine of us have been in this room since the second hour, and the sheet has two numbers on it and neither of them is a place. There is a woman of twenty-eight on that rise with eleven tents up and about fifty-eight people on about eight hundred acres of nobody’s common. I would like somebody to say out loud what the second thing is.*”

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*and say it in about nine sentences. I have a wall with fifty-eight in the taking column of that line, and a second line under it with a day on it and nothing above it and nothing below it.*”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“*Then say that last part again,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*I am not going to write a third thing under it this week without saying what it is first,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

Nobody in that room had anything to say.

“*The second thing is that there is no form,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*There is no form in the county of Kell that gives anything to anybody. There is no form that renews a licence and there is no form that takes one away, and there is no form that puts a person on a piece of ground.*”

Nobody in that room answered.

“*Then say the rest of that and say why now,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*There has never been one. About nine people have been waiting since Monday for somebody to say that out loud, so that the rest of us can stop looking for the piece of paper.*”

---

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it before he had found out what was there instead.

He had been in that county forty days. He had said four things at a step on the Saturday, and a number of feet on a rise on the Monday that a woman of twenty-eight took out of his mouth in about four seconds, and had been told that about nine mouths in that hundred would be measuring children by Wednesday.

He wanted the sheet to be finished and he wanted to be in the room and not the author of anything.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, who had come the four miles in the flat and had a daughter of six asleep against her shoulder. “*Because you have been in this room about two hours and about nine of us have watched you look at a sheet nine feet away with your hands behind your back, and you have not said anything and I would like you to either say the wrong half or go outside.*”

“*I am standing in front of a sheet and there is no form in this county that gives a person a place, and a settlement cannot give what the county does not have.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*And so the settlement cannot give the fifty-eight a place, and the second number on that sheet is two hundred and twenty feet of a rise that belongs to nobody, and if it is written down it is written down as a fact and not as a grant.*”

Nobody in that room took it up.

“*That is the true of it,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and it is half of it. You have said it in a room of about nine people before either of us has found out what there is instead of it. About four of us have that now, and about four of those four will say it at the bar on Friday.*”

Nobody in that room thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Aldis Crane. “*A man of thirty-one has now said out loud that a settlement cannot give fifty-eight people a place. I have a holding and a seat and about nine people in this room with me.*”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Hoare Ryley, from the doorway.

“*I would like somebody to tell him what it can give, before the fourth hour, because I have heard that sentence in a market before.*”

---

Then somebody told him, and it was not him, and it took about an hour.

“*It can give them a figure and a day and a thing a person can go and look at,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*That is all there is and it is not nothing, because about nine people in that hundred have never once been told a figure and a day and a place they could walk to and check in about four seconds.*”

“*Then say the rest of that and say what is on the sheet,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*Two hundred and twenty feet of the top of a rise that belongs to nobody. Eleven tents. Fifty-eight people, and that number is the woman of twenty-eight’s, and no person in this county may add to it or take from it. About eight hundred acres round it. The day it was written.*”

“*Then say the rest of it,*” said Aldis Crane, “*because I have read over your shoulder and there are four things on that sheet and I know of about nine.*”

“*And one line,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*It is the only line on that sheet that anybody in this room will argue about. A foot of water went over the low side of the swell under those tents on the second day after the rising morning, and two tents were standing at the ninth hour on that day, and nobody was drowned.*”

Nobody in that room had anything to say.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and say whether any of it is a licence.*”

“*It is not a licence and it does not say it is one. A licence has a day on it and this has a day on it and there is no person on either of them, and a licence is a thing one person gives to another person. Two hundred and twenty feet of a rise is not a person and neither is the sack that used to have a sheet in it.*”

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*because that is the first time in four weeks that anybody in this hundred has said out loud that there is no licence, and I have been carrying the true of that sack since the day the water turned.*”

Nobody in that room thanked anybody.

“*Say it,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*There is no licence,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*There is one sheet of paper with a figure and a day on it and no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it, and four words at the top that say for the common, in season. It was under a churn hoop on a rise that belongs to nobody.*”

Nobody in that room said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Aldis Crane, “*and say where that paper is.*”

“*The water turned on the morning it came over the tally stone and this is the twenty-eighth day after that morning. The sack is not on that ground and it is not in that town. There is no copy of it in this county and the man who writes on the wall has never seen it.*”

“*Then say that last part,*” said Hoare Ryley.

“*There is no form anywhere in the county of Kell that renews anything or gives anything.*”

About four people in that room said something at once.

---

Then a woman of forty-one put a parcel and a day on that sheet with no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it.

That is what she has had on a wall and on a paper under an oak bar since the ninth day from the rising. She said out loud why, and it was the reason she has given twice.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because a woman has put six acres on a sheet about four feet wide with no name at the top of it. About nine of us have read that parcel on that wall every morning for five weeks, and about nine of us have never worked out who put it there.*”

“*Six acres of the second close off the dyke, and twelve shillings and sixpence a year, and the day I was told at a step in a market that it was not mine any more,*” said Hester Nunn. “*And no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it. A paper with a name at the top of it in a county where about nine people read that wall every morning is a paper nine people can be argued with, and I would rather somebody argued with a parcel.*”

Nobody in that room thanked anybody.

Then the sheets came off the board at about the fourth hour and there were two of them and about nine of the people in that room had put something on one or the other of them, and there was still nothing at the top of either.

---

And then they went out and looked, because about nine of the people in that room had been told a figure and a day and a place and about nine of them had not been there.

The water on the low side of the flat was about nine inches deep over about four acres of it, and the wind at about the seventh hour pushed it over the top of a swell about eleven feet wide. That is the reason a man can be drowned four miles from where he can walk, and there is no hill in that country at all.

Nobody on that common thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, to the man of thirty-one, on the flat, out of the wind, in about nine people’s hearing. “*Because you have been in that room about two hours, and about four of us have your sentence in the wrong shape. You have not asked one of us what a settlement can give. I am going to put my arm out along this rise and I would like you to look at the end of it.*”

She put her arm out along the top of it.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*That is the whole of the second thing on that sheet and there is nothing at the end of my arm. A person can point at a rise and a person can point at eleven tents and a person cannot point at two hundred and twenty feet, and that is not because the number is wrong. It is because there has never been a place in this hundred that a person could point at and put a name to, and now there is a figure and a day and about nine hundred yards of top with nobody standing on it, and about nine of us have been out here to look at it.*”

Nobody on that common had anything to say.

The fever was forty-four weeks and three days old. Fifty-eight people are on about eight hundred acres of nobody’s common, with eleven tents up on two hundred and twenty feet of a rise about eleven feet wide. There is no form in the county of Kell that gives anything to anybody or renews anything, and the one sheet that ever said they might stay said it until the water turned. The water turned twenty-eight days ago.

A settlement gave them a figure, a day, and the true of about four inches of water going over the low side of a swell. It is not a licence and it is not a column and it is not a form. A man of thirty-one said out loud in a room of about nine people that it could not be any of those things either, and was told in the same hour that he had said half of it before anybody had found out what there was instead of it.

And a woman of twenty-eight stood on the top of a rise about nine hundred yards long with her arm out along it, and there is nothing at the end of her arm, and that is the whole of the place, and no person in that hundred can point at it.
