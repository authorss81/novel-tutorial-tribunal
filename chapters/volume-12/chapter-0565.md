# Chapter 565: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Second Week, And A Monday, And On Which The Water Off A Cut Drain Comes Back Up A Trench Four Feet Wide Where The Cut Had Been Because A Man Of Fifty-Four Took It Out On Saturday At His Own Cost As He Said In Front Of Thirty People He Would, And The Last Day In The Hundred On Which A Season Can Be Given Up By The Person Who Holds It, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-Three Gives Up The Season On Six Acres And Does Not Give It To A Woman Of Forty-One And Does Not Give It To A Stranger And Says Out Loud That He Is Giving It To A Room, And Nobody Thanks Him For It And He Is Not A Man Of The Hundred And There Is No Seat For Him

Seventh and last day of the hundred and second week. His three hundred and fifteenth morning. One hundred and sixty-five days after the settlement. The fever forty weeks and two days. One hundred and nineteen days since the division. Sixty-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The morrow is the first day of the hundred and third week, and the rising is in the morning, and the clerk was not asked for the number once today and that was the first time in about nine days.

---

The drain was a mile of water and it was four feet deep and it ran north off the top of the long grazing, dead straight, cut by two men in an hour and a half in the first week of March.

It was filled in on Saturday by eleven men and a barrow and a team, at the holder’s own cost, out of a farm four miles south, in one day and a half, and the water that came back went where it had gone for two hundred years.

It came back on Saturday night. By Sunday morning there was a trench of black mud four feet wide where the cut had been, and a mound of earth along one side of it, and a foot of standing water in the old line, and it was not going anywhere.

A man of fifty-one who has cut ditch in that district for about thirty years stood at the top end of the trench at about the seventh hour on Monday morning, with nine men behind him, and did not say anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have been looking at that trench since the light came and none of the nine of us has said a word and the rising is tomorrow.*”

“*There is water in the old line and there is going to be more of it, and it is going to go down the meadow on Thursday because that is what the meadow is for, and the meadow ends in about eight hundred acres that belong to nobody.*”

Nobody on that bank thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because a man of fifty-four paid for the taking out of that drain in front of about thirty people on Wednesday, and nine of us have stood here since the sixth hour and none of us has worked out what that bought.*”

“*It bought a fortnight of water on the north side and a season of keep for a man of fifty-four, and it did not buy the common, and it was never going to buy the common, because the common is at the bottom of this district and everything in this district ends up at the common.*”

---

Then it was the last day on which a season could be given up by the person who held it. That is a thing about the river and not about the calendar. About nine people in that hundred had it in their heads since Friday and one of them came at about the ninth hour.

He was about thirty-three and he had six acres of the sally bed on the top side, and the rate on it was twelve shillings and sixpence, and he had held it for four years, and he had held it by paying it out of a cart.

He came to the weigh-house door at about the ninth hour with about thirty people in it and did not go inside, and stood at the step with the bar across it, which is the place a man stands when he is offering something and not when he is asking.

“*Say what you are standing on,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because you have come to the step at the ninth hour on a Monday and this is the last day a season can be given up and about thirty people are in this room and about nine of them are waiting for you.*”

“*I am giving up the season on the sally bed. Six acres. I am not selling it and I am not giving it away and I am not giving it to a person. I am giving it to the room.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because I have written that figure on that sheet nine times in nine years and there is no column on it with a person’s name at the top and a man cannot hand a season to a room.*”

“*Then write nothing on it. Let it lapse in the morning with everybody else’s. If I do not pay the rate on it by the seventh hour tomorrow it is nobody’s, and if it is nobody’s then it is not in the book at all, and if it is not in the book then it cannot have come off me, and that is what I want and it is the only thing I have ever wanted out of a piece of paper.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say the reason, because in about four days there will be about nine people in this hundred saying a man gave a holding away in a panic the night before the rising, and I am going to have to write down whatever you say here.*”

“*A man with about seven hundred sheep took the season on the second close off the dyke on the thirteenth of April. He paid the rate on it and the penny on it. It is his until the water turns and it has been his for a hundred and sixty-five days, and a woman of forty-one has had nothing out of it but a fence and a road and a hedge that is a hedge.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I know all of it and about nine people in this room know all of it, and you have not said one word that makes it new.*”

“*Then here is the new part. I cannot stand in there tomorrow.*”

He looked at about thirty people in a room about sixty feet long.

“*There are about thirty men in this room in the morning and I am not one of them. I am four miles up the drove with about nine acres of sward a man sowed in March, and it is dead. I have a brother with sixteen acres on that water.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because those are facts about your family and not about a room, and you have stood at this step about nine minutes.*”

“*The rest of that is that I have been told once this week that there is a number of boards going into that gate. I was told it by a stranger at my own door on Thursday night. I have not been in this room in four years and I am not going to be in it tomorrow while a man tells me what a stranger said at my door.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything, and the man on the step did not come in.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said you are not coming, and a hundred is a hundred and you have not been in it for four years, and you may go on not being in it for four more, and nobody here can make you.*”

“*I have been not in it for four years. I want it to be nobody’s in the morning and I want that written nowhere and I want it done before I have had time to think about it, and I have brought the twelve shillings and sixpence with me and I am going to give it back to the sheet and not to a man.*”

He put a purse on the oak bar and it was the first thing on that bar all morning that had not been a season, a slate or a loaf.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have brought the rate to a step and about thirty people are standing here and this is not a thing that has ever happened on this bar in about two hundred years, and I want to know what you want said about it afterwards.*”

“*Nothing. I want nothing said about it. And if you say anything about it I will tell you that you said it wrong, and I have the rate in my hand and I will put it back in my pocket, and then the six acres will be a man’s on Wednesday the same as they are today.*”

“*That is the first threat anybody has made at this step since the toll was taken off the Four Gates, and there is no gate here.*”

“*Then it is not a threat. Then it is a man telling you what he will do with his own twelve shillings and sixpence, and about nine people here understand that and about nine do not.*”

---

It went back in the pocket.

The man stood at the step for about four more minutes and then he said the other thing, and about thirty people heard it.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have put your money away and about nine people here thought you were about to do a kind thing and you have not.*”

“*I am going to give it to nobody. Not to the woman it was taken off, because she refused a stranger’s money in a market ten days ago in four sentences and she was right and I was in the market. Not to a stranger, because he has a bad leg and under two pounds in that county and he was at my door on Thursday and he did not ask me for anything and I would not let him.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have now named two people you are not giving it to, and one of them is in this room, and I do not know which.*”

“*The woman is not in this room. The woman is a woman of forty-one and she is at a well two miles off and she has not been in this building since April.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you are giving up a year of a man’s living for a reason that is about two people not being in a room, and I have not heard a man in nineteen years give up a thing for a reason that could be checked.*”

“*Then write that down. Both halves. A man of thirty-three gave up the season on six acres of the sally bed on Monday, the day before the rising. He gave it to a room and not to a person.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because that is four lines and a man is not stood at a step for nine minutes for four lines.*”

“*The reason he gave it to a room is that he is not a man of the hundred, there is no seat for him, and he was not going to be in one tomorrow while a man told him what a stranger had said at his door.*”

He put his hand flat on the bar.

“*Write it without his name. I will not have it in a book with my name on it. There is no column for a person on that sheet and I am not going to be the first one that somebody adds.*”

“*Then it is a fact about six acres and not about a man.*”

“*That is the first time anybody in this hundred has asked me for that and I would like somebody to write it down that I asked for it.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

---

Then the clerk did the thing about the money, and he did it in the open because everything in that building is done in the open now.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have written nothing and you have written nothing twice, which is not the same as writing nothing once, and about nine people will think I have refused him twice.*”

“*I have refused him once. There is no form in this hundred that lets me take twelve shillings and sixpence off a man and hold it, and there is no form that lets me give it to anybody. So the purse goes back in his pocket and the six acres lapses in the morning with the other hundred and twenty-nine, and there is no figure in the square at the right-hand end of that line tomorrow because there is nobody to put one there.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because a line on that sheet with nothing in the square at the end of it is not a thing that has ever been on that wall in two hundred and thirty years.*”

“*Then it will have been on it for one day and there is nobody in this county that can tell me it is wrong.*”

---

Ilyan stood at the wool table at about the eleventh hour and did the thing he does, and about thirty people were in the room and about nine of them were watching him.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*I am standing in front of a line on a wall with nothing in the square at the end of it, and I am going to say the number it will cost, and nobody will stop me, and that is the whole of what I am and it is why you asked me to be in the room.*”

“*Say the number.*”

“*Two shillings and a penny the acre on six acres is twelve shillings and sixpence a year. That is one hundred and fifty pence. And four pounds of the whole rate is your wage, and the rest cuts the ditches, and one hundred and fifty pence of that is the ditch between the sally bed and the low meadow for a year.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and then say what happens to the ditch, because that is the part about nine people are waiting for.*”

“*The ditch fills in. In about two years, because that is what you told a market on the fourth day of the hundred and first week and what a man of fifty-four proved in this room on Wednesday, and the two figures agree and I have not found a way to make them not agree.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have now said out loud that the man who gave it up is not the one who will lose the ditch and that is true and it is not new.*”

“*It is not new to me. That is what I have learned in eleven days in a county where a record is on a wall. A thing on a wall does not cost the person who put it there. It costs the next person, and the next person is not on the wall, because there is no column on that sheet with a person’s name at the top of it.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said out loud in my building that a thing I write costs somebody else, and about nine people have wanted to hear that for nineteen years and about nine people have wanted me not to say it.*”

“*Then you are the ninth one, and I am sorry for it, and I am going to be in this room tomorrow at midday and I am not going to speak unless you ask me, and I have not worked out why I told you that in front of thirty people instead of at your door.*”

“*Because at your door is where everything comes out in this hundred and I have watched you find that out in eleven days, and so have I, and it is the first thing about this place that I have got right in eleven days and it has cost me nothing.*”

The fever was forty weeks and two days old. The last day in the hundred on which a season can be given up by the person who holds it came and went. A man of thirty-three gave up the season on six acres of the sally bed and gave it to a room and not to a person. He gave it because there is no column on that sheet with a person’s name at the top of it, and he would not be the first that somebody adds.

He did not give it to a woman of forty-one who refused a stranger’s money in a market in four sentences ten days ago, and he did not give it to a stranger with a bad leg who was at his door on Thursday night and did not ask him for anything.

And nobody thanked him for it, and he is not a man of the hundred, and there is no seat for him.

And the line on the wall will have nothing in the square at the right-hand end of it in the morning, for the first time in about two hundred and thirty years, and one hundred and fifty pence of ditch will go out of it, and no person in that county can say that is wrong.
