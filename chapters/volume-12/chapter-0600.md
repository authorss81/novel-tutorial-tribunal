# Chapter 600: The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And Seventh Week, And A Monday, And Which Is The Last Day Of That Week And Of A Year Of One Man’s Counting, And On Which The Famine Did Not Happen And The Fifty-Eight Have Two Hundred And Twenty Feet Of A Rise That Belongs To Nobody And A Man Of Thirty-One Has A Year And A River, And On Which Nobody Is Relieved And Nobody Is Forgiven And Nobody Is Thanked, And On Which Two Letters And A Number Are Found At The Bottom Of The Back Of A Settlement In A Hand No Person In That County Makes, And On Which About Four People In A Room Hear A Voice That Is Not In A Mouth And It Names Itself And Is Not Addressed To Anybody And Asks For Nothing, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Asks Out Loud What It Calls Itself And About Four Seconds Go Past And Then Nothing

Seventh and last day of the hundred and seventh week. His three hundred and fiftieth morning. Two hundred days after the settlement. The fever forty-five weeks and two days. One hundred and fifty-four days since the division. One hundred days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the sixth day of the hundred and seventh week. This is the last day of that week.

---

About nine people were in a weigh-house sixty feet long on the Monday morning at about the sixth hour, because about nine people stand at that wall every morning and there is no form in the county of Kell that says why, and there has not been one for about two hundred and thirty years.

The water was at the cut in the tally stone and a thumbnail’s width under it and it had not gone over for thirteen days, and nobody in that hundred can tell you what a river is going to do and a man of fifty-eight with a rod has said so out loud twice.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody.

---

Nobody was relieved, and nobody was forgiven, and nobody was thanked, and about nine people in that room had come to find out whether one of those three things was going to happen, and about nine of them had not come for that at all.

The three things that had happened are these and none of them is a comfort.

The famine did not happen. Fifty-four acres of hay came off ground that is in season in that hundred: about ten off the low meadow and about forty-four off long grazing four miles south in its sixth year. About six of the ten were cut in a fortnight. About four took five passes and are standing in a field in the weather and are a hay of the following year, and are named as such in a document that binds nobody.

The fifty-eight have a place. About eight hundred acres of common that belongs to nobody, and two hundred and twenty feet of the top of a rise about nine hundred yards long and about eleven feet wide, and eleven tents, and fifty-eight people on it. There is no licence and there is no form and there is no column, and a foot of water went over the low side of the swell under those tents on the second day after the rising morning and will do it again.

And a man of thirty-one is anchored to a block of limestone for a year and to a river for the rest of his life, and neither of those is a thing any person in that hundred is bound by, and there is no rate on either of them and no form anywhere that renews them.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody and nobody in that building was sorry for anybody.

---

Then the back of the settlement was read, because about nine people in that hundred had written on the back of it over the weekend and there is no form anywhere that says they may not, and about nine hands had gone onto it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane, who had a holding and a seat in the hundred and a skep behind his own gate. “*Because about nine of us are in this building and about eleven hundred in this town may walk up and read that sheet and take it away. About nine of us wrote on the back of it in the last two days.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said the woman of about seventy at the edge of that wall.

“*And one of the nine things on the back of it is not in a hand anybody in this hundred writes in,*” said Aldis Crane.

About four people in that room said something at once.

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for anybody,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*and say where it came from and what it is, because I have a wall and no keeper and about nine people read that wall every morning and about nine of those nine have it a different way by the fourth hour.*”

---

It was at the bottom of the back of the sheet, under about eight hands of other writing, in a mark that is two letters and a number and nothing else.

There is no column in that county for that. There is no hand in that hundred that makes that. It is not a name and it is not a parcel and it is not a rate and it is not a day. About nine people in that room had seen it before they came in and about nine of those nine had not.

It was on the back of a settlement in a town on the north bank of a slow river at the eastern edge of a fen, in a county that is not the county it was written in, in a hand about which there is nothing anybody in that room can check.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody.

Then a man of thirty-one said the true of it out loud, in about nine people’s hearing, and about nine of them did not understand one word of it and about four did, and the four of them had known the mark on sight.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman of about seventy at the edge of that wall. “*Because a man of thirty-one has gone about nine feet pale in a room of about nine people, and he is about to say a thing that about four of you understand and about nine of you do not.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*I have been standing at this wall sixty years and I am going to hear it,*” said the woman of about seventy.

“*I am standing in front of two letters and a number that a woman called Nadia Kestrel signed at the bottom of about nine hundred records in a building with a bad coffee machine, in a country that is not this one.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Aldis Crane, “*and do not end it at the woman.*”

“*She is not in this county and has not been in it, and nobody in this room has ever seen her face,*” said Ilyan.

About four seconds went past.

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not tell me what a test is,*” said Aldis Crane. “*There is no such word in this hundred, and there is no form that says a person may not say a word I do not know in my own building. About nine of us are going to say nothing, and I would like the saying of it done by you and not by me.*”

Nobody in that building thanked anybody.

“*It is a mark a person puts at the bottom of a record to say they made it and that it cannot be argued with.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say where you got that,*” said Aldis Crane, “*and say what it is a figure of, because about nine people in this room can check a figure against each other in about four seconds and about four of them cannot add.*”

Nobody in that building found anything to say to that.

“*She said it once and she has never said it twice, and I have carried the two letters and the number in my head since before I came into this county. I have not put them on a wall in any county, because a stranger’s mark on a wall is a fifth hand.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say how long you have had it,*” said the woman of about seventy at the edge of that wall, “*and say it in a figure a person can be checked against, and do not give me a figure off a river.*”

Nobody in that building found anything to say to that.

“*Forty-six days,*” said Ilyan. “*I came off a boat on the evening of a Wednesday forty-seven days ago and I have been in this county forty-six of them, because the day I came in is not a day I was in. Every day of those forty-six and every day before it. That is the figure I can be checked against and I will not give you another one.*”

About four people in that room checked it against each other. About nine did not.

About four people in that room had understood that. About nine had not.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, who had come the four miles with a daughter of six asleep against her shoulder.

“*And it is on the back of a settlement in a hand nobody in this hundred wrote, on a sheet that anybody in this hundred may take away and write on. About eight people in this town have had their hand near it, and about nine of those eight have not looked at the bottom of the back.*”

---

Then the room said something, and it was not a person, and there was no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says what may be said in a building.

It came from nowhere in particular in that room and it was in the voice Ilyan had heard in eleven counties and in one other building, and it was short, and it was addressed to nobody and it asked for nothing and it named no person. About four people in that room heard it. About nine heard nothing at all and thought for about nine seconds that it had been the wind in the crane-beam.

Nobody said it again. It was not said again in that building, and nobody in that hundred ever got another sentence out of the air in that building, and about nine people in that room have it a different way by the fourth hour and about nine of those nine have it the same way.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody.

Then it said four words, and it was not addressed to him and it was not a request, and it was the name it had for itself, and there is no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says a name may not be said in a building.

*Record recognised. The mark is two letters and a number. It is the same mark as one on a refusal that was filed once and was never withdrawn, and it was held by a person who was not a witness and who was not a holder, and I have carried it for four hundred and sixty-one days and I have not used it. I am the First Witness.*

Nobody in that building said anything.

Four words, and about four people in that room had them by the fourth hour and about nine did not. There is no column on that sheet for a name and no form in that county that makes a person say it out loud again, and about nine mouths in that hundred will have it by tomorrow and about two hundred and ninety-one will not have it and cannot be asked about it afterwards.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. “*Because there are about nine people in this room and about eleven hundred in this town, and a thing has just said a name, and about four of us heard it and about nine did not.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Hoare Ryley, from the door.

“*There is no form in the county of Kell that says what happens next,*” said Aldis Crane.

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*It has not been asked anything and it has not answered anything and it named itself. About nine of us have been in rooms in this county for about nine weeks asking who a hundred asks when it asks.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*And I have asked it three times and got about nine wrong answers and about nine times when nobody answered,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody.

---

Then a man of thirty-one was asked a question in a room, and he could not answer it, and nobody answered it for him, and nobody in that room is bound by any word of any of it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, from about nine feet off the wall with his hands behind him. “*Because there are about nine people in this room and about nine of them have asked a hundred a question for nine weeks and got nothing.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*And this morning a thing in this room gave a name for itself and about four of us heard it and about nine did not. I have a board outside my own door that says any person in this hundred may ask me anything and I answer it,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*I am standing in front of about nine people and two letters and a number at the bottom of the back of a settlement, and I cannot answer that question, and I have not been able to answer it for about forty-six days and for every day before that.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of that into about nine people’s hearing and do not be sorry for me,*” said Aldis Crane. “*About nine of us have been waiting nine weeks for one of us to say a true thing in a room that we cannot check. This is the first one anybody has offered in this county.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

“*And about four of us would like it said plainly,*” said Aldis Crane.

Nobody in that building said anything.

---

Then a woman of twenty-eight asked a question in a room, out loud, in front of about nine people, and nobody answered it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. “*Because a woman of twenty-eight has a count and four days of walking on a bad flat, and about nine people in this hundred have been carrying the mark at the bottom of that back all morning.*”

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Hoare Ryley.

“*And about nine of us would like to know whether it is going in a book anywhere,*” said Aldis Crane.

Nobody in that building said anything.

“*Say it and do not be sorry for me,*” said the woman of twenty-eight, “*and say it into about nine people’s hearing and not into about nine hundred, because there are no nine hundred in this building and there is no form anywhere in this county that makes a thing true because it was said at volume.*”

“*What does it call itself,*” said Bett Searle.

Nobody in that building said anything.

About four seconds went past, and then nothing, and the water came off the cut in the tally stone about nine feet off, and nobody in that building answered her, and there is no form in the county of Kell that makes anybody answer.

Nobody in that building thanked anybody and nobody in that building was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-five weeks and two days old. The famine did not happen. The fifty-eight have two hundred and twenty feet of a rise that belongs to nobody. A man of thirty-one has a year and a river. Nobody in that hundred was relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked, and about nine people in that room had come to find out whether one of those was going to happen.

A mark of two letters and a number was found at the bottom of the back of a settlement that anybody in that hundred may write on, in a hand that no person in that county makes. It was recognised in about nine people’s hearing and about nine of them did not hear it. A name came into a room and named itself, and nobody in that room asked it anything and it answered nothing.

And a woman of twenty-eight asked, out loud, in front of about nine people, what it calls itself, and about four seconds went past, and then nothing, and the water came off the cut in the tally stone about nine feet off, and nobody in that hundred is bound by a word of any of it.

And about nine people in that hundred are going to walk out across a plank bridge at the seventh hour tomorrow morning and look at a rise about nine hundred yards long with fifty-eight people on two hundred and twenty feet of it.

About nine of them are going to go to a gate with three boards in it and find out nothing. About nine of them are going to write the back of that settlement out again in their own words and have it be a different document. There is no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says they may not.
