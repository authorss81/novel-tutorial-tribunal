# Chapter 574: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Fourth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Remedy Of About Three Hundred Words With Nothing At The Top Of It Which Has Lain Under An Oak Bar Since A Friday Is Taken Off That Bar By A Woman Of Thirty-Eight Who Is Not Its Author And Who Is Better At It, And On Which The Man Who Wrote It Argues For It In About Nine People’s Hearing And Is Told Out Loud That He Is The Worst Man In That County To Be Arguing About A Document, And On Which A Letter Of Four Lines Comes Up A Drain On A Flat Boat And Is Not Read Out To Anybody

Second day of the hundred and fourth week. His three hundred and twenty-fourth morning. One hundred and seventy-four days after the settlement. The fever forty-one weeks and four days. One hundred and twenty-eight days since the division. Seventy-four days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

A remedy of about three hundred words with nothing at the top of it has lain under an oak bar at the north end of that green since Friday morning. Any person in that hundred may read it, take it away, and write on the back of it, and no form in the county of Kell says a person may not.

Five days.

About nine people had read it. Nobody had written on the back of it, and it had been as clean as the front since it was put there, and about four people had said out loud that they were not going to be the first.

---

Nobody thanked anybody for the five days either. About nine people expected it. About four of them wrote a reason on a board, and one of those reasons was about themselves.

The first person to write on the back of it was a woman of thirty-eight with four acres on the top side. She had been up and down the lane twice on the Tuesday. She had asked a man of fifty-eight for a parcel to be written against her own challenge, and she had got it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. He was standing at the bar and he had not touched the sheet. “*Because you have taken it off that bar in front of about nine people. A boy of fourteen is standing here, and about nine of us can read what you write as you write it. That is the only reason I have not taken it off you.*”

“*A bar, a sheet of paper, and about five days. I have not written anything yet. I want to say the first thing out loud before I put a pen on it. About nine people in this hundred will read the back of that in thirty days, and only four of us will remember this.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Say it. Then say why you are the one writing it. I have told you twice this week that I would rather somebody argued with a figure than with a person, and you are a person, and I want to know what changed.*”

“*A figure has been wrong about my ground twice. Once on Saturday last week, when a man of sixty-two read a line that says hay, mown. Once in April, when a man came round with a purse.*”

“*Then say the rest of that.*”

“*You told me last night that the fifth of your five challenges had no parcel against it. I have thought about that for a day and a night. The reason nobody could check those five is that the thing they were about is not a person either.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and then write, and I will stand here and read it as you write it and about nine people will hear it before it is dry.*”

She turned the sheet over and wrote five lines in a hand that was not his, and the hand was the hand of a woman who had signed a form at a market step in April and not a name on anything since.

The five lines said these things.

That the document does not say whose hay it is. There is no column anywhere on a sheet of parchment two men long with a person’s name at the top of it, and this paper has nothing at the top of it either. A man can read the whole of it and go away thinking it is about a water.

That the document does not say how many men there are. About nine, and a fortnight, and a scythe, and about a fourth of those nine are in a hundred of about thirty men and about a fourth of them are one man four miles south who has not been in a room in nineteen years.

That the document does not say what happens to a man who is not in the room when it is read. And that she knows of one. He has six acres of the sally bed, and it is because of a number said at his own door on a Thursday night. She is not writing his name on the back of a piece of paper. Nobody asked her for it, and that wall can be corrected in about four seconds.

And that the document is not about the water. Put the ground on it. Not the person. The ground. There are about thirty-one acres of low meadow and a parcel of six acres that nobody is paying a rate on, and a ground can be walked by a man with a line, and it is what a remedy is done to.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

The fourth of those five lines was the one he could not get out from under.

It said that a man who is not in the room had given a season to a room, and that it was because of a number said at his own door on a Thursday night. It did not name him and it did not name the number. It said the shape of it, in five lines, on the back of a piece of paper under an oak bar.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. “*Because I have been in this hundred nineteen years, and a man of thirty-four in it put four lines on a wall about a room, and about nine people read that every morning, and I do not think a man of thirty-one knew that when he wrote them.*”

“*I knew it. The third line of that notice is mine and the fourth line is mine, and both of them say a number of men in a room, and about four of the six words in the fourth are about a man at a door. I did not put his name on it. I put the shape of it on a wall in the open, and this is the fifth thing I have done in this county with a number in it, and I have said out loud in two rooms today that I would rather be nobody’s instrument than the man who is in the room.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, because about four people heard the first half and about nine heard the second half, and they are not the same half.*”

“*The second half is that she is right, and I cannot say so in a way that costs me anything. A woman of thirty-eight has written four lines on the back of my paper that are truer about my own work than anything I have said in twenty days, and there is no form in the county of Kell that lets a person be thanked for that, so she is not thanked. I am going to say one more thing about it and then stop.*”

“*Say it, and then stop, and mean it, because about nine people in this hundred have been listening to me for eleven days and about nine of them have been waiting for me to run out of things to say.*”

“*I am not going to write anything else on anything in this county. That is the one. It is a decision, and I have not made it in a room with about thirty men in it, and about four of you will believe it and about nine will not, and the nine are entitled to think I have said it because four of you were standing here.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

Then a man of fifty-four came up the bar at about the second hour. He had about seven hundred sheep four miles south. He had been in a weigh-house with about thirty men in it on the rising and had stood at the back of it with no seat. He did not take the sheet.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*A man of fifty-four is standing at the bottom of my bar with his hands behind him, and I would like about nine of us to hear why he has not taken that paper off it and written on the back of it in his own hand, which is the only hand in this hundred entitled to do it.*”

“*A bar with a sheet on it. I have about eleven acres of my own grass in a ditch. I have paid the rate on every one of the eleven parcels and the penny on every one of them, and I have not paid anything at all this week, and I have not been asked for it and I have not offered.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say why you have not offered, because about nine people in this hundred have been saying since the rising that you would put three boards in a gate out of your own purse, and about nine of us have believed it, and I am one of the nine and I would like to know what you have been doing instead.*”

“*I have been mowing the long grazing. There are forty-four acres of it and I am in season on the whole of it and it has been six years. A hay that stands past this week on ground that is mine is a hay I have wasted.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and do not say that a man of thirty-one is the reason.*”

“*I am going to say the reason, because about four of you are standing here. A man of thirty-one came into this town on a Wednesday and said a number at my gate and at nine other gates, and a man of thirty-three gave a season to a room on the Monday because of what he was told at his own door. That is true. I have been asked nothing since the rising. Nobody has asked me whether I meant the boards, and about nine people in this hundred believe I meant the boards, and I have not said one word about the boards in eight days.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

Then the boy of fourteen, who had been standing at the bar since the eighth hour with a rag in his hand, because he is the person who rubs that wall out when somebody wants it rubbed out, said the only thing he said all morning.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because you have been standing there for four hours with a rag and about nine of us have forgotten about the rag, and a sheet with a back on it is the first thing anybody in this hundred has asked a man of your age to think about.*”

“*I am standing in front of a back. A front can be rubbed out in about four seconds. I have known that since a Sunday morning in the last week, when I wrote nine lines in chalk and any person in this town could take a rag to them. A back cannot be rubbed out, because you do not know which side of the paper the words are on.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and say what you are going to do about it, and you have been asked to write a thing five times in your life and you have said you did not know what to do with it four times.*”

“*I am going to do nothing about it. That is the first time I have said that to anybody and I have known it since the eighth hour. A hundred years from now somebody will find five lines on the back of that paper and will not know which of us wrote them. That is a different thing from a wall. I do not know what to call it, and I have a rag.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

Then the man who wrote it argued for it, in the open, in about nine people’s hearing, and he lost it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. “*Because I have a holding and a seat and a skep behind my gate. I have read your four sentences twice and I have read that woman’s five lines once. I would like to know why a man who cannot be sent for is standing at a bar defending three hundred words he wrote himself in a room over a stable.*”

“*I am standing in front of it because the four sentences in it are true. About nine people repeated them at a market and about two hundred and ninety-one could not be asked about them. I have not worked out one thing about the five lines, and I am not going to let a document of mine be corrected in the open by a person I have known for a day without saying that I do not know it is right.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, because about four people in this hundred have noticed that you are the author and about nine have not, and the difference between those two nines is going to decide what happens to that sheet.*”

“*Then here is the rest of it. I wrote those three hundred words standing up in a room over a stable, on a cracked steel pen, and the light in that room belonged to somebody else. I have put a hand on four walls in this county in ten days and two of them were wrong. I am the only person in this county nobody can be sent for, and I am the worst man in this county to be arguing about a document, and I am doing it now, and I would like somebody to say so before I have said the rest of it.*”

About four people said so at once and in about four different ways, and one of them was the woman who had written the five lines.

“*Say the rest of that. Then stop talking and let the four of them say it twice. About nine people in this hundred are going to repeat the next thing you say, and about two hundred and ninety-one are not going to repeat anything at all.*”

He stopped talking.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

---

Then the letter came up the drain, on a flat boat with about nine of the camp’s things on it. A boy of nineteen poled it. He did not come up to the bar and he said nothing about it to anybody.

A man of fifty-four and a woman of about thirty and about nine people on that green saw a boat come into a drain that does not usually take boats. About four of them worked out, in about four seconds, that it was a carrier’s.

It was four lines. It had nothing on the outside of it.

> *I am not coming, and it is the same not coming and it is not a smaller one than the last one.*
> *You have put a number on a wall in a town where anybody may read it. I have a shelf eleven inches wide eleven inches from where I sit and nobody may read it. You have told me about the wall twice in three weeks and not once about the shelf.*
> *Do not write to me about a settlement. I will read about it in whatever is written down afterwards, which is the only kind of reading there has ever been for me.*
> *— S. Q.*

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane, at about the eleventh hour, to a man of thirty-one who had a piece of paper in his hand and had not put it away. “*Because about nine people on this green saw a boat come into a drain, and about four of them have worked out that it was not fishing. One of us has a piece of paper in his hand and has not read it. I have a seat, and about nine years of wanting to ask a question, and I am going to ask this one.*”

“*A piece of paper in a stranger’s hand. It is four lines. It is not for anybody here, and I am not going to read it out loud at a bar where about nine people can be checked against each other.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and say whether it is about the settlement, because about nine people in this hundred have been told there is a settlement coming and about nine have not.*”

“*It is a refusal of the same kind as the first one. It is not a smaller one. It is not an invitation. I am not going to say whose it is or how far off it came, and I have told nobody in this county anything about it in twenty days, and I am not starting at a bar.*”

“*Then say the rest of that. You have given me the shape of an answer and not the answer. That is the third time in eleven days, and about nine people in this hundred have watched you do it three times.*”

“*I have a hand on four walls in this county and I have put a number in four mouths in it. That is what my hand is for and I have never pretended it was anything else. Today I defended my own paper at a bar, and about four people told me I was the wrong man to be doing it, and they were right.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-one weeks and four days old. A remedy of about three hundred words with nothing at the top of it had lain under an oak bar for five days. About nine people had read it, and about four of them had said out loud that they would not be the first to write on the back of it.

The first person to write on the back of it was a woman of thirty-eight with four acres, and she wrote five lines. The fifth one said that the document is not about the water and that the ground should be put on it, and a ground can be walked by a man with a line.

Then the man who wrote it stood up in the open and argued for it in about nine people’s hearing. About four of them told him in about four seconds that he was the worst man in that county to be arguing about a document, because he wrote it and cannot be sent for. He stopped talking.

And a letter of four lines came up a drain on a flat boat and was not read out to anybody. About nine people on that green saw the boat, and about four of them worked out in about four seconds what it was.
