# Chapter 581: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Fifth Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which About Nine Mouths In That Town Have A Decision That Was Made In A Yard And About Nine Of Those Nine Have It Another Way, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Goes To A Pump To Find Out What Was Said And A Man With A Barrow Tells Him The Money Is On The Bar And A Man With A Holding Tells Four People On A Corner That A Stranger Has Paid A Rate, And On Which He Holds The Low End Of A Nine-Foot Plank In A Ditch For About Five Hours And Takes Nothing For It And Writes Nothing Down About It

Second day of the hundred and fifth week. His three hundred and thirty-first morning. One hundred and eighty-one days after the settlement. The fever forty-two weeks and four days. One hundred and thirty-five days since the division. Eighty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

It did not rain on the Wednesday and the mud of the Tuesday had gone nowhere. The lane up to the sally bed was ankle-deep at the second hedge and about nine ruts stood full from a cart that had gone up it in the night. Nothing went up it in the day.

By the fourth hour about nine mouths in that town had the decision.

About nine of those nine had it another way.

Nobody in that town thanked anybody for a decision that had been made in a yard in the mud by a man who holds nothing.

---

Then a man of thirty-one went out to find out which nine, and that was his mistake, and he has said so since.

A decision said in a yard goes to about nine mouths by the fourth hour and to about nine more by the ninth, and the shape it arrives in is the shape of whoever carried it. There is no form in the county of Kell that lets him ask anybody what shape it arrived in, because asking a man what he heard you say at a pump is asking him what he remembers, and there are nine lines on a board outside the water-clerk’s door that say nobody in this hundred is to be asked that.

So he went and did it anyway, at a pump, with his bad leg against the trough.

He had been in that county twenty-seven days. He came off a boat on the evening of the Wednesday twenty-eight days before, and the count is of days and not of the arrival day itself. He had under two pounds in a purse. He had put a hand on four walls in that county and two of them were wrong. He had a sentence out in about nine mouths that he could not take back, and on the Tuesday he had gone up a lane in the mud and told the man whose ground it is that he would not put one hundred and fifty pence on a bar.

The pump is at the top of that green and about nine people use it in the morning.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said a man of about thirty-four with a stroke beside his name in chalk on the end wall of a smithy. He had a barrow and a wet sleeve and he had not washed yet. “*Because you have come up to that trough on a leg and asked me what I heard you say yesterday.*”

“*I am standing in front of a trough. I said out loud in a yard that I would not put money on a bar for the season on six acres. I would like to know what you heard.*”

“*That the money is on the bar.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that, and say where you got it, because there is no money on that bar and I have not been near it.*”

“*A man of about thirty with sixteen acres put money on that bar on Friday and you were standing nine feet off it saying you would not. Yesterday you went up his brother’s lane and said it to him. About nine of us have it that you changed your mind on Saturday and went up there to say so to his face.*”

Nobody at that pump thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of it and do not be sorry for me, because I would rather hear it at a trough at the fourth hour than have it carried up about nine streets by somebody with a tray.*”

“*That is what I heard. I have worked out nothing about it. I am a man with a barrow.*”

“*Then say the one thing you have worked out.*”

“*That a man with a barrow hears a decision and not a decision, because a barrow is about going somewhere with something in it and a decision is not going anywhere.*”

---

The second place was worse, and it was not worse because it was wrong. It was worse because it was true with the middle taken out of it.

A man of about fifty with a holding and a seat in the hundred had come down off the top side to the smithy for a hammer, and had said in front of about four people that the six acres of the sally bed were taken after all and that a stranger had paid the rate on them in a yard on Tuesday.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. He had come out of his own door with no slate under his arm and had been standing at the corner since the third hour. “*Because about four of us have just heard that a stranger paid a rate yesterday, and there is no rate on that sheet with a stranger’s name against it, and about nine of us have been at that wall every morning since Tuesday.*”

“*I did not say who paid it.*”

“*You did not say who did not pay it either, and in about four seconds there were about four people on this corner who had a man in a yard holding six acres of his brother’s ground, and there is no column anywhere on that sheet that a thing like that can be checked against.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of it in about nine sentences,*” said the man with the hammer, “*because I am a man with a holding and about nine people are about to repeat what I said, and I have not got nine sentences ready.*”

“*Say this instead and say it once. Nobody has paid anything on that line. There is a figure in the square or there is nothing in it, and there is nothing in it, and it has been nothing in it since Monday before the rising. Ask him. He is standing there and he has been in this county twenty-seven days and he cannot be sent for by anybody, which is the only reason anybody is talking to him at all.*”

About four people in that street looked at a man of thirty-one. About nine looked at the ground.

Then Ilyan said the true of it out loud, once, to about four people, and it was the fifth time in twenty-eight days that the true of a thing had gone out of his mouth before the man it belonged to had got to it.

That is the habit. He makes himself the carrier. And about nine people heard the correction and about nine did not, and the two nines did not ask each other anything, and the figure on that corner had by then gone up about nine streets in the shape it had left his mouth at a trough.

---

He went down to the north wall of the weigh-house at about the fifth hour because it was there and he had to walk past it.

Nobody was at it. The lamp on the bracket is lit at the seventh hour and about nine people are at that wall between the seventh and the ninth, and at the fifth hour there was a boy of fourteen with a rag in his hand doing nothing at all, because there is nothing on that wall to rub out.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Toby Henshaw. “*Because you have come and stood here and looked at that square for about nine seconds without saying anything, and I stand here every morning with this, and the last three mornings I have had nothing to do.*”

“*A square with nothing in it. You told a man on Sunday that nothing cannot be rubbed out.*”

“*Nothing cannot be rubbed out. And on Tuesday a man of about fifty stood where you are standing and said out loud that a stranger had paid the rate on those six acres, and about nine of us heard it, and I have had a rag in my hand all Wednesday waiting to see whether anybody would put something there and nobody has.*”

“*And what will you do if something does go there.*”

“*Nothing. A front can be rubbed out in about four seconds. That was the whole of what I had to say about it on Sunday.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody.

---

Then he went up the lane in the mud and did the one thing that nobody’s can do.

Garr Ryley was in his yard with a barrow. He had taken out a day of sallow on the Tuesday and about eight days of it were left in the ditch. A plank lay across the ditch-mouth on the low side, where the water had taken the ground out from under it, and it was about nine feet long and it had been lying there since the Friday.

There is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may not hold the other end of a plank. There is no form anywhere that says anybody will thank him for it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Garr Ryley, from the barrow handles, without turning round. “*Because you have come up this lane a second day running, and yesterday you told me a thing I did not ask for, and today you have come up it again, and about nine people saw you come up it.*”

“*I am standing in front of a plank. I am nobody’s until the year turns. There is nobody in this hundred who can be sent for to hold the other end of a plank, and I can be at this one in about an hour.*”

“*Then say the rest of that before you pick it up, because a man who takes hold of a thing in my yard is a thing I have to have an opinion about.*”

“*I will not pay the rate and I will not say a thing about a man who holds a season. I will hold that plank and I will not take anything for it and I will not write it down anywhere, because a stranger’s hand on a fifth thing in this county is a fifth hand, and I said in this yard on Tuesday that I would put no more of them on.*”

Nobody in that yard thanked anybody.

Garr Ryley turned round with the barrow handles in his two hands and looked at him for about four seconds, the way a man looks at wet rope.

“*Then hold it and do not talk, and understand what holding it is. About nine men have cut that ditch with a barrow and about four of them have held that plank, and not one of them has ever been paid, and there is no column anywhere for that. Nobody’s is not a thing you get. It is a thing you can be told to go home from at any hour of any day by any man with a gate, and you cannot be sent back because nobody has you to send back.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*There is no rest of that. Hold the plank.*”

---

He held the low end of it from about the sixth hour until the light went off the water. The mud was cold through the wood and his bad leg went at about the seventh hour and stayed gone. Sallow came out of the ditch in black wet lengths about as long as a man’s arm and went up on the bank on the top side where the grass was, and about nine loads went up that bank.

Twice in the afternoon Garr Ryley told him to stop talking and once told him to go home, and both times he stopped talking and neither time he went home, and neither of those was a decision. It was a man with a barrow and a man on the end of a plank, and there was no form in the county of Kell that says which of the two is which.

Nobody in that yard thanked anybody and nobody in that yard was sorry for anybody.

---

By the ninth hour about nine people in that town had it, and about nine of those nine had it a different way from the first nine, and there is no form anywhere that lets anybody say which of the two nines is the true one.

The fever was forty-two weeks and four days old. A man of thirty-one who had said in a yard in the mud that he would not put money on a bar had a man of thirty-four at a pump saying the money was on it, and a man of about fifty on a street corner saying a stranger had paid the rate on six acres on Tuesday, and neither of them had lied, and about nine people heard him put the true of it and about nine did not.

He held the low end of a plank in a ditch for about five hours and took nothing for it and wrote nothing down about it.

And the square at the right-hand end of that line was still empty, and about nine people read it every morning, and nothing can be taken off a wall with a rag because there is nothing on it to take the rag to.
