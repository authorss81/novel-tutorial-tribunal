# Chapter 567: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Third Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Year Goes And Nothing Is Settled, And The First Act Of The New Year Is Done By A Man Of Fifty-Eight At About The Seventh Hour To A Camp Of Fifty-Eight People On The Low Side And It Is Done To A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Rather Than By Her, And A Woman With A Daughter Of Six Walks Four Miles Into A Town Of About Eleven Hundred In Standing Water And Asks A Clerk To Write A Number She Does Not Want Written And Is Told There Is No Column For It And Says So Before She Is Asked, And The Worst Off Person In That Day Is Not The Worst Off Person In The Week

Second day of the hundred and third week. His three hundred and seventeenth morning. One hundred and sixty-seven days after the settlement. The fever forty weeks and four days. One hundred and twenty-one days since the division. Sixty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The first act of the new year is not a decision. Nobody votes on it and there is no minute of it.

It is a man with a slate walking down a plank bridge at about the seventh hour, with nine inches of water on the flats and a piece of chalk in his other hand, and what he does is put a figure on a wall.

That is how it starts every year, and in about two hundred and thirty years nobody has ever stopped it.

On this Wednesday there were four things on that slate. A hundred of about thirty men had sat in a weigh-house for about nine minutes on Tuesday and had gone out into about three hundred people, and everything said in that room was on the slate.

---

The four things were: the eleven, read off; the three boards and the three boards in a yard at Coldcombe; the water on the low common on Thursday; and the two hundred and twenty feet.

The fifth thing was not on the slate and he added it himself at about the seventh hour, after a woman had stood in front of the wall for eleven minutes.

---

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, at about the seventh hour, to a woman who had come four miles over a plank bridge in nine inches of water with a daughter of six on her hip and mud to the knee on both of them.

“*I am standing in front of a figure in a square that says two hundred and twenty feet, and I have read it four times, and I understood it in about four seconds, and I have come four miles to tell you that I do not want it written.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that. I wrote it yesterday in front of thirty men and you were not in the room. About nine people in this hundred have told me in four days that a number on that wall is the only thing anybody can be checked against, and I am one of the nine.*”

“*I know you are. And I am not here to stop you or to take it off. I am here because you have written a number in a square, and the number in the square is the number of the holding, and you have written the number of a piece of ground in it.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say it slowly, because I have been up since the fifth hour and I have been wet since the seventh and I am not going to miss one word of the rest of it.*”

“*The rest of that is that I measured that rise with my own feet in April against two gate-posts I know the distance of. Eleven tents at about twenty feet of frontage each is two hundred and twenty feet, and there are about nine hundred yards of the top of that rise, and that figure is true, and I have said it in front of thirty men and I would say it again.*”

“*And it is not about us.*”

“*It is not about you. That is what I came to say. You have written a true number about a width of ground in the place where a hundred puts the number of the people who hold it. About nine people in this hundred read that sheet every morning, and about nine of those will be right about what it says and wrong about what it means.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said nine and nine twice today and I want the rest of it out of you before I say the thing I have come to say.*”

“*The rest of that is that eleven households put fifty-eight people on that rise in eleven tents four days after a fire, and a number went on a sheet of paper in a sack under a churn, and there is no copy of it in this county and I have never seen it. And on Tuesday a man wrote a different number on a wall in this town, in the wrong place, and it is still the only number about that common in the county that anybody can walk up and read.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody and nobody at that wall was sorry for anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you came four miles in nine inches of water and you have not asked me for anything, and I would like to know what you came for.*”

“*I came to tell you that you have written the only number about my common that exists on a public wall, and that I want it taken off, and that I want another number put there instead, and I want it to be fifty-eight.*”

About four seconds went past and about nine people reading the wall in the morning went on reading it.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said a thing that is not possible in about four sentences and I have answered a hundred and thirty times this week and I would like to be wrong about this one.*”

“*I am not asking you to write fifty-eight. There is no column on that sheet with a person’s name at the top of it and no column anywhere that says how many people. You told a man last week that the name of the holder goes in the column that is the ground because there is nowhere else to put it. So fifty-eight cannot go in the square. You would have to rule a new column, and no form in this county lets anybody rule a column.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you came four miles standing in the mud, and I am not sure whether you came to ask me for a thing or to tell me that I was right.*”

“*Both. I came to tell you that you were right, because on Friday I told a stranger that a wall was worse than a sack under a churn, and I have had five days to find out whether it was true, and it is not.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you said a sack was worse than a wall and a wall is better and both of those are on your side.*”

“*A sack is worse because there is nothing in it anybody else can see. And a wall is worse because about nine people read it every morning. A number of feet has been on that wall one day and it has been read by about nine people and repeated by about nine people, and a woman of about seventy asked me at the pump last night what feet a common has.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because she is seventy and she asked you at a pump and you have not told me what you said to her.*”

“*I said a common has about nine hundred yards of edge and about eight hundred acres behind it, and she said that is not what she asked, and I said what did you ask, and she said she asked how many of us there are.*”

---

Then she asked for it, and it took about nine sentences, and about thirty people heard it. The wall has about nine people at it by seven and about thirty at eight.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*I am standing in front of a wall and I am asking for a fifth thing on it and I know what I said four minutes ago.*”

“*Say what the fifth thing is.*”

“*On the line for the low common there are now two numbers and neither of them is a number of people. I want a third one that is a number of people and I do not want it in the square, because the square is the number of the holding and there is no holding. I want it in the taking column, under the ground, where it says what goes on it.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say why the taking column, because about nine things I have been asked in four days have been a stranger and about four have been a woman standing in the mud.*”

“*Because the taking column is a column of use. It says what goes on a thing. And what goes on the low common is fifty-eight people, and there is no other column for it, and nobody has ever put a person in that column because a person is not a use, and I am telling you that a person is a use.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and then say what you want written, and I will write it if it can be written and I will tell you if it cannot, and there is no form that lets me refuse you.*”

“*Fifty-eight. On the line for the low common, in the taking column, under the ground, with the day on it. And no name on it, and no name on anything, because I have a daughter of six and she is not going to be on a wall in this town before she can read it.*”

He looked at the wall for about four seconds and then he wrote it, and he wrote the day on it, and he did not rule anything and did not head anything.

fifty-eight

Then he put the chalk down and turned round and there were about thirty people at the wall and about nine of them were saying it out loud.

“*Say the rest of that, because about nine people are saying your number out loud in the open and you have not asked them to and there is nobody in this county they can be sent to about.*”

“*That is the part I have thought about since Friday, in a tent, in the wet. That number is going to be in about nine mouths in this hundred by tomorrow and about thirty more by the end of the week. There is no form anywhere that says a man may not repeat it. It will be a number about eleven tents and about fifty-eight people and about nine children, and there is no column on this sheet for a child either.*”

“*Say the rest of that. You have come four miles and asked for a thing and got it, and now you are telling me it is a mistake, and I would like the whole of it.*”

“*It is not a mistake. It is that a number on that wall cannot be asked about. It can only be repeated. And in a day, when there is a foot of water on that common, about nine people will be able to say the true of it in front of about thirty people, and I will be one of the nine. I will have got it from a number that a man wrote on a wall and not from anything I have seen with my own eyes.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just told me that a thing that can be checked is better than a thing that cannot, and that a wall is not a thing that can be checked.*”

“*A wall can be read by anybody. That is not the same. A man of fifty-four can be asked whether he paid the rate and he will answer. A number cannot be asked. I have a day and a foot of water coming and about nine people are already saying my number out loud, and by Thursday I will not be able to tell you whether the nine are right.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody and nobody at that wall was sorry for anybody.

---

Ilyan had been standing about nine feet off since about the seventh hour and had not said anything, and at about the eighth hour he said the thing he had been holding since Friday, and it was the worst thing he did in five days.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Bett Searle, without turning round.

“*I am standing in front of a number you asked for and did not want, and I am going to tell you what happened on Friday, in the open, in four sentences. About nine people at this wall heard a man say it and none heard it from you.*”

“*Say what you are standing in, because you have been nine feet off since the seventh hour in nine inches of water and you have a bad leg, and about thirty people are at this wall and two of them are mine.*”

“*I am standing in front of what I did. On Friday, on a plank bridge, in the open air, in front of about nine people, you corrected my count of your camp from sixty to fifty-eight, and you named the two that were subtracted, and I wrote it on a sheet of paper and I did not ask you first.*”

Nobody at that wall said anything, and the number stayed where it was on the sheet.

“*Say the rest of that, because about thirty people are standing at a wall and about nine of them heard it on Friday and about nine of them did not, and you are about to say the part that is about you.*”

“*The rest of that is that on Friday I did not know that a number said out loud in the open by a man who cannot be sent for is a number about nine people will have by the eighth hour. I knew it and I said it anyway, because I had said numbers out loud for a hundred and sixty-seven days and nobody had come back at me about one.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because there is no rest of that and you are about thirty-one and I am twenty-eight and I have known you five days.*”

“*There is no rest of it and I have had five days of it. It is not the first time I have made a number out loud on purpose to be useful and had a person be the worse for it. It was in a market four hundred miles from here, to a woman who told me a number said out loud would be a thing her neighbours had by morning, and that was a good many hundreds of mornings ago.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said a thing about a woman four hundred miles off in a market and she is not in this county and I do not know her name.*”

“*Her name is on the outside of a sheet of paper about four feet wide on a wall about fifty miles off. It is her hand. She wrote to me in four lines that she is not coming, and I have not told one person in this county her name and I am not going to.*”

About nine people at that wall looked at him and about four of them looked at the number.

“*Then that is the worst off person in this county today and it is not me.*”

“*Say what you mean by that, because you said it out loud in front of about thirty people and about nine of them have a number of my people in their mouths already.*”

“*The worst off person in this day is not you. It is a woman of forty-one who lost a season in a market twelve days ago and told the man who took it that he was doing it properly. It is a man of thirty-three, who gave up six acres yesterday to a room and is not a man of the hundred. It is a man of fifty-four, who took a drain out at his own cost and lost a season of keep. You have lost nothing today except the ability to correct the nine people saying your number, and you spent four days walking.*”

Nobody at that wall thanked anybody and nobody at that wall was sorry for anybody.

Then she said the last thing of the morning and about nine people heard it and about four understood it.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have been in five days and you have said my number out loud twice and told me about a woman four hundred miles off in front of thirty people, and about nine people here have heard you do it and about nine have not.*”

“*Say it again, because I am wet to the knee and I have a child on my hip.*”

“*The number on that wall is now the truest thing anybody has written about that common and about nine people will repeat it by tomorrow and not one of them will know that a woman of twenty-eight walked four miles into standing water to try to take it off before it went up. And I have put it there twice, and the second time is the one that counts, and there is no column anywhere in this county for the difference.*”

---

The fever was forty weeks and four days old. The first act of the year was done at about the seventh hour by a man of fifty-eight walking down a plank bridge in about nine inches of water. It was done to a woman of twenty-eight rather than by her, and it was a number of people written in the column of use, on a wall that has no column for a person.

A woman with a daughter of six had walked four miles to ask for a number to be taken off, and had asked for it to be put there instead. She said in about four sentences that a sack under a churn is worse than a wall, because a sack has nothing anybody else can see, and a wall has about nine people who read it every morning.

A man of thirty-one stood nine feet off in about nine inches of water for an hour, and then said in front of about thirty people that he had said a number out loud in the open on Friday to be useful and had a person be the worse for it. He said it was not the first time, and that there is no column anywhere in this county for the difference.

And a woman of twenty-eight said out loud in front of about thirty people that in a day about nine of them will be able to say the true of that common and she will be one of the nine, and she will have got it off a number that a man wrote on a wall and not off anything she has seen with her own eyes.
