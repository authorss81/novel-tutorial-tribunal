# Chapter 562: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Second Week, And A Friday, And Which Is The Market Day, And On Which A Market Of About Three Hundred People Comes To A Green With Nothing On It And Goes Home Again, And A Man Of Fifty-Eight Who May Not Call Off A Market Says Out Loud At The Stone Step That He Has Done It And That There Is No Form In The County Of Kell That Lets Him Do It Or Stop Him Doing It, And A Man Of Thirty-One Counts Out Loud In Front Of About Nine People How Many Men Fill The Chairs In A Room On The Rising And Works Out That One Of Them Is A Man Four Miles South Who Has Never Been In The Building, And Puts That On A Wall In The Open Where About Nine People Read It Every Morning Of Their Lives, And Nobody In That Building Says His Name

Fourth day of the hundred and second week. His three hundred and twelfth morning. One hundred and sixty-two days after the settlement. The fever thirty-nine weeks and six days. One hundred and sixteen days since the division. Sixty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The rising is in four days and the clerk has been asked for that number seven times today and has given it seven times.

---

A market in Withermere is on a Friday. There is no other day and it has not moved in about two hundred years, and the standpipes and the barrows and the men who come in from the drove arrange themselves around that fact and could not tell you how they know it.

There is a stone step at the north end of the green with an oak bar across it, and between the hour of ten and the hour of twelve anybody may stand at the bottom of it and offer a parcel’s season for sale.

On this Friday there was nothing at the bottom of it, and about three hundred people came onto the green between the eighth hour and the tenth and stood about in the wet, and went home again by the eleventh.

There is hay in a Withermere market. There is no hay in this one.

---

He went down at about the ninth hour because there was a thing on the step and about nine people in front of it and nobody on it.

The water-clerk was standing behind the oak bar with the slate under his arm and no chalk in his hand.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because you have been in this county eight days and you are about to ask me why a market is not on, and I am going to say it out loud in the open air either way.*”

“*I am standing in front of a market that is not on.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because there has been a market on this green every Friday since before I came here and about three hundred people have just walked off it.*”

“*I am standing in front of the reason, and you are the reason, and you have not got your hands in your pockets, which means you did it on purpose.*”

Nobody at that step thanked anybody.

“*I called it off,*” Ashcombe said. “*At about the sixth hour this morning, and I put a notice on the board outside my own door at about the seventh, and it is still there, and it is eleven lines and it has nothing at the top of it, and you may go and read it if you want to.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you told me on Saturday that a notice on a wall in this town is worth exactly the number of people who read it and do something about it.*”

“*Which is why I did not put it on a wall first. I put it in three places before I put it on a wall, because a market in this town is not a wall. It is three hundred people and about forty carts and a boy with a stick.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*The rest of it is that I have no right to do it. There is no form in this hundred, in this county, or in this water that says the water-clerk may call off the market of Withermere, and there is no form anywhere that says he may not. There is no bench and no counter and no person I can be sent to about it. I have done it and I cannot be sent for and I have not been asked.*”

“*Then why.*”

Ashcombe looked down the green at about two hundred yards of wet nothing.

“*Because on Sunday the ground under this will not carry forty carts. The rising is in four days and the water off the cut is over the low common six days from tonight, and the green drains through the same ditch as the low meadow, and I would rather be the man who stood on an empty step this morning than the man who watched a boy of nine go down in it on Friday.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just done a kind thing in the open and there is going to be about thirty people here who heard you do it and about nine who will say you had no right.*”

“*There are about nine, and they are right, and I would rather they said it to my face on a Friday than on a Monday with nobody standing near.*”

He put the slate down on the bar.

“*And I have said that in the open air in front of about nine people, which is the first time in nineteen years I have done anything in this hundred that I had not been asked to do, and I did it at the sixth hour this morning and I have had nine hours to think about it and I would do it again.*”

Nobody at that step thanked anybody and nobody at that step was sorry for anybody.

---

Then he went into the weigh-house and did the thing he does, and this time he did it out loud, and about nine people were in there and every one of them reads that sheet every morning of their lives.

“*Say what you are standing in front of.*”

“*I am standing in front of the second column and I have been counting the figures in the squares, and I have got a number, and I am going to say it out loud, and if I have it wrong I would rather you told me than that I said it quietly to myself.*”

“*Then say the number.*”

“*There are about thirty holdings on that sheet with a season in the square this year. The hundred is about thirty men, one for every holding that has a season on it. And on eleven lines of the thirty the name in the first column is the same name, and it is a man of a farm four miles south.*”

Nobody in that building said anything, and the clock on the wall went round.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said that a man is in a room eleven times and there is one chair.*”

“*I have not said that he is. I have said his name is on eleven lines. There is no column anywhere on that sheet with a person’s name at the top of it and there never has been one, and the name goes in the column that is the ground because there is nowhere else to put it.*”

“*Then what.*”

“*Then nobody in this hundred can tell me whether a man who holds eleven seasons holds eleven chairs or one, and there is no form anywhere that says, and I have been in this county eight days and I have never once seen him in this building.*”

Ashcombe put the green book down on the wool table.

“*Say the rest of it in front of about nine people and not to me, because there are about nine of them and they can be checked against each other and I cannot.*”

“*The hundred meets at midday in this room on the rising. It has met on the rising for nineteen years. And there is a man who holds eleven of the seats and has not been in the room once in nineteen years, and if he does not come on Tuesday then there is no seat for him and no rule that says he has one, and if he does come then he fills eleven and the rest of you fill nineteen and nobody has ever written down which of those two things this room does.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything for about four seconds and one of the about nine people at the back said the word *Bray* out loud, and it went round the building before anybody had decided to pass it on.

“*Say your own name, because a thing that is on a wall with nothing at the top of it is a thing anybody in this town can read for two hundred and thirty years and I will not have that be a thing a stranger said.*”

“*I am the man who came down the water on Wednesday. I put eleven lines on that wall on Sunday with nothing at the top of them and a man came nine miles to correct one of them in the open, and my name is not on that notice either and I have not put it there.*”

“*Then you have just said, in my building, in front of about nine people who read this sheet every morning, that a man four miles south holds eleven of my seats and has never once been in the room, and you have said it in the open, and there is nobody in this town you can be sent to about.*”

“*There is no form anywhere that says he must come either. That is the second half of it, and the second half is the one that has cost me the night.*”

He put his hand flat on the wool table the way a man does in a county where the table is also a bench.

“*Then it is on the wall. Put it on the wall with the rest, and rule nothing and head nothing, because that is what you do and I would rather have your bad wall than your good memory.*”

---

He nailed it up at about the eleventh hour and it was four lines and a third of a man long.

The first line said that on eleven lines of thirty the figure in the square was the same figure and the name in the first column was the same name. The second said the hundred was about thirty men, one for every holding with a season on it. The third said the hundred had met on the rising for nineteen years. The fourth said that a man who holds eleven of the seats had not been in the building in nineteen years and that no form in this county said whether that was one seat or eleven.

There was no heading and there was no signature and there was nothing at the bottom of it.

Nobody rubbed it out. A man of about fifty came and stood in front of it at the twelfth hour with his hat in his hands and read all four lines twice and did not touch it, and he is a man who has a seat.

Then Ilyan went and found the man with three acres, because the man with three acres had told him at a door on Thursday night that he wanted the six boards in.

---

The man was in his own yard with a hurdle and about three acres of new sward on the strip behind him, sown in March, dead now, and he had been told about the four lines by about nine people before Ilyan got there.

“*Say what you are standing in,*” he said.

“*I am standing in your yard and I put four lines on a wall this morning that say a man who holds eleven seats has never been in the room where they are dealt with, and I came to tell you I put them there before you heard them from the green.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I want to know whether that is a kindness or a thing you did to a man who was not in the room.*”

“*It is neither. It is a true number about a room, and there are about nine people in this hundred who can be checked against it and about nine who cannot, and I would rather it were checked by somebody who was in the room.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*I put a number on a public wall on Sunday about a man I had met for four minutes, and it was wrong in one line, and a man came nine miles to say so, and the correction is under it. And this morning I put another number on the same wall about a man I have never met, in the open, in front of about nine people. That is twice now, and it is my hand both times, and it does not come off.*”

“*And what did you want.*”

“*I wanted to know how many men fill the chairs in a room that meets in six days, because I am going to stand in it, and because a man of fifty-eight told me on Tuesday that a decision about the level of a fen is not taken by the people who happen to be in a room.*”

“*Then you wanted a count.*”

“*I wanted a count and I got one and it was eleven and it was about a man and not about water, and I have been in this county eight days and I have not once got a number that was about water.*”

Nobody in that yard thanked anybody.

“*Then come inside and read them and do not say it to me, because I have been in the street since the seventh hour and I have heard it nine times and I would like to hear it off a wall.*”

They read the four lines in his own kitchen, standing up, and about four feet from the wall of a man who has a seat.

“*Say the rest of that,*” the man said, when they had read them twice, “*because you have been in this county eight days and you have just told a hundred people that a man four miles south owns a third of the room they sit in, and he is the man who paid their ditches.*”

“*He is. And he paid them and he lost two years of it doing it, and he has a drain of his own going in that is the only part nobody can defend, and he is going to take it out at his own cost before the rising.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said three things about the same man in one breath and about nine people in this hundred would call two of them a lie.*”

“*They are not a lie and they are not consistent, and I have been here eight days and I cannot make them consistent, and that is why I wrote four lines about a room instead of saying one sentence about a man.*”

The man of three acres read them a third time and then folded the paper back onto the nail.

“*Then the rising is in four days and he has never once been in that building in nineteen years, and there is no seat for a man who is not of the hundred.*”

“*That is what the four lines say.*”

“*And you are going to be in it.*”

“*A water-clerk of fifty-eight has asked me to stand in it. I said yes in about four seconds and I have not worked out what I am for.*”

Nobody in that kitchen thanked anybody.

The fever was thirty-nine weeks and six days old. A market of about three hundred people came to a green in a town of about eleven hundred and found nothing on it and went home again, and a man of fifty-eight who may not call off a market said out loud at a stone step that he had done it and that no form in the county of Kell lets him or stops him.

And a man of thirty-one counted out loud in front of about nine people how many men fill the chairs in a room that meets in four days, and found that one of them is a man four miles south whose name is on eleven lines of a wall because there is no column on that wall with a person’s name at the top of it.

And that man will hold eleven seasons in about four days, and there is no seat for him in the room where they are dealt with, and no rule that says there is, and nobody in that hundred can be sent for about any of it.
