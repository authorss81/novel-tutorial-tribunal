# Chapter 599: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Seventh Week, And A Sunday, And On Which There Is No Room And No Slate And No Bar And There Is One Fire In One Parlour In That Town, And On Which About Nine People Are In It At About The Eleventh Hour, And On Which A Woman Of Twenty-Eight Says Out Loud That The First Move Of A Settlement Cost Her Four Miles A Day For Five Days And A Licence That Had Lapsed Twenty-Eight Days Before, And On Which A Man Of Fifty-Four Says Out Loud That Forty-Four Acres Of Long Grazing Is In Because He Was Asked For It And Said Yes And Not Because Any Form In The County Of Kell Made It So, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says His Own Half Out Loud And Is Not The Carrier Of It, And On Which A Woman Of About Seventy Says Out Loud The Question A Man Of Thirty-Four From Another County Asked In A Chalk Town And That Nobody In Two Counties Has Answered

Sixth day of the hundred and seventh week. His three hundred and forty-ninth morning. One hundred and ninety-nine days after the settlement. The fever forty-five weeks and one day. One hundred and fifty-three days since the division. Ninety-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The day before was the fifth day of the hundred and seventh week. The day after that is the seventh and last day of that week.

---

There was no room on the Sunday and no slate and no bar, and there has never been a form in the county of Kell for any of the three on any day of the week.

There is a fire in one room in that town on a Sunday, and it is in the parlour of an inn with four beds. The woman who keeps them has a fire in it every day of the week, and about nine people have said in a fortnight that that is not the same thing.

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

---

About nine people were in it at about the eleventh hour. About four of them had come for the asking. About five had come because a settlement is in this county now and about nine people have been carrying it since Friday morning.

He had been in that county forty-five days. He had a year down, from the crossing of that block of limestone to the crossing after it, and he has counted from the water since Thursday morning without looking at it once.

He wanted to hear somebody say what it had cost, out loud, to somebody who was not him.

---

Then a woman of twenty-eight said what it cost, and it cost her four miles a day.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane, who had a holding and a seat in the hundred and a skep behind his own gate. “*Because about nine people are in this parlour and about eleven hundred in this town read that wall every morning, and about two hundred and seventy of them cannot afterwards be asked what they thought of what was read at a step on Friday.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say that last part,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*About a woman in this room has walked four miles in the flat on Monday, on Tuesday, on Wednesday, on Thursday and on Friday, and there is no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says anybody owes her for it.*”

“*I am standing in front of a fire and about nine people and about four miles a day for five days, and on Saturday there were eleven tents up on that rise that were not up on Friday, and I did not ask the hundred whether they wanted them.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for anybody,*” said the woman of about fifty who keeps four beds. “*Because about nine of us have watched a woman put eleven tents on a rise that belongs to nobody in about four seconds of deciding and then spend a week walking four miles to stand next to them, and nobody in that hundred has been asked whether she wanted to.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at the walking,*” said the woman of twenty-eight. “*It cost the licence. There has not been a licence since the water turned on the rising morning, and that is twenty-eight days.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*What I did on Monday was put eleven tents on about eight hundred acres of nobody’s common with no form, no column and no day on it, and I did it with a daughter of six on my hip,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

---

Then somebody else said what it saved, and it was a man who was not asked and had come in out of the cold.

He was a man of fifty-four and he had come four miles on his own legs and he had about seven hundred sheep four miles south.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. “*Because about nine people are in this parlour and a man of fifty-four is standing in the only way in with his coat open and he has not said a word and about four of us have worked out that he has come to say something and about nine have not.*”

“*I am standing in front of a fire in a house with four beds in it and I have not been in this room before. About nine of you have read about my hay at a step on Friday, and about two hundred and seventy of you heard a woman read it and cannot be asked about it.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not be sorry for me,*” said Aldis Crane. “*About nine of us in this hundred have had three boards in a yard for thirty days and a condition that could not be met and a settlement that says nothing about them. If a man of fifty-four has come four miles into a parlour, then about four of us would like to know which half of it this is.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say it,*” said the woman of fifty who keeps four beds, “*and say it into about nine people’s hearing and not into about three hundred.*”

“*Forty-four acres of long grazing four miles south in its sixth year with about nine acres of new sward in it,*” said Owain Bray. “*In season. Every penny of two shillings and a penny the acre on two hundred and ten acres paid, which is twenty-one pounds seventeen shillings and sixpence a year. Asked for, not given. I said on Wednesday that anybody may come and ask me and that I may say yes or no and that nobody is to blame me if I say no.*”

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*I said yes on Friday afternoon, on my own ground, to about four people and not to nine. And the settlement says in it that no person in the county of Kell is bound by one word of it, and that is a true thing about the settlement and it is not a thing about my hay.*”

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

“*Then say that last part and say it out loud,*” said Hoare Ryley.

“*My hay is in because I said it was,*” said Owain Bray. “*There is no form in this county that would have put it in if I had said no, and nobody in that hundred may now say I was made to say it.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody and nobody in that parlour was sorry for anybody.

---

Then a man of thirty-one said his own half out loud in about nine people’s hearing, and about four people had it and about nine did not, and he was not the carrier of it this time.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Hoare Ryley, who had come in with mud to the knee and had a barrow handle under his arm. “*Because about nine people are in this parlour and a man of thirty-one has put down a year, and about nine of us have it in a shape and about nine of us have it in another shape.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say that last part,*” said the woman of twenty-eight.

“*The two nines have not asked each other,*” said Hoare Ryley, “*and he has not said out loud in this room what he put it down for, and about four of us would like to know.*”

“*I put it down so that about nine people in a hundred can put a thumb on what I said at a step on Friday and say whether it was true on Saturday morning. That is the whole of it and it is not worth a year and I have not said that it is.*”

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not end it at the thumb,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*And I have not said it is a settlement either. It is a line about a person on a sheet that binds nobody and that anybody in that hundred may check at a block of limestone with a hand flat on it. About nine people have carried four pounds eleven shillings and threepence out of this room in the last three days and about four of those nine have got it wrong.*”

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody and nobody in that parlour was sorry for anybody.

---

Then a woman of about seventy asked the question that has been standing in that town since the ninth day from the rising, and she asked it out loud, and about nine people in that parlour heard it and nobody answered it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” she said, “*because there are about nine people in this room and about eleven hundred in this town and a settlement in six hands on a wall. There is a thing underneath all of it that nobody has said out loud since the last day of the hundredth week.*”

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Then say that last part,*” said Aldis Crane.

“*And I am going to say it and about nine of you are going to hear it and none of you is bound by it,*” said the woman of about seventy.

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody.

“*Say it and do not answer it,*” said Aldis Crane, “*because a man of thirty-four from another county asked it in a chalk town nine weeks and about nine hundred days ago and nobody answered him there either.*”

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

“*Whether a person of the record and not of the place can become a person of the place,*” said the woman of about seventy, “*without being taken into the shape of the thing the place tells about itself. That is the question. It has been asked three times in about nine weeks in two counties and it has not been answered once. I am seventy-odd years old and I have watched this town go from a wall that a man keeps to a settlement that nobody keeps, and neither one of them has answered it, and I do not think either of them can.*”

About four people in that room said something at once and about nine did not.

Nobody in that parlour said anything.

Nobody in that parlour thanked anybody and nobody in that parlour was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-five weeks and one day old. About nine people were in a parlour with a fire in it on the last full day of a week. A woman of twenty-eight said out loud that the first move of a settlement had cost her four miles a day for five days and a licence that had lapsed twenty-eight days before. A man of fifty-four said out loud that forty-four acres of long grazing four miles south in its sixth year is in because he was asked for it and said yes, and not because any form in the county of Kell made it so.

And a man of thirty-one said his own half out loud in about nine people’s hearing and was not the carrier of it, and about four people had it and about nine did not.

The question was asked once in that parlour and not answered. It is not answered in this county and it is not answered in the other one either. There is no form in the county of Kell that makes anybody answer, and there is no column on any sheet in it for a person.

And about nine people in that hundred have both of those two things. About nine people have neither, and the two nines have not asked each other, and nobody in that room is bound by a word of any of it.
