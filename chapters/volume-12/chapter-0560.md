# Chapter 560: The Second Day Of The Hundred And Second Week, And A Wednesday, And On Which A Man Of Fifty-Four With About Seven Hundred Sheep Comes Four Miles South Into A Weigh-House In A Town Of About Eleven Hundred People Because His Name Is On A Wall, And Is Right In Front Of About Thirty People About The Covenant And About The Two Years When Eleven Households Did Not Pay A Rate And The Ditches Were Not Cut, And Is Wrong About A Drain And Says So Himself Before Anybody Asks Him, And Is Also Right That Taking The Drain Out Will Put The Water Onto A Common Where Fifty-Eight People Are Living, And A Water-Clerk Of Fifty-Eight Says Out Loud That He Can Hold The Water Either Way And Not Both Ways, And A Man Of Thirty-One Is Given A Choice And Takes Four Doors, And The Rising Is In Six Days

Second day of the hundred and second week. His three hundred and tenth morning. One hundred and sixty days after the settlement. The fever thirty-nine weeks and four days. One hundred and fourteen days since the division. Sixty days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The rising is in six days and everybody in that town knew the number, and the clerk had been asked for it nine times that morning and had said it nine times, and it was a day of a river and not a day of a number.

---

He came in at about the ninth hour with two men and a dog and a ledger under his arm, and he was about fifty-four and he was not in a hurry.

Nobody stopped him at the door, because anybody may go into that building and read a wall.

About nine people were in there and by about the tenth hour there were about thirty, and some of the thirty left again before the middle of the day and two of them came back in the afternoon.

“*Mr Bray.*”

“*Mr Ashcombe.*”

“*Say what you are here for, and I will say what I have done, and then you may say what you want and I will not stop you, and about thirty people are in my building and I am not going to have a hand put over anybody’s mouth in front of them.*”

“*Your notice on the wall.*”

---

He was right in about nine sentences and he did it in the order of a man who has had four years to think about it and has never once been asked.

“*Two years ago the hay failed on the low meadow. It was the wettest hay-time since before I was born and the meadow went under in the second week and it stayed under. The rate on those eleven parcels is what cuts the ditch between the pasture and the meadow, and the rate is two shillings and a penny the acre.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have told me the rate and not the year it stopped, and I am going to ask for the year.*”

“*The two years before that, nobody on those eleven could pay it. They had nothing to put on their own ground. A rate on a season you cannot graze is a rate on a name.*”

“*Say the rest of it, and do not soften it, because there are about thirty people in here and four of them have come out of the four closes.*”

“*The ditches filled in. That is the whole of it and it is not a crime and it is not a conspiracy.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because a man who says the ditches filled in in a room that knows why is not telling anybody anything.*”

“*The low meadow went under. The water came across the pasture and put about nine acres of good grass out, and I had seven hundred sheep and nowhere to put a third of them in the wet. Every year I could hold them I held them, and every year the holding cost me more than the land was worth.*”

He put the ledger on the table and did not open it.

“*And then in April I went round with a purse. And I paid the rate on all eleven, and the penny on all eleven, and I did it in four days.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because four days is fast, and I want to know what a man can do in four days that nine men have not done in two years.*”

“*I did not go in at night. I did not lie to one of them. I have not had a conversation with a single one of the eleven about it since, because there is nothing to say to a person whose ditch you have paid for.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it, because that is nine sentences and it is the best case anybody has made in this room and I have been in this county six days.*”

“*The rest of it is that the rate is now mine on those eleven, and I will cut the ditch better this year than it has been cut in the two years I have been watching it, and I will mow the low meadow in the second week.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have told me what you will do with your own money and not what you will do with theirs.*”

“*I will take about seven hundred sheep on to about two hundred and ten acres of pasture in the wet, and the ground will carry it, because the water will be going north and not south. About nine people in this hundred will call that theft. There is not one form in this county that says so.*”

---

Then Ilyan said the thing about the drain, and he said it badly, and it went round the room in about four seconds.

“*Then you have a problem that is not the covenant, and it is the first week of March, and it is a drain.*”

Nobody moved.

“*Say the rest of that, because he has just made the best case anybody has made in this room and I am about to say a thing about a man I have met four minutes.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*The drain is the only part of it I cannot follow.*”

“*It is a mile of water that was not there in February. It is why four closes flood. It is why about eight hundred acres of common go under on the second day after the rising. And it is a rule about a parcel and not about a water.*”

Bray looked at him for about four seconds.

“*You are the stranger.*”

“*I am.*”

“*Then I am going to say the true of that in four sentences, and two of them are against me, and I have known since Friday.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I have never in five days heard a man say that.*”

“*I cut it because my own parcel was under nine inches of water in the first week of April, two years running. I cut it at the top of my own ground on my own season, and I put my own water back on my own ground.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have told me what you meant to do and not where it went.*”

“*I did not know where it went. I have been told where it went. It goes on the low meadow, and off the low meadow into four closes that are not mine, and onto a common that is nobody’s. I could not see it from where I was standing. That is the whole of what a man is.*”

He turned round to the room.

“*I will take it out. Not when it is convenient for me. Now, before the rising, and I will pay for it out of my own purse.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because a man who gives up a season in one breath has usually worked out what it buys him, and I want to know what it bought you.*”

“*The ground on the north of the cut will be under a foot of water for a fortnight. I will lose about seven hundred sheep’s worth of keep doing it. I am telling about thirty people in a weigh-house instead of nine. If there is a better way of doing that, I do not know it.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because a man has just given up a season in public and I have not heard the shape of it yet.*”

“*The rest of it is that if I take it out, then the water goes back where it was, and where it was is the low meadow, and the low meadow is the bottom of this district, and the low common is on the far side of the meadow. So the water goes back, and it still ends up at the camp, because the camp is downhill of the place it went back to.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you are telling about thirty people that the right thing floods a camp, and I have been in a tent with a sheet of paper in a sack and I know what the figure on it is.*”

“*There are fifty-eight people living on that common under a sheet of paper in a tent. The water will be on them in about eight days. There is no form anywhere that says the water may not be on them, because the common is nobody’s, and a nobody has never been able to be drowned.*”

Nobody in that building said anything for about four seconds and about nine people in it were standing very still.

A woman of about twenty-eight stood up at the back near the door with a daughter of six on her hip.

She had walked four miles from the low side to hear a man say that. She said one thing, and it was the only thing said about that common for the rest of the day, and there was a great deal said about everything else.

“*That is the first true thing anybody has said to me about that common since the day I came off the high ground.*”

---

Then the water-clerk put both hands on the wool table and said the other thing, and he had been holding it since the sixth hour of the morning.

“*There are three boards in at Withermere and the gate takes six, and the water is a foot below the tally line, and I can raise it or I can leave it, and there is nobody in this hundred who can do it except me and there is no form anywhere that says a man may not.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have not told the room what raising it does.*”

“*Six boards holds it off the seven holdings and off the cut on the low meadow. Six boards puts a foot of water on the four closes and on the low common in about eight days.*”

“*The four closes are not richer than the seven. The seven are not better men than the four. I have said that in this room once and I will say it again, because about four people in here believe the first half of it and about nine believe the second half, and none of them believe both.*”

He turned round.

“*Three boards keeps the common dry and the camp on it dry, and lets the four closes fill in over about four years, and the hay go. Four years is a long time when you are thirty-four and it is no time at all when you are sixty.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I want to hear the reason you have not given.*”

“*I have been a water-clerk in this hundred for nineteen years and I have held the level the way it has been held. This year the level is a thing I have to put my name against in front of the hundred and about three hundred people in a green in the same hour, and I am not doing it in this building on a Wednesday with about thirty people in it. A decision about the level of a fen is not taken by the people who happen to be in a room.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because that is the third time this week you have told me the true of a thing and I have not once been able to help with it.*”

“*I am going to say it out loud. Tonight. Eleven doors, one at a time, before anybody writes anything. You will take the four closes and I will take the other seven, and if we do not both go then neither of us goes.*”

“*I am going to say the number of the boards and the number of the closes and the number of the days, and I am not going to have anybody come out of that building able to say afterwards that they were told it after it was done.*”

He looked at the man with the bad leg.

“*Which brings me to the thing I have been not asking about since Thursday.*”

---

And then the thing came, and it was not in the room and did not have a name on it and about four people in that building saw it arrive.

**CLAIM: A rule that can be kept by one man while another man is ruined is not a rule the hundred has agreed to be governed by. It is a rule the hundred is governed by, and did not agree to, and could not be asked. The agreeing is the thing that has to be asked about, and it cannot be asked about on a wall.**

**EVIDENCE: A sheet of parchment two men long on the north wall of a weigh-house. It is ruled in three columns. The ground, with a parcel and a number of acres. The taking, with what goes on it and how many. The year, with a figure in a square at the end of every line. There is no column anywhere with a person’s name at the top of it.**

**It has been read in the open in this town every morning for about two hundred and thirty years. On eleven lines the figure in the square is the same figure. The name of the man who took them up is written in the column that is the ground, because there is nowhere else to write it, and that is not the fault of anybody.**

**A man of fifty-four paid two shillings and a penny the acre on two hundred and ten acres. That is twenty-one pounds and seventeen shillings and sixpence. Four pounds of it paid the water-clerk and the rest cut the ditches. The rate on those eleven was not paid for the two years before this and the ditches were not cut and the low meadow went under, and in April eleven households lost their season to one purse. He has broken nothing. He has done the covenant exactly.**

**BURDEN: In the four years before this, eleven families in this hundred lost a season without anybody taking it from them. The loss was water, and a rate. No person in this county can be sent for about either.**

**In six days the hundred meets in a weigh-house, about thirty men, and about three hundred people will be standing in a green in the same hour, and every one of them will hear a number of boards. If a stranger from another county walks into four of the eleven doors tonight and tells four families what is going to be done to their water, then he has done in four hours what a clerk did in about four seconds on a step in a market on a Friday. There is no keeper in this town to hold him to it afterwards. The sentence will be out of his mouth about four seconds after the door opens.**

**He cannot be paid by anybody in this hundred and he cannot be sent for by anybody in this hundred. Both of those are the only two things about him that are worth anything here.**

**CHOICE: Take four of the eleven doors tonight, one at a time, and leave the clerk the other seven. Say out loud to each of the four what the water is going to do and what is going to be said in the room on the rising, and be answerable for having said it, knowing that nothing you say can be withdrawn. Or leave all eleven to the man whose name is on a board outside his own door, who has not been asked a question about a person in nineteen years, and who will go into those doors as the water-clerk and not as a stranger.**

He chose the four rooms, out loud, in about nine seconds, in front of about thirty people, and nobody thanked him for it, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may be thanked for anything.

The panel went when he chose it. It does not stay, and there is no form in this county that says it does.

---

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

A man of fifty-four with about seven hundred sheep agreed in front of about thirty people to take out a drain that he had cut in the first week of March, and lost a season of keep doing it.

He said out loud that the water would go onto about eight hundred acres of common where fifty-eight people were living. A woman of twenty-eight with a daughter of six on her hip said that it was the first true thing anybody had said to her about that common. A water-clerk of fifty-eight who is paid four pounds a year said in front of a room that he could hold the water either way and not both ways, and that a decision about the level of a fen is not taken by the people who happen to be in a room.

And a man of thirty-one with a leg that had stopped working on a bank the day before was given a choice about four doors, and took the four doors in about nine seconds.

He knew that he could not be sent for and could not be paid, and that those were the only two things about him worth anything in a county where a woman had been told the whole of the true of her life by a man who could not lie because of a board.

And the rising is in six days. And the covenant is not broken. And the leaf of a struck household in a chalk town about fifty miles off is still not struck back, and the right of refusal is still unrestored.

A man of eighty-one asked out loud in a room whether a person of the record and not of the place can become a person of the place. A water-clerk of fifty-eight said it was a question for the hundred, and would be asked in six days in a weigh-house, in front of about thirty men and about three hundred people in a green. It was not answered today.

And the man who was right about the covenant was also right about the drain.

He said out loud, in that room, that if he did the right thing with the drain then the water would go onto a camp of fifty-eight people on a common that belongs to nobody.

Nobody in that room worked out which of those two things he was wrong about. He did not ask. It was not settled, and the water is a foot up, and the rising is in six days.
