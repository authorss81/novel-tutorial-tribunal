# Chapter 552: The First Day Of The Hundred And First Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Walks Twenty-One Miles From A Chalk Town To A Wharf And Is Put On A Plank And Counted Out Loud Nine Times By A Boy Of About Nineteen, And A Carrier Of Forty-Four Names His Fare After Three Thousandweight Of Stone Is Down Into The Boat, And Three Shillings And Sixpence Is Said Out Loud Twice, And A Man Of Thirty-One Offers To Work His Passage And Is Refused In About Four Seconds By A Reason About Answering For, And A Boat Is Away From A Wharf At About The Ninth Hour With Three Thousandweight Of Stone On Her Deck

First day of the hundred and first week. His three hundred and second morning. One hundred and fifty-two days after the settlement. The fever thirty-eight weeks and three days. One hundred and six days since the division. Fifty-two days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

That last sentence is a fact and it is worth saying plainly, because the man carrying it has been walking since the sixth hour and has been in two counties.

He said it to nobody. He has been keeping it since a Saturday in a square in a market town two days from a chalk town, and it was never his county’s count, and up to yesterday it was a town two days from where he was standing, and from today the town it was begun in is four hundred miles off and behind him and he is going further.

Nobody had told him that was allowed. There is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may keep a number.

---

The chalk stops about four miles below Bramblefold, without a line you could put a boot on, and after that it is a clay country and then a gravel country and then a flat one, and by the time the river came up out of the trees on his left it was running brown and slow and about a foot and a half below the top of its bank.

Twenty-one miles. He had done it before, once, in the other direction, in a cart.

The leg was worse at the fifteenth mile than it was at the start and he did not say so and he did not stop, because stopping on a road in a country where he knew nobody was a thing he had decided against in a chalk town about nine weeks before and had not revisited.

He bought bread and a piece of cheese at a bakehouse at about the eleventh hour for threepence, and the woman who sold it wrapped it in a paper with somebody else’s name on it.

---

The wharf at Coldcombe is not in Coldcombe. It is a mile and a half below it, where the river turns, and it is about ninety feet of stone facing with a crane on it that was painted green about eleven years ago.

There were four boats on the water and one of them was being loaded with a block of stone.

That was the first thing in a hundred and fifty-two days that he had not seen in a chalk town.

The stone came off a wagon on a plank that two men walked, and it was about three thousandweight, and it was for a sluice at a town four days down the water, and a man of about nineteen was on the plank with a bar and a man of about fifty was on the wagon.

Ilyan stood on the stones for about nine minutes and watched, and then he did the thing he does.

He counted.

---

The man of about nineteen saw him do it on the third walk of the plank and stopped with the bar across it.

“*Say what you are doing.*”

“*Counting the loads.*”

“*Say why.*”

“*Because you have walked that plank with a three-thousandweight stone on it and I have watched you do it nine times, and if you have to do it eleven more then somebody should know now and not at the bottom of the boat.*”

The boy of about nineteen looked at him for about four seconds, and the man of fifty on the wagon did not turn round.

“*You are not from up the road,*” he said.

“*I am from Bramblefold.*”

“*Then you are from chalk, and I am sorry for you, and you are wrong.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I have been told I am wrong twice this week by two people who were under nine seconds about it.*”

The boy put the bar down on the plank and stood on it.

“*It is not nine times. It is going to be about forty and I know that and he knows that and neither of us has counted since we were fifteen. The number is not the thing. The thing is that he cannot see the near end of the plank from the wagon and I cannot see the far end of the wagon from here, and so one of us says out loud and the other one answers out loud, and the number is only so that the other one knows it was heard.*”

“*Say the rest of it.*”

“*You have counted nine and you have not asked whether anybody wanted to know, and that is the whole of what is wrong with you and it is not a small thing. On my father’s boat in the north a man counted the loads once to see if he could be useful and he was useful for a year and then he counted a different thing and we did not sail for six weeks.*”

Nobody on that wharf thanked anybody and nobody on that wharf was sorry for anybody.

---

The carrier was standing on the second boat with a coil of rope he did not need and he had been watching the whole of it.

“*Ashcombe,*” he said. “*No. Orwin Slee, and that is my boat and that is my boy, and if you want to be useful go and hold the other end of that plank and do not count anything.*”

Ilyan went and held the other end of the plank.

He was on it for about three hours and a half, and his leg went at the second hour and stayed gone, and at about the eighth hour in the evening the stone was down into the boat on a bed of sallow and the plank came off and he could not get up off the stones without putting a hand on a bollard, and nobody said anything about that either.


---

The man of fifty came down off the wagon and put a pint of water on the plank and drank it standing up, and he had been on that wagon two days and had come from nine miles off with a team of four.

He looked at Ilyan’s leg and did not ask about it.

“*That stone is for Withermere,*” he said. “*And I will tell you the whole of it because you will be sitting on top of it and there is no point in a man hearing a thing on a wagon that he finds out at a landing.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*It is for the sluice at Withermere. Six boards, and three of them are in, and the other three are lying in a yard because the clerk has asked for them twice and been told there is no money for them.*”

“*Who is the clerk.*”

“*Water-clerk. The man who can be sent for about the water, and about him, and about the season on two hundred and ten acres of the old pasture, and about everything else anybody in that hundred wants to know.*”

“*Then he is the one person there everybody goes to.*”

“*He is, and he is paid four pounds a year, and he answers to anybody who asks him, and I have hauled stone for eleven men in eleven years and I have never once been asked a question at a gate.*”

He put the pint on the plank again and looked at the river.

“*You have not asked me my name and that is the first time in two days that has happened to me and I would rather you carried on like this the whole way down.*”

Nobody on that wharf thanked anybody.

---

Then Slee counted the money out loud on the stones, because he is a man who counts on the stones.

“*Three shillings and sixpence down to Withermere. That is the fare and it is the same whether there are two of you or four, and I do not carry freight that is going to the low side for nothing.*”

“*Say why that number.*”

“*Because I have a boy of nineteen who is worth eleven pounds a year to me and a boat that costs four to keep in the water for a week, and I am not going down there for the look of it and I am not going down there free.*”

“*That is three and six.*”

“*That is three and six. Threepence of it is a candle and the rest is the boat.*”

Ilyan had a pound and threepence in the world and it was in a purse and the purse was in a coat.

He counted it out on the stones because the man he was paying to had counted on stones, and he counted the fare off the top of it and gave it over, and then he counted the rest and said that out loud.

“*One pound and eighteen pence.*”

“*Say what that is, because you have given it to me before you have said it, and I have four boats and I have carried about two thousand people on them and about nine of them have ever said a number out loud.*”

“*One pound and eighteen pence is sixty-six days of a bed and bread at threepence a day, and I have not got a bed in this county and I have not got a bag.*”

“*That is the truest thing anybody has said on this stone in about nine years, and it does not get you a free passage, and I am sorry about it and here is your boat.*”

---

Then he offered to work it, and he offered it badly, and Slee refused him in about four seconds.

“*You already asked that with your back, and I watched you, and it was the wrong question.*”

“*I offered to hold the plank. I have held it for three hours and a half.*”

“*Yes, and I have a man of fifty on that wagon who came for the day and gets ninepence and a dinner, and I know what he is worth to me, and I have had him since I was twenty-two. If I took you for the crossing then you would be a man in my crew in the water, and I would answer for you to the man whose stone that is, and I have four boats and one boy and I have not had to answer for a stranger since I was thirty-four.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because that is four seconds and I want the whole of it.*”

“*The whole of it is that you are not dangerous and you are not useless, and there is a difference between those two things and it is called knowing what a man is worth before the water is under him.*”

Nobody on that wharf thanked anybody.

---

She went at about the ninth hour with the tide making and the stone under a tarpaulin and the boy on the pole and Slee at the stern with a line he was not using.

Before she was out of the light Slee put his head round the cabin and said one thing, unasked, in the voice of a man giving a rule of the water rather than an opinion.

“*You want to know what you have bought. You have bought a year, and not a passage, and there is no form on any of these four boats that says so, because nobody has ever wanted it to be true.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I keep to this water about nine weeks in the year and I am off it for the rest of it. A man who is off the water at Withermere between the waters is a man in a town of about eleven hundred who is told to his face that he is a stranger.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I have got a bag and no bed and that is the whole of what I have to work with.*”

“*There is no inn there that will not want the bed back. I have been off this water since the level dropped out of the channel at Coldcombe. In the water-season I carried a man down who had never been there in his life, and he stood on my own deck and said the whole of it in one sentence, and I have carried that man ever since and he is not a friend of mine.*”

“*What was the sentence.*”

“*He said: they will not ask me anything here. That is what is different. I have been in four towns where I was asked about everything and one where I was asked about nothing at all, and I have not worked out which of those I would rather have.*”

The bank went past on the right and the light went and there was a smell off the water that was nothing like chalk, and that was the first hour of the last day of a hundred and fifty-two days in a town that had a book with four columns and no fifth in it.
