# Chapter 557: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And First Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Writes Eleven Lines On A Sheet Of Paper With Nothing At The Top Of It And Carries Them Nine Yards Up A Green To Have Them Nailed To A Wall Beside A Sheet Of Parchment Two Men Long, And The Man Whose Name Is On The Board Outside The Door Says Before He Nails Them Up That They Will Change Nothing, And Nails Them Up Anyway, And Then Three People Come And Ask About Them, And The Third Of Them Is A Man Who Says They Are Wrong In One Particular And Is Right, And The Wall Is Not Rubbed Out

Sixth day of the hundred and first week. His three hundred and seventh morning. One hundred and fifty-seven days after the settlement. The fever thirty-nine weeks and one day. One hundred and eleven days since the division. Fifty-seven days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

He wrote it on the back of a sheet of paper he had bought for a farthing at the chandler’s, and he wrote it standing up on the bed, and he had a boot with no sole on it and the other boot tied round with a piece of string.

It was eleven lines. It had nothing at the top of it.

He had thought about the top of it for a long time.

---

The first line said that on the eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth and fourteenth of April a man of about fifty-four of a farm four miles south had taken up the season on eleven parcels of the old pasture between the dyke and the low meadow. The second line said two hundred and ten acres. The third said the rate and the penny, at two shillings and a penny the acre, which is twenty-one pounds and seventeen shillings and sixpence a year for the whole of it.

The fourth line said the name.

The fifth line said that in the first week of March a drain was cut on the long grazing, at the top of it, running north for a mile, and that the rule in this hundred governs a parcel and not a water.

The sixth line said where the water in that drain goes.

The seventh, eighth, ninth and tenth lines named the four closes that flood first, and the eleventh said that the rising is in nine days and that after the rising every season in the hundred lapses.

There was no heading, and there was no signature, and there was nothing at the bottom of it either.

---

He carried it nine yards up the green to the north wall of the weigh-house and the water-clerk was in there cutting a length of twine off a spool with his thumb.

“*Mr Ashcombe.*”

“*I have not been asked anything yet today and it is a Sunday, and you are about to, and I can see the paper.*”

“*I want this on that wall.*”

“*Say what it is.*”

“*Eleven lines. A parcel list, a figure, a name, a cut, and where the water goes.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said what it is and not what you think it is, and I have been in this building nineteen years and those are two different sentences and one of them is on a board every day this week.*”

“*I think it is a remedy.*”

He said it out loud, in a building with the door open, at about the ninth hour on a Sunday morning with about nine people within about thirty yards of the wall, and he had said it out loud because he had said everything out loud for a hundred and fifty-seven days and had never once had it cost him anything to stop.

Ashcombe put the twine down.

“*You are from a town with a book in a cupboard,*” he said.

“*How do you know that.*”

“*Because that is the only place a man comes from who thinks a piece of paper in the open is a remedy, and I do not say that to be unkind to you, and I am going to nail your eleven lines to that wall in about four minutes, and I am going to say this first.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*It will change nothing.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it, because I have brought a piece of paper about fifty miles and I would like the whole of what is going to happen to it.*”

“*I will nail it up beside the sheet, because you have asked me and I answer anything, and if I do not do it then you will say in a year that the one person in this hundred anybody could ask did not do the one thing anybody asked him to do.*”

He got a nail out of his coat, which is the only place anybody in that town keeps nails.

“*And it will change nothing, and I am telling you that before it goes on, so that you cannot say afterwards that nobody warned you. A sheet of paper on a wall in a town where about eleven hundred people may read it is worth exactly as much as the number of people who read it and do something about it, and on a good week that is about nine, and on a bad week it is none, and there is no instrument in this county that can make it be more than that.*”

He put the nail in the head of the first line and looked at it.

“*And you have written it with nothing at the top of it.*”

“*I have.*”

“*Then in about a year a person in this town will read eleven lines and will not know what they are, and will take it for a list of the ground, and will be wrong, and will be wrong quietly, and that is the one thing I will not have done in my own building.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just told me it will change nothing and now you are telling me what I got wrong, and I would like to know whether you are going to do both.*”

“*I am going to do both, and I have wanted somebody to make me do both for about four years.*”

He nailed it up, and the sheet beside it is two men long, and the notice is eleven lines and a third of a man long, and about nine people read the sheet every morning of their lives and had read the notice inside four minutes.

---

He was back at the wall at about the eleventh hour and there were three people there, and that was three more than he had any right to expect before noon on a Sunday.

The first was a man of about thirty-four with a spade over his shoulder, who was the brother of the man of fifty-one, and who wanted to know whether the name on the fourth line was spelled the way he thought.

The second was a woman of about sixty-two whose four acres were the second of the four closes named on the seventh line, and who stood and read that line four times and then said *that one* and nothing else, and went away.

The third was a man of about forty-four who did not live in the district and had a coat on him that had not been on a boat.

“*You have got a thing wrong and I have come nine miles to tell you so and I would rather you took it than that you argued with me about it.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*The long grazing, the forty-four acres, that is his sixth year. He took that up six years ago off a man who died and it was in order and it was at the re-letting with thirty people in this building and everybody heard it.*”

About four seconds went past in that weigh-house.

“*Say the rest of it, because I have had a wall in front of me for two hours and I have not seen that and I have been in a hundred and fifty-seven days of looking at records and I have not once looked up the column that goes left.*”

“*The six other lines on your notice are this year’s. The long grazing is his sixth. That is the whole of what is wrong with it and it is the whole of what is right with it, and it is a better thing than you have written and I have come nine miles to give it to a stranger for nothing, because it is not nothing to you.*”

He stood in front of the wall with a piece of chalk and did not use it for about four seconds.

“*Then the weapon is the ten small ones in one week and not the seven years.*”

“*The weapon is ten small ones in one week. The seven years are a man doing a thing properly. If you had put the seven years at the top of your notice instead of the ten then about nine people in this hundred would have read it and said the man was right, and you would have had nothing, and the notice would have been a true thing and been useless and worse than useless.*”

---

The clerk wrote eleven more words on the bottom of that notice in his own hand, with the day on it, and did not rule a line and did not head it, and about nine people read them that afternoon.

It said that the long grazing was the holder’s sixth year, that the rest were this year’s, and that a man of about forty-four from out of the district had said so in the open and had come nine miles to say it.

Nobody in that town rubbed it out. A man of about thirty-four came at about the fourth hour with a rag, out of the ordinary way, and stood in front of it, and did not touch it, and went home.

The boy with the pole came at about the fifth hour and read the whole of it standing, and he took longer over the eleventh line than over the other ten together, and the eleventh line is the one about the rising.

“*Say what you are looking at, because you have read that line four times.*”

“*I am looking for a number of days.*”

“*There are nine, and it is on your own paper, and I have read it four times today and I am the only person who has read it four times today.*”

“*What is your number.*”

“*I have not got one. My sister has got about sixty people and a licence until the water turns and there is a line on that notice about four closes and none of them is the common she is on.*”

He put his finger on the sixth line and left it there.

“*That is the line. Whoever put this up has written where the water goes and has not written where it stops, and I have been on that water for six days and I can tell you where it stops, and it stops on the low side where the tents are, and there are about sixty people on it and about eight hundred acres of common round them that is nobody’s and that nobody has ever cut.*”

He took his finger off the paper.

“*I am not asking you for anything. My brother says you are a stranger and says that a stranger is worth exactly what a stranger is worth, and he is right about that, and he did not say it in front of you.*”

---

Ilyan stood in front of the wall at about the sixth hour with a boot in his hand and read the whole thing from the top, which is what anybody in that town does, and worked out what he had actually done.

He had wanted a record to be an instrument. In a chalk town, a record is what a person is entered into and is not asked about, and it is slow and it is safe and it is wrong.

In this town, a record is what a person is asked about in front of about thirty people and cannot get out of, and it is fast and it is unsafe and it is also the only thing in a county of this size that anybody in it can be checked against.

His eleven lines were on a wall. Six lines of them were right and one was wrong and a man of forty-four had come nine miles to correct the wrong one in public, and the correction was under it, and the notice said it was wrong in a town of about eleven hundred people, and a man who had lived here for six years could not take any of it back.

That is the cost of putting your hand on a public record. The hand stays on it. And nobody in that town thanked anybody and nobody in that town was sorry for anybody, and the clerk went out at the seventh hour to cut a ditch on ground that was not his, and the water was up about a foot, and the rising is in nine days.
