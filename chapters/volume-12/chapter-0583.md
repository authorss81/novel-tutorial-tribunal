# Chapter 583: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Fifth Week, And A Friday, And Which Is The Market Day And The Third Market Held In That Town Since The Rising, And On Which About Three Hundred People Come Onto A Green And Six Acres Go On A Stone Step With Nothing In The Square For The Third Week Running, And On Which A Man Of Fifty-Four Says Out Loud At That Step In Front Of About Three Hundred People That He Has The Money And Says A Condition And Does Not Say No, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says In The Open Which Of The Two He Is Standing Behind And About Four People Have It As A Man Who Has Picked A Side

Fourth day of the hundred and fifth week. His three hundred and thirty-third morning. One hundred and eighty-three days after the settlement. The fever forty-two weeks and six days. One hundred and thirty-seven days since the division. Eighty-three days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

A market in Withermere is on a Friday and it has not moved in about two hundred years. This was the third one held since the water came over the tally stone and the first one since the standing water went off the low side of that green.

About three hundred people came onto it between the eighth hour and the eleventh.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody for the seven days either.

---

The sally bed went on the step at about the ninth hour, because it is the only parcel in that district whose season is not taken up.

Twelve shillings and sixpence a year. Six acres. A figure in a square at the right-hand end of a line with nothing in it, for the third week running.

The clerk read it out the way he reads the rest of them, in the order of the three columns, and the third column was where he stopped, and then he read the third column again.

About nine people at that step had about twelve shillings and sixpence between them and about nine had not. Nobody put money on the bar.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman with the slate, who was not the clerk and had said so. “*Because a man of thirty with sixteen acres put silver on this bar on the Friday before last and a water-clerk took it off him in the open, and there has been a stranger standing nine feet off this step every time since, and about nine of us would like to know what he is doing there.*”

“*I am standing in front of a bar with nothing on it and a man who is not on the step at all and does not have to be.*”

“*Then say why nobody put anything on it,*” said the woman with the slate, “*because a bar that has had nothing on it for two Fridays running is a different thing from a bar nobody wanted, and I would like the difference said out loud at a step where about nine of us can be checked against each other.*”

“*Because a man of about thirty with sixteen acres put it on here on the Friday before last and a water-clerk took it off him in the open, and about nine of us watched a rate offered to a ditch go back into a pocket, and there is no form in the county of Kell that lets that be done twice.*”

“*Then say the rest of it and do not tell me that is why. That is what happened. I am asking why nobody has gone round the back of it since.*”

“*I have. I went to a bar on the top side on Wednesday and asked whether a rate paid by one man on one parcel cuts the ditch that takes the low meadow off the whole of the pasture. The man there said it cut the ditch that took his four acres off his brother’s eleven and nothing else. I asked a woman who keeps four beds whether she thought it did, and she said she had four shillings and twopence on two acres and had paid it, and would say nothing else, and she had a fire going.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and do not answer her,*” said Hoare Ryley, who had come in off the drove with nine men who had cut since the seventh hour. “*Because a man of fifty-four is coming up this green from the south side and about three hundred people are on it, and there is no form in the county of Kell that lets anybody keep a question warm for four days.*”

---

Then he was asked, and he answered, and the answer was not no.

He came up the green on his own legs at about the ninth hour with mud to the ankle, the way he had come into a weigh-house sixty feet long on the rising with no seat in it. About seven hundred sheep four miles south. Forty-four acres of long grazing in its sixth year with about nine acres of new sward in it that a man sowed in March and that went under in April. In season on the whole of it, having paid two shillings and a penny the acre on two hundred and ten acres across eleven parcels, which is twenty-one pounds seventeen shillings and sixpence a year, and having broken no rule and lied to nobody.

The question was seventeen days in the carrying.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, from the bottom of the stone step with no slate under his arm. “*Because there are about three hundred people on this green and a man of fifty-four is standing on it, and that question was put at the edge of ten acres on the Monday with about thirty people and he stood there about nine minutes and said nothing, and nobody has put it since, and there is no form in this hundred that says he had to be asked again in a place like this.*”

“*I am standing in front of a market of about three hundred people and a question seventeen days old, and I have been asked it twice and I have answered it once, and the first time I did not answer it and I am not going to pretend that was the same as this.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say it in about nine sentences,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because about thirty of the three hundred are going to hear it and the rest of them are not, and about nine of the thirty will have it a different way by the ninth hour, and there is no column anywhere on that sheet that a thing like this can be written into.*”

“*I have the money. I said on the rising that I would still put the other three boards in and pay for them out of my own purse, and I have it, and I have not spent it, and nobody has asked me for it until this morning, and I am not going to be asked for it in a market by a man with a wall.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that, and say what the condition is, because about thirty of us have now heard the first half and we are going to say the second half to about nine people before lunch.*”

“*The condition is the hay. I will put three boards in that gate when the hay is off that meadow.*”

About four people in that market said something at once.

“*Say the rest of it, and say why, and do not say because the harvest wants me.*”

“*Because on Monday I would be standing at the top of ten acres asking nine men for a fortnight, and in the same week I would be the man who put a foot of water on four closes. About nine people would put those two together and about nine would not, and I have not worked out which nine, and neither has anybody in this green, and I am not going to stand in front of three hundred of you and hand you a reason you cannot check.*”

Nobody on that green said anything.

“*Then say the rest of that, and say the thing about the gate, because a water-clerk has a figure for that gate and a grazier does not, and about nine of us have heard one of them say it and not the other.*”

“*Three boards is a foot below the tally line and six boards is the line. That is a true thing about a gate.*”

“*Then say whether it is a true thing about that common,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because I have carried that figure on a wall for twenty-eight days and nobody has ever asked me where it came from, and you are the only man in this green who owns a gate.*”

“*It is not, and that is what I am saying. The foot of water that went over the low common on the second day after the rising morning did not come through my gate. It came off the low meadow, whose ditch had a foot of sallow growing in the whole of it, and a man on the top side is taking that out with a barrow about nine yards a day.*”

“*Then say the rest of it,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*I have said that out loud once and about nine of you did not hear it the first time. So I am saying it in a market today where about thirty of you can be checked against each other, and what I am saying is that three boards hold a level and nobody in this hundred has worked out what they hold it off.*”

About thirty people on that green had heard it. About two hundred and seventy had not.

“*Then say the last of it,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because about nine people in this hundred have believed for seventeen days that you meant it, and about nine have believed you did not, and the two nines have not asked each other, and you have just told three hundred people which nine you are.*”

“*I am not saying no. Nobody has heard me say no and nobody is going to. I am saying the hay comes off that meadow and then I will put three boards in a gate in a yard four miles north, and I will say it out loud on the day I do it and four men will hear it. That is all of it.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

---

Ilyan had come to that market at the seventh hour and had stood about nine feet off that step since the eighth, the way he had stood at the top of ten acres on Monday and was told to stand further off.

He wanted to hear whether the boards were money.

His mistake was at about the tenth hour, and it was the oldest one he has, and about nine people heard it and about two hundred and seventy could not be asked about it afterwards.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane. He had come in from the top side and had a holding and a seat in that hundred and a skep behind his own gate. “*Because you have been standing nine feet off that step for two hours and a man of fifty-four has just told three hundred people a condition, and about nine of us have worked out that you are the reason he felt he had to give one, and about nine have not.*”

“*I am standing in front of a bar with nothing on it.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and say which of the two you are standing behind, because a stranger who is nobody’s is a different thing from a stranger who has not picked, and about thirty people in this market cannot tell which you are.*”

“*I am standing behind the harvest and not behind the six acres.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Then say the rest of it, and do not take it back, because about nine of us heard it and about nine of those nine have it as the stranger having chosen a side, and a side in this hundred is a season in everything but the column.*”

“*I said it in a yard on Tuesday and I have said it here, and it is the same sentence and it is not a new one, and I have known since Sunday that saying a true thing about a man in a room costs the voice and saying it on a green costs the voice and the whole of it.*”

About nine people in that market worked that out in about four seconds. About four of them worked out a different thing.

Nobody in that market thanked anybody.

The fever was forty-two weeks and six days old. A market of about three hundred people was held on a green for the third time since the water came over the tally stone, and a parcel of six acres went on the step with nothing in the square at the end of its line for the third week running and nobody put money on the bar.

About thirty of those three hundred heard a man of fifty-four say that he had the money and would put three boards in a gate when the hay was off the low meadow, and that he was not saying no.

About nine of those thirty had it a different way by the ninth hour, and the rest of the market, about two hundred and seventy of them, cannot be asked about any of it, now or ever.
