# Chapter 575: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Fourth Week, And A Thursday, And The Ninth Day From The Rising, Which Is The Day A Woman Of Forty-One Named In A Street Five Days Before While Refusing A Stranger Her Name, And On Which She Does Two Of The Three Things She Said She Would Have Done And Says Both Out Loud In About Nine People, And On Which The Third Thing Is Done By The Man She Refused It To And Not By Her, And The Question Is Not The One He Carried Fifty Miles And Is Not Answered By About Nine People And Is Answered Wrong In About Four Seconds By About Four Of Them, And She Decides About A Paper With Nothing At The Top Of It And Puts A Parcel On It And Not A Name

Third day of the hundred and fourth week. His three hundred and twenty-fifth morning. One hundred and seventy-five days after the settlement. The fever forty-one weeks and five days. One hundred and twenty-nine days since the division. Seventy-five days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The rising was on the first day of the hundred and third week, a Tuesday. This is the ninth day from it.

---

She had walked down to the low side on the Wednesday and back, and she had done it in her own time and told nobody she was going.

She had four rooms and two children. She did not pay the twelve shillings and sixpence a year on six acres, because a man four miles off took that season on them in the middle of April and has paid it himself. She had a man who was at sea or was dead and had not been decided. She was the quickest person in every room she had been in for twenty days.

The lamp on the bracket over the north wall is lit at the seventh hour. At the ninth hour there were about nine people at that wall, because that is the hour they are at it. About four more were in the room. A man of sixty-two with a shepherd’s crook in his hand had come in early for a Saturday.

Nobody in that room thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because I have answered anything anybody in this hundred has asked me for twenty-one days. You have come down here on a Thursday afternoon with two children not with you. About nine people are at that wall, and I would like to hear what you want before a man of thirty-one says anything.*”

“*Three things said out loud in about nine people, and then I want to go home. The first is that I have been on that common and back, and I have seen it with my own eyes and not off a wall. I am the eleventh person to say that this week and I am the first of the eleven who has not been asked to say it.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and put it in a mouth. A man of fifty-eight told a market that about nine people would be able to say the true of that common in a day. The day was the Thursday, and this is the Thursday.*”

“*I saw about nine hundred yards of rise with the top of it about eleven feet wide, and about nine hundred yards of standing water in lines between that and the meadow, and a meadow of about thirty-one acres with about two thirds of it under. I saw four of the eleven tents still up on the top. I saw a boy of nineteen with a pole and about nine hundred pounds of eleven households on a flat boat, and he did not speak to me either, and he is my brother and I did not ask him to.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and then say the second thing, and I have been at this wall every morning for nineteen years and about nine people have never asked me what is on the end wall of a smithy on a Friday night.*”

“*I have read everything in this county that has the word foot or fifty-eight on it. That is nine things and two of them are in a book.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything.

“*Then say the nine, and say which two, and I will stand here and be the thing that gets read, because I have a wall and no keeper and about nine people in this hundred would rather I read it out than not.*”

“*The north wall itself, with two hundred and twenty feet in a square and fifty-eight in the taking column. Four lines about a room with about thirty seats in it. Six challenges, five of them with no parcel against them. An eleven-line notice on the end wall of the smithy. The nine lines in chalk outside your own door. A notice of a market that was not on, in three places and on a wall. The remedy of about three hundred words under the oak bar, and five lines on the back of it that a woman of thirty-eight wrote on Wednesday and that about nine people read yesterday.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say the two that are in a book, because about four people in this room have not heard about the two in a book and about nine have, and I would like the nine to know they were right.*”

“*The day the water was last over the tally stone is in the green book, and it is the first day of this year and it is the morning every season in this hundred went. And in the same green book, on a line for the sally bed, there is nothing in the square at the right-hand end, and that is the morning it went too. Both of those are in a book eleven feet from where a licence that said *until the water turns* is in a sack under a churn with no copy of it in this county, and neither of those three things is on a wall.*”

About four people in that room said something at once and about nine did not.

---

Then the third thing was done, and it was not done by her.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, to a man of thirty-one, at about the ninth hour, with about nine people at a wall and about four in the room. “*Because a woman of forty-one named this day in a street five days ago, and told about nine people that she would decide about a paper on it. I have not asked you a question about a thing in this county since Sunday. I would like to know why you have not asked her for one.*”

“*A question. I have had it since Friday at about the eleventh hour in a street. I have not worked out a way of saying it in a room, and I have had five days to work one out and I have not got one.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Then say it, because about nine people in this room can be checked against each other and about four of them cannot, and I would rather you said it wrong in here than right in a doorway at four doors.*”

“*Who is it that a hundred of about thirty men asks, when it is asking.*”

About four seconds went past.

About four people in that room said it at once, and in four different ways, that the answer was the water-clerk, and one of them said it as though the question had been about a person and the rest said it as though it had been about a meeting.

“*Say the rest of that, and do not answer them, and I have told you twice that the question is not the one this room thinks it is.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything.

He said it again, in the words he had carried about fifty miles and in the words of a man of eighty-one who had asked it twice in a chalk town and been silent twice.

Nobody in that room answered it.

Then a woman of forty-one said a thing about it out loud, in about nine people, that about four of them heard as an accusation and about nine heard as a fact.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” she said, to him. “*You are standing in front of about nine people who have all just heard a question they cannot answer, and you asked it because I named a day and you needed a question for it, and I have watched you do that in three rooms in twenty days and I am the first person in this county who has said so out loud.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that, and I have no answer to it, and I am not going to give you one in front of about nine people, and I am also not going to take the question back, because it was asked in the open and about nine people have heard it and a question that is taken back in a room is a question that was never asked.*”

Then a man of sixty-two with a shepherd’s crook in his hand said the thing that happens to a question in that room, and about nine people heard it and it was not an answer.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because I have not read that wall on a Saturday for two days because of what is on it, and because about nine of us have just heard a question go out of this room unanswered, and you have wanted to say something in this room for nineteen years.*”

“*I have. Once a week for nineteen years I have stood under that lamp and read two men long of parchment out loud, and about nine people have listened, and not one of them has ever said no. I have wanted to say a thing of my own in here for nineteen years and I have not said it, because the only person I could say it to is the man who wrote it.*”

“*Then say the thing, and say it in about nine people’s hearing, and I have a wall and no keeper and this is the hour that about nine of us are in front of it, and I would rather it went out of this room at the ninth hour than at a market on a Friday.*”

“*A question that goes out of this room goes out of the door and down about nine streets, and about nine people in this hundred have it by the fourth hour, and about nine of those nine will have said it a different way, and the rest of eleven hundred people in this town have not got it and are never going to. That is what happens to a question here. I have been watching it happen to figures for nineteen years and I have never watched it happen to a question.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and then say whether that is a reason to stop asking, and do not answer it for me.*”

“*It is a reason to ask it in a place where about nine people can be checked against each other, which is a parlour, and a lane, and a bar, and a wall at the seventh hour. And I have been in a hundred of about thirty men in this room twice and it is the only place in this county where about thirty people say the same thing at the same hour. And I am not going to tell you whether that is worth doing, because I have not worked it out and I am sixty-two and I have had nineteen years to work it out.*”

Then the woman of forty-one said the thing that had been true since Friday, and about nine people heard it and about four of them understood the whole of it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe to her. “*Because he has asked your question in my building in about thirty people’s hearing. About four of them answered it wrong in four seconds and about nine of them did not answer it at all. There is no form in the county of Kell that makes anybody answer. I have never in nineteen years seen a question asked in this room go out of it unanswered twice.*”

“*I am standing in front of a man who asked my question, and I did not ask it, and I told him on Saturday in a street that he had asked it, and that about four of us would have said it if he had not. He has now said it in a room, and it was my day, and I named the day, and that is the whole of my part in it.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say why you named a day and not a question, because I have been thinking about that for five days and I have not got a way to say it that is not a complaint.*”

“*Because I do not know what the question is. I have worked on it since Saturday. The only one I can get to is whether there is a thing in this hundred that can be asked of anybody at all. I said that one in a street, and about nine people repeated it before the end of the day. I have not got a second one, and I am not going to use somebody else’s because I have run out of days.*”

“*Then say the rest of that.*”

“*Because a question that is out is a question about nine people, and a day is out too, and a day that somebody else has to fill is a day about four of us. I have been in this town twenty days and I would rather be a date than a figure.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

---

And then she decided about the paper with nothing at the top of it, in about nine people, and she did not put a name on it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because I have a remedy of about three hundred words under an oak bar with a woman’s five lines on the back of it, and a man of thirty-one stood at that bar on Wednesday and was told he was the worst man in this county to be arguing about a document. I would like to know what the two of you have come in here for.*”

“*I have come to say what I will do about a paper with nothing at the top of it. I will not have my name on it. I did not work it out in a doorway and I did not work it out in a market. I worked it out at about the fourth hour of last night with a bar on a fire and two children asleep.*”

“*Then say it, and I will put it on that bar in the clerk’s hand in about four minutes and there is no form in the county of Kell that says I may not.*”

“*The second close off the dyke. Six acres. Put the parcel and the acres and the day on it. The day is the day I was told at a market step that my season was not mine any more. Put no name at the top of it, and no name at the bottom of it either.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything for about four seconds.

“*Then say why. A man of thirty-one asked you for a name on a Saturday and you refused him in four seconds, and about nine people in that street heard the reason, and the reason was that a paper with a name at the top of it is a paper nine people can be argued with.*”

“*The reason has not changed and the answer has. A paper with my name at the top of it is a paper about me. A paper with six acres and a day on it is a paper about a rate of twelve shillings and sixpence a year, and a rate is a thing a man can be checked against, and a woman of forty-one cannot.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and then say what you want done about the six acres, and I am going to do it in about four minutes and I want you to have said what you want in front of about nine people first.*”

“*I want nothing done about them. I want them on a piece of paper under a bar, so that in a hundred years there is a line in this county that says a woman of forty-one had six acres of the second close off the dyke, at twelve shillings and sixpence a year. And that a man four miles south took the season on them in the middle of April and paid it himself. That is all true, and none of it is mine any more.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

Then it went on the wall in the clerk’s hand with the day on it. No name at the top of it. No name at the bottom of it. About nine people read it in about four seconds, and it is the easiest reading anybody has done in front of that wall in about two hundred and thirty years, because a ground can be looked at and a person cannot.

The fever was forty-one weeks and five days old. A woman of forty-one walked down to the low side on the Wednesday and back, and said so in about nine people. She read out the nine things in that county that have the word foot or fifty-eight on them, and said that two of the nine are in a book. The two are the day the water came over the tally stone, and the morning a parcel of six acres had nothing in the square at the end of its line.

A man of thirty-one then asked, in a room of about thirty people, who is it that a hundred of about thirty men asks when it is asking. About four people answered it wrong in about four seconds. About nine people did not answer it at all. There is no form in the county of Kell that makes anybody answer, and nobody in that room is bound by any of the answers that were given.

She had named that day five days before, in a street, while refusing a stranger her name. He had carried a figure of five days in his head since the Saturday morning, and it was right. About four people in that room had heard the whole of it and about nine had not.

And what went on the wall at about the fourth hour of the afternoon was a parcel of six acres and a day, with no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it. It is the first line anybody in that hundred has put on that wall in about two hundred and thirty years that a person can be checked against.
