# Chapter 570: The Fifth Day Of The Hundred And Third Week, And A Saturday, And On Which The Week’s Sheet Is Read Out Loud In A Building With The Door Open And What Is Read Out Is About Thirty Names That Nobody Asked For And A Number Of Feet That Is Still On A Wall, And A Remedy Of About Three Hundred Words Goes Under An Oak Bar And A Man Of Thirty-One Asks A Woman Of Forty-One To Put Her Name At The Top Of It And She Says She Will Decide On The Ninth Day From The Rising And That Day Is Five Days Off, And On The Ninth Day From The Rising There Is A Harvest On Ground A Man Of Fifty-Four Is In Season On And A Man Of Thirty-One Has Four Doors Behind Him And A Woman Of Forty-One Has Given Him A Day And Not A Name

Fifth day of the hundred and third week. His three hundred and twentieth morning. One hundred and seventy days after the settlement. The fever forty-one weeks and no days. One hundred and twenty-four days since the division. Seventy days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

It is a Saturday, and a boy of fourteen writes the week’s sheet in chalk on the end wall of the smithy for a man who pays him a penny. This week he had been asked to write something else first and had said he did not know what to do with it. This week he wrote it.

There are about thirty names on it. About nine of them are the names of people who are not of this hundred, and about nine of them have a holding behind their names with no figure against it.

There is a line of it about the low common and it says the number of feet that is on the north wall of the weigh-house, and the number of feet is not a number of people.

Nobody in that town thanked anybody.

---

The hundred’s hundred and thirty holdings have thirty figures in the squares, and on Tuesday eleven of them were read out again with the same name at the end of them and nobody in the room said a word, and one square has nothing in it at all. A man of about sixty-two read it out and was corrected by a man of about fifty about a figure that has not been right since the water-season before last.

And at the bottom of that sheet, under the four lines and under the two hundred and twenty feet and under the six challenges and under the nine-line rule, in the clerk’s own hand with the day on it, there is now a fifth thing.

It says that a remedy of about three hundred words with nothing at the top of it lies under the oak bar at the north end of the green. Anybody in the hundred may read it and take it away. There is no form in the county of Kell that says a person may not.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe to a boy of fourteen with a piece of chalk, at about the ninth hour on a Saturday morning in a town of about eleven hundred people.

“*I am standing in front of a sheet with about thirty names on it that nobody asked for, and I have written them. I did not know what to do with them for about four days and I have known for about two days, and I would like to say how I knew.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*Because on Wednesday a woman stood in front of the north wall for about eleven minutes with a figure in the taking column and a daughter on her hip, and about nine people were at that wall, and on Thursday about thirty people walked down onto a common. And if I do not write the thirty names then in a year about nine people will remember the water and about nine will remember a number of feet. There is no form anywhere that says the names of the people who went down have to be anywhere.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you are fourteen and you write a sheet in chalk for a penny, and there is no form in this county that says a man may put a name in a thing.*”

“*There is no form. There is a piece of chalk and a wall and a penny on a Friday night, and my father has told me the names are on it and I did not know six of them and about four of them are men I have never seen in this town.*”

“*Then you have written four names of men you have never seen, which is the whole of what your four days were about.*”

“*It is. And about nine of the thirty are of this hundred and about nine are not, and I have put a stroke beside the ones that are not, and the man of fifty-eight read it and did not say anything about it.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because a stroke beside a name is a heading of a kind and there is no form anywhere that says a person may not have one.*”

“*There is no form. And I have not told anybody what it means. It means that they came. And nobody else knows it means that, and if somebody asks me I will tell them, and that is the first thing I have written down that I have not been asked for.*”

---

Then the remedy was in the thing in hand, and it was still in hand, and nothing had been done with it for a day.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the water-clerk to a woman of about forty-one with a basket on her arm, at about the eleventh hour on a Saturday morning, in about nine people.

She had two children not with her. She had been in that market on the fourth day of the hundred and first week. She had asked a plain question at a step, been told the true of it, and said out loud that he was doing it properly. She had refused a stranger’s twelve shillings and sixpence in four sentences.

“*I am standing in front of a man of about thirty-one with a bad leg and a sheet of paper with nothing at the top of it, and I have come about his leg.*”

Nobody at that door thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because I have offered to pay a rate in this county once and was refused in four sentences, and about nine people in this hundred heard it, and I would like to know what my leg has to do with a paper.*”

“*Say what your leg has to do with it.*”

“*Nothing. And I am going to ask you for something and it is not money and it is not the leg, and I am going to ask you to put your name at the top of a piece of paper with nothing at the top of it.*”

About four seconds went past and about nine people in that street stopped.

“*Say the rest of that, and say why me, because there is a woman of twenty-eight who has been on that wall twice this week and a man of fifty-eight whose name is on a board outside his own door, and you have asked me.*”

“*Because I am the one of the eleven whose parcel is in it and cannot graze, and because you asked a plain question at a step in the open fifteen days ago and were answered in the open. Those are the first two reasons. The third is that you are the quickest person in every room you have been in for sixteen days, and I need the first two and not the third.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and do not say the third one twice, because about nine people are standing in this street and about nine of them have worked out which of the three you left out.*”

“*The third one is because you are the quickest person in every room, and a paper with your name on it would be believed by about nine people in this hundred because you are quick, and I do not want it believed by about nine people because you are quick. I want it believed because it can be checked, and I have not worked out how to make a paper that can be checked in nine days.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are asking me to put my name to, in about nine sentences, because I am going to decide about it and I want the whole of it before I decide, and I will not decide it in a doorway.*”

“*It is three hundred words with nothing at the top of them. Four sentences of it were read out at a market on Friday morning and the rest of it is under the oak bar at the north end of this green.*”

“*That is a notice, not a remedy.*”

“*It is a remedy. It is not a rule and it is not a covenant and it is nobody’s duty, and if nobody adopts it then nothing happens and no man in this hundred has done anything wrong. A remedy is a thing a person does to a thing.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say what happens if nobody adopts it, and do not tell me it changes nothing, because I have heard that from three people in sixteen days and about nine of us have stopped believing it.*”

“*It does not change nothing. On Thursday about thirty people walked down onto a common because of a number on a wall and about four of them got an old woman and a boy of nine out of a line of tent-pegs. That was a thing I helped happen and there is no form in this county that says I may be sent for about it, and there is no form anywhere that says it should not have happened.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have just said out loud in a street that it was a thing you helped happen, and about nine people have been telling that story differently since Thursday.*”

“*About nine people have. And the four sentences I read on Friday said it in the open, in order, with the numbers in them, and there is a copy under that bar and anybody in this hundred may read it and write on the back of it.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody and nobody in that street was sorry for anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and then say what you want of me and in one sentence, because I have heard the whole of it and I am going to give you an answer and it is not what you have been standing here for.*”

He took the sheet out of his coat. It had nothing at the top of it. About half a page of a stranger’s hand at the top of a blank, and nothing above either.

“*I want your name at the top of that.*”

“*No.*”

It came quickly and about nine people in that street heard it.

“*Say the reason and not the sentence, and say it out loud in the street, because there are about nine of us here and I would rather be refused in front of nine people than in a room.*”

“*Because a paper with a name at the top of it in a town where about nine people read that wall every morning of their lives is not a paper anybody can check. It is a paper nine people can be argued with, and I have argued in this town for sixteen days and I would rather somebody argued with a figure than with a woman of forty-one who lost six acres in a market.*”

About four seconds went past.

“*Say the rest of that, and then say what you will decide instead, and I am going to hear it and I am not going to argue with it.*”

“*I will decide it on the ninth day from the rising.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because a ninth day from the rising is five days from now, and about nine people in this hundred do not have five days, and you have just told me a date and there is no date in this county for anything.*”

“*The rising was on Tuesday morning and it was the first day of this week, and today is the fifth, so the ninth day from the rising is the third day of the week after, a Thursday, and that is five days off. And it is a day of a river and not a number, and you have known me fifteen days and you know I am not going to give you a figure of days when the only count either of us has is the one the river gave us.*”

“*Then say what you will have done by the ninth day from the rising, because I will be at the step on that morning and I am not going to ask you for it in a street.*”

“*Three things. I will have been down to that common and back and I will have seen it myself and not off a wall. I will have read everything in this county that has the word foot or fifty-eight on it, and that is about nine things and two of them are in a book. And I will have asked one question out loud in a room where about nine people can be checked against each other, and I have not yet worked out what the question is.*”

“*Then say it, because you have said you have not worked it out and I want you to know that about nine people in this hundred will assume it is about me.*”

“*Say that you may assume that and about nine people will, and about nine will assume the other thing, and neither of the two nines is going to ask the other nine, and that is the sentence on the board outside the water-clerk’s door that nobody has broken yet.*”

“*Then I will tell you what I would have said, and I am telling you so that you know I have said it. About nine people in this street will hear me say it and about nine of them will tell somebody. That is the first time in sixteen days I have said anything about this in the open, and it is about a date.*”

“*I would have said: is there a thing that can be asked in this hundred of anybody at all?*”

About four seconds went past and about nine people in that street heard a number of them say the same word at the same time.

“*Say the rest of that. You have just asked a question at a gate on a Saturday and about nine people have repeated it, and no form in this county says they may not, and about four of us would have said it if you had not.*”

“*Then it has been asked, and I did not ask it first, and that is on me, and I have had sixteen days to work out a way of saying it that nobody could repeat and I have not got one.*”

Nobody in that street thanked anybody and nobody in that street was sorry for anybody.

---

The boy of fourteen was paid his penny on a Friday night. On the Saturday morning he had written about thirty names in chalk in a town of about eleven hundred people, and he said he had known for about two days that a stroke beside a name means that they came.

And a remedy of about three hundred words went back under the oak bar at the north end of a green on a Saturday morning, having been there since a Friday, and no person in the county of Kell has been bound by any word of it.

And a man of thirty-one asked a woman of forty-one to put her name at the top of a paper with nothing at the top of it, and was refused in about four seconds in front of about nine people, and was told the reason in nine sentences, and was given a day instead of a name.

And that day is the ninth day from the rising, and it is five days from now, and there is no form anywhere in this county that puts a person at a step on it.

And there is a harvest coming on the long grazing, and about nine acres of new sward a man sowed in March is in it. A man of fifty-four with about seven hundred sheep is in season on the whole of it and has been for six years. He is the man who cut the drain and took it out and lost a season of keep. Nobody in this county is going to ask him what his ground is worth.

A man of thirty-one with a bad leg and under two pounds in a purse has four doors behind him and about nine people in each of four streets who have a number he put there. A woman who is the quickest person in every room she is in has given him five days instead of a name.

And the ninth day from the rising is five days away, and it is the third day of the hundred and fourth week, a Thursday. No form anywhere in the county of Kell puts anybody at a step on it. And he has not worked out yet what the question is that he is going to ask in a room where about nine people can be checked against each other.
