# Chapter 571: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Third Week, And A Sunday, And On Which A Dyker Of Fifty-One Who Is Named For The First Time Walks The Top Of A Low Meadow Of About Thirty-One Acres That Is Under For The Second Time In Six Days And Goes Down Onto A Common Of About Eight Hundred Acres And Puts To A Woman Of Twenty-Eight In Front Of About Nine People The Question Nobody Has Put To Anybody In This County, Which Is What Fifty-Eight People On Ground That Belongs To Nobody Are Doing On A Sunday Morning And Whether That Is A Thing Anybody In The County Of Kell Can Be Sent For About, And Is Told That It Is Nothing And That Nothing Is The Whole Of It, And Works Two Counts Out Loud On The Mud And Is Five Days Right And Is One Day Late, And A Boy Of Nineteen Is Back On The Water And Says Four Words To A Stranger And Will Not Say Them Twice

Sixth day of the hundred and third week. His three hundred and twenty-first morning. One hundred and seventy-one days after the settlement. The fever forty-one weeks and one day. One hundred and twenty-five days since the division. Seventy-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

The water was still up on the flats on the Sunday morning, three days after it had gone over the common, and the ground under the eleven tents was mud to the axle and had not been anything else since the Thursday.

The rise is about nine hundred yards long and the top of it is about eleven feet wide, and fifty-eight people were on the top of it, and about nine of them were under nine years old.

That is the whole of the ground there is.

---

He had been cutting ditch in that district for about thirty years and he was called Hoare Ryley and he was fifty-one. He had four acres of his own on that water and his two brothers had eleven between them, and the six of the eleven was on the sally bed on the top side and it had nothing in the square at the right-hand end of its line for the first time in about two hundred and thirty years.

He came down off the drove at about the seventh hour onto the top of the low meadow, and the meadow was under, and he stood on it for a while and then went down off the meadow onto the flats and across about nine hundred yards of standing water to where the tents were.

Nobody stopped him. There is nothing on that common to stop a man.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Bett Searle. She had a daughter of six on her hip and she had not slept on the Saturday. “*Because you are a man of this hundred and I have never seen you on this ground, and you have come across nine hundred yards of water to say a number to me, and about nine of my people are standing here.*”

“*I am standing in front of a rise about eleven feet wide, and I want to ask you a thing, and I have not worked out how to ask it, and I have been not asking it since the Thursday.*”

Nobody on that common thanked anybody.

“*Say it, because I have watched about nine people not ask me things this week and one of them was myself.*”

“*What are fifty-eight people on a common doing this morning,*” said Hoare Ryley. “*And is that a thing anybody in this county can be sent for about.*”

About four seconds went past, and the water moved about nine inches on the flats and came back.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have asked me the first half of it out loud in front of nine people and the second half is the one I am afraid of, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may not ask a woman a question on a common on a Sunday.*”

“*You are on a common that belongs to nobody, and it has no holder and no season and no line on that sheet with a figure in the square. Fifty-eight people on it are doing nothing at all.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because nothing at all is a thing I have never heard said about this ground and I have cut ditch for thirty years.*”

“*It is the only ground in this district where a person cannot be sent for. Every other acre between the tally stone and this one has a name in the first column of that sheet and a rate on it and a man who owns the ditch. This has none of those, and that is not a mercy. A nobody has never been able to be drowned and a nobody has never been able to be turned off either.*”

Nobody on that common said anything.

Then he did the thing that got him a name in this county, which was to work a number out loud in the open where about nine people could check it.

“*Then say the number of days, because I have been carrying two of them since Thursday night and they do not agree, and I would rather be wrong in front of nine people than right nowhere.*”

“*Say which two.*”

“*The water came over the tally stone on the Tuesday, and it is the Sunday, and that is the fifth day from the rising. And the water was on this common on the Thursday, and that is the second day after it, and I had nine days off a Monday in my own head and that lands on the Wednesday and not the Thursday.*”

“*Then say it again, because you have just said two numbers in front of nine people and about four of us cannot add and I am not going to have a man of fifty-one on my ground with a wrong one.*”

“*Nine days off a Monday. The Monday was the seventh and last day of the hundred and first week. Nine off that is the Wednesday, and the water was on this common on the Thursday, and I was a day late and I am saying it here before anybody else does.*”

“*Then it is on you and not on me, and there is nobody in this county that can be sent for about it, and that is the first useful thing a man from this hundred has said on this ground.*”

Nobody on that common thanked anybody and nobody on that common was sorry for anybody.

Hoare Ryley stood about nine feet off in the water and looked at the low meadow across nine hundred yards of flat.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have told me what fifty-eight people are doing and you have not told me why the meadow is under, and I have cut that ditch for thirty years and I know the answer, and I am not going to be the man who says it in a room.*”

“*Because there is a foot of sallow growing in the ditch that takes the meadow off the pasture. And the sallow is there because that ditch is cut out of the rate on six acres of the sally bed, and the rate on six acres is twelve shillings and sixpence a year, and a man gave that season to a room on Monday morning. There is nobody paying it and the ditch is not being cut and the meadow is under.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and then say the man’s name, because I am asking and I have asked you one question and I would like to leave with a name.*”

“*No. You may leave with the number and you may leave with the six acres, and the man is a Ryley and I am a Searle and both of those are in this camp and in this hundred, and that is enough for a Sunday morning.*”

---

The man who answered the same question differently had a spade and a lamb and he was about thirty-four, and a boy of fourteen in a town four miles off had written his name in chalk on an end wall with a stroke beside it and did not know what the stroke meant.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Bett Searle to him, when he had put the spade in and come up.

“*I am standing in front of a question a dyker has asked you twice, and I have got the other half of it.*”

Nobody on that common thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because about nine of us heard him ask it and about nine of us have been asking it ourselves since Thursday and the two halves do not fit and I would like one of them to be wrong.*”

“*A licence is a season. That is what it says on the sheet in the sack under the churn, four words at the top, *for the common, in season*, and a figure and a day.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say it in three sentences, because about nine of us are listening and one of us has a bad leg.*”

“*A season does not have to be given back by anybody, because nobody gave it. A season is taken up and not lent. And he is asking you whether you can be sent for about having one, and the answer is that there is nothing on earth to send you for. He knows that. He has asked it the way a man asks a question he already has the answer to.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say the part he did not say, which is that if a season is not lent then the water is not yours either, and a licence is a thing a man wrote down and the water is a thing a river did.*”

Nobody on that common said anything.

Then it turned out that the figure on a wall four miles off had already been used once without anybody meaning to use it.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Ilyan. “*Because I have been on that common four days and I have not asked one question that I did not carry in with me, and I am asking one now.*”

“*Then ask it, because you have a bad leg and you have been standing in a foot of water for about nine minutes and about four of us have watched you decide not to go down, and the ground under this rise is not mud you can stand on.*”

“*Last night a man in this camp asked a man in Withermere what that common was, and the man in Withermere read him a figure off a wall. Two hundred and twenty feet. And the man in this camp has been on the top of this rise since the Wednesday and he is a man of about sixty and he says the number describes the front of eleven tents and not the fifty-eight of us and that he asked a second man and got the same figure and a different explanation.*”

Nobody on that common thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, and do not ask me to do anything about it, because I have put four numbers on four walls in this county in ten days and I am not going to be a fifth.*”

“*Then do not, and go and sit down, because the water is up for about nine weeks in the year and a boy of nineteen has two days to get about nine tents off that top and he is not going to carry a man with a leg.*”

He got as far as the north end of the rise and the leg went at about the ninth hour and stayed gone. He sat down on the mud with his back against a tent peg. About nine people walked past him and did not stop.

A woman of about thirty with a child on her arm stopped, took him above the elbow without saying anything, and walked him four hundred yards of it. She put him on the flat where the ground was firmer and said one sentence on the way.

“*You are going to be asked this week what you saw on this common, and there is nobody in this county who can be sent for about you saying it, and I would like you to work that out before somebody asks you, because the men who will ask you are not going to ask it nicely.*”

Nobody on that walk thanked anybody.

---

Then the boy came round the end of the rise with a pole over his shoulder and about nine inches of water round his boots, and he was nineteen, and he was the brother of the woman, and he did not come over to them.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Ilyan.

The boy set the pole down in the mud and did not take it up again.

“*Somebody’s, and not yours,*” said Reuben Searle.

Nobody on that common thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you said four words to me on a flat boat in the water-season and I have carried them seventeen days, and I would like to know whether you meant them then or whether you have worked out something better since.*”

“*I have been asked it four times this year and that is the answer I have, and I have not worked out anything better. And I am poling a flat boat and about nine of the eleven tents have to come off that top, and I have two days to do it in while the water is in, and I am not talking to a man on the water.*”

“*Say the rest of that, and say what the two days are, because about thirty of us were on this ground on the Thursday on the strength of a number and nine of the eleven tents went about eleven feet, and I have not worked out one thing about it.*”

“*The water is up for about nine weeks in the year and it was out of the channel at the wharf when I was a boy in the dry, and it is in now and it will not be in by the middle of the week. Two days is what I have got to move about nine tents and about nine hundred pounds of what is in them, and I have got a flat boat and about nine people who are not mine, and that is all of it.*”

Then he said the other thing, and it was the last thing he said all morning, and he said it to nobody in particular.

“*And the word for what has happened to eleven of us in this county is the taking, and my sister has said it four hundred times and I have said it about four, and neither of us has a licence any more because the water turned on the Tuesday, and there is no form anywhere that gives one back.*”

Nobody on that common thanked anybody and nobody on that common was sorry for anybody.

The fever was forty-one weeks and one day old. The water was on a common of about eight hundred acres three days after it had gone over it. Fifty-eight people and about nine children under nine were on the top of a rise about eleven feet wide, and there is nowhere on that common to put a tent that is not either the top of that rise or standing water in lines.

A man of fifty-one called Hoare Ryley walked nine hundred yards of standing water to ask a woman of twenty-eight whether fifty-eight people on ground that belongs to nobody were doing something that could be sent for about. She told him they were doing nothing at all, and that it was the only ground in this district where a person cannot be sent for, and that it was the reason the low meadow was under for the second time in six days.

He worked two counts out loud on the mud in front of about nine people and was a day late about one of them and said so himself before anybody else could, and there is nobody in the county of Kell who can be sent for about either of them.

And about nine hundred pounds of eleven households is going off that rise onto a flat boat this morning, because the water is up for about nine weeks and will not be up by the middle of the week, and there are about nine hundred yards of it in lines that a cart cannot go over.

And four miles off, in a town of about eleven hundred people, there is a figure on the north wall of a weigh-house that says two hundred and twenty feet. About nine people read it every morning of their lives. It is a figure about the top of a rise about nine hundred yards long, and it does not say that there are fifty-eight people on it.
