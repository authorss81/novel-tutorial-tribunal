# Chapter 578: The Sixth Day Of The Hundred And Fourth Week, And A Sunday, And On Which About Thirty Men Come Into A Weigh-House Sixty Feet Long For The Hay And For Six Acres With Nothing In The Square, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Says Out Loud In Front Of About Thirty People That He Can Put Six Acres Back In Season And He Can Speak For The Harvest And He Cannot Do Both, And On Which The Sentence He Cannot Take Back Is Said Back By The Ninth Hour By A Man Of Thirty-Three Who Gave That Season To A Room Thirteen Days Before

Sixth day of the hundred and fourth week. His three hundred and twenty-eighth morning. One hundred and seventy-eight days after the settlement. The fever forty-two weeks and one day. One hundred and thirty-two days since the division. Seventy-eight days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

About thirty men came into the weigh-house on the Sunday afternoon. There were thirty-one in it by the third hour, because a hundred in that district is about thirty men and one man came who has no seat in it.

The hay had not begun. It should have begun on the Tuesday. Ten acres of a low meadow of about thirty-one stood over their time. Forty-four acres of long grazing four miles south stood with them, with about nine acres of new sward in it that a man sowed in March and that went under in April. A hay that stands past its time on that ground goes to seed.

Six acres of the sally bed on the top side had nothing in the square at the right-hand end of their line. A man of thirty-three had given that season to a room thirteen days before, on the Monday, and had given it to a room on purpose and not to a person. The rate on those six acres is twelve shillings and sixpence a year, which is one hundred and fifty pence. Nobody was paying it. The ditch between the sally bed and the low meadow has about a hundred and forty yards in it with a foot of sallow growing in the whole of it.

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. He stood under the lamp with no slate in his hands. “*Because about thirty men are in this room on a Sunday, and the hay is five days late, and six acres in this hundred have no season on them, and about nine people in this town put their own ground on that wall yesterday.*”

“*We are standing in front of two true things,*” said Hoare Ryley. He was fifty-one. He had cut ditch in that district for about thirty years. He had four acres of his own on that water and his two brothers had eleven between them. “*And one man in this room cannot do both of them, and I have known it since Thursday week and I am saying it now because nobody else has.*”

“*Then say the two,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*The first is six acres of the sally bed with nothing in the square. Twelve shillings and sixpence a year is one hundred and fifty pence, and one hundred and fifty pence is a barrow of sallow and a plank and a man holding the other end, once a year. My brother has taken out a year of that sallow alone with a barrow in five days. He has about eleven more days of it. In about two years the low meadow is gone, and not in a wet year.*”

“*Say the second,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*The second is forty-four acres of grazing in its sixth year, with about nine acres of new sward in it that a man sowed in March. The man is fifty-four. He has about seven hundred sheep. He took up eleven seasons on two hundred and ten acres and paid every penny of the rate and the penny, two shillings and a penny the acre, which is twenty-one pounds seventeen shillings and sixpence a year. He is in season on the whole of the forty-four and the harvest on it feeds this hundred. It has to come down in a fortnight with a scythe and about nine men, and the fortnight began five days ago.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything.

“*Say the rest of that,*” said Iven Ashcombe, “*because you have said two true things and about thirty men have heard them, and you have not said why one man cannot do both.*”

“*Because I am not the man,*” said Hoare Ryley. “*I hold four acres in season and I cut ditch. The empty square is not mine. It is my brother’s. He stood beside a stranger in this room on the Monday and gave that season to the room and not to a person, and the stranger had said a number at his door on the Thursday night before it. That is on the stranger and it is on the room, and I am saying so in front of about thirty men.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

---

Ilyan stood up from the bench at the side. He had been in that county twenty-four days. He had come off a boat on the evening of the Wednesday thirteen days before the rising, and one day and a half was said of him two days after it and four days was said of him five days after it, and every count since is of days and not of the arrival day itself. He had under two pounds in a purse. He had four doors behind him and four streets with a number he had put in them. He had a hand on a wall twice over that he cannot take off.

He wanted two things that morning and he had wanted them both since Friday at a stone step. He wanted six acres of the sally bed back in season, with the rate paid and the ditch cut and a household of that hundred whole on its own ground. He wanted the harvest on forty-four acres got in a fortnight, with about nine men and a scythe, before it went to seed. He had money enough in his purse for the first. He had a voice in that room for the second. He had known since Friday that the purse and the voice cannot be used by one man.

His mistake that morning was the one he had confessed twice and not left. He made himself the carrier. He stood up to carry both things at once, in front of about thirty men, with a bad leg and a cracked steel pen in his coat and a letter of four lines in the inside pocket of it that he had not read out to anybody.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe to him. “*Because you have stood up, and about thirty men are in this room, and a man of fifty-one has said out loud that one man cannot do two true things, and you have carried numbers into four rooms in twenty-four days to be useful.*”

“*I am standing in front of six acres with nothing in the square and forty-four acres with a harvest on them. The six are twelve shillings and sixpence a year. I have it in my purse. The forty-four are in season to a man of fifty-four who paid the rate and the penny on two hundred and ten acres and broke no rule and lied to nobody. I can speak a true thing about that man in this room because I hold no season in this hundred and no man in it can be sent for about me.*”

“*Then say the rest of that,*” said Iven Ashcombe.

“*I can put six acres back in season and I can speak for the harvest, and I cannot do both, because the hand that pays the rate is the hand that holds the season, and the hand that holds the season cannot say a true thing about a man in that room.*”

About four seconds went past. About thirty men in a room sixty feet long heard a sentence go out of a stranger’s mouth that cannot be unsaid.

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

---

**CLAIM: A man who pays the rate on a parcel holds the season on it for a year, and a man who holds a season in that hundred is one of about thirty men, and everything he says in that room is then said by a holder.**

**EVIDENCE: A sheet of parchment two men long on the north wall of a weigh-house, ruled in three columns, with no column anywhere for a person’s name. Six acres of the sally bed on the top side with nothing in the square at the right-hand end of the line. Twelve shillings and sixpence a year is one hundred and fifty pence. Forty-four acres of long grazing in its sixth year with about nine acres of new sward in it, in season to a man of fifty-four who paid two shillings and a penny the acre on two hundred and ten acres.**

**BURDEN: In about two years the low meadow of about thirty-one acres is gone where the ditch is not cut, and not in a wet year. In a fortnight the harvest on forty-four acres goes to seed where the scythe does not go. A stranger with under two pounds in a purse can pay one hundred and fifty pence once. He cannot pay it and stay a stranger.**

**CHOICE: Put the six acres back in season on ground his own care put out of season, and be one of thirty, or speak for the harvest on ground that is in season to another man, and stay nobody’s. He cannot do both.**

---

“*Say the rest of that,*” said Garr Ryley. He was thirty-three. He had six acres of the sally bed and nothing in the square. He had stood beside a stranger in that room on the Monday thirteen days before and given that season to the room. He had stood in eleven inches of water on his own ground three days after it. “*Because you have said it in front of about thirty men and about nine of them will have it by the fourth hour, and I am the man whose ground it is about.*”

“*The rest of that is that the number said at my door on the Thursday night was yours. I gave that season to this room four days after it and not to you. There is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may not. You have under two pounds and I have a barrow and a spade and no team. If you put your silver on that bar then I am whole and you are one of thirty, and everything you have said in this room for twenty-four days is then said by a holder.*”

“*Then say whether you are asking me to,*” said Ilyan.

“*I am not asking you to. I am saying what it is. A room does not cut ditches. I have taken out five days of sallow with a barrow in between the beasts. I have about eleven more days of it for a year of it. Nobody in this hundred is going to ask me about it.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house said anything.

Then the man of fifty-four stood up at the back. He had come four miles on his own legs. There was no seat for him because he is not of the hundred. He had stood at the back of that room on the rising with no seat and had said he had been wrong about the drain and was right about the common.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because you have stood up, and about thirty men are in this room, and nobody has asked you whether you meant the boards, and that question is now twelve days old.*”

“*I am standing in front of forty-four acres with a harvest on them and three boards in a yard four miles north because there is no money for them. I said on the rising that I would still put the other three in and pay for them out of my own purse. Nobody has asked me whether I meant it. I am not answering it today, because a stranger has said a true thing in this room that about thirty men cannot all act on.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody and nobody in that weigh-house was sorry for anybody.

---

Then the sentence went round the room, and by the ninth hour it was in somebody else’s mouth.

It began with about four men at the back saying it wrong in about four seconds, the way a question goes out of a room and down about nine streets. By the fourth hour it was in about nine mouths in the town. By the ninth hour it was back in the weigh-house, said by the man whose ground it is about, standing in front of about nine people at the lamp with mud on his boots.

“*He can put six acres back in season and he can speak for the harvest, and he cannot do both,*” said Garr Ryley, “*because the hand that pays the rate is the hand that holds the season, and the hand that holds the season cannot say a true thing about a man in this room. That is what he said, and I am saying it after him, and it is about my ground and his voice, and neither of them is mine to give.*”

The fever was forty-two weeks and one day old. About thirty men had come into a weigh-house sixty feet long for the hay and for six acres with nothing in the square. A man of thirty-one had said out loud in front of about thirty people that he can put six acres back in season and he can speak for the harvest and he cannot do both.

And the sentence he cannot take back was said back by the ninth hour by a man of thirty-three who gave that season to a room thirteen days before, standing beside the stranger he had stood beside on the Monday.
