# Chapter 590: The Fourth Day Of The Hundred And Sixth Week, And A Friday, And Which Is The Market Day And The Fourth Market Held In That Town Since The Rising, And On Which About Nine Men From A Low Meadow Stand At A Stone Step And Say Out Loud That Six Of Ten Acres Are Down And Lying In The Wet Since The Ninth Hour On A Monday And Four Are Standing And Going, And On Which A Man With A Holding Says In The Open That The Four Will Be Cut This Afternoon And There Is No Form In This Hundred That Lets Him Send For About Four More Men, And On Which A Water-Clerk Says Out Loud For The First Time That A Condition A Man Of Fifty-Four Set Himself Cannot Be Met, And On Which A Woman Of Forty-One Is Asked In Front Of About Three Hundred People Whether She Will Put Her Name On A Piece Of Paper In Order To Take Six Acres Back And She Does Not Say No

Fourth day of the hundred and sixth week. His three hundred and fortieth morning. One hundred and ninety days after the settlement. The fever forty-three weeks and six days. One hundred and forty-four days since the division. Ninety days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

---

A market in Withermere is on a Friday and it has not moved in about two hundred years. This was the fourth one held since the water came over the tally stone.

About three hundred people came onto the green between the eighth hour and the eleventh, and about nine of them came up the lane from the low meadow with their boots full of standing water.

There was standing water on the low side of that green again, because the river had come back to the line on the Tuesday and had not gone over it.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody for the seven days either.

---

The hay went onto the step at about the ninth hour, because it is the only parcel in that district whose season is not taken up.

Six acres of the sally bed. Twelve shillings and sixpence a year. A figure in a square at the right-hand end of a line with nothing in it, for the fourth week running, and the second line under it, put there on Thursday with the day on it and nothing above it and nothing below it, saying nothing at all except that a man of fifty-eight had stood on a rise and looked at it.

About nine people at that step had about twelve shillings and sixpence between them and about nine had not. Nobody put money on the bar.

Then about nine men from the low meadow put the state of the hay on that step in the open, in front of about thirty people, and about four of the three hundred turned round.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Hoare Ryley. He had been cutting since the seventh hour and had come up with water to the thigh. “*Because about six of ten acres of that meadow are down and have been lying in the open since the ninth hour on the Monday of this week, which is eleven days, and about four acres are standing and going and are three days past the fortnight, and about nine of us have said so in three places this week and about nine of those places have not got here yet.*”

“*Then say the rest of it and say what a hay of the following year is,*” said the woman with the slate, who was not the clerk and had said so. “*Because a man of about fifty said in a parlour that a hay that stands over its time takes four passes instead of one and by the fourth pass it is a hay of the following year, and about nine of us in this market have that and about nine have four days late on it.*”

“*That is the figure and it is his and it is right. Six of ten down and lying in the wet since the ninth hour on the Monday of the hundred and fifth week, which is eleven days. Four standing with the head of them gone grey. A fourth pass on that four is a scythe a fortnight and about nine men and it is not a hay, it is a thing that has been cut four times and none of them were a hay, and by the time it is in a barn it belongs to whatever year comes next.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of it and say who decides,*” said Aldis Crane, with a holding and a seat in the hundred and a skep behind his own gate. “*Because there is no form in the county of Kell that says anybody decides that a hay gets cut, and about nine men have been cutting it since a Monday in the hundred and fourth week because nine men went, and about nine of us have known that since and about nine have not.*”

Nobody on that green said anything.

“*Then I will say it in the open so that it is a thing that was decided and not a thing that happened. We cut the four. Today, this afternoon, with the same nine men and about four more if about four men come, and there is no form in this hundred that lets me send for about four men and I am going to stand at the top of ten acres and wait.*”

“*And when it is cut, where does it go,*” said a woman of about seventy at the edge of that step, who had asked at a pump what feet a common has. “*Because no barn in this hundred will take wet hay and about nine of us have watched a cart stand in a lane for six hours and go off it in the night.*”

Nobody on that green said anything.

---

Then somebody said the thing that had been true for two days and had not been said out loud.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe, from the bottom of the step with no slate under his arm. “*Because a market of about three hundred people is on this green and there is a condition standing in it that cannot be met, and about thirty of you heard it eleven days ago and about nine of those thirty have worked out what it does to a man of fifty-four and about nine have not.*”

“*Say the rest of that,*” said the woman with the slate.

“*A man of fifty-four said on this green eleven days ago that he would put three boards in a gate when the hay was off that meadow. That hay is not off. Four acres of it are standing on that meadow and they are going and about nine men have cut six of ten and there is no morning left this week in which the rest of it comes down in a way anybody would call a hay. And there is no form anywhere in this county that says a man must do a thing because a condition he set himself has not been met.*”

About nine people on that green turned round and looked down the lane, where a cart was standing with a man of about thirty-four at the near end of the shafts and nobody else.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*Then say the rest of that and say what a man has,*” said Hoare Ryley.

“*He has said he is not saying no. He has said it twice, once on this green and once in a weigh-house with about thirty men in it. He has said the hay comes off first. He has not said it comes off this year and neither has anybody else.*”

Nobody on that green said anything.

The carrying was twenty-four days. The three boards were in a yard four miles north with no money for the rest of them and no form anywhere that says a man must answer a question asked at the edge of a field on a Monday or on a green on a Friday.

---

Ilyan had been in that county thirty-six days. He had held the low end of a plank. He had been priced at four pounds eleven shillings and threepence by a woman who keeps four beds. He had said a true thing about a hole in a sill at a gate and had it taken out of his hand in about four seconds. He had answered a question in a room with the door shut with a wall and had been told by the woman who asked it that a wall cannot be asked anything at all.

He wanted to hear whether three boards were money.

He did not ask. That was his mistake and he has said so since, and about nine people on that green worked out inside four seconds that a man who has been in a county thirty-six days and has not asked one question in a market of three hundred people has decided something.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Aldis Crane, to him, in the open, at about the tenth hour, in front of about thirty people within about nine feet of him. “*Because there is a bar on that step with nothing on it and six acres of sally bed on it with nothing in the square, and there is a man in this market who has under two pounds in a purse and has told about nine people in this market that he has it.*”

“*I am standing in front of a bar with nothing on it.*”

“*Then say the rest of that, and say it to about nine people, and say it in about nine seconds, because about four of us in this market have been working out all morning what a man who says he will not put money on a bar does when about thirty people ask him in front of about nine feet.*”

“*No.*”

About four seconds went past, and then nothing.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not pretend to have said something.*”

“*I am not going to say anything else, and I would like the nine seconds back, because I have just found out what I would have said and it was not a good thing and it was not a bad thing either. It was a sentence for nine people. A man who says no in a market of about three hundred people has said no to about nine of them who can repeat it, and a man who says yes has said yes to two hundred and ninety-one who cannot be asked about it afterwards. There is a third thing and I have not got one for a green.*”

About nine people in that market heard that. About nine did not.

Nobody on that green thanked anybody and nobody on that green was sorry for anybody.

---

Then a woman of forty-one was asked for something, in the open, in front of about three hundred people, and she did not say no.

She had four rooms and two children and a man who was at sea or was dead and had not been decided. She had six acres of the second close off the dyke at twelve shillings and sixpence a year, and a man four miles south had taken that season on them in the middle of April and has paid it himself.

On the north wall of the weigh-house there was a paper under the oak bar with a parcel on it, and the acres, and the day she was told at a market step that her season was not hers any more, and no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it either.

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said the woman of about seventy at the step. “*Because there are about three hundred people on this green and about nine of them know what is on that paper and about nine of those nine know there is no name on it, and I am about to ask you something out loud and about two hundred and seventy people are going to hear me do it and be unable to be asked about it afterwards.*”

“*Then ask it and do not put my name on anything.*”

Nobody on that green thanked anybody.

“*On this bar, in about four minutes, in the clerk’s hand, with the day on it and the parcel on it and the acres on it, and with your name at the top of it — would you take your six acres back before the year turns.*”

About four seconds went past, and about thirty people on that green heard the question, and about nine of them looked at a man of thirty-one standing about nine feet off the step with his hands in his coat.

“*Then say the rest of that and do not answer a man of thirty-one,*” said Hester Nunn, “*because he has not answered anybody this morning and about four of us have noticed that, and it is not mine to lean on and it is not yours either.*”

Nobody on that green said anything.

She did not say no.

The fever was forty-three weeks and six days old. A market of about three hundred people was held on a green for the fourth time since the water came over the tally stone, and about nine men from the low meadow stood at a step and said out loud that six of ten acres are down and lying in the wet and four are standing and going, and a man with a holding said in the open that the four would be cut this afternoon and that no form in the county of Kell lets him send for about four more men.

A man of fifty-four has carried a question about three boards for twenty-four days and has said twice that he is not saying no, and a condition he set himself has been said out loud on a green for the first time and cannot be met.

And a man of thirty-one who has said in a yard that he will stay nobody’s until the year turns did not say no on a step in front of about thirty people, and about nine of them heard the nine seconds and about nine did not.

And a woman of forty-one was asked, in the open, in front of about three hundred people, whether she would put her name on a piece of paper in order to take six acres back, and she did not say no, and about two hundred and seventy people who heard the asking cannot be asked about it.
