# Chapter 559: The First Day Of The Hundred And Second Week, And A Tuesday, And On Which The Day Before Was The Seventh And Last Day Of The Hundred And First Week, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Offers In The Open Air In Front Of About Nine People To Be The One Who Can Be Asked About Eleven Parcels And A Drain And Fifty-Eight People, And Is Refused In About Four Sentences For A Reason That Is The Same Reason Wearing The Other Coat, And Then Offers For The First Time In A Hundred And Fifty-Nine Days To Be In A Room And Not The Instrument Of It, And Is Accepted In About Four Seconds By A Man Who Says He Has Never Been Able To Use It, And Then Works On A Dyke From The Seventh Hour To The Fifth And Cannot Walk Off The End Of It, And Carries A Question To The Only Person In That Hundred Who Can Be Sent For About Anything And Is Not Answered

First day of the hundred and second week. His three hundred and ninth morning. One hundred and fifty-nine days after the settlement. The fever thirty-nine weeks and three days. One hundred and thirteen days since the division. Fifty-nine days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county. The day before was the seventh and last day of the hundred and first week.

---

“*Say what you are standing in front of,*” said Iven Ashcombe. “*Because you have been in my building four days this week and I have not asked you a question, and a man who is not asked is either very patient or has come to do something.*”

“*I have come to do something.*”

“*Say what it is and do not dress it, because I have answered about nine thousand questions in nineteen years in this room and I can hear a dressed one from the step.*”

“*I want to be the one.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because I have been in this building nineteen years and I would like to hear the whole sentence before I answer it.*”

“*I want to be the one anybody in this hundred can be sent for about the eleven parcels and the cut drain and the fifty-eight people on the common. I have wanted it in about nine rooms in a hundred and fifty-nine days and I have been refused in every one of them, and I am asking you for it in the open air in front of about nine people so that you cannot say I did not ask.*”

“*No.*”

“*Say the reason and not the sentence, because I have been refused nine times and every one of the nine was right.*”

“*In your town you offered to be the keeper of a thing and a woman of thirty-four told you in a porch that a line a man who cannot be sent for writes has nobody behind it. That is the right answer there and it is the wrong answer here, and if I give you the same answer I will have given you a sentence about a different county.*”

“*Say the rest of it, because that is the first time in a hundred and fifty-nine days that a refusal has come with the other half of it.*”

“*The other half is this. There is no office in this hundred and there is no form and there is no book with a keeper’s name in it, and if you stand in this building and say a thing, then a woman of forty-one is standing in a market being told about a man she cannot answer for, and a man of fifty-four is standing four miles off reading about a stranger, and neither of them can be sent for about you, and neither of them ever will be.*”

He put his hand flat on the wool table, the way a man does in a county where the table is also a bench.

“*And that is the whole of the difference between where you came from and where you are, and I will give it to you in four sentences and I have thought about it since Thursday. A man nobody may ask is not a free man. In your town you learned that. In this town a man everybody may ask is not a free man either, because then the asking is the work and the man is only the thing being worked on.*”

About nine people in that building heard the whole of it and about four of them understood the second sentence.

---

“*Then I will ask you for the other thing,*” he said. “*And it is a smaller thing and I have not asked anybody for it in a hundred and fifty-nine days, and I want you to know that before you answer it.*”

“*Say it.*”

“*Be in the room on the rising. Not behind the table and not at the step and not in front of anybody. In it. When you write the new season on the wall, and when the eleven are read out, and when you say what you are going to do about the water — I will be in that room.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have asked me for the smallest thing anybody has asked me for in four years and I want to know what you think it buys.*”

“*Nothing. It buys that a thing said in that room has a man in it who is not of this hundred, who cannot be paid by anybody in this hundred, and who cannot be sent for by anybody in this hundred. If it is repeated afterwards, I was in it. That is all. I am not going to speak. I am not going to hold the sheet. I am not going to write anything down.*”

Ashcombe accepted it in about four seconds.

“*Yes,*” he said, “*and I am going to tell you why I said yes so fast, and it is not because I want a witness and it is because you are the only person in this hundred who will be in that room and who cannot be paid by anybody in it, and that is worth more here than it was worth where you came from, and I have not been able to use it in about nineteen years.*”

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*I have never been able to use it. I am the one everybody asks. I am on a stone. I have a rate and four pounds a year and a hundred that meets in my building, and if I say a thing in that room it is the water-clerk saying it, and about nine people believe me and about nine do not, and there is nobody in the room who can be asked by me afterwards. That is what I have not been able to use and you have walked in from another county and offered it.*”

Nobody in that weigh-house thanked anybody.

---

And then the man who had offered the thing he was refused asked the other question, and it is the one an old man gave him on a step in a chalk town eight days before.

“*There is a man of eighty-one in my last town who has asked me to carry a question to a place where nobody knows him, and I have carried it, and I am going to ask it, and you are the only person in this hundred anybody can be sent for about.*”

“*Say the question.*”

“*Whether a man can be named in a place and still not be of it.*”

He did not answer it. He got the green book off the beam and put it down.

“*That is a question for the hundred and not for me. The hundred is about thirty men, one for every holding that has a season on it, and it meets in seven days at midday in this building, and you may stand in it and speak, and anybody in this district will be able to say afterwards that a stranger asked it and that he was told to ask it there.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said no twice this week and every time the second half has been better than the first.*”

“*I am not going to tell you now what will be said in it. If I told you now you would go into that room on the rising with a thing in your head that the rest of this town does not have, and then whatever I said would be weighed against it, and it is the only thing I have got.*”

He put his hand on the green book.

“*And I have not been asked that question by a man in nineteen years and I have wanted somebody to ask me and not be answered.*”

---

Then he worked.

The dyker was called Hoare Ryley and he was fifty-one and he had four acres on that water himself and his two brothers had eleven between them, and he did not say any of that again.

They were on the main dyke from about the seventh hour. There were nine men and it took until about the fifth hour in the afternoon, and what they did in that time was put back a bank that the water had taken in nine days, and stop the water going on to nine acres of new sward that a man sowed in March, and find out where the level had actually got to.

The water was up about a foot and the ground was soft and the men worked with their boots off and Ilyan worked with his boot that had no sole and the one that was tied with string.

His leg went at about the ninth hour and stayed gone and then went further, and at about the eleventh hour he was on his hands and knees in the mud with a spade, and at about the second hour in the afternoon a man of about twenty-eight without saying anything took the spade off him and worked it himself, and put him on the bank with a basket.

He carried the basket for two hours. That is what he did for two hours, and he could do it, and it was the first whole thing he had been able to do in about four days.

At about the fifth hour Hoare Ryley said the number out loud, because that is what a man does on a bank at the end of a day, and nobody had asked him to.

“*Nine men, one day, and about four hours of that before the light went. A drain cut in March did all of this in an hour and a half.*”

Nobody on that bank said anything for about four seconds.

“*Say the rest of it, because you have said the true half and I would like the other half before I sit down.*”

“*The other half is that we can put it back every rising for about nine years and he will not stop, and there is nothing in this water that will make him, and in nine years I will be sixty and my brothers will have gone and it will be a stranger’s water.*”

---

He got off that bank at about the sixth hour and could not walk off it.

A man of about twenty-eight took his arm at the top without asking and walked him two hundred yards up the towpath and left him at the bridge, and said one sentence on the way and did not say it unkindly.

“*You will not be able to get to the inn tonight and everybody in this town can see that, and about nine of them will have an opinion about it tomorrow, and you should know that before you decide what to say about it.*”

Nobody on that towpath thanked anybody and nobody on that towpath was sorry for anybody. The fever was thirty-nine weeks and three days old, and the rising is in seven days, and a man of thirty-one had carried a question about fifty miles for an old man in a chalk town and had been taken to the one room in the county where the question could be asked of about thirty men, and had not been given the smallest part of the answer.

And he had offered to be the instrument of a hundred and fifty-nine days in about nine rooms and been refused nine times, and had then offered for the first time to be in a room, and been accepted in about four seconds by a man who said he had never in nineteen years been able to use anything like it.

And the water was up a foot, and a drain cut in March by a man who paid for it had undone four hours of nine men’s work in an hour and a half, and a man of fifty-one had said the number out loud on a bank at the fifth hour of the afternoon, and nobody had asked him to, and about thirty men will be in a weigh-house in seven days and one of them is going to be a stranger who cannot be sent for.
