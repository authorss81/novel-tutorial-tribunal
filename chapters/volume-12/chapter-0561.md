# Chapter 561: The Third Day Of The Hundred And Second Week, And A Thursday, And On Which A Man Of Thirty-One Goes Out After Dark To Four Doorways With A Number In His Mouth Before Anybody Has Written Anything, And Is Refused At The Fourth Of Them Before He Has Finished The Second Sentence By A Woman He Has Met Once On A Step, And Agrees With The Other Three, And Says A Number That Is One Day Wrong Out Loud In The Second Doorway And Is Corrected In That Doorway By The Man He Said It To, And A Man Of Fifty-Eight Comes Up The Lane With A Slate And Says He Has Done Six And Has One Left, And Nobody In Four Streets Thinks He Is Doing Anything

Third day of the hundred and second week. His three hundred and eleventh morning. One hundred and sixty-one days after the settlement. The fever thirty-nine weeks and five days. One hundred and fifteen days since the division. Sixty-one days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town in another county.

The rising is in five days. A water-clerk of fifty-eight has been asked for that number nine times today and has given it nine times, and it is a day of a river and not a day of a number.

---

It was dark by the ninth hour, there was no moon, and the ground between the four closes was a mile and a half of trodden mud. He was doing it on a boot with no sole on one foot and a boot tied with string on the other.

The four of them were a mile and a half apart, which was the part nobody in the weigh-house had thought about. The seven are on the top side and all within half a mile of one another, because the top side is a road. The four are at the bottom of the district, down off the dyke.

He had the list on the back of his hand in pencil, no lamp and no candle, and a farthing bought neither.

---

The first door was the narrow strip of three acres and the man who opened it was about thirty-three and had a lamp, because there was a child asleep up the stairs.

“*Say who you are, because it is dark and I have the latch up.*”

“*I am the stranger off the boat. I came down the water on Wednesday. I am standing on your step and I have come to say a number to you before anybody writes anything down.*”

The man did not open the door any further. He kept the latch up and the lamp where it was and neither of them counted it.

“*You have come to say a number to me.*”

“*Three boards are in at the sluice and the gate takes six,*” said Ilyan. “*And the water is a foot below the tally line, and six boards puts a foot of water on four closes and on about eight hundred acres of common on the low side. Three of those four closes are the ones behind you. That is the whole of what I have come to say.*”

Nobody in that doorway thanked anybody.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have said it very fast and I am thirty-three and I do not run.*”

“*The water comes on the second day after the rising and not before, and the rising is on Tuesday.*”

“*Then you have told me a thing that costs me beasts.*”

“*I have told you a thing about four closes. Whether that costs you beasts is a different question and it is yours. I will tell you my whole answer to it and then I am going to leave.*”

The man in the doorway waited.

“*I have eleven acres of high pasture above you and my beasts have been on them for eleven years. I do not want the water kept off. I want the six boards in. Every year the strip goes under and I get a fortnight of keep off it out of the mud in the first cold.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because you have been out in it for eleven years and I have not.*”

“*I am not going to argue with you about it. You know what you are asking for and I have been in this county seven days.*”

“*Then you have told me what you came to tell me and I have told you what I want, and only one of those is a number. And you will be telling this in four places tonight and in about thirty men in the morning, and I will be the only one of the four of us who wants it the other way. You may want to know that now, at the door, while there is one of me, instead of at the wall.*”

“*Then I will say it at the wall as well, and I will say whose it was.*”

The man took the latch off and did not stand aside and did not invite him in.

“*You will say it at the seventh hour by the north wall, where about nine people read that sheet every morning of their lives. Say it in my doorway and by the seventh hour there will be six people in this street who have it from you and none from the clerk.*”

“*I have not said that to the clerk.*”

“*Then you will not.*”

He closed the door and the latch went and there was light under it for about a minute and then there was not, and nobody in that lane thanked anybody and nobody in that lane was sorry for anybody.

---

The second door was the five acres and it was the one where he got the number wrong.

The man was about forty-four. He came out onto the step without a coat, because he had heard somebody’s feet, and he listened to the whole thing without interrupting, and about nine words into it Ilyan said eight days.

“*Say that again, because you have just said one thing differently from the other three and I want to know which of us is right.*”

“*The water is off the cut on the second day after the rising.*”

“*That is a day of a week and I asked you for a number of days.*”

“*The clerk said eight days, on Wednesday, in that building, in front of about thirty people.*”

“*And what day is today.*”

“*Thursday.*”

“*Then you have brought me Wednesday’s number to Thursday’s door.*”

That was the whole of it and it took no time at all and neither of them raised a voice.

“*Work it out in front of me,*” said the man. “*Out loud, with the subtraction, and then I will know which of us is right.*”

Nobody in that doorway thanked anybody.

“*Eight days from Wednesday.*”

“*Say the number.*”

“*Eight days from a Wednesday, and today is a Thursday, and one day has gone since that Wednesday. Eight less one is seven.*”

“*Then work it out the other way, from what I can see.*”

“*The rising is on a Tuesday, and that is five days from tonight. The water is two days after the rising. Five and two is seven, and eight less one is also seven, and it is the only reason I know it is right.*”

He got it out in one breath, and it was the first time in a hundred and sixty-one days that a figure had come out of his mouth with the working still on it.

“*Say the rest of that, because you have made a fool of yourself in my doorway at nine o’clock and I want to know whether you are the sort of man who makes a fool of himself once.*”

“*I made one at the ninth hour two days up the water, on a wharf, and a carter told me I was no use apologising to him. And a woman who keeps an inn of four beds told me to stop counting out loud in her parlour. I have not done either since.*”

“*And the number.*”

“*Seven days from tonight. And the six boards, and four closes, and about eight hundred acres.*”

“*Now say the other half. Everybody in this street is going to hear what is going to happen to me inside a week, and not one of them will be able to do anything about it, and that is the part you have not said.*”

“*There are about thirty men in that weigh-house at midday tomorrow and a man with a slate will write a number of boards in front of them, and the man whose name is on a board outside his own door has promised on that board that any person in the hundred may ask him anything and he will answer it. And you may not.*”

“*Nobody in this hundred can be sent for about me. That is the one thing about me worth anything here, and I have not worked out whether that is the better position or the worse one, and there is a man in that building of fifty-eight who has had nineteen years to work it out and has told me he has not.*”

The man on the step looked at him.

“*Then you are the first person in this hundred who has said out loud that he does not know, and you have said it on my step, and about nine people in this street will have it by the seventh hour and it is not mine.*”

“*It is not yours and I cannot take it back. That is what I am.*”

“*Then go on to the next one and do not say my name.*”

He did not say his name and he went down the lane to the four acres.

---

The third door was the four acres. The woman who opened it was about sixty-two and she had the lamp in her hand and not the latch.

“*Say what you are, because I have had four people at this door since Friday and two of them wanted money.*”

“*I want money and I am not going to get any of it from you, and I say that first because it saves you about four seconds.*”

Nobody in that doorway laughed.

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*Three boards in a six-board gate. Six boards holds it off seven holdings and off the cut on the low meadow, and puts a foot of water on four closes and on the low common. The second day after the rising, which is the Thursday after next, and that is seven days from tonight. I have said it at three doors tonight and I am saying it a fourth because you have a brother and he is not here.*”

“*My brother is on the water and he is not back till the ninth hour and the ninth hour is when the water is worst.*”

“*Then I will come at the ninth hour and say it to you and your brother both. And if he says no then you can tell him you heard it from a stranger at eight and from your brother at nine, and those are worth more than the one.*”

“*And if he says no and I say nothing, what then.*”

“*Then you will have said nothing at the wall on Wednesday and about nine people will have said something, and that will be what you wanted.*”

The woman did not shut the door.

“*Come at the ninth hour and not before. I have four neighbours on this lane who will count how many times a stranger stands on my step in one week and add it up before he has gone a mile.*”

He was earlier at the next one and there was a light in the window.

---

The fourth door was the second close off the dyke and the six acres, and he had stood in the open air under a market step with that woman on a Friday six days ago and been refused in four sentences, and she had not got a step and she had not got a lane.

She had the door open before he knocked, which was not a courtesy. It was a woman who had seen a shape in the dark coming down a lane she knew.

“*You have been on the low side since the eighth hour and I have counted the doors you went into. I count everything that comes down this lane after dark, and there is nothing else to do in a house with two children and no man.*”

Nobody in that doorway thanked anybody.

“*Say the number, and I know you have been saying a number all evening because that is what a man from off the water does.*”

“*Seven days from tonight the water is off the cut and over the low common. Six boards puts a foot of it on this close and on three others.*”

“*No.*”

It came before the end of the second sentence. He had not got to the second one.

“*Say the reason and not the sentence, because I have been refused in four rooms in six days and every one of them gave me the reason after the sentence.*”

“*A number said in a doorway is a thing my neighbours will have by the seventh hour, and by then four people will have said it four ways, and by the ninth hour there will be a fifth version on the water and it will be the wrong one. And on Wednesday morning there will be thirty men in a room and a number of boards on a wall, and not one of those will be the number I heard in my own doorway tonight.*”

About four seconds went past and she did not close the door.

“*Say the rest of that.*”

“*You have been to three doors and I have watched you go to two of them, and I have had four times in six days of people standing at me saying a thing that is going to cost me money. I am not saying no to the water. I am saying no to a doorway.*”

“*Then what do you want instead.*”

“*I want it said once. Not four times. At the north wall, at the seventh hour on Wednesday, in the open, where about nine people read that sheet every morning of their lives. And I want to be standing there when it is said, because a thing said in the open is a thing nine people can be checked against, and a thing said in a doorway is a thing one person has.*”

“*That is what the man with three acres said to me at the first door.*”

“*Then he is not as slow about you as I was. You may tell him so, and tell him I did not say his name.*”

“*And then I will tell you the thing you came here to get out of me, and you will not like it. I have thought about it since Friday and I am going to say it at the wall on Wednesday and not in my doorway tonight.*”

“*Say it now, because I have come a mile and a half and the ground is wet.*”

“*No. That is the whole of the reason.*”

---

He had three agreements and one refusal and nothing on any wall, and he had four streets behind him and about nine people in each of them who had heard a number before the seventh hour.

That was his cost, and he had known it would be before the fourth door and had gone anyway, because the agreement was that the two of them would go into eleven doors on the same evening and if they did not both go then neither of them went. The whole point of the evening was that nobody in that hundred would be able to say afterwards they were told it after it was done.

Then the man of fifty-eight came up the lane from the top side with a slate under his arm and four inches of mud on the bottom of his coat, and he had not got a lamp either.

“*Six,*” he said.

“*Six of seven.*”

“*Six of seven. The seventh is a man of about thirty-six on the top side who is out on the water till the ninth hour, and he does not open a door for a man he has not asked about.*”

“*Then we have not both gone.*”

“*We have both gone. You have done your four between the ninth hour and the eleventh tonight and I am going to do my seventh at the seventh hour in the morning, and all of those are before anybody writes anything down, and there is no arrangement between us that required the two things in the same hour.*”

“*Say the rest of that, because a thing said at the tenth hour tonight and a thing said at the seventh hour in the morning are not the same thing.*”

“*They are the same thing in this hundred and not in any other I know of, because here anybody may ask anybody, and so there is nobody to go and check on you at the seventh hour in the morning. That was the whole of why we agreed to do it.*”

Nobody on that lane thanked anybody and nobody on that lane was sorry for anybody.

“*Say the number of the boards. I have said it four times tonight and I want to hear it once from you.*”

“*Six. Three are in and three are in a yard at Coldcombe because there is no money for them, and I had that off a man with a team of four who has hauled stone for eleven years and has never once been asked a question at a gate.*”

“*The closes.*”

“*Four. Two of them flood most years whatever anybody does about the water. The other two went under in the first week of April for the first time in about nine years, and I did not tell you that and I should have, and that is the whole of what I have got that is new.*”

“*The days.*”

“*The rising is in five days and the water is off the cut on the seventh day from tonight. Nobody may give the rising a date, because it is a day of a river and not a day of a number.*”

“*Then go home, and do not say her name, and do not say a number in a doorway again tonight. There is one more house on this lane that has not been told anything and they are asleep.*”

He went and knocked on a fifth door that was not a close and was not on the list, and told a man of about twenty-six all of it in a kitchen where nine people sat round a table, and the man stood up and said the words *the water-clerk’s man* twice and did not apologise for either.

Nobody in that kitchen thanked anybody.

The fever was thirty-nine weeks and five days old. A man of thirty-one had gone to four doorways in the dark on a foot with no sole on it and had been refused at the fourth of them before he had finished the second sentence, and had been told the reason for it at that same fourth door, which was that a number said in a doorway is a thing four people will have by the seventh hour and five by the ninth.

And a man of fifty-eight came up a lane with a slate under his arm and said that six of his seven were done and the seventh was at the seventh hour in the morning, and that there was nobody in that hundred to go and check on a stranger at the seventh hour in the morning, which was the whole of why the two of them had agreed to do it.

And nothing was on any wall.
