# Character State After Batch 0005

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; early Stage 1, Case Opener. **Provisional standing is restored by the ruling, limited.** He may file and be heard in this matter with a named advocate, he may not be cited as a permit, and he must be produced at the public review to answer for the cost of his own record. **This is the volume's resolution and not a stage advance. He gains no perception, no new party, and no new authority. He does not become Stage 2; the Seam Reader stage belongs to Volume 02.**
- **What he won:** a charter amended with ten terms, an appeal route, a visible exit, a named maintenance burden, a public review date, and the Ro household's access, tenancy, and work record — none of which required a signature from the household and none of which he argued for alone.
- **What he lost, in the same ruling, and it is not the price of the win:** his name is on a citable precedent that four districts will read, and it cannot be un-entered. He asked for it in a corridor, gave no reason in the public record, and got a rider on a bicycle four days later with a print from a poor district on the low water in which *lawful traveler* means the opposite of what it means here. The household's door would have opened identically without his name on the document.
- **The third entry he cannot take back.** He has put his Earth reproduction sequence into a public Veyran record twice and cannot un-enter either. The precedent is the third. Whatever he does next is the fourth.
- **His language cost him, in public, once and in the place it mattered.** He followed about a third of the second reading of the draft charter and lost the thread at the enumerated cases and the phrase *within the intention*, and stood up and said so in front of about three hundred people without being asked. Harrow entered it on the ground that a precedent that hides the fact that its author cannot read it is a precedent that will be used against him. **He still cannot read a legal instrument, a foreman's letter, or the machinery of a covenant. He can read load talk, board notices, a district schedule's working numbers, the employment register, and a count book, and he can add up a ward rate in his head and be wrong by a factor of two because he does not know which hand puts a prop in.**
- **His growth this batch is delegation, a signature, and a corridor.** He did not argue a remedy. He did not reach for the bell account. He did not spend the sealed item in the preservation index, and he did not chase the preservation authority, and he said in the lane that the guild had been right to refuse him the pause proposal. He asked for a citable document and was frightened too late.
- **New refusals he has made:** he will not claim the settlement's precedent as his own achievement; he will not certify an instrument he cannot read; he will not touch the four props taken from the fourth bridge without its keeper's answer; and he has said in public that if the district prints his name on a document other people have to live inside, the argument is his to lose and not the household's.
- **Immediate want:** get to the public review without the Halloway print becoming the template, and without being able to read the four districts' copies of his own settlement.

## Sera Quill

- **Status:** **Formally licensed**, as a recordwright of a public record and **expressly not as the attached record-holder's advocate**, to the review date, on four terms: no withdrawal from a matter without an entry in the public record naming her reason; answers for the record and not the outcome; carries the terms of the matter to the review and to any other district that asks; **and takes no matter of her own while she holds this one.** She is not his counsel, not his reward, and not the Ro household's advocate.
- **What it cost, said out loud:** a shop, rooms, and a business that has been quietly carrying her family's unfiled withdrawal claim for six years. She said in open court that the family claim is not in this case and is not going to be.
- **What she found, on her own initiative and under her own license:** the one surviving line on the facing leaf of the removed Quiet Chamber page — *a refusal is entered, and the entry is against the burden and not against the person, and the entry is open to the ward* — entered with the fact that a shadow of more exists and that she declined to read it. **She did not hand this to Ilyan as a gift and he did not ask for it. It was her instrument.**
- **Boundaries held this batch, each of them in front of witnesses:** she refused the district's use of Tamsin's account to support a maintenance acknowledgment and read the child's fifth term aloud to do it; she refused to let the continuity line be taken out of the ruling; she did not let Ilyan certify the charter text; she filed a read-back in the guild's words beside the instrument rather than letting him summarise it; and she told him in a corridor to be frightened of the difference between a precedent and a template.
- **Relationship:** closer and further away than at any point in the volume. She said, on a step, that she will be the record of a bridge and will correct him in front of a copy, and that if it becomes affection it will be a separate entry and it will be dated. **No romance beat has occurred in Volume 01.**

## Sel Harrow

- **Status:** Trialwright. Presided over the full hearing on the span, at the fourth morning bell on the last day of the week, sitting through the night by lamplight. **The ruling is his and it changes a charter.**
- **Two procedures he invented in public and named as his own:** that the burden of each remedy would be stated by the party objecting to it, and — carried from Chapter 39 — that a reopening sets aside a record while a scheduling sets a day. Both are preserved for appeal against his own rulings.
- **His refusals are the shape of the batch.** He let a hard question about the bell go on purpose and said so out loud. He entered Ilyan's admission that he cannot read the instrument. He entered Dain's procedural observation *and* Dain's objection. He let the phrase *lawful traveler* stand on the ground that it is the charter's word. He ruled that the original wording of the maintenance-review condition is not recovered and is not supplied. And he refused for the third time to rule on the continuity line, in the same words, and said the court is not going to be asked about that in four years.
- **He preserved the district's objection to the refusal of *break the span* and expressly invited it to an appeal.** He is the court's witness in any appeal and the appeal is unused.

## Tarin Keel

- **Status:** Route-arbiter, neutral boundary, and the field's record. **He said nothing in the field this batch**, which was noticed, and which is the correct professional thing to do at a hearing where four other people were loud.
- He let Tamsin Ro walk out past the rope without an order and watched her hand and not the rope, as he has done since the first night field. He came down the western steps at dusk with the boundary roll and no lamp.
- **He will testify under oath that he does not know who cut the mark in the count book, and said once in the last batch that he would not enjoy it.** He has still not been asked to testify at anything.

## Oren Sedge

- **Status:** Ninth-bridge keeper, twenty-two years in the post, and the man who signed the acknowledgment he refused.
- **He costed the keeper's pause**, which the court had ordered and nobody had been able to do: two bells for a load that can turn, six for one that must be reversed with a full approach behind it, eight as the ceiling, six hands, at the ward rate — and stated that a clause nobody pays for is a bell nobody rings.
- **He signed the guild's maintenance acknowledgment in his own name and not the name of six crews**, accepting the work unpaid out of the guild's empty account, the bell's wheel and pin, and a term that ends with the keeper, so that no successor is bound and a term can be refused by the man who carries it. He said in open court that the guild has not voted for it and that he is signing as a keeper.
- **He gave away the guild's four props** to the ninth bridge, which are the guild's own working safety equipment and are not the guild's to give, and found out from a four-word message from a keeper on another bridge that he had been keeping them without asking.
- **What he still carries:** an unpaid crew, a count on a propped deck, a wife-of-the-hall's opposite — the ward's question about who pays — and a clause he wrote that has never once been used.

## Ilsa Bray

- **Status:** Ninth-bridge keeper and load-counter, six years on the scale.
- **The band holds and a third number now exists beside it.** One and a half on a light load and up to three on a heavy one, never observed to accumulate, all taken with a moving cart. Then, under a *standing* load, out three, back, and **held at one, not returning** — the first time in the case's history. She came back with her own name on the line and the count of the removal in the book.
- **She wrote the word *nothing* in a count book**, which is not a thing a count book contains, and made Coll keep it, and Oren watched it go in.
- **She reframed the keeper's power.** The bell has never held a load; it empties the deck; the west end is where the hole is; a cart with a full approach behind it cannot turn. A keeper's clause cannot be bigger than the machine it is rung on. That sentence is now term five of a charter.
- **She taught Tamsin Ro the guild's letters over two evenings and then refused to help her any further**, and Tamsin wrote the line on the board herself.
- **Unresolved and unchanged:** she has still not decided whether the levy ledger's cut-out name and the plate's cut are one hand, and the room has still not been asked to decide for her.

## Neris Ro

- **Status:** The Ro household is a **recognized party to an amended charter**. Access at a named hour without registration at the ward rate and not at a toll; the lower-road tenancy protected until an appeal or review says otherwise and not ended by a refusal; a portable work record; a landlord bound by the protection; and a right to refuse a specific burden that is entered against the burden and not against her.
- **What she never gave:** the flood account, and a maintenance acknowledgment. Neither was bought and neither was required. The refusal account stays disputed, preserved, and refused.
- **What she paid:** her household's name, in her own hand, on a citable document that four districts will read. She said it out loud in a lane at two in the morning, dictated the exact sentence about the roof so that a nine-year-old would not have to hear his own street from a recordwright, and said the word *carrier* was not to be used in front of her son. She still holds the right to stop everything herself.
- **Her reason, told once in a lane and never repeated in public:** an inspector offered her daughter's account in place of a signature. It has not been used and it is on nobody's record and it is a template, and templates travel.

## Tamsin Ro

- **Status:** Ten. Legally safe under an amended charter, on a span propped over a channel, with the bell's pin inspected on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope.
- **She chose to testify, at the span, in daylight, with the rope in front of her, and it was her account of a machine and not a meaning** — rope, wheel, worn pin, hammer, bell, post, and nothing on the span. She pulled the rope once so that everybody would know which rope she meant. She had told the room in advance that she would stop if she wanted to stop and get off the span and stand on the steps, so that it could not later be called a refusal.
- **Her four conditions on the court's copy were all met, including the one that mattered: the court said out loud, in a public field, that the account went to the guild first and that nobody made her.** The fifth term was read aloud by Sera Quill in front of three hundred people and killed the district's best procedural argument, and Tamsin knew it would, and had said in advance that it would.
- **She supplied the part nobody asked for.** The pin is worn where nobody can see it, the post stands on the side with the hole under it, and a person pulling a slack rope is a person pulling a rope. The ruling says so.
- **She sent Ilyan away from the lane on the roof day because he had not got a roof to put on**, and her line under the sworn column is the word **AND NAMES**, which she learned from Ilsa Bray and wrote herself.

## Meridith Vale

- **Status:** District maintenance clerk for the lower ward, nineteen years in the post, holder of a newer seal and a standing print authority. **He came down to the span nineteen minutes early, in daylight, and gave the office's limit away in public before it could be taken from him.**
- **He paid the pump's shortage and insisted, on the record and in his own hand, that D. Rusk not be required to say thank you** — because in four months nobody would otherwise know that a pump worker had been told in public that her thank you was not required.
- **He entered the reason his own office cannot pay the wage it has just offered**, on the back of a slip, in his square hand, with the hour on it: a line in a book may be paid only to a body with a name, and the ward is not a body, and it has no name because naming a ward was once used to take a road off people who were not in it. He declined to make that a finding and called it scar tissue, and said the man who entered the participating-party register is the man who cannot pay the toll, and that he would rather say it in daylight than have it said about him later.
- **He is not a villain, has never been one, and has never once entered a false reason.** His own unanswered question — who draws the wage behind the preservation authority, and what happens to the wage and the person when the person leaves — is untouched.

## Tovin Dain, Maren Kett, Halden Moot, Bevin Larch, and Hanne Kolm

- **Tovin Dain:** argued for breaking the span, on the ground that a charter is not the problem and the missing clause is, and made the district's own cost explicit and non-removable — a named crossing for every household that loses this one, at a named route and a named cost, *before* the span comes down, with the fourth bridge's toll in his own mouth on purpose. He then made the procedural argument that the bell account is evidence that a known condition was unanswerable, which was entirely correct and was killed by a ten-year-old's fifth term. He asked that the district's own gap be entered: the wages imply a recognition the household has not given, and the district is right and the district is carrying the difference on its own book where anybody can read it. **He is now paying for stopped hours the district cannot route, and he is right, and it has cost him the money.**
- **Maren Kett:** argued for one legible rule and was undone by being asked who pays it, and said *I cannot name them* in a public field and asked that it be recorded. He warned in open court that the phrase *lawful traveler* would be read by somebody as *proper* in a poor district with a sanitation problem, was told the phrase is the charter's own word and is going in the instrument, and was believed by nobody. He is going to Halloway's room under protest, with his written instruction produced and read. **He is right more often than not and has still lost nothing except his own argument.**
- **Halden Moot:** the quorum clerk, procedurally correct throughout, and the man who entered in the open that the attached record-holder has no constituency. The ward seats are still empty and the bench still says *WARD — VACANT*.
- **Bevin Larch:** twenty-four. Delivered the district's roll at the rope, wrote Ilyan's admission that he cannot read the instrument, entered Neris Ro's name in the new column as *Halide Sen* and spelled it as she said it, and stood on a public approach for the four most important seconds of his working life without a single person telling him what to do.
- **Hanne Kolm:** sixty-one. Kept twelve years of landlord's book unsworn and with not one name in it, and her sister has still not spoken to her about it. **Swore a second instrument**, a book about burdens with names in it and no addresses in it, recording the burden refused, the person who carried it instead, the date, and what it did to the span, recording no consent, and asked for it as a column rather than a book, because a book is a thing you can lose and a column is a thing you have to look at. She said out loud that she had been wrong for twelve years about there being no other kind of sworn book, and that she was sixty-one and going to be asked.

## The new local names

- **Wist Anhalt:** keeper of the fourth bridge, forty-nine, twenty-nine years in the post. Sent four words refusing a request nobody had made him — the props are yours, I object — is not a party to the hearing, is not represented, and has his objection preserved and answered in the ruling. Sent two hired hands to help pack the seat without being asked. **He is owed an answer and has been given one, and nobody has asked him whether he wanted it.**
- **Puck:** a guild hand with a lamp and a carpenter's rule who went in at the drain side when the seat could not be reached from under it, put the rule seven inches into the west course, and said twice that he is not a measure. Packed the re-bedding. **His first name is still not entered anywhere and the ward clerk read that out twice.**
- **Halide Sen:** of a district three valleys away, the twenty-third name on the new column, who walked eleven miles because somebody at a low-water culvert told her the ninth bridge was open to people who are not on a list. Spelled her name with a y and said everybody writes it with an i. Said on the far side: *then my sister can come too.*
- **Halloway:** a poor district on the low water, four miles below the weighhouse, with a sanitation complaint in front of its own bench, whose clerk printed the ninth-bridge settlement two days after the ruling and used *lawful traveler* to mean *proper* at a low-water culvert. **This is the district available for the Volume 02 reversal.**

## Continuity note

Volume 01 ends with a bridge that is safer than it was and a charter that is not what it was, a household with a door, a keeper who can clear a deck and not stop a cart, a child whose five terms are in a sworn column, a district paying wages it cannot route, a recordwright who is not his counsel, and a man whose name is on a document that is already being read wrong four miles away. The historical right of refusal, the authorship question, the C—2 chain, the preservation authority, the fifth mark in the count book, and the First Witness are all exactly where Batch 0004 left them. No new enemy has been introduced and the planned ending is untouched.
