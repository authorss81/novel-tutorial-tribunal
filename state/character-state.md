# Character State — end of Chapter 450, end of Volume 09

**This block supersedes every block below it. Nothing below the rule has been edited in this phase.** Chapters 1–450 are canon and *The Regent's Exception* is finished. **Nobody has been relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked, including the office, which is right about the roads, and including the Regent, who is right about the roads too.**

**DATES.** No month exists in this volume's calendar; every day is printed as week and day (`outline/volume-09.md` §1). **NAMES.** Cast table in `outline/volume-09.md` §2. A name in a record differs from a name in a mouth. Sef gave his name to Ilyan and asked it not be written down; the office had it anyway (`437`). Ground-floor clerk Tam Ferrow is **29**. **Bevin Tarr has cut 4,000 lines, is paid for two dies, and has now cut a third head and a third mark he did not cut.**

## 1. What 441–450 changed

**ILYAN VESTER** (mid-thirties, co-witness, bad leg/ear, cracked pen, cold bitter tea, two hundred mornings). Want, mistake and cost his own in every chapter: wanted the fourth line of the exception and said so in a room, which is the fifth time and has never stopped him (`441`); called Skell a function out loud before he had said it to himself and was made to say it twice standing up (`442`); wrote the burden line in his own hand in a room where it could be read, on purpose, because that is what he wants (`443`); wanted the foot line filled by somebody he would not have to watch, and did not say so (`444`); went out to a road and said the wrong true thing into eleven people's faces, and the road was held by a barrow (`445`); read the offer and refused it line by line and then could not answer the sixth question, and could not pay back the thing he had said in the mud, and stopped trying (`446`); left no line in six pages for a ward to say no in, and said out loud why (`447`); wanted a bar down for five weeks and got it by accident from a clause about a council four hundred miles off, and broke Skell's instruction in the last three words of his own sentence (`448`); wanted one question in a book and knew as he said so that this was the one question nobody can be asked about (`449`); said out loud on a road that he has stopped trying to pay a debt he cannot pay (`450`).

**FENNA RUSK** (44, roll-keeper, a thing in a column that is also a person in six rooms). Named by her own ward in the first line, the second line and the foot of a form, three times in two days. **Asked at the seat, in a room four hundred miles off, who put the name of the person not of this city in the line beside her own, and said his name in front of about thirty people, and would not be relieved about it and was not.** Holds the roll of eleven pages under a stone and has not been able to say whether it is right once in nineteen days. Declines, in `442` and `449`, to let her own position be made the argument. `441`–`450`

**HESSA MARL** (28, cannot say no, the only person anybody can be asked about a door). Made, in the fourth line of an exception, **the keeper of a place of passage out of this country**, by a document and without being asked. She said the one part she can do — the visits go in the office's book — and refused, out loud, to say whether she would count a person going out, and said she has never once been able to hold a want. **Fourteen days in the box; the office is now not held; there is no office to keep the book of visits. She loses nothing in this band and gains a document.** `441`, `448`, `450`

**WRAY COPE** (34, of the seat, person appointed in the register of disputes, in this city since the fifth day of the eighty-second week). **Entered the declining and the writ's silence together in the same book, and would leave neither out.** Entered the declaration whole with a note that he has no line in his book for one. Asked once, in a corridor, for the working, and refused once. `441`, `446`, `448`, `449`

**DARRIN SKELL** (52, nine hundred doors, thirty-one halls). Sent by his ward as the first of thirty-one answers and therefore a person the office can ask about a road. **Called a function in a room by a man who then said it again standing up.** Did the working on the exception's second line (80s a year against 44s in eleven years), on the four wards, on the twenty-two, and on the twenty-five names, and said the room would use the figure 22 and that 22 is not a number of people. Said the office's second line is *a permission and not a right*. Asked a man to get the terms into one sentence and would not let him put anything in it the room would want. `441`–`448`, `450`

**RENN CALLOWAY** (58, Low Road twenty-nine years, eleven chalk strokes and no figures). Stood in four yards of mud at the fourth hour of the night with a lamp and eleven people. **Said what holds the road, which is a barrow, and that nobody can bar what has not been written down.** Asked a room to write down, as a fault, that there is no form which says a man may be sent for. `445`–`450`

**IVEN MARROW** (61, Regent of the Shelf nineteen years; **named aloud at `446`; a man and not a villain**). Wrote the third line of the writ. **Came up the Low Road on foot and went round the four hurdles in four yards of mud at the fourth hour of the morning, and asked for the mud to be entered and not the walking.** Named as the office in `441`. Offered a way out of this country, a seat and four pounds a year, in writing, in five correct lines. Was refused, line by line, in his own terms. Asked the sixth question, which is on no sheet, and was answered *I do not know*. Was defeated by a refusal rather than by an argument, said it was correct, refused the room's villain, said a road with no office on it is a road decided by whoever is standing nearest it and that he has no doubt the council will fill the office, and went down the road in the rain before the light, round the mud, because he had been on it once. `441`–`449`

**TAM FERROW** (29, ground floor). Copied the six-page form fair over three hours and did not copy the man's name, which he wrote himself. Carried four sheets up a hill on his own back, twice, for a mile and a half, for no reason he can name. Stood in the mud at the ninth marker with a book and a pencil and wrote down that a man was right and that being right is what happened. Wrote *I do not have the answer* underneath the one question in this city nobody can be asked about. **Read out four sentences, and six, and four, and never once left a name or a day out of one.** `443`–`450`

**BEVIN TARR** (die-cutter, four thousand lines, paid for two). Cut the third head, the Regent's name, and said the office has been cutting it into brass for two years. Was shown a two-word mark pressed into a declaration and said he did not cut it, and that either it is a fourth mark he does not know about or somebody four hundred miles off has cut a thing they have not paid him to cut. Said *huh* and did not apologise. `441`, `449`

**NELL FISK** (30, reads a plate aloud on the flat, in the open, twice). Read the office's request out in a doorway, twice, to nine people on a stair. Read all six pages of the form aloud in the rain outside the press, twice, and worked out in about four minutes that the empty second foot line was not a question. `442`, `443`, `447`

**BESS LOW** (60, nine years, never once counted). Answered in `447`: a ward can be silent and be protected by nothing — not by the form, not by the office, not by him. `447`

**OTT DRAY** (54, thirty-one sheets for the office in nineteen years, thirty-two in all, never asked what was in one). Carried four ward answers up a hill on his own back for two loads and about a hundred and ten steps, and noticed that nobody had asked him about it. `444`

**SEF** (16, doorway since the eighth morning of the fever, not on or off any list). **Not in this volume's climax, and that is correct.** He has not been sent for since the eighty-fourth week.

## 2. Unmoved

Ansa Rell, 68, in bed, in no register. The boy of eight, better, cannot be discharged, never named aloud in a room. The carpenter's boy of nineteen, arm mending. About a hundred and forty dead, not partitioned, not summed, and now governed as a category by a signature that is not a person's. **Nobody produced: every arrival predates the band or stated route beforehand, and the one man who walked four hundred miles into this city in the rain said who he was before he was asked.** Kerwin Dace, 38, still out. Tarin Keel, 46, walking north. Sera Quill, 28, four hundred miles off, unthanked, her family's claim of six years unfiled.
