# Character State After Volume 02 Batch 0001

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; **Stage 2, Seam Reader.** His limited standing is restored by the ninth-bridge ruling and confined to that matter. He may file and be heard in it with a named advocate; he may not be cited as a permit, a schedule, a levy, an acknowledgment, or a bearer of labor; and he must be produced at the public review to answer for the cost of his own record. He is not a witness for anybody outside that matter and is not entitled to sit in a district's room.
- **What the batch cost him.** Four things in this city's record out of his own account that he cannot un-enter: two copies of his private *Sovereign's Wake* sequence, the precedent he asked for in a corridor, and **the first appeal, filed on the twenty-third.** He entered the sizes of all four in the record himself, so that a clerk in four years does not assume they are the same. And he said out loud, in a field, at a sink-stone, in daylight, what the record cost and who paid it: a load of district sand went back up a hill, a woman's afternoon is in his leg, and a lane of forty houses lost its contractor for a day and a half.
- **What he did not get.** He asked for a declaration that the ninth-bridge settlement binds no district that has not adopted it. It was true, it would have gone in in nine minutes, and it would have left the low fen and the cut banks with a copy of nothing and two keepers with no clearance power and no pay line. **He withdrew it on the record, on the twenty-first, after a two-line letter from Ansel Nyle told him in advance that he did not want the power and wanted the term.** He got a smaller thing: that a citation is not an adoption, and four hours a day at a gate for eight days in chalk at a pump.
- **Stage 2, honestly bounded.** He perceives the **assembly** of a rule inside a hearing field — which piece is bolted to which, and what a rule rests on when it is resting on nothing with a name — and it appears as physical mismatch. **He sees how the rule is assembled and not why its authors wanted it, and a coherent rule can still be unjust; his finding in the field is that the best-made instrument in the batch is unjust and that this is the problem and not the excuse.** It works on documents about money and not on a man. It is not reliable: he read the seam once and put the burden on the visible person, and the field showed him the visible person was only the nearest end of it.
- **The named cost, and it accumulates.** The left leg, from the hip down, occupied by a gate hand's weight; gone by the fifth bell if he stands on a bank, present in a house with the door shut, gone again by the fourth bell. The low water in his nose in two layers, the day over the night and the flat over the water, in a lane with hay in it. A chain on a gate moving in his right hand about four hundred yards away at the wrong end of the afternoon. **The accumulation changed a decision before it changed his body: in Chapter 58 he could not look at a seam in a room and had to be taken off his feet by a route-arbiter and a boy of nine, and the thing he could not do was the thing he had wanted to do.**
- **The token.** The spent blackglass seal went warm on a bank. Touching it takes the low water out of his nose for about fifty minutes and brings it back with a third layer. **He has understood in the body that the passage and the afterimage are one operation, that one was done to him without consent and the other is being done by the same somebody, and that the token is why he is here. He has not resolved it and has said he is not going to chase it.** He still carries the signed, unmerged *Sovereign's Wake* refusal report and he has not spent the sealed item in the preservation index and will not.
- **His language is still two hundred guild words, and in a city of instruments it is worse, not better.** He could not tell a Halloway room whether a district's gate was inside or outside the ninth-bridge access condition, said so standing up in front of sixty people in a room where he had no standing, and named the reason: a span has a deck and a load path and a keeper, and a culvert gate has none of those things, and the difference is in the machinery of a legal instrument and not in the plain sense of the words. **What he is for now is numbers.** Forty-one people at the sixth bell and nine at the ninth; eleven carts turned back; four hundred and eleven loads on a flat, counted by him and wrong by maybe forty. He says out loud, in a field, that a number he cannot get is a number he cannot use.
- **His immediate want:** get to the public review with the appeal disposed of, without the ninth-bridge print becoming a template nobody can argue with, and without being able to read the copies.

## Sera Quill

- **Status:** **Formally licensed** as a recordwright of a public record and expressly not as Ilyan's advocate, to the review date, on the same four terms. **Her third term — carrying the terms of the matter to any other district that asks — is the engine of this batch, and it turned out that a district which has not asked has no door in it.** She went in the front anyway, put the ninth-bridge terms in a room in the first five minutes before anybody could refuse them, and found a wall nineteen years old with a kind clerk behind it. She has said out loud that this is her fault in the specific sense that she carried the terms to a place that never requested them and that the carrying did not make a door.
- **What she found, and what she is careful about.** She read the facing leaf again on the twenty-ninth and found that she had been reading a place into a sentence for a fortnight. The line says a refusal's entry is **open to the ward** — that the entry is not the ward's and is not sworn in the ward, and the question for nine districts is who will take it. **This is a consequence of the one surviving line and not a recovery of the clause, and she has said so in the room and has not let it become a finding.**
- **Boundaries held this batch, each in front of witnesses:** she said in the first minute of a strange room that she was not a witness for Ilyan and that Halloway had not asked; she corrected the volume's framing of the pay line before a room could act on it; she stopped the closing scene of Chapter 60 from becoming a speech in a lane; she said in front of a magistrate that her license is not the magistrate's to use and that she intends to be used.
- **Relationship:** professionally close and personally unaddressed. She wrote down the load count when he was frightened of keeping it and did not comment on his hand. She has said that if it becomes affection it will be a separate entry and it will be dated. **No romance beat has occurred in this batch and none is planned.** Her family claim is separate, unfiled, and not his instrument, and the prohibition on the sealed preservation-index item stands.

## Sel Harrow

- **Status:** Trialwright. Presided over a hearing field opened at a low-water culvert, coupled the ninth-bridge record to Instrument H-9, and discharged the field in nine minutes on the twenty-second.
- **His refusals are the shape of the batch.** He said twice, on the face of the field, that the court has no power over the gate and takes no view on whether it should be open or shut. He told the room what his ruling was going to cost before he ruled, and named the two keepers by their districts. He entered Ilyan's *his left leg is not his* **without asking for an explanation**, on the ground that an explanation would be a thing the court could hear and use. He adjourned the common-rule sitting on the conflict and not on a missing document, and entered a magistrate's own note that the court is afraid.
- **He has still not ruled on the continuity line.** It did not appear at all in Volume 02's first movement, and its absence is now a thing a reader can notice.

## Ansa Rell

- **Status:** Magistrate, forty-four, eleven years in the office. **Introduced in Chapter 55, per `outline/series.md`'s antagonist ladder rung 2.** She represents institutional authority and emergency clarity. She convened the common-rule sitting, required the burden to be stated before the opinion, borrowed Harrow's procedure and said where she got it, put the name of an unasked-for witness back where it came from, and adjourned on a category rather than a document.
- **She is right and she has not won.** One legible rule is cheaper than nine, and she said so in the well of a basalt court in daylight, and she wants it city-wide by the end of the month. **Her instrument would be procedurally valid and would place its burden where nobody is named.** She has not been defeated and is not to be defeated in the next batch; her defeat produces a difficult ally, not a redeemed servant.
- **What she said to Ilyan that he is going to have to live inside:** if he had won the limitation, then in four districts the answer to a man with a bell, for the rest of his life, would be *that is the ninth bridge, and the ninth bridge is the ninth bridge* — quoted, not argued with. And: *you did the right thing and I am not going to be thanked for it, because what you did was not administration. It was a person declining to be convenient, and there is no mechanism anywhere for that.*
- **She has put Mr Vale's question in writing: whether a district may pay a person who has no address. Sitting on the twenty-ninth.** The court's own first note is that a clean instrument can be written in an afternoon and the other thing cannot be written at all. The second note names the missing thing: **a piece of paper that a person can swear to in a room with no address in it.**

## Tarin Keel

- **Status:** Route-arbiter, neutral boundary, and the field's record. He put the rope up at a culvert with four stakes and no order, in a smell, in front of about two hundred and forty people.
- **He said the sentence the batch needed:** *take your hand off the rope* — and the reason, which is that a man standing in a field and a man standing in something else do not look the same to a man who has watched a hundred of them. And he asked the one question a neutral boundary is for: four hundred and eleven loads on that flat, and who said yes to them.
- **He carried Ilyan off a step on the twenty-seventh and did not make a thing of it.** He has still not been asked to testify at anything, and would testify under oath that he does not know who cut the mark in the count book, and would not enjoy it.

## Wenna Tace

- **Status:** Twenty-nine, a refuse hauler at the low water, and the only person on her street who reads, which makes her the reader of every notice in a poor district. She has been doing it for eleven years, in the flat voice she counts a load in.
- **What she wanted:** eleven months of arrears — forty-one hauls at about a third of the district's own haul rate — and she said so out loud and did not pretend to be brave about it. **She got the forty-one on the nineteenth, on a bench minute that says the hundred and fifty hauls in the same eleven months that are in no book of the district cannot be entered by anybody in the room, because nobody there can say what a haul is where there is no cart.**
- **What she understood that nobody in a court had said:** the preambles. The sentence that got her eleven months is the sentence about a trespasser, and the sentence beside it in the same charter is what traps her, because a barrow is a load and a keeper admits a person on foot who offers no load. And the first time a precedent is used honestly in this city, it is not obedience; it is extraction.
- **What she cannot do:** the exit. The charter's refusal is entered in a register kept in **the ward** and sworn by **the landlord of the lower road**, four miles up the fen road, and it does not travel, and she found that out by asking. And the appeal reaches only a person affected by a ruling **on this span**, and the culvert is not the span.
- **What she asked:** *who do I write to.* Nobody answered in the room and Harrow let the silence sit on purpose before answering it himself, and being able to name a person turned out to be the whole of what a right is when the person asking is four miles from the ward.
- **She is the want that is bigger than the thing it was got from,** and Corva Silt entered that in the district's day book.

## Bryn Sallow

- **Status:** Forty-four, gate hand at the low-water culvert, thirty years in the post, one foot on the sill-stone and one on the bank. She reads a number and cannot read a clause.
- **She asked for the true shape of the thing and refused the clean one.** Asked for her hours in numbers for a burden schedule, she wrote: *from the first light on the rise to the dark on the fall, every day, by the gate hand. Not my hours. I have never counted them and I am not going to start now for a page* — because a number would make it a wage and a wage wants a rate and a rate wants a body and the ward is not a body, and the four of them would have gone round again for four days. **She is the first person in this city to have her own hours on a page, and the hours are not a number, and the court says so on the face of the schedule.**
- **She is the cost.** She worked H-9 at the sixth bell in front of eighty people, having read a number and not a clause, and told a carter she could not do it, and he told her she could not do it *because she had not read it*, and he took four loads of district sand and lime back up the hill, and the clerk entered *the gate hand was not instructed; no person instructed the gate hand.* She is the nearest visible end of a burden whose far end is a ward with no name. **Somebody's afternoon is in Ilyan's leg and it is hers and she has not been told and would not thank him.**
- **She demanded to be a name and not an office.** Four hours a day, by name, in chalk, at a pump — not *gate hand* — and she said she has been a post for thirty years and has written her name on a gate in chalk twice a year since she was twenty-eight because somebody had to write who was on the gate, and nobody ever took it down.
- **She also stated the real civic cost of paying for the gate, and she is right:** if a wage for the gate hand has to be found, it comes off the water, off the buckets, on Weir Lane, and it will be the first tax the people of that district have paid anybody in nineteen years, and it will have been invented by a court in Orison on the strength of a bridge.

## Corva Silt

- **Status:** Thirty-four, clerk of the Halloway bench for six years, holder of the district's print authority. **The mechanism, and the district's own archivist of her own mistake.** She printed the ninth-bridge extract on the second day after the hearing and sent a copy to the man whose name was at the bottom of it, and she has never been a villain and has entered the truth about her own instrument every single time she was asked.
- **What she said, unprompted and first:** the print authority runs to any public instrument and there is no fee and no permission; she read the fourth paragraph four times because it was the sentence she was putting her district's name into, and the fifth once at night on a lamp; the fifth said a ward rate paid to a person at the moment of the work in chalk on a day-book line; **this district has no ward and has a woman.** She did not know there was a woman in it and neither did the court.
- **What she did:** struck out her own clause two — the penal-road clause that is the same sentence as a bridge's and that sent the district's own sand back up the hill — and insisted that the bench minute it formally on the third of next week rather than let it be hers alone, on the ground that she would rather be shouted at later than have it be her doing. She closed the day book in six days' arrears on Wenna Tace with a clause forbidding the deduction of the hour of work, which is Bryn Sallow's line, and she read it back before she entered it. She entered Oakes's refusal and the board's end date and the reason on the reverse. She wrote the schedule's defect into the day book in a lane, corrected herself in the same breath without waiting to be asked, and said that a district that pays a favour is a district that has learned to rule by habit.
- **Her own limit, in her own words:** *a notice that says who may open a gate and does not say it is the gate hand is a notice that says anybody may open a gate.*

## Cael Rundle

- **Status:** Fifty-eight, ratepayer of Weir Lane, thirty-one years, a bad chest in the wet, a daughter of fourteen, Marta, who is not in the traveller book and walks to the pump every morning and was told by a carter that she was a trespasser.
- **He moved for the paper to be gone and did not want the water**, and he said so in open court and the room believed him, and he was right. **He did not withdraw the motion when the answer came back correct**, because what he actually wanted turned out to be a line in a district book on which an hour of work can be paid to a person by name, and he asked for that instead and got a date for it. He offered his cart to take a woman four miles up the road to a sworn column, and would not be thanked for it because the refusal of his motion had cost him the thing he had been aiming at. His motion came back on the nineteenth on the back of a weighing-house letter from Ordwin Sarn and he read it out on a bank.
- **His daughter asks about it every morning and nobody has told her anything that is true.**

## Nell Ambrey, Oakes, Bream, Miko Ferren, Dessin Nahl

- **Nell Ambrey, ~60, convenor of the Halloway bench.** Entered the sanitation complaint eleven weeks before Chapter 51, is right about the smell and about the sheet, and is losing both. Somebody might have come.*

- **Dessin Nahl** is the clerk of the Orison chamber, wrote nine of the eleven names on the docket board, brought a man a cup of water in a gallery without being asked, and read a docket out loud in a lane because that is what a clerk does with a docket in a lane.

## Hanne Kolm

- **Status:** Sixty-one. Landlord of the lower road, keeper of a twelve-year unsworn tenancy book with not one name in it, and swearer of the one sworn instrument in this city that has been sworn outside the district it was sworn in.
- **What she did:** on the twenty-fourth she walked the four miles down the fen road in daylight with the box under her arm and would not let anybody carry it, because a ratepayer's cart had already been offered and was not wanted. She swore a **schedule to her own sworn volume** at Halloway, recording the burden refused, who carried it instead, the date, what it did, no consent, and no address — and put on the face of it that she is the landlord of a road four miles from the place it was done, and that the court neither accepts nor refuses it, and that it is her doing and not the court's. **It is not a new instrument, not a new column, and not a new volume. What is new about it is that nobody will take it.**
- **Her argument, unchanged and now load-bearing:** a book is a thing you can lose and a column is a thing you have to look at. A gate hand said the same sentence to her without having heard it, and Ilyan noticed, and Sera did not say she had said it first.
- **Her sister has still not spoken to her about the tenancy book in six years.**

## Ansel Nyle and Ordwin Sarn

- **Ansel Nyle**, fifty-one, keeper of the low fen's **one** bridge, in the post since he was nineteen, filed nothing for thirty-two years. He adopted the ninth-bridge settlement entire as the low fen's own maintenance term on the fifth day by copying a public instrument out of a book, and told the basalt court that it is not a formality. **He has called the deck clear twice; both loads were peat carts that could not turn; both were off the deck in nine minutes; both were paid out of road funds to the person on a line in chalk.** He wrote the finding the room could not: the ward line does not go short on a thin week, because the ward line comes out of road funds and the toll does not, and a man who has to stop a load for nine minutes gets his nine minutes from the same place whatever the week looks like. **He sent Ilyan a two-line letter telling him not to do him a kindness, and it arrived before the decision and it is the reason the decision reads the way it does.** He has not thanked anybody.
- **Ordwin Sarn**, keeper of the cut banks, adopted it on the ninth. His clerk is a woman of thirty-four who cried about the water on the seventh day and says that is normal and happens in every district and is the only part of this nobody warned her about. He is the man who sent the Bridgeward's road man down the cut banks road with a form and a list of dead hands, and he has had a bad fortnight about it and wrote it on a scale-house wall in his own weighing hand: *in six years I have paid every one of them on my own book and I have written the hours in my own hand and the hours have never been checked by anybody else, and a man who is dead cannot have his hours disputed.*

## Bram Orren

- **Status:** Seventeen. Bell-runner and apprentice mapmaker. **He is still not a rescue and not a constituency, and he is the only record of what actually crosses.**
- **What he brought and what he refused to do with it:** a toll at Carrisand in the Bridgeward, taken for eight years, a good road, a woman of seventy-one called Wen Lask collecting it, a form copied term for term from the ninth bridge with eleven dead names entered as the witnesses against it, and no way on earth to challenge it, because a challenge must be served on every party named and every party named is dead and the clerk's office closed the week the form went in. **He wrote it in his line book and did not put it in anybody's file, and he told Ilyan the cost of chasing it: a good road becomes a bad road for the only reason anybody in that family has ever heard of, which is that a man from the city came and asked.** He is seventeen and he runs a line and he said he does not know.

## The Ro household, Oren, Ilsa, Neris, Tamsin

- **Oren Sedge** still runs the ninth bridge. The props came out on the twentieth day of the unloaded period, the span reopened unloaded, the count runs every change with two hands under the deck, and the pay line at the ward rate is a district day-book line that a district clerk named Oakes has now refused to renew on a bridge four miles away on the ground that a district which pays a favour has learned to rule by habit. **The guild is still on the levy rate out of its own empty account and its six crews have still not voted for any of it.** The fourth bridge's four props go back on the same cart before the review or the district pays, and Wist Anhalt's four words are nailed to the frame, and he is not a party, and he sent two hands unasked.
- **Ilsa Bray** sent the four lines that answered the fair question nobody could answer, in her own counter's hand, and wrote *I am not a lawyer and I count loads* underneath them. She still has not decided anything about her grandmother, the woman paid off the load book, and the levy ledger's cut name, and the room has still not asked her to.
- **Neris Ro** got the second piece of paper in a month about her own household and did not ask for it. She asked for the arithmetic to be entered while the woman who can check it was standing up, and she is a person who is owed something rather than a person who refused something, and that is the first time in this case that has been true of her in a public record.
- **Tamsin Ro** came down the fen road on the twenty-fourth of her own accord, was not on anybody's list, was not called, and asked a gate hand three questions about a chain. She came back up the bank and said the difference between her rope and the gate's handbell is not the machine. **The difference is that somebody looked at the pin.** She remains a witness of a machine and not of a meaning, and nobody is to call her again.

## Meridith Vale

He has paid a wage every week out of a road fund on a line in chalk on a frame, and he entered the reason his own office cannot pay a ward rate on the back of a slip in his own hand with the hour on it, and he said out loud that the man who built the register gate is the man who cannot pay the toll.

## Tovin Dain

## Maren Kett

- **Status:** Argued for one legible rule in Volume 01, was right about its cost, and has spent this batch watching what it does. He went to Halloway under protest with his written instruction produced and read, in which the second half was not written until a sheet was already nailed to a gate.
