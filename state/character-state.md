# Character State After Volume 02 Batch 0002

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; **Stage 2, Seam Reader.** His limited standing is restored and confined to the ninth-bridge matter. He may file in it and be heard in it with a named advocate; he may not be cited as a permit, a schedule, a levy, an acknowledgment, or a bearer of labor; and he must be produced at the review. **He is now named, as a person with a duty and a consequence, in a charter of the district of Halloway, four miles from a bridge, which he cannot refuse without the district's appeal being stayed.**
- **What the batch cost him.** **Five things** in this city's record out of his own account that he cannot un-enter, named with their sizes in his own hand on the twenty-fourth: two copies of a private sequence from *The Sovereign's Wake*; **the precedent** he asked for in a corridor, now printed on a gate board and in a form in nine districts; **the first appeal**, which worked perfectly and reached nobody who needed it; and **the carrier's clause**, the fifth, which is the only one of the five somebody can call out and ask to see him, and which cost a district a day and a half of a cart. He said the record is not the price of the precedent, and it is not the price of a carrier either.
- **What he did in the choice.** He said nine words: *I am for the charter, and the reason is that a gate is not the problem.* He was out-argued by his own advocate for the rest of the hour, he was not a party to either text, and he was not counted. He accepted the carrier's clause in nine words and does not regret it. **He did not make a speech and nobody thanked him for the clause.**
- **Stage 2, honestly bounded and now with a practice.** He perceives the **assembly** of a rule inside a hearing field and the limit holds: he sees how a rule is put together, not why its authors wanted it, and a coherent rule can still be unjust. **The batch's new fact is not about the perception. It is that the afterimage does not clear when he rests — it clears when he is inside a burden of his own.** He had it backwards for eleven days. It is a four-hour limit and not a cure, and it is load-bearing for somebody else's claim, and stopping it makes a woman's thirty years a shape again.
- **The rendering.** The token was used once, for fifty minutes, on a sill-stone at the sixth of the month. **The third layer resolved: the thing in his nose is not a memory of a low water and never was a smell. It is a rendering, made for a person, and it is a picture of an oak gate from above and behind at about two hundred feet in a light with no source, the same picture every time from the same height, first seen in eighteen seconds on the edge of the flat on the nineteenth and mistaken for the low sun on the mud.** A rendering is made for somebody and therefore has a vantage, and a vantage is a place, and the place is not on that flat and is not in this city. **He does not know where it is. The fourth layer, which came back under the other three, is a door at the same height and the same distance, and the vantage has not moved.** He has not resolved it and has not said he is going to.
- **His language cost him in the worst place yet.** At the sitting on the category he named the seven words he does not have — *district, resident, ratepayer, household, person affected, body corporate* — and said out loud, in front of eleven benches and a magistrate, that he could not do the hearing in the law's words. It is entered. Odile the court translator said it has happened four times in her trade in eleven years, that three were a man who wanted somebody else to do it, and that she had decided which this was was not her trade. **The finding that won the batch came out of a trade's working vocabulary, and the two facts are the same fact.**
- **Who knows about the leg.** Sera Quill, Harrow, Roke, Ansa Rell. Tarin worked it out and said it first. **The sixth is Bryn Sallow and she still does not know** — she has said the other half of it, that he has made her thirty years a job for a man who has a leg, and she does not know that the leg is hers.
- **His immediate want:** the review in four weeks, with the appeal disposed of, the carrier clause in a district four miles away, a rate on a pump in a lane he does not live in, and a question from a woman of seventy-one that nobody in this city can answer.

## Sera Quill

- **Status:** **Formally licensed** as a recordwright of a public record and expressly not as Ilyan's advocate, on the same four terms, to the review date. **Her third term is what put the ninth-bridge terms in a poor district's room and the charter on a bank in the mud, and her fourth term is what she said out loud in a room in the last chapter and nobody has asked her about since.**
- **What she did in this batch, and it is against him more than beside him.** She wrote the carrier's clause with its consequence, told him she chose the consequence on purpose, entered that she chose it, and named the reason: a clause that cannot be broken is a post, and he had been a post for a month and it made him a gate. **She refused to put a name in the fifth term she had not asked**, told the court she had not asked one person who could hold it, and said that entering her own failure would cost her licence and she would rather it did it in her own hand. She cut a district out of the charter and told a landlord of fifty-four what it cost his protection, and he asked for it and got it.
- **The two things that are hers, named and not resolved.** The quarter of a sentence on the facing leaf, which now has to be read as a duty and not a place — *open to the ward* does not say the ward's clerk holds it, and a ward is a duty — and **a district where a page is open to a person, which is not the same sentence and which she wrote beside the first one on the same page and will not say it is the same sentence.** And a family claim six years old, unfiled, needing a page that may not exist, which she has not looked for in a month because she is holding a charter. **The prohibition on the sealed preservation-index item stands.**
- **Relationship:** professionally close and personally unaddressed. He did not argue with a word of it. **No romance beat has occurred and none is planned for the rest of this volume.**

## Sel Harrow

- **Status:** Trialwright, eleven years. He sat as the court's witness in the first appeal, which is his office in any appeal, and which is why he could not rule on it.
- **He invented the duty to answer**: a bench that has received the instrument shall answer, in public, within eleven days, whether it holds any page a person affected may swear to in its own room, and the court has no power to compel the answer. He said a duty to answer is the smallest power he has ever given out and that he is frightened of it, and that in four years of this case a right nobody had used is the only thing that ran.
- **He refused the continuity line for the fourth time, in the same words** — *I will not rule on a line I did not enter* — entered it as an unentered line, named nobody, and said he will refuse on the seventh time and that on his eleventh year as a trialwright he has come to the conclusion that a line he did not enter is a line he may not rule on, and that this is not modesty and not neutrality.
- **He said no power over the wards twice on the face of the record** and said so the second time on purpose so that it would be entered as the second time. He also said out loud that a field is not a place to keep sitting in, refused to draft a word of the charter because a trialwright who drafts cannot rule on his own draft, and asked the question nobody had asked anybody: *who in this room reads.*

## Ansa Rell

- **Status:** Magistrate, forty-four, eleven years in the office. **She is out-voted, undefeated, and not redeemed.** She put the question that began the batch in writing nineteen years ago and it was heard on the twenty-ninth, and she had a clean instrument in her bag since the eleventh day and put it on the table and let somebody else write a better one.
- **She voted her own weight for the clean instrument, in writing, signed, for ever**, and said the whole of her case out loud before the vote rather than after it: it is the right answer, it is the correct answer, and a rule with no person in it is the exact shape of a thing that has no one who can be asked to say no. She told the chamber she was going to be careful about one thing, and the thing is that eleven benches have answered in public and seven said they hold nothing, and that she has taken an answer that was owed and has cost nothing.
- **Her own entries, which stand:** the eleven-day answers, seven *no* and one *yes* from a desk nobody had ever asked; a bench that did not answer at all, entered as not being in breach of anything because the court cannot compel it; the first, that a ward is a duty and not a place and a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does; the second, that a city which has publicly established that it holds nothing a poor person can swear to is a city in which an empty page is an insult.
- **She is a difficult ally and not a servant.** She said of Ilyan's withdrawal in Volume 01 that what he did was not administration but a person declining to be convenient and that there is no mechanism anywhere for that, and she is now the magistrate whose own instrument lost.

## Maren Kett

## Tarin Keel

## Wenna Tace

- **Status:** Twenty-nine, refuse hauler, reader of her district, paid by it forty-one times in eleven years at about a third of a haul rate. **She holds the fifth term of the charter of the district of Halloway: she keeps the page and swears an entry in it, and she may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one.**
- **What it cost her and what she said about it:** it costs her the appeal, she knew it, she took it, she asked for both halves of that sentence to be entered, and she asked that it be entered that she did not volunteer and that a trialwright asked her a question in a room instead of a magistrate. The page has two entries. She wrote the first in the middle of the page and not at the top, because a person who keeps a page does not put the most important thing where it is easiest to find.
- **What she wants and still wants:** the hundred and fifty hauls in the last eleven months that are in no book of the district and cannot be entered by anybody in any room in this city, because there is no rate for them. She made the court enter that the charter does not provide for the largest single want of the people she read it to, and why. **Do not confuse her question about wheelbarrows with Perdy Sill's. In the fifth of the case Wenna Tace was the woman with the barrow who put the two contradictory sentences of the ninth bridge's own settlement better than Ilyan could, and asked what a wheelbarrow had to do with any of it. The charter she read out in the mud in the third of this month has conspicuously no clause about a barrow in it, that absence is her doing, and it is now entered as the largest want of the people it was read to.** She is the person who has the most use for him and the least use for his name, and she has now told a magistrate that his legs are load-bearing and that nobody is paying for them.
- **She is not a constituency and is not a lawyer and is not a delegate, and she has said so from the floor of a basalt court in front of nine benches.**

## Bryn Sallow

- **Status:** Forty-four, gate hand at the low-water culvert, thirty years in the post, one foot on a sill-stone and one on a bank. **She refused the clean instrument's rate's cousin in words that went into the record: the rate is the right size, I should have had it thirty years ago, I am not going to argue against it in front of a court, and I am not going to pretend I am grateful either, and both go in the same line.**
- **She did not come to the hearing, and the reason was entered first and in her own words**: if the gate hand is not at the gate the gate is shut, and the gate being shut is a flat nobody has counted and about two hundred people.
- **She let her hours be counted.** She refused for thirty years on the reason that a number makes it a wage and a wage wants a rate and a rate wants a body. Ilyan argued her out of it with a sentence about anger, told her in advance that she would be owed by two people who sleep on Weir Lane, and she let a man from a bridge count her tide for six days in a load-carrier's figures that are wrong by four minutes in an hour. **She refused to have the thirty years counted and the number is entered as a shape: over a hundred thousand hours, with no rate in the world for it and a number that can be divided.**
- **She is owed.** The district's own rate, a third of a haul rate, on the balance of the tide from the eighteenth, six and a half hours a day, by name, in chalk, at the moment of the work. The thirty years is entered as owed by the district and not paid. She entered the cost herself, in advance, and said the order of the refusal and the rate is deliberate and is hers.
- **She does not know what is in his leg** and would not thank him for it. She knows his clearing is what makes her hours a number and has said so in front of two people.

- **Abel Kearn, 35, a builder out of the upper street.** Stood up in a basalt court and said he lives in the ward and is in neither of the room's two words for it. A working man with a bad thing and no vocabulary for a charter. He is not a legal actor and must not become one.
