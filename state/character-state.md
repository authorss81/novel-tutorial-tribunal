# Character State After Volume 03 Batch 0001 (Volume 03 is open; Volume 02 is closed)

## THE COAST, and who is on it

### Ilyan Vester, thirty-one

- **Stage 2, Seam Reader. STAGE 2 AT THE END OF CHAPTER 110.** No new party, no bench, no body, no office, no remedy he chose alone. His limited standing is confined to the ninth-bridge matter and the carrier's clause is a duty with a consequence, and the fourth mile is four hundred miles off and irrelevant.
- **HE WAS PRODUCED NOTHING AND NOBODY SENT FOR HIM.** A counsel of nineteen years named him to a woman in a lane, for no reason he gave her; she walked eleven miles to the head of the coast road and paid a day's hire of a cart out of a day she did not have. He arrived on the sixth day of a tide with three shillings and eleven miles of road behind him and a sheet of paper in his coat.
- **THE COAST WORKED OUT HE CANNOT READ AND HE TOLD NOBODY.** A fishwife looked at him and said nothing. Corrie Tallow read the tariff to him, he asked for it again, she said *never mind, you have got it*, and she never mentioned it. **Bettany Rook worked it out in about four seconds in front of the counter in Chapter 102, caught it, turned it into a question about a painted bar staff so that the room would think she was testing him, and has never said a word about it since — and in Chapter 106 she watched him read her own sentence back to her one piece at a time, at a counter, and said nothing whatsoever and stood there with her hands flat on it.** Corva Silt and no clerk is still the only person in the world who has been told, and that is still true.
- **The afterimage, the left leg, the left hand, the rendering, the vantage, the door and the token are all exactly where Volume 02 left them.** The leg is a gate hand of forty-four's weight and is the wrong weight for a man of thirty-one, and a carter at the crossing told him to his face that it was a working leg and not a falling one. **The left hand will not grip: it took half a hand's length of a line over the side of a boat and Dell Gannock gave him a length of manila, which is the right thing to hold and is not a rope, and did not ask.** The rendering is unchanged in kind and the vantage has not moved and the door in it is at the same height and the same distance it was at on the third of the second month, and on a moving deck he could not look away from it. The token was not touched.
- **THE ONE TIME THE LEG CLEARED, and it is the established practice used, not advanced.** On the fourth of a tide at Thornglass, carrying an iron bar out of a fire and back, forty times, about four hours. **The same four-hour limit; it clears inside a burden of his own; it is the same trade as the notice he carried on a road in the fifth month of Volume 02, where the paper was the weight and the woman was not. It is the first time since the eighteenth of the fifth month, which is the only other time it has ever cleared.** The bar was his own work and nobody's name was on it, and the woman the weight belongs to is four hundred miles off and is still in his leg, and it came back worse in about a minute. **He told nobody on this coast and is not going to. There was nothing here that depended on him standing or not standing, and that was the trade and he made it with his eyes open at about the fifth turn. The cost is larger than it was, because the thing he traded it against is four hundred miles off and he chose that.**
- **What he said out loud twice on the coast and once to himself:** *I am here to find out how not to do a thing to a person.* He wrote down on one page, in one hand, the question he asked Alma Gannock and the question he did not ask, with the reason, the date, and that it is his, and handed it to a woman with a ledger, and was told it was the most honest piece of paper she had read in six years and the least use anybody had ever made of one.
- **What he is carrying that he cannot put back:** an account of eleven households that came back as seventeen crossings, in a shop, off a boat, in three days, and the person whose words it was has not been told it changed and cannot be told in a way that would help.
- **Sera Quill is not on the coast and did not come, and there is no romance beat in a hundred and ten chapters.** She would not give a reason he could use. What she sent him is one sheet with two questions on it — *Whose account are you carrying, and who will hear it* — and he did not understand it until the third of a tide, seven days after a counter four hundred miles behind him, and it was the reason he understood Pether Voke's refusal. **Her right to stop everything herself stands, including from him, and nobody on this coast asked him about a household and he did not mention one.**

### Bettany Rook, fifty-one — the volume's best speaker, and not its villain

- **Harbourmaster's clerk for nineteen years, harbourmaster for six, of Kestrel Reach, about nine hundred people.** Four rooms and a loft over the loft; the upper room holds the water and is not opened to the public, and the reason is not secrecy and she says so in nine words.
- **THE WANT IS NOT THE STRAIT. IT IS HER OWN READING, AND IT IS RUNNING OUT.** The light off the Reach has been going for two seasons; she wears a hood on the water side because of it; she asked the glassmaker one question in the spring, in the ordinary way a person asks, and he told her the truth; she has told nobody in the guild, because the day she tells them is the day she is moved to a counter and the guild would be right to do it. **She took the hood off in Chapter 102 in front of a stranger and put it back on and has never mentioned it again.**
- **She is right about the strait** and says so: last year a boy of fourteen went out on the fourth in a punt with a bad lock and came back, and without the guild there would be a great many fourteen-year-olds finding out where the channel goes, one at a time, on a bad day, with nobody on the bank. **She is right about the money** and has voted to take a reading man off the crossing and has said so out loud. **She is right that consent is a door with a person in it.** **She is right that the seventh is the one day she does not know and she refused a question about it on sixty people in the rain rather than give a number.**
- **What she did that cost her:** refused a question at the tide stone and told the room why; offered the guild's own book with the key on her belt and then refused the offer herself, because the guild's book is a fire going to happen; painted the days on a post at the harbour mouth alone, in a bad light, with her hood up, an hour, for nothing; **entered in the day book on paper — so that a man can draw a line through it if it is ever wrong — that a person who guides a cart over Wether Cross is not a trespasser by that fact alone and is not counted as a cart, and entered in writing that she will not put a name on that line because a name on it would be a name the boy could be found on, and then told the council in advance what it costs.** She has entered that the council will not like it and that she has never told them the crossing runs on four pounds a year and does not know what she would do about it.
- **What she did not do:** she did not fire Corrie Tallow's pencil into a pane, and she copied it onto a shelf in the paper book, dated, in the stall-holder's own hand where it would go, with her name at the top of it, and **deliberately did not put it on the list for the fire**, and said why out loud twice: *a thing that is not the record can be wrong and a thing that is the record has to be true.*

### The rest of the coast

- **Corrie Tallow, forty-eight**, rope, oil and tallow at Wether Cross. Thirty-one years of tide in a penny exercise book in pencil and the only right one on that road. Wanted it entered for four years and walked eleven miles to fetch a man. **She is not the holder of a formal term, not a reader of a district, not the door, and not the person a rate is read out to a room; the reason she cannot do anything about the tariff is the water and the price of a day at the counter, and the reason her thirty-one years cannot go into the record is a pane, which is a second and separate failure and is the glassmaker's as well as hers.** She refused the door twice, in the open, for a reason about a stall that is open on every one of the six tides: *if I go up there with a figure it will not be a figure, it will be me, and they will hear her, and it will be a sentence about me and not about the tariff.* **Paid: five days of her stall and a half-crown of custom off a crew of eleven years, and both numbers said out loud in a room.** She is the person who found the rate on the ground with a stick and a board.
- **Nim Hask, eleven**, thirty-nine tides a week at a penny, three shillings and threepence, eight pounds nine a year. **Not a child asked to testify and not a party to anything. He was stopped by a chain once and was let through in nine seconds by a woman who put a line in a day book about him without putting his name on it.**
- **Bel Hask, forty-four**, mends nets, has a cart. Wants the boy off the crossing and cannot afford to stop sending him, and the reason is a cart that does not come round does not earn — **a want, not a concealment, and nothing to do with Volume 02's payer.** She stopped sending Nim after the second of the second tide and the cart went eleven miles the other way on the fourth at about nine pounds of freight and a day and a half of a horse she did not earn, and the sum she did four times says the boy is the cheapest thing on the road, and she says plainly that it is not the money: *a boy comes back and says it went, and if he does not come back then something is wrong, and I would rather pay three shillings and threepence a week than find out.* What the money bought her is not in the tariff: Corrie Tallow takes the slate down to the far stones, four hundred yards down, because the ninth crossing of a day is the only one on that flat worth a slate, and counts every one that goes over, so if the boy is not on the flat she will know before he is off it. She has not had that since he was six, and on the sixth of the third tide she stood at the top of the lane and watched him the whole way for the first time in six days. **Her fourth-day argument in the road works of the guild is a sentence of the batch: *the guild says the first of the tide, and it was the first, and it was a season, and here it is with the days not on it.***
- **Pether Voke, fifty-four**, speaker of the Slackwater assembly, ferryman's mate for thirty-one years, four generations of Gannocks and one Voke on that water. **Said the batch's one refusal, out loud, in a net loft, before thirty-one people, and was not talked out of it, and lost a tide and said the number before he started.** He is not a villain and he is not a function; he is the cost of the volume's spine and he is named and his reason holds.
- **Efa Lund, thirty-four**, keeps the shore's count of what crosses the flat in a book of her own, eleven years, no pay, not asked, and her mother did the same. Said *seventeen* and explained the difference. **Wrote the refusal on the back of a fishmonger's notice, read it out twice, handed it to the man it was refusing, and told him it is no use to anybody and that is what it is for.** She is the only person in the batch who is angry at him and she is right and she is not satisfied.
- **Hollis Thrale, sixty-one**, glassmaker at Thornglass, forty-one years at the fire, **deaf in the right ear, as his father was and as his father's father was, since the kiln was built in the year of the bad seventh, and nobody in the family has ever been asked about it.** **Right about his craft in every particular that can be tested and wrong about what a pane is for.** Right that a pane cannot be crossed out and that a stall-holder's pencil must not be fired into glass. Wrong that permanence is what a record is for: *a photograph is the only thing on this coast that cannot be argued with, and if you cannot argue with a thing then you cannot ask it anything.* **He holds a record he cannot use and the reason is that it cannot be produced: the guild's charter pane and the guild's tide table stand in the tide office at Kestrel Reach and have never been out of that building, and the pane on his own shed wall is a second tide table he made for himself three years ago and it is not the record.** His and the guild's do not agree in two columns out of eleven, the third and the eighth, the two that record a place and not a depth; his is the later one, he would swear to his, he had it on his wall for a year before he saw it, and he has known for about two years. He cannot get at the guild's to put the two side by side, and he cannot be sure his own is right, because a channel moves, and there is no instrument on that coast that would say so in a room, because the only thing that would say so in a room is a pane and the only pane in a room is theirs. **He told nobody until a man off a road stood in front of it, and then he told that.** This does not make the guild wrong about the water: the guild's people are right in their hands and the guild's glass is a photograph of a morning, so what is stale is the record. He put Ilyan down off his floor and did not ask, and let a woman of twenty-four put him down a step, and told him he had carried a bar well and that it was a thing about glass and not about him. **The man in the cold arch on the sixth of a tide is not Bettany Rook's enemy and has never been anybody's enemy and would have done it for the same money off the council.**

## Continuity note for Batch 0001

The volume's thesis is Volume 02's shape moved to a different substance and it is not Volume 02 with the water turned up: Volume 02's pressure was *a page is for keeping the record of a duty and the duty goes to whoever is standing nearest*; Volume 03's is *a crossing is a passage, the passage is a thing only somebody local can give, and the body that sells it is the only body that cannot navigate.* **Nothing in the batch is a room that declines to say anything, a round of written answers from a set of benches, a bench minute as the hero of a chapter, a public answer that turns out to be a price, or a new local arrangement with a separately recorded refusal.** The batch's spine is the first refusal of consent and it is correct, it is recorded in full on the back of a fishmonger's notice, and it costs eleven pounds of freight, two men's day, four years and a boy's thirty-nine tides. The batch's cost is people's days and one woman's five days of a stall and not a document's price. The batch's discovery is about the coast and not about a mystery: **a charter that is a pane cannot be changed for a year, and a tariff that does not name its days charges a bad day the price of a good one, and a card printed from one plate put a burden line on a road that a man who cannot read copied off the wrong half of a sheet, and the person at the end of it is eighty-one and has never been asked.** And the batch ends on a question that is not about a person, which is the first time in fifteen days that the worst question on that coast has not been about a person.

## Sera Quill
