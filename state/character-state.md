# Character State After Volume 02 Batch 0003

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; **Stage 2, Seam Reader.** His limited standing is restored and confined to the ninth-bridge matter. He may file in it and be heard in it with a named advocate; he may not be cited as a permit, a schedule, a levy, an acknowledgment, or a bearer of labor; and he must be produced at the review. **He is named, as a person with a duty and a consequence, in a charter four miles from a bridge, which he cannot refuse without the appeal being stayed — and the batch used it once, and he walked eleven miles when he was only obliged for four.**
- **The six things in the record he cannot un-enter, named with their sizes in his own hand.** Two copies of a private sequence from *The Sovereign's Wake*; **the precedent** he asked for in a corridor; **the first appeal**, which worked perfectly and reached nobody who needed it; **the carrier's clause**; and now **a figure of thirty years' work entered into a district's book without the consent of the person it is owed to, who refused it, who was not compelled, and who cannot be overrule — wrong by at least two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings and by an amount he cannot name, and written in a load-carrier's figures.** The only thing he can offer against the wrongness is that he said it was wrong before he did it. He said it in nine words on the sixteenth, in a room, in a voice like a bridgekeeper's, and he has never made a speech.
- **He is now a witness in one more matter in a district he does not live in, and a witness can be produced.** He answered a bench's question he did not need to answer. Nobody has thanked him and he did not ask, and he asked for that to be entered.
- **What the batch cost him in the body.** He took a barrow-load of a stone off a chapel flat with a woman who did not ask him, eleven loads, four hundred yards, unpaid, because the ninth bridge's tenth term says a district which pays a favour has learned to rule by habit and there was nobody to hire him. **His left hand opened in four places and is a scar at the base of his thumb in four years, and there is no line anywhere in this city that pays a man for a hand.** The leg is unchanged in kind: it clears inside a burden of his own and does not clear when he rests, it held for eleven miles of walking, and it stopped forty yards past the end of a lane.
- **The token, fourth use, and it took the words.** From the second of the third month until the sixth he could see a line of writing and could not get sense out of it. He told Corva Silt *I cannot read that* at a counter and she sat down and did not enter it. **Corva Silt is now the second person in the world who knows that Ilyan Vester cannot read, and it is not on a page.**
- **The rendering, one step further and not resolved.** It has a floor. **The vantage is a room** — boards, a table, a lamp with the wick down, a window with weather on the wrong side, four people at the table, one of them about forty-four with her back to him and her right foot up on something. He has never seen her at a table. The gate was the subject and not the place. The vantage has not moved and the table is the size of a table you could put a hand on and he is not going to explain that. The Shale Mirror question is touched and not resolved.
- **His language cost him differently and it is the same thing.** He can follow every line of a form that has ruined a man and cannot do one single thing about it, because the charter is another district's, the form is a form, and a weighhouse is not a party to anything, and the rule that stops him speaking is a rule and the rule is correct. **What he has instead of a remedy is a definition, a direction, and a woman with a barrow, and the man has to walk the lane twice.**
- **Who knows about the leg:** Sera Quill, Harrow, Roke, Ansa Rell; Tarin worked it out and said it first; Bryn Sallow still does not know. **Who knows about the reading: Corva Silt, and no clerk.**
- **His immediate want:** a procedure for what happens to a page when the holder cannot keep it; a carrier's clause with no successor; a city that has told the truth about itself in public and cannot live with the price; and his own sixth thing, which is in a stone room in a record he does not control.

## Sera Quill

- **Status:** Licensed as a recordwright of a public record and expressly not as Ilyan's advocate, on the same four terms, to the review date and after. **The review is held and she carried the terms of the matter to it, and the third term — carries the terms to the review and to any other district that asks — is what put nine sheets in a kitchen eleven miles from a bridge.**
- **What she did against him in this batch, and it is the strongest thing she has done.** She refused, in a room over a pump, to let him hold a page for a place with no district, and gave the reason in full: a holder is a person in a district and a carrier is not; a holder may not be a party and a carrier is the only person who may carry the terms; and the day he opens a page anywhere the appeal in Halloway is stayed for about four hundred people, and no instrument in this city says how a second carrier is sworn in, and nobody in this city will be able to tell him he did it. **She entered that she did not see it when she wrote the fifth term, and that a man cannot be a door in a lane and the only door at the same time, and that he would have done it if she had not been there.**
- **The two things that are hers, unchanged and not advanced.** The quarter of a sentence on the facing leaf, which has to be read as a duty and not a place, and a district where a page is open to a person, which is not the same sentence and which she wrote beside it and will not say it is. And a family claim six years old, unfiled, needing a page that may not exist, which she has still not looked for. **The prohibition on the sealed preservation-index item stands.**
- **Relationship:** professionally close and personally unaddressed. She told him in a stone room that a man who enters six things against his own name is frightened of waiting for a better room and wrong about the reason, and he did not answer, and the reason it stood is in the fourth term of a licence and neither of them ever talks about the fourth term. **No romance beat has occurred and none is planned for the rest of this volume.**

## Sel Harrow

- **Trialwright, eleven years.** He invented his **fourth procedure** in public at the review and named it as his own: how a carrier is sworn in, where an instrument says *until another carrier is sworn in that district* and no instrument says how. He said a duty to answer had frightened him and this had not and that both sentences were true and that he was more frightened of the second. He said the court has no power over any such bench and that the procedure is not precedent.
- **He refused the continuity line for the fifth time**, in the same words — *I will not rule on a line I did not enter* — entered it as an unentered line with a date, named nobody, and said he would refuse on the sixth and on the seventh. **The seventh refusal is still ahead and is a volume-level promise.**
- **He said no power over the wards twice on the face of the record** and refused to draft a word of the charter. In Chapter 79 he entered that the court has no power over the charter of the district of Halloway and takes no view, and that this is the third procedure he has invented in public and he intends to go on inventing them, and that none of them is a power and all of them are the smallest thing he can do with a record.

## Ansa Rell

- **Magistrate, forty-four, eleven years in the office. Out-voted, undefeated, and not redeemed.**
- **Her position at the end of the batch, in four sentences at a review:** a yes with a reason from a body that holds paper and seven nos with reasons from bodies that hold nothing and one bench that has changed its answer in writing because of a woman — the duty to answer has cost a woman a room and got a bench a hearing, and she did not see that coming; twenty-two names and eleven places is the standing of every court in this city and she has never been able to make a place, and the scarcity has made a public answer into a price and one desk into a monopoly without meaning to; **a city that has said out loud that it is empty is a city in which a person with a document and nowhere to put it has a problem that no bench in this city can hear, and that is not a remedy and she is going to carry the letters and is not going to pretend that carrying them is a court.**
- **She answered Wen Lask's question at the review, out loud, in full, and entered the answer as not being the answer**, and entered that the court's not-being-annoyed at the boy of seventeen is the closest this case can come to an admission and is not one.
- She told a magistrate's clerk what an honest *no* costs in one question and one answer and entered the price of the asking in the same book as the asking.

## Maren Kett

## Tarin Keel

- **Route-arbiter, neutral boundary, the field's record.** He put the early-calling question in writing on the nineteenth and answered it in writing on the twentieth: a callable early requires a named reading, and he has read it at every change since the third of the second month, and it has been inside the band every time, and he is a field man and there is no field. He still has not been asked to testify at anything and would testify under oath that he does not know who cut the fifth mark.

## Wenna Tace

- **Twenty-nine, refuse hauler, reader of her district, paid a third of a haul rate for about two hundred loads a year and **about a pound and eleven a quarter** for keeping nine sheets and a page. She holds the fifth term: she keeps the page and swears an entry in it and may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one.**
- **What the batch made her.** She is the door. About five people came to a room over a pump in one day and one of them wanted her named in an appeal and she had to say no. **Her finding, entered in the page: a holder is consulted by everybody and named by nobody, and it is one rule doing both things, and it is working, and she is the result of it.** She wrote it down herself, in the middle of the page, where she puts the things that matter.
- **What she asked for and what it cost.** On the seventh of the third month she asked a bench of four for a rate for a haul, having wanted one for eleven months, having cut the barrow out of that charter herself, and having written the want on a page so that it could not be used against her. She named three costs before asking: the rate will be set by the person who wants it; she must read it aloud at a pump at the sixth bell on the first of every quarter; and nobody in that district can appeal it. **The bench set it at the district's own rate, which is a third of a haul rate, so a haul in Halloway is two thirds short, and the hundred and fifty hauls of the last eleven months are entered as owed and not paid because the district has no money.**
- **A second barrow, entered on a page for the first time in her life.**
- **The continuity line is still under the fifth term of the copy in her room and she has still not read it**, and the reason is the whole of the batch's best small mechanism: a charter ends at the fifth term and there is nothing after it, and she reads a charter in order, and she has read the fifth term to about four hundred people and never once read past the end of a term. She told Ilyan not to read it to her and said she wanted it in a page with a date and a figure on what it cost her when somebody reads it. **Nobody in this case will tell her.**
- **The question the batch ends on, in her words:** there is no procedure in this city for what happens to a page when the holder cannot keep it, and in about four years a court is going to ask how an appeal survives in a district that is being squeezed, and the honest answer is *as long as a woman of twenty-nine can still read and haul at the same time*, and that is an answer about a person and this city cannot write a person down.

## Bryn Sallow

- **Forty-four, gate hand at the low-water culvert, thirty years in the post, one foot on a sill-stone and one on a bank.** She is owed six and a half hours a day at a third of a haul rate by name in chalk, and the thirty years before it is entered as owed and not paid.
- **A figure has been entered against her district and her thirty years for the first time in their existence: one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings. She refused it. It was entered over her refusal, in front of her, by a man she had not asked, and the refusal and the figure are on the same page in two hands, and she was not compelled and cannot be compelled and cannot be overrule.**
- **She corrected the count herself, unasked, when a man was about to write it wrong: the working tide of the turn is one day in seven and the week comes to about sixty-three hours and not seventy-three and a half.** She said she would not have it written that it was her words on his figures, and that in four years somebody would be able to say the woman supplied the tide and the man supplied the hours and neither could be checked. **Her correction is entered as hers and it took two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings off the figure.**
- **On the fourteenth she wrote both sets of hours side by side — his figures and hers, four minutes apart in a four-hour day — and under them: *TWO FIGURES, BOTH HONEST, BOTH WRONG A LITTLE. THE LINE DOWN THE MIDDLE OF THE SLATE STAYS. A PERSON IS NOT A MEASURING INSTRUMENT. I HAVE SAID THAT ON A FIELD AND IT GOES ON A PAGE AND IT IS STILL TRUE.***
- **She still does not know what is in his leg**, and she does know that his clearing is what makes her hours a number, and she has said so in front of two people, and she told him a number is a claim and a claim wants somebody to answer it and she has gone thirty years without being a claim.

## Corva Silt

- **Thirty-one, clerk of the Halloway bench for six years, holder of the district's print authority, and the archivist of her own mistake. Thirty-one, not thirty-four.**
- **She is now the second person in the world who knows that Ilyan Vester cannot read, and it is not on a page.** She sat down and put her pen down and did not enter it, and then named the four roles he is entered in — reader, carrier, the man who said a gate is not the problem, and a device in his pocket that takes his reading — and said she is not going to be that second head for free and is not going to refuse either, and if the day comes when the day comes that is what is in her head and he will have to decide whether to be in the room when she says it. **She then sat him down on a step and read four sheets out loud to herself in a loud voice in her own office, because a bench has never read a line of a figure aloud.**
- **She is the archivist of the ninth sheets**: the copy in the room over a pump is short of the second half of the fourth term and she has entered why, in her own hand, that a clerk does not copy a man's name into a district's paper when a district has not asked for a man.
- **She set four times in eleven days against nine yards' money and sent the docket himself, in his own name, asking for it to be accounted for.** She has still asked to be relieved of the print authority five times in six weeks and been refused five times and will not ask a sixth.

- **Meridith Vale, clerk of the ninth-bridge's district, nineteen years.** His fund decided the review: **four hundred and ten a year, two hundred and ten for two hands, fifteen for the telling, forty-four pounds and sixteen for the charter, ninety-six for four props, forty-four pounds and four shillings left, fifty-one pounds and sixteen short.** He bought the props, took the old four out, and entered that **he did not ask the keeper of the fourth bridge and takes no view and would not have known what to do with the answer.** He has never once entered a false reason.

## Continuity note

Volume 02's third movement exercises the machinery and finds out what it is worth. **The carrier's clause was used once, for a district that never asked for the charter and could not pay for the paper, and the using cost a man eleven miles he was not obliged to walk, a woman a day of her time, a district a crooked sheet of paper, and about four hundred people a door that now has a name on it in one place and a four-mile line on it in another.** A word printed at the top of a form took a working man's season, because the word is not a label on a rule but a claim that the rule is temporary, and the charter it was summarising has no end on it. A holder became a door with no room behind it, because one rule keeps her out of appeals and puts her in front of everybody. A debt of thirty years got a figure and the figure is wrong, and the woman refused it and was not compelled and cannot be overrule. A city that said out loud it holds nothing found that a public answer is a price and that its one *yes* is a yes about paper. A place of sixty-one people found that the only page it could be given would have to be held by the man whose holding it closes a door four miles away. **And a public maintenance review in the open air decided a sum, bought four new timbers for a bridge that has wanted four for twenty-nine years, opened that bridge's deck in the weather, and took four hours a day away from a man of twenty-two whose name nobody had entered anywhere.** The appeal is in use and is not secured. The Quiet Chamber's quarter sentence and the page over a pump are side by side in a record and are not the same sentence. The Bridgeward has been asked a question twice, answered once, and the road has not been touched. Ansa Rell is out-voted, undefeated, and a difficult ally. The continuity line has spoken seven times and been refused five. **No new enemy has been introduced and the planned ending is untouched.**
