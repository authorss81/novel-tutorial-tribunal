# Character State — end of Chapter 480, Volume 10 Batch 0003

**This block supersedes everything below it. The Volume 09 block below is history and is in git at `90c3efd` and `1e8801a`.** Chapters 1–450 are canon and closed. Chapters 451–480 are canon. **Nobody has been relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked.** In this band the miller was right about a hundred and seventy households nobody had asked him about, the county page is still the only authority and still has no power, the steward was right three times in a form and was not relieved, the chair of the water board was right about the cause and wrong about nothing and is not thanked, **the nine men of the board who put nineteen houses out of the water were right, and four of them said out loud that they wished it were not so, and the man of thirty-four who paid two pounds for a lot was owed a hearing and got one that could not answer him.**

**DATES.** No month exists in this volume's calendar. **The town keeps no year; its own reckoning is days from the division, `hall = chapter − 446`, and the days are printed as days.** A *year* survives only as a spoken span and as a form of words in a cause set out in writing. ## 1. What 471–480 changed

**ILYAN VESTER** (**thirty-one**; co-witness, bad leg, cracked steel pen, cold bitter tea, **two hundred and thirtieth morning at 480**; **day 28 of his time in Halloway**). Want, mistake and cost of his own in every chapter, and **THE OWED INSTRUCTION IS DISCHARGED A SECOND TIME: in `477` and in `478` something he did is not the thing that goes wrong.** `471`–`475` are as logged in the previous block; `476`–`480`:

- **`476` — HE IS THE ENGINE AND HE IS WRONG, AND HE IS RIGHT.** He said in a room with about thirty people in it, and not in a lane, the sentence *a household is a house* — **which is Coll Sarn's, said at a wool stall in the rain on the twelfth day after the division, and which he repeated because the boy is on nobody's book and cannot be asked about a word he used at a stall.** It became the ground of a board's finding. He gave the reason himself: the row's case in that room rested on the word *household* being a person, and he said it better than the row would have said it, and nobody had asked him to. **Cost: it is the third time in six days that a sentence of his has been the ground of something, and nineteen houses are out of the water, and the cause was correct and the board was right and the reason it worked is a boy of twenty-two who cannot be asked about it.**
- **`477` — HIS MISTAKE IS NOT THE ENGINE.** The engine is a letter he did not write. **His cost is a second sheet.** Folded in four, nothing on the outside, out of a bag, in the hand he asked her not to use: six years, a refusal, a household dead except one person, a claim not entered because a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it. **He read it in a room over a nail shop on the Wednesday night and carried it three days and did not say what it was, and about nine people in a shop watched him not say it.** He has now said it out loud in a market square in about four minutes and it cannot be un-read. **And he named the four instruments of Halloway in a square on the Friday morning — a miller's second book, a copyist, a minute, a woman who refuses things in lanes — and he is on none of them, and the naming is what shut the first one.**
- **`478` — HIS MISTAKE IS NOT THE ENGINE.** The engine is a man of thirty-nine with a counter being right. **His sentence of Friday is the reason the book is shut, and a woman of thirty-four tells him out loud, in a shop with about nine people in it, that being un-askable is a hole and not a gift, and that he has been spending it like a purse for twenty-six days.** **Cost: the only instrument in Halloway that has never been in a room is shut, and about a hundred and seventy households of this town are in it and do not know.**
- **`479` — HE SAID NOTHING FOR FOUR HOURS AND THAT WAS CORRECT.** He had been asked for his view three times in twenty-seven days and had said nothing in the first and everything in the second and a number in the third. **He wanted the sentence to be his and it was a nail in a shed and a woman of thirty with a child on her hip said it in the right words, and about two hundred people heard it, and it does not die with him.** He said one fact at the fourth hour, and it was the only correct thing said in that square in four hours, and a number on the back of a hand dies with the hand.
- **`480` — HE AGREED, IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS, TO BE READ OUT OF A ROOM HE WAS NOT ASKED TO LEAVE.** A copyist asked him to stay out of the Stone House on the Sunday morning so that she could read the ninth of eleven lines — which is him — in a room he was not standing in. **He said yes and did not come further than the doorway, and about nine people in that room heard a man of thirty-one agree to be a line on a list, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may be read about in a room he is not standing in and none that says he may not.** **Cost: the best thing anybody has done for him in twenty-eight days is a reading, and he cannot be asked about it afterwards, because he is not in the room and nobody in the room can be asked what it was like.**

**SEFA LUND** (38, keeps the sluice at the Fen, nine years, at the fourth house of nineteen, **never paid**, a bad hip, a key to gates that are open, **and out of the water as of the thirtieth day after the division**). **THE OWED INSTRUCTION IS NOT RESET AND IS NOT UNDONE.** In `463` she stated her rule and was given the other half of it and did not take it; in `467` she acted on the half she had not taken, was wrong in her own mouth, and paid for it, and the corrected count did not go on the paper. **In `473` she is not reset: she is the woman who turned out to have been the only person keeping an account of the row.** She says, in her own mouth, in a kitchen, in front of about eleven people: *keeping it is a claim, and I said in a lane on the eleventh day that I did not want to be a person with a claim, and a form was put in four sentences in front of about nine people and I refused it, and that is the only thing I have ever wanted out of nine years of turning that bar.* **The accounts are made of what the row has been told and by whom and on what day, four men and four accounts and three weeks and eleven other rows in this town, and nobody made the claim, and she cannot give it up, and the only person who could hold it is a copyist paid two pounds a year who has entered sixteen things nobody asked her to enter.** **In `476` she is named in a minute of the water board for the second time in this town — the first was the attestation on the back of the county's statement on the seventeenth day, which she agreed to — and the second is a requirement, which she did not agree to, and she says it in a square in about four sentences, and the word *required* is on the line and it is the first time that word has been in a minute of this town in four hundred years. And she says the ninth clause out of Wenna Crale's head in that room, out loud, and the Book does not come down.** `473`, `476`, `480`

**WENNA CRALE** (34, copyist to the House, eleven years, £2 a year, **the only public officer of Halloway that is paid anything**). **THE EIGHTH OF TEN THINGS IS HER, AND SHE SAYS SO IN A SQUARE.** In `471` she put the sheet of ten things into the press a day earlier than the chair named, said so out loud, was refused the eight by the chair, and read all ten out herself in a market square at the fourth hour — and the eighth of them was that a copyist has entered eleven things no body of this town asked her to enter, which is true. She gave the days, one at a time, and the reason: *a person not of this town cannot be entered in a book, and I am the only person in this town that can be asked about any of this, and a thing nobody can be asked about is a thing that does not happen.* **She has now entered the second and third corrections of the roll, read out nine documents, added an eleventh line to the sheet in her own hand and a different ink, and asked a man of thirty-one to stay out of a room so that she could read him out in it. She says the reason: a man in a room is a man the room can be asked about, and a man in a room can say no.** Nobody thanked her. `471`, `475`, `477`, `479`, `480`

**PERDY SALLOW** (47, chair of the water board, nine members, nine years). **The twenty-fifth day after the division came and went and he did not call the board, and he said out loud in a market square that he could not tell whether he was a man who kept a courtesy or a man who built a wall on the sixteenth day and wrote the word courtesy on it so that he could sleep, and that there is no form in the county of Ostrey for a day.** Said the reason the out-of-roll book is shut: *an answer is a minute, a minute is a use, and the day this board uses that book the board owns it.* **On the thirtieth day he put a requirement in a minute, named a woman in it, and then struck the line out of the minute in front of thirty people because the person required is a household of this town and clause nine binds her, and said the reason: he had wanted that line since the sixteenth day and could not have it.** He is right every time and the rightness does the damage. `471`, `476`, `477`, `479`

**AMOS TRILL** (61, steward of Halloway forty years, **caused clause one to be struck and was right about it and says so**). **Did not call the House on its own covenant, and gave the best reason in the volume: he has been forty years the only man in this town nobody had to wonder what he wanted, and it is not a virtue on a form, it is the only power he has ever had, and there is a clause on the back of the board that would have stopped what he did four weeks ago and he has had eighteen days to go and look at it and will not, because looking at it puts him in a room where he has to say what he did, to forty-one men and a copyist.** **Corrected the roll a second time on the twenty-eighth day, on eleven rows nobody asked him about, and said in a room that nobody asked him to do it and that nobody will be able to say he should not have, and that is exactly what he said about the first one.** Corrected it a third time on the thirtieth day in about four minutes, in the same form, the other way, and the third line of the fenrow's own request is cited by name in the correction. He has said out loud that asking for nothing is a thing that can be used by anybody who is not careful. `474`, `475`, `476`

**ORDWIN CAPE** (61, the fourth house of nineteen, kept the sluice twenty-two years, cut reed thirty years, **a county pension of eleven shillings a year he still cannot hand over**). **Wrote the cause that expelled the row.** On a bill, on the Sunday evening, with a day on it, and nobody asked him: *in the ninth year before the division the water stood above the sill of the houses at the Fen for eleven days.* He was there; his mother was in the fourth house; the water came up the stairs to the level of the third. **Three houses were above the sill and he has not said which three, and said in the room that he will say it if anybody asks and will say it in that room.** He asked for it to be in the minute that he was not asked and that he wrote it, *because if it is ever found out that I was asked then this paper is worth nothing to anybody and it is the only thing I have got.* He is right and he is not thanked. `476`, `480`

**IVO CRAY** (**34**, **NEW IN 471–480**, of the ninth lot on the Four Hundred, paid two pounds for it in the week of the division, has a bill of sale in his coat). **The first person in this volume who is neither the fenrow nor the stranger nor the steward nor the board, and who wants something for himself.** Walked four hundred yards up a lane with a bill of sale in his coat and asked whether the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward belongs to him, and about twenty people on a stair heard a schedule read out that says *and the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, and the race, and the sluice, and the two closes below it* in the same hand and the same size of writing as five acres of lot nine. **He is owed a hearing and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets him be given one.** He asked the steward to call the House and was refused. **He is the first of the majority in this business to find out what it is worth**: he did the sum out loud in a market square and said that if the bar is not turned on the Thursday morning then it is not a season, it is a year. `472`, `474`, `476`, `479`

**MAB HARROW** (34, washes at the sluice, rents the second room of the fourth house, **not a head of household, so never in the House, for eleven years**). **She is the one who says the other half of it, out loud, in a shop with about nine people in it, and the cost of it is that she has been carrying four accounts of a row all week and adding them up wrong.** On the twenty-fifth day after the division she gave him the first half — *you are the only person in this county that nobody can be sent for, and the half of that which is a gift is that you can walk into any room* — and said she had not decided the other half. **In `478` she gave the other half: when you have gone out of the room there is nobody in it who was sent for, and that is not the same room, and that half was true on the Friday morning and it has got truer every day since, and on Wednesday it put nineteen houses out of the water in four minutes in a form that nobody had to be asked about. It is a hole and it is not a gift, and I have watched you spend it like a purse.** `471`, `473`, `478`

**COLL SARN** (22, born in the ninth house of nineteen, up off the low ground at twenty, four beds in this town and none of them his own, has never been asked what he is). **Said a household is a house, in a market square in the rain, on the twelfth day after the division, and Ilyan Vester repeated it in a room on the thirtieth day because the boy cannot be asked about it afterwards, and it became the ground of a board's finding.** He is the one who holds the count — nineteen doors and twenty-two households, on a bill, since the fifteenth day. He asked a man of thirty-nine, in his own shop, why a book of nine hundred people cannot come out, and was answered. `464`, `473`, `476`, `478`

**GARRICK NYE** (44, of the county). **Not in this band and not moved.** His page is the only authority on the frame and it is twenty-two miles off and the office has no power. **He was quoted in a square on the thirtieth day as the reason a season is a season, by a man of thirty-four, and the board came to the same figure from a flood.** `469`, `480`

**OTT VERNAY** (39, four years at the mill, **about nine hundred names in a book of his own, with a mark against each**). **Said no a second time and gave a better reason: you told a town of four thousand people in a square on Friday morning that I am a public instrument, and a public instrument is a thing that gets opened.** *I was one of the four and I have not been able to be one since Friday morning, and I have not been able to be one because you said it out loud.* And: *about a hundred and eighty dashes, and about a hundred and seventy of them are not the fenrow, and every one of them is a house that has been paying me for four years off the water list and has never once known there was a question about it.* `461`, `467`, `478`

**SERA QUILL** (28, recordwright, **four hundred miles off**). **Her answer of the twenty-third day came into Halloway on the twenty-ninth in a bag and is four pages in the round hand of the counters, which he asked her not to use, and it is addressed to the water board of Halloway and not to a man, and it is not signed with her own name.** She did not open the range and says she has written down eleven times in six years that she has not opened it and that nobody has ever asked her why. **Her argument, which is right: an exit is a right when the person it is for can open it without asking anybody's character, and that is not a sentence but a thing with hinges on it, and there is no such thing in the building she sits in or in any of the nine she has worked in.** **And there is a second sheet, folded in four, with nothing on the outside of it, in the small upright hand, about a refusal given six years ago by a household that is dead except her, and a claim that has not been entered because a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it and that person is not findable, and that she has not gone and looked for it herself, and that if she looks and the name is in the range then her name goes on the claim as the person presenting it.** She is not redeemed by any of this and her family’s claim of six years is no longer only hers. `477`
