## 1. What 451–460 changed

**ILYAN VESTER** (**thirty-one**; he says thirty-one and the chapters narrate him as a man of thirty-one; co-witness, bad leg, cracked steel pen, cold bitter tea, two hundred and ten mornings). He came to a town to be a man on no paper and was a man on no paper for four hours. Want, mistake and cost his own in every chapter, and **the mistake is a worse one in every chapter than the one before**:

- **`451`** said out loud in a room of about thirty that he wants a town where his name is not on anything, and that the price of the sixth want is that he is not going to get it, and then gave away in four seconds the one fact that would have let the woman who asked him leave. She said he should not have said it in a room. **Cost: eleven days he could have had.**
- **`452`** read a two-hundred-word notice out in a market square to forty-one people because a man he had met ninety seconds earlier asked him to, and afterwards said out loud that reading a notice is what makes a stranger somebody's man in a town. **Cost: a town that knows his name, which is the thing he came for.**
- **`453`** asked to be one of the two hands on the board, was refused in front of the board, and asked for the refusal to be written down. **Cost: he watched a book he needed from nine feet up for four days.**
- **`454`** put a figure into a market because he cannot leave a figure alone, and had to take it apart in the same square on the same day, and the correct figure is that he does not know it.
- **`455`** wrote out, for nineteen houses, a sentence about what they are owed that they had not asked for, and a woman of thirty-eight struck his fourth paragraph out in her own hand and handed the paper back.
- **`456`** said out loud, in a room of sixteen, the date the right of refusal was taken out of the law, and a woman asked him how he knows, and he could not answer in a way anybody in that room could check. **The word *unverified* is now in a minute of a water board because of him.**
- **`457`** offered to carry the objection of nineteen houses, and was told in a lane, in front of about eleven people, that a man who links people's accounts does not speak for a witness — in the words he had said himself in another city a week before — and **asked her to say it in the lane and not to him, and it went into the minute.** **He has now broken his own stage's law twice and said so both times.**
- **`458`** offered to pay a mill bill out of his own pocket in a shop with nine people in it before anybody asked him, and named the source of the money in a town that has never heard of the forms it comes from. **Cost: two hundred seconds in a market town's shop.**
- **`459`** was right, loudly, and the rightness did the damage: a clause four is alive in a fair copy, and the word in it is *household*, and the fenrow are not households, and the clause that is supposed to be the answer is the one that shuts the door. **His self-count in that chapter is five errors in seven days and his own list of them is five, and the chapter used to say three in nine.**
- **`460`** was asked for his view in an open square and said he had no standing and said nothing, and asked that the minute record that he said nothing. **Cost: the House did it without him, in the fenrow's name, in about nine seconds.**

**WENNA CRALE** (34, copyist to the House, eleven years, **£2 a year and the only public officer of Halloway that is paid anything at all**). Learned the old hand from her grandmother, dead nine years, who learned it off a fair copy, so **there are three people between the hand of the Book and anybody who can use it.** Turned the board over in front of nine men without asking anybody, and read the ninth clause in about two minutes. Found the second margin on the Friday and told nobody for two days because she did not believe it. **Refused his number and told him to send it, and said out loud that in this town a thing in a book is a thing a person can be asked about and that this is the one thing here worth having.** Entered the fourth line of a notice nobody had sent for, because a stranger read it out. `451`–`460`

**AMOS TRILL** (61, steward of Halloway forty years, **caused clause one to be struck and was right about it and said so to a room, and would make the arguments again**). Has held the out-of-roll book for forty years and never once been the man who used it, and says so, and says the reason: a steward nobody can predict what he wants is the only power he has ever had. **Declined, in a public square, to enter the fenrow on the roll, in their own name, in about nine seconds, and said he had held the answer since the Friday.** Entered both margins and entered the stranger's sentence under the word *unverified*. Entered the clause that this House has not the means to verify it. `452`–`460`

**SEFA LUND** (38, keeps the sluice at the Fen, nine years, **at the fourth house of nineteen**, **never paid, because a common is not a service**, a bad hip, and a key to gates that are shut). Told a stranger straight away that she would not shout it in a square, and gave the reason, and she was right. **Refused the out-of-roll entry in front of about nine people, and was right to, and then said out loud in front of two hundred people that she did not agree to not have a household.** Wound a shut gate twice a day for nine days, which is a thing nobody in that country has to do. **THE REVIEW'S FINDING, RECORDED AND NOT REPAIRED: she is right in every appearance and pays no cost anywhere in the ten chapters, which makes her an instrument rather than a person. Batch 0002 must give her one thing she is wrong about, or one thing the refusal costs her.** `452`–`460`

**MAB HARROW** (34, washes at the sluice, rents the second room of the fourth house, **not a head of household, so never in the House, for eleven years**). Asked the three questions of the week, each one about four words long: *who is it for*, *say how you know*, and *whose*. Refused credit in a shop by a man who did not know her and said he would have refused anybody. **Her questions are the band and they were not asked by Ilyan.** `455`–`459`

**PERDY SALLOW** (47, chair of the water board, nine members). Offered nineteen households the out-of-roll book and was refused in a lane, and then said in about four sentences that he had made an offer that was no offer, and had been doing it wrong since the year before last, and that the only reason it had taken this long is that nobody had anything to sell him. Said out loud in a wet lane of two hundred people that the roll for next year is made in the autumn and that anything not on it and not in the book is not a household of this town, and then **refused to put it in a minute because the minute is the only thing this town has ever used against anybody.** `456`, `457`

**GARRICK NYE** (44, of the county, has scoured the Hallow twice, nineteen years and nine years ago). Right about everything he measured. Put his thumb two inches into the heel of a frame and said two years, and said he has never said a thing was a year away unless he had a way of stopping it, and that he has not got one. Named the thing only a clerk can do. `455`–`457`

**CULLEN SKAY** (19, sixth house of nineteen, carries a roll under her coat in the rain because her grandmother cannot come out). Would not let a stranger read her roll, and was told, and did not have to say why. `452`, `456`, `457`

**ORDWIN CAPE** (61, fourth house of nineteen, kept the sluice twenty-two years before Sefa Lund, cut reed thirty years, **a county pension of eleven shillings a year**). Did the sum out loud in a lane in front of about thirty people and was thirty-nine pence short, and said he has never once been asked to hand any of it to nineteen houses and would do it and had told nobody until about four seconds ago. `458`

**OTT VERNAY** (39, four years at the mill, **nine hundred names on a book**). Refused credit to a woman he could not find in a roll, in a shop with nine people in it, said why out loud, and said the mill would not shut. **A refusal by a man with no standing whatever who is right, and the third of the band.** `458`

**DOFF ANSTEY** (55, carrier, thirty-one years on that road, will not guess at a man he drives for in a square). Said the milestones stop nine miles out and the last one has a letter on the north face and nothing on the south face. `452`
