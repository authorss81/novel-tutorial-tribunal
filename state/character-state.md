# Character State After Volume 02 Batch 0004

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; **Stage 2, Seam Reader.** His limited standing is restored and confined to the ninth-bridge matter. He is named, as a person with a duty and a consequence, in a charter four miles from a bridge, **and the carrier's clause has now been used twice: once for a district eleven miles off and once for a bench of three. Past the fourth mile the clause is a favour and a favour cannot be entered, and both times he walked further than he was obliged and was paid nothing.**
- **The seven things in the record he cannot un-enter, named with their sizes in his own hand.** The first six are as stated before. **The seventh, entered on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month, at the second bell, in a room over a pump, with a clerk writing it down: he has been produced as a witness a second time, in a district he does not live in, about a number he put in a bench's book without the consent of the person it was owed to. A witness can be produced twice and can be produced three times. He was asked three questions in advance by a clerk who has read four sheets of his own figures aloud in her own office by herself, and he was told them in advance, and that is not what a witness usually gets. And, entered against himself and costing: from the twenty-sixth of the fourth month he is not counting the gate hand's tide, and the number stands, and it is wrong, and the only man who could have made it more right by counting more carefully has stopped.** He asks that it be entered that he stopped for a reason that is about his own body and not about her, that he believes that, and that he would like it entered that he believes it. There is no line anywhere in this city that pays a man for having been produced.
- **What stopping cost him in the body.** Counting was a burden of his own and the only thing that ever cleared the gate hand's weight out of his leg. **The leg is his own now and does not clear.** The low water is in two layers with a third under both, the door in the picture is at the same height and the same distance it has always been, and the rendering is unchanged in kind and unresolved: a room, boards, a table the size of a table a hand could go on, a lamp with the wick down, a window with weather on the wrong side of it, and four people at the table, one of them about forty-four with her back to him and her right foot up on something. **The token is not touched. The vantage has not moved. The Shale Mirror is not touched.**
- **What he said at a door in Fallowditch, which is the batch's one piece of new knowledge from him:** there is one man in this city that four hundred people can be sent for, and he is not a bench and he cannot make anybody answer, and nobody has ever told anybody what happens to that when he cannot walk. It is not in a charter, not in a bench's book, and not in the nine sheets he carried up a lane, and he does not know. **A clerk of that bench wrote four days later in her own book that a sentence very like it is written in a clerk's own book in a chamber in Orison on the twenty-eighth of March, and that nobody in this case has read it, including the man who said the thing.**
- **Who knows about the leg:** Sera Quill, Harrow, Roke, Ansa Rell; Tarin worked it out and said it first; **Bryn Sallow still does not know, and on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month she told him at a gate that she has been counting her own tide in her own feet since she was fourteen and that there is no slate and that she would have gone on with it.** Who knows about the reading: Corva Silt, and no clerk.
- **His immediate want:** what a page is for, which a hauler of twenty-nine asked out loud in front of two hundred people and which nobody in this city can answer; and what happens to a right whose receiving office is his own back, which he has now said out loud at a door and cannot say again anywhere.
- **His standing did not grow.** He is not a party, not a bench, not a body, and he may not be cited as anything. He is not rewarded for carrying a document: he was paid nothing, the second walk was a favour, and the Fallowditch rule was arrived at by a bench of three that had never read the charter it wanted.

## Sera Quill

- **Licensed as a recordwright of a public record and expressly not as Ilyan's advocate, on the same four terms, after the review.** **The third term is what put nine sheets in a kitchen eleven miles off a bridge in the last batch and nine sheets in a room over a chandler's shop in this one, and she refused to send him and gave the reason in full: a recordwright's carrier is a thing she is and not a thing she hands to the man the fourth term names, and if he goes he goes because a clause with his name in it says he goes, and those are two different sentences and she will not let a ledger read the second as the first.**
- **The batch close belongs to her and it is a question and not an answer.** A hauler of twenty-nine sent her a letter with her name at the bottom of it asking what a page is for, and she read it, and did not answer it, and put a licence with four terms on a table and read the fourth one twice. **The fourth term says she takes no matter of her own while she holds this one, and nobody has ever asked her whether that term is about matters or about questions.** She wrote four lines in the back of her own book, in her own hand, with a date on it, that are in no record but hers, and among them this: *a question is not a matter. a matter is a claim, a subject, a burden, a witness, a remedy and an exit* — and that the answer to the question is in a room four miles from where she is standing, and that the page she is looking for is a page of her own and she has not looked for it in six years, and that tonight the question is about a page of somebody else's, and that is not the same thing, and she wants it written down that she knows the difference. **She did not go out and did not open the book.**
- **The two things that are hers, unchanged and not advanced.** The quarter of a sentence on the facing leaf, which has to be read as a duty and not a place; and a family claim six years old, unfiled, needing a page that may not exist, which she has still not looked for. **The prohibition on the sealed preservation-index item stands.**
- **Relationship:** professionally close and personally unaddressed. **No romance beat has occurred and none is planned for the rest of this volume.**

## Ansa Rell

- **Magistrate, forty-four, eleven years in the office. Out-voted, undefeated, and not redeemed.**
- **Her position at the end of this batch, in one speech at the First Verdict on the twenty-fifth of the fourth month, in the open air:** a city can afford eleven places and has always been able to afford eleven places, and what it cannot afford is the work that makes a matter ready; a body that pays a person to prepare the paper has bought the paper; a rate that has to come out of a bucket gets read aloud four times a year by a woman with a barrow who is not allowed to appeal from one word of it, **and that is not a future, it is the nineteenth of this month, and there is a book about it and a man's name in the book twice.** She said that the work is done by people who are not paid, not named, cannot be produced, and whose names are in no column anywhere. **She voted for the clean instrument in March, was out-voted four to one, said the whole of the case out loud, was not defeated and is not redeemed, and said this is the same speech in a different month and she is not going to pretend it is smaller.**

## Maren Kett

- **Of the Orison chamber, nineteen years.** Named advocate of the first appeal, **argued against his own client for three hours**, knew the outcome on the day he took the brief and took it anyway, and then was not in this case again from the seventeenth of the second month to the twenty-fifth of the fourth.
- **He asked the question nobody had thought of asking: what does it cost, in money, to make a matter ready, and who pays it.** The answer is a record, and the record is that the whole of twenty-two names on a docket board in four months has cost less than ninety-six pounds of timber.
- **What he paid, and it is not a name on a board: a counsel who enters a question in the yard is not a witness to it, cannot be examined on it, cannot be served with it, and cannot carry it to the people who wrote the words, because a question of his does not travel without a party and there is no party in the matter.** The board went to twenty-one on the fourteenth of the third month when Abel Kearn withdrew his own name and back to twenty-two on the twenty-fifth of the fourth, and it has been twenty-two for nineteen years.
- He is a **counsel**, not a clerk, and is right about the limitation and right about what the limitation costs.

## Wenna Tace

- **Twenty-nine, refuse hauler, reader of her district, paid a third of a haul rate for about two hundred loads a year and about a pound and eleven a quarter for keeping nine sheets and a page. She holds the fifth term: she keeps the page and swears an entry in it and may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one.**
- **What the batch made her do.** She asked, in public, in front of a bench of four, whether the rate for a haul could be appealed by any person, and was told no — it was set by a bench of four, the only person who asked for it is the person who asked, and a holder may not be a party or appeal or appear in support or be named. **The answer is in the district's own book and will be read out at the pump four times a year, and she did not get the money and she got the sentence, and she said so out loud and wanted it read that way.** She then took on a second reader herself, a boy of nineteen who is not a holder and is not a clerk and is not paid and was not asked, **because a page with one pair of eyes on it is a page that cannot be checked.**
- **What she wrote into her own page on the fourth of the fourth month, and got read back to her by a boy who cannot fully read it:** *an appeal is a thing a person carries. a rate is a thing a person is inside of. I have to stand on this bank four times a year and read it out and I am allowed to say whatever I like and nobody has to answer me, and that is not an appeal and nobody in this city has a name for it.* And: *I did not get the money. I got the sentence.*
- **The batch close is hers.** At the sixth bell on the twenty-ninth, on a bank, with about two hundred people behind her: **nobody has ever told me what this page is for** — not what it may be used for, which she has worked out twice a week in about four minutes, but what it is *for*. **She sent it to a recordwright at her own cost, out of a pound and eleven a quarter, with her own name at the top of the letter and the recordwright's name at the bottom, because she is the holder and may not be in a support of anything and is not going to start in a letter.**
- **The continuity line is still under the fifth term of the copy in her room and she has still not read it**, and the reason is still the whole of the best small mechanism in this case: a charter ends at the fifth term and she reads a charter in order.
- She is **not rescued, not healed, and not given a procedure by a man.** Everything she did in this batch, she did herself, and she paid for it.

## Bryn Sallow

- **Forty-four, gate hand at the low-water culvert, thirty years in the post, one foot on a sill-stone and one on a bank.** She is owed six and a half hours a day at a third of a haul rate by name in chalk, and the thirty years before it is entered as owed and not paid.
- **The thirty years now has three figures and the one in her own coin is hers: six thousand hauls at two thirds, which she offered at a gate in front of a barrow and a chain and asked to have read out four times a year, because she would like to be six thousand of anything and has never once in her life been six thousand of anything.** She got the figure the only way a person at a gate can get one, by watching the barrow go past about two hundred times a year for thirty years, and she says out loud that there is no arithmetic in the county that turns ten and a half hours into a barrow. The other two are one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds as entered and one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds after her own correction, and none of the three can be paid and none of them makes another. **The hundred and fifty hauls are NOT hers: they are Wenna Tace's own unpaid hauls of the last eleven months, and the two debts are now read out side by side at the same pump in the same unwritable unit.**
- **She went into the room over a pump for the first time in thirty years**, having heard from a boy who cannot read that there was a sum on a slate about her thirty years, **and she was not angry at a number. She was angry at Oakes, and she said the reason out loud: for thirty years she had been the ward, and a ward is a duty, and a duty cannot be answered, and that was the only thing that had ever protected her, and a bench of four and a man from a bridge took it off her in three weeks, and she has to be angry at a person about it for the first time in her life.** She was not thanked and she did not ask.
- **She still does not know what is in his leg.** She knows his counting was the reason her hours were a number, and she told him so, and on the twenty-sixth she told him he has made himself a man who cannot be produced on a number, and then told him the other thing, which he did not ask for: she has been counting her own tide in her own feet since she was fourteen, there is no slate, and she would have gone on with it.
- **She stopped working the gate for four seconds when she heard the thirty years read out twice from two hundred feet away, which she does not do.**

## Corva Silt

## Continuity note

Volume 02's fourth movement is about durability, money, and the exhaustion of a right that depends on one poor district's goodwill. **A clerk of the ninth bridge's district sent a bill instead of a notice, and the bill said that a poor district's law is paid for by a bridge's road fund four miles away, and that a hundred and five pounds has come back in a line it cannot be moved into. A man of twenty-two was told at the cut banks, in Sedge's own mouth, that he was not let go, and that the loss looks recoverable and is not, and that there is no job. A woman of twenty-nine asked in public whether a rate she asked for could be appealed, and was told no, and got the sentence instead of the money, and took on a boy of nineteen as a second pair of eyes at her own cost. A man who sells salt withdrew his consent to a rate and could not undo it and cannot un-say it, and a rule that this batch made says his name will be read out at a pump four times a year by the woman who cannot appeal from it. A bench of three eleven miles off a fen road built a room of its own and stopped doing its boundary. A bench of four found that the only durable thing in a poor district is a book a bench can come back with, and that what it has is a list of things it owes. A counsel of nineteen years asked the question nobody had thought of asking and found that a place on a docket board costs less than four timbers and that this city has no line on which a person is paid for reading, and that the only change on the ground in three weeks was a woman of sixty-one walking four miles for water. And Ilyan stopped counting a gate hand's tide, and the number he had put in her book stands, and it is wrong, and the leg that the counting cleared is his own now and does not clear, and at the sixth bell on a mud bank a hauler of twenty-nine asked the first question anybody has asked out loud in four months in this city: nobody has ever told me what this page is for.** The appeal is in use and is not secured. The Quiet Chamber's quarter sentence and the page over a pump are still side by side in a record and are not the same sentence, and nothing in this batch is better than that. The Bridgeward has not been touched. Ansa Rell is out-voted, undefeated, and a difficult ally. The continuity line has spoken nine times and been refused six. **No new enemy has been introduced and the planned ending is untouched.**
