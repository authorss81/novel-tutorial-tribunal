# character state

The cast of volume 21 at the end of band 5 (`1041`–`1050`). Every name here was made
in a mouth in an earlier chapter of this volume. On these ten pages the names arrive in
descriptor lines: no name is spoken by anybody inside a quotation on any of the ten.

## Band 5 — what each wanted, refused, gave and withheld

- **Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, nobody's.** Want to be somebody, unchanged. Asked three
  things on the page himself, and no more: the barrow question (`1046`), leave to
  explain the asking (`1047`), and an hour with the broom (`1050`). All three were
  refused with a working. Refused one: Sena Dorr's asking for his account (`1042`), with
  her own working handed back to her. Mistakes of his own, in his mouth: wanting the
  confirming to finish it (`1041`), wanting to step in and smooth a clean refusal
  (`1043`), thinking a man's refusal was about him (`1044`), wanting her to have the
  chair so the landing would be easier (`1045`), wanting to explain the asking
  (`1047`), wanting to thank her (`1048`), thinking the boy should have been allowed
  because he liked him for asking plainly (`1049`), being glad in a small way to be the
  man who could refuse (`1042`). Cost: what the Saturday of week 171 took, carried
  without a shape and without a figure in any wording.
- **Barnaby Crove, fifty-four, cannot read.** Asked his sixth-hour question every
  morning, unanswered. On `1046` was asked the barrow question by Ilyan and refused it
  to his face in his own words: he will say neither mine nor not mine, because either
  saying puts him in it and he is not in it. Was told neither wrong nor right, and
  nobody shaped it afterwards. On `1047` refused Ilyan leave to explain the asking,
  because an explaining is another thing to carry with no way of setting it down. Kept
  his hands on wall and shafts and did not soften either refusal.
- **Wenna Pyle, thirty-eight.** Confirmed on `1041` what the wood is and that she
  confirms rather than hands over, settling nothing. Refused bench time to Garrin and
  re-saying to Wat, each with a working from her own hold. Asked one question on `1050`
  and got nothing.
- **Orla Fennimore, forty-four.** Asked the chair again on `1045` and was refused with
  Corvin's working; said she will stop asking. Said the ordinary form on `1048` from the
  wall, forestalling thanks first, and refused Sena Dorr leave to enter it in her book,
  with the working that a said thing is not a checked thing. On `1049` she said nothing
  at all and the saying stayed where she had said it.
- **Corvin Tace, forty-three.** Gave three figures, never a fifth; two absences on
  off-mornings in words of his own. Refused Garrin's leave for the drain, Wat's yoke
  move, and Ilyan's broom time, each with a stone reason, and told Wat that settling it
  with himself was not the same as settling it with him.
- **Simon Rook, about fifty-two.** List empty throughout. Asked a question on six of
  the ten mornings (`1043`, `1044`, `1046`, `1047`, `1049`, `1050`) and got nothing
  every time; kept his hands off the wood, and on `1042` came up after the others had
  gone and did not ask at all.
- **Sena Dorr, forty-nine.** Asked for Ilyan's account on `1042` and was refused by the
  man himself, with her own working handed back. Asked on `1048` to enter the chair
  saying in her book and was refused by the woman who said it. Book shut throughout.
- **Garrin Tolley, thirty-eight.** Asked for bench time on `1041` and leave for the
  drain on `1044`, and got two refusals with workings; owned on `1044` that he wanted
  to be the man who went.
- **Wat Marshe, thirteen.** Gave his own count each morning; asked for the wood to be
  said again (`1043`) and for leave to move his yoke (`1049`), refused twice with
  workings, and owned both wants as his own mistakes. His own saying on `1049` was about
  his own wood and stayed his own.

## Earlier bands

## Ilyan Vester — thirty-one, nobody's

The lead. In this county `ch − 554` days; the cut across his palm `ch − 685` days
old. He put a piece of ash down and said nothing, then said the name out loud
himself at `993`, and gave the reason in his own mouth: *so that it is me saying the
name and not just nobody saying nothing*.

He speaks on all ten mornings of band 4 and gives four figures every morning: days
in the county, the cut, the standing offer with the subtraction, and his own count of
wrong things.

**What he wants:** to be somebody. He said it out loud at `1000` and has not
re-argued it in another shape since. At `1040` it is a whole figure — a hundred and
eightieth wrong thing in a hundred and eightieth morning — and he asks that nobody do
arithmetic at him about it, and nobody did.

**What it costs him:** asking is the only thing that costs him nothing. He has not
asked for one thing in four hundred and eighty-six mornings. Band 4 is the first week
in which he was asked for nothing and gave it, and he says that is what he learned
and that it is not much.

**What he refused this band, and why:**

- He would not say how many times that name has been said, in this county, on that
  landing or down any road, and said he would do the same with a figure if one were
  brought to him (`1032`).
- He would not say his own account out loud with the landing empty, and gave Sena
  Dorr back her own working with the figure left off it, because she is the only ear
  on that landing that would know the difference and he would rather be the only one
  holding that sentence than make somebody the only person who has it (`1033`).
- He wanted both Corvin and Barnaby to keep their figures, and said he is not going
  to say whose he wanted it more for, and not out of kindness: the day a figure comes
  out of a mouth on that stone, somebody does the working out, and everybody standing
  there is in it, including him (`1038`).
- He said the thing he wants is not to know what the wood is but to have been the man
  who did not have to be told, and that it is the worst thing he has said on that
  landing (`1036`).

**Where he is now:** he is going to be on that landing at the seventh hour and at the
ninth hour for as long as it lasts, he will not ask anybody for anything while he does
it, and he does not care what those mornings were worth.

## Barnaby Crove — fifty-four, holds the gravel, cannot read

`1 + (ch − 722)` mornings of asking: three hundred and nineteenth at `1040`. He asks
at the sixth hour every morning, in the same words, and nobody answers him.

**He is the person the name on the strip is for**, and the only person on that
landing who cannot read what is said about the name. He said at `1000` that he has
been asked on two mornings now that other people have given names to, that it has
made no difference to the question, and that he does not hold with names.

Band 4: he kept a whole figure in his mouth for the whole week and never put it
down. He said out loud that he is never going to ask anybody about that name, and
gave the true reason, which is that if he is owed a figure then a person will one day
ask him whether it arrived, and he cannot say (`1039`). He wants to be refused. He
said nobody has answered him in four hundred and eighty-six mornings, that he has
stopped waiting for it, and that stopping the wanting from being somewhere a person
could take it is not the same as stopping to want, and is a smaller thing than it
sounds. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him he had it right or wrong.

He works out what a man on his road was doing and cannot, and gives that up.

## Simon Rook — about fifty-two, cannot see well

Keeps a list of things he cannot check, and has said more than once that the list is
a list and not a complaint. **The list is empty.** He puts his hand on the wall, along
the wall, takes it off and puts it further along, and that is all he has ever had off
it.

Band 4: at `1034` he took his hand past the end of the wall on purpose and held it in
the air over both pieces of ash and out the far side, and brought it back without
touching anything, and nobody put it there. His working: he could not see, he would
have to make something up, and a man who makes something up about a thing he has
touched has decided what it was like from having his hand there — which is saying it
with his hand instead of his mouth, *and my hand cannot be asked to stop*. He would
rather hold the distance for a week than have a hand on that strip for a good reason.

He asked a question and got nothing on nine mornings of the band and asked nothing on
the tenth, which is `1034`, where he says out loud that he is not going to move that
hand whatever he is asked. At `1039` a man with a broom put a figure into the air and
offered it to him and he refused it, giving the working: a figure handed to a man who cannot check it is
a figure of the man who handed it over, dressed up as something the other man is
holding.

## Corvin Tace — forty-three, holds that landing nineteen years

Gives four figures off one stone and has never once given a fifth, and is not to be
asked for one in this volume. He heard a word going round in a kitchen at `1000` and
had not been in a kitchen in nineteen years, and a landing does not change because a
word has done a journey.

Band 4: he changed the way he sweeps — from the bottom of the flight upward, so that
the sound comes up behind him — and said so out loud before he did it (`1031`). He
said out loud that there is a person on that landing the name is for and that he does
not know which, and gave the working for not finding out: asking puts every person on
the stone in it, including the ones who keep their mouths shut, and the one it is for
cannot check him (`1038`). He put **nineteen years** into the air on `1039` and said
in advance it was not on offer. He then offered the landing a sentence to say every
morning for as long as he held the stone and took it back on `1040`, because a man who
says that every morning is a man who says that, and in a year nobody will hear a man
refusing to ask.

He works the top step with the sole of his boot, which he has not done in nineteen
years, with nobody watching.

## Wenna Pyle — thirty-eight, saws lengths and sets studs

About three hundred yards down that cart road, in an open-sided shed behind a
wheelwright's, with her own cup carried out because there is no table in the shed.
She named the wood out of her own trade at `992` — *a pair of notches cut for a wedge
is the end of a handle* — and said on her own morning that she was not giving it to
anybody. She gave the figure of how many things are lying on that landing, once, at
`995`, with the working.

Band 4: she is the person the travelling name reached. She says a voice came down the
road in the wind with a man's name in it and went into her shed while her own saw was
going, and that nobody carried it, and that she has it in her head now and cannot put
it down, and that if a name gets said back to you you have a second copy of it, so
she will not say it to the man who said it or the man it is for. At `1036` she came
back to say the word she has been carrying is the wrong word, handed it back without
saying which word it was, and asked not to be corrected.

**She owed the confirming in band 5 and said it on `1041`:** what the wood is, said
out loud, in her own mouth, which made it owed and not given. She was not used for her
own account.

## Sena Dorr — forty-nine, reads for a living, holds the ninth of the nine positions

Twenty-three years of reading aloud. The ninth position is a public archive and not a
stone, and it has a head on it that other people's heads are written down in. The
count of asking was spent at `947` and never reached her; it must not be given to her
in this volume, and she is not to ask for it.

Band 4: she came up that road four times, at the fourth hour on `1032`, `1033` and
`1038` and at the eighth on `1040`, and asked a question on each of the last three
that she could not check. On `1033` she asked Ilyan to say his own account out loud
once with the landing empty — she has never asked anybody for anything in her life —
and was refused **by a person, in his mouth, with the working given**, and was handed
her own working back with the figure left off it. The other two were refused by the
shape of the morning rather than by a person, which she says is worse. At `1040` she sat down on the coping of the wall with the book
open flat on her knee, turned a page with her thumb, did not read it, and sat there
after everybody had gone down.

## Garrin Tolley — thirty-eight, thirty-two notches

The board lives inside his coat against his ribs. He is a figure of what is on a
board and not a figure of a person.

Band 4: carried a count of how many times he had heard the name up a bank in his
mouth and put it back where it came from without ever setting it down (`1032`), and
was told by a man with a broom that he had not brought a number, he had brought the
counting, and wanted somebody else to hold it. He told the man with a broom he was
wrong, loudly, and was not told he was right. He asked whether he might cut a notch
for the strip and was refused with the working — in a year he would not know which
notch was his, and asking means telling them it is there (`1037`). At `1040` he said
out loud that he has had no job all week, that nobody went down that lane, and that
he would rather carry a thing with nothing on it than a thing with one mark on it he
cannot check in a year, and sat down on the wall at the bottom of the steps.

## Orla Fennimore — forty-four, keeps twenty-nine chairs

At a house that is not in this county. She stands at the wall at the bottom of the
cart road and does not come past it, and says what she is not part of before any of it
is done. Her one question, from `1000`, is *what is a person to do with a place they
are not in*, and it is not answered and is not meant to be. She may not be asked
whether she is in it, and may not be told that nobody is answering her.

Band 4: for the first time in a week she came past her own wall and asked whether
the chair on that landing was one of hers, and was refused with the working — a chair
standing in one place a long time is not evidence, and the day that chair has an owner
on that landing the voice in the wind has an owner too (`1035`). She said she would
stop asking anybody about it, and if it turns out to be hers one day it will turn out
without her asking. She ate the heel of a loaf she had brought up with her and offered
to nobody.

**She owed the ordinary form in band 5 and said it on `1048`** from the wall, having said
first that it does not answer her question.

## Wat Marshe — thirteen, `ch − 653`

Gives his own count himself and refuses to convert it into a figure of things. He may
not be told he was right in any chapter of this volume, and the figure of sixty-three
stitches is on no page and nobody is to tell him he found anything.

Band 4: he said a yoke is cut for shoulders and does not belong to them, and said he
does not know that it is right because he is thirteen. He laid the yoke flat across
the mouth of the lane and said out loud that it stopped nothing and that he was not a
person barring anything, and then carried on up the bank with his pails.

## Named and not on that landing

- **Ada Renk**, sixty-four. The name in ink on the ninth line, with a day against it
  in a different place and no day in the arrangement's record. To whom `four hundred`
  belongs and to nobody else.
- **Nevin Trask**, sixty-one. A book of heads and carts, three lines in eleven years
  on Tuesday and six by Wednesday. Left three of the nine positions blank on purpose.
  The book is still shut; if it opens, it opens by his hand.
- **Ivo Kellow**, thirty-four, in an ash, `ch − 630`, with a day against his name.
- **Marda Vell**, twenty-nine, in a reed, `ch − 629`, who has cut off her own second
  figure herself.
- A woman of forty-one who washes lime at the bottom of the drain and asked out loud
  for a person to stand with her, and whom nobody has named. Volume 21 may not solve
  this and may not have a person put themselves down beside her.
- A woman of twenty-eight who has said no twice and is not asked again.
