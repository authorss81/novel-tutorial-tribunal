# Character State After Batch 0004

## Ilyan Vester

- **Status:** First Defendant; early Stage 1, Case Opener. **His provisional standing has LAPSED.** The grant made at the first hearing ran one review cycle; the cycle ended at the seventh morning bell; the review was continued into a continuity docket rather than concluded, so no new ruling was made, and a grant that ran out cannot be extended by any clerk. He will be heard at the full hearing as a witness to his own attached observation. He holds one preserved entry in the case, the routing observation of the previous batch, and it binds nobody.
- **Physical state:** Sleep-deprived, stiff-handed, cold-handed, and steadier than he has been since he landed. He has been outside the rope, at the chalk line, and at a clerk's tray for thirteen days. No new perception and no new power.
- **Constituency:** none, and now recorded as none. The lowland conservancy's citation is withdrawn, the assembly's limited word makes his name uncitable as a permit, the Ro household is a party under objection, the ward seats are unfilled, and the guild asked for the hearing rather than him. He has no district behind him and a household that will not sit for him.
- **Immediate want:** Get through one morning of testimony, two days off, with a position somebody else is willing to stand behind, without reopening the record, and without spending the sealed item in the preservation index that Sera has forbidden him to spend.
- **Moral change:** He has let go of being the man who holds the finding. In daylight, in a workroom, with the count running, he delegated the physical witness to the keeper, the measurement to the counter, the road to the road man, the boundary to the route-arbiter, and kept only the record, and said in public that if a court finds a fifth thing he will not have been the one looking for it. He has also accepted, without trying to be clever about it, that the district's caution and the office that rerouted a bridge's own clause are the same *kind* of thing and that he cannot prove they are the same and is not going to try.
- **Losses he did not cause:** his standing, his constituency, his easy access to records, and the case's only real lever. He gets none of them back in this batch and does not try.
- **New refusals he has made:** he will not take the regional witness-list post; he will not put Tamsin's bell account in the field as a witness to his own theory; he will not put the two items in the preservation index next to each other in anything filed; and he will not build a finding on a continuity line he cannot trace.

## Sera Quill

- **Status:** Provisional advocate, limited scope: the bridge charter, the maintenance record, the disclosure, and the copied-precedent review. Not formally licensed. Not the Ro household's advocate. Not Ilyan's general counsel. Not the ward's advocate.
- **Want:** Keep the Ro household's case, the Saltwheel arrangement, and her family's withdrawal claim separate, and trace the C—2 routing through custody rather than through a person or an office.
- **Boundary held and tested three times in one batch.** She refused, in a public field and in front of the district, to let the Quiet Chamber continuity request be used to trace the continuity line. She refused, in a lane, to let Ilyan's only remaining lever be spent, on the ground that nobody knows what happens to an item a court has ruled on. And she corrected his draft of the load-bearing facts in public and was right about two of the four.
- **Correction of the two facts.** The drift is a band, not a figure: one and a half on a light load and up to three on a heavy one, and a rounded two is what got written on a clerk's tray. And the schedule lapsed because a term ran out on an instrument nobody re-issued, not because the guild refused; the refusal is why the instrument was unsigned, and the schedule should never have rested on a signature. She left the two archive-adjacent facts alone, which is a vote.
- **New knowledge she holds:** the preservation authority's index — eleven items in the week the working plate was executed, all routed C—2, two named in class, neither carrying a name. She declined to ask what is in the sealed item, in the cold room, in front of the desk, and the print-shop clerk entered the refusal in her day book. She has not looked at it and has told Ilyan not to.
- **Relationship:** professional, exacting, and slightly warmer at the end of the batch than at the start, in a lane, in one sentence, and then she went and stood in another lane. Trust remains conditional, specific, and revocable. No romance beat has occurred.

## Sel Harrow

- **Status:** Trialwright. Appeared in person for the first time since the first field, at the Chapter 39 scheduling. Read the file standing in the cold for forty minutes. Confirmed the request was built out of four things and no archive. Stated that Ilyan's standing has lapsed and that the court therefore cannot take a scheduling request from him. Ruled that a reopening sets aside a preserved record and a scheduling sets a day, said aloud that he is ruling on a distinction he has just invented, and preserved the district's objection for appeal.
- **The most useful refusal of the batch:** he refused to rule on the continuity line that appeared in his own ruling's translation, on the ground that a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter. He also asked, in the open, whether Ilyan knows who says it, and heard him say no, and had the refusal entered.
- **He entered the continuity consolidation himself** and says he would enter it again, and knows that it is the same kind of caution as the office that rerouted the bridge's maintenance term, which is a thing nobody in the room has yet been able to prove and everybody in the room can now feel.

## Tarin Keel

- **Status:** Route-arbiter and cautious working ally. He has taken the neutral boundary for the full hearing and the record of the field, and he will testify to what a hearing field does and does not do.
- **Three field actions, none of them requested by Ilyan.** He refused, publicly and by name, to let a ten-year-old's bell account be taken as a witness to Ilyan's theory. He walked under the rope, picked the guild's count book up off the bench with both hands, and refused to let it be secured as a district exhibit without a court clerk's written order and his own signature on the time. And he refused Bram Orren's line book as a constituency before Ilyan could misuse it.
- **His reason is his own want, not the case's:** he once enforced an order on a village on the strength of a record he did not test, and the village went under before the water did, and the record was correct, and the record was correct in an office. He does not hold a record in a field again without a signed entry saying what he is doing.
- **He will testify under oath that he does not know who cut the mark.** He said he would not enjoy it.

## Bram Orren

- **Status:** 17. Bell-runner, apprentice mapmaker of the Briar March. Appears in prose for the first time in this batch, in two scenes. The line he runs crosses the ninth bridge because the bridge is a day shorter than the four above it, and the people who use it cannot pay a district to carry a paper for them. He has been running the line since he was twelve. His father is the March's second mapmaker; the March does not claim him to a bridge that is in Orison, and he said so out loud the first time.
- **What he brought:** an oiled-cloth map of the ground, drawn from himself, fourteen children between the second bridge and the flats, and a footman at the salt wharf who would not give his name, showing that the old channel on the district's map is not where the water goes and that the western abutment stands in the bed of the real one. The guild checked it with a lamp and a second pair of hands and the stone confirmed it. He said plainly that he was not there to be useful and that he wanted the figures for his line book.
- **What he wants:** the span open, four standing arrangements on his line intact, and the route to keep running for people who cannot afford a formal appeal. He is not a rescue, not an ally who solves Ilyan's problem, and not a constituency. He is a boy with a route and a want, and Tarin refused his line book in front of Ilyan before Ilyan could turn it into one.
- **The work he does:** the only record of what actually crosses the span once the district's copy has stopped being entered, and the reason the lower road's lost roof delivery is a knowable fact rather than a rumour.

## Oren Sedge

- **Status:** Ninth-bridge keeper, twenty-two years in the post, and the guild's decision-maker. Won two arguments in two rooms with his own hands and no advocacy, and refused a third.
- **What he won:** two loads of crushed stone, four sacks of lime, fiber delivered to the line, and one load-held day with six hands at the levy rate, in the district's own office, by asking for the guild's own wage and not for the clause. The district conceded because declining would have required its clerk to certify in writing that a span with an emptied seat was fit for unlimited load.
- **What he refused:** a regional witness list, because a guild that keeps a list of pauses is named in every dispute about a pause and cannot then say no to anything; and advocacy, on the ground that describing a span and asking a room for something are different and he now knows which one he can do.
- **What he carries:** an empty guild account, a span with a west seat that is empty on the underside, an unfixed seat, a buyer's stone at a yard, an arrangement that ends at the fourth morning bell, and a ten-year-old's account of a bell in his own book with five terms in his own hand and a declaration that his guild is not a neutral body.
- **His current position on the bell:** a bell is a rope over a worn wheel on a pin, and a keeper can ring it and cannot make the span stop, and the clause that would let him stop it is in a locker on a plate cut along a load-book edge.

## Ilsa Bray

- **Status:** Ninth-bridge keeper and load-counter, six years on the scale. Her refusal of the forward schedule from the previous batch stands in the guild's book as offered and declined.
- **What she found:** the band. She had been rounding the joint's deviation to two fingers because two is a number a clerk can enter, and the true readings are one and a half on a light load and up to three on a heavy one, and the joint has never been observed to accumulate. She read Tamsin's account back once, was corrected once, and did not improve it. She named the fifth mark in the count book as a count-book cut for a load that walked and did not come back, of which she has made two in six years and there has never been one on this bridge.
- **What she took:** the measurement for the full hearing, and kept the half because it is hers and she can be held to it.
- **Unresolved and unchanged:** she has still not decided whether the levy ledger's cut-out name and the plate's cut are one hand, and she has still asked the room to stop her deciding. Nothing in this batch changed that, and her grandmother's book remains a separate thing from the unnamed woman who was paid off the load book.

## Joren Vell, Dalla Rusk, Lio Fenn, and Pev Sorrel

- **Joren Vell:** took the road and the load sequence for the full hearing, on the ground that the road is nobody's this week and it will be somebody's on the fourth morning bell and he would rather it were him than a schedule that has not been kept. He wrote into the Chapter 38 arrangement that **no household pays anything**, and that the Ro household pays nothing, in letters readable from a cart seat, and he has never in his life been able to say that about anything he has written.
- **Dalla Rusk:** took the pump, paid from the pump's own take at the ward rate rather than from a ward fund that does not exist, and entered two days of shortage in the arrangement in public. She said in writing that she is not the ward, that eleven households are served by the pump, and that she is not going to be the person who wrote eleven into a book.
- **Lio Fenn:** his tally is still the source of the ward rate, and he has still declined to give the ward's position. He named the Chapter 38 load-fee as acknowledgment inferred from continued use with money in it until Joren made him hear the difference.
- **Pev Sorrel:** holds the whistle at the road corner, at the ward rate, every change, under an arrangement that has a date on it. He said one line at the drafting stage, to the four people in the room, and it was: *do not crowd me out of my own arrangement.*

## Neris Ro

- **Status:** The Ro household is a **participating party in the ward's common maintenance register, pending verification, with no verification date entered**, and a party with an open objection in the fourth column of a book with a print strip on the back, and a sworn tenancy, a portable work record, and a stable crossing hour.
- **What the register cost her:** not a signature but an authority. A participating party may be consulted by the district without her permission, which means Tamsin can now be asked about the load path and the bell without Neris in the room. That is what the entry bought and that is what it took.
- **Consent boundary:** the flood account remains disputed, preserved, and refused. The maintenance acknowledgment remains refused, on the ground that the charter has no exit. She will not repeat her refusal in public again and has said so to a clerk.
- **The reason she gave Ilyan, in a lane, and to nobody else:** an inspector sat on her bench on the eighth day and explained that if Tamsin said the bell rang and was not answered, the household would not be refusing maintenance — she would be describing a fault in the bridge. The district offered her daughter as a reason, kindly, and she has been protecting a door ever since.
- **What she did in this batch:** refused to send Tamsin, in public, without explaining. Agreed to a copy of the account with her own terms. Reserved the right to stop everything herself if her daughter is put in a room, without consulting anyone, including Ilyan. And named Marda Rennick and her son Besh as the household that loses the shelter roof first, and refused to have it written into the arrangement as anything but a price.

## Tamsin Ro

- **Status:** Ten. Legally safe under a relief that now rests on the court's own refusal to be administratively careless rather than on any schedule, and on a charter that is still not amended. She is to be called to no room.
- **What she offered, unasked and unwanted:** that the ninth-bridge warning bell is a rope over a wheel on a worn pin and a hammer inside a bell, fixed to the post and the rope and to nothing on the span — not the joint, the brace, the count, or the release — and that it is rung by a person, and that when it rang on the day the span moved the people on the span got off and got off and the span went on, and that nobody on the span thought the bell was going to stop the span. She was asked twice what the bell *meant* and was never once asked what it *was*.
- **Where the account lives:** in the guild's book, not the court's, in her own words, read back once by Ilsa Bray and not corrected, witnessed by Neris without a signature, with five terms in Oren's ink.
- **Her five terms:** an account of a machine and not of a meaning; said once, in the workroom, with the span in view; not written for her and not improved; withdrawable until read aloud once and thereafter it stands; **and it may not be used to cure or support any refusal, and any use that puts it in front of a person as a reason to sign is a misuse of the witness and not evidence of the bell.**
- **Her conditions on the court's copy, all four of her own:** a copy and not the book, with all five terms written on the copy in the guild's hand so they cannot be separated; she is not called; the fifth term is read first if anyone uses the account in front of her mother; and the court must say out loud that the account went to the guild first and nobody made her.
- **Her standing requests, unchanged and now load-bearing:** *I am not a reason for the bridge*, entered in the file that went to the review; and the Chapter 40 instruction to Ilyan that the pause clause must be built on a machine and not on a child, in a room she is not standing in, or people will look at her instead of at the rope.

## Meridith Vale, the entering clerk

- **Status:** District maintenance clerk for the lower ward, holder of a newer seal and a standing print authority. Nineteen years in the post. He is the same man who entered the Saltwheel renewal, the authority row, the materials store, the routine maintenance update, the two-book ledger, and the household's refusal.
- **Acts in this batch:** entered the true reason for the district's concession on the back of the supply slip in his own hand with the hour, because conceding was cheaper than the argument and the file should say so; entered the lapse of the maintenance schedule in three short true lines, in the guild's sight, at the count, with the cause on the back; and said his office's limit aloud, in front of five people, for the first time: **the office has no power here.**
- **Limit, now on the record:** he is not a villain, does not think he is doing harm, and is the one person in the case who can be shown, step by step, doing exactly what he was sent to do. He has now also told Ilyan, unprompted, that going on after the preservation authority will make records stop coming to everybody, and that the question is the wage and not the sin. He asked to be annoyed with in a month about the materials store, because there is a version of the last fortnight in which the ground floor of that shelter is a lime store and he wrote the entry and slept well.
- **Institutional context:** the ninth bridge belongs to Orison's lower ward; Vale is that ward's maintenance clerk; Dain attends in a borrowed capacity with no standing on the bridge; Bevin Larch is the ward's schedule clerk and is twenty-four.

## Bevin Larch, Tovin Dain, Maren Kett, and Halden Moot

- **Bevin Larch:** delivered the register notice in the lane because a notice read in a lane is a notice read in front of witnesses; entered the Ro household's objection in his own hand and appended, on his own authority, that the register records no consent and has no column for it; and entered the cause of the schedule's lapse in a public ward roll, reading it back before he inked it, on the ground that a record which does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by. He said he expected to be asked the next day why he wrote it himself and that he would write it again.
- **Tovin Dain:** the district's most coherent voice. He held the line that a span which cannot be stopped is cheaper to maintain, that the district cannot fund a power it has neither created nor voted for, and that a stopped load is an hour of somebody's work the district has not bought. He folded his objection away unread twice. He moved to secure the count book and was refused by Tarin. He told Ilyan to his face that the object of his eleven-day objection won an argument in a room where nobody needed him, and that the better objection is not about Ilyan at all.
- **Maren Kett:** stopped the fen-crossing room from building a common rule on a gap in somebody's shelf, which was the correct thing and which nobody thanked him for. He is right more often than not and has still lost nothing.
- **Halden Moot:** entered, in the open, that the attached record-holder has no remaining constituency, and that his one entered matter in the case binds nobody. He came because a thing that binds a name has to be recorded by the person who wrote it. The ward seats are still empty and the bench still says *WARD — VACANT*.

## Relationship and continuity note

Ilyan ends the batch with less standing, less constituency, less access, and one lever he has been told not to spend, and with four people who have agreed to carry work he used to think was his. Sera is more useful to him and more annoyed by him than at any point since the first field, and says one warm thing in a lane. Tarin is the only person in the case who has acted in the field three times without being asked and who would testify under oath that he does not know. The Ro household's relationship to the case is witness-based, consent-based, visibly separate from his, and now institutionally entered against its will. The guild has a full hearing because the guild asked for it. No romance beat has occurred.
