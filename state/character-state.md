## 1. What 501–510 changed, and it is a new town and a new set of people

**ILYAN VESTER (thirty-one; TWENTY-ONE DAYS IN BRAMBLEFOLD, HE WALKED IN ON THE MONDAY OF THE NINETY-THIRD WEEK; two hundred and seventieth morning; ONE POUND AND THREPENCE, WHICH IS EIGHTY-ONE DAYS EXACTLY).** **HE CARRIES A COUNT THAT IS NOT THE TOWN'S: the days since a page was read out in a room with the door shut, in a town two days off and in another county, begun in another town and carried with him like a man carrying somebody else's letter. Barnaby Rill told him out loud in a porch that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a man may keep a number and none that says he may not, and Bramblefold has not adopted the count and has not been asked to.** Named 10 of 10 in each band, with a want, a mistake and a cost of his own in every chapter, and the chapters where he is right and gets nothing are `504`, `511` and `520`. **HIS TEN MISTAKES IN THE SECOND BAND ARE ONE HABIT AND SO ARE THE FIRST TEN, and the second habit is the same one: he tries to be the instrument, or the voice, or the carrier, and a person in the room asks him a question he cannot answer in full. THE FOUR REFUSALS HE TAKES IN 513, 514, 518 AND 520 ARE FOR REASONS HE HAS HEARD BEFORE, and understanding each of them in about four seconds is the difference, and understanding is the cost.** **HIS VOLUME 10 ENDS AND HIS VOLUME 11 BEGINS ON THE SAME ACT: he walks out of a town of four thousand people on a Sunday because a recordwright of thirty-four said out loud in a square that she had read of a town where a book is a door, and he walks out of it after being stopped before the second syllable of a thank-you.** **AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT HE CARRYING INTO BRAMBLEFOLD IS IN THE INSIDE POCKET OF HIS COAT: four copies of documents he cannot be sent for, and one page folded in four that came back four hundred miles with two names in a witness line, one of which is a hand he put there himself, and which he offered to a woman with a key on a Tuesday and was told to pull it back in, and she did not look at it.** He is on nobody's book in two counties and has one pound and fourpence, which is eighty-one days. **He is entered at the back of that town's book on an unruled leaf in a hand that is not the book's hand, with his name, the day, and the words whose record is not findable, and there is no column on that leaf for anything else.** His ten mistakes are in `state/batch-summary.md` §0 and they are one habit: making a thing legible so that it can be held. **In `508` he says the difference out loud for the first time in a hundred and eight days — I understand it and it does me no good whatever — and asks that it be noticed that he has noticed, and a copyist says it is noticed and it is not entered, because there is no form.**

**MARDA RILL (34, recordwright of Bramblefold nineteen years, keeps the descent book, ONE OF FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT COUNTRY WHO CAN READ THE HAND ON THE FRONT OF IT AND THE ONLY ONE OF THE FOUR WHO IS UNDER SEVENTY).** **SHE SAID SO HERSELF IN A SQUARE IN ANOTHER TOWN, and three chapters and five state files said nine readers, and the four is the true one.** **THIS IS HER BAND AND THE WHOLE OF IT IS ABOUT WHAT A RECORD IS OWED TO THE PEOPLE IN IT.** She did not ask Edith Crow to come and said so out loud and entered it as the tenth time in nineteen years she has been the one who has not asked. She wrote the entry of a man of thirty-one on a fresh leaf and read it back to him in a porch before she put it away. **She took a sheet of headings out of his hand at a door and put it on the sill outside and shut the door, and said out loud that the three things a shut door says are that it will be written down, that it is not being said in front of a market, and that the people in it have been asked to be in it, and that the third is the only one anybody in that town has ever been short of.** She did the whole of the arithmetic of her town in pence on the back of a board and said the reason out loud in a room, which is that a name is worth between four pounds sixteen shillings and six pounds this year. She entered a claim and two objections and ruled a space with no heading on it, and refused to head it, and said that a heading is a promise about what goes in it. **She is paid two shillings a year by each of about sixty households, which is six pounds a year, unmoved in nineteen years, and the households hold the fold, and a man by her fire said in a room that nobody has ever once asked her whether it was enough and that he would like that entered.** Nobody entered it and she did not press.

**ABEL TARN (62, a weaver, two rooms over a joiner's shop at the bottom of the town, the last of a name on the struck leaf).** **He stopped in a market about forty feet away when he heard the word struck and said it is about Kiln Row because his mother is on it, and about two hundred people turned round at once, and he said his name the way a man says a thing he has said to a guild and a court and his own mother, and not loudly.** He asked for one thing in the open air and did not ask for anything else: *is that half-acre mine. Not my mother's name. The half-acre.* **He said that the page has a holding in it and a figure in the fold and seven feet in the ground behind the meeting-house, and that a name is not a word, and that his mother's name is the name of a half-acre with a wall round it that his father built out of the kiln's own white, and that there are four of them in it now and they have not been on that page for a hundred and forty-one years and the wall is still standing.** His name is in the ruled space and his claim is entered and nothing is carried. **He has eleven pounds. Nobody thanked him for standing up in a market and nobody was asked to.**

**IN THE SECOND BAND HE GAVE THE TOWN THE ANSWER IT HAD BEEN LOOKING FOR IN A MARKET SQUARE AND SAID THE OTHER HALF OF IT IN FOUR SECONDS: *I did not say he was*, when a man of thirty-one had been trying to make a square kinder to the man of forty-four.** He is the one who says in `512` that a bill of sale is between two people and a page in a book is between a person and a thing that was dead before he was born, and that a half-acre is not a man and does not have a bill of sale and is not going to argue with anybody. **He is in the room on `514` and says *that one is mine* at the fourth name and is told she is going on.**

**HARL WENLOCK (44, AND NOT THIRTY-FOUR, WHICH IS WHAT FIVE STATE FILES AND TWO CHAPTER TITLES SAID AND WHICH NO CHAPTER EVER SAID. He holds the holding numbered five, the next one along, and has held it twenty years; his grandfather bought it off a family that was leaving for four pounds and he has the bill of sale).** **He is right about the book and right about the wall and wrong about one thing, and he is not a villain and never will be.** He was the other half of a market scene and neither of the two men said the other half. He pointed out that he has been pointing at a cold joint since he was seventeen, and said that a pointing-out is not a man on a wall, and then said the other half: it is out now, it is on the lane, and it is in a column of that book in the same hand, and by Thursday about two hundred people will know it and there will be no way of putting it back. He identified the boundary stone and said a man in a hurry cuts the day of the week on a thing instead of the date. He said he has seen the four letters and a stroke on about nine stones on that wall. **He told a man of thirty-one that he is asking nine men to be found with, and that a man is found in this town by somebody saying so and not by being asked, and that a page with nine names and the second hour on it would be a worse record than no page because everybody will know there was no page. His own name is against the first objection and he said he did not want to be told to take it back and that he is not a man anybody is going to be able to find.** He put a stone in a shelf inside a wall with his own hand and nobody asked where.

**IN THE SECOND BAND HE IS RIGHT ABOUT THE BOOK AND ABOUT THE WALL AND WRONG ABOUT ONE THING AND HE SAYS SO, AND HE IS NOT A VILLAIN AND NEVER WILL BE.** He said in a market square the thing he has been holding for nine years, which is that a book with four columns and no fifth cannot be argued with, only asked, because a fifth is a thing you put things in and this one has nowhere to put your answer, and that nobody in that county has ever asked it anything. **He then put a boundary stone into a shelf in the last standing course at the end of a nine-foot gap, with nothing cut on it at all, and said that one was worse than the four letters on the garden wall. He says a wall with a gap in it is a wall that somebody can come through, and that no man in that county is going to shut it before the weather turns, and that from that day the gap is a document. And he says he cannot put the gap in the book for a better reason than that he cannot be sent for: he is one of the sixty households, he has been in that book since he was seventeen, and he has never once in nine years been the only person who knew where a thing was wrong.**

**EDITH CRO (68, a widow on the upper row, four rooms and a yard, paid for twenty-two years out of a mangle and a drying ground and eleven weeks a year teaching children to read in a room over a chandler's).** **She said no to her own family in about four sentences, before anybody argued with her, and asked that it be written down that she said it before and not after.** She said that if the page goes back in she is descended from somebody on it and there is a boy of nine at Kiln Lane with her husband's brother's name and no blood of ours in him who will be able to come to that room and say he is her cousin; and that she has never had a title to four rooms and a yard in her hand and never wanted one, because everybody on that street knows and that is what a street is; and that a book is not a street. **She then said that anybody may write that down and ask her about it on any day of the next year and she will say the same, and that that is the first time in a long while that anybody in that town has offered her something she could actually use.** Her name is the second objection. She was not thanked and was not asked.

**BARNABY RILL (81, kept the book before his daughter, cannot read the third hand and can read the first and a half of the second, and says so. THE FOURTH HAND IS NOT HIS AND NEVER WAS: it is the man who rebound the book ninety years back, and Barnaby Rill learned that hand off his father at about seven. A chapter and a state file each said it was his own, and an arithmetic of sixty-one years that only fits a man of eighty-one had been given to his daughter of thirty-four.)** **He came down a lane on a stick, looked at a word cut into a stone, and did not go down into the lane. He came out into a market square on a Thursday at about the twelfth hour, which is after a weaver of sixty-two has said the whole of what he wanted, for the first time in about nine years, said what he was told in a kitchen by his father, and would not say his father's word for the answer in the open air, and gave his reason: a thing a man repeats for sixty years stops being his opinion and starts being the town's. He was born sixty years after the leaf was struck and there is nobody in that county older than him who was not a child when it was done, and he asked for it to be written down at the head of the sheet that he did not ask.** **And in a porch on a Wednesday he answered the two questions the volume owes: a count is a dated thing and then a man saying how many; this town has not had a dated thing since the leaf was struck and the last one was a year of the long frost, which is a season and not a day; and the man of thirty-one's fifty-eight days are counted from a common at Halloway sold to eighty lots at two pounds the lot in a week no person in that room can name.** He said that the day anybody noticed the striking was a Tuesday, and that a Tuesday is a day that does not carry a number, and that a day of a week is a better record than any of the four columns and the only one of the five in that town anybody can be checked against. He said he has been in that town eighty-one years and could not tell whose the four letters and a stroke are, and has not been able to since he was about thirty, and that the next four people who can read that book are younger than him and one of them is not going to have the trick. He asked for the half to be said out loud, and said that the *four pounds sixteen against six* sentence is the whole of what this town is arguing about, and that a shape is not a man. He was not thanked and was not asked and he came.

**ODALIE FENN (61, a Keeper of returned names, eleven days in Bramblefold at the start of the band, has written to nine towns in four years, sleeps in a barn at Kiln Row, and is not of that county).** **She is not the villain and the volume does not make her one: she is right about the leaf and wrong about the rate, and the man of thirty-one said the second half of that in a lane and was not corrected by anybody, because the person who corrects him is four hundred miles off and has not read what he said.** She said in a market that a name is not put back by nine people, and that the hundred and ninety in the square are the ones she has to say it to. She had said a version of it thirty-one times in four years and it comes out the same shape every time. **She asked a boy of nine from the Four Gates about the gates at the end of `510` by way of the man of thirty-one, and about four people in that porch thought about it for the rest of the afternoon and nobody answered him.**

**SIMON VERGE (54, of the county of Aldwick, nine miles off, where the county offices are).** **He came down on the Wednesday, went back on the Thursday, would not stay the night, and read four lines of a return from a hundred and forty-one years back in a lane in about ninety seconds.** He said the return does not say what the question was, and that the order is in neither of that office's two books and never was, and that a return is made and a return goes in a book, and that in those days a thing that was done to a person was not an order, it was a consequence. He said he is not here to defend that office and not here to say it was wrong either. **He has twenty-one other returns to make and nobody has asked him for any of them.** A man of the county and not a villain.

**TOBY HENSHAW (14, writes the week's sheet in chalk on a board on the end wall of the smithy, a penny for the week out of the twenty-eight pounds a year that the fold brings in, can count and cannot yet add).** **He wrote a dead man of about eighty-four into a column of holdings because there was nowhere else to write him, worked out himself that the man ought to have gone in the ground column, and was right, and the recordwright told him to write him there and to say it was her doing if anybody asked, and he was not thanked and did not expect to be. THE GROUND COLUMN HAD NEVER HAD ANYTHING IN IT BEFORE, and two later chapters used to re-assert the error as though it were still standing.** He is the boy of fourteen whose brother's boy is coming into the ground of the dead man of about eighty-four.

**WYND KETTLE (about seventy-two, a widower, four acres at the bottom of the lane, walks two miles every Wednesday in a wet coat in a dry week).** **He stood in a porch for eleven minutes to hear one line read about his holding, and would not go and look at the stake, and says that is what he has said the last three times, and there is a mark worn into the porch bench about two-thirds along it where he stands.** He is the only living thing in that book anybody might want to see and the reason the recordwright reads one leaf on a Wednesday.

**ORMER (about fifty, sets the top of the common wall, has been finding stakes on it for about thirty-one years and finds them by the eye and has never once in his life been wrong about one).** He will not let a man put chalk in a barrow and does not care whose boot the foot is. **He WAS CALLED ORDWIN FOR THREE PHASES, WITH A CLAUSE DISCLAIMING ANY RELATION TO A LIVING CHARACTER IN ANOTHER COUNTY, AND THE DISCLAIMER WAS THE TELL THAT THE COLLISION HAD HAPPENED.**

**BARNABY RILL IN THE SECOND BAND ADDED A FIFTH THING TO A LIST AND SAID THE REASON HE WOULD NOT STOP FOUR WORDS GOING ROUND A TOWN.** From a chair by the fire, in a room nine feet by seven with the window not shutting, he said that a page cannot be read backwards and a person can, and gave the whole of it in about four sentences: there is no hand alive that ever struck that stroke and no body it can be asked, and every hand after it goes on top of it and cannot lift it, and a person can be read backwards, and that is the only thing in that building that is not a page. **In a porch he heard two women at a pump say the four words and said that four words going round a town is four words going round a town, and that he would not tell them to stop, because a man of eighty-one telling nine people not to say a thing is the same thing with his name on the end of it, and he has seen that done twice by better men than him.** He was in the room on the Saturday the leaf was read and he was not needed for the terms of it.

**ODALIE FENN, sixty-one, a Keeper of returned names, in the town twenty-three days at the end of this band, with a bundle of thirty-two letters in it.** She claimed the third term of the reading and said she had said that sentence, or a version of it, thirty-one times in four years in nine towns to about nine people at a time, and would tell the recordwright who wrote it if anybody asked and would not if nobody did. **She read the whole of what was on the page out loud in a square with about a hundred and ninety people in it, holdings and fold figures and ground included, because a name is not put back by nine people, and she was stopped from doing more by a sheet with three terms on it that she had been given by a woman of thirty-four.** She said in about eleven seconds in a porch that the two objections at the foot of that entry are both against the same three words, and she has been in that town twenty-three days and a man of thirty-one had been carrying the same sentence for five days and did not say it. **She has named three of her thirty-two letters out loud in front of about nine people and has not read them out, and nobody in that porch asked her, which is a thing about four of them noticed.**

**SIMON VERGE, fifty-four, of the county of Aldwick, nine miles off.** He came down again on a Sunday, would not stay the night, and had made four of twenty-one returns that nobody had asked him for. **He said the order is not in the book of orders because nothing was ever on that page: one page, a heading in the same hand as the returns, a blank half-sheet pasted in under the heading, and the paste as old as the binding.** He said that a thing that was hidden is a thing somebody can be found about and a thing that was never written is not about anybody, and that the man of the county has a horse and there is no road to ride him on, and he said it to a man of thirty-one and not to the porch. **He left a copy of the return on the step with no name on it, because the office gives a copy to anybody who asks in writing and nobody had ever asked, and there is no form that says a copy of a return may not be given away by the man who keeps the book of returns, and none that says it may.** He is a man of the county and not a villain.

**EDITH CROW, sixty-eight.** She was in the room on `514` when the leaf was read and the finding of `517` is about her objection and about another one, **and she was not sent for when that finding was entered, and did not know on the day it was entered that a finding had been made about her objection at all.** She is still unpaid and still unasked.

**TOBY HENSHAW, fourteen.** He was told what to do about a gap in a wall and there was nobody to tell him, and he said no out loud to a man of about thirty-five in front of about nine people — *that is seven feet of somebody, you do not put a wall in it* — and about four of the nine understood the whole of what was underneath the sentence. **A recordwright ruled a column on the end wall of the smithy and put the chalk in his hand and did not take it back, and told him to write the day of a week and not a number, and his name is at the foot of the column because he wrote it, and there is nothing at the top of it.** He was paid a penny and there is no form that says a boy may be paid anything else for a column he ruled himself.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-ONE OF THIS TOWN, in two rooms over a milliner's at the bottom of the town, who has had a copy of four pages for about a year and has not mentioned it to anybody including the woman she rents the rooms from.** She was in a square in a town two days off on the thirty-ninth day after the division and wrote four pages out from memory in about four minutes of sitting, and there is a thumbprint on the third page and nothing on the fourth about who wrote it. **She was found in a lane at the bottom of this town, was not asked to say anything in the square, and said one thing: that she had told herself for about a year that a person with a pen and no copier does the only thing available, and that the only reason she had not said so out loud in this town is that nobody has ever asked her anything, and that nobody asking is not the same as nobody listening.** She was not thanked. A man of thirty-one said his own sentence to her and she said she had never heard it and did not know who he was, and that was true.
