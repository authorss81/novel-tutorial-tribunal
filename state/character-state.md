# Character State — end of Chapter 470, Volume 10 Batch 0002

**This block supersedes everything below it. The Volume 09 block below is history and is in git at `90c3efd` and `1e8801a`.** Chapters 1–450 are canon and closed. Chapters 451–470 are canon. **Nobody has been relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked.** In this band the miller was right about the nine hundred people he knows, the county man was right about the frame, the steward was right about the roll and was not relieved, the chair of the water board was right about the request, and the copyist and the woman who keeps the sluice were the only two who refused him anything and both refusals were correct. **The one person in the band who was wrong in her own mouth and paid for it in a way a reader can name is Sefa Lund, and the one person who is right is Sera Quill, four hundred miles off, and she does not get thanked either.**

**DATES.** No month exists in this volume's calendar. **The town keeps no year; its own reckoning is days from the division, `hall = chapter − 446`, and the days are printed as days.** Names in a record are not names in a mouth. Cast table in `outline/volume-10.md` §3. Ground-floor clerk Tam Ferrow is **29**.

## 1. What 461–470 changed

**ILYAN VESTER** (**thirty-one**; co-witness, bad leg, cracked steel pen, cold bitter tea, **two hundred and twentieth morning at 470**; **day 18 of his time in Halloway**). Want, mistake and cost of his own in every chapter, and **for the first time in two batches the mistake is not the engine of the scene in two of the ten.** `461`–`466` are as logged in the previous block. `467`–`470`:

- **`467`** offered to go round nineteen doors with a bill and take the names, in about three seconds, and was refused by a woman of thirty-four who had been round the market four times asking strangers questions and got nothing. **He held the bill for an hour and eleven minutes and said nothing.** Then he said **twenty** in a lane, in front of about eleven people, having been to six of the nineteen doors. **Cost: a number is out in a lane and there is no form in the county of Ostrey for taking one back out of eleven mouths, and the true count is twenty-two.**
- **`468`** sent the letter to Sera Quill with the witness line in it and a paragraph in the middle naming the figure in his own unsent return, which he crossed out and wrote again. **He is right about the witness line and he is wrong about everything Sera Quill says, and she says so in a hand he asked her not to use, and the answer is on a road and he does not get the last word.** **Cost: the figure is now in the hands of the one person in the world he should not have told, and it cannot be got back out of a letter.**
- **`469` — THE CHAPTER WHERE WHAT HE DID IS NOT THE THING THAT WENT WRONG.** He had the man from the county in front of him twice, on the eleventh day after the division with the gates shut and about two hundred people in the lane, and at midnight on the seventeenth with a straight edge and a nail, **and asked him for a minute both times, and never once asked him what the county had.** **He says the reason in his own mouth: asking a man with a county what his county has is asking him to be a second thing as well as the first, and he has spent eighteen days refusing to make anybody carry anything and has been so careful about it that he never once said *ask him what he has got*.** **Cost: nine pounds sat in a book twenty-two miles off for two years, and the frame is a season, and the season he is standing in is the twenty-third day after the division.**
- **`470`** pressed a chair of forty-seven to read ten things out loud in a room with the door shut and about twenty people on the other side of it, and was refused, and did not get them read. **Then he went and did it himself: he wrote his own return out, walked eleven miles on a bad leg, put it on a counter, and told a clerk to enter no person for the answer to go to.** **Cost: a figure is in a book with a head on it and he is the one person who cannot be asked about it — and Halloway, which has never been on a list of any kind, is on one now, at the bottom of his own return, where a clerk will write it and nobody can check it.**

**SEFA LUND** (38, keeps the sluice at the Fen, nine years, at the fourth house of nineteen, **never paid**, a bad hip, a key to gates that are open, **and in `467` the fourth house is found to hold three people who are none of them the other's**). **THE OWED INSTRUCTION IS DISCHARGED.** In `463` she stated her rule — *a thing said in a square belongs to whoever is standing in the square* — and was told the other half of it by a man with a straight edge, and did not take it. In `467` **she acted on the half she had not taken, and said it out loud in her own mouth, and gave the reason, and was wrong:** *the reason is that a thing said in a lane is mine, and a thing said in a square belongs to whoever is standing in the square, and I will not hand this row two numbers about itself to a market in one week.* **THE COST, AND A READER CAN NAME IT: the corrected count does not go on the paper.** It stays in the row, and the wrong number — nineteen, which she said in a lane on the nineteenth day after the division in front of about eleven people — goes to the square at the sixth hour, and she has to say *I was wrong, and I was wrong in a lane, and the reason I was wrong in a lane is that I would not say it in a square*, **out loud, in the place she had claimed was hers.** It is not her job, not her house and not the sluice. It is her mouth. In `470` she asks the question that makes her own request unmakeable — *ask him where the fourteen pounds went* — and is right, and is not thanked. `461`–`470`

**WENNA CRALE** (34, copyist to the House, eleven years, £2 a year, **the only public officer of Halloway that is paid anything**). **Refused, in a room of about thirty, to be the officer of the out-of-roll book, and gave the reason: if the line goes in the press then the clerk is the officer of this board for it, and there is no form that says the clerk of this House is an officer of the water board, and in four years the steward will say the clerk did it on her own.** Copied Sallow's four lines and read them out in a taproom to about thirty people without being asked. **Entered the agreement of nineteen houses that are not of the town into the minute of a water board, and the request of a man of sixty-one, and entered that the correcting of the roll was the steward's form and not the board's and that the board did not know it existed.** Copied a list of works on a Saturday. **Has now been asked three times in two days to enter a number about the Fen and has entered nothing.** `461`–`470`

**PERDY SALLOW** (47, chair of the water board, nine members, nine years). **Said yes in four words** to a request he had walked nine men down a lane to ask, then would not write the line. Wrote down **the shape of the question instead of answering it, and put a day on it — the twenty-fifth day after the division, nine days off — and said out loud in a room over a taproom that he wrote a day down to be a courtesy, and that on the ninth day somebody is going to come and use him, and did not answer a stranger who asked him which of the two it was.** **Sat the board six days early on a Saturday, put in a question nobody asked for, got nine to nothing in about eleven seconds, read out the third line of the House's minute, and went down a lane and got a request out of a row that had refused one.** **On the twenty-fourth day he refused that request in the words of the request, and the reason was the second line of it, which the man who wrote it had copied out of a minute of this board.** He is right every time and the rightness does the damage. `461`–`470`

**AMOS TRILL** (61, steward of Halloway forty years, **caused clause one to be struck and was right about it and says so**). **Told a stranger about a form in his drawer in a market square on the eighth day after the division and did not open it, and opened it on the Sunday morning six days later, and corrected the roll in about four minutes with nine words and a day and his own hand at the foot, and came out of his office and stood under the awning of the mill and said in a square that he had done it, and that he had not slept on it.** **Has still not called the House. He read out the eleven lines and the one line of the dead man and the four lines of his own correction to about two hundred people in a market square on the twenty-first day after the division.** `461`, `466`, `467`, `470`

**ORDWIN CAPE** (61, the fourth house of nineteen, kept the sluice twenty-two years, cut reed thirty years, **a county pension of eleven shillings a year**). **Said yes** to the request of the fenrow on the nineteenth day after the division, in nine sentences, four of them a sum he had done twice, and said the sentence about the ninth clause that is a hundred and thirty-eight years old. **Wrote the four lines of the request of the twenty-fourth day, and copied the second line out of a minute of this board's own sitting because it is the only form of a levy he has ever seen, and the second line is the line the board refused on, and he said so himself.** **And got the fund wrong twice in one morning in front of a man from the county and corrected himself in the room.** `462`, `465`, `469`, `470`

**MAB HARROW** (34, washes at the sluice, rents the second room of the fourth house, **not a head of household, so never in the House, for eleven years**). **Found the mistake of the band in a doorway on the twenty-first day after the division**, by knocking on nineteen doors since the sixth hour and asking one question at each of them, and found the second house is two because a man of thirty-four has paid a shilling a week in coin to a woman of eighty-one for two years, and the fourth house is three. **Wrote the numbers on a bill in her own hand because a stranger was not allowed to.** Went to the mill first and got nine names read out over a counter. **Told an old woman of eighty-one, in about nine words, that the water of the Hallow is not hers, and the old woman asked her a question about the rent, and it was the right question.** `461`, `464`, `467`, `470`

**GARRICK NYE** (44, of the county, has scoured the Hallow twice, about twenty years ago and nine years ago). **Right about everything he measures.** Came into a lane at the eleventh hour of a Thursday in the rain with a straight edge and a nail and measured the north heel at **three-eighths of an inch down in nine years** and said it is a season and not two years. **Went back to Kerby on the Friday, had a page copied on the Monday, and came back on the Wednesday, a day before he was due, because he could not sit on it until Thursday.** **Had the answer to a question nobody asked for two years and had not told the steward, and gave the reason: he was asked for an opinion and not for a page, and a page out of a county book put in front of a room is a county with a grievance, and he is not that, and he wanted to be asked.** **Named the office and then said that the office has no power over any of it.** `461`, `463`, `469`, `470`

**OTT VERNAY** (39, four years at the mill, **about nine hundred names in a book of his own, with a mark against each**). **Refused a stranger and refused the clerk, and said the best reason anybody gave the stranger in nine days: *you want it in your book, not mine*, and *you have got it in your mouth and it dies with you*.** Showed the Fen page to a man of thirty-one and would not let it out of his hands. **Read nine names out loud over the counter to a woman of thirty-four — eight of them living, and one, a man called Pyle Garraw, dead two years, whose four shillings he has carried ever since because he would rather have the debt.** `461`, `467`

**SERA QUILL** (28, recordwright, **four hundred miles off**). **Read his letter twice on the day it came and once on the day after, and did not go to the press, and the reason was not the letter, it was the shelf: the refusals and the returns are in one press in two ranges eleven inches apart, and a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it, and a name in that range is a name a clerk will see has been looked at.** **Wrote in a day book of her own that she has not answered his question, and then wrote the answer in the round hand of the counters, which he asked her not to use, and signed it with the round hand and not with her own name.** **She is right that every exit she has read in six years is a form, and that a right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right, and he is not.** Her family's claim of six years is unfiled. `460`, `468`

**NEW IN 461–470.** **Coll Sarn, 22**, born in the ninth house of nineteen, up off the low ground at twenty, four beds in this town and none of them his own, has never been asked what he is. **Iven Cape, 81**, her own person, the second house of nineteen, Sefa Lund's husband's sister, has not been out of that house since the water came over the Fen nine years ago. **Wat Priddy, about 34**, pays Iven Cape a shilling a week in coin for the back room of the second house and has been two years. **Pyle Garraw**, dead two years, four shillings, ninth blank. **A clerk of the office of the county surveyor at Kerby**, of about thirty, with a bad cough, at the counter at Ostrey. **A clerk of the press at Kerby**, fifty-eight, twenty-six years, who writes down the days that go past her.

**CULLEN SKAY** (19, sixth house of nineteen, carries a roll under her coat in three kinds of weather; **she is the one who counted the rows off the west wall between the seventh hour and the eleventh**). **ORDWIN CAPE, PERDY SALLOW, AMOS TRILL, GARrick NYE, OTT VERNAY, DOFF ANSTEY** — as before, and their ages and years are unchanged: Sallow 47, Trill 61, Cape 61, Nye 44, Vernay 39, Anstey about 53.

**Nobody was produced.** The one person who came into this town from outside said his name, his age, his county, his lack of standing and his day-count, in that order, in the first minute, in a square, to a man he had not met, because he had watched it done on Saturday and worked out what it was for. **In nineteen days he had put the name of the town on a list at the bottom of his own return and his own figure in a book at a counter, and had entered no person for the answer, and neither of those is anybody else's doing.**
