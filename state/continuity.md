## 0. The ground

**THAT CITY** (forty-one miles off, 1 mile and a half between the room at the foot of the hill and the ninth marker, left on the sixth day of the eighty-sixth week and not returned to): ninety thousand people, thirty-one wards, Tallowgate, a press on the ground floor of a black building at the middle of the nine bridges with a form of six pages in its window, and a room at the foot of a hill where about thirty to forty people stand when a sheet is read out. **The Low Road, two cart widths, kept by Renn Calloway, fifty-eight, twenty-nine years, unpaid, eleven chalk strokes and no figures; the ninth marker with four hurdles of sawn ash, nine links of new chain, a plate wired on with one bent nail hole and nothing cut in the bottom corner, and a keeper's box three feet from the fourth hurdle; and on the south side four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half that has no name in any office document in the world and about two hundred crossings a day going up it.** **A hundred and forty people are dead of a fever. The fever is twenty-five weeks and two days old at 460.**

**HALLOWAY.** A market town of about four thousand people on the river Hallow, in the county of Ostrey, **not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench; the nearest counter is eleven miles off at Ostrey.** A stone market square. A mill with one wheel. A poor rate. A water board of nine. **The House of Halloway: nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, elected for a year, sitting on forty-one stools in the Stone House in the winter with the door open and about two hundred people in the square because a stone room will not hold them, in the order of the wards. THE HOUSE DOES NOT SCALE: the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it and there is no form that says it is.** A market on a Tuesday.

**THE STONE HOUSE.** A cold stone room with one hearth in the corner. **On the north beam, nine feet up and about five feet in from the wall, a board about four feet by two and a half** — a vellum on the face, a lead tag at the foot with four seals in it gone the colour of a wet stone. It comes down twice a year, on the audit day and on the water sitting day, by two people onto a long table, in a hurry, with the fire lit. **Against the west wall, a press with four leaves in it, unlocked, which any person of the town may read.**

**THE FEN AND THE FENROW.** The Fen is about a hundred and forty acres of water meadow below the sluice; it floods, it was not divided, and it lies. **The Fenrow is nineteen houses along it, of which about twenty-three people keep the sluice.** The sluice has a wheel nine feet across turned by a four-foot bar, and it is wound twice a day, at the sixth hour and the twelfth, by two people, for an hour, whether or not the gates are shut. **The sluice frame is the water board's and is about sixty years old, and the heel on the north side is a wet black end about two feet long and a man's thumb goes into it up to the first joint.**

**THE RACES.** The Hallow takes the water. Below the sluice a race runs a mile and a bit to the tail of the mill. **The gates were shut on the fifth day of the eighty-seventh week at the sixth hour, which is 457, for a scouring of nine days, and were opened again on the seventh day of that week at the sixth hour, which is 460, five days early.** Between the two, about four hundred people carried water four hundred yards twice a day from a standpipe at the top of the lane, in two tin pails, one a hand's width narrower than the other.

## 3. The money

**1,041 rated households** · **eighty lots at £2 = £160** · **water rate twopence a quarter, 1,041 × 8d = 8,328d = £34 14s a year, which is the whole fund** · **a sluice keeper at £6 a year is £6 of £34 14s** · **the scouring £14, which is two fifths of the fund, levied at 3½d a lot (280d ÷ 80)** · **the copyist £2 a year for eleven years** · **a county pension of 11s a year = 132d against 19 houses at a penny a day for nine days = 171d, short by 39d** · **rating nineteen tenements would add 19 × 8d = 152d = 12s 8d**. ## 4. What is true at the end of Chapter 460

1. **A covenant four hundred years old carries, on the back of its board, a clause a hundred and thirty-eight years old that no household of Halloway shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will.** It is in the minute of a water sitting and it is unreadable except by one woman.
2. **The right of refusal was taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years ago and has not been put back, and the clause is two years older than the taking of it, and nobody in Halloway can verify the date but him.**
3. **Nineteen houses at the Fen are entered in the roll of this year as land and not as households, are not of the town, have no standing in the House, and are not before it.**
4. **The House has declined to enter them, at their own request, in front of about two hundred people, and the minute says both halves.** **The House does not scale: nine wards, one head each.**
5. **The out-of-roll book is unopened for eleven years.**
6. **The water is back in the race. The frame is going at the heel. Nobody has written either of those in a book.**
7. **A stranger read a notice out in a market square and a town knows his name, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may stand in that square.**

## 5. What is not true and has not been made true

**That the fenrow own the water.** The water is common to all **households**, the row holds about twenty-three people so the number of households in it is somewhere between nineteen and twenty-three and is not knowable, and nothing in Chapters 451–460 gives them a household and nothing gives them the right of refusal. **That the striking of clause nine was of that clause alone** — the copyist could not learn it, the paper is not of this town, and it is ninety years since anybody looked. **Who struck it and why.** **What the four letters and a stroke in the witness line say.** **What the clerk's nine-year count at `401:39` was.** **Anything in that city**: the office of Regent of the Shelf is not held, the second seat is vacant, the bar is up, Hessa Marl's book of the visits has no office to be kept in, Fenna Rusk's roll of eleven pages is under a stone, Tarin Keel is walking north, Kerwin Dace's return came back stamped, and about a hundred and forty people are governed by a signature that is not a person's.
