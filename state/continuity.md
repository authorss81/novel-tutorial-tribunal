# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment institution. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

## Opening continuity established in prose

The first movement begins on Earth and shows Ilyan’s involuntary crossing before the Orison action. The signed *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report was never merged, but its sequence was recognized as an external challenge. The black shale fragment beneath Morrowlight’s room answered the wording; the living-person crossing came from a separate legacy null passage. Ilyan carried a spent blackglass token into Orison with no return route, no automatic burden, and no informed consent. The passage consumed the ninth bridge’s emergency reserve and worsened a western-joint drift that was already present.

Ilyan lands at the ninth bridge as the First Defendant. Tamsin Ro, age ten, is rescued from beneath a bridge whose charter protects registered lawful travelers but excludes the Ro household. Oren Sedge’s bridgekeeper crew uses a clamp, drive pin, and named load path to keep the child alive. The rescue does not settle Neris Ro’s access to work, housing, or the bridge.

## Batch 0001 established facts

- The System translates only formal records, notices, and the visible boundary of Ilyan’s open case. Tarin interprets urgent guard speech, Oren interprets maintenance shorthand, and Sera explains legal distinctions. The Bench, not the System, decides and preserves rulings.
- The challenge requires claimant, affected parties, disputed condition, burden, and remedy. The current bridge charter lacks a clear maintenance-review route and any enforceable sunset or exit. Review, appeal, and exit are distinct.
- Ilyan accepted a bounded first-field witness duty sourced to the summons: his identity, report, and attendance become public; the duty ends when the field closes. Refusing would withdraw his own claim and access request, not Tamsin’s eligibility for local review.
- Oren’s old saltglass workroom plate preserves a maintenance-review condition absent from the current public charter. A one-bell maintenance exception belongs to Oren’s named crew and one load path, not to Ilyan or Tamsin. It held only while the crew performed the inspection.
- The eastern district’s petition copied old language and treated an unlisted person’s refusal as a danger to every lawful traveler. When admitted as a disputed precedent, the court physically coupled its backing plate to the current bridge record; the bridge responded to the competing conditions.
- The first night field used an announced one-bell interval. Harrow physically removed the old working plate and inserted the current certified duplicate. The old plate was sealed in the court locker; no archival plate was magically rewritten.
- A continuity line, “Continuity preserves the record before remedy,” appeared beneath ordinary formal translation. Its source remains unknown. The First Witness is not identified.
- Ilyan filed Neris’s private flood account without permission. The Bench preserved the copy; Sera marked it disputed, halted the access request, and suspended representation. The original account remains with Neris; the court copy is in Sera’s custody. The names in the account were not consented to and are not public testimony unless separately offered.
- Tamsin voluntarily offered only that the warning bell rang before the collapse. Neris offered the household’s register, work, and housing facts but refused the flood account. Their consent was renewed separately and did not restore trust automatically.
- Sera’s limited provisional representation resumed after the withdrawal/dispute notation and Ilyan’s acceptance that his future claims would be public. She filed a licensing recommendation for the full hearing but is not formally licensed yet.
- Chapter 10 grants a provisional stay and limited provisional standing for Ilyan: the Ro household may use the bridge for one review cycle, with a public review date at the seventh morning bell after the ruling. Oren’s named maintenance burden and Ilyan’s attached public record are finite. The current charter is not amended. Appeal, durable exit, housing/work protection, and the full local case remain unresolved.
- Ilyan reaches early Stage 1, Case Opener, through the accepted challenge, willing advocate, and bounded stay. He can request one narrow provisional exception but cannot choose another person’s burden or reassign a cost.
- Sera’s public surviving fragment says, “the governed may answer no,” while the current Bench text says, “the governed may petition for correction.” The original Quiet Chamber page’s binding sleeve is empty, and the inventory records that original page’s removal before Ilyan’s crossing. Sera’s private duplicate, which was present the night before the first field, disappeared during or after that field. The record does not yet establish whether the two losses share a hand; the remover and chain of custody are unknown.

## Power and failure constraints

No respawns, save points, character replacement, or unearned level-ups. A failed ruling remains consequential. A burden must be named and may not be silently assigned to an unwilling person. Death is permanent. Ilyan grows through practice, failure, relationships, changed decisions, and permanent costs. A Foundation Challenger may open a blank interval only with notice, consent from people remaining in the full spill zone, and safe withdrawal margins; the final use permanently costs Ilyan the lived memory of filing the *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report.

## Ending constraints

The final adversary is the First Witness already introduced in the Bench. Ilyan must reject the Final Exception even though it will work, give up permanent authority and a personal return route, and help create distributed regional charters. The final image shows an ordinary person refusing a specific rule with visible consequences and remaining part of the community. No new cosmic threat is planned.
