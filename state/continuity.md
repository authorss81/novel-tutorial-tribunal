# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment institution. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

## Opening continuity

The first movement begins on Earth and must show Ilyan’s involuntary crossing before the Orison action. A separate legacy emergency clause pulls him across without informed consent, consumes Orison’s ninth-bridge anchor reserve, and leaves no return route; it does not assign him a future burden before he can answer. The bridge already has a partial load failure, and losing the reserve worsens that fault rather than creating the Ro household’s legal exclusion. Ilyan lands as a First Defendant with a spent blackglass null token and no local standing. Tamsin Ro, age ten, is trapped beneath a bridge whose charter protects registered lawful travelers but excludes Tamsin and Neris Ro, Tamsin’s mother. The immediate choice is among amending the charter, breaking the bridge, or holding a hearing open; Ilyan’s first meaningful action is to preserve Tamsin and force a hearing. No chapter prose has established new facts yet.

The opening batch reserves Neris Ro, age thirty-four and a dye-yard tally worker, as Tamsin’s mother, and Oren Sedge, age fifty-seven, as the ninth-bridge keeper. Their practical needs and refusals must survive the case mechanics: Tamsin wants a voice without becoming an exhibit, Neris needs work and housing access while retaining control over her private flood account, and Oren wants a safe span without making the guild liable for every future crossing.

## Pre-prose Batch 0001 safeguards

- The System’s translation boundary stays narrow. Tarin interprets urgent guards’ speech, Oren interprets maintenance shorthand, and Sera explains legal distinctions; the System does not silently make Ilyan fluent.
- No burden is attached by the null passage. Ilyan’s Chapter 4 witness duty is sourced to the legacy summons: his identity and signed report become public, he must remain inside the court safety perimeter when the first night field opens, and the duty ends when that field closes. Refusal withdraws his own claim and request, not Tamsin’s eligibility for local review, and grants him no authority over the Ro household.
- A **review** re-examines evidence on a date, an **appeal** challenges a ruling, and a **sunset or exit** permits a rule to end or an affected person to refuse it. A **provisional stay** preserves a named access for one review cycle and expires unless the Bench issues a new ruling at that review. Batch 0001 grants only that temporary stay and review date; durable appeal, exit, housing, work, and refusal protections remain for the full hearing.
- Hearing fields test active records but do not rewrite archival documents. Chapter 7’s working-plate change comes from Harrow ordering a physical replacement; Chapter 9’s bridge response follows the court deliberately coupling two records inside the field.
- Chapter 5’s doubled vibration is ordinary strain, not early Seam Reader perception. Ilyan remains Stage 0 through Chapter 9 and reaches early Stage 1 only when the Chapter 10 stay issues.
- A continuity voice briefly interrupts the System’s ordinary translation in Chapter 2. This seeds the First Witness without naming or explaining it.
- The privacy breach occurs in Chapter 5: Ilyan attaches Neris’s private flood account to the emergency docket without permission. Later correction may mark the account withdrawn or disputed, but no one pretends the preserved copy never existed.

## Power and failure constraints

No respawns, save points, character replacement, or unearned level-ups. A failed ruling remains consequential. A burden must be named and may not be silently assigned to an unwilling person. Death is permanent. Ilyan grows through practice, failure, relationships, changed decisions, and permanent costs. A Foundation Challenger may open a blank interval only with notice, consent from people remaining in the full spill zone, and safe withdrawal margins; the final use permanently costs Ilyan the lived memory of filing the *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report.

## Ending constraints

The final adversary is the First Witness already introduced in the Bench. Ilyan must reject the Final Exception even though it would work, give up permanent authority and a personal return route, and help create distributed regional charters. The final image shows an ordinary person refusing a specific rule with visible consequences and remaining part of the community. No new cosmic threat is planned.
