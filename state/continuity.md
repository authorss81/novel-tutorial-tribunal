# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment institution. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

## Power and failure constraints

No respawns, save points, character replacement, or unearned level-ups. A failed ruling remains consequential. A burden must be named and may not be silently assigned to an unwilling person. Death is permanent. Ilyan grows through practice, failure, relationships, changed decisions, and permanent costs. A Foundation Challenger may open a blank interval only with notice, consent from people remaining in the full spill zone, and safe withdrawal margins; the final use permanently costs Ilyan the lived memory of filing the *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report.

## Ending constraints

The final adversary is the First Witness already introduced in the Bench. Ilyan must reject the Final Exception even though it will work, give up permanent authority and a personal return route, and help create distributed regional charters. The final image shows an ordinary person refusing a specific rule with visible consequences and remaining part of the community. No new cosmic threat is planned.

It borrows on load and the water returns it on relieving, so it never accumulates. **The pale line on the deck is the permanent set and is a separate number; the deviation is what one load borrows and gives back.** The fix: take the load off, open the seat, re-bed in crushed stone and lime, three weeks' cure, one day to open and one to bed, and twenty days unloaded. The district bought the stone; the day was won and then lost; the seat is not fixed.
- **The drift's figure is a band, not a number.** Ilsa had been rounding to two fingers because two is what a clerk can enter. **The true readings are one and a half on a light load and up to three on a heavy load, and the joint has never been observed to accumulate.** Sera corrects Ilyan's draft in Chapter 40; Ilsa keeps the half because it is hers and she can be held to it. A reading of three fingers was observed once, in the Chapter 39 hearing field.
- **The drift was visible earlier and was not seen because the span was watched by one person instead of two.** The under-deck watch is a two-hand job — one on the deck, one underneath — and has been entered at one hand since the year the crew list went to one name. The salt crack, which is a surface reading, showed the stain for six weeks. A crew of six at every change would have found it four days before it was found. The two hands that went were the hands that went when the second witness went.
- **Bram Orren, age seventeen, bell-runner and apprentice mapmaker of the Briar March, appears for the first time in prose.** He has run the line across the ninth bridge since he was twelve; the line is used by people who cannot afford a formal appeal, and four households on it have standing arrangements with people who cannot write. He wants the span open and does not want to be rescued, used, or made a constituency. He brings an oiled-cloth map of the ground — channel, banks, roads — drawn from himself, fourteen local children, and a footman at the salt wharf, showing the old channel under the eastern approach on the district's map and the real channel under the western abutment. The guild checks it with a lamp and a second pair of hands and the stone confirms it. He says plainly that he wants the figures for his line book and is refused, asks again Thursday, and states the cost of a closed span to the guild before it costs him his line. He is not a district, not a delegate, not a witness, and not a body that can hold a record; Tarin refuses his line book as a constituency in front of Ilyan before Ilyan can misuse it. The **Orren/Sedge** name collision is handled once, in Chapter 32, in Bram's own words, and does not recur.
- **The guild won materials and a day without Ilyan, in the district's own office, by not asking for the clause.** Two loads of crushed stone, four sacks of lime, binding fiber delivered to the line and not into the yard, and one load-held day with six hands at two days' work at the levy rate, entered in the supply column, with no acknowledgment, no signature, no maintenance term, no successor clause, and no column. The district conceded because declining would have required its clerk to certify in writing that a span with an emptied seat was fit for unlimited load, and Vale entered that true reason on the back of the slip with the hour and his own hand. **The win is materials and access. It carries no review date, no exit, and no settlement.**
- **Vale's own limit, now said aloud in front of witnesses: *the office has no power here.*** He has said it to the district, to the ward, and to a household in a lane, and in Chapter 33 says it in a room, and he adds that the district's loss on the materials store is an entry he would like somebody to be annoyed with him about in a month.
- **The regional maintenance conference at the old weighhouse at the fen crossing, convened overnight.** Forty-one delegates, four districts — the fen, the eastern, the low fen, and the cut banks. The fen and the eastern filed complete term indexes; the low fen and the cut banks filed nothing and are each represented by a keeper who came a long way. The common rule fails **on procedure, not on opinion.** Ottla Prenn's draft eleven is a real clause containing a keeper's pause, and the room cannot adopt it because two districts — the low fen (Ansel Nyle) and the cut banks (Ordwin Sarn) — cannot produce their bridge maintenance terms. The conference's own custody index shows both held at **C—2**, a code with no office written against it in a district column, the same code sawn off a district work slate, off a schedule margin, and in the routing of a pre-arrival paper. **This is the C—2 gap repeated one step up, and it is a procedural failure that nobody expected.** Draft eleven circulates unadopted with the witness question open.
- **The common clause's witness question turned to Ilyan and he refused it.** A regional witness list could be kept by a man whose name cannot be cited, hence cannot be bought, instructed, or removed. He refused on two grounds: a post with no door is a charter with no door, and a witness list must be carried by something boring, local, paid, and impossible to promote — which is a guild, which has no mandate. Oren refused it from the other side, because a guild that keeps a list of pauses is named in every dispute about a pause and cannot then say no to anything.
- **The Ro household is entered in the lower ward's common maintenance register as a participating party, pending verification, with no verification date entered.** The trigger is the register's own draft: when a matter carrying an attached record is entered in a district docket, the parties whose hours, work, and crossing are entered in a ward record are carried into the common register as participating parties, so that one notice may serve the record and its parties together. A participating party may be consulted on and notified of changes to the span's maintenance and load pattern. **The register has columns for party, span, source, hours, date, verification, and objection, and no column for consent, acceptance, acknowledgment, or leaving.** The district had spent the batch complaining that four records are four doors; it has built a gate. Larch appends, on his own authority and at the household's request, that the register records no consent and has no column for it.
- **The refused hold, used again in a new form, and it was used by the district on the Bench's own Saltwheel distinction.** Ilyan asked the Bench to hold the register entry; the district cited the line that the Bench preserves a record but does not restrain an enforcement, and the entry was preserved. Ilyan said in the lane that he taught that distinction, that the next person to use it will cite him and be right, and that the losing is still doing work.
- **Acknowledgment inferred from continued use survives and has produced a new form: the participating party.** A household whose data is in a register is a household the district may consult without asking. Neris's autonomy, not her signature, is what the entry costs, because the district can now ask Tamsin about the load path and the bell without her mother's permission.
- **Tamsin's bell account is in the guild's book, not the court's, under five terms she set.** What the account says: the warning bell is a rope over a wheel on a worn pin and a hammer inside a bell, fixed to the post and the rope and to nothing on the span — not to the joint, the brace, the count, or the release — and it is rung by a person; when it rang on the day the span moved the people on the span got off, because that is what it means to them, and they got off, and the span went on; nobody on the span thought the bell was going to stop the span. Terms: (1) an account of a machine and not of a meaning; (2) said once, in the workroom, with the span in view; (3) not written for her and not improved; (4) withdrawable until read aloud once, and thereafter it stands; (5) **it may not be used to cure or support any refusal or acknowledgment, and any use of it that puts it in front of a person as a reason to sign is a misuse of the witness and not evidence of the bell.** It was read back once by Ilsa Bray and not corrected. Neris witnessed and did not sign. Oren records that the guild is not a neutral body and has its own interest in term five and no reason to want the household's signature.
- **Tarin refused, publicly and by name, to let the bell account be taken as a witness to Ilyan's own theory**, on the ground that everything she says will be true and everything she says will be what Ilyan needed, and that a witness is not a thing a person uses. Ilyan did not reach for his book.
- **Neris's reason for refusing to send Tamsin, told to Ilyan alone in the lane:** on the eighth day an inspector from the district office sat on the shelter bench, asked nothing about the register, and explained kindly that if the child could say the warning bell had rung and had not been answered, the household would not be refusing maintenance — she would be describing a fault in the bridge. The district offered Neris her daughter as a reason. Neris will not explain this in front of a rope and will not say it a fifth time in public.
- **Tamsin's conditions for the court's copy of her account, all four of her own:** a copy and not the book, with all five terms written on the copy in the guild's hand so they cannot be separated by a clerk who wants a sentence; she is not called; the fifth term is read first in that room if anyone uses the account in front of Neris Ro as a reason to sign; and the court must say out loud that the account went to the guild first and nobody made her. Neris reserves the right to stop everything herself if her daughter is put in a room, without consulting anyone.
- **Print run 114 was authorized by a standing authority of preservation**, which is a piece of paper that keeps deciding the same thing for as long as the condition that required it remains, does not need a name because it was never meant to be argued with, and is the most defensible body in the city. It is the office that authorized the form that carried the Ro household's position. **Preservation and enforcement share one signature.**
- **The authority's own index for the week the current working plate was executed shows eleven items taken into preservation, all routed C—2, of which two are named in class:** item one, the ninth-bridge maintenance-review condition, with no name entered against it; item two, a sealed continuity request, not produced, with no name entered against it. The desk's index has shelf, box, week, class, and routing, and no names, because that is the form. The second item is not Sera's request to be traced; she declines to ask what is in it, in the room, and the print-shop clerk enters the refusal in the desk's day book.
- **The print shop answers one question a day, to one person, and keeps a book of every question asked.** Sera's question was spent in Chapter 0003's aftermath; Ilyan's had never been asked. Ondra Fell is the desk clerk. Neral, the lock-and-ledger counterman, gets the party into the cold index room and identifies the cut page's mechanics: the shop does not re-cut stock, a bound volume is unbound and rebound by a binder, and the paste in the replaced page is cold rather than hot, so the volume went back into service the same day. That is a decision about a book, not a person, and it names a trade, not a culprit.
- **Ilyan's access to records is now a cost rather than a weapon.** Vale's warning stands as canon: a preservation authority is the most defensible body in the city; if Ilyan goes on after the office, the only thing that changes is that records stop coming — not to him, to everybody. The question is the wage, who draws it, what it buys, and what happens to the pay and the person when the person leaves. Vale named this himself, unprompted, as a matter of his own job.
- **The district's maintenance schedule lapsed at dusk on its own term**, because the maintenance acknowledgment it depended on was returned unaccepted and not re-issued inside its term. No party breached anything. The schedule's authority was a signature, and signatures run out. The stone and the lime are bought and are at a yard off the fen road at the district's expense, and the load-held day dies with the schedule. The district has no lawful reason to send anybody to the span.
- **The Chapter 38 arrangement is the batch's third and worst local arrangement, and it is written by the bodies that carry it, not by Ilyan.** Four sources: the guild's own account (empty at the fourth change, said so on the board), the road party at the road rate, **the pump's own take at the ward rate** (Dalla Rusk, explicitly *not the ward*, entering two days of shortage in public), and **an eighth of a load-fee per load from nine named cart owners, collected and entered in A.'s book** (Ardan, as a man with nine carts and no mandate, not as the conservancy). **No household pays anything, and the Ro household is named as paying nothing, in letters readable from a cart seat.** The right book records offers rather than refusals for the first time. **End: dusk to the fourth morning bell, and no longer**, chalked on the public frame at cart height.
- **The named cost of the Chapter 38 arrangement: the lower road's shelter roof.** There is no district copy any more, so the ward keeps its own; a fen-road carrier will not put a roof load on a road that ends on a date with nothing behind it, so the bundles go east to households with two pairs of hands. **Neris names the household that loses it first: Marda Rennick, room nine, and her son Besh, nine, who cannot be on a ladder.** A roof is a two-person job and the lower road has one household with two pairs of hands. Bram's line book is now the only record of what actually crossed the span.
- **Bevin Larch entered the cause of the lapse in a public ward roll**, reading it back before he inked it, on the ground that a record which does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by, and said he expected to be asked the next day why he wrote it himself.
- **Harrow appeared in person in Chapter 39** for the first time since the first field. He read the file standing in the cold for forty minutes, confirmed the scheduling request was built out of four things and no archive, stated that a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter, and preserved the district's objection for appeal against his own ruling.
- **The Chapter 39 hearing field coupled five disputed records and the guild's working count book**, at Tarin's insistence that a field which tests records behind a bench tests nothing. The records: the working schedule book version four; the common register entry; the print run 114 distribution strip; the crew list of the current year with one name in the witness column; the maintenance acknowledgment served and refused; and the guild's working count book, entered as a working record and not as an exhibit.
- **A fifth mark appeared in the guild's count book during the field**, in the margin beside the drift line's entries: a count-book cut for a load that walked and did not come back. Ilsa Bray has cut one twice in six years and there has never been one on this bridge. Nobody was near the book; forty people saw it. Harrow's ruling on what a field does: it does not write, it puts records beside one another and lets them show what they do when they disagree, and a working record agrees with the records around it as far as the hand allows. Three hands have been in that book that month. **Nobody can say who cut it, no district objected, and nothing was named.**
- **Tarin Keel's field action preserved that evidence and nobody asked him to.** He walked under the rope, picked the count book up off the bench with both hands, and refused to let it be secured as an exhibit without a court clerk's written order and his own signature on the time. He did it because he once enforced an order on a village on the strength of a record he did not test, and the village went under before the water did, and the record was correct, and the record was correct in an office. Roke wrote it into the field's record and Tarin signed it. He is a man holding a book, and he is the only one in the case who is prepared to say under oath that he does not know.
- **The continuity line appeared inside a Bench ruling for the first time**, clipped and imperative, attached to a ruling about whether anybody may stop a cart: **CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD BEFORE ANY REMEDY IS STATED.** Roke says it is not on his tray and that no clerk in the room typed it. Dain asked for it to be taken out of the record. Harrow refused to rule on it. Ilyan declined, in the open, to build a finding on it, because he cannot say who said it, and Sera refused in the same breath to let the Quiet Chamber continuity request be used to trace it, in front of witnesses. It has now appeared four times. The First Witness is not identified and is not to be.
- **Sera corrected two of Ilyan's four load-bearing facts in public and was right about exactly those two.** The drift is a band, not a figure, and the schedule lapsed because a term ran out on an instrument nobody re-issued. She also left the two archive-adjacent facts alone, which is a vote. And she named the one lever left in the case — the sealed item in the preservation index — and forbade it, because nobody knows what happens to an item a court has ruled on.
- **The delegation is now fixed and public:** Oren Sedge takes the physical witness and will *describe* rather than advocate; Ilsa Bray takes the measurement and keeps the half; Joren Vell takes the road and the load sequence; Tarin Keel takes the neutral boundary and the record of the field; Tamsin Ro's account goes in as a copy with its five terms written on it. Ilyan keeps only the record, and has said in daylight that if a court finds a fifth thing he will not have been the one looking for it. Oren called that either the last honest sentence on the bridge or a man arranging to be blamable, and declined to decide.
