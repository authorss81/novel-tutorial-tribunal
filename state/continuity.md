## 0. The ground

**THAT CITY** (forty-one miles off, 1 mile and a half between the room at the foot of the hill and the ninth marker, left on the sixth day of the eighty-sixth week and not returned to): ninety thousand people, thirty-one wards, Tallowgate, a press on the ground floor of a black building at the middle of the nine bridges with a form of six pages in its window, and a room at the foot of a hill where about thirty to forty people stand when a sheet is read out. **The Low Road, two cart widths, kept by Renn Calloway, fifty-eight, twenty-nine years, unpaid, eleven chalk strokes and no figures; the ninth marker with four hurdles of sawn ash, nine links of new chain, a plate wired on with one bent nail hole and nothing cut in the bottom corner, and a keeper's box three feet from the fourth hurdle; and on the south side four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half that has no name in any office document in the world and about two hundred crossings a day going up it.** **A hundred and forty people are dead of a fever. The fever is twenty-eight weeks and one day old at 480.**

**HALLOWAY.** A market town of about four thousand people on the river Hallow, in the county of Ostrey, **not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench; the nearest counter is eleven miles off at Ostrey.** A stone market square, a market on a **Tuesday** (and on a **Friday** in the fourth week of the eighty-eighth and on a **Saturday** in the fifth week of the eighty-ninth, the fourth and fifth times in nine years, and a Saturday because for three weeks nothing in Halloway happened on a Tuesday), a mill with one wheel, a poor rate, a water board of nine. **The House of Halloway: nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, elected for a year, sitting on forty-one stools in the Stone House in the winter with the door open and about two hundred people in the square, in the order of the wards. THE HOUSE DOES NOT SCALE: the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it. A sheet of forty-one names in the order of the wards is in the press, and against the ninth ward are five names and one room. A corrected roll is a corrected constituency, and a constituency is a set of doors.**

**THE STONE HOUSE.** A cold stone room with one hearth in the corner. **On the north beam, nine feet up and about five feet in from the wall, a board about four feet by two and a half** — a vellum on the face, a lead tag at the foot with four seals in it gone the colour of a wet stone. It comes down twice a year, on the audit day and the water sitting day, by two people onto a long table, in a hurry, with the fire lit, **and it has not been off the beam since the audit nine years back, and it was off it about two hours on the tenth day after the division and about four minutes on the twenty-eighth.** **It has now been read out in a room twice in ninety years, on the tenth day after the division and on the twenty-eighth, and on both of those days it was given to nobody.** Against the west wall, a press with four leaves in it, unlocked, which any person of the town may read, and the copy of the roll, and a sheet of forty-one names, and a certified copy of a page from Kerby, and **a sheet of eleven things, a copy of it beside the door, both with a day on it.** **Any person of the town may read the copy of the roll.**

**THE FEN AND THE FENROW.** The Fen is about a hundred and forty acres of water meadow below the sluice; it floods, it was not divided, and it lies. **The Fenrow is nineteen houses along it, of which twenty-two households and about twenty-three people kept the sluice, and they are not on the roll as households as of the thirtieth day after the division.** The sluice has a wheel nine feet across turned by a four-foot bar, wound twice a day at the sixth hour and the twelfth by two people for an hour, **whether or not the gates are shut — and it has not been wound at either hour since the sixth hour of the THIRTY-FIRST day after the division, which is the first miss and is the thirty-first and not the thirtieth, and the wheel at the mill has been slow since that Thursday and was three days slow on the Sunday.** The sluice frame is the water board's and is about sixty years old, **and the north side has dropped three-eighths of an inch in nine years, and a man of the county says it is a season and not two years, and a man of thirty-four of a lot on the Four Hundred said in a square on the thirtieth day that if the bar is not turned then it is not a season, it is a year.**

**THE RACES.** The Hallow takes the water. Below the sluice a race runs a mile and a bit to the tail of the mill. The gates were shut on the eleventh day after the division at the sixth hour, for a scouring of nine days, and were opened again on the fourteenth day at the sixth hour, five days early. The lane above the sluice is mud to the ankle for four hundred yards and is the worst four hundred yards in the county.

**THE WATER LIST.** A second book of the water board's, kept by the steward, **which goes by tenement because water goes to a building and not to a name. The Fen is on it, nineteen tenements, rated as property, in respect of water. It is a different book from the rate roll and the two do not agree and are not supposed to.** `461`

**KERBY, TWENTY-TWO MILES OFF, ON A ROAD, WHERE THE COUNTY OFFICES ARE.** **The office of the county surveyor at Kerby keeps a book of returns on the sluices and weirs of the county. It has a return on the Hallow sluice made in the year before last and no other page about Halloway in that book at all. It cannot order the board to do a thing, make anybody answer, fine, rate, or put a name in a book of Halloway's. It makes a return and puts it in a book. A clerk of the office works the press and writes down the days that go past her. Any person in the county may walk in and ask for the page.** `469`, `479`

## 3. The money

**1,041 rated households, unmoved in eleven years, made in the week the common was sold** · **1,060 after the correction of the twentieth day after the division** · **1,071 after the second correction of the twenty-eighth day** · **1,052 after the third correction of the thirtieth day, and the fenrow are on it as land again** · **water rate 2d a quarter = 8d a household a year: 1,041 × 8d = 8,328d = £34 14s; 1,060 × 8d = 8,480d = £35 6s 8d; 1,071 × 8d = 8,568d = £35 14s 0d; 1,052 × 8d = 8,416d = £35 1s 4d** · **the board's money is the quarter's: £8 16s 8d, £8 18s 6d, £8 15s 4d, and a corrected quarter on the true count of 1,074 is £8 19s 0d, and the heel is £9** · **the shortfall of the heel is 40d, 18d and 56d a quarter at those three figures, and the shortfall of 40d at 1,060 is half a penny on each of the eighty lots** · **the £14 of the scouring is NOT out of the water fund; it is 3½d a lot on the eighty lots, charged in the roll of this year** · **the roll is wrong by 112 pence a year: 24d on three doors and 88d on eleven rows, which is fourteen households and not thirteen, and the true count is 1,074** · **nineteen households at 8d brought 152d a year = 12s 8d = 38d a quarter, and the expulsion raised the shortfall on the rest of the town by exactly 38d a quarter** · **the copyist £2 a year for eleven years** · **a county pension of 11s = 132d against 19 houses × 9d = 171d, short 39d, and there is no form that lets him hand it over** · **the out-of-roll book would have cost the fund nothing at all and was never used.** All with the working in `state/current.md` §3.

## 4. What is true at the end of Chapter 480

1. **Nineteen houses at the Fen are not households of this town, are entered as land of the common, and are put out of the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, by a board of nine, on a cause set out in writing, under clause five, cause four, on cause two, and the finding is correct.**
2. **The right of refusal is unrestored. The ninth clause has been read out in a room twice in ninety years and given to nobody both times, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives it to anybody.**
3. **The out-of-roll book has never been written in in this town, and the day it was named for came and went with no sitting.**
4. **The roll of Halloway stands at 1,052 and is wrong by 112 pence a year, and the true count is 1,074, and the roll for the autumn is made in about a fortnight on a day out of a day by a steward who will do it correctly and will not know.**
5. **Nine of the eleven rows entered on the twenty-eighth day are in the ninth ward, and the fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn, and the House is five there and always has been and the number of seats has never followed the number of households.**
6. **The north heel of the frame is a season, and the season is about four weeks off, and the bar has not been turned, and there is no form in this county that lets anybody require a person who is not a household to turn it.**
7. **The only instrument in Halloway that has never been in a room is shut to a hand's width on a mill counter, and the reason it is shut is a sentence a stranger said out loud in a square two days before.**
8. **A stranger read a notice out in a market square and a town knows his name, and a figure of his is in a book at a counter with *no answer* in a column and no person entered, and Halloway is on the bottom of that return in a column a clerk will write.**
9. **A woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off is right that a right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right, and he does not get the last word, and six years of a claim that is not hers is now in a second sheet in a coat in a market town.**

## 5. What is not true and has not been made true

**That the fenrow own the water.** The water is common to all **households**, the row holds twenty-two households and about twenty-three people, and nothing in Chapters 451–480 gives them a household by their own asking or gives them the right of refusal. **That the striking of clause nine was of that clause alone.** **Who struck it and why.** **What the four letters and a stroke in the witness line say.** **Whether the name in the range four hundred miles off is the four letters and a stroke.** **What the clerk's nine-year count at `401:39` was.** **The number of rated households in Halloway, which is somewhere between 1,052 and 1,074 and cannot be got.** **Whether a corrected roll may be un-corrected.** **Whether a board of this county may borrow against the next quarter.** **Whether a household put out of the water under clause five can be put back in.** **Anything in that city**: the office of Regent of the Shelf is not held, the second seat is vacant, the bar is up, Hessa Marl's book of the visits has no office, Fenna Rusk's roll is under a stone, Tarin Keel is walking north, Kerwin Dace's return came back stamped, and about a hundred and forty people are governed by a signature that is not a person's.
