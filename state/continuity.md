# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment network. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and hearing-field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

### The reversal's consequences and the template

- **The sentence that does the damage is the sentence that helps somebody local first.** On the thirteenth day the room's first honest use of the ninth-bridge settlement was not obedience: it was Wenna Tace extracting eleven months of arrears out of a bench minute by pointing at the sentence that says a person not on a register is not a trespasser by that fact alone. **And the sentence that helped her is the *other* sentence that trapped her** — a keeper admits a person on foot, or with a handcart, or with a wheelbarrow, who offers no load. ### Stage 2, Seam Reader — what it is and what it costs

- **Ilyan is now Stage 2, Seam Reader, and the stage is earned by a practice, a failure, a changed decision, and a named physical cost. He is not Stage 3 and gains no new party, no new authority, and no new remedy.**
- **What he perceives, inside a hearing field on a live covenant, is the assembly: which piece is bolted to which, and what a rule is resting on when it is resting on nothing with a name.** The effect appears as physical mismatch — two shadows from one body, a chain on a handbell trembling four seconds before it is struck, a gate that swings out toward the person the rule leaves out.
- **The limit, held in every scene: he sees how the rule is assembled, not why its authors wanted it.** A coherent rule can still be unjust, and the best-made instrument in the batch is unjust. His finding in the field is *it is well made; that is the problem; somebody made it well.*
- **It works on documents about money and not on a man.** He practised it on Kolm's entry and term four together, got a usable and true result in about four seconds, and paid for it immediately: his left hip went cold and for six seconds he could not have picked up a pencil.
- **He was wrong in public.** He read the seam and said the burden was on the gate hand, and the field showed him it was not: a burden that can be paid to a person is a burden somebody pays, and the word that let her be paid for thirty years was *ward*, and the seam went past her and down through a thing with no name at all. **The misassignment is what he carries, which is why the failure is in the body and not in the memory.**
- **The afterimage, named and persistent:** (a) the **left leg**, from the hip down, occupied by a forty-four-year-old gate hand's weight, gone by the fifth bell if he stands on a bank, back in a house with the door shut, gone again by the fourth bell; (b) the **low water in his nose in two layers**, the day over the night and the flat over the water, in a lane with hay in it, unresolvable to either; (c) **a chain on a gate moving in his right hand about four hundred yards away at the wrong end of the afternoon.**
- **The blackglass token is the same kind of thing as the afterimage.** It is a spent seal of a passage that has already happened. It has never been warm. It goes warm on a bank, and touching it takes the low water out of his nose for about fifty minutes, and it comes back with the water over the flat and a third layer under both. **Ilyan has understood that the passage and the afterimage are the same operation, that one was done to him without consent and the other is being done to him by the same somebody, and that the token is why he is here. He has not resolved it and he is not going to.**
- **Four people have heard him say it out loud** — Sera Quill, Sel Harrow, Sabin Roke (who wrote it down at the direction of the bench and was told nothing further), and Ansa Rell. **A fifth has it and never heard him say a word of it: Tarin Keel, who said the true sentence about it first, in front of sixty people, and was right.** **There is a sixth person who is the whole of it, who has not been told, who would not thank him, and whose afternoon is in his leg and is not going to be given back to him.**
- **Persistent effects accumulate until he rests, changes a decision, or shares a burden.** The accumulation reached the point in Chapter 58 where he could no longer look at a seam in a room without losing the leg, and **what it changed was not a speech: it was that he had to be taken off his feet by a route-arbiter and a boy of nine.**

### The ward — the batch's structural finding

- **The word *ward* is a guild's word for a duty, not a place.** It is in the ninth bridge's term book under a bridge heading, *duties carried, not discharged*, with a star against it meaning somebody had complained about it: *ward. not a place. the work a district owes and has not got round to. a guild's word, old, from before the book is anybody's grandmother's. it means a thing you do, not a thing you live in.* It was found by Ilyan, who cannot read a clause, in a book that had been used as a weight under four benches in the rain, and it was corroborated in three places: **the standing of the wards, a hundred and ninety years old, in which eleven of fifty-five duties have been performed and forty-four have not**; **the traveller book's own front leaf at Halloway, one ward, five duties, none performed, the last performer dead thirty-one years**; and **Hanne Kolm's sworn volume, the one document in this case that does not use the word at all**, because the duties of a lower road are the ones a landlord actually does and nobody on that road calls them a ward.
- **The entered finding: a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does.** A rate may be paid to a ward only if the ward is a body; the ward is not a body because it is not a person; a duty cannot be paid anything, it can only be given work; and the work goes to whoever is standing nearest. **This is why a woman stood at a gate for thirty years and why the word *ward* has let a duty be paid as if it were a wage.** The court has no power to make a seat, a name, or a body out of a piece of work, and Harrow said twice on the face of the record that the court has no power over the wards.
- **The two words this city has for the same poor — *the poor people* and *the carters, hauliers and gate hands* — are both wrong in front of witnesses**, and Abel Kearn, thirty-five, a builder, stood up and said he lives in the ward and is in neither of them. A count taken on the low water returned **five places**, and a place is the category the court came to get rid of.
- **Tarin Keel's count has no line in it: there is no way anywhere in this city to count the people who are not at the pump.**

### The two texts

- **Form L-3, the clean instrument, Halloway's, eight clauses, numbered, published at the fourth bell on the thirtieth.** Written in an hour and a half on the back of a weight receipt by **Bel Ordway**, convener of the weighbridge bench, a cart owner, not a lawyer, who signed it: *not a solicitor and not a drafter and not asked by the court. I wrote this myself and if it is wrong it is mine.* It closes the gate to loads in the district's direction at all times, opens it out of the district on the tide, says the gate is worked by the gate hand and by no other person, pays **four hands, four hours a day, by name, in chalk, out of the district's road funds**, sets a **water rate of a quarter of a penny a bucket** collected at the standpipe and not at a door, says the ninth bridge's *lawful traveller* does not apply to that gate and does not use the word, says a person on foot goes through and **may not** be refused, preserves the sanitation complaint, and expires at the third sitting of next quarter. **It was not made.**
- **The shared charter of the district of Halloway, nine sheets, five terms, made at the ninth bell on the seventeenth.** **The first four terms are copies of terms of the ninth-bridge instrument and are marked as copies. The fifth term is original and is about a person.** Read aloud on a bank in the mud to about a hundred and ninety people on the third of the month.
  - **The second half of the fourth term is the carrier's clause and it names Ilyan Vester**: the terms are carried to any district that asks, at his own cost and without pay, and **if he refuses, or is unable, or is not within four miles, the terms are not carried and the appeal is stayed until another carrier is sworn in that district, and nobody else may carry them.** Sera Quill wrote the consequence deliberately, entered that she chose it, and named the reason: a clause that cannot be broken is a post, and he had been a post for a month and it made him a gate.
  - **The fifth term is the holder: a person in the district who keeps the page and swears an entry made in it, and does not hold it for a district. A holder may not be a party and may not appeal.** **Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, the reader of the district, holds the page, in a room over a pump, and cannot appeal from it.** She was never asked; Harrow asked the question nobody had asked — *who in this room reads* — and she said yes, and then said what it costs, and then said the other thing: *it costs me the appeal and I am going to take it anyway, and put those two things in the same line.*
  - **Bream, fifty-four, landlord of Weir Lane, asked that the lane not be in the charter**, because every road in it runs to Weir Lane and the four rate-setters include two people who sleep there. ### The appeal, heard

- **The first appeal in the record was served in four bells and two carts and it worked.** Fourteen names and one seal. **Corva Silt stood eleven minutes in a lane deciding whether a notice may be left with a person who has not asked for one, left it on the sill, knocked, did not wait, and wrote the time on the back in her own hand.**

- **The disposition, in three parts.** (a) **The limit:** the ninth-bridge settlement binds no span but its own; *on this span* is the court's own drafting, is correct, and **is not widened**; the claimant knew it and filed anyway, in his own words, and nobody improved them. (b) **The receipt:** a review held at one span may be received by any bench in this city as a matter of record, on notice, and is not binding there, and a bench which has received it **may not enforce any part of it against any person unless it has adopted that part by its own act and its own minute.** (c) **The duty to answer:** a bench which has received the instrument **shall answer, in writing, in public, within eleven days, whether it holds any page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected by a received record may swear to, in that bench's own room, at that bench's own counter, without leave. The court has no power to compel the answer and says so on the face of it.**
- **The eleven-day answers: nine of eleven answered.** Seven said they hold nothing. ### The mechanism of the fourth production, and why it is right

- **The custom, in Chapter 129, and nobody on that coast could have told him why and two of them could have told him exactly: NO PERSON ON THIS COAST ANSWERS FOR ANOTHER PERSON ABOUT A CROSSING.** Not the guild and not the assembly and not the harbourmaster and not a clerk; if a thing on a road is a person, that person says what it is, in their own words, standing at a counter, and not one word of it is anybody else's. **It is about four hundred years old and it is in nobody's book and it is the same shape as the reason a man will not sign for a woman, and it is the reason a crossing counts a person and not a cart.** It is right. It is consent's shape and the shape of a person not being used by proxy.
- **What produces him is that he is holding the paper.** The bar road has gone into the guild's quarterly return once a quarter for eight years as a slate of carts out of a basket, and the office writes the number down and does not check it, because a number is not a person and nobody had to be a person about it. **A blank is no good either: a blank is a road with nobody on it, and a clerk two hundred miles off will write *abandoned* on a road that four carts go over every week of the year.** So it has to go to her, and there is no second door and there is nobody to send, and the sheet is tied round with a loop for a hand because a sheet that carries a bar road is a person and a person does not go in a cart with a load of oil, and nobody gives it to anybody: a man with a bad leg was standing in that building and did not know until the second bell the next morning that a sheet had gone. **Nobody sent for him. Nobody had standing to send for him. He has been on this coast thirty-eight days and not one person has asked him for one thing, and the fourth time he was produced on this coast it was because he picked a piece of paper up off a counter.**
- **The cost is a day and four miles up the coast road and four back, on a leg, and a night walk back, and nothing else happened.** The person the instrument is about does not want to be protected, is kind about refusing, and does not give the reason: **I am not going to the Reach, and I am not going to say why, and I have had about eleven minutes to think of a reason that would sound well to a man off a road and I have not found one, and I have decided that I am not going to invent one for you. You may write down *she would not say why* and that is true, and it is the only true thing about it.** The reason is hers and it is not a mystery and it is not given, and the narration does not supply it.
- **THE PROHIBITION HELD. She is not told, she does not find out, and nobody gets credit.** He does not tell her about the card's bottom line; his one question with her on the second of the third tide is spent, and that is why. The page ends with her still not knowing, still knitting, with a boy of eight in the hut about whom nothing at all happens, with him still not having told her, and with him having written it down in four lines with the date and that it is his and no reason on the page. **He is refused by somebody he is trying to help, he does not repair it, he writes it down, and the volume's emotional spine is untouched and unspent.**

### What a pane costs, and what a pane is for, and these are two chapters

- **WHAT A PANE COSTS, in Chapter 125, said by the woman who stands at the fire and not by anybody else: a pane is eleven months.** The glass is turned about forty times over eleven months and every turn is a fire and a half and somebody standing beside it with their hands on iron; there is about one day a year the glass will come out of the arch without being broken by the change in the air, and it is chosen by the tide and not by the man, and if the money is not in the arch on the right day then there is no batch and the next one is a year after it. **The price is a person for eleven months, and the person is whoever is here, and there is one apprentice in that shed and she is twenty-four and paid nothing and has been for three years.**
- **The second fire.** A hole in the end wall with an iron plate in front of it, for the small work, and it is where a woman of twenty-four stands about six days in seven, **because the second fire cannot be left once it has been let in. If it is banked for an hour the glass in it goes cloudy and cloudy is not a thing you can mend.**
- **The guild has paid for a year twice already and got a pane twice, and this batch says it plainly without a speech: the tide table on the guild's wall is three months old and is three months of her with her hands on that bar, and the charter is nineteen years and eight months old and the woman who stood at that fire was called Mare and is dead and her name is inside the lid of the batch book because that is how you keep a name. ### The defence the guild built, and the breach, and the four things

- **The training wall is new in this batch and is the batch's spine for its last four chapters.** Two hundred and sixty yards of stone laid in courses on the **seaward side of a nine-foot hole in a gravel bar at the north end**, four hundred yards up the lane from the crossing. - **What it is for, in the trade's mouth, and the guild is right: a wall of stone does not stop water, it stops gravel.
