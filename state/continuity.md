# continuity

What has to stay true across chapters in volume 21. Anything printed here is a fact
about the page, not a plan.

## Calendar

Volume 21 opens on a Wednesday, the second day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week,
and closes on a Wednesday. Fifty mornings, five bands of ten.

Counters, all checked against the page by hand. `ch` is the chapter number:

| counter | formula | at `1031` | at `1040` |
|---|---|---|---|
| his morning | `ch − 250` | 781 | 790 |
| days after the settlement | `ch − 400` | 631 | 640 |
| days since the division | `ch − 446` | 585 | 594 |
| days since a page was read out with the door shut | `ch − 500` | 531 | 540 |
| days in the county of Kell | `ch − 554` | 477 | 486 |
| the cut across that palm | `ch − 685` | 346 | 355 |
| the standing offer | `ch − 700` | 331 | 340 |
| his own count of wrong things | `ch − 860` | 171 | 180 |
| Barnaby Crove's mornings of asking | `1 + (ch − 722)` | 310 | 319 |
| Wat Marshe's days in the county | `ch − 653` | 378 | 387 |

Two rules about these figures:

- Every `four hundred and …` mention that is a figure of days is the **Kell** figure.
  Each of the ten pages carries it twice, once in the descriptor line and once in the
  speech. The other `four hundred and …` phrases on a page are his count of wrong
  things, which is a figure of a person and is not to be read as days.
- The **offer is not part of a date line.** It is spoken in a mouth, with the
  subtraction out loud, and it is checked against `ch − 700` before the chapter is
  finished. On an off-morning nothing at all comes out of the stone.

## The water, which is a clock

The ladder runs from its own head. The head is `686`, the period is **eight**, and
it does not read the week: `686 − (ch mod 8)`. More water puts **fewer** steps under,
and that pairing is what the ten chapters say.

| inches on the top step | about this many of the ninety under |
|---|---|
| 4 | 90 |
| 8 | 80 |
| 12 | 70 |
| 16, and it holds | 60 |

**Known hazard, not a settled fact.** More water putting fewer steps under is
backwards, and it is physically wrong on the page. It is the spine of four chapters
of this volume and every figure of water, of days of the coming back, of every full
bank and every off-morning in the plan hangs off it. It was found and reported, and
left alone on purpose, because reversing it would move every full bank, every
off-morning and every anchor in four bands at once. **It is owed to a person and is
not settled.** Do not silently correct it in a chapter, and do not build on it
oversight.

**Off-mornings** carry no figure at all: no inches, no steps, no days of the coming
back. In this band `1034` is the off-morning, and it is also the morning the week
turns — the boundary falls **between** `1034` and `1035`, not inside either date
line. `1042` is owed as the sixth off-morning and the first boundary of band 5.

Two whole figures of weeks appear with no figure of days beside them, at `1032` and
at `1039`. Do not add days to either.

## Days of the coming back

Seven at 4 inches, six at 8, five at 12, four at 16, three on a full bank that has
just come off, two the morning after, one the day before it comes off.

## Places

All of these are named in earlier chapters and none is new in this volume:

- **the landing**, and the **dry strip** at the top end of it
- **the flight** of about ninety cut steps; **the top step**; **the fortieth step**,
  with iron in its seam
- **the chair**, standing on the flat of the landing, owner unknown, not given one
- **the lane**, and **the drain** at the bottom of it
- **the cart road**, and the **wall** at the bottom of it
- **the shed** behind the wheelwright's, about three hundred yards down that cart
  road — Wenna Pyle's own figure, first given at `973`, and not to be re-derived
- **the room with the door shut**, in a town in another county

No distance may be printed for the landing, the lane, the bank, the drain or the cart
road, and no figure of how far a voice carried down that road may be printed in any
wording. That absence is the subject of the volume, not an oversight.

## Objects

- **The two pieces of ash.** A forearm long, split down the middle the way wood
  splits when it is meant to, thick end left as it came apart. The second has a saw
  mark on the square end and two shallow notches cut in the flat of it, one of the
  two run out of the side. Both are on the dry strip. **A name is on the second one
  and it is wrong.**
- **The barrow.** Barnaby Crove's, and in band 5 it is asked whether the ash was cut
  for it.
- **The board.** Garrin Tolley's, inside his coat against his ribs, thirty-two notches
  cut in its edge. He asked whether he might cut a thirty-third and was refused with
  the working, and did not cut it.
- **The broom.** Corvin Tace's. It was left lying across the top step at the end of
  `1039`, and Ilyan carried it to the wall out of the wet, and was not thanked and
  did not expect to be.
- **The book.** Sena Dorr's, shut against her knee for twenty-three years, and the
  ninth of the nine positions, which is a public archive and not a stone.
- **The yoke.** Wat Marshe's, laid flat across the mouth of the lane at the end of
  `1037`, where it stops nothing.

## Language and house rules for this volume

- **No month. No season.** Weather and water only.
- `citizen` does not occur. No new final enemy. No new person: every name in volume 21
  was made in a mouth in an earlier chapter.
- The right of refusal is not restored in any wording.
- No line begins with a pipe or with `**`.
- **Ilyan is named in every chapter**, and has a want, a mistake and a cost of his
  own in every chapter, including the ones where he is right. His want is to be
  somebody; he said it out loud at `1000` and it is not to be re-argued as something
  else. His cost is that asking is the only thing that costs him nothing.
- A name is not a figure of people who heard it, and may not be given as *about n
  weeks* even when it is nearly true.
- A thing cut for a use is not that use's. This is the ordinary form. It is said in
  mouths and **is not given a name**.
- Nobody is relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked. Every occurrence of thank in this
  volume is a refusal, a negation, or a list of who is not to be thanked.
- Titles are written last, against the body. A title may not print the name of a
  thing, a giver, the age of a thing in a place, the ordinal of an off-morning, or
  any count of asking.
- A chapter may not end on the same inventory as its neighbour. All ten closing lines
  of band 4 use a different device.
