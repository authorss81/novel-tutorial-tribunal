# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment network. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and hearing-field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

### The reversal's consequences and the template

- **The sentence that does the damage is the sentence that helps somebody local first.** On the thirteenth day the room's first honest use of the ninth-bridge settlement was not obedience: it was Wenna Tace extracting eleven months of arrears out of a bench minute by pointing at the sentence that says a person not on a register is not a trespasser by that fact alone. **And the sentence that helped her is the *other* sentence that trapped her** — a keeper admits a person on foot, or with a handcart, or with a wheelbarrow, who offers no load. A barrow is a load.

### Stage 2, Seam Reader — what it is and what it costs

- **Ilyan is now Stage 2, Seam Reader, and the stage is earned by a practice, a failure, a changed decision, and a named physical cost. He is not Stage 3 and gains no new party, no new authority, and no new remedy.**

- **It works on documents about money and not on a man.** He practised it on Kolm's entry and term four together, got a usable and true result in about four seconds, and paid for it immediately: his left hip went cold and for six seconds he could not have picked up a pencil.
- **He was wrong in public.** He read the seam and said the burden was on the gate hand, and the field showed him it was not: a burden that can be paid to a person is a burden somebody pays, and the word that let her be paid for thirty years was *ward*, and the seam went past her and down through a thing with no name at all. **The misassignment is what he carries, which is why the failure is in the body and not in the memory.**
- **The afterimage, named and persistent:** (a) the **left leg**, from the hip down, occupied by a forty-four-year-old gate hand's weight, gone by the fifth bell if he stands on a bank, back in a house with the door shut, gone again by the fourth bell; (b) the **low water in his nose in two layers**, the day over the night and the flat over the water, in a lane with hay in it, unresolvable to either; (c) **a chain on a gate moving in his right hand about four hundred yards away at the wrong end of the afternoon.**
- **The blackglass token is the same kind of thing as the afterimage.** It is a spent seal of a passage that has already happened. It has never been warm. It goes warm on a bank, and touching it takes the low water out of his nose for about fifty minutes, and it comes back with the water over the flat and a third layer under both. **Ilyan has understood that the passage and the afterimage are the same operation, that one was done to him without consent and the other is being done to him by the same somebody, and that the token is why he is here. ### The ward — the batch's structural finding

- **The word *ward* is a guild's word for a duty, not a place.** It is in the ninth bridge's term book under a bridge heading, *duties carried, not discharged*, with a star against it meaning somebody had complained about it: *ward. not a place. the work a district owes and has not got round to. a guild's word, old, from before the book is anybody's grandmother's. it means a thing you do, not a thing you live in.* It was found by Ilyan, who cannot read a clause, in a book that had been used as a weight under four benches in the rain, and it was corroborated in three places: **the standing of the wards, a hundred and ninety years old, in which eleven of fifty-five duties have been performed and forty-four have not**; **the traveller book's own front leaf at Halloway, one ward, five duties, none performed, the last performer dead thirty-one years**; and **Hanne Kolm's sworn volume, the one document in this case that does not use the word at all**, because the duties of a lower road are the ones a landlord actually does and nobody on that road calls them a ward.
- **The entered finding: a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does.** A rate may be paid to a ward only if the ward is a body; the ward is not a body because it is not a person; a duty cannot be paid anything, it can only be given work; and the work goes to whoever is standing nearest. **This is why a woman stood at a gate for thirty years and why the word *ward* has let a duty be paid as if it were a wage.** The court has no power to make a seat, a name, or a body out of a piece of work, and Harrow said twice on the face of the record that the court has no power over the wards.
- **The two words this city has for the same poor — *the poor people* and *the carters, hauliers and gate hands* — are both wrong in front of witnesses**, and Abel Kearn, thirty-five, a builder, stood up and said he lives in the ward and is in neither of them. A count taken on the low water returned **five places**, and a place is the category the court came to get rid of.
- **Tarin Keel's count has no line in it: there is no way anywhere in this city to count the people who are not at the pump.**

- **The shared charter of the district of Halloway, nine sheets, five terms, made at the ninth bell on the seventeenth.** **The first four terms are copies of terms of the ninth-bridge instrument and are marked as copies. ### The appeal, heard

- **The first appeal in the record was served in four bells and two carts and it worked.** Fourteen names and one seal. **Corva Silt stood eleven minutes in a lane deciding whether a notice may be left with a person who has not asked for one, left it on the sill, knocked, did not wait, and wrote the time on the back in her own hand.**

- **The disposition, in three parts.** (a) **The limit:** the ninth-bridge settlement binds no span but its own; *on this span* is the court's own drafting, is correct, and **is not widened**; the claimant knew it and filed anyway, in his own words, and nobody improved them.
