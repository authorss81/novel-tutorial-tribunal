## 0. The ground

**THAT CITY** (forty-one miles off, 1 mile and a half between the room at the foot of the hill and the ninth marker, left on the sixth day of the eighty-sixth week and not returned to): ninety thousand people, thirty-one wards, Tallowgate, a press on the ground floor of a black building at the middle of the nine bridges with a form of six pages in its window, and a room at the foot of a hill where about thirty to forty people stand when a sheet is read out. **The Low Road, two cart widths, kept by Renn Calloway, fifty-eight, twenty-nine years, unpaid, eleven chalk strokes and no figures; the ninth marker with four hurdles of sawn ash, nine links of new chain, a plate wired on with one bent nail hole and nothing cut in the bottom corner, and a keeper's box three feet from the fourth hurdle; and on the south side four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half that has no name in any office document in the world and about two hundred crossings a day going up it.** **A hundred and forty people are dead of a fever. The fever is twenty-six weeks and five days old at 470.**

**HALLOWAY.** A market town of about four thousand people on the river Hallow, in the county of Ostrey, **not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench; the nearest counter is eleven miles off at Ostrey.** A stone market square, a market on a **Tuesday** (and one on a Friday in the fourth week of the eighty-eighth, the first in nine years, because it rained), a mill with one wheel, a poor rate, a water board of nine. **The House of Halloway: nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, elected for a year, sitting on forty-one stools in the Stone House in the winter with the door open and about two hundred people in the square, in the order of the wards. THE HOUSE DOES NOT SCALE: the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it. A sheet of forty-one names in the order of the wards is in the press, and against the ninth ward are five names and one room.** A corrected roll is a corrected constituency, and a constituency is a set of doors.

**THE STONE HOUSE.** A cold stone room with one hearth in the corner. **On the north beam, nine feet up and about five feet in from the wall, a board about four feet by two and a half** — a vellum on the face, a lead tag at the foot with four seals in it gone the colour of a wet stone. It comes down twice a year, on the audit day and the water sitting day, by two people onto a long table, in a hurry, with the fire lit, **and it has not been off the beam since the audit nine years back, and it was off it about two hours on 456. It has not been read out in a room in ninety years.** Against the west wall, a press with four leaves in it, unlocked, which any person of the town may read, and the copy of the roll, and a sheet of forty-one names, and a certified copy of a page from Kerby, and a sheet of ten things in the chair's own hand going in on the Saturday. **Any person of the town may read the copy of the roll.**

**THE FEN AND THE FENROW.** The Fen is about a hundred and forty acres of water meadow below the sluice; it floods, it was not divided, and it lies. **The Fenrow is nineteen houses along it, of which twenty-two households and about twenty-three people keep the sluice, and the two numbers were the same number for about a day and a half and are not the same number now.** The sluice has a wheel nine feet across turned by a four-foot bar, wound twice a day at the sixth hour and the twelfth by two people for an hour, **whether or not the gates are shut.** The sluice frame is the water board's and is about sixty years old, **and the north side has dropped three-eighths of an inch in nine years, measured with a straight edge and a nail at the eleventh hour of a Thursday in the rain, and a man of the county says it is a season and not two years.**

**THE RACES.** The Hallow takes the water. Below the sluice a race runs a mile and a bit to the tail of the mill. **The gates were shut on the eleventh day after the division at the sixth hour, for a scouring of nine days, and were opened again on the fourteenth day at the sixth hour, five days early.** Between the two, about four hundred people carried water four hundred yards twice a day from a standpipe at the top of the lane in two tin pails, one a hand's width narrower than the other. **The lane is now the worst four hundred yards in the county and will be until the frost, and a carter at the top of it will not take a load down.**

**THE WATER LIST.** A second book of the water board's, kept by the steward, **which goes by tenement because water goes to a building and not to a name. The Fen is on it, nineteen tenements, rated as property, in respect of water. It is a different book from the rate roll and the two do not agree and are not supposed to.** Established `461`.

**KERBY, TWENTY-TWO MILES OFF, ON A ROAD, WHERE THE COUNTY OFFICES ARE.** **The office of the county surveyor at Kerby keeps a book of returns on the sluices and weirs of the county. It has a return on the Hallow sluice made in the year before last and no other page about Halloway in that book at all. It cannot order the board to do a thing, make anybody answer, fine, rate, or put a name in a book of Halloway's. It makes a return and puts it in a book. A clerk of the office works the press and writes down the days that go past her. Any person in the county may walk in and ask for the page.** `469`

## 3. The money

**1,041 rated households, unmoved in eleven years, made in the week the common was sold** · **1,060 after the correction of the twentieth day after the division** · **water rate 2d a quarter = 8d a household a year: 1,041 × 8d = 8,328d = £34 14s; 1,060 × 8d = 8,480d = £35 6s 8d** · **the board's money is the quarter's: £35 6s 8d ÷ 4 = £8 16s 8d, and a corrected quarter on 1,073 is £8 19s 0d, and the heel is £9** · **the £14 of the scouring is NOT out of the water fund; it is 3½d a lot on the eighty lots, charged in the roll of this year** · **the roll is wrong by 112 pence a year: 24d on three doors and 88d on eleven rows** · **the copyist £2 a year for eleven years** · **a county pension of 11s = 132d against 19 houses × 9d = 171d, short 39d, and there is no form that lets him hand it over** · **the out-of-roll book would have cost the fund nothing at all and was never used.** All with the working in `state/current.md` §3.

## 4. What is true at the end of Chapter 470

1. **A covenant four hundred years old carries, on the back of its board, a clause a hundred and thirty-eight years old that no household of this town shall be required to take water, or to be entered, or to answer, against its will. A chair of the water board said out loud in a room that nobody in this town has ever been given it and nobody is going to be given it this year.**
2. **The right of refusal was taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years ago and has not been put back, and the clause is two years older than the taking of it, and nobody in Halloway can verify the date but him.**
3. **The out-of-roll book has never been written in in this town and was never offered to anybody but one woman, in a lane, on the eleventh day after the division, and she said no.**
4. **The nineteen houses at the Fen are on the roll as households as of the twentieth day after the division, by a steward's form, in four minutes, and not by the board's power.**
5. **The fenrow hold twenty-two households, not nineteen. Eleven other rows in the town are entered as land in some year and hold households. A twelfth is entered as land and has a dead man's name against it.**
6. **The roll stands at 1,060, is wrong by 112 pence a year, and cannot be corrected before the roll for the autumn is made on a day out of a day by a man who will do it correctly and will not know.**
7. **The north heel of the frame is a season. It is written on the wrong side of a leaf in a press any person of the town may read, and it is also in a book twenty-two miles off, and the bill is nine pounds and the board has £8 16s 8d in its hand.**
8. **A stranger read a notice out in a market square and a town knows his name, and a figure of his is in a book at a counter with *no answer* in a column and no person entered.**
9. **A woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off has written down that she has not answered his question, and she is right that a voluntary exit is a right and not a privilege, and he does not get the last word.**

## 5. What is not true and has not been made true

**That the fenrow own the water.** The water is common to all **households**, the row holds twenty-two households and about twenty-three people, and nothing in Chapters 451–470 gives them a household by their own asking or gives them the right of refusal. **That the striking of clause nine was of that clause alone** — the copyist could not learn it, the paper is not of this town, and it is ninety years since anybody looked. **Who struck it and why.** **What the four letters and a stroke in the witness line say.** **What the clerk's nine-year count at `401:39` was.** **The number of rated households in Halloway, which is somewhere between 1,060 and 1,073 and cannot be got.** **Whether a corrected roll may be un-corrected.** **Whether a board of this county may borrow against the next quarter, and if it may, what the two documents are.** **Anything in that city**: the office of Regent of the Shelf is not held, the second seat is vacant, the bar is up, Hessa Marl's book of the visits has no office, Fenna Rusk's roll is under a stone, Tarin Keel is walking north, Kerwin Dace's return came back stamped, and about a hundred and forty people are governed by a signature that is not a person's.
