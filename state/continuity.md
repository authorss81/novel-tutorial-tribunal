# Continuity State

## Canon established at bootstrap

- Veyra is a real, living world with civic promises that physically affect roads, water, weather, boundaries, inheritance, and other shared systems.
- The Bench is a distributed judgment institution. Its rules are expressed through covenants, challenges, witnesses, burdens, rulings, and temporary exceptions.
- The System is the translated interface for Ilyan, not an omniscient god. It translates formal Bench records, notices, and field boundaries entered into his open case; it does not translate ambient speech, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom. It cannot read minds, force consent, resurrect the dead, or guarantee moral truth.
- The current Bench descends from the Loom, built collectively by the First Compact. The Warden’s Codification followed the Great Silence and removed or restricted the public right of refusal.
- The First Witness is the old archive intelligence inside the Codification. It is seeded early as a plausible final adversary, not a late surprise threat.
- The Shale Mirror is a one-way translation fragment of the Loom, not the source of Veyra. It connected Morrowlight’s cancelled game to Veyra. Ilyan’s defect report is the record, his tester signature is the external signature, and the repeated reports together form the first external null witness. A separate one-use null passage used a legacy emergency clause to pull him into a hearing field; the Shale Mirror relay itself cannot move people.
- Ilyan Vester is a male former game tester, age thirty-one, with pattern-recognition and documentation skills but no initial supernatural protection. His arc moves from seeking acquittal and escape to sharing responsibility and relinquishing authorship.
- Sera Quill is the assigned recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing. Nadia Kestrel is an Earth anchor and non-romantic co-witness; Tarin Keel and Bram Orren are recurring allies with independent wants.
- The planned final verdict rejects both the game reading and the prison reading. Veyra becomes a plural civic order in which rules are visible, reviewable, refusable, and revisable.

## Opening continuity established in prose

The first movement begins on Earth and shows Ilyan’s involuntary crossing before the Orison action. The signed *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report was never merged, but its sequence was recognized as an external challenge. The black shale fragment beneath Morrowlight’s room answered the wording; the living-person crossing came from a separate legacy null passage. Ilyan carried a spent blackglass token into Orison with no return route, no automatic burden, and no informed consent. The passage consumed the ninth bridge’s emergency reserve and worsened a western-joint drift that was already present.

Ilyan lands at the ninth bridge as the First Defendant. Tamsin Ro, age ten, is rescued from beneath a bridge whose charter protects registered lawful travelers but excludes the Ro household. Oren Sedge’s bridgekeeper crew uses a clamp, drive pin, and named load path to keep the child alive. The rescue does not settle Neris Ro’s access to work, housing, or the bridge.

## Batch 0001 established facts

- The System translates only formal records, notices, and the visible boundary of Ilyan’s open case. Tarin interprets urgent guard speech, Oren interprets maintenance shorthand, and Sera explains legal distinctions. The Bench, not the System, decides and preserves rulings.
- The challenge requires claimant, affected parties, disputed condition, burden, and remedy. The current bridge charter lacks a clear maintenance-review route and any enforceable sunset or exit. Review, appeal, and exit are distinct.
- Ilyan accepted a bounded first-field witness duty sourced to the summons: his identity, report, and attendance become public; the duty ends when the field closes. Refusing would withdraw his own claim and access request, not Tamsin’s eligibility for local review.
- Oren’s old saltglass workroom plate preserves a maintenance-review condition absent from the current public charter. A one-bell maintenance exception belongs to Oren’s named crew and one load path, not to Ilyan or Tamsin. It held only while the crew performed the inspection.
- The eastern district’s petition copied old language and treated an unlisted person’s refusal as a danger to every lawful traveler. When admitted as a disputed precedent, the court physically coupled its backing plate to the current bridge record; the bridge responded to the competing conditions.
- The first night field used an announced one-bell interval. Harrow physically removed the old working plate and inserted the current certified duplicate. The old plate was sealed in the court locker; no archival plate was magically rewritten.
- A continuity line, “Continuity preserves the record before remedy,” appeared beneath ordinary formal translation. Its source remains unknown. The First Witness is not identified.
- Ilyan filed Neris’s private flood account without permission. The Bench preserved the copy; Sera marked it disputed, halted the access request, and suspended representation. The original account remains with Neris; the court copy is in Sera’s custody. The names in the account were not consented to and are not public testimony unless separately offered.
- Tamsin voluntarily offered only that the warning bell rang before the collapse. Neris offered the household’s register, work, and housing facts but refused the flood account. Their consent was renewed separately and did not restore trust automatically.
- Sera’s limited provisional representation resumed after the withdrawal/dispute notation and Ilyan’s acceptance that his future claims would be public. She filed a licensing recommendation for the full hearing but is not formally licensed yet.
- Chapter 10 grants a provisional stay and limited provisional standing for Ilyan: the Ro household may use the bridge for one review cycle, with a public review date at the seventh morning bell after the ruling. Oren’s named maintenance burden and Ilyan’s attached public record are finite. The current charter is not amended. Appeal, durable exit, housing/work protection, and the full local case remain unresolved.
- Ilyan reaches early Stage 1, Case Opener, through the accepted challenge, willing advocate, and bounded stay. He can request one narrow provisional exception but cannot choose another person’s burden or reassign a cost.
- Sera’s public surviving fragment says, “the governed may answer no,” while the current Bench text says, “the governed may petition for correction.” The original Quiet Chamber page’s binding sleeve is empty, and the inventory records that original page’s removal before Ilyan’s crossing. Sera’s private duplicate, which was present the night before the first field, disappeared during or after that field. The record does not yet establish whether the two losses share a hand; the remover and chain of custody are unknown.

## Power and failure constraints

No respawns, save points, character replacement, or unearned level-ups. A failed ruling remains consequential. A burden must be named and may not be silently assigned to an unwilling person. Death is permanent. Ilyan grows through practice, failure, relationships, changed decisions, and permanent costs. A Foundation Challenger may open a blank interval only with notice, consent from people remaining in the full spill zone, and safe withdrawal margins; the final use permanently costs Ilyan the lived memory of filing the *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report.

## Ending constraints

The final adversary is the First Witness already introduced in the Bench. Ilyan must reject the Final Exception even though it will work, give up permanent authority and a personal return route, and help create distributed regional charters. The final image shows an ordinary person refusing a specific rule with visible consequences and remaining part of the community. No new cosmic threat is planned.

## Batch 0002 established facts

- The Eastmere district’s petition and Merefen’s neighboring Saltwheel Road case use wording derived from the ninth-bridge stay. A local clerk may lawfully reuse public wording as a precedent witness; the copy is not a ruling, and adoption is distinct from authorship. The copied petition remains mounted as a public blackglass record and can be coupled by a clerk during a deliberate comparison.
- The affected local places are the Saltwheel Road and the unregistered Cinder Ward. The name Cinder Ward is local usage: the pump workers use it and the district’s papers have no word for it. In Chapter 11, before Ilyan reaches the road, the only name available is the unlisted ward. Local witnesses and workers include Joren Vell, Dalla Rusk, Lio Fenn, Mava Rusk, Meral the petition clerk, and Marshal Tovin Dain. Dalla is Mava’s sister; they argued about the road before the district ever did. They do not form one group and retain separate practical wants and consent boundaries.
- Tovin Dain is the same Merefen marshal in Chapter 15’s ward enforcement, Chapter 17’s assembly witness line, and Chapter 20’s hearing objection. He is an institutional actor throughout, not three officials.
- Ilyan’s attached review is limited to observation, sequence, and documentation of the copied record and disclosure. He is not a claimant or advocate for Cinder Ward. Sera’s provisional scope extends only to that copied-record review; her family appeal remains independent.
- The copied rule makes an unlisted ward’s refusal trigger a road-wide maintenance and closure burden. The actual unsafe condition is a broken drain/water sleeve, altered load marks, and a marked cart route. The district’s “temporary” period originally had no visible end. In Chapter 12 the damage is only discovered: a loaded cart exposes a hollow seam and cracks the crossing-side marker, and Joren states the course has not gone through. The channel breach and the marker’s removal from its socket happen in Chapter 14, when an unheeded cart finishes the crack.
- The district’s continuation rests on a formal entry line in the active petition book. The renewal takes effect only when a clerk enters it, and in Chapter 19 Ilyan watches a district clerk from Merefen cross that line at a watched hour, with Tarin’s record hand beside the book. The entry is lawful until a court says otherwise.
- The common register infers acceptance from continued use. The district’s own added line reads **acknowledgment inferred from continued use**, and the register does not recognize a ward refusal as a separate civic act. This is the district’s strongest and least visible weapon, because it does not need a signature.
- The local road arrangement names the route, work, hours, materials, warning line, water route, public review, and exit condition. The ward’s refusal is recorded, and refusal cannot be used to remove water, shelter, or the right to speak at review. The arrangement ends at the first morning bell unless affected people choose a new interval; in Chapter 20 they choose one additional marked interval. It is not a general precedent and does not amend the copied petition.
- Mava Rusk refuses a separate maintenance acknowledgment but accepts only the named local work and public review. Joren refuses to sign a clean inspection until the ward has a hearing place. Dalla offers the pump and water route; Lio offers the work and work record. None of them speaks for every resident.
- Tarin’s neutral boundary and public witness list survive a containment order. He does not use force against residents or guards. A loaded cart breaks the crossing-side marker and shifts the water route; Tarin then lifts the marker out of its socket. Beneath the district’s newer plate is the wording, “Completion does not erase the cause of refusal,” closely matching Ilyan’s private Earth report. Tarin secures the physical marker, so the socket is empty in later chapters; the stone is not at the road. The speaker and author are unknown.
- Ilyan’s Chapter 13 filing is the batch’s deliberately failed remedy. The Bench preserves the levy ledger entry by entry and refuses to hold local enforcement. The distinction between precedent witness and ruling becomes official while the levy keeps collecting. Later successes in this batch are therefore escalations, not the same answer repeated.
- The original Quiet Chamber page was removed from its shelf before Ilyan’s crossing and its empty sleeve remains under archive custody. Sera’s private duplicate was present the night before the first field and disappeared during or after that field. The dates remain distinct.
- The Quiet Chamber chain contains a formal continuity request with a valid office seal, a clipped continuity cadence, and a C—2 marginal code. The code is ambiguous. The reveal is staged once and then escalated, never repeated: the Kest fragment is found in the levy ledger (Chapter 13), the older witness index gives the full name Sava Kest while the active witness list removes it (Chapter 18), and the old bridge plate’s edge notation shows S. Kest in a second location (Chapter 20). Chapter 17’s comparison delivers a different fact instead of restaging the fragment: the local petition was assembled from the public wording **before** the Quiet Chamber margin was rewritten, so the copier and the person who tidied the record afterward are not the same person, and the archive was made to agree with a copy that already existed.
- A second paper using Ilyan’s private sequence carries the C—2 code and a bridge-plate route mark. Its receipt is dated before the ninth-bridge public notice and before Ilyan’s arrival. These are chain clues, not proof of a culprit or a single voice.
- The ninth bridge’s one-cycle stay remains provisional at the seventh morning bell after the Chapter 10 ruling. The current charter is not amended; enforceable appeal, exit, and durable housing/work protection remain unresolved. The bridge case, historical refusal right, and authorship mystery are not resolved.
- Ilyan remains early Stage 1, Case Opener. He can document an attached review and request a narrow challenge with Sera, but cannot assign another person’s burden, force a witness, rewrite a plate, or choose a local remedy. Sleep deprivation and ordinary road strain are not new powers.

## Batch 0003 established facts

- The batch takes place over roughly a week in Orison: the guild’s work notice, the household records, the public inspection and the Lower Ward Assembly; then Ilyan’s public account; then Sera’s room and the records room; then the inspection refusal, the recovery work, the evidence session; then the morning of the seventh bell, six hours before the bridge review.
- **The missing maintenance term now has physical teeth.** Under the current ninth-bridge charter the keeper may summon a pause but may not release the span. Oren can ring a warning bell and the span does not stop. The clause that allowed a named keeper to pause a load after a named inspection is the same clause that was removed from the charter.
- **The guild’s board is the instrument.** The guild roster is six named crews and ninety-four hands; the bridge levy pays four hands at the count before the first bell, six at each change, and nobody after dusk. The notice lists Ilyan as *attached record — documentation of sequence and public record. No maintenance labor assigned.* The absence of his name from the labor column was read by the district as proof that no named body carried the maintenance, and the assembly’s later limited word formalized the restriction.
- **The attached name is not a force on the stone; it is a force on the people who read it before they reach it.** A cart driver seeing Ilyan’s name in a work slate’s authority row changed his line, took the turn fast, and broke the eastern apron. A guild apprentice named Coll refused to read the scale aloud while that name was on the board; Oren took the book and signed beside him.
- **Meridith Vale, the entering clerk from Chapter 19, is the same man throughout this batch.** He paints Ilyan’s name into the authority row under a four-year-old standing instruction, delivers the district’s materials-store proposal, runs the routine maintenance update that replaced the affected-party column, and enters the new two-book ledger. His name goes on the seal and not on the condition. He is not a villain; he is the mechanism.
- **Acknowledgment inferred from continued use has a stronger local variant: the household’s crossings were credited as labor.** From the night of the stay, a half-hand per crossing was credited daily against the guild’s wage bill as *unlisted support*, reducing the guild’s wage bill. The Ro household was paying for the bridge in footsteps. The line is entered in the guild’s ledger and reprinted in the district’s new ledger as a *position*; it is struck from the wage board in Chapter 30 and recorded in the fourth column instead.
- **The Ro household’s four records.** Access (crossing hours, load path, review date), housing (the lower-road room, the lease, the key), work (the dye-yard shift and record), and testimony (four people, four choices, four limits) are separated and given four recipients. The district’s response is that four records about a household with no recognized address are four doors where there was one locked gate.
- **Neris’s refusal of the maintenance acknowledgment is grounded in the charter’s missing exit.** Signing a maintenance acknowledgment inside a charter with no maintenance review, no appeal, and no exit would bind the household to a rule it could never leave. She refuses the signature and accepts the four concessions around it. Tamsin’s offered fact remains only that the warning bell rang before the bridge moved; she repeats it once, in the workroom, where the span is visible while she is speaking, and refuses the assembly.
- **Ilyan’s public account (Chapter 25) is the batch’s political cost.** He rests his confession on the word *provisional*: every limit in the stay he accepted was a limit on the court — when the court must look again — and nothing in the stay stopped a clerk from taking its shape and using it somewhere else. He did not ask for that clause because asking would have meant asking to amend a charter, and he was relieved not to have to. He refuses general authority and refuses innocence. He produces his Earth reproduction sequence publicly and permanently a second time. The lowland conservancy withdraws its citation and its men ride home. The stay as recorded in Chapter 10 contains no general-adoption clause; the confession is to a limit he never asked to have removed, not to a sentence he never saw.
- **The assembly’s limited word (Chapter 24) is a restriction Ilyan did not ask for.** The attached name may be cited as a record and may not be cited as a permit, schedule, levy, or acknowledgment, nor entered as the bearer of labor. The assembly also requires a public account of his first bridge testimony in three days. The district invokes the Bench’s own Chapter 13 refusal to hold enforcement to defeat Ilyan’s request for a hold on the materials store.
- **The district’s working schedule book and the printed form.** A routine maintenance update replaces the affected-party column and retains the old version in a strongbox in a rented room. The version-three page was cut from the binding gutter with a blade. The replacement column was not hand-written; it was cut from a form printed on court stock in print run 114, distributed to the district maintenance office and to the bridge plate office, received at the second destination six days before the ninth-bridge public notice. The chain is nine ordinary steps. No person is identifiable in it.
- **The maintenance-update mechanism is lawful and invisible.** A replacement is not an event, so no removal appears in the working book. The word *replaced* does the work of a crime.
- **Print run 114 was authorized by a preservation authority** — an office signed with a stamp and no person, whose function is to keep records through a period in which records are being kept. The authorization page was cut out of the print shop’s stock ledger and replaced with an authorization of the same size, stock and week. The same kind of office appears on the C—2 routing. Preservation and enforcement are the same signature. Sera spends one question a day at that desk and has not gone back a second time.
- **Oren pays eight hours of salt-crack work out of the guild’s own account** rather than the levy, in the same entry as Ilsa’s refused schedule, so that the district cannot read the refusal as a refusal of work. It is not a filing and not Ilyan’s win.
- **Bevin Larch** is the lower ward’s schedule clerk. He appears in Chapter 27 at the rope with a notice he could not deliver to anybody.
- **District capacity is now legible:** the ninth bridge belongs to the lower ward, whose maintenance office is Vale’s office; Dain attends as the enforcing officer of the adopting district, in a borrowed capacity with no standing on the bridge; Maren Kett speaks for the eastern routes. Vale’s acknowledgment in Chapter 30 is served by the lower ward’s own office, not by an outside district.
- **A request to reopen the ninth-bridge record before the review suspends every relief entered under that record, including the provisional stay.** The stay is the only lawful thing that puts Tamsin Ro on the span. This is the fact that stops Ilyan in Chapter 26 and again in public in Chapter 29.
- **Sera’s terms (Chapter 26).** The family record is hers; Ilyan may not read, copy, or enter anything from it in the bridge case. He may trace custody in the public bridge and copied-precedent records and may not infer a person from a sequence. She is not a mechanism he reaches for when the road is bad.
- **Ilsa Bray, ninth-bridge keeper and load-counter, refuses a forward schedule** reading *until reviewed and thereafter* with a wage line that has no hours. She offers the physical defect instead: on the third run the west joint comes back to its mark, walks two fingers out, and comes back. Her refusal is recorded by Oren in the guild’s own book as *offered / declined* and is not treated as consent or sabotage.
- **The smallest request of the batch succeeds**: one span, one joint, named hands, paid, ending at the seventh morning bell. No effect on any other road, span, keeper, or district.
- **The sealed saltglass plate is a load record, not a keeper’s plate.** Its cut edge is the load-book edge, and the letters **S. Kest** are cut there. Under the old charter a bridge crew kept two witnesses: the keeper and the book-keeper. Ilsa’s grandmother’s sentence: *a book without a second hand is only a record.* The older witness index gives the full name **Sava Kest**, removed from the active list. The removal is old: the salt has gone white in the cut.
- **The two-finger drift is the bridge’s live physical problem.** The joint settles a fraction on every load and returns; the joint is settling in the direction it settled before the brace, and the brace is holding while the joint is not. It has not yet become an emergency. It is a maintenance-review problem, not a collapse.
- **The new two-book ledger (Chapter 28).** Left book: hands, hours, materials, rate, paid, signature. Right book: position, body, offered, declined. The two are sewn together so that no one can separate the paid work from the refused positions without a signature, a version number, and an hour. The form’s print strip carries the run 114 distribution and the C—2 route.
- **The local-institution review could not convene.** The Lower Ward Assembly has nine benches and six filled; the ward seats cannot be filled because the ward is not an address, and a notice to a petition box is an undelivered notice. Ilyan refuses to fill the ward seats by treating the ward as a single claimant. Tarin offers a neutral boundary and a public witness list without an order and without force. The sitting proceeds as a bounded evidence session with no findings, no decision, and no order.
- **The C—2 routing index (Chapter 29) is the batch’s chain payoff.** The ninth bridge’s maintenance-review condition was rerouted from the bridge plate office to the C—2 continuity desk on the day the current working plate was executed, and the pre-arrival paper carrying Ilyan’s private Earth sequence carries the same C—2 and bridge-plate route with a receipt that falls inside the index’s gap. The C—2 desk has no named clerk and no incoming index after the rerouting date; only a stamp. The two documents may have passed through the same process. No person is identified. The First Witness is not identified.
- **The district serves the maintenance acknowledgment on the guild’s crew** after the household refuses it, assigning the burden to *their successors, until the charter is amended*. Oren refuses on the guild’s own ground and without Ilyan's record, and the guild wins that on its own terms: it will work to its own chalk wage board, at named hours and the levy rate, and it will not carry a term it cannot use.
- **Ilyan's Chapter 30 request is a record, not a remedy.** The Bench records the local arrangement as found, states that the charter is not amended, and names appeal, enforceable exit, and durable housing and work protection as unresolved and not addressed. The assembly’s clerk records that the attached record-holder declined to request the amendment sought by three of the four delegations present.
- **Tamsin’s recorded line:** *I am not a reason for the bridge.* She asked for it to be written down and the clerk wrote it into the file that goes to the bridge review at noon. It is the only entry since the first field written entirely by the person it describes.
- **The plate and the index are one document, cut once.** The comparison is a result, not a new name: same hand, tool, sitting and week, with the salt gone white in both, which gives the plate a date the index never did. The load book and the witness index were cut together. It remains one act and no person. Do not restage this as a re-reveal.
- The pale line on the deck is the joint’s permanent set. The two fingers are what a single load walks before it comes back. Together they are the difference between a bridge that has settled and a bridge that is still moving.
- **Tarin and Joren added:** the ward’s second pair of hands belongs to Pev Sorrel, who holds the whistle at the road corner. Hanne Kolm keeps a twelve-year unsworn landlord’s book; it is entered and sworn in Chapter 30 after Neris refuses to give anything for it. Halden Moot is the Lower Ward Assembly’s quorum clerk and is procedurally correct throughout. Hesk Ardan is the lowland conservancy cart owner who crosses four times and then withdraws the citation.
- Ilyan remains early Stage 1, Case Opener. He gains no new perception. His growth in this batch is a changed judgment and a refusal: he writes a general rule, does not file it, does not destroy it, and says why in a room.
