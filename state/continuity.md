## 0. The ground

**THIS CITY.** Ninety thousand people, thirty-one wards, a hall and a counter in each. A low row of houses with a step worn into a curve, Tallowgate at the low end, a chandler's shop with a room behind it, a black building at the middle of the nine bridges with a press on the ground floor at a penny a page and a window in the room, and a public room at the foot of a hill where about thirty to forty people stand when a sheet is read out, every day for fifty days. Nine hundred doors in a ward, forty of them with somebody under twenty behind them.

**THE ROAD.** The Low Road, kept by Renn Calloway for twenty-nine years, unpaid. Two cart widths, a ledge on the north side; four hundred yards along the head of the ninth weir and then east across flat for about a mile. At two miles the ninth marker: an iron post in a concrete drum, a sill of three courses, a plate wired on with two nail holes, one bent, a name at the head and none at the foot, nothing cut in the bottom corner; four hurdles of sawn ash at knee height and nine links of new chain; a keeper's box with a roof, a shutter and a bench, three feet from the fourth hurdle. The south side: four yards of mud and a ledge of a foot and a half with water under it, nothing on it at all, a barrow that has gone up it every morning for nine years, about two hundred crossings a day.

**THE WEATHER.** Flat, wind off it, wet through October to the end of these chapters. Four bells go over Tallowgate at the change of the light. Rain through the last ten chapters, heaviest on the night of the sixth day of the eighty-fifth week and on the last two mornings.

## 4. What is true at the end of Chapter 450

1. **A person of this place may be entered, removed, conveyed, counted, and stood up for by a ward, and the right of refusal is unrestored and nobody in this city has one.**
2. **The office has been right about everything it has printed in this city for fifty days — fifteen or sixteen documents — and that is still the difficulty.** The last three were the exception, the request for representatives, and the terms.
3. **The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held; the second seat is vacant; the council of the Shelf has to sit.**
4. **Until it does, no bar upon any road in this city may be taken down and no plate concerning any road may be put up** — so the four hurdles, the nine links, the plate and the box are still up, for a reason nobody here asked for.
5. **Twenty-two of thirty-one wards have named persons who may be asked about the roads: twenty-four in the first lines, twenty-one in the second, and one line with the keeping of a box in it and nobody in it. Nine wards named nobody and are on no paper. The office will use the figure 22.**
6. **A form in a press window asks a ward for a person, and a man who cannot be entered is on the fifth page of it and a woman who can be asked about him is under him.**
7. **A declaration of continuity signed by something that is not a person governs everybody in a register of the Shelf, alive or not, and about a hundred and forty of them are dead.**
8. **The bar is up, the south side has no name anywhere, about two hundred crossings a day go up it in the mud, and a road is kept.**

## 5. What is not true and has not been made true

The hundred and forty dead are on no document by name and have not been summed or partitioned. The twelve shillings, the £2 4s, the bell, the porch, the corridor and the three hundred blank names are unpaid and unblanked, and the slate is worth £5 2s 6d that is nobody's claim. The boy of eight has never been named aloud in a room. Tarin Keel's walk and Kerwin Dace's stamped return are open. The clerk's nine-year count at `401:39` may not be resolved. **The right of refusal was taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years ago and has not been put back, and nobody in this city has said what the First Witness is, and nobody has asked it.**
