## 0A. WITHERMERE, the ground of this volume, and it is a new substance

**A market town of about ELEVEN HUNDRED PEOPLE on the north bank of a slow river at the eastern edge of a fen, in the hundred of Withermere, in the COUNTY OF KELL, which is a different county and a different hundred and a different kind of ground from the county of Aldwick.** Not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench of any kind; the nearest counter is **twenty-two miles off at Kell.** **A MARKET ON A FRIDAY**, and it has not moved in about two hundred years, and it is not Bramblefold's Thursday and is not a joke about it. About eleven streets, a green about two hundred yards across with a pump at the top and about eleven houses with their backs to it. **A weigh-house about sixty feet long of sodden brick and tarred oak with a crane-beam out of the middle of its roof, and the season book is on its NORTH WALL.** A smithy, a chandler, a brewing house, an inn called the **Green Paw** with four beds and a parlour, kept by a woman of about fifty. Ground: **no chalk anywhere. Peat, sallow, willow, sedge, and water-meadow hay cut in a fortnight.** There is no mill, no hearth, no beam, no water board, no stone room with a door open, no meeting-house and no chancel, and **the fen has no hill in it anywhere.**

**THE SEASON BOOK. A sheet of parchment about two men long and six feet long, hung from two rings on the north wall of the weigh-house under a lamp on a bracket that is lit at the seventh hour, ruled in THREE COLUMNS and read in the open.** The columns are **the ground**, with a parcel and a number of acres; **the taking**, with what goes on it and how many; and **the year**, one figure in a square at the right-hand end of each line with the figures of the years before going left in a smaller hand, about two hundred and thirty years of a hundred telling itself when it was last spoken to. **The date at the foot, in the clerk's hand, is the day the water was last over the tally stone, and that is the whole of the authentication it has. There is no seal, no counter, no office, no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says what may be written on it, and there is no column with a person's name at the top of it and there never has been one.** THE NAME OF THE HOLDER GOES IN THE COLUMN THAT IS THE GROUND, BECAUSE THERE IS NOWHERE ELSE TO WRITE IT, AND THAT IS NOT THE FAULT OF ANYBODY.

**THE RATE. Two shillings and a penny the acre a year, of which four pounds a year is the water-clerk's wage and the rest cuts the ditches.** The eleven parcels are **two hundred and ten acres**, which is **£21 17s 6d** a year. A holding of six acres owes **12s 6d**; the smallest of the eleven, two acres, owes **4s 2d**. **A DITCH ON THIS FEN FILLS IN ABOUT TWO YEARS WITHOUT THE RATE, and a water-clerk of fifty-eight said so on a step in a market and a man of fifty-four proved it in the same number, and the two figures agree.** **THE RATE IS NOT A PAYMENT FOR HAVING ACRES. IT IS THE DITCH, and without it a ditch on this fen fills in in about two years and the low meadow goes under in a wet year, and that is said out loud in a market at `555` and it is the whole of the plot.**

**THE RISING. Once a year the river comes over the **tally stone**, a block of limestone with a line cut round it at the level of a full water, put there about two hundred and thirty years ago by a man whose name is on it. THE DAY THE WATER IS OVER THAT STONE IS THE FIRST DAY OF THE YEAR, AND EVERY SEASON IN THE HUNDRED LAPSES ON THAT MORNING AND IS RE-LETTED ON THAT MORNING, and about midday the clerk writes the new ones on the wall. IT IS A DAY OF A RIVER AND NOT A DAY OF A NUMBER, AND A YEAR IN WITHERMERE IS THE TIME BETWEEN ONE CROSSING OF THAT STONE AND THE NEXT, AND THERE IS NO MONTH AND NO QUARTER AND NO RECKONING FROM A FROST.** In this volume it is `566`.

**THE SEASON SALE. At a Withermere market, between the hour of ten and the hour of twelve, anybody may stand at the bottom of a stone step with an oak bar across it and offer a parcel's season for sale, and the clerk writes it on a slate and it goes on the wall. THE PARCELS OFFERED ON THE STEP ARE PARCELS WHOSE SEASON IS NOT ALREADY TAKEN UP, and there is no step for taking a season off a man who holds it. Nine were offered on the Friday in this band and none of the eleven was among them.**

**THE HUNDRED. About thirty men, one for every holding that has a season on it, meeting at midday on the rising in the weigh-house. A stranger may stand in it and speak. About three hundred people are in the green in the same hour.**

**THE THREE BOARDS OF THE SLUICE. Withermere has a six-board gate and three are in, and the level is a foot below the tally line. SIX BOARDS holds it off the seven holdings and off the cut on the low meadow, and puts a foot of water on four closes and on the low common in about eight days. THREE BOARDS keeps the camp on the low common dry and lets the four closes fill in over about four years. THE FOUR CLOSES ARE NOT RICHER THAN THE SEVEN AND THE SEVEN ARE NOT BETTER MEN THAN THE FOUR, and a man of fifty-eight has said both halves of that out loud in a room, and about four people in that room believe the first half and about nine believe the second and none believe both.**

**THE COME-OVERS. Eleven households, about sixty-one people, off a fire in the sallow on the high ground four days east. They are on the common on the low side in eleven tents under a licence to stay until the water turns, which is the rising. **THEIR INSTRUMENT IS ONE SHEET OF PAPER IN A SACK UNDER A CHURN WITH A FIGURE AND A DAY ON IT AND NO NAME ON IT AND FOUR WORDS AT THE TOP THAT SAY *for the common, in season*, AND THERE IS NO COPY OF IT ANYWHERE IN THE COUNTY AND THE CLERK HAS NEVER SEEN IT.** The figure is **fifty-eight**, and there is no form in that hundred that says how many people a licence covers, because the licence is the number and the number is the season. The common is **about eight hundred acres** and belongs to nobody.

**A NEW PLACE GETS A NEW DISTANCE AND THE DISTANCE GOES INTO THIS FILE ON THE DAY IT IS INVENTED — index rule 15, and it has caught a real error in this repository twice.** **ALL THE DISTANCES OF VOLUME 12: Coldcombe is twenty-one miles from Bramblefold and its wharf is a mile and a half below the town, where the river turns. WITHTHERMERE IS FROM BRAMBLEFOLD TWO DAYS' ROAD TO THE WHARF AT COLDCOMBE AND ONE DAY DOWN THE WATER, WHICH IS THREE DAYS AND ABOUT FIFTY MILES, and the road he walked was the twenty-one miles on the second day of the hundred and first week, from the sixth hour to the ninth. THE WATER IS ONLY UP FOR IT FOR ABOUT NINE WEEKS IN THE YEAR, and a man who misses the water stands at a wharf until the level drops out of the channel and there is no road round. KELL IS TWENTY-TWO MILES FROM WITHERMERE. THE HUNDRED IS ABOUT NINE MILES BY WATER AND ABOUT FOURTEEN BY A DROVE THAT IS IMPASSABLE FOR EIGHT MONTHS IN THE YEAR. A FARM FOUR MILES SOUTH. A HIGH GROUND FOUR DAYS EAST WHERE A FIRE HAS BEEN. A PLANK BRIDGE OVER THE LOW SIDE AT THE SEVENTH HOUR. A GATE ON THE MAIN DYKE.** **WITHERMERE AND KELL WERE BOTH INVENTED IN THE PHASE THAT WROTE CHAPTERS 551–560 AND BOTH DISTANCES WERE ENTERED HERE ON THAT DAY, WHICH IS THE RULE.**

**AND ONE THING THE NO-MONTH RULE IS OFTEN MISREAD AS, STATED HERE SO THAT A LATER BAND DOES NOT ARGUE WITH ITSELF: *March* AND *April* ARE CIVIL DATES. They are the date a letter is written and a year of somebody else's reckoning is kept in, and they are about the date a purse went round eleven parcels. THEY ARE NOT DIVISIONS OF THE WITHERMERE YEAR, WHICH IS RECKONED ONLY FROM THE RISING, AND NO CHAPTER MAY TURN A DAY OF APRIL INTO A SEASON.**
