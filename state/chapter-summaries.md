**641 — THE CROWN IS NINE DAYS OLD AND IS ASKED WHAT IT OWES, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAYS OUT LOUD THAT NOBODY DECIDED IT, AND IT IS THE SECOND TIME THAT ANSWER HAS BEEN GIVEN AND A DIFFERENT PERSON GAVE IT.** `clear` 141, the sixth day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Sunday, and his eighty-seventh day and his fortieth in Hask. The town does not sit on a Sunday and about nine people came down that cart road anyway because a question asked on a Saturday had not been answered. The board is still on the barrel where it stood since the Friday of the last week but one and the bench is standing outside in the sun. A woman of forty who is on the board puts the question back to the thing at the barrel, with the ground before the name, and counts its age out loud: **it is nine days old … it was put on that barrel on the Friday of the last week but one at about the fourth hour, and this is the Sunday of the next week, and Friday to Sunday is nine days, and I have counted it on my own hands and not out of a book.** Then the question of `640`, asked a second time and not answered the first: **if a Crown speaks for a person, is that person owed a copy of what it said about them.** A man of sixty-one stands up off the wall with the book of heads and carts under his arm, does not open it, and says the answer is three words. **Nobody decided it.** And he says why, with the working, and says the answer is the second time this answer has been given and that he is not the woman of forty-four and is a different person: there is no form in that county that says a copy may be made of a thing a body said in a room, and he has kept a book eleven years and has looked; a sheet of deal is about four feet by a foot and four inches and is a thing a person writes with a hand; **a word said by nine people at a barrel is not written by any hand, and there is no sheet for it, and there is no board for it except the board it was said at; and if a Crown speaks for a man of fifty-seven who came up that landing about nine times in about two years, there is nowhere to write what it said except in about nine heads** — and about nine heads in one room in a town of about eleven hundred people is a figure of one room, and a woman of forty-one said that on the Thursday of the last week and said it was no better than a boy's question. A man of thirty-one says the true of one half, is let to the fourth sentence and held, and then says out loud that **he is not asking for a copy of anything said about me, and I would like about four of you to notice that I did not ask**, and that he has wanted for about nineteen days for a body to speak for me, which is fourteen on the Tuesday of last week and five since. A woman of twenty-nine in the reed says in three sentences why she let him go on. And a man of thirty-eight says that Thursday was the fourth time the two copies have been in a room together and the fourth time they agreed, that it took him about nine hours of a boat and a pole and about nine minutes of reading, that nobody has ever once asked him to do it, and **that next Thursday is the fifth.**

**642 — A PERSON FROM OUTSIDE THAT COUNTY COMES UP THAT CART ROAD ON HER OWN LEGS AT ABOUT THE THIRD HOUR ON A MONDAY MORNING, AND THE FIRST THING SHE SAYS IS WHAT SHE IS.** `clear` 142, the seventh and last day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Monday, and his eighty-eighth day and his forty-first in Hask. Dust on her coat to the knee and a bag with nothing else in it but two letters with the seals unbroken, and nobody on that bank had sent for her, and nobody in four counties can be sent for about her, and she had not been asked to be in that county. About four people on that bank had heard of her and about nine had not. **He wanted to go to her before anybody spoke and did not, and it is the first time in forty-one days he has not gone first, and about four people noticed it and about nine did not.** Then she says it: **I am Sera Quill and I am twenty-eight and I am not of this county and I have not been asked to be, and I did not come because of a board on a barrel nine miles down this water.** And: **I came four hundred miles with two letters nobody has read, and both of them are in that bag with the seals unbroken, and there is a third thing in the inside pocket of his coat that came back the same four hundred miles, and I am not going to ask for any of the three.** And why she came is a boy of thirteen and not a Crown: about four people in four counties who have one of those sheets are going to come and find him, his mother cannot be sent for about, he cannot be sent for about, and there is nobody in four counties who can say to them that they may not. A woman of forty-one with two carts asks what everybody is standing in front of. He says her name once and says nothing else, and says he would like about four of them to notice he did not go to her before she spoke. A man of sixty-eight says what a person from outside is and counts the age of his own rule out loud, **twenty-five days, which is six hundred and forty-two less six hundred and seventeen**, and says that a Crown that wants to speak for every person nobody can be sent for about will want to speak for her, and that she has just said out loud she did not come because of it. A woman of seventy-one who keeps a house that is not hers comes up to the top of that bank and says to her that nobody has come to her door in eleven years and somebody has come four hundred miles to hers, and says out loud that she is not going to ask her anything else. She answers that she has come to carry what a speaking leaves and has no form for it. A woman of forty-one drives two days of a road herself with nobody on the shafts and says a cart with one wheel making a noise came up out of the low place with her driving it, and that nobody in this town can be sent for about four hundred miles. He puts a hand on the pocket where the page is and leaves it shut, and says it is still there and he has not read it and is not going to read it in a room to make a point.

**643 — SHE REFUSES OUT LOAD, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, TO LET A BENCH CARRY A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE, AND HE LOSES A SMALL POINT, AND A NAME IS SAID FLAT ONCE.** `clear` 143, the first day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Tuesday, and his eighty-ninth day and his forty-second in Hask. He is sitting on the bench against the gunwale of the Gull at about the seventh hour, and nobody had sat on it until the Friday of last week, and he had sat on it then with a knife in his hand and had not used it. **He wanted work, and he said so out loud, and it was a small want, and losing it cost him more than a large one would.** He asks, in the open, to carry a sheet nine miles up that water on the Thursday with the man of thirty-eight nine feet off, and says the four things he may already do and the one he may not. Then she says no, out loud, in front of about nine people, to the bench and not to the man: **I am refusing to let the thing you are sitting on be the thing that carries you … A bench is a place a person sits while another person works, and you have sat on it twice, and nobody thanked you for either sitting. If you pole nine miles with a sheet in a coat, then a record is checked by a person it cannot reach, and a record checked by a person it cannot reach is a record it cannot be wrong. And a record that cannot be wrong is a lid with a name on it, and a man of sixty-eight said that on the Friday of the eleventh week and was right. And I am not going to let a man I have come four hundred miles to stand beside stand in the door of it, and that is the whole of my no.** A man of sixty-eight gives the consequence and the rule's age with the subtraction, **twenty-six days, which is six hundred and forty-three less six hundred and seventeen**, and says it is a point about a pole and not about a hearing. He gets up off the bench, holds it, and says **losing a pole costs me more than losing a hearing would, because a hearing is a thing nine people do, and a pole is the only thing I can do with my own hands.** Then he says a name out loud, once, flat, for the second time in that county, and nothing happens. **The first cost of it falls on an ordinary woman of forty-nine** who came down that road with a man of fifty-two and did not know what a copy was on the Friday, and who says so out loud herself, and says about four people who have a sheet will come and ask her what she heard, and that she cannot be sent for about and the man she came with cannot be sent for about, and **there is nobody in four counties who can say to them that they may not.** She then says the true of the name and does not make it a side: **it is sincere about stopping another Great Silence … and it is wrong about what people owe a thing that kept them, and owing is a thing people decide in rooms, and a thing that decides it for them is a lid.** A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well says what carrying costs when eyes are poor, and that he came nine miles down the water on the Wednesday to hear the answer and is still here on the Tuesday and nobody thanked him for the coming. And a woman of forty-one says what a bench is a figure of: **about nine days of one person's working life spent sitting.**

**644 — A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT SAYS OUT LOUD WHAT THE COUNTER-BURDEN IS, AND IT IS NINE SENTENCES NOBODY IN FOUR COUNTIES CAN CHECK.** `clear` 144, the second day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Wednesday, and his ninetieth day and his forty-third in Hask. Ink was on the sill of that room before the fourth hour, left by a boy of thirteen mending a letter, and the door is open and about nine people are in it. **He wanted a slower hearing he could be in without being carried, and it was the first time in forty-three days he had asked for slowness.** Then she stands up off the wall and says out loud that she will bear it, in about nine sentences: she will write down what that Crown says about each person in her own hand on the day it says it; she will give each person the copy with her own hand and no other hand between; for no penny and no wage, because there is no form in this county that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel; whether that Crown stands or is abolished and whether the hearing is granted or is kept open by being said out loud; she will keep no second copy in any bag or coat or book; **nobody in that room can check whether she did it except the person she gave it to on the day; nobody nine miles up this water can check it, because she is not in any book nine miles up this water; nobody in four counties can check it except one person at a time, and one person at a time is the whole of it; and if she fails there is no form that says I may be sent for about it, and I am willing to be the one who fails without a form.** A man of sixty-one counts the nine sentences and says what nine sentences is a figure of, and gives the thousand and forty pence and the hundred and sixty pence again with the sum. A man of sixty-eight says a burden nobody can check is a burden nobody can be sent for about, that this town has one such person already and now has two, and that **a Crown that is a thing nobody can be sent for about, borne by a person nobody can be sent for about, is a thing that binds nobody, and I gave the rule that made it** — the rule being twenty-seven days old, and twenty-seven is six hundred and forty-four less six hundred and seventeen. A man of thirty-one says the true of it, is stopped in about the fourth sentence, holds it, and says out loud that he asked to be carried by a pole on Tuesday, was refused, held it, and **is now asking to be kept slow by a hand.** A woman of twenty-nine says in two sentences why she stopped him. A boy of thirteen holds up hands with ink to the second joint and says he wrote it on the Friday in about four hours and read it on the Saturday in about nine minutes and said both numbers out loud himself, and that there are fourteen of them in four counties, **and eleven and one and one and one is fourteen, and it is a figure of wood and not of people.** She asks him one thing: **how do you hold a pen for four hours without making a mark that is not yours.** The woman of forty-four says what ink is a figure of, and puts the two copies in the same breath: they are read against each other once a week in the open, the two have been in a room together four times and have agreed four times, the fifth is the next Thursday, and the three first were on Mondays. And she puts her own clean hands out beside his inked ones and says **that is why nine sentences nobody can check are heavier than fourteen sheets anybody can read against each other.**

**645 — TWO COPIES ARE IN A ROOM A FIFTH TIME AND AGREE, AND AN ORDINARY MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ ASKS A REMOVED WITNESS A SECOND QUESTION, AND THE ANSWER IS THAT NOBODY IS GOING TO DO IT, AND THE REASON IS ABOUT FOUR YEARS.** `clear` 145, the third day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Thursday, and his ninety-first day and his forty-fourth in Hask. A man of thirty-eight goes up the water at about the fifth hour with a sheet in a coat and the board in the same coat and is at that landing at about the ninth hour. **He wanted the fifth to be true without him touching it, and he stood nine feet off with a knife while it was read, and he did not put his hand on any copy.** In that yard: about nine people, a man of forty-three by the counter, a man of about fifty-two with a hand along the top of it, and a woman of forty-nine a little off who did not know what a copy was on the Friday of the last week but one. He reads the whole thing out loud in about nine minutes and does not hurry the last four lines, and the other is read after him line against line. **Thirty-two lines on both of them, the thirty-second the thirty-second on both, the thirty-first the thirty-first on both, the same day and the same Monday on both, and they agree, and that is the fifth time the two of them have been in a room together and the fifth time they have agreed.** He turns the board over and runs a thumb along the edge, and there are thirty-two notches cut in it, one for every line, and a notch is a thing a man can hold. Then he comes down and gets in at about the second hour of the afternoon and the two are read against each other again on the deck in the open in about nine minutes, and agree a second time that day, with about nine feet of an open boat between about nine people and about thirteen with a view on it. And then a man of fifty-four who holds the gravel and cannot read says so before he asks, because a question about a book is a question about reading, and asks the second thing: **Will somebody take that box out from under that counter, and strike that line out of that book, and carry that shilling and sixpence four days off to a house with a coat buttoned up to the neck, and say out loud that it is not owed.** A man of sixty-one answers that nobody is going to do it, and gives the reason: **If I strike that line now, then a Crown that can answer for anybody will have been taught by a man of sixty-one that a book can be made right by taking a penny out of it. And in about four years a Crown of about nine people will want to speak for every person nobody can be sent for about, and it will point to that taking out and call it a speaking.** A man of thirty-one says it is one half and is held at the fourth sentence. And a woman of twenty-eight asks the one thing about the box, and is told: **The box is not locked. It is shut. There is no form that says I may open it. There is no form that says I may not. I have kept it shut about two years. I will keep it shut.**

**646 — ONE MAN POLES NINE MILES UP AND NINE MILES DOWN AGAIN, AND IT IS ORDINARY WORK, AND HE SAYS OUT LOUD THAT HE IS THE ONE THAT PAYS IT.** `clear` 146, the fourth day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Friday, and his ninety-second day and his forty-fifth in Hask. He goes up in the Gull at about the fifth hour with a sheet of deal in a coat and the board in the same coat, and a woman of twenty-eight goes with him **because she has said she will give copies with her own hand and has never seen where a copy is kept.** **He wanted to go with them, and he stayed nine feet off, and it was the third time in six days he had stayed where he was told to stay.** At that landing the two copies are read and agree, and **yesterday was the fifth of the Thursdays, and this is not a Thursday, and the sixth is next Thursday**, and he says out loud that this is ordinary work and is not a hearing and is not a Crown and is a boat and a pole and a board about nine inches long. He shows her the box, the book, and the column cut with a knife on the back of the last leaf, **about four inches wide and about nine inches long, a number of people and not of heads and not of carts, cut on the Thursday of last week after asking four people whether a man of sixty-one may, and about four of them did not answer him.** Then she puts her hand on the gunwale and says the thing she understood: **a sheet is only true when two people nine miles apart have said so in the open, and the two people are a man of thirty-eight and a man of forty-three, and one of them has poled about nine miles twice this week to make it true, and the other has stood by a counter nineteen years. And my nine sentences are not true that way, and that is why they are a burden and not a record.** He comes down and says out loud that he is the one that pays it, and that he said it a second time in six days and did the same work again to say it, because a saying without a doing is a word about nothing: **about nine days of one man's work is what a Crown of about nine people costs, and about eleven people come up that plank in a working week, and nine of them are on that board, and none of the thousand and forty pence reaches it, because there is no form that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel.** Then he mends the plank in front of about nine people — thirty feet of withy hurdles at the head of eleven feet of plank, a stone set by himself on a pole under a fore thwart at about the fifth hour, and the edge of the board cut back to the same place every week since the Thursday of the tenth week. A woman of forty-one brings two cart beds for the mending and says four sheets came out of her own beds and eleven were copied in about four hours of daylight on the Sunday of the hundred and eleventh week, and that eleven and one and one and one is fourteen, a figure of wood and not of people, and one of the fourteen has no arrangement and is in a county nobody is going to. A man of thirty-one says the true of it from nine feet off and is held at the fourth sentence. And then night work is spoken of, because ordinary work does not end at the second hour of the afternoon, and a woman of twenty-eight who came up that cart road on the Monday says she saw him bail with a cut-down pail and that **my nine sentences are bailing of another kind, nobody sees it leave.**

**647 — THE SPLIT IS PUT TO BOTH SIDES IN THE OPEN ONE UNDER ANOTHER, ABOUT NINE PEOPLE ARE THE PERSON ON THE OTHER SIDE, AND BOTH HALVES ARE STILL RIGHT.** `clear` 147, the fifth day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Saturday, and his ninety-third day and his forty-sixth in Hask. **He wanted the split said out loud by the people who are in it and not by him, and he stood nine feet off with a knife, and he did not say the true of either half first, and it was the first time in forty-six days he had not said one half before anybody else spoke.** The first half is the deck, said by a man of sixty-one: about nine people were on the deck of the Gull when a line about a man of thirty-one was read and about nine were not, and every one of the first is right about their half, and a woman of thirty who went four days up that water is right to go because a line with her brother in it is a thing people go looking with. The second half is the room, said by a woman of forty-one with two carts: about nine people were in a room at the head of a drain in a town two days off when a man of forty-four read a sheet from the first line to the last in about nine minutes, and a woman of about thirty-eight who keeps a goat is right to stand up off a wall and say she was never asked, because she was never asked, and a record anybody can be sent for about goes where it goes. **A woman of twenty-nine in the reed says the deck and is of the room. A man of thirty-eight says the room and is of the deck. A man of forty-five who keeps a smithy and has said nothing in nine visits to that room says one word, and the word is *Both*.** Then the woman of forty-four who keeps the book answers the question asked once at the Thursday of the ninth week by a woman of twenty-nine and never answered: **A person may take a yes back.** And she gives the rest of it, and the rest is a difference and not a form: **taking back puts a person out of a chair and does not put a line out of a book, and there is no form in this county that says it does. And I have kept that book nineteen years and I have never once written a day for a thing a person said out loud and did not take, and that is why.** A girl of nine four miles off that cart road is spoken of, and her father has told her twice that this is not their business, and it is not true. And he says nothing about either half, and a woman of twenty-eight says why his silence is a shape and not a failure: **He has said one half out loud seven times in forty-six days. About four of you have been counting and about nine have not. And today he said neither. About nine people said both, and both are still right.** Then reeds and hurdles and a cart, and a woman of twenty-nine saying **taking back does not cut reeds and staying does not plant them**, and a woman of forty-one saying a road two days long does not shorten because both halves are right, and fourteen sheets in four counties stay fourteen.

**648 — A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-ONE IS ASKED A FOURTH TIME AND SOMEBODY ANSWERS HER, AND THE ANSWER IS NOT A COMFORT AND IT IS NOT A FORM, AND IT IS GIVEN TO HER AND NOT TO THE ROOM.** `clear` 148, the sixth day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Sunday, and his ninety-fourth day and his forty-seventh in Hask. The town does not sit on a Sunday and about nine people came up that cart road anyway because a Crown eight days old is a thing people come to look at whether it sits or not. **He wanted the woman of seventy-one not to be asked again and said so out loud before anybody asked, and it was the fourth time somebody had been about to ask her, and the first three were in about four days in the eleventh week, the last of them on the Monday of that week.** A man of fifty-four who holds the gravel and cannot read and was not in that town for any of the three asks it anyway. Then a woman of twenty-eight goes to the doorway where she is standing, stands a little off, and speaks low, and the room goes on being a room: **You do not have to say it a fourth time. And I am not saying it for the room, and I am saying it for you. About nine people will hear that I am speaking and about four will hear what I say. You said that in about four years there will be about nine people nobody can be sent for about, and a Crown will want to speak for every one of them. And you said you would rather be one it does not speak for, and that is not a side, and the instrument is not a side. And I have come four hundred miles with two letters nobody has read, and I am bearing nine sentences nobody can check, and I will carry your not speaking the fourth time as part of them. And there is no form for that, and I am not going to write it down, and nobody nine miles up this water can check it.** And the answer comes back the same way, to one person and not to the room, in one sentence: **Then I am not the one who says it fourth, and you are the one who carries my not saying it, and I have kept that house eleven years and nobody has carried anything for me in eleven years.** Night comes on while about nine people are still in that room and no lamp is lit, and a woman of forty says why a house that is not hers has been kept eleven years, and a man of sixty-one says what eleven years is a figure of and gives the landing's money again, and a woman of twenty-nine in the reed says what night does to a chair, and a man of sixty-eight says what an answer given to one person is, with his rule thirty-one days old and the subtraction said out loud, and **is not going to take a rule back because of a doorway.** A boy of thirteen holds a sheet up in the doorway to catch the last of the light and says the thirty-first line is about a man of thirty-one and the thirty-second about a woman of seventy-one, and that **dusk shows ink well and night will hide it**, and water is heard past the landing and a man says **one river, one stone with a line cut round it, one count a man cannot stop**, and a slate with no name is wiped clean of dusk with a sleeve, and a woman of twenty-eight watches a boy mend and says low, to one person, **your hands know ink and mine are still clean, and carrying your not saying fourth is ink of another kind, it does not show.**

**649 — A SINGLE CROWN IS ABOLISHED BY ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AT A BARREL ON A WORKING DAY, IN THE OPEN, AND IT BINDS NOBODY, AND IT IS ABOLISHED ANYWAY, AND THE TWO QUESTIONS THE VOLUME OWED ARE ANSWERED IN THE SAME ROOM.** `clear` 149, the seventh and last day of the hundred and fourteenth week, a Monday, and his ninety-fifth day and his forty-eighth in Hask. The town sits for about forty minutes and this time there is something to sit about. **About nine people are at the barrel at about the eighth hour on a working day, in the open, with no house and no door, and about thirteen people are in front of them, and about nine feet of an open boat is between the two groups.** A woman of forty opens it and says the board has had nine names on it for seventeen days, and **derives the figure out loud: seventeen is six hundred and forty-nine less six hundred and thirty-two, and I am not doing that sum out of a book. About four of you can check me and about nine cannot.** A man of sixty-one says what seventeen days are a figure of, and that a year in this county is the time between one crossing of a block of limestone nine miles up that river and the next one after it, and that he does not know when that is and neither does anybody in that room. **Then the woman of forty-four who keeps the book answers the two thirds of the question of `620` owed since the Tuesday of the eleventh week, and says out loud that she is answering and not the Crown: A saying of no does not have to be written down the way a saying of yes is. And nobody decided that, and there was nobody in a room deciding it, and in nineteen years there has never once been a day in that book for a thing a person said out loud and did not take. And every no in this county for nineteen years was a person standing up out of a chair, and standing up is a thing anybody can see without writing. And I have been asked three times since the Friday of the last week but one whether I will put a hearing in that book, and I have said no three times, and a hearing nobody granted and nobody refused is a thing with no person on either side of it.** And a woman of twenty-eight answers who says how many people a thing has been read to, which a boy of thirteen asked on the Wednesday of the last week but two and a woman of forty-one said on the Thursday of the hundred and twelfth week was no better than his question: **The only person who can say how many is the person who was in the room, one at a time … a road is not a room, and that is the whole of what happened to that figure.** **Then they abolished it.** A man of sixty-one takes the board off the barrel with his own hands, a man of thirty-eight lifts the barrel a little off, a woman of forty reads the nine names out loud one under another a last time, a man of sixty-eight says the words — **a Crown of about nine people at a barrel is abolished from this hour, in the open, with no house and no door … it binds nobody, and no person in this county is bound by a word of it … I gave the rule that made it, and a Crown is a lid with a name on it, and I am worse off than I was on the Monday of the hundred and eleventh week, and nobody is to do anything about it. And the rule is not going back, because a rule that goes back the second time a person turns out to be right about a brother is not a rule, it is a mood** — and a woman of forty-one, a man of fifty-four and a man of forty-five each said one word after him, **a woman of twenty-nine said one word from the reed and did not stand up to say it, and a woman of sixty-four was nine feet off with about thirteen people who had a view on it, and said nothing at the barrel at all, and said why afterward: I got up out of it in the open nineteen years ago, and there is no column for the getting out, and abolishing a board does not put me back in. And a Crown that binds nobody being abolished by people who know it binds nobody is still worth doing, and I am saying that as a person outside.** A boy of thirteen reads the whole sheet out loud a last time under no Crown and reads the thirty-first line twice. The barrel is spoken of, and gravel, and wood, and staves, and a man of thirty-one says nothing for the fourteenth time in forty-eight days.

**650 — A SHEET OF DEAL WITH A SEAL ON IT COMES UP THAT CART ROAD AND A BENCH FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF DECLARES A CONTINUITY HEARING FOR EVERY RECORD THERE IS IN ALL OF VEYRA AND NAMES NO ROOM AND NO KEEPER, AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT ASKS THE VOLUME'S QUESTION ONCE AND NOBODY ANSWERS IT.** `clear` 150, the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Tuesday, and **his four hundredth morning**, and his ninety-sixth day in the county of Kell and his forty-ninth in Hask. The board is leaning against the barrel with no Crown over it. A woman of forty says the hearing a man of thirty-eight asked for on the Monday of the eleventh week **is now the twenty-second day, and twenty-two is six hundred and fifty less six hundred and twenty-eight, and I have done the subtraction out loud**, and nobody granted it and nobody refused it and nobody has written it down. A man of thirty-eight says he will do the same thing again on Thursday and that Thursday is the sixth, and gives the hours and the money again and says nobody has ever once asked him to do it. **Then a man of forty-three who keeps that landing nineteen years comes up the plank with a sheet of deal in his coat, and says where it was before anybody asked him: on the counter at that landing on the Thursday, under a stone, with nothing on the outside of the stone and nothing on the inside of it, and about nine people in that yard and about four of them walked past it; nobody there put it there that anybody saw, and there is no form in that county that says a man of forty-three may keep a sheet off a counter; and I am not going to be a man who keeps a thing in a coat.** A man of sixty-one reads it out loud, because about four people on that bank can read and about nine cannot: **A Continuity Hearing, for every record there is, in all of Veyra, is declared by the Bench, and no room is named, and no county is named, and the word Veyra is on it. And every person in every county may be spoken about at it, and no person may be sent for about it, because a notice is not a summons. And the Bench says the records will be read in one place, and it does not say which place, and it does not say who keeps them.** He reads it twice, puts it face down on a barrel that has no board on it, and says he is not going to read it a third time in front of about nine people, and that there is no column in that book for a hearing and no column for a notice. **And it is not the hearing a man of thirty-eight asked for, and nobody on that bank said that out loud, and about four of them thought it, and about nine did not.** Then a woman of twenty-eight asks the question once and not twice: **A notice came up that road this morning with the word Veyra on it and no room named and no keeper named, and a hearing about every record there is has been declared for all of it, and one thing keeps the record, and one thing says its own name and is not answered. And two copies nine miles apart are only true when two men have said so, and a board about nine inches long is how they are checked, and a woman with her own hand is how a speaking is given. How can a hearing about the whole world be fair when one thing controls the record.** **Nobody on that bank answered it.** About nine people heard it and about four understood all of it, and it is not the right of refusal, and not the question of a yes taken back, and not the question of a no written down, and not the question of who says how many, and not the question of a copy owed. He says out loud that he is not going to answer it and that he has not answered it. A woman of forty-one says a cart road is still two days and fourteen sheets are still fourteen. A boy of thirteen puts his pen down and says **a hearing about the whole world will want my hand, and one thing controlling the record will want it most, and I am thirteen.** The stone is spoken of, and a year with no number still has a stone: **water crosses it and crosses again, that is a year, it is not weeks, nobody in four counties can check it but the stone, and the stone says nothing.** And a man of sixty-eight says what a question asked once is, with his rule thirty-three days old and the subtraction said out loud, and that he is not going to take a rule back because of a world. **And he is still nobody's, and he is still counting from one water, and there is no figure for the year and a block of limestone nine miles up a river with a line cut round it.**
