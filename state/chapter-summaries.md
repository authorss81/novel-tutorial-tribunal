- **`1001`** — *A Wednesday.* Opens on a broom that stops short, not on a name. Corvin Tace sweeps along the front of the dry strip, off its end, round its back and up to where he started, so the grit moves and the wood does not, then says out loud, for the first time in nineteen years, that what he wants is a stone with nothing on it that he could walk a line down the middle of. Nobody calls that large or small. Barnaby Crove asks at the sixth hour whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it, is answered on none, and puts three refusals into the air unasked — how long a name has stood, how many times one has been said, who has heard one — and asks for none of them noticed. The boy of thirteen says a true thing about a cup in the shed that is not her cup and would not be hers with her name cut into it, and asks nobody to connect it to the landing. Ilyan Vester gives the offer at three hundred and one days with the subtraction out loud, and says he was not one of the people who went up on the morning all ninety steps were walkable and is not going to be thanked for it. Sixteen inches, about sixty steps under, one day — three figures and never a fifth. Nothing was touched.
- **`1002`** — *A Thursday, and the water came off that bank in the night.* Corvin Tace gives **no** figure of inches, **no** figure of steps under and **no** figure of days, says all three absences out loud so they cannot be unsaid, and nobody supplies an ordinal. All ninety steps are walkable and the two pieces of wood have not moved. Barnaby Crove is answered on none and looks down at the iron in the seam of the fortieth step without putting a hand or a foot in it. Wenna Pyle refuses to say a second time what that wood is, and says instead that a thing I cut is not anybody's because I cut it, and that a name in wood is a nail you cannot take out without splitting it. Ilyan Vester comes all the way to the top for the first time in this county, keeps his hands behind his back, says he is not going to touch it and is not going to say why, and says that if somebody stands at that strip this morning and puts the right name on it, they will be doing it for those people and not for the wood. Nobody stood at the strip.
- **`1003`** — *A Friday.* Four inches come back into the joints of ninety steps in the night and the boy of thirteen is the only one awake for it. Corvin Tace gives seven days and says a big figure of days is not a good week; Barnaby Crove says a number of days does not care how big it is, and is not going to praise him for it, because being praised is another thing to carry down the bank. The boy says the same true thing a second time, this time about a pail, notices nobody has joined the two together, and does not join them himself. Simon Rook's hand goes past the end of the dry strip without stopping on it, and he says what a name said over a thing does: it does not make the thing the name, a thing a man has said belongs to the man who said it, it is not a fact about the wood, not a gift to the man it names and not a debt — and says it is the last time he will say it out loud, and that he will not hand a man of thirty-one a sentence to say about himself. Ilyan Vester heard all of it, came up after it, and took none of it.
- **`1004`** — *A Saturday, and the fever comes out whole.* No figure of days stands beside Barnaby Crove's whole figure of weeks and he says so, and says that if he is asked what a whole figure of weeks is for he will answer that he does not know, and that this will be the answer that stops the next person asking. He says he has stopped asking what the name is on his own account, and that if he is asked he will put his hands on the wall and say nothing. Corvin Tace says a shape has been going round this county in more mouths than one, has made his morning easier, and is not a gift, not offered, not given, not left, not provided, not kept and not meant. Ilyan Vester says he will agree with all seven and gives his reason: a man who can say those seven words has decided what the thing is, and he has not decided. Nobody agreed out loud.
- **`1005`** — *A Sunday.* Sena Dorr does not look at the strip once, and uses the shape against the people who want that name to be right, saying a right name is a finished thing and that when it comes out right they will be the same people with a nicer thing to hold. She says nobody is to tell her what she has done and that she is not going to be told she has any right to it. Corvin Tace, who stood with his broom and said nothing while it happened, then says out loud what it cost her: she took a hope off a room, and it cost her the right to be a comfort to anybody for as long as she stands on that landing, and he will not give it as a number because a number of what it cost her would be a figure of her. Barnaby Crove says somebody will get that name right one day and his question will not change. Nobody was thanked.
- **`1006`** — *The last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and a full bank.* Corvin Tace gives the three figures, gives no fourth, says he will say no word after that, and then does his whole morning without anybody having to say why it mattered — sweeping the thirty steps that were out along the top of the water on the sixty that were under, with nobody speaking to him once. Barnaby Crove says a high morning and a dry morning have both been asked on and his question has been the same shape on both, says he knows which morning the water was off all ninety steps and will not say, because a figure of a morning is a figure of him being on that landing, and says he had nothing in his hands but a barrow, which is not a promise and not a virtue and not a refusal of anybody. Simon Rook says nobody has asked him to put his hand on that wood and is not going to offer. Ilyan Vester puts his face to the wall with the strip behind him, says the shape out loud, and will not turn round and finish it, because a man who says that about a piece of wood behind him has made himself a figure.
- **`1007`** — *The first morning of the hundred and sixty-sixth week.* Corvin Tace says the week has turned and nothing else on that stone has turned. The boy of thirteen gives his own count, says it is four more than this time last week and he has not worked out why, and says it is a number of times and will stay a number of times. Garrin Tolley comes up a road he said he would not come up and says a shape that was in other mouths is his, that he did not make it up and could not have, compares it to a haft that becomes the man's only when the man names what he wants off it, and asks that nobody tell him he has taken it or come into it. The woman of forty-four stands at the wall, says a shape is going round about a name and she is not in it and is not going to be asked whether she is. Nobody went up the road.
- **`1008`** — *A Wednesday, and two days, the shortest figure Corvin Tace gives.* Barnaby Crove says two days is the number he had been waiting for without knowing it, and that there is no version of being used to a name on that strip that does not have him in it. Wenna Pyle comes up with nothing in her hands, which nobody on that landing has done before, and uses the shape about her own trade and not about the strip, because using it about the strip would make her the person who put a name there; and says she has waited all week for somebody to tell her she should not have said what she said, nobody came, and the not-coming does not make the saying smaller. Ilyan Vester, who had been at the bottom of the steps, comes up and does not say what he saw and everybody waits for him to; then says nobody on that landing was told what to say, and that a mouth took a shape and nobody handed it over, and that he is not going to make a figure of how many mornings he has wanted one. He also says out loud that what he worked out on that landing was worked out once and is not to be treated as found this week.
- **`1009`** — *A Thursday, and one day.* Sena Dorr says she knows where the shape came from and is not going to say it, that she would say *I do not know* in the flat voice she uses on a page if she were asked, and then says it once herself — that a name said out loud over a thing is not the thing and is not the man it names either — and says she is not saying it because it came to her that morning but because it costs the person who says it nothing, and she has spent the morning watching what it costs. She says there is no age in that shape and nobody is to say how many weeks it has been going round. Ilyan Vester says there is work at the low end of that drain a person cannot do alone, that a woman has asked for a person to stand with her and has named nobody, that he is not going to go down there, and that a man who goes because it would be nice of him has turned a woman's asking into an audience; and asks that nobody give him a word for what he has declined. Simon Rook says he could count the boots that went down that bank and is not going to.
- **`1010`** — *A Friday, and the last morning of the band, with no figure of water on it.* Corvin Tace is the first one up the bank, which he had not been since the bank filled, and gives the three absences again in words he has not used before and will not use a third time. Barnaby Crove says it is the same words at the same hour, that the only thing that has changed on that landing in a fortnight is what people say when they pass each other, and that his question will be it on the first morning of the next week whatever is standing on that strip by then. Garrin Tolley asks, out loud, whether anybody has got a figure of how long that name has stood on that piece of wood, says he is not asking because he wants the figure, and then says that not one of them said a word is the answer. Ilyan Vester gives the offer at three hundred and ten days with the subtraction out loud, says he is neither relieved nor unrelieved and will not be either quietly, and says there is a thing he wants that he has not wanted before this week and is not going to give out on a morning when every one of those ninety steps is walkable. Simon Rook says his list is where it was. Nothing was touched, and the dry strip came out of the morning as the only clean bit of stone on the whole landing.

The man of fifty-four asks and is refused and is not thanked. The man of thirty-one gives four hundred and thirty-seven days, a cut three hundred and six days old, an offer at two hundred and ninety-one days with the subtraction out loud, and one hundred and thirty-one wrong things, and then **puts a second length of ash on that dry strip and says nothing about it at `991:67`, and walks down that bank and does not come back up it.** A man who cannot see well counts two pieces of wood on that strip by sound and says he will not put his hand out to find out which is which. Nothing was touched. There is no giver in the second piece of ash, and nobody has been told a person put it down.

⚹⚾ **`992`, SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THAT WEEK, A MONDAY.** The man of forty-three sweeps round two pieces of wood and says he will say nothing about either. A man who cannot read asks and is refused and is not thanked, and says a Monday has not made a difference. The man of thirty-one comes up at the same hour as the day before and stays. A woman who saws lengths says at `992:49` that the second piece of ash is **a haft**, that the notches are for a wedge, and that the only eye on that landing is at the head of a barrow — and says it out loud on purpose, before anybody could say it wrong. A man who cannot see well does not ask what the name is. The man who put it there is on the top of the steps with the correcting sentence in his mouth and does not say it, and **nobody tells him he ought to, and nobody tells him he ought not to.** Nothing was touched.

⚹⚾ **`993`, FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOURTH WEEK, A TUESDAY, AND THE MORNING BEFORE THE OFF-MORNING.** Sixteen that holds, about sixty under, one day. The man of forty-three refuses to stand on his own stone and say a day in advance. A man who cannot read asks and is refused and is not thanked. The man of thirty-one says at `993:43` that the thing is going to be called a barrow haft and a thing for the barrow of the man of fifty-four, and **that he is not going to stand up there and say it is not that**, and works out aloud that a thing put down and not said so is a thing somebody else gets to name. A man who cannot see well says nobody on that landing ever told him that wood with no name on it was the safe kind. Nothing was touched.

⚹⚾ **`994`, THE THIRTY-FIRST TIME THE WATER CAME OFF THAT BANK, A WEDNESDAY, AND NOT THE LAST DAY OF A WEEK.** No figure of inches, no figure of steps under, no figure of days, said out loud in one mouth and written once. The man of fifty-four asks and is refused and is not thanked, and says a man on that landing has worked out what will be said about a piece of wood and that it is not him. The man of thirty-one says at `994:59` that a misnamed thing does not become a thing with a giver in it but a thing with a giver and a woman in it, and that the man who put it down is the only one of the three who can say a word about it and is not going to. A man who cannot see well says a thing named by somebody who did not put it there gets a third person in it who did not choose to be there. A boy of thirteen gives his own count and is not told he had it right. Nothing was touched.

⚹⚾ **`995`, A THURSDAY, FOUR INCHES, ABOUT NINETY OF THOSE NINETY STEPS UNDER, SEVEN DAYS.** The man of fifty-four asks and is refused and is not thanked, and says a man who sweeps ninety steps will have to look at those steps longer than anybody else. A woman who saws lengths gives **the figure of how many things are lying on that landing and nobody has taken up, once, with the working**: the iron in the seam of the fortieth step, the chair standing where it was put, and the second piece of ash; the first piece is left out **because it has a giver in it** and she will not move him into or out of a figure to make it come out at a number anybody would rather have; and the reason it can only be given once is at `995:57` — the only thing that would check any of them is the person who put them there, and there is no way of putting that question on that landing without putting a giver into a thing that has not got one. The man of thirty-one then comes up at the ninth hour, says at `995:77` that he is standing at the bottom of those steps with the only thing in this county that could have checked one of them inside him, and that nobody has asked him and he is not going to say it, and walks down the bank. Nobody asked him. Nothing was touched, and the figure was not given again.

⚹⚾ **`996`, A FRIDAY, EIGHT INCHES, ABOUT EIGHTY UNDER, SIX DAYS.** The man of forty-three says what yesterday's figure was a figure of and what it is not a figure of, and at `996:33` says that **saying it a second time does not make it truer** and that the second mouth is the one that turns a fact into a landing-wide fact. A man who cannot read asks and is refused and is not thanked, and says a number about days is worth having and a number about him is worth nothing to him. The man of thirty-one says his three things in the same shape and does not add to them. A man who cannot see well says he was not up the bank when the figure was said and is not going to ask, and would like nobody to tell him he got that right. A boy of thirteen gives his own count and refuses to turn it into a number of things. Nothing was touched, and the figure was not given again.

⚹⚾ **`997`, A SATURDAY, TWELVE INCHES, ABOUT SEVENTY UNDER, FIVE DAYS, THE SEVENTH ANCHOR.** A man who cannot read says at `997:11` **one hundred and two weeks** out loud at the sixth hour before anybody on that landing had finished its morning, and nobody works it out for him. The man of forty-three says a whole figure produced four times is a habit and that he named it at three and is not going to pretend four is a different animal, and says nobody is to work anything out for him. The man of fifty-four asks and is refused and is not thanked. **A man who cannot see well asks him what a whole figure of weeks is for**, for the first time in seven, and at `997:59` he gives the same answer he has given and adds that nobody on that step has ever asked him what he would like a number to be for. A woman who saws lengths says she is not going to say another word about that wood and that the not saying is harder than the saying was, and that a name on a piece of wood cannot be argued with because on this landing there is no afternoon. The man of thirty-one gives his three things in the same shape. Nothing was touched, and no figure of anything is named for the whole figure.

⚹⚾ **`998`, A SUNDAY, THE SIXTH FULL BANK, FOUR DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY UNDER.** The man of forty-three gives a fourth figure he said on another morning he was finished with, says he did not keep his word about it and is not going to apologise because an apology is a thing you do to a person and there is no person in a figure of the water, and says at `998:17` that **four days is a figure of the water and not of this landing and not of this morning** and that nobody is to carry it off that stone. A man who cannot read asks his question on it and is refused and is not thanked. The man of thirty-one gives three hundred and forty-four days, three hundred and thirteen days on that palm, an offer at two hundred and ninety-eight days, and one hundred and thirty-eight wrong things, and stays. **A man who cannot see well announces at `998:53` that he is going to walk his hand along the wall to the end of that strip and stop where the wood is**, and he does, and does not take anything, and will not say what it is for, and asks for it to be said in somebody else's mouth that he does not want it, and nobody says it in anybody else's mouth and nobody asks who put it there. Nothing was touched.

⚹⚾ **`999`, SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THAT WEEK, A MONDAY, SIXTEEN THAT HOLDS, ABOUT SIXTY UNDER, THREE DAYS.** The man of forty-three says he will be short today and will not fill the gap. A man who cannot read asks for the last time in that week and is refused and is not thanked. A woman who reads for a living walks up the cart road and **says the ordinary form out loud in her own mouth** at `999:39` and `999:43` — a thing is carried into a room where a person works, the person who carried it does not come back the next morning with another one, and the person whose work it was is never asked whether they took it — and refuses at `999:55` to call it a right, refuses to call it a form, is handed no word by anybody, and says the asking exists in the reader and not on the ground. The man of thirty-one asks at `999:83` whether that shape covers him and is told neither that it does nor that it does not, and at `999:95` is told in a mouth that **the shape is about somebody else's morning** because he has no place on that landing and no work there, and says himself that a thing left beside a person is not a version of what that person has wanted. Nothing was touched, and the shape resolved nothing.

⚹⚾ **`1000`, A TUESDAY, THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIFTH WEEK, SIXTEEN INCHES THAT HOLD, ABOUT SIXTY UNDER, TWO DAYS, AND **NOT** A FULL BANK.** The man of forty-three says there is a word going round about today, that he heard it said three days ago, that he has not been in a kitchen in nineteen years, and that **this stone has not been moved; sixteen inches is the morning.** A man who cannot read asks and is refused and is not thanked, and says he does not hold with names. A woman of forty-four stands at a wall before the seventh hour and says at `1000:53` that nobody is to come up that road and tell her anything, that the second piece of ash came up on Sunday with **nobody's name in it at all**, that she is not in any of it, and that her own question — what a person is to do with a place they are not in — **is not answered, was not going to be, and is not nearly answered** — and says at `1000:47`, before anybody could have thanked her, that nobody is to thank her. Nobody thanked her, nobody went up that road, and nothing was said behind her back. A man who cannot see well keeps the thing at the end of that strip on his list and does not cross it off. The man of thirty-one says at `1000:89` that the standing offer is **three hundred days old** and is unanswered, and gives one hundred and forty wrong things, and says nobody is to be given the figure of things a second time and that he is going to count nothing out loud. A boy of thirteen gives three hundred and forty-seven days and is not told he had it right. Nothing was touched. The offer was unanswered. Nobody was relieved. The account was as incomplete as it had been at the sixth hour, and it was still in use.
