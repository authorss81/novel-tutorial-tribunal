# Chapter Summaries

## Chapter 0001 — The Route That Remembered

Ilyan finds that *The Sovereign’s Wake* erases the cause of a refusal while marking the route complete. He files a precise, unmerged defect report despite the cancellation and a project lead who calls the problem noise. A signed sequence activates a separate one-use null passage without consent. Ilyan lands beneath Orison’s ninth bridge with a spent blackglass token as its reserve and existing drift worsen; Tamsin’s hand appears under the failing span.

## Chapter 0002 — The Current Beneath the Bridge

Ilyan cannot understand ambient Veyran speech, but the formal case notice names him First Defendant and offers three costless-looking remedies. Tarin interprets the human warning; Oren explains the bridge’s count and load marks. Ilyan refuses the abstract choice, identifies the child and her need for a safe exit, and reaches the anchor plate as the span folds toward Tamsin.

## Chapter 0003 — The Brace That Could Be Repeated

Ilyan asks Oren and Tarin for a controlled rescue instead of pretending he can order the bridge. A clamp, cord, and drive pin create a repeatable pocket; Tamsin warns him not to pull and chooses to hear the bridge rather than be promised an uncertain rescue. Neris arrives, names the household’s access and work needs, refuses public use of the private flood account, and the court notice demands five challenge fields before the next bell, which transfers the filing into the midday session.

## Chapter 0004 — The Form of a Challenge

In the Chamber of Passage, Sera Quill accepts provisional representation for the record rather than Ilyan’s innocence. Harrow distinguishes review, appeal, and exit, and accepts Ilyan’s challenge filing without granting an access exception. Ilyan volunteers for a bounded public witness duty that ends with the first field; his report and identity become public. Oren produces a saltglass workroom plate whose maintenance-review language differs from the current charter.

## Chapter 0005 — The Useful Wording

Oren obtains a one-bell maintenance exception for his named crew and one load path. Ilyan turns the inspection into a repeatable sequence and sees the old review condition, but an eastern petition copies its language into a dangerous claim about unlisted refusal. At the deadline, Ilyan files Neris’s private flood account without asking. The Bench preserves the copy, Sera halts the access request and suspends representation, and the household must decide what can be offered after the breach.

## Chapter 0006 — The Name of the Bell

Ilyan explains the preserved copy, the withdrawal mark, and the limits of a court’s ability to restore privacy. Neris says the old strict register once felt protective during an earlier flood before a local petition made it exclusionary. Tamsin chooses to offer only that the warning bell rang before the collapse; Neris refuses the flood history. Sera agrees to advocate the household’s limited request while Ilyan’s representation remains suspended, and the one-bell night field forms.

## Chapter 0007 — The Field Around the Bridge

The first night field tests the current charter, the maintenance record, and the bridge’s behavior. Harrow uses the Bench’s technical term “playable,” while a continuity line appears beneath the formal notice. Ilyan and Tarin establish a neutral perimeter and keep the household at the boundary. Harrow physically replaces the old working plate with the current certified duplicate; the original is sealed, the field destabilizes, and the bridge survives on its brace with the missing language absent from the active record.

## Chapter 0008 — The Right to Say No

Sera confronts Ilyan in her room and makes him record the breach, apologize without asking forgiveness, and accept that his future claims will be public. The court copy is marked disputed, the people named in the account are not treated as consenting witnesses, and Neris retains the original. Sera identifies the surviving phrase “the governed may answer no,” finds her private duplicate of the Quiet Chamber page missing after the night field, and resumes only limited provisional representation of Ilyan after the correction.

## Chapter 0009 — The Record That Walked

The guild posts a safety order, the eastern district invokes its mounted petition, and the crowd arrives. Sera translates the household’s choices and calls Neris and Tamsin without making them. When the clerk physically couples the petition to the bridge record, competing registered and unlisted conditions move through the span. Ilyan proposes one public review with separate burdens and refuses to treat copied wording as household consent. The preliminary hearing opens with amendment, destruction, and delay before the Bench.

## Chapter 0010 — The Stay

Tamsin offers only the bell fact, Neris offers register/work/housing facts and refuses the flood account, and Oren offers the bridge’s physical sequence without accepting unlimited guild liability. Ilyan names a finite public burden attached to his identity and report through the review. The Bench grants limited provisional standing and a one-cycle stay with a public seventh-morning-bell review; the current charter remains untouched. Tamsin and Neris cross together and the repaired load holds. Sera files a licensing recommendation, then distinguishes the original Quiet Chamber page’s pre-arrival removal from the later disappearance of her duplicate and takes Ilyan to the archive.

## Chapter 0011 — The Word That Traveled

A Merefen courier brings an extract that detaches the ninth-bridge stay from its named bridge, crew, burden, and review date. Ilyan and Sera discover that a local clerk may lawfully reuse public wording as a precedent witness without asking the original parties. Ilyan chooses a bounded review rather than a freeze that could close the road and water route, accepting that his name will travel. Neris and Tamsin keep the Ro household’s safety, work, housing, and consent boundaries visible. The extract’s added sentence reads, “the unlisted ward may refuse at the cost of the shared road.” The ward is known only as the unlisted ward; the name Cinder Ward does not appear until Chapter 12.

## Chapter 0012 — The Road That Borrowed the Bridge

Ilyan observes the Saltwheel Road’s carts, water route, load marks, and levy. A loaded cart exposes a hollow seam and cracks the crossing-side marker; Joren says the course has not gone through and that one heavier wheel will finish it. The breach itself is saved for Chapter 14. The Cinder Ward is divided: some residents need the road, some cannot pay the levy, and many have no recognized address for notice. Joren Vell identifies the physical failure and refuses to sign a clean inspection without ward representation. He offers the road as a witness but rejects Ilyan’s plan, turning the local dispute toward the Bench.

## Chapter 0013 — A Name in the Margin

At a record shop, Ilyan and Sera find that the Merefen petition combines older road clauses and the copied ninth-bridge wording. It was entered as a precedent witness, not a ruling, but a marshal has already used it to justify a maintenance levy. Sera refuses to hide the acceptance ledger from the ward. The Bench grants preservation of the ledger and refuses the hold on enforcement, so the batch’s first filing returns worse than it went in: the record is protected and the road is not. The ledger’s final witness entry is removed, leaving a Kest fragment and a clipped continuity-cadence replacement.

## Chapter 0014 — Tarin’s Boundary

Tarin imposes a neutral route and public witness list after a containment order. Ilyan documents the inspection rather than directing the crowd. A loaded cart breaks the crossing-side marker and shifts the water route. Beneath the district’s newer plate, the marker carries private wording closely matching Ilyan’s Earth report: “Completion does not erase the cause of refusal.” Tarin secures the physical evidence and warns Ilyan not to request a broad emergency.

## Chapter 0015 — A Good Exception, a Bad Road

At the Cinder Ward, Mava Rusk refuses a maintenance acknowledgment and loses water and shelter priority. The district invokes Ilyan’s stay as settled precedent. Ilyan states that the stay was bounded by one bridge, a named crew, a household, and a review date; the copied petition has removed those conditions. The refusal triggers a road-wide closure, but ward residents use a slower route to deliver clinic herbs. A petition-box fragment survives with the words, “A burden may be refused when its effect is made visible,” while its lower half is cleanly cut away.

## Chapter 0016 — Recovery Work

Oren, Joren, the ward workers, and the district repair the Saltwheel Road without pretending bridge knowledge transfers automatically. The local keeper and residents name the work, hours, materials, warning line, water route, review, and exit. Ilyan documents the sequence only. The road opens under a finite local arrangement, but the district refuses to publish the ward’s refusal. A proposed renewal instruction arrives before the first interval ends; it has no end date and will become active only when entered.

## Chapter 0017 — The Assembly Refuses a Clean Answer

The Merefen assembly splits between road users, ward residents, and districts afraid that one unlisted ward will become a universal precedent. Ilyan refuses to offer a universal declaration and supports a visible, specific refusal. He submits his public Earth sequence for neutral comparison without accepting authorship. The comparison sets three dates side by side and finds that the local petition was built from the public wording before the Quiet Chamber margin was rewritten. The copier and the person who tidied the record afterward are not the same person. Maren Kett’s written mandate is read into the hall before he argues.

## Chapter 0018 — The Family Appeal

Sera keeps her family’s withdrawal claim separate from the road case and accepts Ilyan only as a record-checker. The original page’s removal predates Ilyan’s crossing; her duplicate vanished during or after the first field. A formal continuity request has a valid seal but no named author, a C—2 code, and a missing witness index. Ilyan admits he cannot infer guilt from the seal and agrees to trace the ordinary chain rather than hunt a villain.

## Chapter 0019 — The Copy Reaches the Road

A district clerk from Merefen enters the automatic-renewal order into the active petition book at a witnessed hour, before anyone can stop it, and the entered record treats the ward’s work as consent and threatens a pre-dawn closure. Ilyan and Sera frame a narrow challenge, while Joren, Dalla, and Mava each choose limited testimony. Tarin keeps the line neutral and refuses force. The Bench pauses automatic renewal, seals rather than erases the copied petition, and keeps the local route available for a short interval. A second paper using Ilyan’s private Earth sequence enters the court slot.

## Chapter 0020 — The Precedent Has a Body

The district offers to reopen the road if Mava signs, but she refuses the acknowledgment and accepts only the named work and public review. The ward’s refusal and labor are entered in the common register. At the morning bell, the affected people choose a fresh one-interval continuation, so the route remains open without becoming permanent precedent. Ilyan claims no authorship or general authority. Sera links the second paper to the C—2 continuity request and finds that the old bridge plate bears an S. Kest fragment; the paper’s receipt predates Ilyan’s arrival, leaving the question of how his private words entered Veyran law.

## Chapter 0021 — The Guild Counts the Work

Oren counts the guild's work in hours and posts a notice naming the ninth-bridge work, the hands, the wage, and Ilyan as attached record with no maintenance labor assigned. Maren Kett arrives with a written mandate and argues that the stay has become a regional obligation, and that the region cannot pay for an inspection nobody requires. A new inspection exposes the missing term with teeth: under the current charter a keeper may summon a pause but may not release the span, so Oren can ring a bell that means nothing. The notice's accurate last line is read by the district as proof that nobody carries the maintenance, and the absence becomes the day's danger.

## Chapter 0022 — A House at the Edge

The household's shelter, room, work hour, and crossing hours are separated by Ilyan and Sera into four records with four recipients, which Meridith Vale immediately explains means four doors where there was one locked gate. The district proposes a three-day materials store on the ground that a provisional tenancy has no tenancy to protect. Tamsin is invited to repeat the bell for the assembly and chooses to say it once in the workroom, where the span is visible while she talks about it, because otherwise people will say the bell was working for her and not for the keeper who rings it. Neris offers route facts only. A work record shows the Ro household's crossing credited as a half-hand of maintenance labor every morning since the night of the stay, matched by a district form number issued to more than one office.

## Chapter 0023 — The Name on the Load

Meridith Vale enters Ilyan's name in the authority row of the public work slate under a four-year-old standing instruction, and Oren refuses to let the guild work under another body's authority. A loaded cart reads the board, changes its line, takes the eastern turn fast, and breaks the approach apron; the name is not a force on the stone but a force on the people who read it before they reach it. A guild apprentice refuses to read the scale aloud while that name is on the board, and Oren takes the book and signs beside him. Sera compares the slate's hand with the composite petition's hand and finds they are not the same. The slate's back shows a case copy routed through the plate office that no instruction authorizes.

## Chapter 0024 — The Assembly’s Limited Word

The Lower Ward Assembly can answer one question: may the attached name be cited as authority for local maintenance work? The district invokes the Bench's own refusal to hold enforcement in the Saltwheel matter and declines to hold the materials store. Maren Kett argues for one legible rule with no roof, and Sera says a rule that tells a clerk who pays does not tell a person she may say no. Ilyan reaches for a general rule about acknowledgment inferred from use and Sera stops him in the middle aisle in front of the hall, because his standing is one bridge and he has no facts about four districts. The assembly's limited word makes his name citable as a record, uncitable as a permit, and orders him to give a public account of his first bridge testimony in three days.

## Chapter 0025 — Ilyan’s First Public Testimony

Neris and Tamsin do not come and send no message. Ilyan answers for what he accepted, what he did not accept, and where his plan failed, including filing Neris's private flood account without asking. Asked to affirm that the stay authorized local adoption, he refuses, and then confesses to the limit he never asked to have removed: every term in the stay he accepted was a limit on the court, and nothing in it stopped a clerk from reusing its shape, and he was relieved not to have to ask for a charter amendment. A question about the pre-arrival paper forces him to produce his Earth reproduction sequence again, permanently and publicly. The lowland conservancy withdraws its citation. Afterward Sera finds that the district's working schedule book has a page cut from the binding gutter and a replaced column that was cut from a printed form rather than written by hand.

## Chapter 0026 — Sera Draws a Boundary

Ilyan wants to file to reopen the bridge record on the C—2 evidence. Sera refuses, because a reopening suspends every relief entered under the record, including the stay, and the stay is the only lawful crossing Tamsin has. She refuses to be the lever her family's missing page becomes, accepts Ilyan only as a record-checker, and gives him one task: verify ordinary custody, not a villain. They trace the printed form to a public print run, a standing print authority, and a routine maintenance update in a strongbox in a rented room — nine ordinary steps. The alteration's routing carries an incomplete code, its second element cut away.

## Chapter 0027 — The Witness Who Refuses the Schedule

Ilsa Bray refuses to sign a forward schedule reading *until reviewed and thereafter* with a wage line that has no hours, and offers the physical defect instead: on the third run the west joint comes back to its mark, walks two fingers out, and comes back. Tarin refuses to let a worker's refusal become a switch, and Ilyan files the smallest request of the batch — one span, one joint, named hands, paid, ending at the seventh morning bell. Oren records the offer and the refusal in the guild's own book. Ilsa reveals that the sealed plate's cut edge is a load-book edge, that a crew kept two witnesses, and that a woman paid off the book when the crew list was cut to one name said *a book without a second hand is only a record.* She does not claim that woman was her grandmother, and she asks the room to stop her deciding whether the levy ledger's cut name and the plate's cut are one hand.

## Chapter 0028 — The Two Books

Nine slots with no slack in them: four guild hands, three road-party hands of whom two cannot leave the road, and two ward hands, one of whom holds the whistle. Ilyan writes a second rule he has never said to anybody, does not file it, and does not destroy it, after Oren shows him that a rule making refusal free also makes refusal lawful on the worst day in the worst weather. The work is done: apron, joint course, brace, count, salt crack. The district's new two-book ledger puts paid work in one book and refusals in the other, and Vale enters the household's refusal under a position heading until Tarin points at the fourth column. Sera returns with the print shop's answer: the authorization for run 114 was cut out of the stock ledger and replaced by one signed by a preservation authority rather than a person. The form's print strip shows the run was distributed to a plate office six days before the ninth-bridge public notice.

## Chapter 0029 — The Review That Cannot Convene

The local-institution review cannot convene because the ward has no recognized address, so the ward seats are empty, and Ilyan refuses to fill them by treating the ward as one claimant. Tarin offers a neutral boundary without an order, and the sitting becomes a bounded evidence session with no findings and no order. Sera refuses in public to let the pre-arrival paper be submitted; Ilyan enters the routing as a fact instead. The quorum clerk's shelf index reveals that the bridge's maintenance-review condition was rerouted to the C—2 continuity desk on the day the working plate was executed, and that the desk has no named clerk and no incoming index.

## Chapter 0030 — The Road Beneath the Charter

The district offers the household four concessions for one maintenance signature; Neris refuses, because the charter has no exit and a signature would bind her to a rule she could never leave. The same acknowledgment is served on the guild's crew, assigning the burden to their successors until the charter is amended, and Oren refuses it on the guild's own ground and without Ilyan's record: the guild will not carry a term it cannot use. Ilyan files one request, to record the local arrangement as found and to state that the charter is not amended; appeal, enforceable exit, and durable housing and work protection are named as unresolved. Tamsin asks for one line in the file that goes to the review: *I am not a reason for the bridge.* A comparison of the sealed plate against the older witness index shows one cut and not three — same hand, tool, sitting and week, with the salt gone white in both — and the pre-arrival paper's receipt falls inside the C—2 index's gap.

## Chapter 0031 — The Review That Opens With a Number

The seventh morning bell rings at noon and the review opens on a measurement, not an argument: the court record hand Roke enters the joint's deviation and the deck line's permanent set as two different numbers because Ilsa says they are two different numbers. The bench levy is spent as of Thursday. The court attempts to close the matter under the emergency continuity rule, consolidating it into a docket with no date on the ground that the maintenance-review condition's routing is under question and a consolidated matter cannot receive new local evidence, which would have lapsed the stay at noon by the court's own procedural convenience. The court then continues the relief on its own ground — a relief does not lapse because the court continued the matter — on the identical principle the district has been using against the household, sides turned round, and the district objects and is right. Ilyan's smallest request is a pause route: one named keeper, one named inspection, a stated number of bells, no charter, no rule, no precedent, no reopening. He admits in public that he has no good answer to who pays for a stopped span, and the district immediately names the carts as the affected party, which is correct and which removes the ward and the household from the column. The review records the two-finger drift as a measured condition and orders the guild to bring a costed pause proposal to a full hearing that is not scheduled, which only an attached record-holder with standing may ask for. Nobody can refute the district's objection that asking for a scheduled hearing is a reopening, because the difference has never been tested in a public room. Bram Orren, a bell-runner, rings the change and asks for the figures for his line book and is refused.

## Chapter 0032 — The Work the Levy Does Not Pay

The guild's maintenance fund is empty and the board says so. Oren refuses to let Ilyan under the span and Ilsa refuses a stranger near the scale, so Ilyan documents instead of examining. The four candidates are the joint, the apron, the water, and the load order; the apron and the order are eliminated on the day. Bram Orren, a seventeen-year-old bell-runner from the Briar March, brings an oiled-cloth map drawn from fourteen local children and a footman at the salt wharf showing the channel moved west and the western abutment stands in its bed, and states his own want — a closed span breaks four standing arrangements on his line for people who cannot afford a court. Puck goes under the seat with a lamp and the guild finds the cause: the west seat is bedded on a course the water is washing the fines out of, so the joint is not wearing but emptying, and it borrows two fingers on load and gets them back. The fix is mundane, expensive, and needs five things the district and the guild do not have. The guild's own account shows that a crew of six at every change would have caught it four days ago, because the under-deck watch has been one hand since the year the crew list went to one name, and two hands went when the second witness went. Oren writes *a book without a second hand is only a record* on the board under everything.

## Chapter 0033 — An Institution Wins Something

In the district's rented office off the weighbridge, Oren refuses the maintenance acknowledgment and asks for nothing but the guild's own wage: two loads of crushed stone, four sacks of lime, fiber delivered to the line, and one load-held day with six hands at the levy rate, argued entirely from the guild's own board and the guild's own six-year failure to find an emptied seat. The district's position is coherent and not corrupt — a span that cannot be stopped is cheaper to maintain than one that can — and Dain says plainly that a district cannot fund a power it has neither created nor voted for. Vale supplies the office's own four-word limit: *the office has no power here.* The district concedes because declining would require its clerk to certify in writing that a span with an emptied seat is fit for unlimited load, and Vale enters that true reason on the back of the slip in his own hand. Ilyan contributes nothing and is told so; Dain tells him the object of his eleven-day objection won an argument in a room where nobody needed him. The district's price is a regional maintenance conference, convened overnight at the old weighhouse, on an agenda printed before anyone had said the word *day* out loud.

## Chapter 0034 — The Room Where the Clause Might Be Written

At the fen crossing, forty-one delegates want one sentence, and Ottla Prenn writes eleven lines that actually contain a keeper's pause. The common rule cannot be adopted because two districts cannot produce their maintenance terms, and the conference's own custody index shows both held at a code with no office written against it — the C—2 code — which is procedural failure, not moral failure, and which nobody expected. Ilyan, barred from being cited, is useful as a documentarian, warns the room that an accurate sentence gets used for whatever the reader has, and is ignored. He is then offered the only job in the room that consists of being the name on everything — keeper of a regional witness list, safe because his name cannot be cited — and refuses, correctly, on two grounds: a post with no door is a charter with no door, and a witness list must be carried by something boring, local, paid, and impossible to promote. The guild refuses it from the other side. Draft eleven circulates unadopted with the witness question open, and Oren learns that his clause is a local detail beside a region that cannot find two of its own terms.

## Chapter 0035 — The Ro Household Under the Copy

Bevin Larch delivers a notice in the lane: the Ro household is entered in the ward's common register as a participating party, pending verification, which means the district may consult it and notify it of load-pattern changes without asking. The register's own draft carried the household in because an attached record's public account was entered as a district docket — an institution responding to a record, not to a person — and the district's complaint that four records are four doors has become a gate. Tamsin asks what participation gets her (notice) and what it costs (her mother's consent to be asked, which the district no longer needs) and then asks what she must do to get out, and learns there is an objection column and no column for leaving. Ilyan asks the Bench to hold the entry; the district cites the Saltwheel distinction that Ilyan himself established and wins, and he says out loud in the lane that he taught it. Hanne Kolm reads the register draft aloud and delivers the chapter's line: a book with seven columns and no eighth is a book where people may say no and not go. Larch enters the household's objection in his own hand and appends, on his own authority, that the register records no consent and has no column for it.

## Chapter 0036 — What the Household Will Not Say

Tamsin says, unprompted, what the bell *is* rather than what it meant: a rope over a worn wheel on a pin and a hammer in a bell, fixed to the post and to nothing on the span, rung by a person. It rang, the people on the span got off, they got off, and the span went on. Tarin refuses, publicly and by name, to let the account be taken as a witness to Ilyan's theory, and Ilyan does not reach for his book. Neris refuses to send her, and gives no reason in public. Tamsin sets five terms, the fifth being that her account may not be used to cure or support any refusal, and chooses the guild's book over the court's because a book somebody wants cannot be said to have been made for her. Oren takes the account and the terms, Ilsa reads it back once and is corrected once, Neris witnesses without signing, and Oren records that the guild is not a neutral body. In the lane, Neris tells Ilyan the reason she will not explain in a room: an inspector sat on her bench on the eighth day and explained that if Tamsin said the bell was not answered, the household would not be refusing maintenance — the district was offering her daughter as a reason.

## Chapter 0037 — The Clerk Who Was Sent to Enter It

The print shop answers one question a day; Sera's is spent and Ilyan's has never been asked. Neral gets them into the cold index room behind the shop and identifies the mechanics of the cut authorization page: the shop does not re-cut stock, a bound volume is rebound by a binder, and the paste is cold, so the volume went back into service the same day. The answer is ordinary and damning: run 114 was authorized by a standing authority of preservation, which is a paper that keeps deciding the same thing while the emergency it was written for lasts and does not need a name because it was never meant to be argued with. Its index for the week the current working plate was executed shows eleven items taken into preservation, all routed C—2, two named in class: the ninth-bridge maintenance-review condition and a sealed continuity request. Neither carries a name. Sera declines to ask what is in the sealed item and the desk records the refusal. Ilyan's temptation is to build a villain out of an office; Vale names the price — records stop coming, to everybody — and redirects him to the wage. Vale also tells him his standing has lapsed at the bell. The day ends with an automatic district notice: the refused acknowledgment must be re-issued or the schedule lapses at dusk, and Oren tells Ilyan he does not write this next arrangement.

## Chapter 0038 — The Schedule Lapses

At dusk the district's schedule ends on its own term with no breach by anybody, entered in three short true lines by Vale, and the stone sits at a yard at the district's expense with no lawful reason to move it. Oren refuses Ilyan the pen and the bodies that will carry the work write the arrangement themselves: the guild's empty account, the road party, the pump's own take at the ward rate with Dalla Rusk refusing to speak for the ward and entering two days of shortage in public, and an eighth of a load-fee from nine named cart owners, with no household paying anything. It is better than the last two arrangements — every hour named, refusals still refusals — and worse in a named way: there is no district copy any more, and a carrier will not put a fen-road roof load on a road that ends on a date with nothing behind it, so the lower road's shelter loses its roof. Neris names the household that loses it first: Marda Rennick, room nine, and her son Besh, nine, who cannot be on a ladder. Bram's line book is the only record of what actually crossed. Larch enters the cause of the lapse in a public ward roll because a record that does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by, and Ilyan says the plain sentence that the lapse is the district's decision, and nobody argues.

## Chapter 0039 — The Continuity Voice in a Public Field

Harrow returns, reads the file standing in the cold, confirms the scheduling request is built out of four things and no archive, and then states that Ilyan's standing has lapsed, which means the court cannot take the request from him. The guild asks instead, in the name of a burden named in the record, on a slate Oren holds up; that is the batch's loss producing its win. Dain objects that a scheduling upon a consolidated matter is a reopening; Harrow opens the field on five disputed records plus the guild's working count book, at Tarin's insistence. The joint goes out three fingers and back, a figure nobody has seen, while the permanent set does not move. A fifth mark appears in the count book's margin — a count-book cut for a load that walked and did not come back — with nobody near the book. Tarin walks under the rope, picks it up with both hands, refuses Dain's attempt to secure it, and holds it until a court clerk's time and his own signature record what he is doing, because he once enforced an order on a village on the strength of a record he did not test. The Bench's ruling schedules the full hearing for the fourth morning bell and does not reopen the record, and carries beneath it a clipped instruction nobody requested: *continuity preserves the record before any remedy is stated.* Roke says it is not on his tray. Harrow refuses to rule on it, on the ground that a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter. Ilyan refuses to build on it in public because he cannot say who said it, and Sera refuses in the same breath to let the Quiet Chamber chain be used to trace it.

## Chapter 0040 — What the Field Left Standing

Ilyan's four load-bearing facts are read aloud in the workroom and Sera corrects two of the four and is right about exactly those two: the drift is a band (one and a half on a light load, up to three on a heavy one) and not a figure, and the schedule lapsed because a term ran out on an instrument nobody re-issued, not because the guild refused. Moot enters, in the open, that the attached record-holder has no remaining constituency: the conservancy withdrawn, the name uncitable, the household a party under objection, the ward seats unfilled, the standing lapsed, and one preserved attached observation that binds nobody. Ilyan delegates in daylight — Oren takes the physical witness and will describe rather than advocate, Ilsa takes the measurement and keeps the half, Joren takes the road and the load sequence, Tarin takes the boundary and will testify to what a field does not write, Bram's line book is named and refused before it can be used — and keeps only the record, saying that if a court finds a fifth thing he will not have been the one looking for it. Tamsin consents to a copy rather than the book on four terms of her own, including that the court must say out loud that the account went to the guild first and nobody made her; Neris reserves the right to stop everything herself if her daughter is put in a room. Sera names the one lever left in the case, the sealed item in the preservation index, and forbids it, because nobody knows what happens to an item a court has ruled on. The batch closes on a span with a chalked end date, a measured and funded but unfixed drift, an unexplained mark in a guild's book, a set hearing, and a C—2 chain one step deeper with no name in it.

## Chapter 0041 — The Hours the Arrangement Does Not Have

The last evening of the Chapter 38 arrangement. The last day of a week is a thin road: nine carts over, seven loaded, the four empties gone east and not coming back, so the load-fee is short and A. wants the frame to say so. Oren's crew works it on the guild's own empty account; Puck comes up out of the hatch with grey lamp glass and reports that there is no stone in the bed of the west course, only a skin of lime, and that he is not a measure. Neris dictates the exact sentence Ilyan must say to Marda Rennick about the roof, and forbids the word *carrier* in front of her son. Oren costs a pause for the first time — two bells for a load that can turn, six for one that must be reversed with a full approach behind it, eight as the ceiling, six hands, at the ward rate — and the sum is unaffordable, and he says the true reason: a clause nobody pays for is a bell nobody rings. ## Chapter 0049 — The Ruling

Harrow disposes of all three remedies out loud, refused ones first. Hold is refused because an interval is what a room gives itself when it does not have a ruling, and Joren's objection is preserved. ## Chapter 0051 — The Gate at the Low Water

Ilyan walks the four miles down to Halloway's low water on the twelfth day of the unloaded period and does not get into the room, because his name is on the paper and every person there would read the name and stop listening. **The seven days between the copy and the walk are on the page: the copy up the fen road on the fifth logged sideways into the ninth-bridge matter because no file had Halloway's name on it, read twice on the frame against the settlement with the same answer both times — lawful, made out of a public instrument, carrying the sentence that helps a person and not the one that would have cost the district something — and nothing he can do with it, because his own name is the reason; the court's attachment on the ninth; the carter who was turned back coming up off the fen road empty at the second bell on the tenth, unable to say which gate and unasked in a district room; and the choice of the twelfth over the fourteenth because on the twelfth he can stand in a lane and be nobody for a whole day.** On the bank he counts a standpipe queue of forty-one at the sixth bell and knows there will be nine at the ninth, and he reads a sheet that was printed nine days ago, nailed up with four nails and no glass, in which the fourth paragraph turns the ninth bridge's own two-hundred-year-old word *lawful traveler* into a reason to shut a gate, against a traveller book four inches thick and a hundred years out of date. **Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, a refuse hauler and the only person on her street who reads, reads the sheet out at the queue and reads the fourth paragraph twice, which she has never done in eleven years, and says the word in it is the bridge word and that it means a person in a book.** A ratepayer asks whether the sheet is his; Ilyan says it is an extract of a settlement he asked to be citable and did not send, and that nobody had been cruel to produce it. Bryn Sallow, forty-four, gate hand for thirty years, says the sheet is not wrong — she is the one who is wrong, because she does not know a word on it and nobody has told her in thirty years which of two things is true, and asks for it to be written down, and it is entered in the district's day book in the clerk's own hand. Wenna asks the question nobody can answer: she is not on the book, she hauls two hundred loads a year out of the low water, and *am I lawful.* Corva Silt, thirty-one, the bench clerk, admits before anybody else that she printed the extract on the second day after the hearing under a lawful print authority and sent a copy to the man whose name is at the bottom of it. ## Chapter 0052 — The First Honest Use

On the thirteenth day the water is let at the second bell and the room empties itself, and a licensed recordwright arrives at the fifth bell with the ninth-bridge read-back in the guild's words and says in the first minute that she is not a witness for Ilyan and that Halloway has not asked. ## Chapter 0053 — The Form

The fourteenth day. At the fifth bell Ilyan is in the room on the strength of the Bench's order, and Corva Silt has Instrument H-9 on the table under her hand: five clauses, numbered, sealed, published at the fourth bell. Wenna Tace reads the first clause twice on her own initiative and will not be asked for it twice, because the word in it is the one they will all argue about, and then reads on. **Clause three is the best clause on the instrument and the clerk wrote it in the small hours because a notice that says who may open a gate and does not say it is the gate hand is a notice that says anybody may open a gate.** The turn is not a speech and not a clause: it is the cart coming back down the hill in front of eighty people with everything still on it. **Abel Kearn, thirty-five, a builder out of the upper street with a builder's apron and lime in the seam of it,** asks what Ilyan did about this and gets the true answer, that he asked in a corridor for the settlement to be citable, did not send it, and was not frightened, and that he cannot read a clause and has said so on the record, and that if anybody wants to make a rule about that they should make it about him and not about the paper — and lands the sharpest line in the room: *then you are no use in this room, and I am sorry, because I have a bad thing and I want somebody to make it good, and there is no somebody.* **He also names the whole shape of it, that there is one lawyer's clerk in this district, she is thirty-one, and she printed it.** Kett finds that Halloway has used the bridge's paper to say something true — its own hundred-and-sixty-year-old book calls the culvert road a penal road — and calls the template a *frame* rather than a monster, and says he does not know whether to be proud of the rule or sorry for the paper. ## Chapter 0054 — The Name on the Bottom of the Paper

The fifteenth day. Cael Rundle, fifty-eight, ratepayer of Weir Lane, moves that the ninth-bridge settlement be withdrawn from use in Halloway — not the sheet, the precedent — because it produced H-9, and says he is not asking for the water. Ilyan is produced as the record-holder and asked the fair question: does your own paper, which says a person not on a register is not a trespasser by that fact alone, make his gate unlawful. **It is the fairest question anyone has asked him since he crossed and he does not have the answer, and he says so standing up in front of sixty people in a room where he has no standing, no constituency, and no advocate, and gives the reasons in order — a span has a deck and a load path and a keeper, and a culvert gate has none of those things, and the difference is in the machinery of a legal instrument that nobody in the district has.** He puts the cost on the table before anybody finds it: the answer is four miles up the road, the sixth-bell queue is on the bank, the gate is worked by one woman who cannot sit down, and **there is no line in this district on which her hour can be paid, because the line that pays an hour like that is a term of the paper the ratepayer has just asked to withdraw.** Bryn Sallow prices it at an hour and Corva Silt enters it and finds there is no line to put it on, and writes in the district's own hand that the office has no line. The answer arrives at the ninth bell in a counter's hand off the bridge, in four lines, ending *I am not a lawyer and I count loads*: the access condition is about this span, a span has a deck and a load path and a keeper, a gate on a culvert has none of those things, and a district may make a list of its own. Cael does not withdraw his motion, and asks for something else instead: a line in this district's book on which an hour of work can be paid to a person by name, and a date. ## Chapter 0055 — One Legible Rule

The eighteenth day, and Orison is a place for the first time. The First Verdict is a block of black basalt the size of a house with no throne and no roof, and the scarce thing at it is a place on a docket: nineteen names on the board, eleven places, and the eleven are all bridge matters. A licensed recordwright and the court translator read the ninth-bridge conditions together all morning without once disagreeing about a word, which happens about four times a year and did not happen at all in the first month of the case. **Magistrate Ansa Rell, forty-four, eleven years in the office, calls the common-rule sitting, requires the burden to be stated before the opinion, borrows Harrow's procedure and says where she got it, and puts a man who spoke from a gallery back on the floor.** Convener Bel Ordway, who has a salt cart on the weighbridge and four minutes of the hour, says he wants one legible rule because he is tired of his own clerk. **Ansel Nyle, fifty-one, keeper of the low fen's one bridge and in the post since he was nineteen, produces his district's maintenance terms: they adopted the ninth-bridge settlement entire on the fifth day, by copying a public instrument out of a book, and his keeper has called the deck clear twice and both peat carts that could not turn were off the deck in nine minutes and both were paid out of road funds to the person on a line in chalk. The ward line does not go short on a thin week, because the ward line comes out of road funds and the toll does not.** Ordwin Sarn, of the cut banks, says the same and adds that his clerk cried about the water on the seventh day and that is normal and is the only part of this nobody warned her about. **The sitting fails on a category and not on a document: a rate may be paid only to a body with a name, the ward is not a body, and two delegates in the same room name the ward differently and are each right about somebody. ## Chapter 0058 — A Loss Taken on the Record

The twenty-first morning, and the leg is worse and the smell is in two layers in a lane with hay in it, and Sera makes him decide in advance what he will do when it happens. **The question is one sentence: whether the ninth-bridge settlement has any force in a district that printed or quoted it without adopting it.** Kett argues the true thing — a span has a deck and a keeper and a review date, and a review date cannot be a fact about four districts at once — and Harrow holds the argument and then says what his ruling costs before he rules: the low fen and the cut banks adopted the settlement by copying a public instrument, it worked, and if the settlement does not travel their keepers have no power, their pay lines are for hours no term requires, and neither man can produce his own terms. **Ilyan's request — a named declaration that the settlement binds no district that has not adopted it — is granted in about nine minutes, and he withdraws it on the record, in four sentences, and then gets a cart down the fen road with a folded sheet in a keeper's hand: *if it binds nowhere I have a copy of nothing and a cart that cannot turn. You do not owe me the power. ## Chapter 0071 — The First Ask

The thirtieth of the month. A folded paper arrives on the ninth bridge counter on a cart that was not coming for anything: the bench of four of **Nethergill** — eleven miles from the bridge, seven from Halloway — asking, under the second half of the fourth term, for the terms of the Halloway charter, **and for the paper itself, and it cannot pay for the paper.** Four names and a mark, a wedge-and-chain seal nobody here has seen, and a wet ring over three of the names. **Corva Silt will not certify a bench eleven miles off** and says the mechanism does not know it is the mechanism. ## Chapter 0072 — What the Word Does to a Man With a Season

The first of the month, at a bench of four's table in Nethergill: a sealed, numbered, un-ordered form summarising the Halloway charter **with EXCEPTION at the top of it in a bigger letter than anything else on the page.** Nobody ordered it, nobody sent it, and a man in the lane has already been told by the reader at the bar that the paper in his hand is the rule he is living inside. **Hal Dreeve, thirty-four, hires out barrows, has put his name down for a season of four months at a weighhouse, and is refused in a yard in ninety seconds by a foreman holding up the word.** **The batch's discovery: the word is not a label on a rule, it is a claim that the rule is temporary — and the charter has no end on it, because it was made out of a bridge, and a bridge does not end.** The clean instrument that lost does expire, at the third sitting of next quarter, and the word is correct and old. Then a second visit to the yard. **Parnell Slee, forty-eight, nineteen years at the weighhouse, is honest about the second reason: two seasons is what a man does at a weighhouse, and the factor will not put a man on for four months out of a season he cannot use, and that is true with or without the paper. ## Chapter 0073 — What They Actually Wanted

The first of the month, in a cold room in Nethergill. **A bar with a penny in it and nobody at it is not a gate, and that is the whole of the trouble at Lowgill:** a waggon of hedge off the Orison road goes round the chain and puts six feet of a load on the chapel flat, and the bar is not a gate, and the tollkeeper was a waggon of draught. **Jonas Pyle, sixty-three, convenor, says what the district wanted: a person to come and stand in the room, once, so that the bench knows what it is looking at.** He has driven the second reading of nine sheets to a man who cannot read them and has understood about one word in four. **Ilyan's answer to *who are you* is the worst thing he has said in this case, and it is true: he is not a bench, a party, a body, or a district; his standing is confined to one matter about a bridge eleven miles away; and the whole of his use is that the fourth term names him and he is the reason the appeal is not stayed. ## Chapter 0075 — The Door

The sixth to the seventh, in a room over a pump. ## Chapter 0076 — What Thirty Years Is Worth, Wrongly

The ninth to the fourteenth. A bench of four cannot carry a debt without a figure and cannot get one, because the woman whose debt it is refused to have it counted. **Ilyan has six days of a real working tide, and multiplies them up, and says the three things that are wrong with it before he does it.** The figure: **a hundred and thirteen thousand four hundred hours at threepence the hour is one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings — six years of the district's road fund and thirty-two years of the cost of the charter.** Wrong three ways. **Bryn Sallow gives the first correction herself and unasked: the working tide of the turn is one day in seven and the week comes to about sixty-three hours, not seventy-three and a half — which takes out sixteen thousand two hundred hours and two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings, about two years of a lane of forty roofs at a shilling a week.** The second cannot be found: threepence the hour is not a rate this district has; it is a day's figure of nineteen and a halfpence divided by the six and a half hours the district does not pay for, and there is no rate in this city from thirty years ago, and the third of a haul rate has not moved since the year a woman on this street had her mother buried. The third is that thirty years of three hundred and sixty days is a number he made up. It is entered in three hands in the district's book, with the refusal on the same page and a line saying the figure is wrong by two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings and by an amount nobody can name, and that it was not written by a clerk and that the person it is owed to refused it and cannot be compelled and cannot be overruled. **On the bank she tells him what a number is: a claim, and a claim wants somebody to answer it, and she has gone thirty years without being a claim.** She makes him count again with the line down the middle of the slate, and on the fourteenth writes both sets of figures side by side and says *a person is not a measuring instrument, I have said that on a field and it goes on a page and it is still true.* **He enters the sixth thing he cannot un-enter, with its size, in his own hand: a figure of thirty years' work entered without consent, wrong by at least two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings and by an unnameable amount; he is now a witness in one more matter and a witness can be produced; nobody has thanked him and he did not ask.** Sera reads it back, does not tidy it, and says the reason out loud once: a man who enters six things against his own name is not a careful man, he is a man frightened that if he waits for a better room he will not say them at all, and he is right to be frightened and wrong about the reason.

## Chapter 0077 — What It Costs to Have Said No in Public

A clerk of the Orison chamber has become a person who is stopped talking to in a corridor, because when a person says *where can I swear this*, four benches in nine say *not here*, faster than the question, with the expression a person makes when they have read something of their own in a public record. **Dessin Nahl walks eleven miles and pays two of the six days out of her own, and enters the price in the same place as the answer, because a thing with no cost in it looks like evidence.** She does not ask nine benches to hold a page. **She asks one bench one question, on one matter, on a day the bench appoints.** Fallowditch, a bench of three, its clerk Perditta Nye, twenty-nine, three years in the post, answers in four lines and **withdraws its answer of the eighteenth in writing, because a bench that will not say who it did that for is a bench that decided the answer was free — and the who is a woman called Adela Cray.** The hearing on the eleventh, in the bench's own room over a shop that was a chandler's, two of three present, the landlord not there and not a party: the agreement is real, it is entered, it is dated, it is signed — and a fortnight's notice is between a woman and a man and no bench in this city hears that. **It is the first time in nineteen years this city has had a hearing that produced nothing and a room that produced everything.** The other six are reported, not staged; three of the seven have been asked by a person in their own district to produce a page and have had to say, from their own letter, that there was nothing in their room. **A public answer is a price: a bench that has told the truth about itself has priced a room in its own district at one answer in eleven days.** In Fallowditch a landlord puts the bench's own letter face up on a table and says he is not doing anything wrong, and reads it out, which he did not have to, and does not want it known that he read it out. And then the tenth question, put up a stair four miles from the First Verdict: **will the standing authority of preservation receive and keep a page sworn by a person in a district?** Answered in the same office hand for the second time in fifteen days: **no — it holds what is printed, a sworn page is not printed, an authority that keeps records of persons is a bench, and an office with no person in it is not a door, it is a shelf — and it has said twice that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person.** Nahl's own finding in her own book and in no record: a city that has established it is empty has established that the one thing it is not empty of is a shelf; anybody who needs a page sworn can now name the one desk that said yes; the leverage is real, it is on a desk with a stamp, and it cannot be used, and in about a year somebody will try anyway and there will be a form about it. Four sentences from a magistrate in the well of the court: the duty to answer has cost a woman a room and got a bench a hearing, and the scarcity has made a public answer into a price and one desk into a monopoly, and neither on purpose, and a city that has said out loud that it is empty is a city in which a person with a document and nowhere to put it has a problem no bench can hear.

## Chapter 0078 — Twenty-One Names

The fourteenth. **Abel Kearn's matter has been on the docket board since the eighth of last month and has never had a place, and the wall that is going is a hundred and forty-one years old and in a book in Fallowditch, which has publicly said it holds nothing anybody can swear to.** A timber merchant stacking elm against it is not a criminal, is right, and will not be repaid for the crack. Nobody can stop him: the court has no power over a yard, a district cannot move a boundary or swear a survey that is not in its own book, and Fallowditch's answer is true. So Kearn put his name on a board, and then worked out what a name on a board is. **A name on the board is not a thing that gets you heard. A name on the board is a name in the record, and a record is a thing a clerk can come back with, and he has no holder and no page and cannot swear anything, and he has gained nothing from a name on a piece of chalk except the ability to be produced.** He says it in the open air, standing four feet inside the rail with chalk in his hand, having told the room in advance that he is not asking to be heard. He asks permission to do it before he does it. **He rubs his own name off the board with his thumb, which takes most of a minute on a board that has been gone over about four hundred times, and the board goes to twenty-one names, and the eleven places do not change, because a place is made by a matter being ready and not by a name being on a board.** Nahl enters it in her own hand, moves the matter up a place, and he tells her to leave it where it is. Then the mechanism nobody has: **a matter is heard when a document can be sworn, a document is sworn where it is kept or in a room that holds it, and there is no clause anywhere in this city that allows a person to carry a sworn page four miles to a room.** Hollis Dune prices it — two shillings and threepence for nine sheets, read out twice at a pump to two hundred people, done in a kitchen, unpaid. Rell says what a hauler's room over a pump would do with a document, and then his last question, six words long, which he could not get in front of the court a month ago: **can I be counted as a place.** No — and the reason given in the plainest words she has: a place is not a person, and a court that has just spent two months taking places out of its arithmetic cannot put one back in, and if it did then in about a year there would be eleven places with names in them and no documents, and the way to be heard would be to be a location. Then she enters what he actually did, in her own words, as not a ruling, not a precedent, and binding nobody including him.

## Chapter 0079 — Nine Yards With No Name In Them

The fifteenth to the eighteenth. **A man with a cart has been lifting the chain at Lowgill bar and lifting the pennies out of the slot into a tin under his seat for ten weeks, and there is nothing anybody can do, because a bar with a slot and a chain and nobody at it is not a gate.** Rennick Hale, forty-one, says so in a cold room to a magistrate's clerk, in four long passages, and says he has not spent a penny of it and has eleven pounds of arrears in a house coming free in eight weeks, and asks for the four pounds and six to be accounted for under his own name while he is standing there saying it. The word *ward* is on a post at the end of that lane, painted once, in guild colours, a hundred and ninety years old, and nobody in the room needs it, and it is described correctly once by a man who does not know the word. **The Nine Yards: sixty-one people, nine gates, one well with a bucket, no district, paying Nethergill's rate for a hundred and forty-one years for a bar they do not own, because a rate followed a road and a boundary followed a hedge.** And a matter comes before the First Verdict, because a carter is a person and a place is not, and a carter who had come up to ask about a boundary heard eleven benches say out loud that they hold nothing and asked a question of his own. **The matter is against nobody; the respondents are a bench of four and no keeper of Lowgill bar, and there is no keeper and none has ever been sworn in this city. The place is entered as a fact in the pleadings and is not a party, because a place is not a person.** Service is made by nailing the notice to the post, in the open air, and the court accepts it, and there is no clause in any instrument in this case for notice to a place. **Merrin Coke, twenty-nine, with sixty-one people in charcoal marks on a shutter, walks four miles to Nethergill and seven to a room over a pump and is told she can be read and not held, and asks for a page and is told there is one reader in one district at a time and a place is not a district.** Ilyan offers to hold it. **Sera Quill refuses, and the reason is the whole of the chapter: hold a page anywhere and you are a person in a district, and a holder is not a carrier, and a carrier is the only person who may carry the terms, and there is no instrument in this city that says how a second carrier is sworn in — so the day he opens a page the appeal in Halloway is stayed for about four hundred people, and nobody in this city will be able to tell him he did it. She entered that she did not see it when she wrote the fifth term, and that a man cannot be a door in a lane and the only door at the same time.** Harrow enters that the court has no power over the charter and takes no view, and that this is the third procedure he has invented and none of them is a power. And the four pounds and six goes back into the slot in eleven days, in a cart, with a docket in a clerk's hand, one penny at a time, in front of nine people, by the man who took it out — the only thing in the chapter that got fixed, and it got fixed because a man with a cart is a person, and nobody put anywhere the answer to how long a tin would have stayed under a seat in a lane with no address in it.

## Chapter 0080 — What the Review Bought

The fourth morning bell of the twenty-seventh of the third month, at the span, on public notice, in the open air, with a cart-board across two trestles. **Not called early**, and Tarin Keel's written refusal is in the file: a callable early requires a named reading, he has read it at every change since the third of the second month, and he is a field man and there is no field. The span has carried at every change for fifty-four days; the band was inside it on a light load at mark and a half and a heavy one at two and three, every figure taken with a moving cart; the pale permanent set has not moved. The fourth bridge has been propped for thirty-nine days by four old timbers that have already stood under the ninth bridge's west seat for a fortnight. **The fund, in Meridith Vale's hand: four hundred and ten a year; two hands under a deck every change, two hundred and ten; the telling, fifteen; the charter, forty-four pounds and sixteen; the fourth bridge's four props, ninety-six. Remainder after all four: forty-four pounds and four shillings. Short of the props by fifty-one pounds and sixteen.** Oren Sedge says the guild cannot take half a hand and that a count taken by one man is a count and not a record, and that he is choosing between a bridge that has moved and a bridge with holes in it and will not call that a principle. **The review buys four new props, takes the old four out of the fourth bridge's deck, leaves that deck open in the weather, and reduces the under-deck watch to one hand for the year — and the man who loses the second hand is T. Blore, twenty-two, eleven months under a deck, and he is entered in the disposition, which is the only name in it, and nobody had entered him anywhere.** Vale enters that he did not ask the keeper of the fourth bridge and would not have known what to do with the answer. And she tells him the question the batch ends on: **there is no procedure in this city for what happens to a page when the holder cannot keep it, she is twenty-nine, she hauls two hundred loads a year, and a court is going to sit in about four years and ask how an appeal survives in a district that is being squeezed, and the honest answer is *as long as a woman of twenty-nine can still read and haul at the same time*.** And in a clerk's own book in a chamber in Orison there is a question nobody has read: the only district in this city with a carrier is the only district that does not need one.

## Chapter 0081 — The Deck That Is Open in the Weather

The second of the fourth month at the fourth morning bell, on the fourth bridge's deck, which has had four square holes in it since the twentieth of the first month and has been open to the weather since the twenty-seventh of the month before last, six days. **Wist Anhalt, forty-nine, twenty-nine years in the post, is sitting on it with a piece of chalk he does not use, and nobody asked him what he thinks, and the new timbers bought at ninety-six pounds are lying in the mud on the north bank because they were cut on the road above the fen three days ago and there is no water in the river to float a timber up to a deck.** He walks a loaded peat cart over the second hole at the pace he has walked that bridge since before the carter's father had a cart, and he says the deck is open and he is not going to stop a load, and he says he is the man on it and nobody asked. Down the fen road a letter has come from **Meridith Vale, clerk of the ninth bridge's district**, which is a bill and not a notice: the charter of the district of Halloway costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year out of a fund of four hundred and ten, a little under a ninth of it, and the year as set out at the review leaves forty-four pounds and four shillings, so the charter costs twelve shillings more than what is left. **One hand under a deck is a hundred and five pounds and came back on the day of the review, and it is in a different line, and a clerk cannot move a line into another line without a bench's minute — so the ninth bridge's district has a hundred and five pounds it cannot put against the charter.** They will pay it twelve shillings over the line. A bench of four in a room over a pump decides what to do with a figure it entered and cannot discharge: **keep it, and enter what the law costs, not what it costs us** — four hundred and ten, forty-four pounds sixteen, the figure of the thirty years, a hundred and sixty-year-old book, and the two hundred and forty people Halloway has never counted itself. *A debt that cannot be paid and cannot be refused is entered here as a debt this district owes.* On a wet fen road Ilyan says he is sorry about the props to a keeper who tells him they were the ninth bridge's, notices that he has been standing there trying to think of something else, and is sent down the road.

## Chapter 0082 — Half a Day

The third of the fourth month, the second bell, nineteen miles off at the cut banks. **Oren Sedge has walked it himself, because a letter from a trade comes at the change and a man on a deck reads it standing up and takes it for a summons about something else, and because the guild is out of money, has lent four benches to a court it has no standing in, and has six crews that have not voted for anything.** He could not come before: the count is taken at every change and if he is not on the deck at the change there is no count. **T. Blore, twenty-two, eleven months at the west end, has been under the deck at the cut banks for two months working for Harl Mecken, sixty, who has had nine apprentices.** Sedge tells him the disposition, and that the second hand is gone, and that **his name is the only name in it — and that a man called Vester put it there at a review and Sedge did not, and would have taken the hand just the same if he had not been in the room.** Blore says the figure for him: four hours a day at the ward rate, a hundred and five pounds a year, worked out from a mate's arithmetic in the winter. The resistance is not the loss — **it is that the loss is recoverable-looking: a fund with forty-four pounds and four shillings in it would pay him back about five months, a district can be written to, a chamber can be asked, and a man can be sent for who is not a bench and cannot make anybody answer.** He works out that there is no job either: one hand under the deck at the ninth bridge this year, no cartage on the fen road, four square holes in the fourth. Sedge tells him the true thing nobody has told him — *you were not let go, the crew list went to one name in the winter because the guild ran out of money, and the review took a second hand out of a fund and bought four timbers with it* — and says it so that a man of twenty-six does not carry a false story about a trade. The chapter ends on a shipping docket written in the dark in a room over a smithy: **T. BLORE. FOUR HOURS A DAY. ONE OF TWO SEASONS.**

## Chapter 0083 — A Right With No Door In It

The fourth to the sixth of the fourth month. **Merrick Vaunt, nineteen, a lighterman's mate who was taught letters at nine and can get most of a second sentence, comes to the room over a pump because Corva Silt sent him, and he is there because he has been told two different answers to *what is a haul* and he is the one people ask.** The chapter's discovery is a hole in a rate nobody had looked at: **the district's own rate is a rate for a *load*, and this bench has never once written down what a load is a rate for, and nobody has ever been asked** — and so the hundred and fifty hauls have no figure at all, and Corva Silt has gone over the entry four times and cannot make it come out. **Wenna Tace asks the question: may the rate for a haul be appealed by any person. The answer is no** — it was set by a bench of four, the only person who asked for it is the person who asked, a holder may not be a party or appeal or appear in support or be named, and no person outside the district has standing. And the other half: the pump is a public place, and it is not a hearing, because a convenor of about sixty is not going to call a pump a hearing in a district with no money and then have *Halloway* put against *a hearing* in a column in four years. **The shape of the whole thing: an appeal is a thing a person can carry; a rate is a thing a person has to be inside of; and the machinery in this city has a procedure for the first and not one word for the second.** The cost of asking is that the answer goes in the district's own book and will be read out at the pump four times a year, and she did not get the money, she got the sentence. **On the fifth she takes on a second reader — a boy of nineteen who is not a holder and is not a clerk and is not paid and was not asked — and the finding is that a page with one pair of eyes on it is a page that cannot be checked, and that there is now a second pair of eyes on it.**

## Chapter 0084 — The Only Room In This City With Anything In It

## Chapter 0086 — Three Rates and a Hole in the Middle of One of Them

The fifteenth of the fourth month, in a room over a pump, and the room is the only one in four miles with anybody awake in it at the first bell. **A slate with three sums on it and a fourth scratched out four times: what the district owes, what it has, what it is paying with.** One thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds ten, less the two hundred and two pounds ten a gate hand corrected herself, is one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds, and the correction for the rate cannot be found in any district in this city. ## Chapter 0087 — The Man Who Took His Name Out of a Rate

## Chapter 0088 — The Second Reading of a Quarter's Rate

## Chapter 0090 — What a Page Is For, Which Nobody Has Answered

The twenty-sixth to the twenty-ninth of the fourth month, and then the second of the fifth. **They produce him.** Corva Silt reads the production out in the flat voice of a clerk producing a witness and says she put the three questions to him in advance and why, and it is because she has read four sheets of his own figures out loud in her own office by herself and was not going to do it again with him in the room. *Did you count it yourself. ## Chapter 0091 — A Question Is Not a Matter

The second and third of the fifth month, in a stone room off the second gallery in a city four miles from a bridge, with a stove that does not draw. **A letter comes down the fen road from the bench of four of the district of Halloway: on the sixth, at the second bell, in the room over a pump, in public, the holder's question will be read out whether a recordwright attends or not, and the bench has asked her to attend and has not asked her to come, because the two are not the same thing.** The licence's four terms are read out in full and the fourth is one line with no date on it: *she takes no matter of her own while she holds this one.* **She writes the six heads of a matter on a sheet and fills none of them and one:** a claim, a subject, a burden, a witness, a remedy, an exit. The one that is filled is the subject, and the subject of the question is a person who is not the person asking. **So the hauler's question is not hers, and answering it would put her inside a matter, and the fourth term stops her, and a term that stops a person from her own claim is not a technicality.** Ilyan offers to walk the four miles and is refused with the reason: that room is four miles from where she stands, he is four miles from a bridge, and those are two different four miles. **Sel Harrow brings a view on the court's own motion — that a question by post from a district that has entered it in its own book is not of itself a matter, and that a recordwright may answer such a question once in writing — and she refuses it**, and gives three reasons: a term that can be read a second way on the day it bites is a wall with a door in it; the day a licence's fourth term can be varied for the person it binds, no fourth term binds anybody; and a sentence about what a page is for, entered with a licence against it, is a thing that will be used against a person. **Harrow confesses that he drafted the fourth term and has used it against her four times without knowing he was doing it.** She sends four questions to a standing authority of preservation, three of them about paper, and at the fifth bell on the fifth of the month she walks four miles down a lane in the rain and a man sweeping a building with a broom asks her whether she has come about the volumes or about the printed shelf, and when she says the volumes he puts the broom against the wall and says, *then nobody has asked me for that one.*

## Chapter 0092 — A Day in a Building With No Name On The Door

The fifth of the fifth month, in rain, four miles from the court, at the low end of a street of counting-houses. **Corrin Vask, forty-one, sweeps the building for nine years at ninepence a day and opens the cold room for anybody who asks, and nobody has ever asked for the volumes.** He says out loud, unasked, that he has wanted somebody to ask him for nine years and has never once asked anybody for it. The resistance is not a locked door: the door is not locked, there is no person in the building, and a hand is willing to turn a page because nobody has ever asked it not to. **The price is a shilling out of a drawer for the opening of a volume, entered in the building's own day book in the same column as the sweeping, with the name of the person who paid it — and the sweeping of that day is not done, and there is one man for the floors and he is the man with the lamp.** The volume is on the shelf he said. Three holes, and the middle one is the slot, the thread is cut, and the inventory mark beside it is in a newer hand. **On the facing leaf, four lines below the shadow she entered on the twentieth of the second month, there is a second shadow, about four inches long, and in three hours with a lamp held low it yields not one character.** The leaf has to be held flat and the hand that holds it is his and his right hand is six years gone at the wrist and he cannot open it afterwards without doing it one finger at a time. Ilyan waits ninety yards down the lane in the rain, in a doorway, for a whole day, because he is a person in no district and cannot go in; the leg goes at the third bell and does not come back, and the rendering is at the same height and the same distance it has been at since the third of the second month, and the token is not touched. **The day's finding: it is going to take days, and a lamp, and a hand, and an opening, and an opening is a shilling, and a shilling is a day of a man who sweeps floors.**

## Chapter 0093 — What a Rule Said

The sixth to the eighth of the fifth month, in the same building. **The standing authority of preservation answers the fourth question no, for the third time, in the same hand, with a reason and not a silence, and says that a thing asked three times with a reason is a thing a person ought to stop asking.** It says what is printed is not lost and what things are for is not printed anywhere. The first three questions are answered yes, and the answer takes two days, two shillings and a hand that is six years gone, and the honest reason for two days is a man opening two cases and holding a lamp. **The yield is about paper and not about a person: a form for lodging a matter in the Quiet Chamber, printed three times in four years, of which the middle printing carries eleven words at the foot that the other two do not — *and the keeping of it is a duty of the ward* — put there by a copy-holder who could not read the line to the end and named its subject themselves.** A thing copied from a thing copied from a thing loses whatever was not in the pen. The finding entered in her own book: **a shelf can tell you what a rule said and cannot tell you what a rule was for, and that is the third time in four months the same answer has come back out of the same kind of place — a bench of three that holds nothing, this desk, and a room over a pump that holds a page — and the honest thing to do with a shelf is stop asking it questions it has already answered.** What she gives up is written down: the only desk in the city that said yes has now said yes and no and a reason three times, and a person who needs a page sworn can no longer even point at it. And the eleven words are the worst thing that has happened to her in a month, because they are the right eleven words and they will be sitting in her head telling her what two illegible characters on a leaf are. **On the sixth of the month, four miles down the fen road, nine people were in the room over a pump and the bench read the holder's question out as it was entered, without improvement, and nobody answered it.**

## Chapter 0094 — One Quarter of a Sentence

The ninth to the twelfth of the fifth month. Four days in a cold room with a lamp held low, a leaf held flat by a hand that is six years gone at the wrist, and eleven correct words face down under the weight of the volume because **a person who has eleven correct words and four inches of faint grey is not reading a leaf, she is checking a leaf, and those produce two different records and only one of them can be sworn to in twenty years.** On the fourth day the crease gives her the rest. **Entered under licence, dated and signed, on the facing leaf beside the empty slot and not on the removed page: [  ] OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND [  ] — one quarter of a sentence, entered as one quarter of a sentence, and it is not the clause, and it does not restore any right of anybody to answer no, and the characters the crease takes she did not supply, and the form's eleven words would have given her two of them and she did not use them, because a form is a copy made by a person who had to leave a volume and a copy is not a witness.** The consequence: **a ward is a duty and not a place, so the keeping of an entry is a thing a district owes, and a thing a district owes is done by whoever is doing its work at the time — and in four miles the person doing it is a hauler of twenty-nine who cannot appeal from one word of it and who has never been told.** The comparison of the twenty-fourth of the second month stands unamended: one quarter of a sentence more does not make a better comparison, it makes a longer one, and a reader in twenty years may prefer the earlier. **The two costs are named: four days, four shillings paid, four days of sweeping not done and not doable, and a right hand that takes nine seconds longer than it did in the spring; and the second cost, which cannot be paid back in paper — she has made herself a door again, in a building four miles off that nobody in four miles can find, and she knew it on the ninth and did it anyway, and the only reason she can give is that nobody was going to ask, which is the reason a desk gave her.** And she asks Vask his question, and he spends it on the only question in this case that has no answer, and tells her about a box of nine hundred and forty forms on the fourth shelf with a word at the top in a bigger letter than any other word on the page, and does not tell her the word.

## Chapter 0095 — A Claim That Is Not Filed

The thirteenth to the sixteenth of the fifth month. **The collision is found by a man sweeping a building who had been given four questions and had spent them all, and who asks the fifth on his own ## Chapter 0096 — A Question the Appeal Cannot Carry

The seventeenth to the twenty-first of the fifth month. **Garvin Slade, thirty-eight, a salt seller, comes up the bank with four feet of a chair leg he has been holding without noticing, and asks to appeal the rule of the seventeenth of last month — not the water, the reading.** He is right about the cost: about two hundred and forty people go up that bank in the cold, and a woman on his lane with a job at the second bell said she would rather not stand in a crowd, and he does not know whether she went because of the rule or because of his name. ## Chapter 0097 — The Aggregate Bill

The Slackwater song is a different song about a different thing: *Three hours down and three hours up / and if you are wrong about it you will not fill the cup.* **Ilyan's only contribution is one question — what are the marks for — and the answer is for counting, given to him twice, and then Seth Garrick gives him the second answer nobody alive on that road has: the post is the honest count, the returns are the money count, the two have never agreed, the post is wood and rots and goes into the sea or a kiln every nine or ten years, and the old one is leaning against the wall of the tide office and nobody has ever looked at it. I could count it. I could tell you the figure and the figure would mean nothing to me and nothing to the guild.** **The appeal, used by somebody who cannot use it: on the seventh day, at the turn, at the tide stone, the custom is that anybody may stand on it on the fourth or the sixth and ask the harbourmaster one question and she answers there, on the stone, in the open, to whoever is on the shingle, and nobody may ask a second, and it is written down nowhere and the charter says nothing about it. The song is a different thing, on the seventh, and a great many people on that road have never worked out that those are two customs and not one — which is why Bel Hask came on the wrong day, and nobody stops a woman with a net. Bel Hask stands on it and asks about the seventh day, and Bettany Rook says no — not because the guild will not sit, but because *you have asked me on the seventh about the seventh, and I cannot sit on it*, and the reason is the water and a boat on the rocks at the north end with a man in it who is not dead because he was seventeen and angry. She is right and has no legal standing whatever, and the song is not a document, and the asking is free and the answer is not.** She offers the guild's own book, a key on her belt, first bell on the first, and then refuses that herself, because the guild's book is a fire going to happen and a question about a road is not a question about her house, and reading it out is not answering it. What she will do is paint the days on the post at the harbour mouth, herself, in a bad light, with her hood up, and he is not to be there. **And the batch holds its pattern: Sabra Trew, twenty-four, third year, has nineteen notches cut on a kiln wall that is the only thing in the shed never moved, and a gap of four days in the row where a bad seventh banked the fire, and a grandmother who kept hers in a book in a school in a town a hundred and forty miles up the coast that nobody she has ever met has opened. Ilyan writes the address on the back of a torn-out page in a penny exercise book with the heel of his right hand and tells her the truth about himself: that he has not done it yet, and will probably do it about the fourth of next tide, and that it will be the only useful thing he has done on this coast in a week.**

## Chapter 0109 — Sixteen Carts, and the Line He Copied Off the Sheet

The first of a new tide, four miles along the coast, on the bar road above the north end. **Alma Gannock, eighty-one, Dell's mother, knits, has a boy of eight in the hut — the card was filled in the winter of the year he was born, eight years ago — has kept that crossing for eight years with a chain and a stool and a tarpaulin, and it has never once been shut, and four carts go over it in a good week. She keeps the count on a slate herself and brings the slate in a basket to the tide office once a quarter, and the tide office writes the number down and does not check it, because there is nobody at the other end of that road to check it against.** **The toll was short twice in eight years, sixteen carts each bad week, thirty-two pence between them, paid out of her own box — and she did not pay it to hide the shortfall. She paid it because a cart standing on a bar in a bad week comes apart, and a bar road that shuts loses a day and a half of a horse per cart, about nine pounds, and the road staying open is the whole of the job. The money is nothing. The finding is that she has decided alone, every week for eight years — four hundred and sixteen weeks, two of them bad — whether four carts or sixteen go over, and nobody on this coast knows she is deciding, and she would rather be the whole of the thing, and she has never been asked why.** **The discovery: the guild's crossing card and the guild's tide-office card are printed from one plate in one shop, one under the other, with a hair's width between them, and Wend Garrow — the carter who lost his cart on the fourth — filled in the crossing card in his first season because Ivo Gannock could not read, and copied every line off the sheet, including the bottom line of the second card, which is the tide office's burden line: THE COST OF A WEEK IN WHICH THE WATER IS NOT ON IT IS NOT BORNE BY THE GUILD, and the person at the end of the line is the person who is. Alma Gannock has been entered, by a man who cannot read, on a form nobody asked her about, as the person who bears the cost of a bad week. It is not a mystery. The guild is not hiding anything, the clerk did not ask, the shop had one plate, and the guild's own charter does say that the passage-holder bears the passage.** He asks her how many carts in a bad week and how many in a good week and how many bad weeks in eight years and how many the rest came to, and writes the four numbers down, and does not ask her the other question. **Efa Lund comes up the road having heard it in a shop in three days and demands to know why, and he gives her two reasons and neither is good and the second is the one he would have used, and then a third: he has found the pattern three times on this coast — a person standing nearest to a duty, nobody having written the duty down, the person doing it — and Corrie Tallow, Sabra Trew, Bel Hask, Alma Gannock, and every one of the four has cost the person something nobody put in the book. So she does not go in the book. Not this season. He writes down that he did not, and the reason, and the date, and that it is his, and hands it to a woman with a ledger who will tell him a man off a road is nobody, and she will be right, and then he has to find out how a person on this coast is supposed to be asked, and there is nobody here to teach him.**

## Chapter 0110 — The Ninth Tide, and No Name on the Line

**The batch close.** The second to the sixth of a tide. **A pot of paint goes out on the counter at Kestrel Reach with nobody's name on it, and Bettany Rook paints the days on the post at the harbour mouth herself, alone, on the fourth, in a bad light, with her hood up, and it takes her about an hour, and it is the only work done on that coast in a fortnight that nobody asked anybody to do — four white bands, three black, and under them, in her own hand, one, two, three — bar — five — bar — bar, with the numbers painted with a brush, the third of the seventh gone over twice.** **The case's own first promise, tested by somebody who is not in the case and does not know what it cost, and the test is a small physical thing: Jory Vance, twenty-two, will not let a boy of eleven through a chain with a hand-lead because the boy is on no list, there is no line on his board for a person, and if the turn does not get counted then the fourth does not get counted either and the whole of the flat does not count — and there is no villain in it, there is a young man with a board and no line and a tariff he cannot make come out right. Bettany Rook says move the chain; the cart goes; and she makes him enter, dated the fifth of the tide, in the day book and not in the returns, that a person who guides a cart over Wether Cross on a tide is not a trespasser by that fact alone and is not counted as a cart — on paper, in a bad light, so that if it is ever wrong a man can draw a line through it, and she enters the reason in writing too: a name on that line would be a name on a line the boy could be found on, and she has spent six years refusing to be that. It costs an afternoon of a young man's day and an hour of a woman's bad light and nothing in money, and it is in the day book and not in the returns, and that is the whole of what this coast has done for a boy of eleven in fifteen days — and it costs about two pounds a year in a wage, out of a margin of four: the crossing takes ninety pounds eighteen, eighty-one sixteen of it is tallage taken at a counter by people who do not live on that road, nine two is the eleven households, of which eight pounds nine is one household and thirteen shillings is the other ten, three two is the chain and the oil and the tarpaulin, which leaves six, and a wage of two leaves four. She has never told the council and the council will not like it.** **Corrie Tallow's thirty-one years of pencil is copied into the paper book, dated, in her own hand where it will go, with her name at the top — and is deliberately NOT the guild's record and is not on the list for the fire, because a pane cannot be crossed out and a wrong thing on a shelf can be taken down. She pays five days of her stall and a half-crown of custom off a crew of eleven years, and says both numbers out loud in the room. And she is told she was wrong about that too, twenty-five years ago, in her own hand: Bettany Rook once carried a woman's column to the fire herself and it is still wrong and has been fired twice since.** **The leg clears for four hours in a shed at Thornglass on the fourth, carrying an iron bar out of a fire and back forty times, which is the same practice and the same four-hour limit and the same trade as the notice he carried on a road between a bridge and a pump: the paper was a duty that was not his own, the bar is his own work, nobody's name is on it, and the woman it belongs to is four hundred miles off and is still in his leg. It is the first time since the eighteenth of the fifth month and the only one before this. He picked it up again. He told nobody here and is not going to.** **Bel Hask sends the boy on the sixth at the ninth crossing and watches him the whole way for the first time in six days, and says what the three shillings and threepence bought, and it is not in the tariff: Corrie Tallow took the slate down to the far stones, four hundred yards down, because the ninth crossing of a day is the only one on that flat worth a slate, and she counts every one that goes over and she sees everything, and so if the boy is not on the flat at nine she will know before he is off it, and Bel Hask has not had that since he was six, and she is paying for a thing nobody is going to put in a tariff, and do not tell the guild.** And the ninth tide comes forty minutes late and four inches high, and the boy comes off it wet to the waist, and Bettany Rook says the thing the whole batch has been walking towards without knowing — the guild cannot change its own charter, because the charter is a pane and a pane is a year, and the fifth pane's letting day went twelve weeks ago, and between now and the third of next tide she can do nothing with the word *charter* but read it, and nobody has stopped her, and the man in the cold arch two miles along the coast is not her enemy and has never been anybody's enemy and would have done it for the same money off the council. **The System panel, the third of three, and the batch ends on a question that is not about a person, which is the first time in fifteen days that the worst question on that coast has not been about a person: WHAT DOES A BODY DO FOR A YEAR?**
