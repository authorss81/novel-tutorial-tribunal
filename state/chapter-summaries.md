# Chapter Summaries

## Chapter 0001 — The Route That Remembered

Ilyan finds that *The Sovereign’s Wake* erases the cause of a refusal while marking the route complete. He files a precise, unmerged defect report despite the cancellation and a project lead who calls the problem noise. A signed sequence activates a separate one-use null passage without consent. Ilyan lands beneath Orison’s ninth bridge with a spent blackglass token as its reserve and existing drift worsen; Tamsin’s hand appears under the failing span.

## Chapter 0002 — The Current Beneath the Bridge

Ilyan cannot understand ambient Veyran speech, but the formal case notice names him First Defendant and offers three costless-looking remedies. Tarin interprets the human warning; Oren explains the bridge’s count and load marks. Ilyan refuses the abstract choice, identifies the child and her need for a safe exit, and reaches the anchor plate as the span folds toward Tamsin.

## Chapter 0003 — The Brace That Could Be Repeated

Ilyan asks Oren and Tarin for a controlled rescue instead of pretending he can order the bridge. A clamp, cord, and drive pin create a repeatable pocket; Tamsin warns him not to pull and chooses to hear the bridge rather than be promised an uncertain rescue. Neris arrives, names the household’s access and work needs, refuses public use of the private flood account, and the court notice demands five challenge fields before the next bell, which transfers the filing into the midday session.

## Chapter 0004 — The Form of a Challenge

In the Chamber of Passage, Sera Quill accepts provisional representation for the record rather than Ilyan’s innocence. Harrow distinguishes review, appeal, and exit, and accepts Ilyan’s challenge filing without granting an access exception. Ilyan volunteers for a bounded public witness duty that ends with the first field; his report and identity become public. Oren produces a saltglass workroom plate whose maintenance-review language differs from the current charter.

## Chapter 0005 — The Useful Wording

Oren obtains a one-bell maintenance exception for his named crew and one load path. Ilyan turns the inspection into a repeatable sequence and sees the old review condition, but an eastern petition copies its language into a dangerous claim about unlisted refusal. At the deadline, Ilyan files Neris’s private flood account without asking. The Bench preserves the copy, Sera halts the access request and suspends representation, and the household must decide what can be offered after the breach.

## Chapter 0006 — The Name of the Bell

Ilyan explains the preserved copy, the withdrawal mark, and the limits of a court’s ability to restore privacy. Neris says the old strict register once felt protective during an earlier flood before a local petition made it exclusionary. Tamsin chooses to offer only that the warning bell rang before the collapse; Neris refuses the flood history. Sera agrees to advocate the household’s limited request while Ilyan’s representation remains suspended, and the one-bell night field forms.

## Chapter 0007 — The Field Around the Bridge

The first night field tests the current charter, the maintenance record, and the bridge’s behavior. Harrow uses the Bench’s technical term “playable,” while a continuity line appears beneath the formal notice. Ilyan and Tarin establish a neutral perimeter and keep the household at the boundary. Harrow physically replaces the old working plate with the current certified duplicate; the original is sealed, the field destabilizes, and the bridge survives on its brace with the missing language absent from the active record.

## Chapter 0008 — The Right to Say No

Sera confronts Ilyan in her room and makes him record the breach, apologize without asking forgiveness, and accept that his future claims will be public. The court copy is marked disputed, the people named in the account are not treated as consenting witnesses, and Neris retains the original. Sera identifies the surviving phrase “the governed may answer no,” finds her private duplicate of the Quiet Chamber page missing after the night field, and resumes only limited provisional representation of Ilyan after the correction.

## Chapter 0009 — The Record That Walked

The guild posts a safety order, the eastern district invokes its mounted petition, and the crowd arrives. Sera translates the household’s choices and calls Neris and Tamsin without making them. When the clerk physically couples the petition to the bridge record, competing registered and unlisted conditions move through the span. Ilyan proposes one public review with separate burdens and refuses to treat copied wording as household consent. The preliminary hearing opens with amendment, destruction, and delay before the Bench.

## Chapter 0010 — The Stay

Tamsin offers only the bell fact, Neris offers register/work/housing facts and refuses the flood account, and Oren offers the bridge’s physical sequence without accepting unlimited guild liability. Ilyan names a finite public burden attached to his identity and report through the review. The Bench grants limited provisional standing and a one-cycle stay with a public seventh-morning-bell review; the current charter remains untouched. Tamsin and Neris cross together and the repaired load holds. Sera files a licensing recommendation, then distinguishes the original Quiet Chamber page’s pre-arrival removal from the later disappearance of her duplicate and takes Ilyan to the archive.

## Chapter 0011 — The Word That Traveled

A Merefen courier brings an extract that detaches the ninth-bridge stay from its named bridge, crew, burden, and review date. Ilyan and Sera discover that a local clerk may lawfully reuse public wording as a precedent witness without asking the original parties. Ilyan chooses a bounded review rather than a freeze that could close the road and water route, accepting that his name will travel. Neris and Tamsin keep the Ro household’s safety, work, housing, and consent boundaries visible. The extract’s added sentence reads, “the unlisted ward may refuse at the cost of the shared road.” The ward is known only as the unlisted ward; the name Cinder Ward does not appear until Chapter 12.

## Chapter 0012 — The Road That Borrowed the Bridge

Ilyan observes the Saltwheel Road’s carts, water route, load marks, and levy. A loaded cart exposes a hollow seam and cracks the crossing-side marker; Joren says the course has not gone through and that one heavier wheel will finish it. The breach itself is saved for Chapter 14. The Cinder Ward is divided: some residents need the road, some cannot pay the levy, and many have no recognized address for notice. Joren Vell identifies the physical failure and refuses to sign a clean inspection without ward representation. He offers the road as a witness but rejects Ilyan’s plan, turning the local dispute toward the Bench.

## Chapter 0013 — A Name in the Margin

At a record shop, Ilyan and Sera find that the Merefen petition combines older road clauses and the copied ninth-bridge wording. It was entered as a precedent witness, not a ruling, but a marshal has already used it to justify a maintenance levy. Sera refuses to hide the acceptance ledger from the ward. The Bench grants preservation of the ledger and refuses the hold on enforcement, so the batch’s first filing returns worse than it went in: the record is protected and the road is not. The ledger’s final witness entry is removed, leaving a Kest fragment and a clipped continuity-cadence replacement.

## Chapter 0014 — Tarin’s Boundary

Tarin imposes a neutral route and public witness list after a containment order. Ilyan documents the inspection rather than directing the crowd. A loaded cart breaks the crossing-side marker and shifts the water route. Beneath the district’s newer plate, the marker carries private wording closely matching Ilyan’s Earth report: “Completion does not erase the cause of refusal.” Tarin secures the physical evidence and warns Ilyan not to request a broad emergency.

## Chapter 0015 — A Good Exception, a Bad Road

At the Cinder Ward, Mava Rusk refuses a maintenance acknowledgment and loses water and shelter priority. The district invokes Ilyan’s stay as settled precedent. Ilyan states that the stay was bounded by one bridge, a named crew, a household, and a review date; the copied petition has removed those conditions. The refusal triggers a road-wide closure, but ward residents use a slower route to deliver clinic herbs. A petition-box fragment survives with the words, “A burden may be refused when its effect is made visible,” while its lower half is cleanly cut away.

## Chapter 0016 — Recovery Work

Oren, Joren, the ward workers, and the district repair the Saltwheel Road without pretending bridge knowledge transfers automatically. The local keeper and residents name the work, hours, materials, warning line, water route, review, and exit. Ilyan documents the sequence only. The road opens under a finite local arrangement, but the district refuses to publish the ward’s refusal. A proposed renewal instruction arrives before the first interval ends; it has no end date and will become active only when entered.

## Chapter 0017 — The Assembly Refuses a Clean Answer

The Merefen assembly splits between road users, ward residents, and districts afraid that one unlisted ward will become a universal precedent. Ilyan refuses to offer a universal declaration and supports a visible, specific refusal. He submits his public Earth sequence for neutral comparison without accepting authorship. The comparison sets three dates side by side and finds that the local petition was built from the public wording before the Quiet Chamber margin was rewritten. The copier and the person who tidied the record afterward are not the same person. Maren Kett’s written mandate is read into the hall before he argues.

## Chapter 0018 — The Family Appeal

Sera keeps her family’s withdrawal claim separate from the road case and accepts Ilyan only as a record-checker. The original page’s removal predates Ilyan’s crossing; her duplicate vanished during or after the first field. A formal continuity request has a valid seal but no named author, a C—2 code, and a missing witness index. Ilyan admits he cannot infer guilt from the seal and agrees to trace the ordinary chain rather than hunt a villain.

## Chapter 0019 — The Copy Reaches the Road

A district clerk from Merefen enters the automatic-renewal order into the active petition book at a witnessed hour, before anyone can stop it, and the entered record treats the ward’s work as consent and threatens a pre-dawn closure. Ilyan and Sera frame a narrow challenge, while Joren, Dalla, and Mava each choose limited testimony. Tarin keeps the line neutral and refuses force. The Bench pauses automatic renewal, seals rather than erases the copied petition, and keeps the local route available for a short interval. A second paper using Ilyan’s private Earth sequence enters the court slot.

## Chapter 0020 — The Precedent Has a Body

The district offers to reopen the road if Mava signs, but she refuses the acknowledgment and accepts only the named work and public review. The ward’s refusal and labor are entered in the common register. At the morning bell, the affected people choose a fresh one-interval continuation, so the route remains open without becoming permanent precedent. Ilyan claims no authorship or general authority. Sera links the second paper to the C—2 continuity request and finds that the old bridge plate bears an S. Kest fragment; the paper’s receipt predates Ilyan’s arrival, leaving the question of how his private words entered Veyran law.

## Chapter 0021 — The Guild Counts the Work

Oren counts the guild's work in hours and posts a notice naming the ninth-bridge work, the hands, the wage, and Ilyan as attached record with no maintenance labor assigned. Maren Kett arrives with a written mandate and argues that the stay has become a regional obligation, and that the region cannot pay for an inspection nobody requires. A new inspection exposes the missing term with teeth: under the current charter a keeper may summon a pause but may not release the span, so Oren can ring a bell that means nothing. The notice's accurate last line is read by the district as proof that nobody carries the maintenance, and the absence becomes the day's danger.

## Chapter 0022 — A House at the Edge

The household's shelter, room, work hour, and crossing hours are separated by Ilyan and Sera into four records with four recipients, which Meridith Vale immediately explains means four doors where there was one locked gate. The district proposes a three-day materials store on the ground that a provisional tenancy has no tenancy to protect. Tamsin is invited to repeat the bell for the assembly and chooses to say it once in the workroom, where the span is visible while she talks about it, because otherwise people will say the bell was working for her and not for the keeper who rings it. Neris offers route facts only. A work record shows the Ro household's crossing credited as a half-hand of maintenance labor every morning since the night of the stay, matched by a district form number issued to more than one office.

## Chapter 0023 — The Name on the Load

Meridith Vale enters Ilyan's name in the authority row of the public work slate under a four-year-old standing instruction, and Oren refuses to let the guild work under another body's authority. A loaded cart reads the board, changes its line, takes the eastern turn fast, and breaks the approach apron; the name is not a force on the stone but a force on the people who read it before they reach it. A guild apprentice refuses to read the scale aloud while that name is on the board, and Oren takes the book and signs beside him. Sera compares the slate's hand with the composite petition's hand and finds they are not the same. The slate's back shows a case copy routed through the plate office that no instruction authorizes.

## Chapter 0024 — The Assembly's Limited Word

The Lower Ward Assembly can answer one question: may the attached name be cited as authority for local maintenance work? The district invokes the Bench's own refusal to hold enforcement in the Saltwheel matter and declines to hold the materials store. Maren Kett argues for one legible rule with no roof, and Sera says a rule that tells a clerk who pays does not tell a person she may say no. Ilyan reaches for a general rule about acknowledgment inferred from use and Sera stops him in the middle aisle in front of the hall, because his standing is one bridge and he has no facts about four districts. The assembly's limited word makes his name citable as a record, uncitable as a permit, and orders him to give a public account of his first bridge testimony in three days.

## Chapter 0025 — Ilyan's First Public Testimony

Neris and Tamsin do not come and send no message. Ilyan answers for what he accepted, what he did not accept, and where his plan failed, including filing Neris's private flood account without asking. Asked to affirm that the stay authorized local adoption, he refuses, and then confesses to the limit he never asked to have removed: every term in the stay he accepted was a limit on the court, and nothing in it stopped a clerk from reusing its shape, and he was relieved not to have to ask for a charter amendment. A question about the pre-arrival paper forces him to produce his Earth reproduction sequence again, permanently and publicly. The lowland conservancy withdraws its citation. Afterward Sera finds that the district's working schedule book has a page cut from the binding gutter and a replaced column that was cut from a printed form rather than written by hand.

## Chapter 0026 — Sera Draws a Boundary

Ilyan wants to file to reopen the bridge record on the C—2 evidence. Sera refuses, because a reopening suspends every relief entered under the record, including the stay, and the stay is the only lawful crossing Tamsin has. She refuses to be the lever her family's missing page becomes, accepts Ilyan only as a record-checker, and gives him one task: verify ordinary custody, not a villain. They trace the printed form to a public print run, a standing print authority, and a routine maintenance update in a strongbox in a rented room — nine ordinary steps. The alteration's routing carries an incomplete code, its second element cut away.

## Chapter 0027 — The Witness Who Refuses the Schedule

Ilsa Bray refuses to sign a forward schedule reading *until reviewed and thereafter* with a wage line that has no hours, and offers the physical defect instead: on the third run the west joint comes back to its mark, walks two fingers out, and comes back. Tarin refuses to let a worker's refusal become a switch, and Ilyan files the smallest request of the batch — one span, one joint, named hands, paid, ending at the seventh morning bell. Oren records the offer and the refusal in the guild's own book. Ilsa reveals that the sealed plate's cut edge is a load-book edge, that a crew kept two witnesses, and that a woman paid off the book when the crew list was cut to one name said *a book without a second hand is only a record.* She does not claim that woman was her grandmother, and she asks the room to stop her deciding whether the levy ledger's cut name and the plate's cut are one hand.

## Chapter 0028 — The Two Books

Nine slots with no slack in them: four guild hands, three road-party hands of whom two cannot leave the road, and two ward hands, one of whom holds the whistle. Ilyan writes a second rule he has never said to anybody, does not file it, and does not destroy it, after Oren shows him that a rule making refusal free also makes refusal lawful on the worst day in the worst weather. The work is done: apron, joint course, brace, count, salt crack. The district's new two-book ledger puts paid work in one book and refusals in the other, and Vale enters the household's refusal under a position heading until Tarin points at the fourth column. Sera returns with the print shop's answer: the authorization for run 114 was cut out of the stock ledger and replaced by one signed by a preservation authority rather than a person. The form's print strip shows the run was distributed to a plate office six days before the ninth-bridge public notice.

## Chapter 0029 — The Review That Cannot Convene

The local-institution review cannot convene because the ward has no recognized address, so the ward seats are empty, and Ilyan refuses to fill them by treating the ward as one claimant. Tarin offers a neutral boundary without an order, and the sitting becomes a bounded evidence session with no findings and no order. Sera refuses in public to let the pre-arrival paper be submitted; Ilyan enters the routing as a fact instead. The quorum clerk's shelf index reveals that the bridge's maintenance-review condition was rerouted to the C—2 continuity desk on the day the working plate was executed, and that the desk has no named clerk and no incoming index.

## Chapter 0030 — The Road Beneath the Charter

The district offers the household four concessions for one maintenance signature; Neris refuses, because the charter has no exit and a signature would bind her to a rule she could never leave. The same acknowledgment is served on the guild's crew, assigning the burden to their successors until the charter is amended, and Oren refuses it on the guild's own ground and without Ilyan's record: the guild will not carry a term it cannot use. Ilyan files one request, to record the local arrangement as found and to state that the charter is not amended; appeal, enforceable exit, and durable housing and work protection are named as unresolved. Tamsin asks for one line in the file that goes to the review: *I am not a reason for the bridge.* A comparison of the sealed plate against the older witness index shows one cut and not three — same hand, tool, sitting and week, with the salt gone white in both — and the pre-arrival paper's receipt falls inside the C—2 index's gap.

## Chapter 0031 — The Review That Opens With a Number

The seventh morning bell rings at noon and the review opens on a measurement, not an argument: the court record hand Roke enters the joint's deviation and the deck line's permanent set as two different numbers because Ilsa says they are two different numbers. The bench levy is spent as of Thursday. The court attempts to close the matter under the emergency continuity rule, consolidating it into a docket with no date on the ground that the maintenance-review condition's routing is under question and a consolidated matter cannot receive new local evidence, which would have lapsed the stay at noon by the court's own procedural convenience. The court then continues the relief on its own ground — a relief does not lapse because the court continued the matter — on the identical principle the district has been using against the household, sides turned round, and the district objects and is right. Ilyan's smallest request is a pause route: one named keeper, one named inspection, a stated number of bells, no charter, no rule, no precedent, no reopening. He admits in public that he has no good answer to who pays for a stopped span, and the district immediately names the carts as the affected party, which is correct and which removes the ward and the household from the column. The review records the two-finger drift as a measured condition and orders the guild to bring a costed pause proposal to a full hearing that is not scheduled, which only an attached record-holder with standing may ask for. Nobody can refute the district's objection that asking for a scheduled hearing is a reopening, because the difference has never been tested in a public room. Bram Orren, a bell-runner, rings the change and asks for the figures for his line book and is refused.

## Chapter 0032 — The Work the Levy Does Not Pay

The guild's maintenance fund is empty and the board says so. Oren refuses to let Ilyan under the span and Ilsa refuses a stranger near the scale, so Ilyan documents instead of examining. The four candidates are the joint, the apron, the water, and the load order; the apron and the order are eliminated on the day. Bram Orren, a seventeen-year-old bell-runner from the Briar March, brings an oiled-cloth map drawn from fourteen local children and a footman at the salt wharf showing the channel moved west and the western abutment stands in its bed, and states his own want — a closed span breaks four standing arrangements on his line for people who cannot afford a court. Puck goes under the seat with a lamp and the guild finds the cause: the west seat is bedded on a course the water is washing the fines out of, so the joint is not wearing but emptying, and it borrows two fingers on load and gets them back. The fix is mundane, expensive, and needs five things the district and the guild do not have. The guild's own account shows that a crew of six at every change would have caught it four days ago, because the under-deck watch has been one hand since the year the crew list went to one name, and two hands went when the second witness went. Oren writes *a book without a second hand is only a record* on the board under everything.

## Chapter 0033 — An Institution Wins Something

In the district's rented office off the weighbridge, Oren refuses the maintenance acknowledgment and asks for nothing but the guild's own wage: two loads of crushed stone, four sacks of lime, fiber delivered to the line, and one load-held day with six hands at the levy rate, argued entirely from the guild's own board and the guild's own six-year failure to find an emptied seat. The district's position is coherent and not corrupt — a span that cannot be stopped is cheaper to maintain than one that can — and Dain says plainly that a district cannot fund a power it has neither created nor voted for. Vale supplies the office's own four-word limit: *the office has no power here.* The district concedes because declining would require its clerk to certify in writing that a span with an emptied seat is fit for unlimited load, and Vale enters that true reason on the back of the slip in his own hand. Ilyan contributes nothing and is told so; Dain tells him the object of his eleven-day objection won an argument in a room where nobody needed him. The district's price is a regional maintenance conference, convened overnight at the old weighhouse, on an agenda printed before anyone had said the word *day* out loud.

## Chapter 0034 — The Room Where the Clause Might Be Written

At the fen crossing, forty-one delegates want one sentence, and Ottla Prenn writes eleven lines that actually contain a keeper's pause. The common rule cannot be adopted because two districts cannot produce their maintenance terms, and the conference's own custody index shows both held at a code with no office written against it — the C—2 code — which is procedural failure, not moral failure, and which nobody expected. Ilyan, barred from being cited, is useful as a documentarian, warns the room that an accurate sentence gets used for whatever the reader has, and is ignored. He is then offered the only job in the room that consists of being the name on everything — keeper of a regional witness list, safe because his name cannot be cited — and refuses, correctly, on two grounds: a post with no door is a charter with no door, and a witness list must be carried by something boring, local, paid, and impossible to promote. The guild refuses it from the other side. Draft eleven circulates unadopted with the witness question open, and Oren learns that his clause is a local detail beside a region that cannot find two of its own terms.

## Chapter 0035 — The Ro Household Under the Copy

Bevin Larch delivers a notice in the lane: the Ro household is entered in the ward's common register as a participating party, pending verification, which means the district may consult it and notify it of load-pattern changes without asking. The register's own draft carried the household in because an attached record's public account was entered as a district docket — an institution responding to a record, not to a person — and the district's complaint that four records are four doors has become a gate. Tamsin asks what participation gets her (notice) and what it costs (her mother's consent to be asked, which the district no longer needs) and then asks what she must do to get out, and learns there is an objection column and no column for leaving. Ilyan asks the Bench to hold the entry; the district cites the Saltwheel distinction that Ilyan himself established and wins, and he says out loud in the lane that he taught it. Hanne Kolm reads the register draft aloud and delivers the chapter's line: a book with seven columns and no eighth is a book where people may say no and not go. Larch enters the household's objection in his own hand and appends, on his own authority, that the register records no consent and has no column for it.

## Chapter 0036 — What the Household Will Not Say

Tamsin says, unprompted, what the bell *is* rather than what it meant: a rope over a worn wheel on a pin and a hammer in a bell, fixed to the post and to nothing on the span, rung by a person. It rang, the people on the span got off, they got off, and the span went on. Tarin refuses, publicly and by name, to let the account be taken as a witness to Ilyan's theory, and Ilyan does not reach for his book. Neris refuses to send her, and gives no reason in public. Tamsin sets five terms, the fifth being that her account may not be used to cure or support any refusal, and chooses the guild's book over the court's because a book somebody wants cannot be said to have been made for her. Oren takes the account and the terms, Ilsa reads it back once and is corrected once, Neris witnesses without signing, and Oren records that the guild is not a neutral body. In the lane, Neris tells Ilyan the reason she will not explain in a room: an inspector sat on her bench on the eighth day and explained that if Tamsin said the bell was not answered, the household would not be refusing maintenance — the district was offering her daughter as a reason.

## Chapter 0037 — The Clerk Who Was Sent to Enter It

The print shop answers one question a day; Sera's is spent and Ilyan's has never been asked. Neral gets them into the cold index room behind the shop and identifies the mechanics of the cut authorization page: the shop does not re-cut stock, a bound volume is rebound by a binder, and the paste is cold, so the volume went back into service the same day. The answer is ordinary and damning: run 114 was authorized by a standing authority of preservation, which is a paper that keeps deciding the same thing while the emergency it was written for lasts and does not need a name because it was never meant to be argued with. Its index for the week the current working plate was executed shows eleven items taken into preservation, all routed C—2, two named in class: the ninth-bridge maintenance-review condition and a sealed continuity request. Neither carries a name. Sera declines to ask what is in the sealed item and the desk records the refusal. Ilyan's temptation is to build a villain out of an office; Vale names the price — records stop coming, to everybody — and redirects him to the wage. Vale also tells him his standing has lapsed at the bell. The day ends with an automatic district notice: the refused acknowledgment must be re-issued or the schedule lapses at dusk, and Oren tells Ilyan he does not write this next arrangement.

## Chapter 0038 — The Schedule Lapses

At dusk the district's schedule ends on its own term with no breach by anybody, entered in three short true lines by Vale, and the stone sits at a yard at the district's expense with no lawful reason to move it. Oren refuses Ilyan the pen and the bodies that will carry the work write the arrangement themselves: the guild's empty account, the road party, the pump's own take at the ward rate with Dalla Rusk refusing to speak for the ward and entering two days of shortage in public, and an eighth of a load-fee from nine named cart owners, with no household paying anything. It is better than the last two arrangements — every hour named, refusals still refusals — and worse in a named way: there is no district copy any more, and a carrier will not put a fen-road roof load on a road that ends on a date with nothing behind it, so the lower road's shelter loses its roof. Neris names the household that loses it first: Marda Rennick, room nine, and her son Besh, nine, who cannot be on a ladder. Bram's line book is the only record of what actually crossed. Larch enters the cause of the lapse in a public ward roll because a record that does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by, and Ilyan says the plain sentence that the lapse is the district's decision, and nobody argues.

## Chapter 0039 — The Continuity Voice in a Public Field

Harrow returns, reads the file standing in the cold, confirms the scheduling request is built out of four things and no archive, and then states that Ilyan's standing has lapsed, which means the court cannot take the request from him. The guild asks instead, in the name of a burden named in the record, on a slate Oren holds up; that is the batch's loss producing its win. Dain objects that a scheduling upon a consolidated matter is a reopening; Harrow opens the field on five disputed records plus the guild's working count book, at Tarin's insistence. The joint goes out three fingers and back, a figure nobody has seen, while the permanent set does not move. A fifth mark appears in the count book's margin — a count-book cut for a load that walked and did not come back — with nobody near the book. Tarin walks under the rope, picks it up with both hands, refuses Dain's attempt to secure it, and holds it until a court clerk's time and his own signature record what he is doing, because he once enforced an order on a village on the strength of a record he did not test. The Bench's ruling schedules the full hearing for the fourth morning bell and does not reopen the record, and carries beneath it a clipped instruction nobody requested: *continuity preserves the record before any remedy is stated.* Roke says it is not on his tray. Harrow refuses to rule on it, on the ground that a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter. Ilyan refuses to build on it in public because he cannot say who said it, and Sera refuses in the same breath to let the Quiet Chamber chain be used to trace it.

## Chapter 0040 — What the Field Left Standing

Ilyan's four load-bearing facts are read aloud in the workroom and Sera corrects two of the four and is right about exactly those two: the drift is a band (one and a half on a light load, up to three on a heavy one) and not a figure, and the schedule lapsed because a term ran out on an instrument nobody re-issued, not because the guild refused. Moot enters, in the open, that the attached record-holder has no remaining constituency: the conservancy withdrawn, the name uncitable, the household a party under objection, the ward seats unfilled, the standing lapsed, and one preserved attached observation that binds nobody. Ilyan delegates in daylight — Oren takes the physical witness and will describe rather than advocate, Ilsa takes the measurement and keeps the half, Joren takes the road and the load sequence, Tarin takes the boundary and will testify to what a field does not write, Bram's line book is named and refused before it can be used — and keeps only the record, saying that if a court finds a fifth thing he will not have been the one looking for it. Tamsin consents to a copy rather than the book on four terms of her own, including that the court must say out loud that the account went to the guild first and nobody made her; Neris reserves the right to stop everything herself if her daughter is put in a room. Sera names the one lever left in the case, the sealed item in the preservation index, and forbids it, because nobody knows what happens to an item a court has ruled on. The batch closes on a span with a chalked end date, a measured and funded but unfixed drift, an unexplained mark in a guild's book, a set hearing, and a C—2 chain one step deeper with no name in it.

## Chapter 0041 — The Hours the Arrangement Does Not Have

The last evening of the Chapter 38 arrangement. The last day of a week is a thin road: nine carts over, seven loaded, the four empties gone east and not coming back, so the load-fee is short and A. wants the frame to say so. Oren's crew works it on the guild's own empty account; Puck comes up out of the hatch with grey lamp glass and reports that there is no stone in the bed of the west course, only a skin of lime, and that he is not a measure. Neris dictates the exact sentence Ilyan must say to Marda Rennick about the roof, and forbids the word *carrier* in front of her son. Oren costs a pause for the first time — two bells for a load that can turn, six for one that must be reversed with a full approach behind it, eight as the ceiling, six hands, at the ward rate — and the sum is unaffordable, and he says the true reason: a clause nobody pays for is a bell nobody rings. ## Chapter 0049 — The Ruling

Harrow disposes of all three remedies out loud, refused ones first. Hold is refused because an interval is what a room gives itself when it does not have a ruling, and Joren's objection is preserved. ## Chapter 0051 — The Gate at the Low Water

Ilyan walks the four miles down to Halloway's low water on the twelfth day of the unloaded period and does not get into the room, because his name is on the paper and every person there would read the name and stop listening. **The seven days between the copy and the walk are on the page: the copy up the fen road on the fifth logged sideways into the ninth-bridge matter because no file had Halloway's name on it, read twice on the frame against the settlement with the same answer both times — lawful, made out of a public instrument, carrying the sentence that helps a person and not the one that would have cost the district something — and nothing he can do with it, because his own name is the reason; the court's attachment on the ninth; the carter who was turned back coming up off the fen road empty at the second bell on the tenth, unable to say which gate and unasked in a district room; and the choice of the twelfth over the fourteenth because on the twelfth he can stand in a lane and be nobody for a whole day.** On the bank he counts a standpipe queue of forty-one at the sixth bell and knows there will be nine at the ninth, and he reads a sheet that was printed nine days ago, nailed up with four nails and no glass, in which the fourth paragraph turns the ninth bridge's own two-hundred-year-old word *lawful traveler* into a reason to shut a gate, against a traveller book four inches thick and a hundred years out of date. **Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, a refuse hauler and the only person on her street who reads, reads the sheet out at the queue and reads the fourth paragraph twice, which she has never done in eleven years, and says the word in it is the bridge word and that it means a person in a book.** A ratepayer asks whether the sheet is his; Ilyan says it is an extract of a settlement he asked to be citable and did not send, and that nobody had been cruel to produce it. Bryn Sallow, forty-four, gate hand for thirty years, says the sheet is not wrong — she is the one who is wrong, because she does not know a word on it and nobody has told her in thirty years which of two things is true, and asks for it to be written down, and it is entered in the district's day book in the clerk's own hand. Wenna asks the question nobody can answer: she is not on the book, she hauls two hundred loads a year out of the low water, and *am I lawful.* Corva Silt, thirty-one, the bench clerk, admits before anybody else that she printed the extract on the second day after the hearing under a lawful print authority and sent a copy to the man whose name is at the bottom of it. ## Chapter 0052 — The First Honest Use

On the thirteenth day the water is let at the second bell and the room empties itself, and a licensed recordwright arrives at the fifth bell with the ninth-bridge read-back in the guild's words and says in the first minute that she is not a witness for Ilyan and that Halloway has not asked. ## Chapter 0053 — The Form

The fourteenth day. At the fifth bell Ilyan is in the room on the strength of the Bench's order, and Corva Silt has Instrument H-9 on the table under her hand: five clauses, numbered, sealed, published at the fourth bell. Wenna Tace reads the first clause twice on her own initiative and will not be asked for it twice, because the word in it is the one they will all argue about, and then reads on. **Clause three is the best clause on the instrument and the clerk wrote it in the small hours because a notice that says who may open a gate and does not say it is the gate hand is a notice that says anybody may open a gate.** The turn is not a speech and not a clause: it is the cart coming back down the hill in front of eighty people with everything still on it. **Abel Kearn, thirty-five, a builder out of the upper street with a builder's apron and lime in the seam of it,** asks what Ilyan did about this and gets the true answer, that he asked in a corridor for the settlement to be citable, did not send it, and was not frightened, and that he cannot read a clause and has said so on the record, and that if anybody wants to make a rule about that they should make it about him and not about the paper — and lands the sharpest line in the room: *then you are no use in this room, and I am sorry, because I have a bad thing and I want somebody to make it good, and there is no somebody.* **He also names the whole shape of it, that there is one lawyer's clerk in this district, she is thirty-one, and she printed it.** Kett finds that Halloway has used the bridge's paper to say something true — its own hundred-and-sixty-year-old book calls the culvert road a penal road — and calls the template a *frame* rather than a monster, and says he does not know whether to be proud of the rule or sorry for the paper. ## Chapter 0054 — The Name on the Bottom of the Paper

The fifteenth day. Cael Rundle, fifty-eight, ratepayer of Weir Lane, moves that the ninth-bridge settlement be withdrawn from use in Halloway — not the sheet, the precedent — because it produced H-9, and says he is not asking for the water. Ilyan is produced as the record-holder and asked the fair question: does your own paper, which says a person not on a register is not a trespasser by that fact alone, make his gate unlawful. **It is the fairest question anyone has asked him since he crossed and he does not have the answer, and he says so standing up in front of sixty people in a room where he has no standing, no constituency, and no advocate, and gives the reasons in order — a span has a deck and a load path and a keeper, and a culvert gate has none of those things, and the difference is in the machinery of a legal instrument that nobody in the district has.** He puts the cost on the table before anybody finds it: the answer is four miles up the road, the sixth-bell queue is on the bank, the gate is worked by one woman who cannot sit down, and **there is no line in this district on which her hour can be paid, because the line that pays an hour like that is a term of the paper the ratepayer has just asked to withdraw.** Bryn Sallow prices it at an hour and Corva Silt enters it and finds there is no line to put it on, and writes in the district's own hand that the office has no line. The answer arrives at the ninth bell in a counter's hand off the bridge, in four lines, ending *I am not a lawyer and I count loads*: the access condition is about this span, a span has a deck and a load path and a keeper, a gate on a culvert has none of those things, and a district may make a list of its own. Cael does not withdraw his motion, and asks for something else instead: a line in this district's book on which an hour of work can be paid to a person by name, and a date. ## Chapter 0055 — One Legible Rule

The eighteenth day, and Orison is a place for the first time. The First Verdict is a block of black basalt the size of a house with no throne and no roof, and the scarce thing at it is a place on a docket: nineteen names on the board, eleven places, and the eleven are all bridge matters. A licensed recordwright and the court translator read the ninth-bridge conditions together all morning without once disagreeing about a word, which happens about four times a year and did not happen at all in the first month of the case. **Magistrate Ansa Rell, forty-four, eleven years in the office, calls the common-rule sitting, requires the burden to be stated before the opinion, borrows Harrow's procedure and says where she got it, and puts a man who spoke from a gallery back on the floor.** Convener Bel Ordway, who has a salt cart on the weighbridge and four minutes of the hour, says he wants one legible rule because he is tired of his own clerk. **Ansel Nyle, fifty-one, keeper of the low fen's one bridge and in the post since he was nineteen, produces his district's maintenance terms: they adopted the ninth-bridge settlement entire on the fifth day, by copying a public instrument out of a book, and his keeper has called the deck clear twice and both peat carts that could not turn were off the deck in nine minutes and both were paid out of road funds to the person on a line in chalk. The ward line does not go short on a thin week, because the ward line comes out of road funds and the toll does not.** Ordwin Sarn, of the cut banks, says the same and adds that his clerk cried about the water on the seventh day and that is normal and is the only part of this nobody warned her about. **The sitting fails on a category and not on a document: a rate may be paid only to a body with a name, the ward is not a body, and two delegates in the same room name the ward differently and are each right about somebody. ## Chapter 0058 — A Loss Taken on the Record

The twenty-first morning, and the leg is worse and the smell is in two layers in a lane with hay in it, and Sera makes him decide in advance what he will do when it happens. **The question is one sentence: whether the ninth-bridge settlement has any force in a district that printed or quoted it without adopting it.** Kett argues the true thing — a span has a deck and a keeper and a review date, and a review date cannot be a fact about four districts at once — and Harrow holds the argument and then says what his ruling costs before he rules: the low fen and the cut banks adopted the settlement by copying a public instrument, it worked, and if the settlement does not travel their keepers have no power, their pay lines are for hours no term requires, and neither man can produce his own terms. **Ilyan's request — a named declaration that the settlement binds no district that has not adopted it — is granted in about nine minutes, and he withdraws it on the record, in four sentences, and then gets a cart down the fen road with a folded sheet in a keeper's hand: *if it binds nowhere I have a copy of nothing and a cart that cannot turn. You do not owe me the power. ## Chapter 0061 — The Word in the Guild's Book

## Chapter 0064 — The Charter That Is Worse to Write

## Chapter 0065 — A Burden of His Own

The sixth to the eleventh, on the low water. ## Chapter 0067 — What the Appeal Was For

## Chapter 0069 — What It Cost by Morning
