**701 — NOTHING WHATEVER HAPPENS ON THAT BANK FOR A SECOND MORNING RUNNING ABOUT A QUESTION THAT WAS ASKED ON WEDNESDAY, AND A BOY OF THIRTEEN COMES DOWN A CART ROAD AND GIVES A COUNT OF A QUESTION AND AN ANSWER HEARD FROM OUTSIDE A DOOR FOUR MILES OFF.** The third day of the hundred and twenty-second week, a Thursday, his four hundred and fifty-first morning, the fever fifty-nine weeks and five days, one hundred and forty-seven days in the county of Kell, and the water about twelve inches over the top step, three days since it came off all ninety, with the next time the ninety steps can be walked given as the Tuesday of the week after this one. **711 — THE SECOND MORNING OF A THING WITH NO TERM ON IT, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS OUT LOUD THAT IT IS A PRACTICE AND NOT A SETTLEMENT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE ASKS TO BE SOMEBODY'S AND IS REFUSED IN SIX SENTENCES.** The sixth day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Sunday, his four hundred and fifty-seventh morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and one day, a hundred and fifty-seven days in the county of Kell, and the bank still full at about sixteen inches on the fifth morning. **713 — ONE COLUMN AND NOT TWO, A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT WORK OUT WHETHER TWO MEN IN TWO COATS ASKED HER ONE QUESTION OR TWO, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WHO WANTED AN OFFICE BECAUSE A BUILDING CAN BE REFUSED AND A MAN CAN STAND IN FRONT OF ONE.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and fifty-ninth morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and three days, and the bank still full on the seventh morning. **715 — A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR COMES UP A BANK TO ASK WHETHER HER NAME IS ON A SHEET OF PRINTED PAPER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE TELLS HER IT IS NOT BEFORE ANYBODY HAS READ IT, AND IS WRONG, AND SAYS HE HAD NOT LOOKED.** The third day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Thursday, his four hundred and sixty-first morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and five days, and the water about four inches over the top step, one day since it came off all ninety. **705 — A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT CHECKS FOR THE TENTH TIME A THING SHE IS NOT GOING TO SAY, SAYS IT IS THE LAST OF A FORTNIGHT AND THAT SHE HAS NOT BEGUN AGAIN, AND SAYS THAT A LAMP ON A HOOK AND A COLUMN WITH A NAME IN IT ARE NOT THE SAME SHAPE, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE OFFERS FOR THE FIFTH TIME TO BE THE INSTRUMENT OF A DIFFERENT KIND OF WORK AND IS REFUSED IN NINE SENTENCES.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-second week, a Monday, his four hundred and fifty-fifth morning, his one hundred and fifty-first day. **The man of forty-three gives about sixteen inches for the seventh day and gives a figure for a night for the first time in nine days — it came off in the night on the Saturday, four days after it was full on the Friday — and calls it a figure of a river and not of a man of forty-three. A woman of twenty-eight gives the tenth check, says the same sentence nine times and has not said it once, and says a lamp on a hook is a lamp on a hook and a column is a place a thing goes so that it can be found again, and that a shape is not a thing and she has been treating it as one for six weeks. Then a man of thirty-one makes his fifth offer and it is a different instrument: let me be the one who asks, up that road and along the other four, one question each, on their own step, writing down what they said. A man about fifty-two who cannot see well refuses him in nine sentences and gives the reason in the ninth: a woman of forty-four, a man of sixty-one, a man of forty-three and a boy of thirteen have each said a thing they had not planned to say, to him and not to the room, and not one of them has said he tricked them, so he is the best instrument for this in four counties and that is why nobody is going to let him do it. A man of thirty-one says the true of it and says he is not going to be thanked for saying he would have been good at it. A man of sixty-one refuses a total and gives every part of it; a woman of forty-four gives the second reason, which is that a number with a line under it is the last thing before a form. And a man of fifty-four who cannot read says he has been asked nothing this week and asks for that to be said on the last morning of a week.**

**706 — THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY STEPS IN THE NIGHT FOR THE FIFTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK, ABOUT NINE PEOPLE GO DOWN FOR THE FIRST TIME IN THIS VOLUME, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT SAYS THE LIGHT UNDER THE EIGHTH DOOR-STONE IS THE WIDTH OF HIS OWN HAND AND IT WAS THE WIDTH OF HIS OWN THUMB ELEVEN DAYS AGO, AND THERE IS A CHAIR IN THAT ROOM AND NOBODY HAS EVER BEEN IN IT.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and fifty-sixth morning, his one hundred and fifty-second day. **The man of forty-three gives off all ninety at the sixth hour and says four days after full is the working. A man of fifty-four puts his own hand under the ninth door-stone and says an inch and about the thickness of his own finger, same as Thursday and same as Sunday, and that nobody has asked him about it. Then a man of thirty-eight goes along the wall to the eighth opening and stops, and says the light is the width of his own hand at the knuckles and was about a thumb's width eleven days ago, and that a door shut since before any of them came here does not come open a thumb's width in four hundred years because of anything anybody did this fortnight. Three people give three reasons for not going in and none of the three is about the light, and the third is given by a man of fifty-four who cannot read: nobody has ever asked me whether I would be one of the two hands in that room and I am not going to find out by being in it. Then the lamp goes flat on the floor and they look through the gap, and there is a chair in that room, about as high as a man of forty-one's knee, with a back on it, and not cut in the bank, and there is not one stick of wood in a hundred and thirty feet of passage. A man of thirty-eight says the only thing he knows in three words: somebody has sat in it. A woman of forty-four says a form does not make a chair, a person makes a chair, and a person is in the room, and that is the difference and there is no figure of it. A man of thirty-one says he wants to go in, out loud, so that four people can hear him say it and not do it, and says none of the three reasons was about him.**

**707 — AT ABOUT THE FIFTH HOUR A MAN WHO HAS BEEN THE NINTH OF NINE FOR NINETEEN YEARS SAYS HE WANTS TO STOP IN A FORTNIGHT AND WANTS THE DAY OF IT WRITTEN WHERE ABOUT NINE PEOPLE CAN HEAR IT, A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS A NAME WITH NO DAY AGAINST IT IS A NAME NOBODY CAN BE SENT FOR ABOUT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS HE CAN GET A COLUMN FOR IT AND IS WRONG IN SIX SENTENCES.** The second day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and fifty-seventh morning, his one hundred and fifty-third day. **The man of forty-three gives about four inches, one day, and says he got up at the second hour because a man of forty-one told him to and did not say why and he did not ask. Then about nine people go down at the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light, and stand in the ninth room a quarter of an hour before it with the lamp burning and the shelf empty. A man of forty-one says the whole of it in one go: nineteen years, twice a day except about four days he knows to the day, what the ninth of nine means, that he has never been told what the other eight are and has not asked, and that he wants to stop, not today, in a fortnight, with a day on it. He says he has been putting that want in the shape of a person for six weeks instead of the shape of a line and worked it out four days ago and told nobody. A man of thirty-one says there is a column for it before anybody has finished being quiet, and a man of sixty-one takes it off him in six sentences: the sheet was printed four hundred miles off by somebody who has never been here, there is no form here that says a person may add a column to it and no form that says they may not, both have been true since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week, and the sixth sentence is that a man of thirty-one has never once been standing in a county where there is not a column for it. A man of thirty-eight says a column is a question asked the same way every time so that the answer can be found again, and that a man of forty-one asking for one is asking somebody who has never heard the question to write down that he asked it. A woman of forty says a name with a day on it can be sent for about and a name with no day cannot. A woman of forty-four says a form asks the same question every time and does not know who it asked, which is the good news, and that all nine of them will be asked it again on the same day of the week in about nine weeks, which is the other. Then the keeping is done, and it takes about a minute, and nobody is thanked.**

**708 — SOMETHING THAT IS NOT A PERSON SAYS A WOMAN'S NAME OUT LOUD ON A BANK AT ABOUT THE NINTH HOUR AND ASKS HER ONE QUESTION ABOUT A DOOR AND SHE ANSWERS YES, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR WHO HAS BEEN OUT OF EVERY BOOK IN THIS COUNTY FOR NINETEEN YEARS SAYS THE NAME HERSELF BECAUSE NOBODY ELSE HAS SAID IT SINCE A TUESDAY.** The third day of the hundred and twenty-three week, a Thursday, his four hundred and fifty-eighth morning, his one hundred and fifty-fourth day. **The man of forty-three gives about eight inches, two days, and a man of sixty-one gives seven mornings — seven is seven hundred and eight less seven hundred and one — of nothing whatever from a thing that is not a person, and calls it a figure of mornings and not of a thing, and refuses to say what it was doing on the Wednesday of last week because he was not there. Then, at about the ninth hour, a voice out of the middle of the bank says Ada Renk, and asks her one question — will you come at first light and put your two hands on the stone in the ninth room and push it to the far end of the room and push it back — and does not ask the man of thirty-one anything at all. She answers yes without stopping and says afterwards that she has no figure for how long she thought. Then nothing whatever happens. She says three things in order and wants them said before she is asked about the asking: I said yes; nobody can be sent for about the asking or the answer; and she is not going to be asked about the asking today, in nine days, or in four years. Then she gives the true of it with the working inside: nineteen years of saying in about nine rooms that the way to find her is to come to her door, and it took it about nine weeks and about nine doors and cost it nothing, and it came to a bank at the ninth hour where nine people were standing. Then she says the name herself and gives the reason, and it is the fifth time anybody has said it in that county. A man of thirty-one starts a question and stops and says he stopped on purpose, and a woman of twenty-eight says he has the half of it with a name on it and not the half that says why, and she has not got that either and will not give it out of a favour. A man of fifty-four who cannot read says that is the sixth time since the first day of the hundred and nineteenth week that somebody has got close to a list and stopped, and asks for it to be said first by him.**

**709 — A BOY OF THIRTEEN SAYS THAT A WOMAN WHO IS IN NO BOOK IN THIS COUNTY IS ON THE FIFTH LINE OF A PRINTED SHEET WITH A DAY AGAINST HER THAT IS THE WEDNESDAY A THING ASKED A MAN WHETHER HE WOULD WRITE IT, AND SHE SAYS ONE WORD AND IT IS THE SECOND ONE IN EIGHTY DAYS.** The fourth day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Friday, his four hundred and fifty-ninth morning, his one hundred and fifty-fifth day. **The man of forty-three gives about twelve inches, three days, and gives the two counts he does not add: a man of thirty-four in an ash for seventy-nine days and a woman of twenty-nine in a reed for eighty. Then nine or ten people go down the water and along the edge of the drain to the reed, and a boy of thirteen says he has read all eleven names four times since Sunday and that the fifth line is Nell Prince and she has got a day against her, and the day is the second day of the hundred and twenty-second week, and nine is seven hundred and nine less seven hundred. A man of thirty-one says the true of one half of that in four sentences and refuses the other half out loud first, so that four people can tell in nine days that he refused it and did not ask it. A man of forty-three says he is not going to stand in a field and say one word out of a reed is a refusal and is also not going to say it is not one. A man of fifty-four says he will not stand twenty feet off that reed and look at a woman and be the last person to hear her, and that a man of fifty-four who cannot read is the worst person in this county to be put in front of a reed. Then a man of thirty-eight says the mark on the sheet is a square about the size of a thumb and the space cut between two hands over the ninth opening is about as wide as a thumb and square and cut to the depth of the hands and old and empty, and that they are two marks of the same size and the same shape and he is not going to say they are the same thing, and asks nine of them to notice if they are about to join them up before they do it. Then a voice in the reed asks her the same question in the hearing of nine people, and nobody answered it for her, and a man of thirty-one had a question in his mouth at about the fourth second and did not ask it and said so out loud. Then she said one word, and it took her most of the time it takes a man of forty-three to give a reading, and about four of them could tell afterwards what it was about and the rest could not, and nobody asked her, and a man of sixty-one refused to give anybody a figure for what it cost her.**

**710 — THE SHEET IS READ OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN ON A BANK FROM THIS MORNING AND EVERY MORNING AFTER IT, A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT READS OUT THE SEVEN THINGS THAT ARE NOT ON IT, A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR SAYS IN ONE SENTENCE WHAT THOSE SEVEN ARE, AND NOBODY ANSWERS THE QUESTION.** The fifth day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Saturday, his four hundred and sixtieth morning, his one hundred and fifty-sixth day, and the fever is sixty-one weeks old. **The bank is full again at about sixteen inches, four days. A man of thirty-eight reads the sheet out loud again in the order it is written, counts twice, gives the working, says he is going to do it every morning and has not been asked and will not be thanked for it, and says it lives on the counter because a thing nine people have is a thing nine people can be sent for about. A woman of twenty-eight reads out the seven things that are not on it and gives the count before the list: no day of anybody stopping; no column for what happens when the person who said yes does not come; no column for who a person in this county is; no column for what a name on it is for; no column for the person who keeps it; no column for how many were asked; and no column for whether anybody was told they could not. A woman of forty-four says those seven in one sentence — a thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing — and says a person refusing is somebody who can be sent for and says no, and the other one is a field with something in it, and that about nine people in that county have got one of those in about four days and nobody sent them for anything. A man of thirty-eight asks for the figure for how long and a man of sixty-one refuses it in five sentences and says a figure for it is a term. A boy of thirteen gives him one he did not ask for — nine days — and he tells the boy to keep it. And a man of thirty-one says the shape of his answer out loud a week early on purpose, that if he is asked again it will be nothing at all, and a woman of twenty-eight tells him in front of nine people that this is a rule and not an answer, that in about nine weeks it will be a rule about a man of thirty-one in about nine people's mouths and nobody will have been there when it was made, and that she is not going to be the person who holds his silence for him.**
