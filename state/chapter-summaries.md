## 839–842 — VOLUME 17, BAND 0005, CHAPTERS 839–842, FOUR PARAGRAPHS, ONE PER CHAPTER, AND EVERY FIGURE IN THEM TAKEN FROM THE CHAPTER IT IS IN AND NOT FROM ANY OTHER FILE

**839 — First day of the hundred and forty-second week, a Tuesday, a full bank holding at sixteen for the second morning and the next time in three days.** A fifth leaf had been lying under the fourth one on that flat for the whole of four days and Nevin Trask, sixty-one, weighed it in his hand before he said anything and said he did not know it was there. He read the first two lines — that there is one seal which is immediately valid, that it is not Ilyan's, that it may be taken once by the person the arrangement has already named, and that the person is the man of thirty-one — and he stopped on the ninth word, because Ada Renk, sixty-four, stood up off the bank and said one word, which was *stop*, and would not say it again. Ilyan said a thing on a flat has no voice, that he did not ask her to stand up, that he was not going to be thanked for a thing he did not arrange, and that the standing offer and the thing on that flat are two offers and merging them is the one move that would settle it cheaply. The standing offer was given at a hundred and thirty-nine days with the subtraction in it and was not an answer.

**840 — Second day of that week, a Wednesday, a full bank for the third morning and the next time in two days.** Simon Rook, about fifty-two, who cannot see well, read nine positions out loud, gave them in the order they are written in and said that is not an order of importance, and said he can put a line under six of the nine and that the other three are not on the ground, and named that as his eighth thing he cannot check. He said out loud that six being checkable does not make the three checkable, and that he had watched about four of them do that arithmetic in their heads in the last minute. He said six, three and four together and said there is no sum of them. Wat Marshe, thirteen, at a hundred and eighty-seven days, said his four are not the six and not the three, refused to make a figure of ten, said four of them who are going to speak is not consent and is not a plan, and said he is one of them and is not going to be counted twice. Sera Quill, twenty-eight, at a hundred and ninety-eight days, said the second of her two things is a man of thirty-one and also what is written on a leaf, that they are not the same thing, and that she has had them the same for four days because it is easier to be frightened of one thing than of two. Ilyan admitted he has been counting himself inside a figure of four for nine days and called it a small vanity.

**841 — Third day of that week, a Thursday, a full bank for the fourth morning and the next time tomorrow.** Nevin Trask read the rest of the fifth leaf whole, fifteen lines and a blank back, and turned it over twice for the same people, and said he would give the lines out loud and not a summary of them. Line eleven is that no answer of the arrangement may be questioned and that the only remedy for a person who does not accept an answer is that the person remains. Line fifteen is that no person in this county could be sent for about any of the nine. Ilyan verified every figure he could verify with the working, named the three of nine that are not on the ground, and said the thing on that flat is good and works and is not a threat wearing a gift. He said a lesser man than him would have said yes on Tuesday on the child alone and would have been right to, and put it in about nine ears so that nobody could say afterwards he was given eleven days. Ada Renk said the nineteen years is not a reason either, gave both sides of it, and said she is not going to stand on that bank and be a reason for a man of thirty-one.

**842 — Fourth day of that week, a Friday, the water off all ninety steps for the twenty-second time and off since about the fifth hour.** Ilyan refused the only immediately valid constitutional seal in about nine ears with the figures in it, and gave the reason as line eleven and not the child, and said he was refusing all of it rather than nine-tenths of it, and said the reason was not the child, the gap, the nineteen years, or what a woman of sixty-four stood up for, and that he would rather say the wrong reason than let a right one be invented for him. Ada Renk read out one sentence of her own — that a person may refuse a specific rule, and stay in the covenant, and is responsible for the people the refusal touches — and said it did not come off the flat and that she was giving it away. Wat Marshe read it back in three pieces because a man of fifty-four cannot read, and said that remaining is not staying, and that nobody was to tell him he had found it. The cost was an interval and not a feeling: he lost the lived memory of filing the Sovereign's Wake refusal report and kept the report and its facts, and every counter on that bank still ran, and the standing offer is at a hundred and forty-two days and is still not answered.
