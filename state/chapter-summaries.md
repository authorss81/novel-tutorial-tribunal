## VOLUME 09, CHAPTERS 421 TO 430 — what each one is

**421 — Four hurdles and a plate with no seal on it.** A cart brings four hurdles, nine links of chain and an unsealed plate up the Low Road on the third day of the eighty-second week, and Ott Dray, who has carried thirty-one sealed sheets in nineteen years and has never been asked what was in one, says the difference out loud in a row of nine people: a sheet goes from a room to a room, and this one is going to stand in a road. Ilyan takes a reading of the plate on the flat, standing in the open, and asks for it twice, and finds the finding himself: it is the plate that came down on the sixth day of the eightieth week with four letters changed, *taken down* to *put up*, and **both of them say the north side and neither has ever named the south side.** In the room at the foot of the hill, Darrin Skell refuses to guess what a bar with no door behind it is for, and Ferrow copies the plate into the press. Kerwin Dace comes down the hill at the last of the light, says who he is, and says he goes out of this city in the morning with no name in either of his papers, past his own plate. Ilyan has the sentence that would stop him and says, *I heard you*, and writes the room's finding in the margin of a page he has not written on in eleven days. `421`

**422 — A man walks down a row to ask one question, and a woman puts a slate out for twenty people to read.** Dace's cart stops two miles out at the office's own timber and he walks round it, because a cart does not go round four hurdles and a person does; the driver will not take the cart round a ledge a foot and a half over the water. Then Renn Calloway walks a mile and a half down the low row on a bad leg to ask the only person anybody can be asked about a door. Hessa Marl says no three times. He will not leave without the second half and promises not to write it down. **She reads out her own figure instead — nine a day, forty-nine days, 441 doors, on a slate, in a row, in front of twenty people — and he says in front of all of them that it is not the number of doors on his road and that he is not going to write it down at all.** Ilyan is at the fourth door with a divisor in his mouth that would fill the count form in ten seconds, and he keeps it behind his teeth, and it stays with him all afternoon. She says she will go to the keeper of the roll when she has not been given anything for a day. `422`

**423 — The first dispute this city has sent away is a bar, and the form that comes back wants a number of persons.** Ferrow reads out a waxed return: the dispute about the passage over the Low Road is entered in the register at the seat against a person appointed there, who is of the seat and not of this city. The office's answer is four lines and every line is correct. **The dispute is not a person; it is four hurdles and a chain, and the sheet has no line for the person who gave it.** Ferrow then says out loud, in a room, the sentence Tarin Keel said against that wall on the first day of the month, because Tarin Keel is four hundred miles off and will not be in the room again. The count form arrives unwaxed. Skell has 900 doors and refuses the number of persons, because a count of persons is a division, and the office has declined to provide or check it. **There is no line on that form for a person who could not fill it in.** Bess Low gets off the wall for the only time in the band and puts a bell, a porch and a corridor on the table. Hessa Marl names where the only number in this city is and refuses to say the name. `423`

**424 — A name in the wrong line, and a man doing a division out loud because nobody asked him to.** A waxed sheet enters Hessa Marl as removed from this city, in the line for a person removed, with the line for a person applying left empty, copied fair off a paper that a man of this ward carried into a building himself — and Ilyan is standing at the end of the table with a cracked steel pen and knows it. **Her own no is in a book four hundred miles off, her name is in a book four hundred miles off in a different line, and she is the only person in this city who is in both, and neither of them is a person asking her a question.** Skell works out what a kept place is worth out loud, with the working, unasked: 819 pence against a keeper's 48 is seventeen and a sixteenth over seventeen times; against the second row it is three days and a little over three. **A government that prints a price for a visit, a day and a year of being taken out of a city has decided what a person is, and it decided it in a division, and the division was his.** A mile and a half down the low ground, Fenna Rusk says yes to keeping a place and no to a number of persons in the same breath, in her own room, in about nine people's hearing, and the clerk writes both on one line, and the line is longer than the form. `424`

**425 — A woman goes past the office's own timber on foot and comes back up the same four yards of mud.** Ilyan sleeps through the beginning of it. Hessa Marl gets onto a cart at the fourth hour, the cart stops two miles out, the driver will not take it round, and she goes east on foot past the plate and over the rise where the flat stops being flat. He sits on the tail-board for about eight hours with a load that is not his and is the only one who sees the whole of it, and when she comes back with mud on a boot that was clean at the fourth hour he says the thing he has carried for forty years: there is a word for a road a person can come and go on and a word for a road a person cannot, and he has carried the second kind up and down this road for nineteen years and has never seen the first, **and it was a woman in boots and there was nothing on it.** At the end of the low row the finding is two words: *it works.* Skell gives the second half — a person may go and may not, neither is a victory, neither is a finding about the city they went out of, and a woman of sixty-eight in a bed at the top of this city is counted in the answer anyway — and Hessa Marl says the rest: **a government that bars a road has admitted the road goes somewhere.** At the fifth hour, in the room at the foot of the hill, the System puts four lines in front of Ilyan and he chooses nothing out of them and tells nobody. `425`

**426 — The only question in nine weeks that was asked in the form of a question, and the answer.** Ilyan asks Fenna Rusk whether the roll can say whether the person who went out on the seventh day of the week at the fourth hour is the person who came back at the last of the light. **She says no, and says why: a roll counts doors, and the person went out of a door and came in at a door, and it is the same door.** The office's return has a column headed *persons and things*, and a roll in a column is a register, and a person can be struck out of a book and a thing cannot. Skell does the working on what that costs her: 4,004 days, about eleven years, £4 4s — and then declines to say the rest of it, and she says it herself. Ferrow copies eleven pages fair before the original goes and reads the first page back to a woman who cannot see it. *That is not my hand.* **She is the only person anybody could be asked whether a roll is right; she was that person at the second hour and she is not that person at the fourth, and she declines to put a figure on herself out loud.** The roll goes up the hill in the coat of a clerk who paid for the cart out of a wage of four shillings a year he has never had. `426`

**427 — Eight people walk out to the post in four inches of mud, and a man says the price of his own name out loud before he puts it down.** Hessa Marl says the finding in a room, including the part that the year's carriage money is three hundred and sixty-four pounds sitting at the seat and that a person who has not asked has not asked. Then eight people walk two miles to the ninth marker, and Renn Calloway will not go past the post until the man with the bad leg has said the price. Fenna Rusk says out loud that she is in the mud because she could not say no. **Ilyan says it: if his own name goes on a line beside hers, the road has a person on it who is not of this city and cannot be sent for, and nothing takes a name out of a return except a person of the place applying at the seat, and he is not a person of a place, so he cannot apply, and so he cannot be struck out.** The cost is hers: she will be asked about him for as long as there is a road and a form and an office, and he cannot name one person who could stop it. He says what happens if he does not do it: nothing. Skell coins the word — **a co-witness** — Fenna Rusk says she has not heard the word and asks what it is for, and he says: for the road. Ferrow writes it down. Calloway says a road with one keeper and one decider in the same body is held and not kept, and that this morning there are two of them and neither of them is him. `427`

**428 — A form with two questions in one line, and a boy who will not fill in the part that would cost him everything.** A form arrives for a person under age, with one line that asks whether he carried a statement about a road and, if not, the name of the person who did. Sef says the line is asking two things, refuses to choose between them, and asks what happens to a person who answers half a question. **Nothing happens to him.** He then says the other half: the man of the ward who carried a paper into a building and the clerk who filled a line off it are in nobody's form, and he is the only person in this city who could put either of them in it, and no form asks a person of sixteen whether he may. Ilyan is four feet away and cannot ask, and does not name himself, and lets the silence run. The boy gives the reason: if he says the name, the whole fortnight was because of who he knows and not because of what he said. **He gives his own name to Ilyan, in a room, and asks that it not be written down.** Then Bess Low reads out Sera Quill's paper, which is right about four things and wrong about the plan. `428`

**429 — A return with one column and two things down it, and the office's own word at the end of the line.** Ferrow writes the return fair in the press room with the door shut and his own tray on the table, and puts two things down one column: himself, and *a person conveyed to the ninth marker on the seventh day of the eighty-second week at the fourth hour, and returned at the last of the light, the same.* He reads both out twice. Hessa Marl gives the second half: a person who comes back the same is a thing and a person who comes back different is a different thing, **and the column has room for the first and not for the second, and the office has not asked which she is**, and the last word on the line is the office's and not hers. She asks him to write *the same* in the second line, and he writes it because she asked and there is no paper with a reason on it and he will not make one up. Ilyan offers to add his own name and Ferrow refuses — there is no room, and a sheet with a column and a margin is a sheet whose margin the office will read as an opinion. Then the ordinary way: the sheet goes up in a coat, the roll in oilcloth, the cart paid for by whoever sent it, and nobody has a line for it. In the yard, Hessa Marl tells Ilyan he will not stand in the room, because he will be asked about it and she will be asked about him. `429`

**430 — Six clauses, all of them correct, and a boy in a doorway who is not on a list and is not off a list.** The office answers everything: the dispute is not lost, the bar is to remain up, the one column is entered and the distinction is not paid for, the count is required and not provided and not checked, the sums due are noted and not pressed for, a person of the place is defined for the first time by any government about a person in this city, and **nothing in this sheet is a finding about a road.** Ferrow's finding: the office has answered everything correctly and not one line says anything about a road, and he has been waiting fourteen days for one of them to be wrong. Ilyan says the rest in the road and not in a room, naming an age and not a name: the definition does not reach a boy of sixteen, who was asked whether he wished to be struck out and said no, and so can be struck out, and so is not on a list and is not off a list. Calloway says the whole of it from the wall and goes to look at the water under the ninth weir. **Ilyan is left alone in a corridor with a word nobody gave him — co-witness, a piece of work and not a rank, no form for it and none coming — and with the knowledge that the price he named in the mud is going to be paid by a woman with a lamp, after he has gone.** `430`
