# Chapter Summaries

**One paragraph per chapter for the band in progress. This block supersedes everything below it.** **Chapters 1–450 are canon and Volume 09 is closed. Volume 10, *The House That Refused*, is open; 451–480 are canon.** Earlier bands are one line each; chapters are the record.

## VOLUME 10, CHAPTERS 471 TO 480 — *the majority's freedom, and what it costs in pence*

**471 — The day on the paper arrives with nothing on the agenda, and a copyist reads ten things out in a market square, and the eighth of the ten is her.** The twenty-fifth day after the division is a Friday and the board does not sit. Ilyan spends the morning asking the square what the day is for and gets eleven answers, **four of them his own sentence back at him in four versions, three of them wrong, and the wrongest says the fen is nineteen more seats — and he does not put it right, on purpose, in about four seconds, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets him be asked about it afterwards.** The chair comes down into the square on his own, without the minute and without the eight other men, and says out loud that he wrote the day down as a courtesy and that on the twenty-fourth day he said somebody would come and use him, and that he cannot tell whether he is a man who kept a courtesy or a man who built a wall and wrote the word courtesy on it so that he could sleep, **and that there is no form in the county of Ostrey for a day.** He does not answer his own question: an answer is a minute and a minute is a use. **The band's one panel at `471:139`–`471:145`**: a day a man wrote down for himself is a wall, and a courtesy is a wall, and the two are the same shape from the outside. Nothing is chosen. **Wenna Crale has copied the sheet of ten things and put it in the press a day early and says so out loud; the chair refuses the eight — *you may have them when I have found them* — and she reads all ten out herself in the square, and the eighth of the ten is that a copyist has entered eleven things no body asked her to enter, which is true, and which she says in her own mouth with the days.** Mab Harrow gives him the first half of a thing: he is the only person in this county nobody can be sent for, and the half that is a gift is that he can walk into any room. `471`

**472 — A market on a Saturday, and a bill of sale, and a room over a taproom.** The market is on a Saturday for the fifth time in nine years because for three weeks nothing in Halloway has happened on a Tuesday, and about two thousand people are in the square. **Ivo Cray, thirty-four, of the ninth lot, walks four hundred yards up a lane with a bill of sale in his coat and asks whether the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward belongs to him, and nobody in that room can answer him, because the Book is nine feet up over a fire and the clause that answers it is on the back of it, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that gives anybody a hearing.** The schedule says *and the water of the Hallow from the sluice downward, and the race, and the sluice, and the two closes below it*, in the same hand and the same size of writing as five acres. **Then about twenty people on a stair say the same three sentences, and the three sentences are word for word the third line of a minute of the nineteenth day, and a copyist in the room knows it and says the day, and the majority of that room discovers that it has a position and that the position is *we paid for it* and that it is seven days old and not one person in the room knows they are standing in it.** Sefa Lund does the sum nobody asked for: **the heel is forty pence more than the quarter, and eighty lots at half a penny is forty pence, and the eighty lots are not short of it, and the board cannot ask for it because the roll has been made.** And a girl of nineteen asks Ilyan whether he wrote the three sentences, and he says no, and it is true, and it is the largest lie he has told in a month, and he told it because a position that belongs to one man can be argued with. `472`

**473 — A Sunday in the fourth house of nineteen, and four accounts that do not agree.** No document, no vote, no panel. About eleven of the twenty-three people in the row are in one kitchen. **Four men are asked one at a time what they were told and by whom, and the four answers are four different things: a miller told Wat Priddy that the Fen is a page in a man's book and a dash is a house and not a person; a chair told Coll Sarn that the third sentence — *the fenrow have not asked* — is the one that cannot be argued with; a steward told Ordwin Cape there is a door in an office over a shop and it is not locked; a man of the county told Renn Selby that the page saying nine pounds is twenty-two miles off and nobody here has walked to ask for it.** None of the four knows the other three were told anything, and none is wrong. **The two who were told nothing — Mab Harrow and Sefa Lund — have been carrying all of it and adding it up wrong.** And then the finding: **Sefa Lund turns out to have been the only person keeping an account of that row, since the Monday, without deciding to, and nobody asked her to, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person shall keep an account of a row.** She says in her own mouth, in front of about eleven people, that keeping an account is a claim, and that she said in a lane on the eleventh day that she did not want to be a person with a claim, and that the accounts are made of what the row has been told and by whom and on what day, and that she cannot give them up, and that the only person who could hold them is a copyist paid two pounds a year. **The chapter says plainly that he is not in the row, is not told, and does not know, and that he worked out standing in a room over a nail shop that eleven people in a kitchen were doing the only thing in this town he could not do, and that he did not go up the lane, and the reason he gave himself was not the reason.** `473`

**474 — The steward does not call the House, and the Book comes down for the second time in ninety years and is given to nobody.** Ivo Cray asks the steward to call the House on the question of its own covenant. **Amos Trill refuses and gives the best reason in the volume: he has been forty years the only man in this town nobody had to wonder what he wanted, that is not a virtue on a form, it is the only power he has ever had, and he is no longer that man — and there is a clause on the back of that board that would have stopped what he did four weeks ago and he has had eighteen days to go and look at it and will not, because looking at it puts him in a room where he has to say what he did, to forty-one men and a copyist.** Perdy Sallow stands in the doorway and will not come further in, and the two of them argue for four minutes about who is the mouth of a thousand people, and both of them say that neither of them is a man and that there is not one form in the county of Ostrey that says what a man is instead. **Ilyan is asked for his view for the second time and says the truest and worst thing he has said in this town: the only way to reach the House is to be in it, the only way to be in it is to be a head of household in one of nine wards, the row cannot be, and the reason is not that they are not asked and not that they have refused and not that they are nineteen houses with doors — it is that they are not on the roll, so they are in no ward, so nobody in that row can ever be in the room where the minute is written. And it is made of a day out of a day, in the autumn, by a man counting houses.** Then the Book comes off the beam in four minutes, and the ninth clause is read out in a room for the second time in ninety years, **and the copyist says out loud that nobody in this town has ever been given it and nobody is going to be given it this year and there is no form that gives it to anybody.** `474`

**475 — A second correction nobody asked for, and a man of thirty-eight finds his own number on a wall.** About thirty people in the Stone House, four times as many as have ever been in it on a weekday, and eleven new lines in a steward's hand under the four lines of the Sunday. **The roll goes from 1,060 to 1,071, the year's money from £35 6s 8d to £35 14s 0d, the quarter from £8 16s 8d to £8 18s 6d, and the heel from forty pence short to eighteen pence short — and the form does not say whose household and does not say whether anybody asked.** **Baldwin Trove, thirty-eight, a carter, nine years in a house with a lean-to on it, stands up in the middle of a stone room and reads his own line off the wall and says he is not going to say that he did not want it, and that he has wanted a rate on that house since the week he moved in, because a man with a rate is a man anybody has to be civil to — and that if anybody wants to know anything about him they can come up the row.** **Nine of the eleven are in the ninth ward. The House has five heads there and always has and there is no form that says a ward has a number of seats. The fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn, and what has changed is not a number of seats and never was: it is the number of doors.** And a woman of thirty with a child on her hip says the whole of his sentence, in the right words, in the right order, off a nail on a shed, and about nine people turn and look at the place where he is standing, and he has nine seconds to say it was his and uses them on not saying it. **And the true figure is named: 1,074, a number of what the roll would be if the whole of what they found on the Monday were in it, and a number of nothing else.** `475`

**476 — THE MIDPOINT. Nineteen houses put out of the water, correctly, in a form, and a minute with her name in it that she did not put there.** A room over a saddler's shop, about thirty in the room and on the stair, and two things on the table that nobody in that row put there: **a request in writing from thirty-one ratepayers, and a cause set out in writing by Ordwin Cape on a bill on the Sunday evening — in the ninth year before the division the water stood above the sill of the houses at the Fen for eleven days, clause five cause two at the foot of it in the same hand.** He was there. His mother was in the fourth house. **Ilyan says, in a room of thirty people, the sentence of a boy of twenty-two at a wool stall in the rain, because the boy cannot be asked about it: a household is a house. And then the row's own third line — they asked to be charged *knowing that the water is common to all households of this town* — and a request with a footing in it is a request and without the footing it is a note.** The board puts a **requirement** in a minute naming Sefa Lund to produce an account; **the word *required* is in a minute of that town for the first time in four hundred years**; there is no account and there never has been and there is no form that says a person shall keep one. **Then the ninth clause is said out loud out of the copyist's head, the Book not coming down, and the chair strikes his own line out of his own minute because the person required is a household of this town and clause nine binds her, and says the reason: he wanted that line since the sixteenth day and could not have it.** The board finds the cause under clause five, cause four, and it is right. **The steward corrects the roll a third time in about four minutes and the third line of the fenrow's own request is cited by name in the form. The roll stands at 1,052; the fund is £35 1s 4d; the quarter is £8 15s 4d; the heel is fifty-six pence short instead of eighteen — and thirty-eight pence of that is the twelve shillings and eightpence a year the fenrow were paying and were the only people in Halloway who had offered to pay.** And in a square at the sixth hour, **Ivo Cray is the first of the majority to find out what it is worth**: if the bar is not turned on Thursday morning it is not a season, it is a year. And Sefa Lund says, in a square, for the second time in this town, that she did not agree, and this time there is a minute with her name in it that she did not put there, and nobody has given her the thing she asked for in a lane and she has got the opposite of it. `476`

**477 — A letter addressed to a water board and not to a man, and a second sheet folded in four.** The bar is not turned for the first day and about eleven people are at the head of it and about twenty more come up the lane, because the sound of a bar carries and everybody knows what it sounds like. **The bag came on the Tuesday off a carrier who has driven that road thirty-one years; he did not open it in a room; he opened it on the Wednesday night in a room over a nail shop and read it twice and read a second sheet out of it.** **Four pages in the round hand of the counters, which he asked her not to use, addressed to the water board of Halloway, not signed with her own name, and he says out loud in a stone room with about nine people in it: she is not writing to me.** Four lines are read out in a square: *I have not opened the range… that is the eleventh time I have written that sentence in a book of my own in six years and nobody has ever asked me why I do it* / *you are right that a hand is a thing a person can be asked about* / *every exit I have read in six years and a half is a form, and a form is a door, and a door can be opened by whoever is nearest the hinge* / **an exit is a right when the person it is for can open it without asking anybody's character, and that is not a sentence, that is a thing with hinges on it, and there is no such thing in the building I sit in, or in any of the nine I have worked in.** Then he says, in order, everything she wrote nine days ago is demonstrable in a town of four thousand people, in ten days, by three uses of one form on a Sunday, a Monday and a Wednesday, and it is the demonstration he has spent three weeks building: **a right you have to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour; a form is a door; a thing in a book is the handle on the door, and he did not know that when he taught it.** And he names the four instruments of Halloway — **a miller's second book, a copyist, a minute, and a woman who refuses things in lanes** — and says he is on none of them and has been proud of that for nine weeks. And then he says the second sheet: six years, a refusal, a household dead except one person, a claim not entered because a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it and that person is not findable, and **she has not gone and looked for it herself, and if she looks then her name goes on the claim as the person presenting it.** About thirty people in a square. It cannot be un-read. `477`

**478 — The mill counter, refused twice, and the reason is a sentence he said out loud on Thursday.** The wheel at the mill has been slow since the Thursday, and the bar is not turned for the second day, and the miller has put one word in a note in his own book against the name of the mill. **Ilyan asks for the second book and gets the best reason anybody has given him in a month: you stood under the awning of my mill on Friday morning with about thirty people in that square and you counted the instruments of this town out loud, and I am a man with a counter and a book, and you have told a town of four thousand people that I am a public instrument, and a public instrument is a thing that gets opened. That is the reason. Not the four weeks ago reason, which was about my shop. This one is about your square.** And the better reason underneath it: **about a hundred and eighty dashes, and about a hundred and seventy of them are not the fenrow, and every one of them is a house that has been paying me for four years off the water list and has never once known there was a question about it — and put that book in a press and this town has a list of a hundred and seventy households the roll has not got, and a town that has worked out in three weeks that a roll is a door does not then hand the door a second list. It used one. On Wednesday. In four minutes.** **Coll Sarn, twenty-two, asks in the doorway why a man of thirty-nine will not let a book out of his hands. And Mab Harrow, thirty-four, says the other half of the thing she left undecided on the twenty-fifth day: when you have gone out of the room there is nobody in it who was sent for, and that is not the same room, and that half was true on the Friday morning and it has got truer every day since, and on Wednesday it put nineteen houses out of the water in four minutes in a form that nobody had to be asked about. It is a hole and it is not a gift.** `478`

**479 — About two hundred people in a square, and the sentence the town carried was on a nail.** There is no market, because the market is on a Tuesday. **About two hundred people are in the square, nine of them because a list of ten things is going to be read out and a hundred and ninety-one because of the water.** The chair asks the copyist to read the ten in the square — in about eleven words, and he does not read them himself. **The ninth of the ten is that a man of thirty-one read a notice out in a square on the sixth day after the division and nobody asked him to; the tenth is that a house of four thousand people has found out in three weeks what a book done in a morning is.** And then the finding, said by a woman of thirty with a child on her hip in the right words: **a corrected roll is a corrected constituency; a constituency is a set of doors; a book can be a door; and there are four handles in this county and I can name three.** A boy of about fifteen asks who is going to stop it, and about nine people answer at once, and the answers are in a book: **the board cannot, the House cannot because the steward has not called it and the number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it, and the county of Ostrey cannot — the only office that has ever heard of it is twenty-two miles off and can make a return and nothing else — and the Book is nine feet up over a fire and has been read out twice in ninety years and given to nobody both times. And the copyist says it in one sentence: there is nobody in this county who can stop it, and the only things anybody can be asked about are four, and one of the four is a nail.** The carter of about thirty, who got the sentence right off that nail thirteen days ago, says there is no form that says a nail is a document. **Ilyan stands in the square for four hours and says almost nothing, and one of the eleven lines — the ninth — is him.** The sheet acquires an eleventh line in the copyist's hand and a different ink, and about nine people know which of the ten it is and say nothing. `479`

**480 — The band closes on an instrument, and a man is read out of a room he is not standing in.** A sheet of eleven lines in a press; a roll for the autumn in about a fortnight on a day out of a day; a frame that is the third item on a list nine years old. The Stone House, one hearth, the stools put away, about nine people, and the Book not read out again. **The copyist asks Ilyan not to come into the room, and says the reason in front of nine people: a man in the room is a man the room can be asked about, he can say no, he can be interrupted, he can be asked whether he is sorry — and I want to read the ninth of these eleven things out in a room that he is not standing in, because that is the only shape in which this town has ever been able to hear anything, and it has cost him four weeks and it has cost me eleven years.** **He says yes in about nine seconds and does not come further than the doorway.** She reads the ninth out as a document and not as a line on a list: *Ilyan Vester, thirty-one, of a county four hundred miles off this town, and not of this town, and not of the fenrow, and not of the water board, and not of the House. One day in Halloway on the sixth day after the division, when he came into this town at about the fourth hour… and that he has been in nine rooms in this town in twenty-eight days and is on nothing, and that the whole of what that has been worth to him and the whole of what it has cost this town are the same sentence, and that I do not know which is the larger, and I have not asked him.* Then the whole of what the band did not fix, said plainly: the ninth clause given to nobody twice; the out-of-roll book shut and the day it was named for gone; the bar; the heel at nine pounds against £8 15s 4d; and **a witness line with two names, one of them four letters and a stroke, not read by anybody in this county, on a road, in a second sheet folded in four, four hundred miles off, in a press on a shelf with about nine hundred refusals in it, and nobody in this town has opened it and nobody in this town is going to.** And the last two facts of the band: **about two hundred people stood in a square and worked out what a book done in a morning is, and nobody in that square asked whether anybody can be sent for.** He is in the doorway, about nine people are inside the room, his name is in a book in the room and his name is on nothing, and the door of that building is open. `480`

**470 — The second of ten things this band did not fix, and a man of thirty-one eleven miles up a road with his own form in his hand.** Thursday, the twenty-fourth day after the division, and a board of nine in a room over a saddler's shop with about twenty people in the square outside. **A request of four lines, made on the Wednesday afternoon after the page came out of Kerby and entered in the minute by a copyist who was not asked to enter it:** mend the north heel at nine pounds, the cost to be levied on the eighty lots in the same manner as the fourteen pounds of the scouring, the work to be done before the water rises. **Sefa Lund asks the four words that kill it — *ask him where the fourteen pounds went*** — because the fourteen pounds was charged in the roll of this year, and the roll of this year was made in the week the common was sold, and **there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made.** **And Ordwin Cape says the second line of it is the line he copied**, out of the minute of this board's own sitting, because it is the only form of a levy he has ever seen in this town. The board declines, and **the chair says the refusal in the words of the request, on purpose**, and then says the half that is not in them: the frame is on a list, it is the third thing on it, the other two are the cleaning of the tail of the race and a second winding bar, and all three have been on that list every year for nine years. He has a sheet with **ten things this town has had in front of it in three weeks and put down again**, and will not read them out with the door shut, and gives the sheet to the copyist for the press; he names the first — the ninth clause, and that nobody in this town has ever been given it and nobody is going to be given it this year — and the second, which is the north heel. **The out-of-roll book was not written in and never was offered to anybody but one woman in a lane. The twenty-fifth day after the division is tomorrow and there is nothing set down on it.** The roll stands corrected at 1,060 and is wrong by 112 pence a year. A fact about the north heel is on the wrong side of a leaf. **The four letters and a stroke have not been read by anybody and they were on a road.** And then he does it himself: three lines at the foot of the margin of a return that is thirty-two days old — **his two hundred and twentieth morning, written at Halloway, in the county of Ostrey, which is not upon a road of the Shelf** — and eleven miles on a bad leg to a counter at Ostrey, and a clerk of about thirty who asks him the only question a counter asks, and he says **enter no person**, and she writes *no answer* in a column and shuts the book. `470`

## VOLUME 10, CHAPTERS 451 TO 460 — *the town, the board, and the first refusal*

(one line each; the full paragraphs for this band are in git at `df269b3`)

**451 — The ask, the want said out loud, and the going. Wenna Crale, 34, copyist, eleven years, gives her name before anybody asks and asks who the hand on the ninth clause is; it is a hundred and thirty-eight years old. He says out loud that he wants a town where his name is not on anything and that he is not going to get it, then gives away the fact that Halloway is not on a road of the Shelf. He refuses her. She goes home. `451`**

**452 — Nine miles of milestone with the county letter on one side and nothing on the other, and a notice read out in a square by a stranger nobody asked. Forty-one people in the wet, nineteen houses, a girl of nineteen with a roll under her coat. Amos Trill, steward forty years, is the man who asks him; he refuses for the girl, out loud, and says reading a notice is what makes a man somebody’s man. `452`**

**453 — The board comes down twice a year, by two people, in a hurry, with the fire lit, and the audit on the Monday the common was signed away found no common and did not bring it down. Four people can read the copy; two ever read the Book and one is nine years dead. He asks to be one of the two hands and is refused with the best reason anybody gives him in nine weeks. She gives him Hessa Marl’s answer: a book is kept as long as the town is feeling like it. `453`**

**454 — The Fen is on the roll as land and not as households. He says 1,041 + 19 = 1,060 in a market to about two hundred people and has to take it apart the same day; the true figure is somewhere between nineteen and twenty-three and the number is the protection. The steward names the out-of-roll book, a one-line power nobody has ever claimed, and says why he has not used it. `454`**

**455 — A room, a witness and somebody to write it down. He writes four hundred and ten words and a woman of thirty-eight strikes the fourth paragraph out in her own hand and signs across the strike. Garrick Nye is right about the race and the frame. Mab Harrow, from a doorway, asks who it is for and gets three answers. £14 out of £34 14s, two fifths, and the county does not pay for water. `455`**

**456 — The Book comes off the beam for about two hours, clause four is read out, the vote is six to three and the three are named, and Wenna Crale turns the board over unasked. The band’s one panel. Clause nine, a hundred and thirty-eight years old, with a witness line carrying two names and one of them four letters and a stroke. He says out loud that it is a right of refusal taken out of the law, and the word *unverified* goes into the minute. `456`**

**457 — The gates shut for nine days and the thumb goes into the north heel. Sallow offers the out-of-roll book, is refused in a lane, and admits he made an offer that was no offer. Ilyan offers to carry the objection as one document and Sefa Lund tells him what a witness is, in the words he said himself in another city. The roll for next year is made in the autumn, and he will not put that in a minute. `457`**

**458 — Water in pails for four days, four hundred yards, twice a day. Ordwin Cape is thirty-nine pence short. Ott Vernay stops credit to Mab Harrow in a shop with nine people in it and says why, and Ilyan offers to pay and is refused: *you want it in your book, not mine.* Wenna Crale refuses his unsent number and tells him to send it to a book that has a head on it. `458`**

**459 — The back of the fourth leaf: a strike by order upon a paper not of this town, and a woman of twenty-four writing that she could not learn whether the striking was of that clause alone. Clause nine is struck and clause four is not, and the word in clause four is *household*, and that is the word that shuts the door. Ilyan is right, loudly, and it does not help. The steward enters both margins. `459`**

**460 — The House sits on forty-one stools with the door open and declines to enter the nineteen tenements, in the fenrow’s own name, in about nine seconds, and the minute is correct and self-contradictory in the same sentence. Sefa Lund says out loud that she did not agree to not have a household. Ilyan says he has no standing and says nothing. The gates come off five days early and he writes a letter he does not send. `460`**
