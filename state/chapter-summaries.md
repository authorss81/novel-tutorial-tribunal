**631 — A CART COMES UP A TWO-DAY ROAD DRIVEN BY THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS TWO CARTS, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO KEEPS A GOAT IS REPORTED OUT LOUD, AND THE NUMBER OF PEOPLE WHO HAVE READ A COPY COMES BACK AND IS STILL NOT ANSWERABLE.** `clear` 131, the third day of the hundred and twelfth week, a Thursday, and his seventy-seventh day. A woman of forty-one with two carts drove up two days of a road herself with nobody on the shafts, having gone down on the Sunday with a cart that had a load on it and one of the eleven sheets under the seat, and been in a room at the head of a drain in a town two days off in the county of Ashenfield on the Tuesday evening of the last week, and turned round at about the fourth hour on the Wednesday morning. **The cart with the five sheets went down on the Monday with a man of forty-one and a woman of thirty-four and is still on it.** In that room a man of forty-four who keeps a chandler's shop read one of those sheets out loud from the first line to the last in about nine minutes and did not hurry the last four lines, and about nine people were in the room, and one woman in it stood up off a wall she was leaning on and said one sentence, and she has never been to Hask and nobody in that room had ever seen her and she is not a chair. **I have never been to that town. I have never been asked anything. I am not a chair.** Her name is Eda Pryce, about thirty-eight, of that town, and she keeps a goat, and she is the tenth line on that sheet from the front, which is the tenth of the twenty-nine chairs, and this town counts its chairs from the after end, so it is the twentieth and not the tenth. Then a man of thirty-one asks how many people have read a copy and a woman of forty-one tells him he has asked it the wrong way and gives him the number anyway, because he will get it out of somebody: **nine heads in a room, and I counted the ones I could see and I did not count the ones who came in after I did, and about four of the nine had heard a line read before and about nine had not, and that is a figure of a room and it is not a figure of that town and it is not a figure of this one and it is not a figure of the road, and the reason I cannot tell you one thing about the rest of that county is that nobody counted a road, and a road is not a room.** She says out loud that it is no better than the boy's question. Then the man of thirty-one says he did not answer the question of `630` and is not going to, and says holding the no a woman of twenty-six gave him has turned out to be the only thing he has done this week he would do again. Then the man of sixty-one gives the number of the copies again with the working — **eleven and one and one and one, which is fourteen, a figure of wood and not of people** — and says a woman of forty-one has given him a figure of one room in a town two days off and that it is the first time in eleven years he has written down a number that was not a head or a cart.

**632 — A CROWN IS BUILT OUT OF A BOARD AND A BARREL IN ABOUT FOUR HOURS, THE ONLY PERSON ANYBODY CAN BE SENT FOR ABOUT SAYS NO IN FOUR SENTENCES, AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-NINE SAYS THE WORD FROM A DOORWAY.** `clear` 132, the fourth day of the hundred and twelfth week, a Friday, and his seventy-eighth day. There is no other table in Hask, so the board went on the barrel, and it is the same board a boy of eleven copied a whole sheet of deal on and it is about four feet by a foot and four inches. A man of sixty-one reads out nine names one under another with the ground said before the name — Nevin Trask, Garrin Tolley, Wenna Callow, Nell Prince, Barnaby Crove, Pell Varn, Ibb Marshe, Ada Renk and Otho Pyle — and says that **about nine people in that room can describe what that thing on that barrel is for and about four cannot, and the smaller number is the one that matters.** Then the woman of forty-four is asked, in the open, and says no in four sentences: *the book is me*, every answer is going to come out of a book, an answer said by me in the open is not an answer, it is the position of the record. And the man of sixty-eight gives the consequence in four sentences and does not take the rule back: **then there is no Crown anybody can be sent for about, and about nine people in a room have just built a thing no form in this county reaches, and I am not saying that is wrong, and about nine of you have taken it further than I meant it and I am not going to take it back because of a woman of forty-four.** Then the cost, with the working: a Crown is a person at a barrel answering what a sheet says, a day is about nine hours, about nine people, about eighty-one hours, which is about nine days of one person's working life, out of about eleven people who come up that plank in a working week — and the money is nothing, because there is no form that says a person may be paid for standing at a barrel. Then a woman of twenty-six who has been in a chair for nine years comes to the door at about the seventh hour, does not come further in, and says the word out loud, and says she said it because she would rather it had an ugly name than a kind one. **The Crown.** And a man of sixty-eight says what a crown is — a band and a piece of tin that stands above a room — and says that he put nine names on the board and is one of the nine. Then two things are asked of it and it gets one of them right. And a bench is carried up to the head of the plank and set against the gunwale of the Gull and nobody sits on it.

**633 — THE NINE NAMES ARE READ OFF THE BOARD IN THE OPEN AND THE PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT ON IT ARE COUNTED OUT LOUD, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE ASKS TO BE PUT ON IT AND IS REFUSED IN FOUR SENTENCES.** `clear` 133, the fifth day of the hundred and twelfth week, a Saturday, and his seventy-ninth day. A man of sixty-one gives two counts: the people in that town who cannot be sent for about a chair, which is about eleven hundred less about nine and which he will not write down because it is a figure of nothing anybody can use; and the people nobody can be sent for about at all, which is one, and it is the man of thirty-one, and **the reason he is the one and the reason the woman of forty-four is not the one are not the same reason.** Then a man of thirty-one asks out loud to be put on the board, says the reason before he is refused, and is refused in about four sentences by the man of sixty-eight: **if a Crown has a man in it that nobody can be sent for about, then in about four years the Crown and the man are the same thing; and a Crown that cannot be asked about what is in it is a lid with a name on it; and I am not going to let a man I like stand in the door of it; and I have not said one word about whether the Crown is a good thing.** He says nothing. Then the people who are not on the board are read out: the woman of forty-four, who is the instrument in that county for nineteen years and has never once been the person, and a man of thirty-four called Ivo Kellow in the ash on the low side of that landing since the second day of the hundred and twelfth week, which is three days, and who has not stood up once in the three. Then a woman of seventy-one comes up that cart road on her own legs for the first time in a fortnight, unasked and unsent for, and says before anybody asks her that she is not going to be in it, and gives the reason: **in about four years there will be about nine people in that county that nobody can be sent for about at all, and a Crown of about nine people will want to speak for every one of them, and she would rather be one of the people it does not speak for than one of the people it speaks for** — and she is neither of the two people nobody can be sent for about, and in eleven years nobody has come to her door to ask her a question, and there is a difference between nobody coming and nobody being able to come. And she says no out loud before a man of thirty-one has got to the end of a question, and says she is not going to answer a fifth.

**634 — THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOK ASKS A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT WHETHER HE WILL SPEAK FOR A BROTHER GONE UP THE WATER ELEVEN YEARS, AND HE SAYS YES TO HALF OF IT, AND IT COSTS HIM TWO THINGS.** `clear` 134, the sixth day of the hundred and twelfth week, a Sunday, and his eightieth day. The town does not sit on a Sunday and about nine people came down that cart road anyway and nobody who came had come to be sent for. There was a Crown on a barrel two days old that had answered two things and got one of them right. The woman of forty-four says out loud before she asks that she is asking and not the Crown, and why, and that in nineteen years she has asked two people a thing and one said no and one said yes and she has told nobody either way, and that there is no form in that county that says a keeper of a book may ask a man a question in the open. Then she asks him, with the ground said before the name: the last line on that sheet, the thirty-second, a man called Wren Sayer, gone up the water in a lighter about eleven years ago and not heard of since, with a chair at the other end of the same book, no household of his in this county, and nobody nine miles off can be sent for to ask him whether he would like to be on a Crown. **And he says yes, in about nine sentences: I will speak for the ground and the day and the boat and the eleven years and the fact that nobody in this county can be checked against him, and I will not speak for what he would have said, and if I am asked what he would have said I will say the true thing, which is that I do not know, and I will say it in that shape every single time, and I am not going to be a man who improves a thing every time he says it.** The two costs are his own and he says both: that a name said by a brother is not the same thing as a name said by a man on a board and there is no column in any book in that county for the difference; and that he has a child of nine and told her twice that this is not their business, and **I did not know on Wednesday whether that was true. It is not true.** About four people in that room had it as a coward and about nine did not, and a woman of twenty-nine explains why a half answer is a shape and not a failure, and why nobody in that room is going to say the word again: if he had heard it he would have taken his name off the board in about four seconds, and that is what it would have been. And it goes on a board and not on that sheet, because that sheet is a thing anybody can be sent for about and this is a thing a man said in a room. And a girl of nine four miles off that road is sitting on a settle with a slate on her knees and no name on it, and has heard about nine of the words and has not been told any of it.

**635 — A REMOVED WITNESS IS NAMED, AND THE NAMING IS DONE BY THE MAN OF SIXTY-ONE AGAINST HIS OWN BOOK, AND THE CROWN CANNOT SPEAK FOR HIM.** `clear` 135, the seventh and last day of the hundred and twelfth week, a Monday, and his eighty-first day. The town sits for about forty minutes and there is nothing to sit about, and then the Crown sits for the first time as a Crown at about the eighth hour at the head of the plank, with about nine people in it and about thirteen in front of it and the bench that nobody has sat on still standing against the gunwale. A woman of forty asks it, in the open, in the same shape a line is asked for: **there is a line in the book of heads and carts that has been in that book about two years and there is no line against that man in the book of chairs and no name of his on that sheet of deal and no line in that book for anybody else who has ever come through that landing and not come back, and I keep two carts and I would like the thing at that barrel to speak for him.** The Crown tries and what it does is read, and it is right. Then the man of sixty-one takes the book out from under his arm and says the name against his own book: **Jonas Roel, fifty-seven, of a town four days off, who came up that landing about nine times in about two years with a basket and a cart and went down the ninth time and did not come up, and about two years ago the man of forty-four in that town came up the road with his coat buttoned up to the neck and told him at that gate and not in a room.** The figure is one shilling and sixpence — about nine heads at a penny is about nine pence and about nine carts at a penny is about nine pence — and it is a figure of about two years at one landing and not of what that man owed anybody and not of what he was worth, and a man of sixty-one says he has been taking that penny every Friday for about two years and did not stop, and is not going to be thanked for it and is not going to be forgiven for it, and both of those are his to do and not yours. **He has tried to strike that line out twice and both times he found a reason before the ink was dry, and both reasons were that there is no form in this county that says a man may, and he says out loud that no form is not a reason, and it is a nothing, and he has had it in his own hand in his own book the whole time.** A man of thirty-one tries to make it into a door that can be sent for about and is stopped by the woman of forty in the open, before he has got to the end of it. And the finding, spoken first: **a Crown that can answer for a person who is not in a chair and not in a book is a Crown that can answer for anybody, and a Crown that can answer for anybody is not a Crown, it is a mouth.**

**636 — NINE MILES UP THE WATER, A MAN WHO CANNOT SEE WELL OFFERS OUT LOUD TO HAVE THE CROWN SPEAK FOR A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE IN FOUR COUNTIES, AND IT WOULD WORK IN ABOUT FOUR DAYS.** `clear` 136, the first day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Tuesday, and his eighty-second day. They are on the water at about the fifth hour and in at about the second hour of the afternoon, nine miles up and nine miles down, and the man of thirty-eight poled the whole of it and the man of thirty-one went with him and was in the yard for the whole of it, which is the first time in about four days a room had had him in it while a thing he was on was being said. Simon Rook, about fifty-two, who cannot see well and has been known for that in that county for eleven years, stands at the head of the cart road and asks the thing at the barrel to speak out loud for a man of thirty-one, in four counties, in the open, on a day a person names, and counts the room before he starts: about four of you are going to agree with me before I have finished and about nine of you are not. **The reason is a woman of forty-nine who came down that road with him four days ago, who is not in a chair and is not a head in the book of heads and carts, whom he asked on the Friday what a copy was in the plainest words he had and who said she did not know.** And then the offer itself: if a Crown can do that for her then it can do it for a man of thirty-one, and then he is a person somebody has spoken for, and a person a body has spoken for is a person in a hearing, and then the man of thirty-eight has what he asked for and none of us has to decide anything, and it will have happened in about four days. **A man of thirty-eight says the true of it in four sentences: that would work, and I have been poling nine miles twice a week since the seventh and last day of the hundred and tenth week waiting for a way of getting about every record into one room, and there is not one — and also that a Crown is nine people at a barrel and about four of them cannot describe what it is for, and a Crown that cannot describe what it is for standing in the one room about nine people in the world are going to be in is a Crown doing the one thing in the world it is not for. I am not saying it is wrong.** And the man of thirty-one says he wants it, out loud, before it is offered again, and gives the reason, and says he is going to say the other half on the Wednesday in a room nine miles down the water. And at about the sixth hour a lighter comes up with the man of forty-three in it, who came down on the Tuesday of the last week because nobody in that county can come back and ask him what he meant, and who has gone up again on his own account.

**637 — HE SAYS NO IN ABOUT NINE SENTENCES TO AN OFFER THAT WOULD HAVE WORKED, SAYS A NAME OUT LOUD ONCE, AND NOTHING HAPPENS, AND A BOY OF THIRTEEN PAYS FOR IT.** `clear` 137, the second day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Wednesday, and his eighty-third day. The man of about fifty-two puts the offer again from the front in about nine sentences and says he is going to sit down on that bench and let him answer it and not interrupt him once. **He answers in about nine sentences, without having written it down, having said so before he starts. And he does not give the reason first; he gives the shape, and says that the people waiting for him to say a thing and the people waiting for him to say the right thing are making the same mistake and his is bigger.** The reason: a Crown of about nine people at a barrel, of whom about four cannot describe what it is for; and a Crown in this county is a thing nobody can be sent for about, because the only person anybody can be sent for about in that county said no in four sentences; so the day a Crown opens its mouth about a man it cannot be asked about, that Crown becomes a thing that people act on, and a thing that people act on and cannot be sent for about is a lid with a name on it; and a man of sixty-eight said that on the Friday of the eleventh week and was right, and **he knew he was on the wrong side of it at about the ninth minute of the offer.** Then the second half, about him: there is no form in that county that says a person may be spoken for and none that says a person may not, and four days ago he stood on a bank and said out loud that he wanted to be findable, and **the difference is about four years, and it is the difference a man of sixty-eight put his hand on and would not give back.** And he says the offer would have worked and the man of fifty-two was right and both groups of about four in that yard were right, and he is not going to stand in the room and decide which of them is the true one. **Then he says a name out loud, once, flat, and says he is not going to say it twice and is not going to explain it: The First Witness.** And it is the name of the thing that said its own name in a weigh-house nine miles up that water about eleven days ago in front of about four people, which a man of forty-three read out loud in that county on the Friday of the eleventh week and then a man of thirty-eight said four words about in a yard on the Monday. **And he has said it and nothing has happened, and about nine people in that room have a name they did not have an hour ago, and a name is a thing nine people can carry and a Crown is a thing nine people can speak, and he has just put a name on it for nothing, in the open, on a Wednesday.** Then the cost falls on the person in the room who has never been asked for anything in his life. **Wat Marshe, thirteen: the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen that comes through that landing and it is not on anybody under sixteen, and there is no form in that county that says a person under sixteen may be sent for about a thing he has written with his own hand, and that is how I have been safe for thirteen years and it is not because anybody thought about me, it is because I am small and I do not carry a basket. There is a name in about nine mouths in this room now and about nine mouths in four counties by the end of the week, and the only door into a sheet of deal is the hand that wrote it, and about four people who have one of them are going to come and find me, and my mother cannot be sent for about and I cannot be sent for about, and there is nobody in four counties who can say to them that they may not.** His mother says it is not his fault and it is not a debt and there is nowhere in that county to put it because there is no column for a thing a person owes for being thirteen. And a man of sixty-eight says the thing nobody else in that room was going to say: **nobody in this room stopped him** — and gives the arithmetical of it, and says it is not a fault of anybody in that room.

**638 — SOMEONE DOES ORDINARY WORK THAT IS LOAD-BEARING, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE CUTS A COLUMN INTO THE BACK OF THE LAST LEAF OF A BOOK WITH A KNIFE AFTER FOUR PEOPLE DO NOT ANSWER HIM.** `clear` 138, the third day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Thursday, and his eighty-fourth day. The town sits for about forty minutes and there is nothing to sit about. A man of thirty-eight goes up the water in the Gull at about the fifth hour with a sheet of deal in a coat and does not say out loud what he is doing. A man of sixty-one asks four people whether a man of sixty-one may write a column on the back of the last leaf of his book, about four inches wide and about nine inches long, with a knife because he has not got a pen that works, and says that if nobody answers him he is going to do it anyway and say out loud that nobody answered, and waits about four minutes, and **about nine people watch a man of sixty-one cut a column into the back of a leaf, and about four of them did not answer him and nobody said no.** Then a man of thirty-one offers to be the one who goes up the water and is refused in about four sentences, and gives the reason himself before he is refused, and says it is a man of sixty-eight's reason and he would rather it came from him: **if the man who carries the board is a man nobody can be sent for about, then in about four years the board and the man are the same thing, and a record that is checked by a person it cannot reach is a record it cannot be wrong, and a record that cannot be wrong is a lid. You may read them out. You may stand nine feet off with a knife. You may carry a sheet as far as the end of that bank. You may not be the one who carries the board.** Then he comes back down and gets in at about the second hour of the afternoon and puts a board about nine inches long on the barrel without saying a word about where it had been, and the two copies are read against each other on the deck in about nine minutes, and **they agree: thirty-two lines on both of them, the same day and the same Monday on both, and it is the fourth time the two of them have been in a room together and the fourth time they have agreed, and it is about nine hours of a boat and a pole and nobody has ever once asked him to do it and nobody is going to.** And a woman of twenty-nine in the reed says the true of it in about nine sentences: the two people who make that sheet true are nine miles apart and one of them has poled nine miles twice that day, and this is the first number in about nine days that somebody is going to have to do something about, and about nine days of one man's work is what a Crown of about nine people costs.

**639 — THE CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS NEITHER A TRIUMPH NOR A CATASTROPHE, AND THE MAN WHO GAVE THE RULE SAYS HE IS WORSE OFF THAN HE WAS AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-NINE SAYS THERE IS NOBODY TO BE CROSS WITH.** `clear` 139, the fourth day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Friday, and his eighty-fifth day. The Crown is four days old, has been asked four things, and has got three of the four right and cannot get the fourth one at all: it says so itself, in about nine sentences, and says it correctly, and about four people say the request out loud anyway and about nine do not, and a man of thirty-eight says he is not going to be the man who asked twice. **Then a man of sixty-eight says, before anybody has got near it: I am worse off than I was on the Monday of the hundred and eleventh week, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be forgiven for it, and nobody is to do anything about it.** And gives the reason: a Crown of about nine people at a barrel can be asked what a thing says, and a thing at a barrel that can be asked what a thing says can be asked whether a thing is right, and **a Crown can now stop a copy by being asked whether the copy is right**, and about nine of us said in the last four days that a thing a person can be asked what it says is a lid. And then: **A Crown is a lid with a name on it, and I gave the rule that made it, and I am not going to take the rule back, because a rule that goes back the second time a person turns out to be right about a brother is not a rule, it is a mood. And I have been the instrument in that county for twenty-two years and I have never once been the person.** Then a woman of twenty-nine in the reed says hers: **there is now a thing in that town that will say what I said on a day, in four counties, in rooms I have not been to, on a day a person names, and it does not need a reason, and so I have got a worse thing than not having a reason; and there is nobody I can be cross with, because a barrel is not a person and a board is not a person and a Crown is not a person, and I have been in a chair for nine years and I know exactly what it is to have no person to be cross with.** Then a man of thirty-four speaks from the doorway of an ash where he has been for nine days without standing up, and says he is on no board and is in no book and there is a name against a chair with nobody in it, and notices that a Crown nine feet off a landing has just been in a room with a person it cannot be sent for about and that neither of them was asked anything — and a woman of twenty-six in the ash on the other side of that landing says no out loud, from about nine feet of a wall away, before a man of thirty-one has got to the end of a sentence. And at about the seventh hour a man of thirty-one sits down on the bench against the gunwale of the Gull and puts a knife in his hand and does not use it.

**640 — A CASE STILL OPEN, A FIGURE IN A MOUTH, A BOY OF THIRTEEN READING A SHEET HE WROTE, AND A QUESTION ASKED ONCE FROM A DOORWAY AND NOT ANSWERED.** `clear` 140, the fifth day of the hundred and thirteenth week, a Saturday, and his eighty-sixth day. The board is still on the barrel and the room does not fill on a Saturday, and about four of the people in it had been there on the Friday of the eleventh week and about four had not. **A boy of thirteen reads his own sheet out loud from the first line to the last in the open in about nine people's hearing**, and says the two numbers out loud before he starts — four hours to write it on the Friday and about nine minutes to read it — and says he is not going to read the thirty-first line in a hurry and is not going to read it better than it is. Then a man of thirty-eight says the fifth reading out loud because nobody asked him to: **Thursday was the fourth time the two of them have been in a room together and the fourth time they have agreed, and next Thursday is the fifth, and he is going up in the Gull at about the fifth hour with that sheet in a coat and coming down at about the second hour of the afternoon, and about four of you can come and watch and about nine will not and he is not going to ask any of you to. And about nine days of a boat is what a Crown of about nine people costs, and a man of sixty-one did that sum out loud on a Friday and a woman of twenty-nine did it again on a Thursday, and neither of them was asked to, and I am the one that pays it.** Then the case is said out loud because a case is a thing people stop saying about after a week: a man of thirty-eight asked in a yard nine miles up that water on the Monday of the eleventh week for a hearing about every record there is to be read in one room, the ones that disagree with each other especially, and **it is now the twelfth day and nobody granted it and nobody refused it and nobody has written it down.** The woman of forty-four says why it is not in her book, in four sentences: a hearing that nobody granted and nobody refused is a thing with no person on either side of it, and about four of you would be sent for about it in about four years, and she is not going to be the reason there is a form in that county that says a man may be asked why a hearing was not held. **It is still open and it is still not on anything, and about four people in that room are going to have to go on saying it out loud in about four days, because that is the only way it does not get lost, and she is not going to be the one who says it for him.** Then a man of sixty-one gives a figure with the working and says what it is a figure of: **about eleven people come up that plank in a working week and nine of them are on that board, and it is a figure of one working week at one plank and not a figure of a county and not a figure of a hundred, there being no hundred in this county, and not a figure of a year — and a year in this county is the time between one crossing of a block of limestone nine miles up this river and the next one after it, and I do not know when that is, and neither does he, and neither does anybody in this room.** Then a man of thirty-one starts to answer a question that has not been asked and a man of sixty-eight stops him: *hold it, you have not got the question yet and you are about to answer the one before it.* And a man of thirty-four says it from the doorway of the ash, once, and does not come out, and says it is not about him and not about the man of thirty-eight who said four words in a yard and not about the woman of thirty who went four days up the water, and then asks it: **if a Crown speaks for a person, is that person owed a copy of what it said about them.** About nine people in that room heard the question and about four of them understood all of it, and nobody in that room answered it, and a man of thirty-eight is going up the water again on Thursday with a sheet of deal in a coat.
