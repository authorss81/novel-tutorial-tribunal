THE FAMINE DID NOT HAPPEN: fifty-four acres of hay came off ground that is in season in that hundred — about ten off the low meadow, of which about six were cut in a fortnight and about four took five passes and are standing in a field in the weather and are a hay of the following year and are NAMED AS NOT A HAY on the settlement; and about forty-four off long grazing four miles south in its sixth year with about nine acres of new sward in it, and THE FORTY-FOUR ARE NOT GIVEN TO ANYBODY, THEY ARE ASKED FOR. THE FIFTY-EIGHT HAVE A PLACE: about eight hundred acres of common that belongs to nobody and two hundred and twenty feet of the top of a rise about nine hundred yards long and about eleven feet wide, which is eleven tents, and fifty-eight people on it, and THERE IS NO LICENCE AND THERE IS NO FORM AND THERE IS NO COLUMN AND NO DAY ON IT, and a foot of water went over the low side of that swell on the second day after the rising morning and two tents were standing at the ninth hour on that day and nobody was drowned and it will do it again. AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE IS PARTIALLY ANCHORED: ONE YEAR OF HIS FUTURE OPTIONS FROM THE CROSSING OF THE TALLY STONE TO THE CROSSING AFTER IT, AND FROM THAT MORNING A PERMANENT COUNT FROM THE WATER WITH NO FIGURE FOR IT AND NOBODY ABLE TO CHECK IT. **591 — The last reading before the settlement, and a wall that cannot carry a man who has not said anything.** `clear` 91, the fifth day of the hundred and sixth week, a Saturday, the reading at the sixth hour. Toby Henshaw, fourteen, holds the slate for the second time in his life and asks for nothing this time; **he reads it in nine minutes and gets nothing wrong, and tells about four people to say so out loud in about four seconds.** A water-clerk of fifty-eight says out loud, before the boy begins, that there is going to be a settlement, that no word of it is written anywhere, and that he cannot say what it is because a settlement is not a season and his sheet has three columns and no column for it. Nine parcels stand on the list and eight of them have a man who pays the rate and **one of them cannot be corrected at all, because the line it describes is a line nobody holds, and nothing can be taken off a wall with a rag, because there is nothing on it to take the rag to.** The sixth of six challenges still has nothing under the day, and a man of about thirty-four with a barrow and a stroke beside his name in chalk says out loud that he stood in the two acres behind the Green Paw before the light on Saturday and there is a ditch along the top side of them with about a foot of sallow in it and the water stops dead where the sallow begins, and the woman those two acres belong to says out loud that a man standing in a field is not a parcel. **Ilyan's mistake: he says four things at a stone step in about nine seconds — hay, a place for the fifty-eight, three boards, the ditch — and about nine mouths in that hundred have them by the fourth hour, and the water-clerk says out loud that four sentences are not a settlement and that there is no form in the county of Kell that turns four sentences into a thing a person has done anything about. AND WHEN HE PROPOSES THAT THE SETTLEMENT GO ON THE WALL, THE CLERK REFUSES HIM IN THE OPEN: that wall binds nobody, and a settlement on it would be read by about nine people every morning of a year, and about two hundred and ninety-one people in that town would have it and could not afterwards be asked about it.** Closing: the thing the wall cannot carry is a man who has not said anything at all, and about nine people were standing in that street and about four will be able to say tomorrow what the man in the middle of them said.

**592 — No room and no slate and no bar, and five passes and not four.** `clear` 92, the sixth day of the hundred and sixth week, a Sunday. No room, no slate, no bar, and a man of thirty-one walks four miles out with a bad leg over a plank bridge that is crossable at the seventh hour. **He goes to get one thing off a man's own mouth and gets it in about nine sentences: the four standing acres took FIVE passes and not four, and the fifth was the one that was not a hay, and it is in the top corner of that field in rows and there is no barn in that hundred that takes wet hay.** A woman of about seventy asked on Friday where the hay would go and is told at last: nowhere. A man of about sixty with a shepherd's crook points out that the middle of that field is not mown, gives no reason, and says out loud that he has been in that hundred forty years and has never seen four passes in one field in one year and that it is worth nothing. **A man of about thirty-four with a barrow, nine acres and no seat, says out loud to about nine people on that meadow that two loads of wet hay went into his barn on that lane and that he has never told anybody and that a load of wet hay in his barn is the only one of the three things about him that he has got.** Ilyan's mistake, the third in a week: he gives a figure away on a headland before the man who mown it can say it, is told the true of it, and then says out loud in nine inches of water that he is not going to take the next number out of anybody's hand, which leaves about four people in that hundred carrying a wrong shape with no one to correct it. Closing: a cart standing at a gate in about nine inches of water, and it is going to be standing there on Monday, and there is no form in this county that says it may not.

**593 — The first move of the settlement, and it is made by somebody other than him, and it is wrong.** `clear` 93, the seventh and last day of the hundred and sixth week, a Monday; the morrow is the first of the hundred and seventh. A woman of twenty-eight with a daughter of six on her hip and a boy of nineteen with a pole put **eleven tents on the top of a rise about nine hundred yards long and about eleven feet wide before the light, and nobody in that hundred had asked her to, and there is no form in the county of Kell that says she may or may not.** They stand along two hundred and twenty feet, and the figure is on that wall in a square, and a man of fifty-eight says out loud that he wrote it on the rising morning and said out loud before he wrote it that it was a figure about a width of ground and a number of tents and not about people. **THE FIRST THING THE SETTLEMENT GOT WRONG IS THAT A FIGURE OF TENTS WAS USED AS A PLACE, AND THE WOMAN WHO USED IT SAYS SO OUT LOUD HERSELF, BEFORE ANYBODY ELSE GETS NEAR IT: twenty feet is a tent and a fire and a churn and a line, and it was never a figure for fifty-eight people, and she will say it again on a Friday in front of about three hundred people if anybody writes it down as though it were.** Ilyan's mistake, and the worst version of the habit he has: he asks a room for a number of feet that fifty-eight people need, so that anybody can walk it with a line in about four seconds, and is told on the ground by a person who has stood on that rise twice that nobody in that hundred knows it and that in about four days about nine mouths will be measuring children. **Nothing comes down.** Four hands go into a sheet about four feet wide in a room over a stable with nothing at the top of it, one of them a woman of about fifty who keeps four beds who writes and puts no name at the foot of it. Closing: two numbers on a sheet, not the same number, nobody told which is truer, and about nine people in that hundred read both over two days and get them the wrong way round.

**594 — THE FIFTY-EIGHT, AND A PLACE NOBODY CAN POINT AT.** `clear` 94, the first day of the hundred and seventh week, a Tuesday; the day before was the seventh and last of the hundred and sixth. About nine people in a room over a stable. A water-clerk of fifty-eight says out loud that **there is no form in the county of Kell that gives anything to anybody, that there is no form that renews a licence or takes one away and no form that puts a person on a piece of ground, and that there has never been one.** Ilyan's mistake: he says out loud in about nine people's hearing that a settlement cannot give fifty-eight people a place, which is the true of it and only half of it, and is told in the same hour that he has said it before anybody has found out what there is instead of it, and that about four of those four will say it at the bar on Friday. **WHAT THE SETTLEMENT GIVES THEM INSTEAD: A FIGURE, A DAY, AND A THING A PERSON CAN GO AND LOOK AT — two hundred and twenty feet of the top of a rise that belongs to nobody, eleven tents, fifty-eight people and no person in that county may add to that number or take from it, about eight hundred acres round it, the day it was written, and one line that a foot of water went over the low side of that swell on the second day after the rising morning and two tents were standing at the ninth hour on that day and nobody was drowned.** A woman of twenty-eight says out loud that there is no licence and never has been one in this county, gives the twenty-eight days with the subtraction, and says the sack is not on that ground and not in that town and there is no copy of it in the county and the man who writes on the wall has never seen it. Hester Nunn, forty-one, puts six acres down with no name at the top of it and no name at the bottom of it and gives the reason she has now given three times. Closing: a woman with her arm out along two hundred and twenty feet of a rise, and there is nothing at the end of her arm, and that is the whole of the place.

**595 — THE BOARDS, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR ASKS TO BE ASKED.** `clear` 95, the second of the hundred and seventh week, a Wednesday. A man of fifty-four comes four miles on his own legs into the room over a stable, the way he came into a weigh-house on the rising with no seat in it. Twenty-nine days in the carrying. **A woman of twenty-eight says in about nine seconds that nothing is standing on that meadow, and is told in front of all of them, by the man she said it to, that she has just given him a way out and did not know she was doing it and would have said it the same way on Friday.** He says for the third time that he is not saying no, and says he has had thirty days to find a way to say no that he could live with and has not found one, and that he is not going to be the man with three boards in a yard for thirty days who says he is not saying no, because about nine people in that hundred have that sentence now and it has stopped meaning anything at all. **AND THEN HE SAYS THE THING THE WHOLE OF THE FIFTY-FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTY MINUTES OF THIS CHAPTER IS BUILT ON: THAT HE IS ASKING TO BE ASKED — not as a favour, not as a thing anybody owes him, not on a bar — and that any person in that hundred may go and ask him on his own ground, and he may say yes and he may say no, and if he says no nobody in that hundred is to be blame, and there is no form in that county that makes anybody ask.** About nine people go down to the six-board gate and the boards are read in the open by three people, and the true of them is that three boards hold a level and that nobody in that hundred knows what they hold off, and a man of fifty-one and a man of fifty-four put their hands on the same gate in the same nine people's hearing and say the same two true things and do not agree. **THE SETTLEMENT CARRIES THE DISAGREEMENT AND CARRIES WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT — four hours at a full gate with a rod on each side, a hand on the two lines on the timber, a hand in the gap under the sill — AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED TO PAY FOR FOUR HOURS OF A RIVER.** Closing: three boards read at a gate, in the open, by three people.

**596 — THE COST, PAID, AND IT IS SPOKEN IN SOMEONE ELSE'S MOUTH AND THEN IN HIS OWN.** `clear` 96, the third of the hundred and seventh week, a Thursday; about one System panel in fifty chapters and it is this one. The question on the Thursday morning is not what went on the sheet; it is what a man has to give up so that about nine people in a hundred can put a thumb on what he did and check it. **A woman of about fifty who keeps four beds and has never given him her name prices a year of him out loud in about nine people's hearing with the working spoken in nine sentences, and the figure is four pounds eleven shillings and threepence, and it is hers and not his.** Three pence a day because he eats three and not two; three hundred and sixty-five days because a year is the time between one crossing of that block of limestone and the next; a thousand and ninety-five pence; four pounds is nine hundred and sixty of them and a hundred and thirty-five over, which is eleven shillings and threepence. **The other half of the price is one, being one morning, one room and one seat in about thirty, from a water-clerk who has no figure for it in shillings and will not invent one in his own building.** Then he puts down the price himself, out loud: **one year of his own future options, from the crossing of the tally stone to the crossing after it, and there is no form in that county that makes him and none that stops him, and no form anywhere that suspends it or enforces it, and about nine people in that hundred believed it when he said it and about nine did not and the arithmetic is out of the room.** And the second half, which is not a figure and which nobody can ask him about: **from that morning he counts from the water, from the day the water comes over that cut to the day it comes over it again, and every morning in between he knows where that river is without being asked and without looking, and there is no form anywhere in any county that stops a man counting from a river, and he would not take a figure for it if there was one.** A person checks it at a block of limestone with a line cut round it and nowhere else. The woman who priced him says what he bought: **the right to be wrong in front of about nine people and to be asked about it on a Saturday morning for a year, and that it is not a place, there is no column for it, no form that renews it, no rate on it, and that a man who pays it is not one of about thirty men and is not anybody's and is not nobody's either.** Closing: a person checks it at a block of limestone with a line cut round it and nowhere else.

**597 — The settlement in the open, read by somebody who is not its author, to about three hundred people.** `clear` 97, the fourth of the hundred and seventh week, a Friday; **the fifth market held since the rising**, about three hundred people on the green, about nine of them having been somewhere and seen something and about two hundred and ninety-one not having been anywhere. The remedy of about three hundred words in one hand has lain under the oak bar for about four weeks. **THE SETTLEMENT IS ABOUT NINE HUNDRED WORDS IN SIX HANDS WITH NOTHING AT THE TOP OF IT, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO INSTRUMENTS IS NOT A SIZE ON A PAGE: three hundred words in one hand is worth about four sentences to about thirty people at a step, about nine hundred words in six hands is worth about one reading to about three hundred, and a thing that is worth one reading is not worth a market.** It is read aloud at a stone step in about nineteen minutes by **a woman of forty-one who lost six acres in a market, refused her name to a stranger twice, did not write one word of it, and was asked at `590` in front of about three hundred people whether she would put her name on a piece of paper to take six acres back and did not say no.** The whole of it is on the page in the chapter and it carries: the hay, and that the forty-four are asked for and not given; the place, and that two numbers are not the same number; three boards, and that the settlement does not touch them and that what they hold off is not known; the ditch and the rate and the empty square; the wall and its no column for a person; and a man of thirty-one's year. **A man with a barrow and a stroke beside his name in chalk asks at the back of the step where a load of wet hay in his own barn goes, and it goes in at the end of the hay, with the day and the number of loads and no name of the yard, because a settlement is not a promise, it is a list of what a person could not do and did anyway, and anybody in that hundred may walk down that lane and look at the barn.** Closing: about nine of those three hundred had been somewhere and seen something this week, and about two hundred and ninety-one cannot be asked about any of it, now or ever.

**598 — THE LAST READING, AND A DOCUMENT ON A WALL IS NOT AN ANSWER.** `clear` 98, the fifth of the hundred and seventh week, a Saturday, the reading at the sixth hour; the morrow is the sixth day and the day after is the last day of the week. A boy of fourteen holds the slate for the third time in his life. **BEFORE HE BEGINS, A WATER-CLERK OF FIFTY-EIGHT SAYS OUT LOUD THAT HE DID NOT WRITE THE SETTLEMENT ON THAT WALL, that he has been nineteen years the man whose hand that is and that he knows it, and that there is no form in this county that says what a person may do about a thing they find on a wall they keep** — and about nine people in that hundred had it and about nine had not. The boy reads two men long of parchment in about twenty-six minutes, gets one number wrong, and reads a settlement off the front of it that is in six hands and binds nobody. Fifty-eight in the taking column for the fifth week running with nothing in the square. One of the nine parcels cannot be corrected. Two hundred and thirty little figures going left out of the squares are read as a run of them with the boy saying so out loud before he starts. **AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS OUT LOUD, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE'S HEARING, THAT A DOCUMENT IS NOT AN ANSWER BECAUSE IT IS ON A WALL, and names what has been on that wall for about two hundred and thirty years: a wrong parcel a boy took a rag to in about four seconds, a line about a woman of thirty-eight who has never had a crop of hay in her life, a number of feet a man of fifty-eight wrote knowing it was useless, and an empty square nobody can put a rag to because there is nothing on it — and about nine of them heard that and about nine did not.** Closing: nobody in that hundred knows what anybody stands under that lamp after the day after that.

**599 — Sunday, no room, no slate, no bar, and about nine people have both and about nine have neither.** `clear` 99, the sixth of the hundred and seventh week, a Sunday; the day after is the seventh and last day of that week. There is one fire in one parlour in that town and about nine people are in it at about the eleventh hour. **A woman of twenty-eight says out loud that the first move of a settlement cost her four miles a day for five days and a licence that had lapsed twenty-eight days before.** A man of fifty-four comes four miles into the parlour and says out loud that **he said yes on Friday afternoon, on his own ground, to about four people and not to nine, and that his hay is in because he said it was, and that nobody in that hundred may now say he was made to say it.** A man of thirty-one says his own half out loud and is not the carrier of it, and about four people have it and about nine do not. **AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY SAYS OUT LOUD THE WHOLE QUESTION, the one a man of thirty-four from another county asked in a chalk town nine weeks ago: whether a person of the record and not of the place can become a person of the place without being taken into the shape of the thing the place tells about itself.** It has now been asked three times in about nine weeks in two counties and has not been answered once, and the answer given out loud by the person who asked it is that she does not think either a wall a man keeps or a settlement nobody keeps can answer it. Closing: about nine people have both of those two things, about nine have neither, and the two nines have not asked each other.

**600 — THE SETTLEMENT LANDS, AND NONE OF IT IS A COMFORT AND NONE OF IT IS A FORM, AND A NAME IS SPOKEN AND IS NOT ANSWERED.** `clear` 100, the seventh and last day of the hundred and seventh week, a Monday; **the last day of the volume.** About nine people at that wall at the sixth hour, because about nine people stand there every morning and there is no form that says why and there has not been one for about two hundred and thirty years. The water was a thumbnail's width under the cut and thirteen days. **THE THREE RESOLUTIONS ARE STATED AT THE TOP OF THIS CHAPTER AND NONE OF THEM IS A COMFORT: THE FAMINE DID NOT HAPPEN; THE FIFTY-EIGHT HAVE TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY FEET OF A RISE THAT BELONGS TO NOBODY AND THE LOW SIDE OF THAT SWELL WENT UNDER FOUR INCHES OF WATER ONCE AND WILL AGAIN; A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE HAS A YEAR AND A RIVER AND NEITHER IS A THING ANY PERSON IN THAT HUNDRED IS BOUND BY. NOBODY WAS RELIEVED, FORGIVEN, REDEEMED OR THANKED, AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM HAD COME TO FIND OUT WHETHER ONE OF THOSE WAS GOING TO HAPPEN.** About nine hands went onto the back of the settlement over the weekend and **at the bottom of the back, under eight hands, there is a mark that is two letters and a number and nothing else, in a hand no person in that county makes.** A man of thirty-one says out loud what it is — a mark a woman called Nadia Kestrel signed at the bottom of about nine hundred records in a country that is not this one, who is not in this county and has never been seen by anybody in that room — and about four people had known it on sight and about nine had not. **THEN A VOICE THAT WAS NOT IN A MOUTH SAYS TWO SENTENCES IN THAT ROOM AND IT NAMES ITSELF, and it is not addressed to anybody, it asks for nothing, it names no person, and about four people heard it and about nine heard nothing at all and thought for about nine seconds that it had been the wind in the crane-beam.** The four words it said were its own name and they are said once in fifty chapters. He cannot answer when he is asked, and says in about four seconds that he has been unable to for about forty-six days and for every day before those, and gives the checkable figure rather than the one off a river. **AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT ASKS, OUT LOUD, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, WHAT IT CALLS ITSELF. About four seconds went past and then nothing, and the water came off the cut in the tally stone about nine feet off, and nobody in that hundred is bound by a word of any of it.** Closing: about nine people are going to walk out across a plank bridge at the seventh hour tomorrow and look at a rise with fifty-eight people on two hundred and twenty feet of it; about nine are going to go to a gate with three boards in it and find out nothing; about nine are going to write the back of that settlement out again in their own words and have it be a different document. **There is no form anywhere in the county of Kell that says they may not.**
