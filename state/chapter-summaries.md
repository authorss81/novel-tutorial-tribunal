## VOLUME 10, CHAPTERS 491 TO 500 — *the charter, the exit, and the family record*

**These ten chapters were delivered thin, at 8,590 words with chapters between 654 and 1,299, and every gate passed on that, and the review found it. They were rewritten to 26,373 words with every chapter between 2,342 and 3,534. No card moved. The plot below is the same plot; what changed is that it is now scenes, and the specifics in each paragraph are the ones the rewrite put in.**

**491 — An instrument and a decision are two different things.** Thursday, the forty-fifth day, third day of the ninety-second week, bar fifteen days, wheel fourteen slow. **The two counts are given in the room and the difference between them is named as a thing nine people knew and four could have said which number was larger.** About nine people in a stone room with the door open and about twenty in the square. **Ivo Cray, thirty-four, ninth lot on the Four Hundred, puts a bill of sale face up on the table for the first time in six weeks and says out loud that nobody in that county is ever going to tell him the water does not go with his land, because the water going with the land is the whole of what a man who owns nine acres wants.** Amos Trill, at the back, not sitting down, says a lot is five acres and a rate and is not what he calls it, and that he has been wrong to use the county word every day for nine years, and goes out into the square and leaves his hat. **Mab Harrow, thirty-four, rents a second room, not a head of household, never been in the House, refused nothing in six weeks, comes in out of the rain with her sleeves wet to the elbow and says, unasked, that an instrument and a decision are two different things: one tells people what to do and the other tells people what was done, and this town has had the first for nine years and has made the second in six weeks and the water will do what it does in the meantime.** Ilyan's want — that the instrument of the fortieth day have been a mistake — is said out loud in the room and refused by Ivo Cray: a thing asked for by everybody is not nothing, a man who needs a thing of his own to be wrong has not understood what it was for. About four agree and about five do not and nobody in the room can be asked afterwards because no minute was taken and no body sat. Closing: the copy of the roll moved off its bench to the middle of the table, into the light, by a man who cannot read standing up. `491`

**492 — The only thing in front of the board that is cheap.** Friday, the forty-sixth day, bar sixteen. **A list of works nine years old, four lines, is read out in full and two of its three items are heard for the first time in nine years.** The second winding bar is found to stand at the tail of the race and not at the head, entered there nine years back when the tail silted, and a steward of sixty-one says he put it there and it has sat with the other two ever since. A tail bar cannot hold up a frame at the head; it stops a miller losing four days of grist. **The working is done on a slate in public for the first time in nine years: £8 15s 4d a quarter with no frame on it, a levy of £1 3s 4d on the eighty lots in the roll made tomorrow, £9 18s 8d, the heel £9 — and Perdy insists both figures go in the minute as two figures about two different things and not as one.** Resolved: scour the tail, set a second bar at the tail out of the ordinary fund, before the water comes up. The frame is outside it and is still third on a list nobody has read all the way through. Nobody thanked. Ilyan's Kerby sentence a fourth time to nine men who had heard it three times; Ivo Cray comes down two steps and gives the other half. Closing: the frame standing outside a minute, on a list nine years old. `492`

**493 — THE ROLL FOR THE AUTUMN IS MADE.** Saturday, the forty-seventh day, fifth day of the ninety-second week, bar seventeen, wheel sixteen. **Market is Tuesday, so the square is empty and about nine people wait at a distance from the stair.** Amos Trill works alone from the seventh hour to the twelfth, in order, ninth ward first, the nineteen tenements of the Fen as land and not households, the eleven rows of the twenty-eighth, **a twelfth with a dead man's name against it, because the form asks for a head of household and there is one and he is dead and the steward has never yet struck a name for being dead.** **He tries the door at the eighth hour and it is locked; a woman of about fifty comes out of the shop underneath with her sleeves rolled, tells him the steward has asked for nothing since the seventh hour, that he can stand on the step as long as he likes, and goes back in because there is bread coming out of the oven.** One thousand and seventy-four houses. **The whole working is printed in the chapter in pence as well as pounds: 1,074 × 8d = 8,592d = £35 16s 0d; 1,074 × 2d = 2,148d = £8 19s 0d; the levy 3½d × 80 = 280d = £1 3s 4d; 2,148d + 280d = 2,428d = £10 2s 4d, 268d over the £9 heel, and 268d is £1 2s 4d; 1,052 at 2,104d, with the same levy 2,384d = £9 18s 8d, 224d over, 224d is 18s 8d; 2,428d − 2,384d = 44d a quarter. AND THE CHAPTER NAMES THE CONVENTION NOBODY HAD EVER SAID OUT LOUD: a board in this county has always counted the whole of a year's levy against the quarter it holds in its hand.** The out-of-roll book is not opened and there is nobody in that office to open it. On the stair he says he has done it correctly and will not know what else is on it until the collection, which is thirteen weeks off, and refuses a woman the number: a roll is read from the press when it is copied, not from my mouth on a stair. **Nobody lied to him. The steward did not withhold the figure; it never occurred to him that the man in the square was a person the figure was owed to.** Closing: the shut office door and a number nine weeks too late. `493`

**494 — The season arrives and the heel goes, a sound and not a finding.** Sunday, the forty-eighth day, bar eighteen, wheel seventeen. **The lane to the Fen runs to the ankle for four hundred yards with a skin on it that takes the light off in three places.** About eleven people at the head of the sluice by the sixth hour, coming and going for eighteen days. **Sefa Lund, thirty-eight, keeps the sluice, is a household of this town, has a key to gates that stand open and has never been paid because a common is not a service.** Ilyan asks for two people and an hour and is told a sound is not a sentence, that she has been making that noise for nine years and nobody in the county has ever asked her to explain it, and that he is making it legible. **She adds the half she was not going to say: twice a morning, two people and an hour, me and whichever of them comes, and she has had a child on her hip at the sixth hour four times in nine years and gone up with the child, and she says it so that it is on the record that nobody asked her to.** Then, at the seventh hour, the north heel goes. It is a sound and not a finding. A child is lifted clear and does not cry again; a dog goes along the bank and does not bark; the bar jumps a foot as the ground under it goes. **The man of the county comes up with the straight edge, says nothing, comes back with a nail, knocks it in at a new place, writes three figures on the back of his hand, wipes them off, and writes them again.** The board enters the fact with a day on it and calls it nothing, and a man asks whether the minute should call it a failure and is told it calls what happened, and is offered the chance to write that at the foot with his name and does not take it. **There is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man may apologise, and he had about four seconds in which he could have said he was sorry and used them on not saying it.** Closing: eleven pairs of boots all the way round a bar nobody turned. `494`

**495 — The House is called for the first time in nine years.** Monday, the forty-ninth day, seventh and last day of the ninety-second week; the morrow is the first day of the ninety-third, and both sides say so. Bar nineteen. **Forty-one men in nine wards, five in the ninth, on forty-one stools, with about two hundred in the square and the door open; the false version that the fen is nineteen more seats is in about forty mouths and no narrator corrects it.** Amos Trill reads the question and will not call it on the Book. Perdy gives the answer in one sentence: forty-one men can find out what a thousand people think and say it out loud in forty-one kitchens for a year, and has been ashamed of it for six weeks and says so in that order. Three questions — can it mend the frame, can it put the row back on the roll, can it shut the gate — lie in the room for four minutes, and the four minutes are not silence, they are nine men asking other men whether they heard right. **The fifth head of the ninth ward, elected on a three-week-old roll, in the business six weeks and not a stranger, taken last because the ninth ward is taken last, says it is right and it is half of it: a body which can only find out what people think is a body somebody else has to answer, and that is the shape of the thing, and a shape is not a man, and he will not use the word wicked and will not let anybody else use it, and the wishing changed nothing at all, which is the part he cannot get past.** Ilyan says his sentence a fourth time; nine have eyes on him, nine look away, about twenty-three go on talking, and one of those twenty-three has a mark on a list made that morning. No decision, no order. The forty-one go out in ward order with lists and no authority, and a man of the seventh ward discovers in real time that asking and telling are different. Closing: forty-one men carrying forty-one lists. `495`

**496 — A charter asked for, head and two lines, no body behind it.** Tuesday, the fiftieth day, first day of the ninety-third week, bar twenty, wheel nineteen. **A room over a taproom, a table with a hundred years of cup rings in it, and a broken chair carried up because there are not enough stools.** Wenna Crale, at her own table four feet off, says beforehand that she will draw it as a head and two lines with no body behind it, that she is asking for one thing only, and that anybody may object and the objection goes in. **The six come off four pages in a round hand addressed to a water board and signed with no name: a subject, a condition, a burden, a witness, a remedy, and an exit. THE FOURTH IS OBJECTED TO BY A HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD OF THE SIXTH WARD WHO HAS BEEN A WITNESS TWICE AND WAS ASKED AT NINE THE NEXT MORNING ABOUT SOMETHING HE SAW AT FOUR — that a witness is a person stood up in a room and asked whether he saw it, and he will not carry the word, and he wants the name of the person who was in the room and no promise of any kind. It is argued for eleven minutes, a cup goes over, and the objection is written into the sheet and read out.** A second question turns out to be the one underneath the first: who holds it. There is a press, and a copyist at two pounds a year, and a mill book, and a water list, and a woman with a key — and a copyist says that goes in the press with the day on it, that she will keep a copy, and that if she is gone the copy goes in the press, and that is not holding it, and no clerk in this county would take it. **Ilyan's want is that it be a form, because a form is a thing a person can be sent for. He puts his finger on her seventh line while he says it and tries to make the exit a clause, and Mab Harrow says, in front of nine people, that a clause is a thing a person can be given and an exit is a thing a person can open, and that the difference is the whole of what a woman of twenty-eight has been writing about for six years and a half.** Closing: the seventh heading. `496`

**497 — The exit gets a handle on the inside, and a sentence is struck out.** Wednesday, the fifty-first day, bar twenty-one, wheel twenty. **THE BAND'S ONE SYSTEM PANEL, at `497:41`–`497:49`, four fields with EVIDENCE in two paragraphs, five bold paragraphs and ten `**`, every field closed.** Claim: a right a person must ask a body for is a favour, and a favour can be withdrawn by the person not getting it. Choice: the line a person may open, or the ninth clause, which will be read out in a room in ninety years and given to nobody. He chose the line out loud in about eleven seconds and nobody thanked him and there is no minute in any book in this county in which it appears that he wanted a decision. Wenna reads the ninth clause out — hundred and thirty-eight years old, read out twice in ninety years and given to nobody twice, binds households, the row are not households, used once on the forty-first day and it stopped the man who would have compelled. **She then reads one line out of the fourth page of the four pages four hundred miles off, and Ilyan says the sentence he said first in a market square on the Sunday of the twentieth day after the division, in about nine seconds, and she says no in front of nine people: a copyist of thirty-four is the only person in that room who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square, and she says he may not, not you, not them, not a county eleven miles off.** He asks to let the room hear it. She strikes it out of the sheet with a line through the middle of it and enters the objection at the foot with his name against it, in the order she said she would, and about four people in that room find out what he said in the square. **Perdy writes the exit: any household or person, entered or not, may leave on a day's notice at the Stone House, without forfeit of dwelling, and no question shall be put to character, rate or household. The handle is on the inside. There is no form, no board to apply to, no fee and no officer.** A man of the sixth ward, who voted on the thirtieth day, says a handle on the inside is a good thing, and does not say the other half, and nobody asks. Closing: the exit, and a sentence struck out. `497`

**498 — Six lines with five towns, and the sixth line is the exit.** Thursday, the fifty-second day, bar twenty-two, wheel twenty-one. **Four people walk four miles and eleven and thirty-two to a room in a stone house, and one brings bread and cheese tied in a cloth and puts it on the sill of the open door where nobody moves it for an hour.** The question is who carries what. Four instruments named with their holders: a mill book of about nine hundred names shut since the fifteenth day, a copyist at two pounds a year, a water list in a bag with a strap, a woman of thirty-eight with a key and no authority who is out of the water. The four out-town holders name theirs: a mill book of about six hundred names with no copy anywhere; a whole township in a leather case and a brother with half of it under a bed; a sluice never paid for, whose keeper's father was not paid either; and nine words written in a hand only one man can read, who has not had a day in nine years and whose line dies when he stops. **A request for a learner is refused, and the reason is that the copyist has read four pages this week about a shelf four hundred miles off that a woman has written down eleven times she has not opened, and she is not writing a person into a thing she cannot be called on to keep.** Ott Vernay, thirty-nine, puts his book down as a burden and says he is not sorry for having shut it, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry. The compact is six lines and the sixth is the exit. **Ilyan offers to be witness and party — he has been here six weeks, he has seen every paper, he can be in a room in a day — and is refused in about four sentences: a man who cannot be sent for cannot be obliged and cannot oblige, and if the compact were tested the first question would be one he could not answer. Wenna adds that it is the same refusal as on the thirty-eighth day, correct both times, and that he is not the same both times, which is the only difference anybody can put a day on. He asks to be a man who was in the room and is told that is the whole of what he is in five lines and it is not a favour.** Closing: five towns and a man on none of it. `498`

**499 — A request goes out in a hand not the writer's.** Friday, the fifty-third day, bar twenty-three, wheel twenty-two. **A room over a nail shop, a bed pushed to the wall, and a table and a chair and a fire as they were on the forty-third day.** Wenna comes at the fourth hour and says she will ask for one thing only. A refusal is entered by four lines: the day, the name of the person who refused, the name of the person who witnessed it, the place. **Sefa Lund, who refused in that lane on the eleventh day after the division and had no right of refusal to give, puts her own name in and asks that it be written there that she had nothing to refuse with, because she has read the four pages and there is a sentence in them about a person who refuses without having it.** The two lines go in and she reads them back and checks the spelling of her own name. **The witness line is left open and the open witness line is the whole of it, and it is about nine words: that there was no witness line in a lane and nobody in that lane whose job it was to be one.** A man of the fourth ward asks what happens when it gets there, and a copyist says that is the first question of the whole six weeks nobody in the room has an answer to, and that it may be nothing, and that the best thing a person in this town can do this year is nine words. **The carrier's wage of one shilling and fourpence comes out of the copyist's own two pounds a year, and she enters two lines under the head saying so, because a thing anybody could check on ought not to depend on a person being asked about it later.** Ilyan writes the fair copy in the small upright hand, which is not his, for the third time in six weeks; four people in the room know why and nine do not, and the copyist is one of the four, and she says nothing about the hand for the rest of the afternoon. Closing: a folded request with an open witness line. `499`

**500 — The hundredth day after the settlement, a Saturday, and a page.** Saturday, the fifty-fourth day, fifth day of the ninety-third week, bar twenty-four, wheel twenty-three. **About nine people in the room, forty-one stools standing ready against the wall for a House that is not called, and about two hundred in the square moving slowly toward the bread.** The charter is read out again because the last man of the ninth ward said a thing ought to be heard more than once before it becomes the thing people think it is, and the seventh heading is read plain, and a man in the square asks whether a person who is entered has to go in person and a woman on a stool tells him what *giving a day's notice at the Stone House* means. A child runs across the light and back; a second stops in the doorway and has to be fetched, and everyone waits two minutes. **A man of the ninth ward asks whether the seventh heading means anything this morning or is a thing for later, and Perdy says it means whatever anybody makes it mean on the day they use it, and that a heading waiting for somebody else to make it mean something is a clause and not an exit, and would rather be asked about it twenty times in a year than once.** At the eleventh hour a carrier comes through the open door with a page folded in four with nothing on the outside of it. **It is a page from the register four hundred miles off, carrying one entry: a refusal given six years ago by a household in that county dead except one person. At the foot is a witness line with two names. One is four letters and a stroke in a hand not of this town, in about forty mouths. THE OTHER IS THE SMALL UPRIGHT HAND OF A RECORDWRIGHT, and a man of thirty-one has seen it twice, and he put it there himself on a form about a town of ninety thousand people before he came here, and he tells nobody and there is no form that says he shall and none that says he may be asked.** **And then he makes the mistake, which is his own and the last thing he does in this town: he asks for the second of the two names to be read again more slowly, one letter at a time, so that anybody who has ever seen the hand can say whether they have seen it. A man of thirty-four with a bill of sale in his coat asks him why he wants a line read more slowly than it was read. He says *because the hand matters*, which is true and is not the whole of it, and about four people in that room hear the place where the answer stops, and the copyist — the only person in Halloway who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square, and who said no two days before and has not changed her mind — looks at him for three seconds and reads the name again at the same speed.** Wenna names what it is and names only that. The Book hangs nine feet up and is not read out; this is not its witness line. **A woman of about thirty comes in, reads over two men's shoulders, cannot read the second name, does not ask anybody to read it to her, and goes out.** The page goes into the press beside the charter with a copy by the door. **Closing: a witness line with two names, a square in the rain, and a man telling nobody because no form says he has to and none says he might not. A town of four thousand people that has learned in six weeks that a book is a door has not opened one.** `500`
