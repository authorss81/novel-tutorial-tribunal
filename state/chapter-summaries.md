## Volume 21 — *The Name Already On It* (1001–1050)

### Band 5 — `1041`–`1050`, *The thing it is owed* (complete)

- **`1041` · Said Before Wrong · 170/7, Monday · 16 holds, about 60 under, one day.** Wenna Pyle says what the wood is and says she is confirming a name and not giving a thing, settling nothing. Garrin Tolley asks her for half a day at her bench and is refused with the reason that the bench holds a single worker. Ilyan says her saying finishes nothing and that he was wrong to want it to. Ends on a cup set upright for her to find.
- **`1042` · Stone Gone Quiet · 171/1, Tuesday, off-morning · none.** Week turned between `1041` and this. Sena Dorr is at the wall at the bottom of the road from the light, comes no further off it, and asks Ilyan for his account once he is up on the steps; he refuses, hands her own working back, and owns the glad part of refusing her as unkept. Ends on a palm lifted off warmed stone.
- **`1043` · Sawdust On Boots · 171/2, Wednesday · 4, about 90, seven days.** Wat Marshe asks Wenna to say again what the wood is and is refused with the reason she will not carry it round handing it out. He owns that knowing would not teach his carrying. Ends on wet rings drying where his pails stood.
- **`1044` · Asked Downhill · 171/3, Thursday · 8, about 80, six days.** Garrin Tolley asks Corvin Tace for leave to stand with the lime-woman and is refused with the reason of sight and sweeping. He owns he wanted to be the man who went. Ends on a hand held over a board to still it.
- **`1045` · Water Over Ankles · 171/4, Friday · 12, about 70, five days.** Orla Fennimore asks whether the chair is hers and is refused with the working about doors and voices. She will stop asking. Ilyan owns he wanted her to have it to make the landing easier. Ends on her standing at her wall past going.
- **`1046` · Asked At The Wall · 171/5, Saturday, full bank and anchor · 16 holds, about 60, four days.** Ilyan asks Barnaby Crove whether the barrow that wood was cut for is the barrow that comes up that bank. Barnaby refuses in his own words and is told neither wrong nor right. Ilyan opens his mouth to shape it and does not. Ends on hands kept at sides down a flight.
- **`1047` · After The Asking · 171/6, Sunday · 16 holds, about 60, three days.** Ilyan asks Barnaby for leave to explain the asking and is refused with the reason explaining is another thing to carry. He owns the explaining-want as a second mistake. Ends on hands washed before taking shafts.
- **`1048` · What Use Is Not · 171/7, Monday · 16 holds, about 60, two days.** Orla Fennimore says from the wall first that it does not answer her question, then that a thing made for a use is not the user's. Sena Dorr asks leave to enter the saying and is refused with the reason checked things and said things differ. Nobody thanks her. Ends on palms warmed against each other.
- **`1049` · Cover Going Down · 172/1, Tuesday · 16 holds, about 60, one day.** Boundary between `1048` and this. Wat Marshe asks Corvin to carry his yoke to the wall and is refused with the reason it would look kept. He owns leaning would not make it his. Ends on a yoke laid where it stops nothing.
- **`1050` · Going Down Empty · 172/2, Wednesday, off-morning · none.** Last morning of the volume. Ilyan asks Corvin for an hour with the broom and is refused with the reason of hands and stone. He owns wanting to have held it. Ends on the boy setting his pails down at the foot of the flight and taking them up again and going on, and names nothing that is up on the strip.

### Band 4 — `1031`–`1040`, *The name that travels* (complete)

- **`1031` · Nobody Carried It · 169/4, Friday · 16 inches and it holds, about 60
  under, three days.** A woman who saws lengths comes up the cart road with nothing
  in her hands to say that a voice came down it in the wind with a man's name in it
  and went into an open shed while her own saw was going, and that nobody carried it.
  She gives the working herself: no man on that road with a message, no woman with a
  message, and she did not go and ask anybody for it. She says a name gets said back
  to you and then you have a second copy, and will not say this one back to anybody
  and will not be thanked for coming up. A man with a broom has begun sweeping the
  flight from the bottom upward because the sound comes up, and says so before he
  does it. Ends on a saw laid down with its teeth turned away, which is not the way
  she leaves it.
- **`1032` · A Figure Of Himself · 169/5, Saturday, a whole figure of weeks and no
  figure of days beside it · 16, about 60, two days.** A man of thirty-eight carries a
  count of how many times he has heard that name since he stopped saying it, gets as
  far as the beginning of it and puts it back where it came from. A man with a broom
  refuses the figure with two workings, the second being that a man has not brought a
  number up that bank, he has brought the counting and wants somebody else to hold it.
  He is told he is wrong, loudly, and is not told he is right and not told he is
  wrong. The man of thirty-one says out loud that he is not going to say how many times
  that name has been said anywhere and will do the same with a figure brought to him.
  Ends on a jaw going on working on nothing all the way down a road.
- **`1033` · Asked For Out Loud · 169/6, Sunday · 16, about 60, one day.** The woman
  who reads for a living asks the man of thirty-one to say his own account out loud
  once more with the landing empty, so that she can hear whether it sounds like a
  thing that happened. She has never asked anybody for anything. He refuses, and gives
  her back her own working with the figure left off it, and says he would rather be
  the only one holding that sentence than put it in the one office whose whole work is
  that things can be checked. She says she wanted it, and that wanting it is hers and
  nobody is to take it off her. He says out loud that he was glad when he refused her
  and that a man is not allowed to keep the glad part without holding the rest of it.
  Ends with him standing with his back to the whole landing.
- **`1034` · Past The End Of The Wall · 169/7, Monday, the last morning of that week
  and an off-morning · no figure of anything.** The water came off the bank in the
  night and the whole flight stood out of it. A man with a broom gives three absences
  in a fifth set of words and says he will not say them in a fourth and let the fourth
  be the one that finally makes them smaller. A man who cannot see well works his hand
  along the wall until the stone runs out, keeps it out anyway, holds it in the air
  over both pieces of ash and out the far side, and brings it back without touching
  anything. Nobody put it there and nobody is going to ask him to. Ends on a hand
  brought down the way a hand comes off a hot plate, touching nothing on the way.
- **`1035` · One Of Mine · 170/1, Tuesday, the week turned in the night · 4, about 90,
  seven days.** A woman of forty-four who keeps twenty-nine chairs at a house that is
  not in this county comes past her own wall for the first time in a week and asks
  whether the chair on that landing is one of hers. She is refused with the working:
  everything on a landing eventually stands still in one place, and the day that chair
  has an owner on that landing the voice in the wind has an owner too, and an
  explanation is a thing that closes a door. She says she will stop asking. A man of
  fifty-four says a barrow handle is cut for a hand and is not that hand's, and says
  he would rather be in company with a thing of that shape than on his own with it.
  Ends on a man standing on the head of his own broom working the top step with his
  boot, with nobody watching.
- **`1036` · The Wrong Word · 170/2, Wednesday · 8, about 80, six days.** The woman
  who saws lengths comes up with her cup and gives back the word she has been
  carrying since Friday, says it is the wrong word, does not say which word or where
  she got it or how she worked it out, and asks not to be corrected — a name gets
  taken off people and she is not going to be given the right one out of a book. The
  man who cannot see well asks her whether the word is the name and gets nothing, fast.
  The man of thirty-one says the thing he has not said out loud yet: that a man who has
  been nobody for four hundred and eighty-two mornings does not want to be somebody
  because of what he knows, and wants it for himself. Ends on a woman at the wall
  eating the heel of a loaf she brought up and offered to nobody.
- **`1037` · One More Notch · 170/3, Thursday · 12, about 70, five days.** A man of
  thirty-eight asks whether he may cut a notch on the board he carries against his
  ribs, for the strip, and is refused with the working: a notch is a figure a man cut
  with a knife in his own hand on purpose, and in a year he would not know which one
  was his, and asking means telling them it is there. He does not cut it. A boy of
  thirteen gives his own count and says a yoke is cut for shoulders and does not belong
  to them, and says he does not know that it is right because he is thirteen. Ends on
  the yoke laid flat across the mouth of the lane, where it stops nothing.
- **`1038` · Which Of Them · 170/4, Friday, a full bank · 16, about 60, four days.**
  A man with a broom says out loud that there is a person on that landing the name is
  for and that he does not know which, and gives the working for not finding out, and
  says the question and his own figure stay in his mouth separately so that nobody
  joins them up. A man who cannot read says out loud that he is never going to ask
  anybody about that name, because if he is owed a figure then one day a person will
  ask him whether it arrived and he cannot say. Nobody thanked him and nobody told him
  he had it right. Ends with a man taking his hands off the wall one at a time and
  walking the barrow down through standing water without either hand going near the
  strip.
- **`1039` · Put Into The Air · 170/5, Saturday, a whole figure of weeks · 16, about
  60, three days.** A man with a broom says the three figures on ice and then puts
  **nineteen years** into the air and says in advance it is not on offer. A man who
  cannot see well asks a man who cannot read for his own count, is refused, and is
  offered the nineteen years instead, which he also refuses, giving the working that a
  figure handed to a man who cannot check it is a figure of the man who handed it over.
  A woman who saws lengths asks one question with a length under her arm and gets
  nothing and says she expected nothing. Ends with the man of thirty-one carrying a
  broom up the bank and standing it against the wall out of the wet, and being told he
  is not going to be thanked for it.
- **`1040` · The Sentence Taken Back · 170/6, Sunday · 16, about 60, two days.** A
  man with a broom takes back, inside a quarter of an hour, the sentence he offered on
  Friday — that he would say every morning at the seventh hour, for as long as he held
  the stone, that he did not know which of them the name was for — because a man who
  says that every morning is a man who says that, and in a year nobody will hear a man
  refusing to ask. A man of fifty-four says nobody has answered him in four hundred and
  eighty-six mornings and that he has stopped putting the wanting where a person could
  take it, which is not the same as stopping to want. A man of thirty-eight says he
  has had no job all week. A man of thirty-one says he will be there at the seventh
  hour and at the ninth hour for as long as it lasts and will ask nobody for anything
  while he does it. Ends on a woman sitting on a wall with a book open on her knee,
  having turned a page she does not read, and nobody saying anything out loud on that
  road for the rest of the day.

### Bands 1–3, one line each

- **`1001`–`1010`** — the name on the strip before anybody has said it again. The
  ordinary form is found already in use by somebody who did not invent it. No new
  name is given to anything in the band.
- **`1011`–`1020`** — the midpoint, landed on `1014`: it is better for that piece of
  wood to be called the wrong thing by the man who put it down than to be called the
  right thing by anybody. Said about a piece of wood, not about the man it is for. It
  costs him something and the something is not given a figure.
- **`1021`–`1030`** — the question owed to the person a thing is for, by the person
  who named it. A person puts themselves in the name and is refused by a person in a
  mouth with the working given, and is not told they are wrong or right. Somebody
  begins to say a wrong name on purpose and is stopped before the sentence is
  finished.
