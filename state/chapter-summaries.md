**THE ARCHIVE LORDS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH, BECAUSE IT IS THE THING THE NEXT BAND HAS TO CARRY AND NOBODY ELSE WILL WRITE IT DOWN: a custody faction is people whose whole business is holding other people's records so that nobody loses them. They are not liars and not cruel and not working for something. THEY ARE RIGHT ABOUT MOST OF IT. A sheet of paper in a room over a milliner's is a sheet of paper in a room over a milliner's, and a woman of about fifty-one is the only person in a county who can say how four pages were made, and if she is hit on a head by a barrow on a Tuesday then the only record of how those four pages were made is gone. THE ERROR IS NOT VICE AND IT IS THAT A THING WHICH IS SAFE BECAUSE SOMEBODY HOLDS IT CAN BECOME SAFE BECAUSE NOBODY MAY TAKE IT, AND THAT IS A DIFFERENT THING AND IT LOOKS IDENTICAL FROM THE OUTSIDE. NOBODY IN THE FACTION WANTS A RULING CLASS; A MAN IN IT SAYS OUT LOUD, AT `531`, AT `533` AND AT `540`, THAT HE DOES NOT WANT ONE, AND IS RIGHT, AND IS ABOUT TO MAKE ONE ANYWAY. BY THE END OF THE BAND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE IN BRAMBLEFOLD CAN NAME THE DIFFERENCE OUT LOUD AND ABOUT NINE CANNOT, AND THE CHAPTERS SAY SO TWICE AND THE TWO SETS OF NINE ARE NOT THE SAME SETS.**

**THE MIDPOINT, AND WHAT IT WAS: a man of about seventy-two of this town was asked one question, in a room with the door open, in front of about nine people, whether he was asked a question in a square in a town two days off on the morning of the thirtieth day after the division, and what he said out loud was that he does not remember, and about four people in that room understood that this is the finding and about nine understood that it is a failure. Nobody was withholding anything. Both towns are remembering correctly. There is no instrument in the county of Aldwick that can hold two true accounts of one morning, and the record is the only thing that cannot hold both, and it has four columns and no fifth.**

**526 — THE MIDPOINT. A man of about seventy-two is asked one question in a room with the door open, and he says that he does not remember.** `clear` 26, a Thursday, which is the market, and about two hundred people are in the square and hear none of it. The asking was arranged by a woman of sixty-one in a barn at Kiln Row in about nine sentences and she says plainly that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that says a person may be asked what they remember, that she has been told no four times in four years and not told anything at all once, and that she is not going to ask him and is going to bed. **The recordwright says out loud, before she asks it, that she will not ask him what the question was and will not ask him what he said, and the reason is that a man of about seventy-two asked to remember a thing in front of about nine people is a different room from the one this town has been working in for a fortnight.** He came up the lane at about the half hour after the first on a Tuesday-shaped errand on a Thursday, and nobody told him why, and she says out loud that that is wrong. About nine people are in the chancel and he is given a chair. **He says he was in a porch here at about the ninth hour on that morning and was asked nothing, and then he says THAT HE DOES NOT REMEMBER, and asks that it be written down that he said that and not that he said he was not asked.** A man near the door says twice that the page is wrong and is told by a man of seventy-two that a page is a thing in a hand, that a name on a page is a thing somebody wrote and may be right or wrong, and that if his not-remembering is turned into a page being wrong then in a hundred years there will be a person in that town who was told that his name was taken off a thing and never was. **He then says out loud that he is not a thing that is got back, that he has come up a lane on a Thursday nobody asked him to come up, and that the man of thirty-one had made a page into an instrument and had made it into a man in a chair.** He is entered at the back of the book and the entry says the name stands and is neither withdrawn nor confirmed and that there is no column in that book for a person who does not remember and none has been ruled. And the recordwright says out loud that this town remembers a Tuesday a hundred and forty-one years back with four words on it and no order, and that the other town remembers a Wednesday fifty days back with a woman's letter in it and about two hundred people who did not sleep, and that both are correct and neither is about the other, and that there is no instrument in the county of Aldwick that can hold two correct accounts of the same morning. **Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was relieved.** Closing: a name that stands, and no instrument that can hold two. `526`

**527 — Two accounts go round one town in the same mouths, and a woman of thirty-four says nobody has ever asked her what she remembers.** `clear` 27, a Friday. The four words and the four sentences are going round the same town in the same mouths and about nine people can give you both and about nine can give you only the one they heard first, and **the number of people who can give you both is the number of people who can be asked about the other one.** The sentence about a thing said in a room being a thing about nine people turns out to be three people's: a weaver of sixty-two said it to the man of thirty-one in two rooms over a joiner's shop on the fifty-ninth day after the division, a woman of sixty-one has said it thirty-one times in four years in nine towns, and a letter of four lines from a town four hundred miles off says it too and adds that the people there think the people here ought to be asked whether they want it. **She reads that letter out in a porch, to about nine people, and nobody asks her where it is from, and it also says that they do not know what to do with a person who says no, that they have not found out in about nine years, and that there has not been a week of those nine years without one.** She says out loud that a person who has been asked nothing is the one person in that town who knows what it is like. **And then the recordwright, in a smithy, in front of about nine people, says that she is the only person in that county who can be sent for about either account, that she has been that for nineteen years, and that nobody has ever asked her what she remembers, and that there is no form that says a person may be asked what they remember, and that in about nine rooms in a hundred and twenty-seven days she would have said something and has not said it, and that there is a difference between a thing you will say when somebody asks and a thing you say because you have not been asked in nineteen years.** He asks. A man of eighty-one says from the chair, unasked, that a thing a person remembers is not a copy and is not a copy of a copy, and that he has been that person twice in this town and has said nothing both times. **528 — Two accounts are read out in full in a square and the square decides nothing, and about five of nine cannot remember which they heard first.** `clear` 28, a Saturday, and it is not a market, and the chapter says so in its third line. **529 — A man of fifty-four rides nine miles and makes a return that records two answers on the back of a form, and signs the back and not the front.** `clear` 29, a Sunday. **530 — A boy of fourteen is asked a question in a smithy that nobody in that smithy can answer, and a third column is ruled and not headed.** `clear` 30, the seventh and last day of the ninety-seventh week, and it names the morrow as the first of the ninety-eighth.
