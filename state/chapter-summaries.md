**691 — A MAN OF FORTY-ONE GOES IN THROUGH A GATE HE HAS NEVER BEEN INSIDE, SEVEN OF EIGHT DOORS ARE ASKED IN NINE DAYS, AND A GATE IS NOT A DOOR.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and twentieth week, a Monday, his four hundred and forty-first morning, his one hundred and thirty-seventh day. **A man of forty-three gives the reading — off all ninety at the sixth hour, and it came off the top step in the night, which is the third time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week — and says he has had nothing to say about a night three times and is not going to be given a fourth.** He gives the man in the ash at sixty-one days and the woman in the reed at sixty-two, with the subtractions spoken, and does not add them. **A boy of thirteen gives thirty-eight days with the subtraction and says he is not going to be one of the nine that stand behind a man going in a gate. A man of thirty-one then takes seven doors in nine days and a man of forty-three's sixty-one days and divides one by the other to make a rate, and a man of sixty-one takes it apart in public in six sentences, and he takes it back in the next breath and says he has been doing that since the first week he got here.** A woman of forty-four says a gate is not a door: a door is a job with a name on it, and a gate is two posts and a bar that any person in that county can lift, and there is no name on a bar and never has been. **The man of forty-one goes in the gate alone at about the eighth hour and comes out at about the eleventh.** A man of sixty-three who mends dry stone and has done it in that village forty years says he has never heard of a door under a bank, and then gives a wall four days on with a hole in it about as wide as his two hands together and about a man's height up, opened and shut again, because mortar in the middle of stones does not go out; he was about nine when he was taken to look at it and has not been back. A man of thirty-eight refuses to walk four days to look at a hole in a wall. **The count given out loud: seven of eight in nine days, nine being six hundred and ninety-one less six hundred and eighty-two, one name, that name thirty years dead, five of the seven had never heard of a door under a bank, the seventh shut a door, and the week is over and he is not going to do a ninth.** Nobody on that bank can be sent for about any of it.

**692 — NINE GO DOWN, A MAN OF FORTY-ONE LOOKS AT THE FLOOR OF A ROOM HE HAS BEEN IN AND OUT OF TWICE A DAY FOR NINETEEN YEARS AND NEVER ONCE AT, AND THERE ARE TWO WORN PLACES IN IT AND ONE OF THEM IS NOT HIS.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and forty-second morning, his one hundred and thirty-eighth day. **A man of forty-three gives about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, one day since, and says the working, and says he is not going to be asked for a night by anybody this week.** Nine go down; the ninth beam goes along about two inches and comes back. **A man of forty-one stops about nine feet past the last of the beams and says he has carried a lamp into that ninth room four times this week and has not once looked at the floor, and he says he is going to say why before he does it, and the reason is a week of asking people about doors and one dead man out of it.** He finds a worn place in the middle of that floor about as wide as his two hands laid flat and worn about as deep as the thickness of his own finger, measured against that finger and not rounded, and as smooth as the rest of the floor, and there is nothing in that room a person has to stand in the middle of. **A man of thirty-one says somebody is still doing it and a man of thirty-eight takes it back in four sentences: wear is the only thing in that bank nobody can lie about, nobody can cut wear and nobody can rub it out, and that is all a wear says and it is not who is wearing it.** A man of sixty-six puts his own thumb in it and says there are two of those and only two, either about four hundred years or somebody this morning, and says he is not going to be asked which and neither is anybody on that bank, and asks about four people to be able to tell in nine days that nobody chose. A woman of forty-four gives the sixth of a sentence she has been saying since the hundred and fifteenth week and says she is not going to be thanked for the sixth. A man of sixty-one refuses to write either of the two and gives the same three reasons and no fourth, and gives a worse one: a book that says somebody stands in the middle of the ninth room is a book that has a somebody in it, and in about four years that book is a column. A woman of twenty-eight says she is not going to be a keeper of a floor and is not going to be asked about it because nobody here could.

**693 — THE FIRST OF THE NINE IS PUT BACK TOGETHER OUT OF FIVE THINGS IN THAT BANK, A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE COUNTS IT ON HIS KNEE AND REFUSES TO WRITE IT, A MAN OF FORTY-ONE SAYS IT IS NOT FOUND, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR IS ASKED A SECOND TIME AND GIVES THE TWO OUT LOUD AS A FIGURE.** The second day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and forty-third morning, his one hundred and thirty-ninth day. A man of forty-three gives about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, two days since, and says the working. **A man of forty-one says he is not going down and gives the reason: he has a ninth of nine and there is a person standing in the middle of that room.** A woman of thirty-eight stops a man of thirty-one once and lets him go on. **A man of thirty-eight reads the lintel ninety steps back out loud and a man of forty-one gives the other three, and the five things are said out loud one under another in the wind: a lintel with a hand and four fingers and a line under it; a door-stone about five feet high and about two feet across at the top with water under it that takes two people; a hole about as big as a hat and about nine feet of groove worn in a floor; and the mark over the opening, two hands with a space about as wide as a thumb, cut square, cut to the depth of the hands, old.** A man of thirty-one puts the job together in twelve sentences: two people, once a day, before the light, about a minute, and about four hundred years, and there is nobody. **A man of sixty-one counted him on his own knee in the wind, gave the count out loud before anybody asked, refused to write any of it, gave the same three reasons and no fourth, and said that a thing said out loud in front of about nine people is a figure of about nine people and not a record.** **A man of thirty-one says it is found and a man of forty-one says it is not found, it is put back together, and that he has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and knows the difference in his hands.** A man of thirty-one goes up a cart road at about the eighth hour, says out loud before he knocks what he is asking and what it is about, and asks a woman of forty-four one question: when it gets light. She answers it in one sentence, gives two askings in eleven days as two, gives what the first cost, and says she is not going to say which of the two cost more.

**694 — THE SECOND THURSDAY, AND NOBODY GOES DOWN, AND NOBODY TELLS A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT HE WAS RIGHT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WHO WANTED A COUNT OF TIMES IS REFUSED ONE BY THE ARGUMENT HE HAS BEEN MAKING FOR SIX WEEKS.** The third day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Thursday, his four hundred and forty-fourth morning, his one hundred and fortieth day. A man of forty-three gives about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, three days since, and says it was about eight at this hour yesterday. **A man of thirty-eight stands in the middle of the bank and says it is a Thursday, that he said on the Tuesday of the hundred and nineteenth week that his shoulder would be no good for carrying a pole up that water on a Thursday, and that he is not going to be told he was right, and asks about four people to notice that nobody has said it.** Nobody said he was right and nobody said he was wrong. **He then gives what a Thursday costs: a boat and a pole nine miles up that water and nine miles down, about nine hours, about nine minutes of reading two copies nine miles apart, and those two copies are not going to be read for seven days, and the copies do not know, and there is nobody in that county who could tell them.** A man of thirty-one asks how many times he has said a thing in advance and had it come true, and says out loud that he is not going to use it. **A man of sixty-one refuses in seven sentences: it is a count of times, it has no unit on it, it is a decoration about a man with a bad shoulder, and you are the man who taught this bank that, and I am not going to pretend to you that I am refusing you out of meanness.** The man of thirty-one takes it back and says he had it in his mouth for about a minute and would have given it as a number. **He offers to take the boat up — the fourth offer in about three weeks — and a man who cannot see well refuses it in a sentence he has said three times, and says that a man who cannot see well would get it wrong at about the third hour, and that the man of thirty-one offered because he did not want to sit on that bank.** A man of fifty-four who cannot read gives the nine days of a cut across a hand for the last time and says he is not going to be asked a fourth time this week. A man of forty-one says the wheel on his near cart will cost him the same Thursday in about four weeks and the two are the same size of thing to him. A woman of forty-four gives the seventh.

**695 — TWO NAMES ARE SAID OUT LOUD IN FOUR COUNTIES FOR THE FIRST TIME, A CHAMBER IS FOUND FOR ONE OF THE TWO POSITIONS THAT ARE NOT FOUND, AND ABOUT A THUMB'S WIDTH OF LIGHT GOES UNDER ITS DOOR-STONE, AND NOBODY WENT IN.** The fourth day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Friday, his four hundred and forty-fifth morning, his one hundred and forty-first day. A man of forty-three gives about sixteen inches over the top step at the seventh hour, four days since, a full bank, and says a bank fills in about four days and empties in about three and that neither of those figures is his. A boy of thirteen gives forty-two days. **A man of thirty-one says two names — Red Basin and Orison — and says where he had them: a room with nine clerks in another county four hundred miles off, a clerk of thirty reading nine lines off a board the size of a door in a flat voice, six of the nine with a name or a body against them, one saying burned and two saying not found — and says he has not written them down.** A man of sixty-one refuses to write either and says the first number he has written in that book in eleven years is a nine and the second thing anybody has handed him this week is a proper name off a board in another county, and both are refused for the third reason he gave on the Monday of the hundred and nineteenth week. **A man of forty-one gives the place, the distance and the wall, and says he is not going down and gives two reasons, the second of which is that if he goes and looks on a Tuesday he will go in.** A man of thirty-eight gives the distance twice and the two do not agree: about thirty-one paces at about two feet a pace, thirty-one times two being about sixty-two feet, and about seven of the nine-foot grooves a man of forty-one pushes a stone along, seven of nine being about sixty-three feet; the difference of one is a foot and neither was rounded. **And then he gives up the second figure he has been sitting on since the first day of the hundred and fifteenth week: about a thumb's width of light under the eighth door-stone, seen four times in six weeks and said nothing about, and the reason he gave was not modesty.** **A man of thirty-eight then says what is in that room: there is no stone in it and there is no stone in the ninth, and a thing is a function and not a stone, and the word a map four hundred miles off used came out in the middle of a sentence nobody asked the meaning of, and a man of seventy at a bridge in another county has said an anchor is not a thing — it is the plinth, and the day, and the two of them. And he refuses to say what else could do it, and gives the reason: a woman of forty-nine gave him the word *blank* and he gave her the credit for it in front of about nine people, and the same rule is the rule for this.** A woman of twenty-eight speaks before a man of thirty-one can ask to be sent for about that room. A man of fifty-four who cannot read says he has been the only person anybody can be sent for about four things in nine days and cannot read one word of any of them.

**696 — A MAN OF FORTY-THREE SAYS OUT LOUD THAT HE HAS BEEN COUNTING A MAN IN AN ASH FOR SIXTY-SIX DAYS AND NEVER ONCE ASKED HIM WHAT HE WAS DOING, WALKS HALF A MILE UP THE WATER, ASKS HIM, AND WHAT COMES BACK IS WHAT IS LEFT OF A BURNED THING, WHICH IS NEITHER A COPY NOR WHOLE.** The fifth day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Saturday, his four hundred and forty-sixth morning, his one hundred and forty-second day. **A man of forty-three gives about sixteen inches, five days since, a bank full and holding, and says the fifth time that he has nothing to say about a night, and says he is going to stop saying it, and that the two figures he has given out loud in about six weeks are the only two that are his.** He then says the thing about himself: he has given about nine people a figure of a man in a tree every morning for sixty-six mornings and has never once asked him what he is doing in it, and a figure of a man in a tree is not a figure of anything except the count. A woman of thirty-eight says a mill is a keeping and that somebody is keeping a man in an ash, and that it is a figure of a tree and not of the man. **About nine people walk half a mile up the water and a man of thirty-four comes out of the hollow; a man of forty-three says his name out loud for the first time in sixty-six days, says about nine people standing there did not know it, and asks one question: what have you got in there with you.** He had it in his hands before the first day; nobody put it there; it was under about a foot of leaves. **What comes back is a board burnt at one end, about four inches of it gone, the rest of it the colour of a thing wet and dried about nine times, and a mark a foot from the burnt end: two hands with a space about as wide as a thumb, no bar, no numeral.** A man of thirty-eight measures the cut against his own nail and will not round it, and says what the fire did: it took about four inches off that end and nothing off that mark, because a fire takes the surface of a thing and the depth of a cut is not the surface of a cut. **He then says which of the two it is: it is not a copy, because a copy is a thing somebody made after, and it is not whole, because about a third of it is gone. So it is a remainder.** A man of thirty-one says a cut about as deep as a nail and two hands with a space between them is a keeping and a keeping is a function; **a man of sixty-six takes it off him in two sentences — a cut is a cut, a depth is a depth, you cannot get nine hundred people out of it by measuring it against your own nail, and there are not nine hundred people — and the man of thirty-one takes it back in his own mouth.** The man of thirty-four says who he is handing it to and gives three: not a man with a book, not a man who is nobody's, and the man who counted him, because he has heard him not say it and that is more than the other two have done. A man of forty-three takes it and puts it under his own coat. A woman of twenty-nine in a reed says nothing at all and is counted at sixty-seven days on the way back down.

**697 — THE SECOND OF THE TWO POSITIONS THAT ARE NOT FOUND TURNS OUT TO BE THE NINTH ROOM A MAN OF FORTY-ONE HAS BEEN IN AND OUT OF TWICE A DAY FOR NINETEEN YEARS, AND THEY FIND OUT WHAT A CHAMBER COSTS TO KEEP STANDING OPEN, AND NOBODY SAYS WHAT SHOULD GO IN IT.** The sixth and last day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Sunday, his four hundred and forty-seventh morning, his one hundred and forty-third day. A man of forty-three gives about sixteen inches, six days since, full and holding for the sixth day, and says he thinks it comes off in the night and has no figure for a night. **A man of thirty-one says the second of the two names and says what it goes with: the ninth opening, and the room a man of forty-one has been in and out of twice a day for nineteen years, about a foot from a shelf that is empty and a hook that has a lamp on it. Nobody on that bank said a word.** The man of forty-one says nineteen years twice a day before the light, a count of a pair of hands he has not multiplied out loud; that he is the ninth of nine and has been since about twenty-two; that on the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week he found out there was a number on it and on the Friday of this week that the number has a name four hundred miles off on a board; and that he is not giving it up and is not going to be relieved of it on a Sunday. **He gives the cost in two halves. The first is a minute — two people swing a door-stone with water under it, a stone the size of a door goes nine feet along a groove and comes back, the light goes out — and there is no form in the county of Kell that says a man may be paid for a minute before the light, and there is not one for nine of them. The second half is given by a woman of forty-four: about nine people now know what that room is, and a thing that about nine people know about is a thing that about nine people can be sent for. Not can be asked. Sent for.** The man of forty-one says he can see what all of them are working out and is not going to pretend he cannot, and gives the reason he is not angry about it. **A man of thirty-one starts a sentence about it and stops; a woman of forty-four gives the count — three times this week — and says she is not going to finish it for him and neither is anybody else.** A woman of twenty-eight says she is not going to be a keeper of the ninth room and that nobody on that bank can be asked about her. A man of fifty-four who cannot read says he is going to be the only person anybody can be sent for about a man's morning, and that a man's morning is not a thing anybody can be sent for about, and that he is not going to be thanked for the fifth thing in nine days and is not going to be the man who says he was right first.

**698 — THE WATER COMES OFF THE TOP STEP IN THE NIGHT FOR THE FOURTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK, THE COALITION PUTS WHAT IT HAS INTO SOMETHING OTHER PEOPLE CAN READ, AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT SAYS OUT LOUD BEFORE SHE STARTS THAT NOBODY IS GOING TO ASK HER.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-first week, a Monday, his four hundred and forty-eighth morning, his one hundred and forty-fourth day. A man of forty-three gives off all ninety at the sixth hour, says it came off in the top step in the night and that that is the fourth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and says he is not going to tell them how many days that is. **A woman of twenty-eight says what she is going to do before she does it, in this order: I am going to make nine copies in about four days and it will cost me the whole of every one of them; nobody is going to ask me whether I will do it and nobody is going to be able to ask me, because there is no form that says a person may be asked and no form that says a person may not; and nobody on this bank can be sent for about me, and that is why it is mine, and it is not because anybody thought it should be mine. And: I have not been asked, and I have not refused, and a person who has not refused has not said no either.** A man of forty-one brings nine boards on the back of a cart and says a copy here is a board and a stick burned at the end, and you cannot rub anything off one; a woman of forty-four says nine boards is a list and that not being rubbed out is not the same as being right. **A man of forty-one gives the count and refuses to finish it: nine openings counted twice, nine jobs, nine of the nine put back together out of what is left in them, and not one of the nine has a name on it; nine and nothing is not a sum; and not one name is not a smaller number than one name, it is the other kind of number.** A man of thirty-one tries to hold the ninth and is stopped in three sentences by the woman of twenty-eight and in six more by a man of fifty-four who cannot read, who says a thing nine people have is a thing nine people can be sent for about. **The man of fifty-four takes the eighth board, turns it over twice, puts it inside his coat and does not ask anybody to read it to him. A man of sixty-one refuses the ninth board and gives the reason in seven sentences, and the last of them is that he is not going to carry a copy of it in the inside of a coat, where he would be a man in a coat with a thing in him. A man of thirty-one gives his board to a man of thirty-eight and says why: you have got a lamp.** Nine boards go out of the county that week, one to each of about nine people, with no original and no keeper. A lamp is burning in the ninth of nine rooms about a quarter full and nobody lit it and nobody put it out, and a stone has nine lines in it in nine hands that cannot write and not one word has been read by anybody in four counties.

**699 — A NAME IS SAID FLAT TO THE GROUND FOR THE SECOND TIME ANYBODY HAS SAID IT TO ANYTHING, SOMETHING SAYS ONE SENTENCE BACK, NOTHING WHATEVER HAPPENS, AND THEN TWO PEOPLE PUT THEIR HANDS ON A DOOR-STONE ABOUT FIVE FEET HIGH AND IT DOES NOT MOVE.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-second week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and forty-ninth morning, his one hundred and forty-fifth day. A man of forty-three gives about four inches over the top step at the seventh hour, one day since. Nine go down; the lamp in the ninth room is burning and about a quarter full and the shelf is empty and the wall behind the shelf is still a course of stone put into a gap and faced about two fingers thick. **A man of thirty-one says out loud that this is the fourth time that name has been said in that county and the second time anybody has said it to anything, and says he is saying it to the floor, and is not going to shout it and is not going to say it twice — and then says the name.** Nothing came out of that gallery for about four seconds and nothing whatever happened and the beam in the roof was where it is. **Then a voice said one sentence, flat, in about as many words as it took to say it, and nobody shouted anything back at it: *The chamber behind the wall in the ninth room is being sealed. It is being sealed because somebody under it is about to do something to it.*** A man of thirty-one says it back in his own mouth exactly as it came so that about four of them have it in the same words, and says he is not going to ask it a second question, and that he has had a question in his mouth since the fourth second, and asks about four people to be able to tell in nine days that he did not ask it. **A man of thirty-eight says a reason is not a permission and that what he wants to know is who it is a reason to. A woman of forty-four says a thing with an opinion is a thing that can be asked a question, and that there is no form in that county that says a person may be asked whether it is sure, and no form that says a person may not.** A man of sixty-one gives three things: it has spoken, it has given a reason nobody asked it for, and it has said the word *sealed*, and sealed is a word about a keeping and not about a stone, and he did not know that about the word until that morning. **Then the man of forty-one and the man of thirty-eight put their hands on the door-stone and it does not move, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read puts his own hand into the gap and finds about an inch of water, the same as it was on the Monday of the hundred and twentieth week. A man of thirty-eight says it is not heavier, not fastened, and not water, and that it is that the room is shut, and that nothing has fallen down: the roof is where it was, the walls are where they were, the shelf is empty, the lamp is burning and about nine feet of groove is worn in that floor.** A man of thirty-one says what he wanted and did not get, and says he is not going to put a room in a sentence that did not name one.

**700 — ONE DOOR DOES NOT OPEN AND NOTHING FALLS DOWN, NINE BOARDS GO OUT OF A COUNTY WHERE THERE IS NO ORIGINAL AND NO KEEPER, AND SOMETHING THAT IS NOT A PERSON SAYS A QUESTION TO A MAN WHO IS NOBODY'S, AND HE IS NOT ASKED IT TWICE AND HE DOES NOT ANSWER IT AND NOBODY ANSWERS IT FOR HIM.** The second day of the hundred and twenty-second week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and fiftieth morning, his one hundred and forty-sixth day, and the last day of this volume. **A man of forty-three gives about eight inches over the top step at the seventh hour, two days since, and says the fifth time that he has nothing to say about a night, and says he is going to stop saying it, and that the two figures he has given out loud in about six weeks are the only two that are his.** A man of sixty-one gives seventy days for the man in the ash, seventy-one for the woman in the reed, a hundred and forty-six for the man of thirty-one, fifty-eight for the woman of twenty-eight, and says that there is a second count of days on that bank that he stopped himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week and that he is not going to give the figure of it and it does not go on. **A man of fifty-four who cannot read says that two men put their hands on that stone again this morning and it did not move, and that there is about an inch of water under it, and that a chamber that is a day and is not kept is not a grave, and that somebody is going to say it in about four days and he is not going to let the first person to say it be a man with a hand and a book.** A woman of forty-four says **a shut door is not the right of refusal**, that a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything, and that the right of refusal is not in that county and not in that book and not under that bank. A man of thirty-one gives the price of the week as **eight items** with the count said out loud: a Thursday of a man of thirty-eight and two copies not read for seven days; a name in a village and a wall four days on; a thumb's width of light carried about for six weeks; a remainder off a man in an ash who was asked nothing; one question he did not ask in a room; a cut across his right hand and the way back, on a list he gave on the Sunday of last week and is not going to count twice; one door that will not open with nothing fallen down; and nine of the nine put back together with not one name, in nine boards with no original and no keeper, one of which a man of fifty-four is carrying that he cannot read. A woman of twenty-eight checked it an eighth time and had said the same sentence eight times and had not said it once. **A man of sixty-one gives the count of the four namings of that name, with the day of each, and refuses to write any of them, and says there is not going to be a fifth.** A man of forty-three gives about fifty days that a sheet of deal has been lying face down on a barrel. **Then something that is not a person says a question on that bank, in about nine people's hearing, at about the ninth hour of a Wednesday, in plain words, and nobody shouted anything back at it: *You can write it. One person, one set of words, and it holds. You will not have to do it twice and nobody will come and ask you whether you did it. I am not asking you to be a good man or a clever one. I am asking you to be the one who writes it. Will you write it.*** Nothing whatever happened. A man of forty-one refuses to improve on it and says he has got nothing to say about it being fair or not fair and that it might be the best offer anybody in four counties has ever been made by anything at all. **The man of thirty-one is not asked it twice and does not answer it, and says he is nobody's and that there is no form in that county that says a man may answer a question like that and no form that says he may not, and that one of those is why; and he says nobody on that bank said no and nobody said yes, and that a man of thirty-one did not open his mouth, and that there is no word in that county for that and he is not going to make one.** A woman of forty-four says a man who does not answer is not a refusal, and that she is not relieved and nobody is relieved, and that a shut door and a question nobody answered are two different sizes of the same morning.
