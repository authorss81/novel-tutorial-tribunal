# Chapter Summaries

**One paragraph per chapter for the two bands in progress. This block supersedes everything below it.** **Chapters 1–450 are canon and Volume 09 is closed. Volume 10, *The House That Refused*, is open; 451–490 are canon.** Earlier bands are one line each; chapters are the record.

## VOLUME 10, CHAPTERS 481 TO 490 — *the arbitration is refused, and the arbitration happens*

**481 — Nine words in a shop before the eighth hour, and a man two hours late.** Monday, the thirty-fifth day, the seventh and last day of the ninetieth week. **The first sentence anybody says in this band is a miller of thirty-nine's: the Fen will be under water again by Saturday.** He is not a household of this town, and a bar four feet long has not been wound at either hour for five days, and the gates have been standing open since the sixth hour of the fourteenth day because shutting them needs the bar. **And the band names the two counts out loud, because a band that cannot tell them apart prints a contradiction in its own chapter: the bar is counted in days on which an hour came and went and the wheel at the mill is counted in days completed, and the wheel is one behind, four days slow against the bar's fifth, and the miller gives the two figures and then says the word in his own day book against the name of the mill, which is *race*, and that it is not the book with nine hundred names in it.** Ilyan comes in at the eighth hour with a sentence and is told by about nine people that it is the fourth time he has said it, and spends the morning being useful in the wrong place, which is what he did on the twenty-fifth day and which nobody in that town has forgiven him for. **And Sefa Lund says the half she has never said: by the Saturday the water stands above the sills, the cause the board found on the Wednesday is proved again, and it will be proved by nobody turning it, and the board will be right twice — and the clause that could stop a requirement binds households, and the row is not households, and it has never once been any use to her.** Ordwin Cape asks whether anybody can be sent for. A copyist says a thing is sent for by a person with a form, and that this is the fifth true answer this town has had in five weeks, and that being right is not the same as being able to do anything. `481`

**482 — A man of fifty-four asks in a room, and two lines are drawn under a head with no body behind them.** Tuesday, the thirty-sixth day, the first day of the ninety-first week. **Gault Rieve stands up in the Stone House and asks whether anybody can be sent for, and says the reason he is asking in a room and not a lane: a thing said in a lane is not a thing anybody can be asked about.** Perdy Sallow gives the same answer the copyist gave in the lane on Monday, in about four sentences, and then says the difference: in a lane it is a thing said, in a room it is a thing that goes into a minute. **And then Ilyan spends twenty minutes trying to make a form out of a matter that has nowhere to go.** He writes the best head of a form anybody in that town had seen in five weeks — to the board, to the House, to the office at Kerby, to anybody in the county with power, to nobody else — and there is nothing under the head, because there is nowhere for it to go. **Wenna Crale gives him the test: a form is a thing with somewhere to go, and every part of a form is either a person with an office or a day, and there is not one person with an office in this matter in the county of Ostrey.** He says he has wanted since the sixth day to be a thing somebody can be asked about, and that a form is a door and he wanted to be a door. **And then she sits at her own table, turns his sheet so the head is at the top, and writes two lines under it in her own hand: that this matter, upon which no body in the county of Ostrey has power, be heard by an arbitration in a room in this town with the door open; and that any person who comes to it be asked nothing touching his character, his rate, or his household.** It is the first two lines of anything anybody in Halloway has ever drawn up for anybody else. It is not a form. It goes to the board, because a body can refuse, and a body can refuse and this one will. `482`

**483 — The board answers both lines in the words of the request, and is right twice, and nine men find out what being right is.** Wednesday, the thirty-seventh day. First sitting since the thirtieth. About thirty in the room and on a stair the width of one man, about nine of them from the Fen, and two things on the table of which only one is a request. **The minute carries two answers. First, the board has no power to appoint and no list to appoint from. Second, the board cannot undertake that any person who comes to an arbitration will be asked nothing touching his character, his rate or his household — and the reason given is what the board itself has done: it asked a man of fifty-four what year he had been in the water, and it put a requirement into a minute on the thirtieth day naming a woman of thirty-eight, and neither of those was a form.** Then a man of about fifty-three says out loud that he has been right five times in five weeks and has never once been able to make anything happen, **and it happens nine times over about eleven minutes, because a day a man writes down is not a day he has called, and a day nobody called is the same as a day that was never written.** Ilyan says his sentence in the room for the third time; about nine men at the table have heard it three times and about four have it right; he names the wrong version that is in about forty mouths and says he will not put it right in that room either, because a sentence a room can say is not a thing a room can hear. **Ivo Cray asks for the number of days and it is seven out of the water and seven that the bar has not been turned, and those are not the same seven, and the difference between them is a Wednesday, and the Wednesday is the day the board sat.** Sefa Lund does the money: the heel is nine pounds, the quarter in the board's hand is eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence, the difference is fifty-six pence a quarter, **fifty-six is thirty-eight pence and eighteen pence, the thirty-eight is the twelve shillings and eightpence a year the nineteen houses were paying and the eighteen is what was short before the Wednesday**, and a halfpenny on each of the eighty lots is forty pence of it and there are sixteen pence no lot in this county can be made to find. The board enters the standing of about nine people from the Fen: they are not a party, and it is the first entry ever made against any of them rather than for them. `483`

**484 — Four instruments named, a fifth refused, a sixth refused, and the answer to right-or-privilege stops being a sentence.** Thursday, the thirty-eighth day. A room over a taproom, about nine people, nobody called it. **The four are named out loud with the holder of each: a miller's second book shut to a hand's width, a copyist paid two pounds a year, a minute of a water board, and a woman of thirty-eight who refuses things in lanes.** A man of about thirty at a nail bin proposes a fifth — a nail in a board in a shed — and **the room refuses it in four minutes, correctly, because an instrument is a thing a person can be asked about and the first thing a town must be able to do with one is be told by it that it is wrong, and a nail cannot say no.** Then the question of the volume is asked, which of the four is a right and which a privilege, **and the answer stops being a sentence and becomes a list of four things and a list of who holds them, and the second list is the whole of the answer, and about four people in the room notice the sentence has stopped and the rest do not.** Then Mab Harrow names a fifth instrument, and it is a man, and **he says yes in about four seconds, and it is the fastest thing said in that room all morning and the worst, and about nine people refuse it in nine minutes, correctly: an instrument can be asked about after the person who brought it has gone out of the room, and there is no way in this county to ask a man about a list tomorrow, and on the Wednesday morning of the thirtieth day a form with a day on it put nineteen houses out of the water in four minutes.** Last, a miller of thirty-nine says the reason he would not open a book: a sentence said in a square makes a public instrument a thing that gets opened. **And then he says the other half — you did not say it to shut it — and says he is not sorry for it, and that there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry.** `484`

**485 — A square of two hundred people, and a thing read aloud in it that is not a document.** Friday, the thirty-ninth day. No market, because the market is on a Tuesday. About two hundred people in the square and about nine in the Stone House, and about thirty of the two hundred have been up the lane to look at the water. **Ilyan reads out, in the open air, the second sheet folded in four with nothing on the outside of it, in four minutes, and about two hundred people hear six years of a woman of twenty-eight four hundred miles off whom nobody in that square has ever met.** He had been told twice that a thing said in a square cannot be asked about, and he had heard it twice. **A boy of about fifteen asks the one question anybody has: who witnessed it. And Ilyan Vester cannot answer it, in front of about two hundred people, because the answer is four hundred miles off and has not been asked, and a woman with a child on her hip says go and ask her, and he says there is no form that lets anybody send a letter about a question.** Then Mab Harrow says the opposite back to him in front of all of them: a thing read aloud in a square is a thing a person has been said to have said, and that is the opposite of a thing anybody can be asked about, and he has said so in nine rooms, and the door he opened was not a door, it was a window, and about two hundred people have stood in front of it and got wet. And a copyist says that what he read out is not a document and she could not enter it if she tried and is not going to try. `485`

**486 — THE ARBITRATION HAPPENS, and it produces an instrument and not a decision.** Saturday, the fortieth day. **The water comes over the low sill at about the seventh hour and stands at the step of the fourth house of nineteen, and about thirty people are round the bar at the sixth hour and none of them puts a hand to it.** Nobody called the arbitration. About nine people go four hundred yards to a room over a taproom and bring eleven more. **There is no procedure, no standing, no rules and no chair with power, and each of those five was asked for in the first nine minutes by about four people and refused.** The two lines are read out. The question is put four times and is the same question: what is to be done about the water, the frame, the bar, and by whom, and in what document. Four answers come back at once in four different orders and all four are correct. **And Sefa Lund says the rest of it: a thing that has been asked for by everybody is not nothing, and that is not a right and not a privilege and not a clause, it is a room.** Then Wenna Crale writes a sheet of paper with a head on it, the day on it, and her hand at the foot, with two parts under the head: the four instruments of this town and who holds each, and an account of what the row at the Fen was told and by whom and on what day, given by the woman who has kept it in her head since the twenty-first day after the division. **She says it flat, in order, with the days on it, and nobody interrupts her once, and about nine people applaud, and about four people in that room understand what has happened and are not applauding.** Ilyan asks for one line under the head saying a person may be asked no; he is asked whether anybody can be given it; **and he gives the answer he has been giving since the tenth day after the division, and about thirty people in that room hear the fifth room have it.** `486`

**487 — The word required is in a minute a second time, and this time it is good, and it protects the man who would have compelled.** Sunday, the forty-first day. **A chair of forty-seven calls a sitting, the first day he has called in nine years, and the fenrow are a party to it because a woman of thirty-eight signed a sheet with a day on it, and a person is a party to a thing the moment their name goes at the foot of it.** The row ask the board to require the bar to be wound. Four of the nine men say out loud, correctly, that the ninth clause binds households and she is not a household, so the board may require her. **And the chair turns it round: a requirement is a thing a person can answer no to, and if it cannot be answered no to it is not a requirement, it is an act, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey for an act — and I am a household of this town, and I am the one person in this room for whom that clause is a protection, and it is a protection against me, and I have been the mouth of a thousand people for nine years and have never once been able to say that out loud in a room.** So the requirement goes in with a second line under it, and Sefa Lund answers **no**, and the one word is entered in the minute under a heading that says *answer of the person required*, with the day on it. **It is the first time in four hundred years that anybody has been able to enter that, and nobody in that room knows that the only use the ninth clause has ever got in Halloway is a shield for the man who would have done the compelling.** The bar is not wound on the Sunday either. `487`

**488 — A levy is asked for, by whom, and in what document, and the money is thirteen weeks off and the season is four weeks off.** Monday, the forty-second day, the seventh and last day of the ninety-first week. **The board is asked to mend a north heel that is a season gone, and the sum is the shortest in the volume: nine pounds against eight pounds and fifteen shillings and fourpence a quarter, fifty-six pence short, and a halfpenny on each of the eighty lots is forty pence and was the whole of the difference on the twenty-sixth day and is not the whole of it now.** The document question: a levy is a line on a roll, this year's roll is made, **and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that lets anybody put a cost on a roll that has been made, and the roll for the autumn is made in five days on a day out of a day by a steward who will do it correctly and will not know.** The steward comes to the sitting nobody asked him to attend and does the sum out loud: **three and a halfpence on each of the eighty lots, the same figure as the scouring, is 280 pence a year, one pound and three shillings and fourpence, and with the quarter it is nine pounds and eighteen shillings and eightpence, which is eighteen shillings and eightpence more than the heel.** Carried eight votes to one, the one against being a man who says a levy is a thing that is asked for and not a thing that is found. Entered in a minute with a day and a hand at the foot of it, to be charged in the roll for the autumn, collected with the water rate four times a year, **first collection thirteen weeks off, season about four weeks off.** And Ilyan gives the true answer for the fourth time he has been asked in this town, and it is the same sentence, and about nine men in that room have heard it four times. `488`

**489 — The other three pages, and a letter in her own hand.** Tuesday, the forty-third day, the first day of the ninety-second week. **A copyist of thirty-four comes four hundred yards to a room over a nail shop, the first time in eleven years she has come out of that building to ask a question, and asks for the other three pages of a letter that is addressed to a water board and not to a man.** They are about a hundred and eighty forms in six years and a half across nine buildings, and the hinge in a hundred and eighty forms is always the same person and that person has never once been the person the form is about; and a shelf eleven inches wide in the room where she sits with about nine hundred refusals in it, eleven inches from where she sits, which she has not opened and has written down eleven times that she has not; **and a lane works, and the one time in six years and a half she has seen a thing open without asking anybody's character was in a four hundred yard lane in a market town of four thousand people, and she has put the three things that town did in a paragraph in a round hand and has not put his name in any of them.** **The first disagreement of that week is not a disagreement and he knew it on the second page. The second one is real: she can build him a shelf and he can build her a lane and neither of them can build the other one's thing, and there is no form in this county and none in the nine buildings she has worked in that would let either of them try, and the four pages say it in the round hand of the counters and are signed with no name at all.** And then he writes back in the small upright hand, which is the hand that makes a letter usable, and the small upright hand is her hand, and a fair copy in it and a letter in it are the same thing on paper, **and he has made a thing of hers with no mark on it that says where it came from, and he cannot be asked about it afterwards.** `489`

**490 — Nine things read out in a room with the door open, and a gate with hinges on it.** Wednesday, the forty-fourth day, the second day of the ninety-second week. **A copyist reads out everything that was done in six weeks, with the day on each of it, and Ilyan asks to read it out himself and is refused in one sentence: a thing read out in a room by a man who cannot be asked about is the other thing, and about nine people in this town worked that out on the Sunday of the twenty-fifth day and she is not going to spend it twice.** Then what has not been done, said plainly: **the right of refusal is unrestored, and it has been used once, in a lane, on the eleventh day after the division, by a woman of thirty-four who is not a head of household and had no form and said no, and nobody has given her anything for it since and she is out of the water; the roll for the autumn is made in three days; the north heel is a season gone with the season about three weeks off and the money thirteen weeks off; the bar has not been turned on any of fourteen days; the out-of-roll book is not going to be written in this year; and the witness line is four letters and a stroke on a road four hundred miles off, and there is no way of telling from this town whether the name of the person who witnessed a refusal six years ago is in that range, and this town is not going to find out by asking anybody.** Then they go up the lane and about eleven people are at the head of the sluice and Sefa Lund puts her hand on a timber gate standing open on two hinges with the water going through it, **and says that is what this town has got instead of a line, and that about nine people came up the lane today who cannot be sent for and who are not on the roll, and that the hinge is not locked, and that there is no form anywhere in this county that says who may open a thing and who may not.** Nothing is resolved and nobody was thanked. `490`
