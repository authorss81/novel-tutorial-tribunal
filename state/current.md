## Where Volume 02 stands

**The volume's climax has been staged and paid for.** Mr Vale's question — whether a district may pay a person who has no address — was heard on the twenty-ninth in the open air at the First Verdict, and the answer turned out to be a category question, and the category turned out to be wrong. **The word *ward* is a guild's word for a duty a district owes and has not got round to, not a place anybody lives in**, and it was found in a bridge trade's term book by a man who cannot read a clause, and corroborated in a hundred-and-ninety-year-old standing of the wards in which eleven of fifty-five duties have ever been performed, in a traveller book's own front leaf, and in the one document in this case that does not use the word at all. **The entered finding: a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does.** A rate can be paid to a ward only if the ward is a body, and the ward is not a body because it is not a person, and the word that let a woman be paid at a gate for thirty years is a word for a job, and a job is done by whoever is standing nearest.

**Two texts were put in front of the room. Both cost the water.** Form L-3, eight clauses, written by a weighbridge convener with a cart on the scales and signed by him as his own and nobody else's, closes a gate, is payable, pays four hands, unhouses nobody, and puts a quarter of a penny on every bucket in Weir Lane for ever with nobody in it who can say no. The charter of the district of Halloway is nine sheets, four terms copied off a bridge and a fifth that wants a person, and costs half a farthing a bucket — smaller only because the gate is shut. **Ansa Rell voted her own weight for the clean instrument, in writing, signed, for ever, and was out-voted four to one, and is not defeated.** Bryn Sallow did not come to the hearing and the reason is on the record in her own words. Harrow asked the question nobody had asked anybody — *who in this room reads* — and Wenna Tace, who had been standing there for three hours, said yes, and took the page, and gave up the appeal, and asked for both halves of that to be written down. **The charter was made at the ninth bell on the seventeenth with a person in its fifth term and with a carrier's clause that names Ilyan Vester and stays his district's appeal if he refuses.**

**Ilyan took the smaller charter and paid for it.** His whole contribution to the choice was nine words: *I am for the charter, and the reason is that a gate is not the problem.* He is now the fifth thing in this city's record that he cannot un-enter, and it is the only one of the five somebody can call out and ask to see him, and Maren Kett told him on a step that he has made himself findable and that it took a month and required no good argument. **Stage 2 has become a practice and not a power: the afterimage does not clear when he rests, it clears when he is inside a burden of his own, and it is a four-hour limit and not a cure, and the woman whose hours he is counting has said out loud that he has made her thirty years into a job for a man who has a leg.** The token was used once and the third layer resolved into a rendering — a gate from two hundred feet, at the same height, every time — and a rendering has a vantage, and a vantage is a place, and the place is not in this city. **The fourth layer is a door. He has not resolved any of it.**

**The first appeal in the record was heard, and the discovery is what it is for.** It was served in four bells and two carts and it worked. The finding is about the *limit*, not the expansion: *on this span* is the court's own correct drafting and will not be widened, and the claimant knew it and filed anyway, and the output is a **duty to answer** — any bench that has received the instrument must say in public within eleven days whether it holds any page a person affected may swear to in its own room, and the court has no power to compel the answer. Nine benches answered. Seven said they hold nothing. One said **yes** and it was the standing authority of preservation, which holds every instrument printed in this city for four years and where no person has ever asked.

**It cost, on the ground, within a day.** Half a farthing a bucket on a barrel lid at a gate; four props carried back to a bridge that has wanted four for twenty-nine years; an account carried with a deficiency and no new props this year; a gate hand owed over a hundred thousand hours at a rate two of her neighbours set; a page kept by a woman who cannot appeal from it; a landlord who swore forty names for the first time in his life and got one wrong; a clerk who asked to be relieved of a print authority for the fifth time and was refused; **and a keeper of the fourth bridge who was asked what he wants for the first time in a month and said he wants the four words taken off a nail and put in a book.**
