**A right that exists and has no door was asked about, out loud, and the answer was no.** Wenna Tace asked a bench of four whether the rate for a haul could be appealed by any person and was told that it was set by a bench of four, that the only person who asked for it is the person who asked, and that a holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one and may not be named in one. **The finding: an appeal is a thing a person can carry, a rate is a thing a person has to be inside of, and the machinery in this city has a procedure for the first and not one word for the second.** She did not get the money; she got the sentence, and it is in the district's own book and will be read out four times a year. She then took on a second reader herself — a boy of nineteen who is not a holder, is not a clerk, is not paid and was not asked — because a page with one pair of eyes on it is a page that cannot be checked.

**A decision somebody could not take back was made by a salt seller.** Garvin Slade withdrew his consent to the rate in his own hand and in about two dictated sentences. The rate could not be withdrawn, because it had been read aloud to about two hundred people on the first of the month, and his name could not be rubbed out, because a clerk does not rub out a name. So a rate in the district of Halloway now carries underneath it the name of a man who does not agree with it, and a withdrawal is a different kind of object from a rate, because nobody can be inside one. **A rule made by the bench for itself says such a thing is read aloud again inside the quarter at a pump by the woman who cannot appeal from it, and the cost of that rule is about two hundred and forty people going up a bank in the cold, paid by the people who go up it.**

**The one thing that changed on the ground in three weeks was not a rate.** It was a woman of sixty-one, Ottoline Fenn, who takes in washing and has drawn at that standpipe for nineteen years with a yoke and two buckets four times a day. She stopped on the sixth of the fourth month and now walks four miles to the ninth bridge's pump at the fourth bell before the count. **She has told one person, the man who withdrew his consent, at his counter, and she has not told the district, which still believes she comes down to that pump every morning, and the lost penny cannot be entered as a loss because nobody collects buckets.**
