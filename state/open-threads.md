# Open Threads

## Immediate threads after Volume 02 Batch 0004

1. **What a page is for.** This is the question the batch ends on and it is Batch 0005's engine. **A hauler of twenty-nine, on a bank, at the sixth bell, in front of about two hundred people, has asked out loud for the first time in four months in this city: nobody has ever told me what this page is for — not what it may be used for, which she has worked out twice a week in four minutes, but what it is *for*.** She has sent it to a recordwright at her own cost, with her own name at the top of the letter and the recordwright's at the bottom, because she is the holder and may not be in a support of anything. **The recordwright read it, did not answer it, and put a licence with four terms on the table and read the fourth one twice, and wrote four lines in the back of her own book that are in no record but hers.** The fourth term says she takes no matter of her own while she holds this one. **The next batch may put the family claim's missing page in play as a thing she is prevented from chasing, and may not resolve it.**
2. **The appeal's survival as a durable right.** It advanced by three things, none of them a ruling: a definition of a load entered by a bench of four on the fifteenth of the fourth month; a man's name under a rate that cannot be withdrawn; and a rule that any person of a district may ask for a page to be read aloud at a pump, twice a week, and not be asked why. **All three cost somebody an hour, a day, or a bank of mud in the cold, and none of them is secure. A right that depends on a boy of nineteen being willing is not a right.**
3. **The bill that was never a notice.** Meridith Vale's letter of the second of the fourth month: the charter costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year out of another district's fund, the year as set out at the review leaves forty-four pounds and four shillings, and **a hundred and five pounds came back into a line the charter cannot be moved into without a bench's minute. A poor district's law is paid for by a bridge's road fund four miles away, and the payer is not the district asked and cannot be asked.**
4. **Two debts and no money.** The thirty years is now one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds *and* a hundred and fifty hauls at two thirds, in the gate hand's own words, and neither can be paid. The hundred and fifty hauls have **no figure at all**, because the district's own rate is a rate for a load and there are no loads in this district. A bench of four may agree a rate for a load or may not, and it has not.
5. **A washer's four miles.** Ottoline Fenn, sixty-one, has stopped using the standpipe and walks four miles to the ninth bridge's pump at the fourth bell before the count. She has told one person — the man who withdrew his consent, at his counter. **She has not told the district, and the lost penny cannot be entered as a loss because nobody collects buckets.**
6. **The continuity line, nine appearances, six refusals**, refused in the same words, nobody named, and the holder has still not read the line under the fifth term of the copy in her own room. **Harrow has said he will refuse on the seventh, and the seventh refusal is still ahead and is a volume-level promise for Chapters 91–100.**
7. **A form with EXCEPTION at the top of it** in nine districts, and the finding of Batch 0003: the word is not a label, it is a claim that a rule is temporary, and a form that says so can be used against the person who relies on the rule. It cost a man of thirty-four a season of four months and a roof. **Nobody can stop the printing, and the first district to print the charter's terms on a form headed with the word is still not found.**
8. **A short copy of the charter is already in circulation**, missing the second half of the fourth term, brought down a lane on a cart with no seal and no clerk. **Every copy of a term in this city stops where a person begins, and the thing that gets left off is always the part with a consequence in it.**
9. **A public answer is a price, and one desk is a monopoly.** Nine benches answered in public in eleven days; six of the seven *no*s answered again with reasons; Fallowditch withdrew its answer in writing because of a woman called Adela Cray and now makes its own rules with a date on them. **And the only *yes* in the city is a yes about paper.** Nahl's finding is in her own book with a date on it and in no record, and **it has now been echoed, in a room over a chandler's shop eleven miles off a fen road, by a man who does not know it exists and cannot have read it.**
10. **The Nine Yards.** Sixty-one people, nine gates, no district, paying Nethergill's rate for a bar they do not own, and a matter on a docket that concerns them and in which they are not a party. The four pounds and six went back into the slot. **The finding stands: a place can only be given a page by making somebody a body, and the only candidate is the carrier, and a holder cannot carry.**
11. **The fourth bridge's deck is open in the weather.** Four new timbers at ninety-six pounds, bought at the review, cut on the road above the fen on the thirtieth of the month before last and lying in the mud because there is no water in the river to float a timber up to a deck. **The man who keeps that bridge is the man sitting on it and nobody asked him.**
12. **T. Blore, twenty-two, has been told.** On the third of the fourth month, at the second bell, at the cut banks, in Oren Sedge's own mouth, with no messenger and no page. He is nineteen miles off, working a deck for Harl Mecken, and he is **one season of a span short of an apprenticeship that wants two**, and he was told the true thing nobody had told him, which is that he was not let go. **He has not been to a room, has not put his name down anywhere, and wrote on a shipping docket in the dark: T. BLORE. FOUR HOURS A DAY. ONE OF TWO SEASONS.**
13. **Corva Silt is a second head and she produced a witness.** She knows Ilyan cannot read, it is not on a page, and she is now the clerk who produced him twice, read four sheets of his own figures aloud in her own office by herself, put the three questions to him in advance, and said out loud once that a number can be wrong in a way that can be argued about and that it is the only door anybody has found out of a shape.
14. **The Bridgeward, two steps along and no further.** Not touched in this batch. Wen Lask's question has been asked in public twice and answered once, and the answer is entered as not being the answer, and the road is a good road and has not been touched.
15. **The rendering, the vantage, the room, the door, and the hand.** The vantage is a room with four people in it and one of them is a gate hand he has never seen at a table. It has not moved. His left hand is a scar at the base of the thumb. **The token is not touched and the fourth use still stands: it took the words.**
16. **Abel Kearn's wall and a survey in Fallowditch.** He took his name off a board rather than be produced, and on the twentieth of the fourth month he watched a woman of sixty-one walk past the ninth bridge for the eleventh day running and worked out that a mechanism you take off a person who has not offered it to you is the machinery that put his name on a board. **He has not used it and has said he is not going to.**
17. **What a load is worth.** A bench of four may agree a rate for a load in its own book, in its own hand, and may not appeal it, and it costs about fourpence a year to write down. **Nobody has done it and the hundred and fifty hauls have no figure.**
