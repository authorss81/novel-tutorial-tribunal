# Open Threads

## Immediate threads after Volume 02 Batch 0005 (Volume 02 is closed)

1. **What a page is for — answered, as a consequence and not as a remedy.** On the first of the seventh, at the sixth bell, at the turn, on a mud bank, to about two hundred people, the holder of the fifth term read out the quarter of a sentence off a facing leaf, with the gaps marked: **[  ] OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND [  ]**. **A ward is a duty and not a place, so the keeping of an entry is a thing a district owes, and a thing a district owes is done by whoever is doing its work at the time, and in four miles that is a hauler of twenty-nine who cannot appeal from one word of it.** A page is for keeping the record of a duty. This district has never written down a duty in any book at any time. **The right of refusal is not restored and the quarter sentence does not restore it.**
2. **The fragment and the three printings.** The middle of three printings of a form for lodging a matter in the Quiet Chamber, in a series running by plate in a building with no name on the door, carries eleven words — *and the keeping of it is a duty of the ward* — that the other two printings do not. **The word *the keeping of it* is a word the copy-holder made up, because the form could not read the line to the end, and a form is a copy and a copy is not a witness, and she did not use it to fill two characters she could not read.** The desk has been asked three questions and has said yes and no and a reason three times and **is not to be asked a fourth.** The First Verdict has no power to compel any of it and the quarter sentence is a fragment.
3. **The counting-house and the man who sweeps it.** Corrin Vask, forty-one, nine years, ninepence a day, a right hand six years gone at the wrist, four days of a leaf held flat, and a shilling a day entered in the building's own day book in the same column as the sweeping of its floors. **He wanted to be asked one question and was asked one, and he spent it on a box of about nine hundred and forty forms on the fourth shelf with a word at the top of each that he cannot define and would not say because she had not asked him that one.** A recordwright has made herself a door in that building and knows it.
4. **The two debts, now in one unit and in no coin.** A load is a barrow. A hundred and fifty hauls is a hundred and fifty loads. Six thousand hauls is about six thousand loads. **A unit is not a price, and the price of a load is the price of a tide, and the hauler has said out loud in a bench's own book that there is no day in this district that is a day, and that answer will be read out four times a year for the rest of her life.** The figure of one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds stands because a bench of four cannot strike a number a witness entered.
5. **A washer's four miles, and her answer.** Ottoline Fenn, sixty-one, **the eighty-sixth day** on the first of the seventh, told one person and not the district, and on that morning used the rule of the twenty-ninth of the fourth month for the first time in the two months since it was made, and was asked a question by a holder who has no right to ask it, and answered it: **she did not tell the district because the district does not know, because if the bench knew that a washer of sixty-one was paying a penny a day then the next time there was a hole in the fund somebody would work out where a penny a day was coming from and it would not be a person, it would be a ratepayer.** The lost penny cannot be entered as a loss because nobody collects buckets. The eighty-six mornings cannot be entered as a burden. There is no instrument in this city that carries them.
6. **The continuity line: ten appearances, seven refusals**, in the same four words, nobody named, and **the holder has still not read the line under the fifth term of the copy in her own room.** The tenth appearance is in a copy made by a clerk copying word for word out of a copy, and the clerk has entered that she did not know what she was copying. **Nobody in this case will tell the holder, and this batch did not, and the seventh refusal was said out loud in the open air before about ninety people and the sentence did not change.**
7. **The form with EXCEPTION at the top of it**, in nine districts and in a box of about nine hundred and forty in a counting-house, and the first district to print another district's charter terms on one is Coldwater, where a clerk of fifty-one ordered four hundred and sixty-three of them on a correct sum, read the word, thought about it for the better part of a minute, printed it anyway twice more, and entered all of it against herself. **Perdy Sill, thirty-eight, gave up a pitch at the bottom of a slipway. A form does not take the clause. It takes the paper, and the paper is the only thing a poor person in this city can put in front of a man who has a cart.** No court and no bench has power over a form and nine benches have now said so in rooms with no reader and about nobody.
8. **The bill, and the ranking in the spring.** The charter of the district of Halloway costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year out of a fund of four hundred and ten four miles away, the year as set out at the review leaves forty-four pounds and four shillings, and the charter costs twelve shillings over the line. **Meridith Vale has said out loud, with a date, that when the ninth bridge's year is set in the spring he will write that charter below the hand under the deck, because a fund is a list of lines written in the order of what they buy and he cannot put a room above a body, and he has refused in advance a minute of his own bench that would let a bench of four four miles off ask for the hundred and five.** The price of a poor district's law in the spring is the price of a hand under a deck, and that hand is a man of twenty-two nineteen miles off who is not named in the charter and is not a party.
9. **A copy that is short.** A copy of the charter travelled seven miles with no seal and no clerk, is sworn, is true, binds everybody in a district of about four hundred people, and is short of the second half of the fourth term, which is the part with a consequence in it. **The giving was done by a drover on a cart on the twenty-seventh of the second month and nobody will ever be able to say who gave it.** A woman of forty-four with eleven years of pay days on a slate in a man's house came down a lane to ask whether the work record is portable, and it is, and there is no record to carry, and there is no line anywhere in this city on which a person is paid to put a name on a figure.
10. **A page with no successor, and a carrier with a duty.** The carrier's clause has now been invoked three times. The man named in it was produced a third time on the seventeenth of the sixth, in a district he does not live in, about a matter in which he is not a party, and the sending cost a district four shillings and sixpence of cart, a clerk's day, a day of a boy of nineteen who is not paid and a day of his leg. **There is still no instrument in this city that says how a second carrier is sworn in, and about four hundred people in one district depend on a man of thirty-one walking.**
11. **The Bridgeward, pushed for the third and last time and not answered.** On the first of the seventh, at the sixth bell, a boy of seventeen read out a question on a mud bank and nobody took it into a file. **A form at a gate on a good road has a line for a person to be named against each term; eleven names went in and all eleven are dead; the last of them is a woman of seventy-one's father and she has written over it twice and does not know why.** His question is about a person and not about a region: *whose name is the last one in that line, and what is a person doing on a form that nobody has to answer to, and how would anybody find out.* **The road is not touched and the toll has never been short, and this is one step further along and no further, and Volume 03 must not answer it.**
12. **Salla Kern's slate.** Eleven years of pay days in a man's house, no names on the figures, a man of sixty-one who said *is that so* and went on loading peat. **She is not refused and not helped.** The finding is the batch's: *a figure with no name on it is not a record about a person.* What she does next is not known.
13. **Ilyan's record has cost him a third production and the eighth thing.** The eighth thing is that his name is printed four hundred and sixty-three times in a form in a district eleven miles off, in a document that cites him when his standing forbids him to be cited as anything, and a man with a sledge has used it to take a pitch off a woman of thirty-eight. **And the trade: carrying a notice cleared his leg for four hours for the first time in two months and nine days, and the paper was the weight and the woman was not.** The leg does not clear and has not since the twenty-sixth of the fourth month. The rendering is unchanged, the vantage has not moved, the door in it is at the same height and the same distance, the token is not touched.
14. **The fourth bridge's deck is open in the weather.** Four new timbers at ninety-six pounds, bought at the review, still lying in the mud on the north bank because there is no water in the river to float a timber up to a deck. The man who keeps that bridge is the man sitting on it and nobody asked him. **Not touched in this batch and not to be given a second want.**
15. **A bench of three eleven miles off a fen road with a room of its own and four entries in it, and a boundary that is not going to be done this spring.** Perditta Nye's ordinary work has stopped. **Not advanced in this batch.**
16. **T. Blore, twenty-two**, one season of a span short of an apprenticeship that wants two, and no job. **Not advanced in this batch.**
17. **Corva Silt has asked five times in six weeks to be relieved of the print authority, been refused five times, and did not ask a sixth time** — and in this batch said out loud that a note on the back of a form is a thing a person says in a lane and a form is a thing a person holds up in front of a carter. **A request that is never made and never refused goes on being true for ever, and the only way anybody finds out is the way a washer of sixty-one found out, which is by walking four miles.**
18. **Hulda Vane, fifty-one, clerk of Coldwater**, has entered the whole of it against herself, and her bench of five minuted in five hands what it had adopted and had not noticed. **She is not relieved and cannot be.**
