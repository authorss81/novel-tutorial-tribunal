## 1. Running after 450

1. **The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held, and the second seat at the council of the Shelf is vacant.** The council has to sit, and when it does the third line of the writ takes the roads, their keeping, any bar and the taking down of any bar back to an office. **A man of sixty-one has said in a room that it will, and nobody in this city has a way to stop it.** `446`, `448`
2. **Until then no bar on any road in this city may be taken down and no plate may be put up** — so the four hurdles, the nine links, the plate and the keeper's box are still up on the north side of the ninth marker, for a reason nobody in this city asked for. `448`, `450`
3. **The six-page form in the press window.** Ilyan's name on the fifth page in his own hand, Fenna Rusk's under it in a fair hand, *placed* struck through and *put* written over it. **The second person can be asked about the first for as long as the form stands, and there is no line anywhere for him to be taken off it.** `443`, `447`, `448`
4. **Twenty-two wards, nine never asked, and one line with the keeping of a box in it and nobody in it.** The office will use the figure 22. **There is no line in the six pages for a ward to say no in, and the man who wrote it said out loud in a room that he left it out because he is afraid of what a refusal would be asked to carry.** `447`
5. **The co-witness line.** Two names in one column, neither can take the other off, the office holds the mud, the day, the eight and the words said before the entry was made. **The two of them said out loud on a road on the last morning that neither of them can pay the other back and that he has stopped trying to.** `450`
6. **Ilyan's unsent return** — his name, four margin lines, a number, dated the morning he wrote it, **twelve days in his coat at 450**, and the only figure in this city in no book at all, and about thirty people want it. `438`, `450`
7. **Fenna Rusk's roll of eleven pages**, under a stone in a room behind a chandler's shop since the sixth day of the eighty-third week, knot reversed, folded across. **She has not been able to say whether it is right once in nineteen days, and there is nobody in this city who can see the fold and tell her, and no form that gets a person four hundred miles to look at a page.** `431`, `450`
8. **Hessa Marl in the box** north side, at the hour a cart comes in, four hundred yards from a crossing the office has now written down as a place of passage out of this country with her as its keeper. She counts crossings on her own slate: fifteen days, about two hundred a day, £5 2s 6d at the first row's rate, against nine visits in the office's book. **She will not say whether she would count a person going out. Nobody has asked her.** `441`, `448`, `450`
9. **The declaration of continuity** — entered whole, in a book at the seat, with a clause that governs everybody in a register whether alive or not, and a mark in a die that the city's die-cutter says he did not cut, and a date in a reckoning this city does not use. `449`
10. **Tarin Keel walking north**, entered fifth row on the office's own motion, never to be asked about city or road. **Kerwin Dace out**, return back stamped Cray Crossing with a name nobody here put there. `433`
11. **Sef, sixteen.** Not named on any list, not a power and not a rank, gave four doorways in order in the eighty-fourth week and has not been sent for since. **He was not in this volume's climax and that is correct.**
12. **Renn Calloway**, twenty-nine years on the Low Road, unpaid, with no form in the county that says a man may be sent for, which Marrow asked a room to write down as a fault and not as a virtue. `448`
