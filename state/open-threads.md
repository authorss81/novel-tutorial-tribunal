## 1. Running at Halloway after 460

1. **Nineteen houses at the Fen are not of this town, have no standing in the House, are not before it, and cannot be entered on the autumn roll without their own request — which they have not made.** **The House declined for them and the minute says both halves, and the woman it was done to said out loud in the square that she did not agree to not have a household.** The roll is made in the autumn. `460`
2. **The out-of-roll book.** A line, a name, a day. **Never written in in this town; the last entry anywhere in this county was a man at Ostrey four years back.** A one-line power the water board has and has never claimed, and the steward of forty years has the whole of his reason for never using it and has said it out loud. `454`, `457`
3. **The ninth clause, on the back of the board, a hundred and thirty-eight years old, with a date and a signed name and a witness line.** **Two names in the witness line and one of them is four letters and a stroke in a hand that is not of that town.** Nobody can read it, including the only person there who can read the Book and including Ilyan. **This is the volume's plant and it has not been paid.** `456`, `460`
4. **The two margins on the back of the fourth leaf of the copy.** *Struck by order upon a paper brought to me to be fair copied; the paper is not of this town and I have seen the hand of one man only on it* — and *I have not been able to learn whether the striking was of this clause alone.* **The paper is not in the House and is not in the county, and nobody has looked in ninety years.** `459`
5. **The rot at the north heel of the sluice frame.** A man's thumb goes into it to the first joint. **Two years. Nobody has written it in a book and the county cannot get a paper into a minute.** `457`
6. **The letter to Sera Quill, written and not sent**, with two names in a witness line in it and his own instruction to her that he has not sent. Her family's claim of six years is unfiled. `460`
7. **Ilyan's unsent return, in the inside pocket of his coat: twelve days old at 450, twenty days at 458, twenty-two at 460.** **Wenna Crale has refused to copy it into the town's book and told him to send it**, and said why, and the reason is better than anything he has said in nine weeks. `458`
8. **Wenna Crale's burden.** She is the only person who can read the old hand, she is paid £2 a year, and **there is no line anywhere in four hundred years of this town that says the reading may be done by somebody else.** She said that out loud on the Monday morning of his first day. `453`
9. **A stranger read a notice out in a market square and a town knows his name**, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person not of this town may stand in that square. `452`
10. **The number of fenrow households is somewhere between nineteen and twenty-three, because the row holds about twenty-three people, and it is not knowable**, and the number is the protection. `454`

## 2. Running in that city, untouched for nine days, and he does not know

The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held and the second seat is vacant; the council has to sit; the four hurdles, nine links, plate and box are still up for a reason nobody asked for; **Hessa Marl's book of the visits has no office to keep it in and she has said out loud that she does not know what happens to a book when there is no office;** Fenna Rusk's roll of eleven pages is under a stone behind a chandler's shop and she has not been able to say whether it is right once in twenty-seven days; **the declaration of continuity governs about a hundred and forty people who are dead**; twenty-two wards, nine silent, and no line anywhere for a ward to say no in; **a form with his name at the foot of the fifth page and a woman's name under it, with *beside* struck through and *under* written over it**; Renn Calloway, the bar, and a south side with no name anywhere; Tarin Keel walking north; Kerwin Dace's stamped return; Sef, sixteen; Bess Low, who has never once been counted.

## 3. Held, still not opened

South side unnamed and the only way past; nobody able to take the bar down; the removal of a woman who never left; the carrier's wage with no line; `co-witness` as work and not a rank; **the standing long question, in a clerk's own hand four hundred miles off: what did the First Witness sign, and which people are still governed by that signature**; about a hundred and forty dead, unanswered and unsummed; the twelve shillings and the three hundred blank names untouched, the blank correct; the bell, the porch and the corridor unchanged.

## 4. What 451–460 did not do

Nobody relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked. Nothing paid. Nobody expelled, and the word *expelled* is not in the volume. No new final enemy; the First Witness is not named, not addressed and not answered in 451–460. **No second document turned out to be the answer to the Bridgeward.** Nothing on a bar road is offered as the answer to anything.
