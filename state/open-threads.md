## 0. Hazards, not threads

1. **A `chapter:line` pointer moves when a line moves.** Pointers here were read after the repair; an unread one is a guess.
2. **Measure, never inherit.** Re-run the instrument in `state/batch-summary.md`; do not quote figures.
3. **There is no month.** `month` is at 0 over 461–470. Any date must come from `outline/volume-10.md` §1 or be a day-count. **The last band carried five instances of `month` in a loose spoken span and all five were cut; `in about a month, for a month` survives only as a person talking, and there is no `month` in any printed date line and none in 461–470 at all.**
4. **`chapter − 410`, the rail, is retired for this volume** and may not be printed in a date line. Do not restore it.
5. **He arrived on 452 at about the fourth hour, so 452 is day 0 and 470 is day 18.** **461 is day 9 and every "nine days" in 461 is a day-count in the town, not a week.**
6. **That city is forty-one miles off and not four hundred. The counter at Ostrey is eleven. Kerby is twenty-two. The lane is four hundred yards. The four hundred is Sera Quill's distance and nobody else's** — eight instances in three bands have been wrong and one of them was in a card in this band's own prompt. **A new place gets a new distance and the new distance has to be written in this file the day it is invented.**
7. **`hall` is a DAY count, `chapter − 446`, printed as days.** `hall = 0` is chapter 446, the seventh day of the eighty-fifth week, and the seventh day of a week is a Monday. **The twenty-fifth day after the division is chapter 471, a Friday, and a chair of the water board wrote that day on a piece of paper in a room over a taproom on the sixteenth day and has not touched it since.**
8. **The chapters say *six weeks* and *nine weeks* in that city about eighteen times and they mean the run of the matter, not elapsed days.** Spoken convention, never printed. No band may convert it.
9. **The House of Halloway does not scale.** Nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, five of them in the ninth ward. **The number of households in a ward has never been the number of seats in it, and a sheet of forty-one names in the press has a room against the ninth ward and nobody has ever counted it. No card may build on *seats scale with households*.**
10. **THE MIDPOINT IS NOT SPENT AND THE PLACEMENT RULE BINDS.** *The town's majority uses its freedom to expel a minority* may not land in 451–460 and may not land in the first third of the batch that carries it. **It is not in 451–460 and it is not in 461–470.** Batches 0003 and 0004 only, **and never in 471–474.**
11. **THE ORDINAL OF A DATE LINE COMES OFF THE TABLE.** Ten of ten agreed in 461–470, the second clean run in two batches. **A band that writes ten date lines in a row has to read each ordinal off `outline/volume-10.md` §1.**
12. **THE BOOK DID NOT COME DOWN AT THE DIVISION-WEEK AUDIT.** It has not been off the beam since the audit nine years back. `453`, `456`
13. **A YEAR'S FIGURE AND A QUARTER'S FIGURE ARE NOT THE SAME FIGURE, AND A BAND THAT ADDS ONE TO THE OTHER WILL PRODUCE A SENTENCE THAT REFUTES ITS OWN CHAPTER.** The roll is wrong by 112 pence **a year**; the board's money in its hand is **a quarter**; 112d a year is 28d a quarter; the quarter is £8 16s 8d on the roll as it stands and £8 19s 0d on the corrected count, and the heel is £9. **Print the unit in the same sentence as the figure.** `469`
14. **A COUNT OF HOW LONG AGO IS A DAY-COUNT AND NOT A WEEK-COUNT, AND THE IDIOM *THE FRIDAY BEFORE LAST* IS WRONG WHENEVER THE LAST OCCURRENCE WAS ON THE MOST RECENT ONE.** The last band had eleven of these: *the Saturday before last* for an event four days old, *a fortnight ago* for an event seven days old, *nine days ago* for an event eight days old, *thirteen days* for fourteen, *twenty-first day* for the twentieth, and *six days off* where the day was nine days off. **Prefer the day-count from the division, which is checkable, over a named weekday, which is not.**
15. **A CARD MAY CARRY A FIGURE THAT THE CANON DOES NOT SUPPORT, AND THE CHAPTER MUST REFUSE IT AND THE REFUSAL MUST BE RECORDED.** Two did in this band: the out-of-roll book's "once in twelve years", and the county book "four hundred miles off". **The chapters are the canon and the card is design.**
16. **A NEW INSTITUTION ATTACHED TO THE VOLUME'S CENTRAL PLANT IS A FLAG.** The first draft of 468 put a register of defect-return signers into a letter, with a number the letter did not contain, in the band whose job is to advance the witness line by reading a road and nothing else. **The witness line is a hundred and thirty-eight years old and the only new machinery in this band is a county office that can make a return and nothing else.** Do not build anything else against the plant.
17. **A REPAIR LOGGED AS CLOSED THAT WAS NEVER APPLIED.** Six now, and the two newest are the worst kind because the table says they are done. `469:117` carried *eighteen days in this town* under a date line saying *seventeen*, under a §3 row reading `469:117, 469:135 | eighteen | seventeen`. **Only one of the two instances was fixed.** The defence is free and it is the only one that works: **after a repair pass, re-open the file and grep for the finding's own figure. Do not trust the row.** A figure that appears in a table, in a summary and in a prompt is still wrong if it is wrong in the chapter, and `469:77`'s 112 pence shows the other half of this — **the same class of error, a record that cannot be checked against the thing it describes.** Grep the chapter.
18. **NAME WHAT A FIGURE IS A FIGURE OF, in the sentence that uses it, whenever two figures of the same shape sit close together.** `469` had 152 pence four lines above 112 pence — what nineteen households bring in, and what the roll under-charges by — and named neither. **Both were correct. A reviewer and a reader both merged them, and the reviewer proposed rewriting three canon chapters to fix a line that was already right.** The failure is not arithmetic; it is an unlabelled figure. Cost of the label: one sentence.

## 1. Running at Halloway after 470

1. **The out-of-roll book.** Never written in in this town, never offered to anybody but one woman in a lane on the eleventh day after the division, refused in a lane, and still in a drawer in a house with the key in the chair's pocket. **The fenrow are on the roll by a steward's form and not by the board's power, and no minute in any year says the book was offered.** `454`, `457`, `462`, `465`, `470`
2. **The ninth clause, on the back of the board, a hundred and thirty-eight years old, with a date, a signed name and a witness line carrying two names, one of them four letters and a stroke that nobody in that town can read.** A chair of the water board said out loud on the twenty-fourth day after the division that nobody in that town has ever been given it and nobody is going to be given it this year. **This is the volume's plant and it has not been paid.** `456`, `460`, `470`
3. **The two margins on the back of the fourth leaf of the copy.** *Struck by order upon a paper brought to me to be fair copied; the paper is not of this town and I have seen the hand of one man only on it* — and *I have not been able to learn whether the striking was of that clause alone.* **The paper is not in the House and is not in the county, and nobody has looked in ninety years.** `459`
4. **The north heel of the frame.** Three-eighths of an inch in nine years, measured with a straight edge and a nail at the eleventh hour of a Thursday in the rain, and a season instead of two years. **It is on the wrong side of a leaf in a press any person of the town may read, and it is in a book twenty-two miles off at Kerby, and the bill is nine pounds against £8 16s 8d in the board's hand, and there is no form that lets a board borrow against the next quarter.** `457`, `463`, `469`, `470`
5. **A sheet of ten things in the chair's own hand going into the press with a day on it.** He read out the first two and would not read the other eight. **The second of the ten is the north heel.** `470`
6. **The roll stands corrected at 1,060 and is wrong by 112 pence a year.** Nineteen doors are twenty-two households, eleven other rows in the town hold households and are entered as land, and a twelfth row has a dead man's name against it and cannot be got at. **The roll for the autumn is made on a day, out of a day, by a steward who will do it correctly and will not know.** `466`, `467`, `470`
7. **A corrected roll is a corrected constituency.** Nine wards, one head each, forty-one in all, five of them in the ninth ward, and the fifth head of the ninth ward is not elected until the autumn. **Nobody in Halloway had ever been told what the House is made of and a man of thirty-one said it in a square and it was the true sentence and the worst thing he has done in this town.** `466`
8. **The letter to Sera Quill and the answer to it.** He sent the letter on the twenty-second day after the division with the witness line in it and a paragraph about a figure that is the only one of its kind in ninety thousand people. **She has not answered his question, has written down in a day book of her own that she has not answered it, and has written back in the round hand of the counters because he asked her not to, and she is right that a voluntary exit is a right and not a privilege, and he is not, and the answer is on a road and he has not read it.** Her family's claim of six years is unfiled. `460`, `468`
9. **Ilyan's return, sent.** In a book with a head on it at a counter eleven miles off, with *no answer* in a column and no person entered for it to go to. **The figure is the only one of its kind in ninety thousand people and the office that wants it does not have it, and he is the one person who cannot be asked about it.** `458`, `470`
10. **Wenna Crale's burden.** The only person who can read the old hand, paid £2 a year, and **there is no line anywhere in four hundred years of this town that says the reading may be done by somebody else, or the copying of a list, or the keeping of a day book.** `453`, `470`
11. **A stranger read a notice out in a market square and a town knows his name, and he has now put Halloway — a town that has never been on a list of any kind — on one, at the bottom of his own return, where a clerk will write it and nobody can check it.** `452`, `470`
12. **The number of rated households in Halloway is a figure that has been found rather than a count, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a person may find one.** `467`

## 2. Running in that city, untouched for nineteen days, and he does not know

The office of Regent of the Shelf is not held and the second seat is vacant; the four hurdles, nine links, plate and box are still up for a reason nobody asked for; **Hessa Marl's book of the visits has no office to keep it in and she has said out loud that she does not know what happens to a book when there is no office;** Fenna Rusk's roll of eleven pages is under a stone behind a chandler's shop and she has not been able to say whether it is right once in twenty-seven days; **the declaration of continuity governs about a hundred and forty people who are dead**; twenty-two wards, nine silent, and no line anywhere for a ward to say no in; **a form with his name at the foot of the fifth page and a woman's name under it, with *beside* struck through and *under* written over it**; Renn Calloway, the bar, and a south side with no name anywhere; Tarin Keel walking north; Kerwin Dace's stamped return; Sef, sixteen; Bess Low, who has never once been counted. **And a sheet with a head on it is in a book at a counter with a clerk of about thirty and a bad cough, and she shut the book, and nothing in that city has moved.**
