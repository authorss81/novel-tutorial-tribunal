## Goal and outcome

**The volume's design gives this batch, verbatim from `outline/volume-04.md`: *the hidden transfer of the burden is exposed as a fact about a register and not as a claim about a man. Halm is right about it and it is still a harm. The form with a word at the top of it comes back, and it is Volume 02's device and it may not be restaged; what comes back instead is the fact that a form can be numbered again.*** **It is done, and it is done as a fact about a register.** **Nothing was won.**

A reeve of fifty-four establishes in a lane that a discretion is not a refusal and that a thing which has run out has not been withdrawn, and works out on a bin-board that the district's own cut has made its lease about a hundred loads cheaper. The reader of the district comes down the lane unasked and says why she is not going to sign. A carter of forty-six offers in the same lane, without being asked, to make an application he cannot read, and the man who invented the practice is not allowed to ask him. A hundred numbered sheets are printed for a pound and five shillings, paid for by a carting company out of its own pocket, and a boy of nineteen says the whole thing out loud twice in a lane: **a form can be numbered, and a number can be cited by a clerk who has never met you, and the same act can be produced against you.** The account's own book of entries is copied at fourpence a sheet out of a reeve's apron and read in a room four miles off, in numbers, and the transfer is named in it as one comparison that closes: **the register's own valuation of a piece of paper, translated into this district's own book, is exactly the price of the piece of paper, and the load is not in it.** The matter is not decided on the day it is heard, because for the first time in this county a person carried a matter and asked for a day. A Chancellor comes to a yard for three hours about seven thousand loads of grain, says four true things and a fifth that is his, and is right about all of it, and it is still a harm, and the two of those are not in tension and no room in this county is going to make them one. A convenor of sixty-one reads eight items out of his own book and for the first time in nineteen years there is no half of his sentence to take back. And a reeve of fifty-four writes a number on a sheet at the second door, in her own hand, on purpose, and it goes up a towpath in a basket, and the finding is that a number is the cheapest thing in the world to put on a person and the most useful thing in the world to put on a document and the two are the same act and the act is done by whoever is holding the pen.
