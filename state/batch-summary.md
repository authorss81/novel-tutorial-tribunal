# Batch 0003 Summary — Volume 03, Chapters 121–130, *The Saltglass Coast*

## Goal and outcome

The third prose batch of Volume 03, and the batch `outline/volume-03.md` gives the false merge in its full instrument and the fourth production to. **The three Gannock brothers are staged, and they are staged as themselves and not as a cameo in somebody else's chapter, and the disagreement between them is the batch's spine and it cannot be settled.** The batch's finding is the narrowest and the worst thing in the volume so far, and it is ordinary: **three accounts of one crossing, the same three questions asked in the same words, and one sentence at the top of a page, and every phrase in that sentence is out of one of the three true accounts, and the three were in three different places.** The merge is legal. Nobody is asked to agree to anything. Nobody on that coast ever finds out.

**The batch's cost is a carter of thirty-three, and it is a person and not a document's price.** A day and a day's hire, two men paid for a day whether they were there or not, a day and a half of the long way round, and a load of stone that is paid for and does not care. He is not a mystery, he is not a villain, he is not angry in a speech, he is right about most of it, he has no standing and no way to find out, and **he has never met any of the Gannocks and is not going to.** He is also the man who filled in a guild crossing card eight years ago for a man who could not read, and **the two facts about him are in the same story and are not joined on the page, and the card is not mentioned in Chapters 124–130 and may not be.**

**The batch's production is the fourth, and the mechanism is right, and it is four hundred years old, and it is in nobody's book: no person on this coast answers for another person about a crossing.** It cost somebody else a day. What produced him was that he was holding the paper. Nobody sent for him and nobody had standing to send for him, and he had been on this coast thirty-eight days without one person asking him for one thing. **The whole shape of the failure is that a correct instrument on a coast with no consent in it produces the person standing nearest instead of the person the instrument is about.**

**The batch's cost said twice, in the trade's mouth and not in a room: a pane is a person, a fire and a year, and the year is eleven months of the back of a woman of twenty-four who is paid nothing, and it is paid for in advance, and nobody has asked her.** A new firing was ordered and did not begin, exactly as the guild's fifth pane was ordered and did not begin. **The glassmaker is right about what a saltglass record is and wrong about what it is for, and the person who tells him is the twenty-four-year-old and not Ilyan, and he is not persuaded and is not rescued and is not given a revelation.**

## What the batch delivers, in order

He is right about his own absence and wrong about the other two. All three accounts are given and **none of the three of them is lying**, and the reason is in the prose: the shallow where the boat is and the flat where the cart was are four hundred yards apart and are not the same water.
2. **Chapter 122, the second, mostly outside, a hard above the tide line with the boat on her bilge and the garboard up.** The whole of the coast's water in a boat's carpenter's mouth and in the negative, and **a bar day is the only day the shallow is a road**, true about eleven times in twenty, and the eleven is not a fixed eleven. 3. **Chapter 123, the third, in a net loft over the drying yard with a fire in the grate and the tide coming in under the boards — not the guild house, not the tide office, and not a room with a table in it, because that is where Batch 0002's false merge happened and this batch's instrument is the opposite of that room.** The same three questions asked in the same words, three accounts written in full and read back to the people they belong to, and **one sentence at the top of the page.** A woman of thirty-six reads it over his shoulder, says no twice, names the exact mechanism, asks for one thing and gets it, and is thanked for nothing and is not a villain for having asked. **The failure is not that he lied and not that he moved her words. 4. **Chapter 124, the fourth, a bar day, mostly water and weather.** The shallow crossed in front of the reader and the reason it is a road, given as physics and not as feeling: the ebb is short and hard on a bar day and it brings the sand out of the hole in the middle of the bar and fills it, and for the length of that ebb the hole is not a hole. **Then a cart's wheel comes off, and the smallest possible physical test is a length of line across a rim at four places, and you cannot argue with a thumb, and it settles nothing.** A sprung nave with shingle in the felloe ends, a plate and pegs, a punch in the hoop, and a wheelwright two Reaches down who says **a repair is not a date** and that a maker's mark is a smith's name and worth nothing, and that you can get a condition off a wheel and a condition is not a season.

The deaf ear is the family's and not an accident of one man's childhood. 6. **Chapter 126, the sixth, a bar day, the guild's own works on the flat.** A chain, two shorter posts, nine yards of sea wall, a man of fifty-four who has stood on that chain for nineteen years. **In the middle of it the merged account is used, and the use is reasonable, and it is correct as a precaution, and nobody knows why.** A page in a dealer's hand says six inches at the north end; the guild's own instrument on a wall four hundred miles says four feet; the difference is three foot six; there is no way to put them side by side in a room, because the only thing that would do it is a pane and the only pane in a room is the guild's and it does not come out of that room. **What the man on the chain worked out over two days and said out loud to nobody is that he did not have to know which of the two was right; he only had to be sure he was not the man who put a loaded cart over the flat on the strength of a sheet a dealer copied out of a stranger's book.** The tide is held. A carter of thirty-three loses a day. He says what it cost, says he is not saying the man is wrong, says he wants it said, and asks one question and gets a true answer that does not help him.
7. **Chapter 127, the seventh, a bar day, the turn, and the person it hit.** Gell Roke with a hammer and a boy of about ten with the pot, painting the mark under the water at the harbour mouth, which is the only moment in the week a man can do it. The chain nine times. 8. **Chapter 128, the first of the seventh tide, a good day. 9. **Chapter 129, the second of the seventh tide, the fourth production, and a cost in a body and a day.** The guild's quarter going up the coast road, and a line in it for a person with no name at the end of it, and a harbourmaster who will not write one and will not ask anybody to write one, and says why to a clerk of twenty-two because she is the only person on that coast who will. **And the custom: no person on this coast answers for another person about a crossing.** A return about a road that is a person cannot be made out by anybody but the person; a blank is worse than nothing, because a clerk two hundred miles off will write *abandoned* on a road that four carts go over every week of the year; there is no second door and nobody to send. **So it goes in the cart that goes up the coast road that afternoon, with a piece of string round it, and a man with a bad leg was standing in that building and took it and did not know until the next morning.** Then four miles up the coast road and four back, and a hut, and a woman of eighty-one knitting, and a boy of eight about whom nothing at all happens, and an ask of four minutes, and a no, and **a reason that is hers and is not given and that he is told she is not going to invent one for him.**
10. **Chapter 130, the third of the seventh tide. ## The two relationship milestones the batch owed, and what they look like on the page

- **Ilyan and the ferry family's three brothers: the first time he has to choose between a truthful account and a usable one, and the first time he carries somebody else's words and changes them by carrying them.** He does not choose. He assembles, and then the assembled thing is used, and by the time he knows it has been used a carter has lost a day. **The words he carried and changed by carrying them are three of them — Cleve's *a cart came off*, Dell's *the channel was not in the low*, and Sorrel's *the water was under six inches* — and he changed them by putting them in a different place, and he did that on purpose, as the thing he is best at, and he had said out loud four days earlier in a drying yard exactly what he was going to do and why it is a bad thing.** He does not get credit for noticing it. He writes both versions on a page with the date and that it is his and **no reason on the page, for the fourth time in his life**, and decides not to take it back, because there is nothing to take it back to, and because the measure was reasonable, and because he is the man who made it, and because nobody on that coast will let him put it down. **The first time he has to choose between a truthful account and a usable one, he makes the usable one, and he makes it out of three true ones, and the cost is not his.**

## The three Gannock brothers, staged, and the disagreement that cannot be settled

- **Cleve Gannock, thirty-one**, the youngest of the three, dealer in what the flat boat brings in, the only one of them who does not go on the water, a house with a good roof. **Wants one sentence said out loud once in front of both brothers, and the sentence is that their father crossed on an ordinary day with an ordinary cart.** He is right about his own absence — he was at the head of the lane with the beasts and did not turn round — and wrong about the other two, and he is not a liar and does not think he is.
- **Dell Gannock, forty-two**, with the wheel. **His account: the cart was in the water to the axle the whole nine hundred yards and Os Tern was in the water to his middle on the off rein for four hours and their father walked at the far end with his hands on the boards and his boots off, not frightened, sixty-one and not a hand at a wet cart.** He said it once and then he stopped, and when his brother said *then a man on a bank would have called it ordinary* he said **that is your business and not mine.**
- **Sorrel Gannock, thirty-six**, the flat boat and the towrope, and the only one of the three who will take a boat over the shallow on a bar day, and has done for eleven years in her father's place and for nothing. **Her account: under six inches at the lowest the whole two hundred yards, the channel across the shallow and not along it, four hours watching the gravel go, and I never saw your cart, Dell. I never saw it. Not once.** She is not asked to be believed and the reader is not asked either.
- **THE MECHANISM, and it is the only thing that lets all three be right: the shallow at the north end, where the boat is, is two hundred yards of gravel with a soft place cut through the middle of it; the flat, where the cart was, is nine hundred yards of shingle with a channel down it that moves; they are four hundred yards apart and they are not the same water and have never been the same water.** A man at the north end on a good day sees the channel lying across the shallow, because the ebb out of it drains and leaves it flat. A man at the wheel in the middle of the flat sees the channel lying along the flat, because that is where the running water from the last tide put it. **On the morning in question the channel had come out of the low about three weeks before and had made a new one across the flat and had not made one at the north end at all.** Where the channel was on that morning is not a thing any of the three was in a position to know, and the difference between the three accounts is not a lie: it is three people who saw three different pieces of a thing that took four hours.
- **The disagreement cannot be settled and Batch 0004 may not settle it**, and settling it would require one of the three to be wrong in public about their father, Ivo Gannock, two years dead, who could not write and could not read and was proud of a card for two days.

## The mechanism of production, and why it is right, and the fourth time

- **The figure: the guild's quarterly return, which carries the bar road as a slate of carts out of a basket once a quarter and has done for eight years, and the office writes the number down and does not check it. And a line for a person at the crossing, voted five to four on the fourth of the fourth tide, with no name at the end of it.**
- **The custom: no person on this coast answers for another person about a crossing. Not the guild, not the assembly, not the harbourmaster, not a clerk. If a thing on a road is a person, that person says what it is, in their own words, standing at a counter, and not one word of it is anybody else's.** It is about four hundred years old. It is in nobody's book. It is the same shape as the reason a man will not sign for a woman, and it is the reason a crossing counts a person and not a cart. **Nobody on that coast could have told Ilyan why it is right and two people on it could have told him exactly.**
- **A blank will not do either. A blank is a road with nobody on it, and a clerk in a building two hundred miles off will write *abandoned* on a road that four carts go over every week of the year.**
- **What produces him is that he is holding the paper.** The sheet has to go to her; there is no second door and there is nobody to send; it goes in the cart that goes up the coast road that afternoon with a piece of string round it and a loop for a hand; a man with a bad leg was standing in that building and took it and did not know until the second bell the next morning that a sheet had gone. **Nobody sent for him. - **He cannot refuse it, because refusing stops nothing and the sheet does not come back — the finding from Chapter 113, which is his and not this batch's invention. A marked echo of that sentence is permitted. The slip of paper with five words on it is not re-staged and he writes none.**
- **The instrument is right and it is fast and it costs somebody else a day. - **Alma Gannock is not told, does not find out, and nobody gets credit.** She was not told about the card's bottom line; his one question with her, on the second of the third tide, is spent, and that is the reason on the page, and the reason is his.
