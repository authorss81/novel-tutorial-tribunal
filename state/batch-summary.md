# Batch 0004 Summary — Volume 01, Chapters 31–40

## Goal and outcome

The case moved out of local institutions and into the record itself. The seventh-morning-bell bridge review opened on a measurement, tried to close the hearing into a continuity docket, recorded the two-finger drift as a measured condition, and left the charter unamended. The batch's spine is that the Bench's own procedural caution — preserving a record it cannot trace — is a good rule, and it is the same *kind* of thing as the office that rerouted the bridge's maintenance term, and Ilyan could not prove they were the same and was not going to try.

The physical danger got worse in understanding and no better in fact. The drift's cause is mundane, fixable, and expensive: the west seat is bedded on a course standing in the bed of a channel that moved west six years ago, and water is washing the fines out from under the joint. The joint is not wearing. It is emptying, and it borrows two fingers on load and gets them back while there is anything left to get back. The guild bought the stone and won the day, and the day evaporated with the district's schedule.

The batch ends with the bridge physically safer and legally no more secure, a named local arrangement with a chalked end date, a full hearing set for the same morning the arrangement ends, a C—2 chain one documented step deeper with no person in it, and Ilyan with no standing, no constituency, and no constituency-holder to be angry at.

## Key events

- **The noon review opens on a number.** The court record hand Roke enters the deviation and the permanent set as two different measurements, on the keeper's word, because a record hand records what the keeper says. Ilyan's smallest request: not a charter, not a rule, not a precedent, not a reopening — a *pause route* for one named keeper, one named inspection, a stated number of bells.
- **The Bench attempts to close the hearing under the emergency continuity rule.** The maintenance-review condition's routing is under question, the chain after the rerouting is incomplete, and so the matter is consolidated into a continuity docket with no date, and a consolidated matter is not carried forward on local evidence. The move is procedurally defensible, nobody has been crooked, and it would have caused the stay to lapse at noon by the court's own procedural convenience.
- **The court continues the relief on its own ground.** Odile reads the court into it: a relief entered under a record does not lapse because the court continued the matter. The district objects and the district is right, and the ground the Bench gives is the *same* ground as the Saltwheel distinction that has been used against the household all batch, with the sides turned round. Nobody in the room is a villain. Tamsin Ro's crossing now rests on the court's refusal to be administratively careless, which is better than a schedule and worse than a door.
- **The carts are named as the affected party**, which is correct, which the district offers, and which removes the ward and the household from the affected-party column. Ilyan admits in public that he does not have a good answer to who pays for a stopped span.
- **The levy is spent.** The ninth-bridge maintenance fund closed Thursday afternoon. The guild works for nothing from the next morning and writes that on the board in the biggest chalk it has.
- **Bram Orren appears.** A seventeen-year-old bell-runner and apprentice mapmaker from the Briar March whose line crosses the span. He brings an oiled-cloth map drawn from fourteen children and a footman at the salt wharf, showing the channel moved west and the western abutment stands in its bed. The guild checks it with a lamp and a second pair of hands and the stone confirms it. He states his own want plainly — a closed span breaks four standing arrangements on his line for people who cannot write to a court — and asks for the figures for his line book, and is refused, and asks again Thursday. The *Orren*/*Sedge* collision is handled on the page once and does not recur.
- **The cause is mundane, fixable, expensive.** Take the load off, open the seat, re-bed in crushed stone and lime, cure three weeks. Five things needed: materials, hands, a day, a span with no load, and a wage. The district has none of the first four and the guild has none of the fifth.
- **A crew of six would have caught it four days ago.** The stain was in the salt crack's entry for six weeks and the salt crack is a surface reading. The under-deck watch has been one hand since the year the crew list went to one name, and a stain under a seat cannot be seen from above or reached from below alone. Two hands went when the second witness went.
- **The guild wins without Ilyan.** In the district's own office, Oren refuses on the guild's own ground and by not asking for the clause: two loads of stone, four sacks of lime, fiber delivered to the line, and one load-held day with six hands at the levy rate. The district concedes because declining would require its clerk to certify in writing that a span with an emptied seat is fit for unlimited load. Vale enters the true reason on the back of the slip in his own hand. Dain tells Ilyan to his face that the object of his eleven-day objection won an argument in a room where nobody needed him.
- **The district's price is a regional maintenance conference**, convened overnight at the old weighhouse at the fen crossing, on an agenda printed before anyone had said the word *day* out loud.
- **The common rule fails on procedure, not on opinion.** Ottla Prenn drafts a real common maintenance clause with a keeper's pause in it. The room cannot adopt it because two districts cannot produce their maintenance terms, and the conference's own custody index shows both of them held at a code with no office written against it — the same code that is sawn off the back of a district work slate, off a schedule margin, and in the routing of a paper carrying Ilyan's private words. Maren Kett stops the room from building a principle on a gap in somebody's shelf. Draft eleven circulates unadopted with the witness question open.
- **The witness question turns to Ilyan.** He refuses, correctly: his name is uncitable, so he cannot carry a list; and a post with no door is a charter with no door. His answer is that a witness list must be carried by something boring, local, paid, and impossible to promote, and the only such thing in the room is six crews with no mandate. Oren refuses it too, from the other side. Nobody is named.
- **The Ro household is entered in the ward's common register as a participating party, pending verification.** Not forgery: the register's own draft carries a party into a common register when an attached record's public account is entered in a district docket, so that one notice serves the record and its parties together. The district had spent the batch complaining that four records are four doors; it has now built a gate. A participating party has no column for consent, and the register has a column for objection and no eighth column for leaving.
- **The refused hold, used again in a new form.** Ilyan asks the Bench to hold the register entry. The district cites the Saltwheel distinction and wins, and it wins with the sentence Ilyan himself wrote the precedent for. He says so out loud in the lane: he taught the district that distinction, and the next person who uses it will cite him and be right. Larch enters the household's objection in his own hand and appends, on his own authority, that the register records no consent and has no column for it.
- **Tamsin offers what nobody asked for and nobody wanted.** The bell is a rope over a worn wheel on a pin and a hammer in a bell; it is fixed to the post and the rope and to nothing else on the span. It is rung by a person. When it rang on the day the span moved, the people on the span got off, because that is what it means to them, and they got off, and the span went on. Nobody had ever asked her what the bell *was*; everybody had asked what it meant. She sets five terms, including the fifth: her account may not be used to cure or support any refusal, and any use that puts it in front of a person as a reason to sign is a misuse of the witness and not evidence of the bell. She chooses the guild's book over the court's because a book somebody *wants* cannot be said to have been made for her.
- **Tarin refuses to let the bell account be taken as a witness to Ilyan's theory**, publicly, by name, and is right. Ilyan does not reach for the book. That is the chapter's cost and it is the chapter's work.
- **Neris's unstated reason for refusing to send Tamsin**, told to Ilyan alone in the lane: an inspector came to the shelter on the eighth day, was pleasant, asked nothing about the register, and explained that if the child could say the bell was not answered, the household would not be refusing maintenance — she would be describing a fault in the bridge. The district was offering Neris her daughter as a reason.
- **The print shop answers one question a day.** Sera's is spent; Ilyan's has never been asked. Neral, the lock-and-ledger counterman, gets them into the cold index room behind the shop and identifies the cut page's mechanics: the shop does not re-cut stock, a bound volume is unbound and rebound by a binder, and the paste is cold, not hot, which means the volume went back into service the same day. That is a decision about a book, not a person.
- **The answer is ordinary and damning.** Print run 114 was authorized by a **standing authority of preservation**, which is a piece of paper that keeps deciding the same thing for as long as the emergency it was written for lasts and does not need a name because it was never meant to be argued with. Its index for the week the current working plate was executed shows **eleven items taken into preservation, all routed C—2**, and two of them named in class: the ninth-bridge maintenance-review condition, and a sealed continuity request. Neither has a name in the column where a name goes.
- **Sera's item is not looked at.** The second item is a continuity request with a valid office seal, a C—2 code, and a missing witness index, and it is the most likely document in the city to be related to the continuity line. She declines to ask what is in it, in the cold room, in front of the desk, and Ondra Fell enters the refusal in the desk's day book. Ilyan does not put the two facts next to each other in anything filed.
- **Ilyan's temptation and its cost.** He wants to build a villain out of an office. Vale stops him and names the price: a preservation authority is the most defensible body in the city; if Ilyan goes on after it, the only thing that changes is that records stop coming — not to him, to everybody. The question is the wage, not the sin. Ilyan loses his easy access to records and keeps the harder line.
- **Vale tells him his standing has lapsed.** The grant ran one review cycle; the cycle ended at the bell; the review did not conclude; no new ruling was made. Ilyan's own grant could not be saved and nobody is permitted to extend it.
- **The schedule lapses at dusk on its own term.** No party breached anything. The instrument the schedule rested on was a signature, and nobody re-issued it, and the stone sits at a yard off the fen road at the district's expense and the district has no lawful reason to send anybody.
- **The arrangement is written by the bodies that will carry it.** Oren refuses Ilyan the pen. Four sources: the guild's own account, the road party, the pump's own take at the ward rate (Dalla Rusk, explicitly *not the ward*, two days short in public), and an eighth of a load-fee from nine named cart owners collected in A.'s book. No household pays anything, and the Ro household is named as paying nothing, in letters big enough to read from a cart seat. The right book records offers, not refusals, for the first time. End: **dusk to the fourth morning bell, and no longer**, chalked on the frame at cart height.
- **It is visibly worse than the Chapter 28 and Chapter 30 arrangements.** The district's copy has stopped, so the ward keeps its own; the roof delivery is now conditional on a review nobody has promised, and a carrier will not put a fen-road roof load on a road that ends on a date with nothing behind it. **Neris names the household that loses it first: Marda Rennick, room nine, and her son Besh, nine, who cannot be on a ladder.** A roof is a two-person job and the lower road has one household with two pairs of hands.
- **Larch enters the cause of the lapse in a public ward roll** because a record that does not say why is a record somebody can be frightened by, and says he would write it again and would be asked tomorrow why he wrote it himself.
- **Harrow returns.** He reads the file standing in the cold for forty minutes, confirms the request is built out of four things and no archive, and says the thing nobody had said: the district's objection that a scheduling is a reopening is the first time in this court's history anyone has had to say it, and the reason is that for twenty-two years nobody distinguished them, because nobody wanted a full hearing badly enough to be frightened of it.
- **Ilyan's standing makes the request impossible.** He cannot ask. The guild asks, in the name of a burden named in the record, on a slate Oren holds up. Ilyan's own job in the case is described in the room as a continuation of his case, and the guild is a fresh request. This is the batch's institutional loss and it produces the win, and Ilyan does not get it back.
- **The field couples five disputed records and the guild's working count book.** Tarin insists on adding the count book, on the ground that a field that tests records behind a bench tests nothing. The joint goes out **three** fingers and back, a figure nobody has seen. The pale drift line does not move; the brace holds.
- **Tarin's field action preserves the evidence.** A fifth mark appears in the count book's margin — a count-book cut for a load that walked and did not come back, a mark Ilsa has cut twice in six years and which has never once been on this bridge. Dain moves to secure it. Tarin walks under the rope, picks it up with both hands, and refuses: it is a guild record in a field, entered as a working record and not an exhibit, and a court clerk's time and his own signature are required before he will hold it again. He does it because he once enforced an order on a village on the strength of a record he did not test, and the village went under before the water did. Harrow's ruling on the field's effect: a field does not write; a working record agrees with the records around it as far as the hand allows and disagrees about the rest. Three hands have been in that book this month. Nobody can say which, and neither can the court, and no district objected, because a mark it cannot explain is a mark it cannot cross-examine.
- **The continuity line appears in a Bench ruling for the first time**, clipped and imperative, attached to a ruling about whether anybody can stop a cart: *CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD BEFORE ANY REMEDY IS STATED.* Roke says it is not on his tray. Dain asks for it to be taken out of the record. Harrow refuses to rule on it and says why in public: a court which cannot say what a line in its own translation is has no business amending a charter. Ilyan declines to build a finding on it, in the open, because he cannot say who said it, and Sera refuses in the same breath to let the Quiet Chamber continuity request be used to trace it, in front of witnesses.
- **The full hearing is set for the fourth morning bell** — the same morning the local arrangement ends. A reopening sets aside a preserved record; a scheduling sets a day. Harrow holds them different, knows he is ruling on a distinction he has just invented, and preserves the district's objection for appeal.
- **Sera corrects two of Ilyan's four load-bearing facts in public and is right about exactly those two.** The drift is a band, not a figure — one and a half on a light load, up to three on a heavy one, and a rounded number is what got written on a clerk's tray. And the schedule lapsed because a term ran out on an instrument nobody re-issued, not because the guild refused; the refusal is why the instrument was unsigned, and the schedule should never have rested on a signature. She leaves the two archive-adjacent facts alone, which is a vote.
- **The delegation.** Oren takes the physical witness and says he will describe, not advocate, because he now knows the difference. Ilsa takes the measurement and keeps the half because it is hers. Joren takes the road and the load sequence. Tarin takes the neutral boundary and will testify to what a field does not write. Bram's line book is named and refused, in the room, by Tarin, before it can be used. Ilyan keeps only the record and says in daylight that if a court finds a fifth thing he will not have been the one looking for it, and Oren says that is either the last honest sentence on the bridge or a man arranging to be blameless and declines to decide.
- **Moot enters it in the open**: *the attached record-holder has no remaining constituency.* Conservatory withdrawn, name uncitable, household a party under objection, ward seats unfilled, standing lapsed, one preserved attached observation that binds nobody.
- **Tamsin's consent is hers to give** and she gives it on four conditions: a copy and not the book, with all five terms written on the copy so they cannot be separated by a clerk who wants a sentence; she is not called; the fifth term is read first if anyone uses the account in front of her mother; and the court must be told out loud that the account went to the guild first and nobody made her. Neris adds: if the day comes when somebody puts her daughter in a room she will stop the whole thing herself, without consulting anyone, including Ilyan.
- **Sera names the one lever left and tells him not to pull it.** The sealed item in the preservation index has no author, no witness, and no office behind its name, and if a court rules on it nobody knows what happens to an item a court has ruled on. It stays untouched. They go in with four facts, a guild's book, a print strip, a lapsed schedule, and a district with no obligation and an argument it has not yet won.

## Relationship and practical changes

Ilyan's growth is delegation and a loss. He loses his standing through no fault of his own — the Bench's consolidation consumed the clock on a grant made at the first hearing — and he does not get it back by being cleverer; the guild asks for the hearing instead, and the win belongs to the guild. He loses his constituency officially, in writing, in a public record. He loses the easy version of the archive, because chasing a preservation authority is what makes records stop coming, and he is told so by the clerk it applies to. He gains nothing except a signed delegation, a corrected draft, and a hearing two days off.

Sera is competent, independent, and professionally annoyed. She refuses to let the Quiet Chamber chain be used, in public, in a field. She corrects two of four facts and is right. She names the one lever and forbids it. And she says the only warm thing anyone says in the batch, and says it in a lane, and then goes to stand in one.

Oren wins two arguments with his own hands in two rooms and refuses the third. He describes rather than advocates. He writes a load-held day's cost in a bell-runner's words on his own board, and puts a child's five terms in his own book with his own declaration that the guild is not a neutral body.

Tarin is the batch's field conscience and his act is the one Ilyan did not ask for. He refused a child's testimony in front of witnesses, held a count book out of a marshal's hands under oath, and warned Ilyan about Bram before Ilyan could use him.

Tamsin is the strongest person in the batch and is treated as such. Neris tells Ilyan the reason she has protected a door for two weeks, in a lane, and the reason is that the district offered her daughter as a reason.

## Panel accounting

**Four System-rendered entries across the batch, one each in Chapters 31, 35, 37, and 39.** No chapter uses two. Lighter than Batch 0003's six. Every one is a specific record — a review entry, a register entry, a preservation index, a hearing-field ruling — and none states a new general rule. All other bold text in the batch is a physical object in the fiction: the court record hand's tray, the guild's chalk board, Oren's four candidates, the district's supply slip, the two drafts at the weighhouse, the ward register notice and objection, the guild's book, the lapsed schedule entry, the arrangement and its four offers, the ward roll, the notice board, and the guild's rewritten work notice.

## Batch ending

The ninth bridge carries a named local arrangement with a chalked end date, four named sources, a pump two days short, and a load-fee that no household pays. The west joint is measured at one and a half fingers on a light load and up to three on a heavy one and has never been observed to accumulate; the permanent set of the deck line has not moved. The stone is bought and is at a yard. The load-held day was won and then lost with the schedule. The maintenance-review condition is still absent, and a keeper may still summon a pause and may not release the span. The count book has five entries and a mark in the margin that three hands and one court clerk cannot account for, and Tarin held it so that the record survived its own anomaly. The full hearing is set for the fourth morning bell, which is the morning the arrangement ends. Ilyan will hear it as a witness to his own attached observation, with no standing, no constituency, and one preserved entry that binds nobody.

The C—2 chain has one documented step more: a standing authority of preservation authorized a public print run without naming a person, and its own index shows eleven items taken into preservation in the week the working plate was executed, all routed to a desk with a stamp and no clerk, of which two are named in class and neither carries a name. The Quiet Chamber losses are still two events with two dates. The plate and the older witness index remain one document cut once and are not restaged. The continuity line has spoken four times and is not identified. The First Witness is not named. No new enemy has been introduced and the planned ending is untouched.

Chapters 41–50 open with the three remedies — amend the charter, break the span, or hold the hearing open — and the Ro household's legal access, housing, and work in front of the Bench, on a morning when a ten-year-old's account of a rope and a wheel is the only testimony in the case that describes how the bridge actually failed, and is in a guild's book, and cannot be shown to have been written by anybody.
