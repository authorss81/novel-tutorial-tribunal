### What the band owed, and whether it is on the page

1. **A name is heard by somebody who was not there when it was said**, and that
   person is a person on the page. **On the page.** Wenna Pyle at the foot of the
   steps: a voice came down the cart road in the wind with a man's name in it and
   went into an open shed while her own saw was going. The working is in her own
   mouth — nobody carried it, there was no man on that road with a message and no
   woman with a message, and she did not go and ask anybody for it.
2. **The figure of how many times that name has been said is not printed in any
   wording on any of the ten.** **On the page, and it survives the midpoint.** A man
   of thirty-eight carried it up a bank, said he had it exact, and put it back where
   it came from without ever setting it down. A man with a broom refused it and gave
   two workings. The man of thirty-one said out loud that he was not going to say it
   in this county, on that landing or down any road, and would do the same with a
   figure if one were brought to him. No *about n weeks*, no figure of who heard it,
   no figure of how many mornings ago.
3. **No chapter may print the shape *he took his hand along the wall*.** **On the
   page: that shape does not occur in any of the ten.** It occurs **four** times
   elsewhere on disk — `1017:67`, `1022:65`, `1028:47`, `1029:61`. Simon Rook puts
   his hand on the wall and goes *past the end of it*, so that it is in the air over
   both pieces of ash and out the far side, and says that a hand that has gone past
   the end of a wall has not found anything and has touched nothing, and that he
   could not see and would have to make something up, and that a man who makes
   something up about a thing he has touched has decided what it was like from having
   his hand there — which is saying it with his hand instead of his mouth, and his
   hand cannot be asked to stop.
4. **The standing offer.** On the page at three hundred and thirty-one on `1031`,
   one higher on each morning, three hundred and forty on `1040`, with the subtraction
   out loud in his own mouth on all ten. Asked on none, answered on none, withdrawn
   on none.
5. **No new name is put on anything in any of the ten.** On the page. No mouth says
   what that wood is.
6. **No hand goes on that wood to take anything off it.** On the page in all ten.
   Each of the ten now states this in its own words — see the repair note below; an
   earlier version of this receipt claimed the sentence was in the same words in all
   ten, which was wrong, and is corrected here.
7. **A question about that name is asked and not answered on every morning of the
   band.** On the page. A question is asked and unanswered on **nine** of the ten — `1031`, `1032`,
   `1033`, `1035`, `1036`, `1037`, `1038`, `1039` and `1040`. The tenth is `1034`, where no question is
   asked at all and the man it would have been put to says out loud that he is not going to ask it,
   which is the strongest form of the ten.
8. **No head count of people in any wording.** On the page. Six were in the first
   write and every one was cut by hand, because each of them referred to persons.
9. **The ordinary form is in mouths in four trades on three mornings and is given no
   name**: a chair is cut for a back and is not that back's, and a barrow handle is
   cut for a hand and is not that hand's, both on `1035`; a yoke is cut for shoulders
   and is not theirs (`1037`); a thing a man asks a day against is not that man's day
   (`1038`). The words *right of refusal* do not occur on any of the ten.
10. **No figure of how many things are left on that landing, in any wording.** On the
    page. Every title names nothing.

### What advanced and what closed

**Advanced, in mouths:** a name got off that landing without a carrier; the woman it
reached has it in her head and cannot put it down; a man of thirty-eight carried a
figure up a bank, put it back, and said out loud that he has had no job all week; a
man with a broom said out loud that he does not know which of them the name is for and
gave the working for not finding out, and offered the landing a daily sentence and
took it back inside the same week; a man who cannot see well held his hand in the air
over the strip and brought it back; a man who cannot read said he would rather be
owed nothing he cannot check than be lied to about in a year; a woman of forty-four
was refused her one question and went back to her wall.

**Closed: nothing.** The count of asking is spent and was not spent again. The figure
of how many things are left was given once, at `995`, and was not rebuilt. The right
of refusal is not restored in any wording. No mouth said what that wood is.

### Repair pass after review

Findings from `logs/batch-0004.review.log`, and what was done about each, is written
up in `reviews/volume-21/batch-0005-review-repair.md`. In short: a paragraph that was
byte-identical in nine chapters was rewritten in all ten; the lead was named in the
prose of all ten, as the volume outline requires; two chapters had a speaker
addressing him before he had arrived and one had him arriving twice, and the visit
order was corrected; the titles were cut from 125–159 words to short ones that name
nothing; descriptor paragraphs that restated the speech under them were trimmed; and
sixteen paragraphs over a thousand characters were split.
