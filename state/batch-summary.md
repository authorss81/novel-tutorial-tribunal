# Batch 0005 Summary — Volume 01, Chapters 41–50

## Goal and outcome

This is the volume's payoff and it delivers the concrete resolution `outline/volume-01.md` fixes. The ninth-bridge charter is **amended**: the maintenance-review condition is restored, an appeal route and a visible exit are written into the charter for the first time in the case, the Ro household receives recognized access, housing protection, and a portable work record, the maintenance burden is named on both sides, a public review date is set, and the bridge stays open under a narrow, renewable, time-limited exception. The chapter that put the clause text on the table is the chapter in which Oren Sedge signed the maintenance acknowledgment the guild had refused for three weeks, in his own name and not in the name of six crews.

The batch's two turns are both structural rather than rhetorical. **The hearing turns on a physical fact**: a salt cart held still on the west end produces the first reading in the case's history that does not come all the way back — out three, back, and *held at one, not returning* — under the one condition nobody had ever put the joint under, which is that the load was not going anywhere. That finding destroys the district's own framing of a keeper's pause as a power to stop a carter, and it hands the case its correct mechanism: the bell has never held a load in ninety years, it empties the deck, and a keeper's clause cannot be bigger than the machine it is rung on. **The hearing turns again on something a person pays for**: the district's money is offered, accepted, and then found to be arithmetically impossible, because a ward rate may be paid only to a body with a name and the ward is not a body. The person who finds it is the district's own clerk, who entered the reason on the back of a slip in his own hand with the hour on it, and who says out loud that the man who built the register gate is the man who cannot pay the toll. The payment is finally made in Dalla Rusk's phrase — the ward rate is a number and not a recipient, so pay the person at the moment of the work — and the first wage on this bridge in years is posted in chalk on the frame, by name, to six hands who cleared a deck.

Ilyan wins a settlement and loses something in the same ruling, and the loss is not the price of the win. He asked, in a corridor and then again in daylight in a public field, for the settlement to be entered as a **precedent witness available to other districts**, which put his name on a document four districts will read and cannot be taken back. The household's door would have opened identically without his name on it. Four days after the ruling, a rider brings a two-day-old print from a poor district on the low water: the same words, in the same order, in a different column, and the phrase **lawful traveler** — the charter's own two-hundred-year-old word for *registered* — doing the opposite work at a low-water culvert. Kett has warned him about the phrase in open court, been told it is the charter's word and cannot be rewritten on the last day of a hearing, and been believed by nobody. Sera told him in a corridor to be frightened of the difference between a precedent and a template. He is not.

## Key events

- **The pause is priced, at last, and the price is unaffordable.** Two bells for a load that can turn, six for one that must be reversed with a full approach behind it, eight as the ceiling, six hands under and above, at the ward rate. Oren states the reason a clause nobody pays for is worthless: a keeper who can take a carter's afternoon for free is a bell nobody rings within a week.
- **The district answers, and Dain has been carrying the answer since the fourth day.** The district will not carry a keeper's power — a district that funds a power by habit is a district that has learned to rule by habit — but it will carry the hour, at the ward rate, on a named day-book line, in chalk, paid to the person who stood there, and it will restore the under-deck watch to two hands, which is the second witness whose loss let an emptied seat go unseen for six weeks.
- **Ilsa reframes the mechanism.** The bell has never held a load; it empties the deck; the west end is where the hole is; a load with a full road behind it cannot turn. So a keeper's power is a *clearance*, not a *hold*, and the pause must be bounded by the loads standing on the deck rather than by anything a keeper says.
- **The physical turn.** Puck puts a rule seven inches into the west course from the drain side: the stone is gone, the lime is a skin, the water comes up four inches in a day. A salt cart with a full approach behind it stands on the west end. Out three, back, back — **held at one, not returning**, the first time in the case's history. The pale permanent set does not move, and Oren says so into the record. When Oren rings the bell, the entire court gets off the deck, including Harrow with the tray over his head, and the cart stays on the empty seat: a released span with a load on it and nobody on it. The cart is off in nine minutes and the joint returns all the way.
- **The money problem.** The district's four payments are all accepted and all impossible. Vale's reason is on the back of a slip in his own hand with the hour on it: a line in a book may be paid only to a body with a name, the ward is not a body, and the ward has no name because a year ago somebody named a ward and the naming was used to take a road off people who were not in it. He declines to make that a finding and enters it as scar tissue. Dalla Rusk supplies the fix nobody had written down — the ward rate is a number, not a recipient.
- **Hanne Kolm swears a book about burdens and not about tenancies.** Twelve years of an unsworn landlord's book with not one name in it, kept out of a sworn volume on purpose, and a sister who has not spoken to her about it in six years. She will swear a second instrument that records the burden refused, the person who carried it instead, the date, and what it did to the span, with no consent and no address in it, and she wants it as a column, because a book is a thing you can lose and a column is a thing you have to look at.
- **The props cost a second bridge.** Four of the guild's own props are the only thing that can hold the deck while the seat is open, and they come off the fourth bridge, whose keeper Wist Anhalt, forty-nine and twenty-nine years in the post, sends four words: the props are yours, I object. He is not a party and is not represented, and the ruling names his objection and orders the props replaced or paid for out of the district's road funds before the review. The ninth bridge's settlement may not spend the fourth bridge's safety in a document that does not name it.
- **The roof.** Nine bundles have been sitting at the eastern yard since the ninth day. The unloaded period runs from the seventeenth to the sixth, so the roof goes over on the day before the span closes, on six pairs of hands at the district's expense, at the ward rate, posted in chalk. Room nine is done first. Joren Vell refuses to write a roof into a bridge document, in public, on the ground that he has watched a thing somebody wanted written in the same hand as the thing the arrangement was about go to the east bank twice, and he will not do it a third time in front of witnesses.
- **Tamsin testifies at the span, with the rope, and it is a machine.** Rope over a wheel, wheel on a pin, pin worn on the underside where nobody can see it, hammer in a bell, fixed to the post and the rope and to nothing on the span. Harrow reads the five terms and lets a hard question go on purpose, and says out loud that he is letting it go. Dain's procedural argument — that the account is evidence that a known condition was unanswerable, so a maintenance acknowledgment would be a recognition rather than an imposition — is correct, and the fifth term kills it anyway, and the acknowledgment dies, and Dain asks that the district's own gap be entered: the wages imply a recognition the household has not given.
- **The bell's pin.** Tamsin supplies the part nobody asked for. The pin wears through, the rope goes slack, the wheel stops, and a person pulls a rope that produces nothing and does not know it. The wheel and pin are entered as span maintenance, inspected on a named day by two hands, one of whom has pulled the rope, and the ruling states that a keeper who rings a bell that does not sound is a person pulling a rope.
- **Oren signs.** The guild's own acknowledgment, in his own name, unpaid, with a term that ends with the keeper so that no successor is bound and so that a term can be refused by the man who carries it. The guild has not voted for it and he says so.
- **Ilyan cannot read the instrument and says so in front of three hundred people.** He follows about a third of Odile's second reading and loses the thread at the enumerated cases. Harrow enters it, on the ground that a precedent that hides the fact that its author cannot read it is a precedent that will be used against him. Sera has a read-back in the guild's words filed beside the instrument, and it contains the sentence the case turns on: *a keeper may not stop a load; what a keeper may do is call the deck clear; the deck being clear is the whole of it.*
- **Sera is licensed, and it costs her a shop and a habit.** A licensed recordwright of a public record, not Ilyan's advocate, on four terms: no withdrawal without an entry naming her reason; answers for the record and not the outcome; carries the terms to the review and to any district that asks; takes no matter of her own while she holds this one. She says in public what that is — six years of paying for rooms out of a business that has been quietly carrying her family's unfiled withdrawal claim — and says the family claim is not in this case and will not be.
- **The Quiet Chamber yields one phrase and no more.** On the facing leaf of the cut page, under the cut thread, the first line of the following sentence survives: *a refusal is entered, and the entry is against the burden and not against the person, and the entry is open to the ward.* There is a shadow of more and she declines to guess at a shadow in a public archive. It is one line of a removed page. It does not resolve the right of refusal. It explains why a ward register cannot be repaired by adding a column, and why a sworn book about a burden is a different instrument from a sworn book about a tenancy.
- **The ruling is ten terms and two refusals**, with the district's objection to the refusal of *break* expressly invited to an appeal. The maintenance-review condition is restored and the original wording is expressly **not recovered** and not supplied. The appeal route does not suspend access, housing, or work, and the court's own seventh-morning-bell ground — a relief does not lapse because the court continued the matter — is written into the charter so that nobody has to ask for it again. The exit is a refusal entered against a burden and not against a person, in a sworn column kept in the ward, taking nobody out of anything and withdrawing nothing already entered. An unlisted person is not a trespasser by that fact alone.
- **The continuity line appears a fifth time**, clipped and imperative, attached to a ruling the court did not make, and **the word has changed**: *stated* has become *entered*. Roke says it is not on his tray. Sera stops Dain from taking it out of the record, on the ground that a city which strikes out a line it cannot account for is the city that struck a page out of a binder with cold paste. Harrow refuses to rule on it for the third time, in the same words, and says the court is not going to be asked about that in four years.
- **The first wage.** The district's day-book line opened in chalk on the frame, six hands named, two bells, at the ward rate, and the pump's shortage of two days and half a day on Thursday paid, with D. Rusk not required to say thank you, entered on the record by Vale's own instruction because in four months nobody would otherwise know that a pump worker was told in public that her thank you was not required.
- **The template, four days later.** A rider, fifteen years old, eleven miles, a print two days old from Halloway — a poor district on the low water with a sanitation complaint in front of its own bench. The same words, in the same order, in a district's typeface, in a district's column, and in the fourth paragraph *lawful traveler* means something else entirely. Nobody can stop it. Kett will go and stand in their room, under protest, and asks that his written instruction be produced and read.

## Panel accounting

**Four System-rendered entries across the batch, one each in Chapters 42, 45, 47, and 49**, at most one in any chapter, and none stating a general rule. All four are records of this hearing: the opening entry naming the parties, the remedies, and the continued relief; the district's answer as produced, with the payments and the impossibility; the witness's account entered as a copy with its five terms; and the ruling, with the continuity line beneath it. **All other bold text in Chapters 41–50 is a physical object in the fiction**: the frame's chalk, the guild's costed slate, the board, the district's roll and the reason on the back of a slip, Oren's maintenance acknowledgment, the count book's entries including the word *nothing* and the line *held at one, not returning*, the ward's sworn refusal column, the strip of board beside the lawful-traveler register, the read-back in the guild's words, the amended charter as Roke read it, the day-book line, the Halloway print, and the inside of the back cover of Sera's ledger.

## Relationship and power changes

Ilyan's growth is a decision and a cost, not a power. He asks for the precedent in a corridor, is told to be frightened of what a precedent is, is frightened anyway, and gets a rider on a bicycle four days later. He is not rewarded for a good sentence. He is not punished for the archive either: the Quiet Chamber phrase is found by Sera, on her own initiative, under her own license, for her own reasons.

Sera is licensed, and the license is not his reward and not his counsel. She is the record of a bridge and she says so. She will correct him in front of a copy. She says in a corridor and later on a step that if it becomes affection it will be a separate entry and it will be dated, and no romance beat occurs in this volume.

Neris gets the crossing, the room, the work record, and a refusal she can enter — and pays for it by making her household's name a thing four districts will read, and by having named it herself three times. Tamsin chooses to speak, at the span, with the rope, under five terms, and supplies the bell's pin because nobody asked her what needed looking at. Oren signs in his own name. Tarin holds the boundary and says nothing in the field at all this batch, which is the correct professional thing and is noticed. Dain wins the refusal of *break* and loses the money, which is more than he expected. Kett is right about a legible rule and is proved right about its cost by a print he did not see coming.

## Deliberately left unresolved

The historical right of refusal, which one line of a removed page does not recover. The authorship question and the Shale Mirror. The C—2 chain and the standing preservation authority and the wage behind it, which nobody has chased and Vale's warning about records still stands. The responsible person for the plate/index cut, the levy ledger's cut name, and the woman paid off the load book, which remain three separate things and which Ilsa has still not been asked to decide. The fifth mark in the guild's count book, which is unexplained, and which Tarin would testify under oath he cannot account for. The Quiet Chamber's missing original page and Sera's missing duplicate, still two events with two dates.

## Review pass

An independent reviewer subagent audited Chapters 41–50 and **fourteen concrete defects were fixed**, with the prose preserved and no planned beat removed. The substantive ones: Ilyan's restored standing and his personal continuing burden were asserted in the state files but were **not in the prose**, and Harrow now reads the whole limited grant out twice before Ilyan answers, and Ilyan names the burden himself, and Sera enters the clause *and not the price of the precedent* as hers. The Quiet Chamber yield had been over-read by merging the known fragment *the governed may answer no* onto the facing leaf; it now yields one line, beginning with a lower-case *a*. Chapter 44 broke the three-measurement rule three times and had Ilsa saying the joint returned after the cart came off while the cart was still standing; all four fixed. Chapter 47 contradicted Chapter 36 and itself about the bell's pin. Chapter 50's dates could not all be true and were rebuilt on one anchor. The ruling silently changed the district's own rate and left the cart fee with no ending; both are now entered by Harrow on the record. A wrong age for the fourth bridge's keeper, a conference dated a fortnight early, a leaked chapter reference, three weekday names, a day cited before it happened, and a day-count drift of one across the batch were all corrected. ## Batch ending

The ninth bridge stands on four props over a channel with the west seat bedded and eleven days to cure. The count runs every change with two hands under the deck. The bell is rung at every change and its pin is looked at on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope. A strip of board an inch and a half wide is nailed beside the lawful-traveler register with twenty-three names on it, the twenty-third being a woman who walked eleven miles because somebody at a culvert told her the span was open to people who are not on a list. The lower road has its roof. The district's day-book line is open and has been paid. A print is already in a poor district on the low water doing the opposite work. And far away, past four bridges, another span takes a load at dusk and answers with one clear note that sounds like a page turning over in a room nobody has ever been let into.
