# Batch 0003 Summary — Volume 01, Chapters 21–30

## Goal and outcome

The ninth-bridge case moved out of the copied-precedent neighborhood and into local institutions: the bridgekeeper's guild, the Ro household's landlord and dye-yard, the Lower Ward Assembly, the district maintenance office, and the river-ward public. Ilyan's attached name stopped being a resource and became a liability that the assembly formally restricted, and his first public testimony cost him the regional support he had gained.

The batch ends with a practical local arrangement recorded as found, the guild's finite burden named in a wage board with hours, the Ro household's and the Cinder Ward's refusals recorded in the same book as the paid work, and a bridge review due at noon on the seventh morning bell with the charter still unamended.

Ilyan's requests mostly returned less than they asked for. The assembly refused to hold the district's materials store, citing the Bench's own refusal in the Saltwheel matter. The examination produced an honest answer and a withdrawn citation. The local-institution review could not convene and degraded into an evidence session with no findings. The one request that succeeded was the smallest of the batch: a paid, finite west-joint inspection ending at the review bell.

## Key events

- The guild counts its work in hours, names the work, and posts a notice that lists Ilyan as *attached record — documentation of sequence and public record. No maintenance labor assigned.* The accurate line is read by the district as evidence that nobody carries the maintenance, and the absence becomes the day's political danger.
- A new inspection exposes the missing term with teeth: under the current charter a keeper may summon a pause but may not release the span. Oren can ring a bell that means nothing. The guild's whole maintenance review has been operating without a clause.
- The Ro household's shelter, room, work hour, and crossing hours are separated into four records with four recipients. A district materials store is proposed on the ground that a provisional tenancy has no tenancy to protect; the assembly refuses to hold it, and it later lapses unoccupied, because a materials store needs a count before and after that the district could not produce and could not re-serve once the tenancy became an admitted record. Neris refuses the acknowledgment on the ground that the charter has no exit, so a signature would bind her household to a rule she could never leave.
- Neris and Tamsin are invited to strengthen the public case and decline. Tamsin offers her bell fact once, in the workroom, where the span is visible while she is talking about it, and refuses the assembly. Neris offers route facts only and refuses the fifth repetition of her refusal.
- Ilyan's name is entered in the authority row of a public work slate by Meridith Vale under a four-year-old standing instruction. The name is not a force on the stone; a cart driver reads the board, changes his line, takes the turn fast, and breaks the eastern apron. A guild apprentice refuses to read the scale aloud while that name is on the board. Oren takes the book and puts the boy's name beside his own.
- The Lower Ward Assembly's limited word: the attached name may be cited as a record and may not be cited as a permit, schedule, levy, or acknowledgment, and may not be entered as the bearer of labor. The assembly also requires the attached record-holder to give a public account of his first bridge testimony in three days.
- Sera stops Ilyan in the middle aisle from offering a general rule that no acknowledgment may be inferred from continued use. She is right: his standing is limited to one bridge and he has no facts about four other districts.
- Ilyan's public account rests on the word *provisional*: every limit in the stay he accepted was a limit on the court — when the court must look again — and nothing in it stopped a clerk from taking the shape and using it somewhere else. He did not ask for that clause because asking would have meant asking to amend a charter, and he was relieved not to have to. He refuses general authority and refuses innocence. He produces his Earth reproduction sequence again, permanently, in public. The lowland conservancy withdraws its citation and rides home.
- The district's working schedule book has a version-three page cut from the binding gutter. The replaced affected-party column was not written by hand; it was cut from a printed form. The chain is nine ordinary steps from a public print run to a household paying for a bridge with its own footsteps.
- Sera refuses Ilyan's plan to reopen the bridge record on the C—2 evidence, because a reopening suspends every relief entered under the record, including the stay, and the stay is the only lawful crossing Tamsin has. She accepts him as a record-checker only: verify custody, do not infer a villain.
- Ilsa Bray refuses a forward schedule that reads *until reviewed and thereafter* with a wage line that has no hours, and offers the physical defect instead: on the third run the west joint comes back to its mark, walks two fingers out, and comes back. The missing maintenance-review condition is physically observable. Oren pays eight hours of salt-crack work out of the guild’s own account, not the levy, and signs it without looking at Ilyan. Ilyan files a paid, finite inspection request — the smallest request of the batch.
- Ilsa reveals that the sealed saltglass plate's cut edge is a load-book edge, not a keeper's stamp, and that a crew under the old charter kept two witnesses: the keeper and the book-keeper. The second hand was removed from the active crew list the way the levy ledger's witness was. Her grandmother's sentence: *a book without a second hand is only a record.*
- Recovery work: four guild hands, two ward hands, one road hand, one whistle, nine slots, and no slack. Ilyan writes a second rule he has never said to anybody — a burden may be refused without loss of access, work or shelter — does not file it, and does not destroy it, after Oren shows him that a rule making refusal free also makes refusal lawful on the worst day in the worst weather. The refusal rule from the fish-market hall is not reprinted; it is in the guild’s count book, the district’s fourth column, and two sewn books.
- The print shop answers Sera’s one question of the day: the authorization for run 114 was cut out of the stock ledger and replaced by an authorization signed by an office, not a person — a preservation authority that exists to keep records through a period in which records are being kept. The form that carried the Ro household’s position was printed under a preservation authority, and that is also the office that routed Sera’s family’s request. She tells Ilyan not to do anything with it yet.
- The new two-book ledger puts the paid work in the left book and the refusals in the right, with position, body, offered, declined. Vale enters the household's refusal under a position heading rather than a refusal heading until Tarin points out that the fourth column exists because Vale filled two of its lines himself.
- The ledger form's print strip reads **RUN 114. DISTRIBUTION: 1 — DISTRICT MAINTENANCE OFFICE. 2 — BRIDGE PLATE OFFICE. RECEIPTED AT 2: SIX DAYS BEFORE THE NINTH-BRIDGE PUBLIC NOTICE.** The paper the ward's refusal is written on was delivered to a routing desk before the case began.
- The promised local-institution review cannot convene: the ward has no recognized address, so the ward seats are empty. Ilyan refuses to fill them by treating the ward as one claimant. Tarin offers a neutral boundary without an order, and the sitting proceeds as a bounded evidence session with no findings and no order.
- Sera refuses in public to let the pre-arrival paper be submitted. Ilyan enters the fact of the routing as an attached observation instead, which costs him the larger prize.
- The quorum clerk's shelf index reveals that the ninth bridge's maintenance-review condition was rerouted from the bridge plate office to the C—2 continuity desk on the day the current working plate was executed. The C—2 desk has no incoming index after that date and has no named clerk, only a stamp.
- The district offers the household four concessions for one maintenance signature and is refused. It serves the same acknowledgment on the guild's crew, assigning the burden to *their successors, until the charter is amended.* Oren refuses on the guild's own ground and without Ilyan's record: the guild will not carry a term it cannot use, because the charter does not let a keeper stop a load.
- Ilyan files one request: to record the local arrangement as found and to say in the record that the charter is not amended. Appeal, enforceable exit, and durable housing and work protection are named in the entry as unresolved and not addressed.
- Tamsin asks for one thing to be written down: *I am not a reason for the bridge.* The court clerk writes it into the file that goes to the bridge review, which has not yet been read by the review.

## Relationship and practical changes

Ilyan loses public support. He admits that a sentence he liked and did not object to is the reason a levy could be called a precedent, gives up the version of his own words he had been keeping back, and declines to ask for the charter amendment three of four delegations wanted. His growth is changed judgment and delegation, not a new power: he writes the rule he wants, does not file it, and says so out loud. Sera stops him in public, refuses to hand him the archive, and makes him a record-checker with a division of labor. Tarin disagrees with Ilyan's narrow request and still protects the line. Oren and the guild win something on their own terms. Tamsin and Neris refuse the uses that would have strengthened the case, and the household's crossings are struck from the wage bill at last. The one unearned advantage the batch had drifted into — Ilyan following local speech as a matter of course — is removed: he has the guild's working vocabulary and nothing more, and three scenes mark the place where it runs out.

## Batch ending

The ninth bridge holds on a two-finger drift that returns and returns. The guild works to a chalk wage board with hours in it. The Ro household's room is entered as a record and its acknowledgment is refused, and its crossing is no longer counted as labor. The Cinder Ward's refusal is in the fourth column of a book whose back carries a print strip delivered six days before the public notice. The comparison of the sealed plate against the older witness index shows one cut and not three clues that happen to agree: same hand, same tool, same sitting, same week, with the salt gone white in both, which gives the plate a date the index never did. It is one act and it is still not a person. The routing index shows the bridge's own maintenance condition was rerouted to the C—2 desk on the day the working plate was replaced, and the desk has no incoming index and no named clerk. The pre-arrival paper carrying Ilyan's private Earth sequence carries the same route and a receipt that falls inside the gap. The copied petition is still mounted. The charter is still missing its maintenance review, its appeal, and its exit. The seventh morning bell rings at noon, and the next question is whether local institutions will reopen the bridge record under pressure, and whether a preservation authority can preserve and prosecute in the same signature.
