## Goal and outcome

**The volume's design gives this batch, verbatim from `outline/series.md`: *Ilyan's own testimony is used to classify an impoverished district as an uncooperative witness.*** The batch's job was to put that on the ground inside ten chapters by a lawyer or a clerk and not by him, in a deposition, in a hand that is not his, in words he said about himself and not about the district, in the second half, in the middle of the chapter, with nobody relieving him of it.

**It is done and it is done cleanly. Nothing was won.** A bench of four has to decide who signs an application and who goes to a sitting four miles off, and finds out that the person who can read is not the person the bench trusts and a person cannot be both. A carter puts his sheet on a trestle in his own yard and says the price before he says the fact. A wheelwright settles a claim out of money instead of grain and a district gives away its last nine pounds to a man who is owed fifty-four pounds. A boy of nineteen asks a stranger's advice about a claim and enters it, and asks for nothing else, and a month of nothing changes. ## The money, and the four checks a reader can do in a minute

**Every figure in Chapters 161–170 was recomputed in a script in this pass, from the figures printed in the chapters, and not read out of a state file. Twenty-seven checks were run. Twenty-six pass. The one that fails is a figure with no chapter behind it, and it is the batch's most important finding.**

1. **The claim: four hundred and six loads at the county rate of two shillings and eightpence a load is fifty-four pounds two shillings and eightpence.** 406 × 32d = 12,992d; 12,992 ÷ 240 = 54 remainder 32. **The account held nine pounds. The carter takes all nine and forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence waits to a year, and the district is at nothing.** 12,992 − 2,160 = 10,832d = £45 2s 8d.
2. **The cut: eleven thousand loads in the granary, about three thousand four hundred in the cut, four carts doing about three hundred, so about three thousand one hundred loads on hire at three shillings, of which three thousand are paid for. The district is a hundred loads short and a hundred loads is fifteen pounds.** 100 × 36d = 3,600d = £15. **And fifteen pounds is four hundred days of the ninepence wage line and six times a reeve's annual wage.**
3. **A sheet, on the register's own scale of three shillings a load: a man with one cart moves about four hundred loads = sixty pounds; with a second, about nine hundred = a hundred and thirty-five; so a second cart is about five hundred loads = seventy-five pounds, and it cost two shillings and eightpence.** 400 × 36 = 14,400d; 900 × 36 = 32,400d; 500 × 36 = 18,000d.
4. **Thirty-four second carts released is thirty-four times five hundred = seventeen thousand loads, and the district is a hundred short, which is a hundred and seventy times what is needed, in the wrong county, two days too late.** 34 × 500 = 17,000.
5. **And the one that fails: £462 − £460 is £2 and not £2 3s.** 110,880d − 110,400d = 480d = 2 pounds exactly. **`outline/volume-04.md` line 126 gives the working as *462 − 460 = £2 3d*, the Batch 0002 prompt's *THE MONEY* section repeats it, and the Batch 0001 sections of three state files assert it. No chapter of Volume 04 ever stated the difference** — 154:93, 157:35, 160:62 and 168:77 all give both figures and none subtracts them. **Chapter 169:17 states it for the first time in the manuscript and states it correctly as two pounds, and Marda Fen says the same thing three more times on the quay at 169:29.** A later pass must take £2 and must not "correct" 169.
6. **The lease, and it is a figure nobody had ever put in the prose: a fortieth of everything in the granary, and about eleven thousand loads, is two hundred and seventy-five loads, payable in grain, before the cut.** 11,000 ÷ 40 = 275.
7. **The reeve's arrears, derived and given for the first time: a wage of two pounds ten a year, eleven weeks out, is ten shillings and sevenpence.** 600d ÷ 52 × 11 = 126.9d.
8. **Forty-one loads at three shillings is six pounds three shillings**, which is what a reeve has carried out of her own authority in nine years, and 41 × 36 = 1,476d.
9. **Nine hundred and forty uncollected exception forms out of a run of four thousand, of which about three thousand and sixty were taken up.** 3,060 + 940 = 4,000. **And a hundred forms printed by a district cost about two pounds five**, 100 × 3d = 300d.
