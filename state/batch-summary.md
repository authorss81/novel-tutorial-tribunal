# Batch 0002 Summary — Volume 01, Chapters 11–20

## Goal and outcome

The ninth-bridge stay’s public wording traveled into the Eastmere petition and a neighboring district’s Saltwheel Road case. In Merefen’s unregistered Cinder Ward, the copied rule made a household refusal trigger a road-wide maintenance and refusal burden. Ilyan and Sera investigated the copy, the road’s physical failure, the local labor, and the chain of adoption. They secured a bounded local review and a practical, renewable road arrangement, while preserving the dangerous copied petition in the record instead of erasing it.

The local road now operates under a named route, visible work and hours, a finite review interval, a public exit condition, and a recorded ward refusal. The district cannot treat work or continued use as consent to a permanent acknowledgment. The arrangement was renewed for one additional marked interval after the morning bell, not converted into general precedent.

Sera’s parallel archive search remains unresolved but advanced. The original Quiet Chamber page was removed before Ilyan arrived; Sera’s private duplicate disappeared during or after the first night field. A formal continuity request, a missing witness index naming Sava Kest, a C—2 routing code, and a second paper using Ilyan’s private Earth sequence now connect parts of the chain. The responsible person and the meaning of the connection remain unknown.

## Key events

- A Merefen courier brings an extract that detaches the ninth-bridge stay from its named bridge, crew, burden, and review date. The added sentence reads, “the unlisted ward may refuse at the cost of the shared road.” Ilyan chooses a bounded review rather than an immediate freeze that could close the road and water route.
- Ilyan’s request is attached to the existing bridge record because the copied petition was already coupled to it. His role is observation and documentation, not local advocacy; Sera’s provisional scope extends only to the copied record and disclosure.
- Joren Vell, Dalla Rusk, Lio Fenn, Mava Rusk, and other local workers show that the road’s failure begins with a broken drain, water route, and changed cart behavior—not with a person’s refusal. The district’s “temporary” burden has no visible end, and the office’s end-assessment is never published.
- Meral’s composite petition and levy ledger show that labor, payment, refusal, and closure were merged into one administrative sequence. The Bench preserves the ledger but refuses the hold on enforcement, so the batch’s first filing returns worse than it went in. The ledger’s removed witness entry retains the Kest fragment, and its replacement line carries the clipped continuity cadence.
- Tarin keeps a neutral line, refuses force, and secures a broken crossing-side marker. Beneath the district’s newer plate is the private wording, “Completion does not erase the cause of refusal,” closely matching Ilyan’s Earth report. The marker is evidence, not an identified speaker.
- At the midpoint, Mava refuses a maintenance acknowledgment and is threatened with loss of water and shelter priority. The district invokes Ilyan’s stay as settled precedent. Ilyan states the original stay’s limits; the ward finds a slower side route, delivers clinic herbs, and the district’s attempt to use his name against the refusal becomes visible.
- Recovery work names the road’s actual sequence: open the lower course, pack the base, reset the edge, maintain a visible load line, and keep the pump route open. The district supplies materials but does not obtain the ward’s acknowledgment. The local arrangement is written in both the ward’s and district’s copies. A proposed renewal instruction arrives before the interval ends; the formal automatic order is entered after the assembly and archive review.
- The Merefen assembly refuses a universal answer. Maren Kett’s written mandate is read into the hall before he argues, so his opposition is a stated instruction rather than a change of position. Ilyan accepts neutral comparison of his public Earth sequence but refuses the authorship claim. The comparison sets three dates side by side and finds that the local petition was built from the public wording before the Quiet Chamber margin was rewritten: the copier and the person who tidied the record afterward are not the same person.
- Sera keeps her family withdrawal appeal separate. The original page was removed before Ilyan’s crossing; her private duplicate vanished during or after the first field. A valid continuity seal proves an office acted, not who acted or why.
- Ilyan, Sera, Tarin, Joren, Dalla, and Mava each choose what to offer at the automatic-renewal pause. The renewal takes effect only when a clerk enters it, and a district clerk from Merefen crosses that line at a watched hour with Tarin’s record hand beside the book. The Bench pauses the renewal for a short interval, seals but does not erase the copied petition, and keeps the local route available under its named arrangement.
- The district’s common register is made to include the ward’s refusal and the work actually performed. The copied petition remains a precedent witness, not a ruling. The register also carries the district’s added line, **acknowledgment inferred from continued use**, which is its most dangerous weapon because it needs no signature. The local arrangement survives one more marked interval through a fresh choice, not automatic renewal.
- The second paper’s receipt mark is dated before the ninth-bridge public notice and before Ilyan’s arrival. Its wording, **“At the final route, completion erases the cause of refusal,”** derives from his private Earth report. The C—2 route and the old bridge plate’s **S. Kest** edge notation identify a chain, not a culprit.

## Relationship and practical changes

Ilyan learns that a public remedy can become a local weapon when its conditions and burdens are stripped away, and that his first filing in this matter returned worse than it went in. He remains dependent on local workers, roadkeepers, witnesses, and Sera’s law; he documents rather than commands. Sera continues limited provisional representation, protects the separation between the road case and her family appeal, and accepts Ilyan only as a bounded record-checker. Tarin remains a cautious ally who protects neutral lines and refuses force, and he witnesses the hour in which a lawful clerk makes a disputed order true. Oren’s bridge knowledge does not replace local road practice. Tamsin and Neris appear again with limited consent, crossing under escort because the stay still moves them, keeping the Ro household’s safety, work, housing, bell fact, and flood-account boundaries active.

## Batch ending

The Saltwheel Road has a local practical arrangement: a named route, finite work, public hours, a visible exit, a public review, and a recorded ward refusal. The arrangement is not a general precedent, and the copied petition remains dangerous in the public record. The ninth bridge’s one-cycle stay remains provisional; its current charter still lacks enforceable appeal, exit, and durable housing/work protection. The next question is how Ilyan’s private Earth sequence entered a Veyran legal record before he arrived, and who or what routed it through the missing Sava Kest chain.
