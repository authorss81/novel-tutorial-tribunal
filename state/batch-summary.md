# Batch 0004 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 81–90, *The City of Exceptions*

## Goal and outcome

This is the fourth prose batch of Volume 02 and it is **the half of the volume's resolution that is not about Sera**. It was given one job: the appeal has to stop depending on one poor district's willingness to keep paying half a farthing on a pump. **It advanced that by exactly three things, none of them a ruling, and all three of them ordinary: a definition a bench of four wrote down about its own rate; a man who put his name under a rate and could not take it off; and a rule that any person of a district may ask for a page to be read aloud to them at a pump, twice a week, and not be asked why. None of the three is secure. All three cost somebody an hour, a day, or a bank of mud in the cold, and the batch's finding is that the cost of a durable thing in a poor district is always paid by a person, and never by a body.**

**The batch's engine is money, and it is the batch's engine that the resolution turns on.** Meridith Vale's letter of the second of the fourth month is a bill: the charter of the district of Halloway costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year out of a fund of four hundred and ten that belongs to a bridge four miles away, and the year as set out at the review leaves forty-four pounds and four shillings, so **the charter costs twelve shillings more than what is left, and the one hundred and five pounds that came back when the under-deck watch went to one hand is in a different line, and a clerk cannot move a line into another line without a bench's minute.** That is the whole of the appeal's survival in one sentence and it is a sentence about a ledger, and nobody in this case has ever had to read it before, because nobody had ever been made the bill.

## The five findings, each worse than anybody wanted

1. **A figure a bench entered and cannot discharge is a more durable thing than a figure it can pay.** The thirty years stands at one thousand two hundred and fifteen pounds after a correction the woman owed gave herself, and it cannot be paid, cannot be refused, cannot be overrule, and cannot be struck out of a book that is not the witness's. **What the district discovered is that the only durable thing in a poor district is a book a bench can come back with, and that what it has just discovered it has is a list of things it owes.**
2. **A rate is a thing a person has to be inside of, and the machinery has a procedure for carrying an appeal and not one word for the second.** Wenna Tace asked in public whether the rate for a haul could be appealed by anybody and was told no — it was set by a bench of four, the only person who asked for it is the person who asked, a holder may not be a party or appeal or appear in support or be named. The only public place a rate is read is a pump, and the convenor refused to call it a hearing. **She did not get the money. She got the sentence, and it is in the district's own book and will be read out four times a year.**
3. **A withdrawal is the first thing a poor person in this case can point at and use.** Garvin Slade withdrew his consent to a rate in his own words and a bench of four could not undo the rate, because it had been read aloud to about two hundred people, and could not rub out his name, because a clerk does not. So a rate now carries, underneath it, the name of a man who does not agree with it, and **it is a different kind of object from a rate, because nobody can be inside a withdrawal — a person can be next to one.** The cost of the rule that made it read aloud is about two hundred and forty people standing in the cold, paid by the people who go up the bank.
4. **A place on a docket board costs about four pounds fifteen in four months, and the price of making a document is not in anybody's bill.** Maren Kett asked the question nobody had thought of asking. The whole of twenty-two names on the board is cheaper than four timbers. **A document is made by a person who can read, and this city has no line on which a person is paid for reading**, and in this case the price has been paid by a refuse hauler, a gate hand, a cowman's wife, a landlord's mouth, a clerk, a boy of nineteen, and a washer of sixty-one who has stopped using a standpipe. The court can afford eleven places. It cannot afford the readiness behind them, and Ansa Rell says so and says the reason is that paying a person to prepare the paper buys the paper, and that is why she is out-voted and undefeated.
5. **The one thing that changed on the ground in three weeks was a woman of sixty-one walking four miles for water.** Eight buckets a day is a penny, a penny a day is seven pence a week, one pound ten shillings and fourpence a year, and in a district of about two hundred and forty people that rate falls on her at eight times what it falls on most of the rest of them. **She has not told the district. She told the man who withdrew, at his counter, because he asked her.** The lost penny cannot be entered as a loss, because nobody collects buckets, and a tax that loses a customer loses nothing that anybody can count.

## The man who lost the second hand

**T. Blore is told, at the second bell of the third of the fourth month, at the cut banks, nineteen miles off, in Oren Sedge's own mouth, with no messenger, no clerk and no page in it.** Sedge could not come before: the count is taken at every change and if he is not on the deck at the change there is no count. He says the disposition, and that the second hand is gone, and that **his name is the only name in it, and that a man called Vester put it there at a review and Sedge did not, and would have taken the hand just the same if he had not been in the room.** The resistance is not the loss. It is that the loss is recoverable-looking: forty-four pounds and four shillings in a fund would pay him back about five months, a district can be written to, a chamber can be asked in writing, and a man can be sent for who is not a bench and cannot make anybody answer. He works out all of it and then works out the other thing, which is that there is no job either. **What the guild actually took off him is a half day, which is four hours, and four hours a day is the figure in every line about him.** And he is told something true that nobody has told him by a person with their own reason: *you were not let go, the crew list went to one name in the winter because the guild ran out of money, and the review took a second hand out of a fund and bought four timbers with it* — so that in four years, when somebody asks, the answer standing in a room is not that a trade dropped a man in the year of a case.

## What it cost Ilyan

**The seventh thing, entered in his own hand on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month, in a room over a pump, at the second bell, with a clerk writing it down.** He is produced as a witness a second time, about a number he put in a bench's book without the consent of the woman it was owed to, and he says out loud that he is stopping counting the gate hand's tide. **The reason he gives is in the body and not in the record: counting is a burden of his own, it is the only thing that ever cleared his leg, and he has worked out that what he has been doing is holding a woman up for three hours a day so that a bench of four can read a number, and the number is wrong, and the woman said no.** And the finding is worse than the stopping: **a bench of four has no power to strike out a number a witness entered, the number stands, it is read out at a pump four times a year, it is wrong, and the only man in this city who could have made it more right by counting more carefully has stopped.** The leg is his own now and does not clear, and the low water is in four layers and the door is at the same height and the same distance. The rendering is one step further and is not resolved: the vantage is a room with a table a hand could go on and a lamp with the wick down and a window with weather on the wrong side of it and four people at the table, one of them about forty-four with her back to him.

Corva Silt says the thing that made it necessary, out loud, once, and does not put it in the book: **a number can be wrong in a way that can be argued about, and that is the only door anybody has ever found out of a shape, and it is not a good door.** And Bryn Sallow, at the gate, at the turn: *you have made yourself a man who cannot be produced on a number* — and then the other thing, which he did not ask for, and which is the batch's best answer: **she has been counting her own tide in her own feet since she was fourteen, there is no slate, she would have gone on with it, and in four years a clerk will read a book in which a man says he stopped for a woman and there will be nothing at all in it about the woman.**

## The second ask, and a clerk who made herself a holder

**The carrier's clause is used a second time, by the bench of three at Fallowditch, which cannot pay: a sheet of its own paper, four names, a mark, a wedge and chain, four and six out of nine pounds in the year, and the rest walked.** The licence's third term is the mechanism and Sera Quill refuses to send him and says why: a recordwright's carrier is a thing she is and not a thing she hands to the man the fourth term names, and if he goes he goes because a clause with his name in it says he goes, and she will not let a ledger read that as the other thing. **Past the fourth mile the clause stops obliging him and the favour cannot be entered, and he walks eleven miles and is paid nothing and the leg stops two hundred yards short of a shop that had been a chandler's.** What comes back is a finding nobody in this case has stated: **a clerk of twenty-nine has made herself a holder of a room nobody made her a holder of, by making a rule in her own room, and her bench's ordinary work has stopped, and a hedge eleven miles of boundary is not going to be done this spring.** And at the foot of the ninth sheet, under the fifth term, in the copy and not in the sheet, a bench of three of a district that has adopted nothing reads out a line nobody entered, in public, because it was reading the sheet.

## The continuity line, and the number nobody can do anything about

**The eighth appearance is the line being read out in a room over a chandler's shop in Fallowditch on the twelfth of the fourth month, by a clerk of twenty-nine who was reading the ninth sheet out word for word and did not know what it was. The ninth appearance is the sixth refusal**, in the same words, at the counter in a stone room, with nobody named, and a trialwright of eleven years saying he will refuse on the seventh. **The letter sat on a tray in a chamber for twenty days with nothing on it asking anything, and the only thing that happened to it was a stamp and a number on a spine.** The holder has still not read the line under the fifth term of the copy in her own room, and she reads a charter in order, and a charter ends at the fifth term, and nobody in this case will tell her.

## Relationship and power changes

**Ilyan is not rewarded for carrying a document.** He is paid nothing, the walking past the fourth mile is a favour, the district that asks cannot pay, and the Fallowditch rule was copied from nothing. What he is given in this batch is the seventh thing, entered against himself, and the leg, which stops clearing. **Sera is opposite him once and is right.** She refuses to send him, gives the reason in full, and says the two sentences must not be joined. Her family claim is not touched and is not looked for; the fourth term of her licence sits on a table in the last paragraph of the batch and she reads it twice, and she writes down that she knows the difference between a question and a matter, and that is as far as this batch goes and no further. **No romance beat occurs and none is planned.**

**Wenna Tace is the person this batch is about and she is not rescued.** She asks a question in public and is told no; she takes on a second reader herself and pays for the paper out of her own keeping money; she writes a question and sends it to a recordwright at her own cost and puts her own name at the top of it and the recordwright's at the bottom, because she is the holder and is not allowed to be in a support of anything. **Garvin Slade is this batch's decision.** Bryn Sallow is this batch's anger. **Abel Kearn is not given a mechanism and says why in one line.** Kearn's wall, his mother's house, and the timber merchant are untouched and may not be advanced.

## Judgement calls a later reader should know about

- **The haul rate is not re-derived.** The entry as it stands in Chapter 80 — the district's own rate is a third of a haul rate, a haul is paid at three times the district's own rate, and a haul in this district is two thirds short of a haul — is treated as canon and is *not* resolved and *not* re-stated as a self-cancelling sum. What the batch adds is the reason it cannot be priced: **the district's own rate is a rate for a *load*, and this district has no carts, so a barrow here is four loads to a cart that does not come, and the four in that sentence is a fact about a cart and not a price.** The hole is named and not filled.
- **The thirty years is now also a hundred and fifty hauls at two thirds**, in the gate hand's own words, at her own asking, and it is the smaller and truer of the two figures and can be paid no better than the other. Bryn Sallow still does not know what is in his leg.
- **Chapter 89 does not restage the withdrawn name on the docket board.** It references the withdrawal once in a single clause in the opening paragraph and does not perform it; Maren Kett's cost is a different one, and it is that a counsel who enters a question is not a witness to it and cannot be examined on it, so his question cannot travel to the people who wrote the words.
- **Word range for the batch: 3,556–5,110.** Chapter 90 carries the batch close and is the longest; it is not padded. Chapter 88 is the shortest and is a complete scene. **Do not treat any of these figures as a target.**

## Batch ending

A man of thirty-one stops counting a gate hand's tide, and the number he put in her book stays wrong, and there is no line in this city that pays a man for having been produced. A man who sells salt has his name read out under a rate he did not agree with, four times a year, for as long as the woman with the barrow is the holder. A washer of sixty-one walks four miles to a pump in another district because eight buckets a day is a penny and the rate falls on her at eight times the average, and she has told nobody except the man who withdrew. A bench of three eleven miles off a fen road has made itself a room and stopped doing its boundary. A counsel of nineteen years has asked a question in the open air that cannot be called back and cannot reach the people who could answer it. **And a woman of twenty-nine, on a mud bank, at the sixth bell, with about two hundred people behind her and a boy of nineteen and a clerk and a bench of four, asks the first question anybody has asked out loud in four months in this city: nobody has ever told me what this page is for.** A recordwright in a city four miles from a bridge reads it, does not answer it, and puts a licence with four terms on a table, and reads the fourth one twice. The appeal is in use and is not secured. The Quiet Chamber's quarter sentence and the page over a pump are still side by side in a record and are not the same sentence, and nothing in this batch is better than that. The Bridgeward has not been touched.
