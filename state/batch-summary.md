# Batch 0002 Summary — Volume 03, Chapters 111–120, *The Saltglass Coast*

## Goal and outcome

The second prose batch of Volume 03, and the batch `outline/series.md` reserves the midpoint reversal for. **The midpoint reversal is delivered, in Chapter 117, in the second half, in the middle of the chapter and not at the end of it, put on the page by a person who is not Ilyan Vester and who is afraid of it before he sees it, unconfirmed by anything, and worth nothing as a map.** The batch's spine is delivered: *a copy loses the only interesting thing in four hundred years, and a woman of twenty-four who cuts marks for a living is the one who works out that it was lost, and cannot say what she is frightened of, and is not wrong to be.* **No System panel says anything about the pattern. Nobody says the word *map* about it. The only mention of *The Sovereign's Wake* in the batch is once, in narration, in Chapter 117, and it is the thing Ilyan played and filed a report on, and the sentence containing it says he could not have said what it had to do with four hundred weeks in chalk.** The recognition is not here and is not attempted.

**The batch's cost is somebody's day and somebody's back.** Four days on a coast road and eleven shillings that were the money for a desk; a day of a stall at four shillings and twopence and a day and a half of a sister at the fish quay; an afternoon of a lamp-oil shop; fourpence of nails and two hours of a man of seventy-one's daylight; the oil a man of thirty spent on a cart up the coast road; and two miles of coast road walked on a leg carrying a gate hand of forty-four's weight out of a city four hundred miles off. **The batch's discovery is about the coast's own record-keeping and is not about a mystery: a week is the only piece of time on this coast that everybody agrees is a piece of time, so every list on four hundred miles of it is a list of weeks, and every one of them has holes in it, and a four-day hole in a list of weeks is not a mark — it is a smudge, and a copyist reads a gap between two weeks as nothing.**

## What the batch delivers, in order

1. **A schoolroom a hundred and forty miles up the coast, a book in a press, and a woman who needs a number she can swear to.** Belva Cray, fifty-one, eleven years keeping a school of twenty-six children in three rooms, the third of them being the coal store with the door off. Nine families of eleven have voted to move the children down the coast to a school with a wall and a stove. She needs the number of days a year the low road is under and she has a feeling. **What comes back is not an answer: a week is a week and a bad week is one day or nine, and there is nothing on four hundred miles of this coast that will take a week and give her back days** — and the only number anyone on that coast has is a song that says nine and is wrong most years.
2. **The council, nine members, and a vote of five to four.** A reader's wage of two pounds a year for counting the crossings of Wether Cross in persons rather than carts, out of a road whose margin is four, in a season three pounds two short. **The four who voted no are right about the money and the five who voted yes are right about the boy, and both sides were right, and the finding is that the charter was not on the table at all** — nobody had thought to ask the council, and the money for the fifth pane was due at Thornglass the day before and did not come.
3. **A word on a piece of paper, and the coast's version of being sent for.** Five words, no seal, no form, carried by a man who was going that way anyway. Not a summons, no hearing, no penalty either way, no second door. **It worked, and that is the finding, and nobody tested it, and it was not tested by refusing because refusing stops nothing and the paper does not come back.**
4. **Three songs on a coast of four hundred miles, and nobody knew there was more than one.** **A wrong number in a song is a wrong number for a hundred years, because the only way to change a song is to sing a different one, and then the road has two numbers and the wrong one is still in everybody's mouths.** And the second use of the tide stone: a woman asks on the right day about a song, and the harbourmaster refuses in the open on about sixty people, and the reason is that the stone is for the water and a song is not the water, and there is no body on four hundred miles with standing over a song.
5. **The grandmother's book, and the wall, and the four days that got lost.** Nineteen notches on a kiln wall, one of which is a hole four days wide; a copy in a school a hundred and forty miles up this coast; and a four-day hole in a list of weeks is a smudge, not a mark, and a copyist reads a gap between two weeks as nothing. **THE MIDPOINT REVERSAL. Sabra Trew, twenty-four, paid nothing, third year, has not answered a letter, is the person who sees it, and is frightened, and cannot say what she is frightened of.**
6. **The rate again, and the smallness that is the finding.** A bad seventh costs that crossing three pence a week and thirteen shillings a year if there were a bad seventh every week of a year, which there has never been. A man to stand at the far end of the flat is two pounds, which is thirty-eight shillings. **The guild is not making money out of the boy and never was: it takes three shillings and threepence a week off a woman who mends nets and spends three pounds two a year on a chain across nine hundred yards of shingle it does not maintain, and nets six pounds, and thirteen shillings is not worth a fight and two pounds is not worth a bad year.** The boy is still on the flat nine times on the worst day of the week. This is the batch's second failure and it is not a refusal of consent and is not staged as one.
7. **The cost of connecting two places, in its full instrument.** One of them counts tides and one of them counts firings of an arch and they are not the same count. **A sentence said on a bench in the open is a different object from the same sentence answered in a room with a table in it, and the difference is not in the words.** *The two of them are the same list, going back* becomes *the two records agree*, and the join is a woman of fifty-one answering a question kindly, and the person whose words changed was forty yards under an awning and not in that room.
8. **The year, put to the harbourmaster and refused correctly.** A pane put to Bettany Rook in a yard and refused in about nine sentences, and she is right: the guild's people are right about the water in their hands and the guild's glass is a photograph of one morning, and **what is old on this coast is the record and not the harbourmaster.** And what she does in the fourth week of a season when she cannot do her job, which is dictate nine sheets of figures to a clerk of twenty-two in four hours and forty minutes in front of four people and then about nine, and never once be asked about it. **A rule that cannot be changed for a year is not a rule. It is a season.** Said out loud by Danno Ray, a carter with a load of lime, to about nine people on a shingle, and not answered by one of them.
9. **The batch close: the post counted for the first time, a board nailed to it, and a hammer.** Four thousand and sixty-eight crossings, a hole for every ten, a bar for every hundred, two hours and eleven minutes, and about fifty notches on the underside that a man cutting in a hurry cuts twice. Seth Garrick says out loud on thirty people that he has had about five thousand in his head for four years and was wrong by about nine hundred. The returns are of carts that paid: three hundred and ninety-one in the season. **The difference is every person on this coast who has ever put a foot on a shingle flat, and one boy of eleven is two thousand and twenty-eight of the four thousand and sixty-eight.** And Gell Roke, seventy-one, hands the hammer to the person nearest and calls it the ward of that road, and Ilyan takes it, and the leg goes, and a man of seventy-one takes the hammer back at the fourth swing without a word.

## New people on this coast

## The close, at full length

**A partial, real, paid-for change.** The tally post at Wether Cross is counted, in public, for the first time in the history of the guild, and the figure is cut into it and written on a board nailed to the front of it, and the returns and the post are put on the same board with the difference named, and the difference is *everybody*. **The burden is named and visible and it is on the board in Gell Roke's own hand and he says he is not sure it is a good one: THE COUNT IS MADE AT THE TURN, ONCE A YEAR, BY WHOEVER IS HERE.** It cost an afternoon of a lamp-oil shop, a day and a half of a slate, fourpence of nails, two hours of a man of seventy-one's daylight, the oil a man of thirty spent on a cart he did not have to go, and nothing in money, because the council voted the wage two pounds a year on the fourth day of the fourth tide and cannot pay it.

**The thing Volume 02 was about, in a new shape, and the test is a small physical thing and not a speech.** A man of seventy-one who has never been paid for nineteen years and never asked hands a hammer about four feet to a man of thirty-one who is standing nearest, and says *that is the ward of that road now*, and the man takes it — and does not put it down, and does not say he cannot, and does not make himself a door about it — and the leg goes in at the second swing, and a gate hand of forty-four's out of a city four hundred miles behind him arrives in two layers with a third under both, and the room with the lamp down and the four of them at the table and one of them with her back to him is sitting exactly where it has been sitting since the third of the second month, and **a man of seventy-one takes the hammer back at the fourth swing without a word and puts it in the bag and closes the bag.** **A ninth-bridge term used on this coast as if it obviously applied: a ward. A ward is a duty and not a place, and a duty goes to whoever is standing nearest and cannot be appealed from, and no one on that coast has any way of asking a man whether he would like to be whoever is here.** Nobody says a word, then or afterwards, and about four of about thirty people worked out what it was.

**The ending is a discovery and not a verdict**, and it is not about a person: on a coast of four hundred miles there are about nine people holding about nine things in their heads because there is no column for any of them and no office and no clause and no way of writing one down, and every one of those nine is on its way out, and the whole apparatus of that coast — a charter, a tariff, a card, a song, a post, a pane, a book in a school, a slate in a basket, a bench in a room with a painted staff on a wall — is a machine for making nine things into one written thing, and the machine has just been shown, on a wet shingle, by a man of seventy-one, not to work.
