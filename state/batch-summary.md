# Batch 0001 Summary — Volume 03, Chapters 101–110, *The Saltglass Coast*

## Goal and outcome

This is the first prose batch of Volume 03 and it is the volume's first movement: **the coast, the guild, the second site, the first refusal of consent, a rate in a different coin, the saltglass, the songs and the marks, the appeal used by somebody who cannot use it, the cost of connecting two places, and the batch close.** `outline/series.md` assigns Volume 03 two resolution lines and one midpoint reversal. **None of them is delivered in this batch and all three are placed.** The midpoint reversal — the coast's songs and toll marks carrying a pattern that resembles the hidden map in *The Sovereign's Wake* — **is not in this batch at all, except as a plant**: nineteen notches on a kiln wall, a four-day gap in a row, and a grandmother's book in a school a hundred and forty miles up the coast. Nobody recognised anything and no one was told.

**The batch's spine is the first refusal of consent, and it is delivered, correct, recorded in full, and paid for.** A matter in two places means asking two communities to be in the same matter. **Slackwater Reach, about four hundred people, poorer, with its own assembly of thirty-one seats, refused in the open at the first bell of the third of a tide, in about four minutes, by a man of fifty-four who said his cost out loud before he started and was not talked out of it.**

**The batch's cost is people's days and one woman's five days of a stall.** Eleven pounds of freight and two men's day before a word was said; four days of a stall-holder's wanting; five mornings of a stall and a half-crown of eleven years' custom; an afternoon of a young clerk's day and an hour of a woman's bad light; a boy's thirty-nine tides a week. **No document in this batch has a price and no room in it declines to say anything.**

## The seven findings

1. **A licence is seven days and the water is on the bar for three of them. A season's passage is sold for seven days a week of the tide at thirty-one shillings, it does not name the days, and it cannot be refunded on a day the water is on the bar.** The chart is on a wall and free to read. **The price of finding out which three days those are is a day at the crossing, and the price of not finding out is a cart.** Every other finding in this volume is that sentence at a different size. The guild is not dishonest and is not asked to be: it is right that last year a boy of fourteen who went out on the fourth in a punt with a bad lock came back, and that without the guild there would be a great many fourteen-year-olds finding out where the channel goes, one at a time, on a bad day, with nobody on the bank.
2. **A guild controlling the only safe route is not a criminal act and is a catastrophe waiting for one bad week, and the person who says so is a guild officer doing her job.** Bettany Rook, fifty-one, harbourmaster's clerk for nineteen years and harbourmaster for six, said it to a stranger in a yard in the rain, and her want is not the strait: the light off the Reach has been going for two seasons, she wears a hood on the water side, and she has told nobody because the day she tells the guild is the day she is moved to a counter and the guild would be right to do it. **She took the hood off once, in front of him, and put it back on.**
3. **A tariff that does not name its days charges a bad day the price of a good one.** One penny a tide. Nine hundred yards of shingle, crossable six times a day on the first, second, third and fifth and nine times on the fourth, sixth and seventh, because on those three days the strait is shut and every cart that has to come round comes over the flat. **The guild has refused twice to write a different price for a day of water, because a tariff that says *dangerous* is a tariff that can be refused and a man who refuses a tariff is a man you have to argue with on a bad day, and there is nobody at that counter to argue with anybody on a bad day.** A boy of eleven makes thirty-nine tides a week, three shillings and threepence, eight pounds nine a year. The commonest figure among the households that pay anything is threepence a week. **That is thirteen times, and it was found on the ground with a stick and a board, and it is not the eight times, and it is not a bucket.**
4. **A charter that is a pane of saltglass cannot be changed for a year, and the year is a person and a fire and a letting day the tide chooses.** A batch goes into the arch on a tide and is turned about forty times over eleven months and is let go on a day the fire agrees to, about once a year. **A copy of a pane is not a copy. A copy is a year.** The fifth pane — the one that would carry the burden clause — is on an order sheet with a date against it and no money against it, and its letting day went eleven weeks before Chapter 107, so it is not late, it has not begun, and between the sixth of a tide and the third of next tide the guild may write on anything it likes and not one word of it will be the charter. **THE BATCH'S REAL MECHANISM: the pane's authority does not rest on the guild. It rests on the man who stood beside the arch on one morning.** The guild holds the only legible copy because it pays for the only copy, and the moment it cannot pay, the coast has no record. **The glassmaker made a second pane for himself three years ago and hung it on his own shed wall, and the two panes do not agree in two columns out of eleven, and he has known it for two years and told nobody until a man off a road stood in front of it, and there is no instrument on that coast that could settle it in a room because the only thing that settles it in a room is a pane and the only pane in a room is the guild's.**
5. **A pane cannot be crossed out, and that is why a stall-holder's thirty-one years of pencil cannot go into it — and it is also why a wrong thing in a pane is a rule for ever.** The guild is right about this and the glassmaker is right about it and the woman selling rope has been told no four times for four years. The volume's thesis arrives as craft. **And the same fact is why a line got onto a crossing card: the crossing card and the tide-office card are printed from one plate in one shop, one under the other, with a hair's width between them, and a man who cannot read filled in one of them and copied every line on the sheet, including the tide office's burden line — *the cost of a week in which the water is not on it is not borne by the guild, and the person at the end of the line is the person who is* — so a woman of eighty-one has been entered on a bar road as the person who bears the cost of a bad week, for eight years, and has never been asked.**
6. **The asking is free and the answer is not, and the person who can be asked has no standing whatever.** The coast's one road that works is a song sung at a tide stone by one man on the seventh at the turn: anybody may stand on the stone and ask the harbourmaster one question and she answers there, in the open, to whoever is on the shingle, and nobody may ask a second, and it is written down nowhere. **A woman of forty-four stands on it and asks about the seventh day and is refused, correctly, by a person who is not a bench and not a court and who has no legal standing at all: *you have asked me on the seventh about the seventh, and I cannot sit on it*, and the reason is the water and a boat on the rocks with a man in it who is not dead because he was seventeen and angry.** The guild's own book was offered with the key on her belt and then refused by the person offering it, because the guild's book is a fire going to happen and a question about a road is not a question about her house, and reading it out is not answering it. **This is a different failure from Volume 02's and is not the same failure.**
7. **A carried account changes as it is carried, and the person whose words changed was not in the room and did not know.** Eleven households, said twice in a net loft, came back as seventeen crossings in a shop off a boat in three days. **Both are true and they are not the same claim, and the difference between them is a difference of what you would do about it, and there is no road on this coast that can carry a difference of what you would do about it.** Not a lie and not a mystery. It is what happens when a number about a tariff walks into a room that wants a number about a sand. **The three Gannock brothers are named and are not staged, and are saved.**

## The refusal, at full length

**Pether Voke, fifty-four, speaker of the Slackwater assembly, ferryman's mate for thirty-one years, picked four times and has said he will not stand again after the third and stood again because nobody else would.** He lost a tide before he started and said so at the first bell — eleven pounds of freight and two men's day — so that nobody could say afterwards he did it for a romance. He said no once, in the open, and sat down, and let anybody say anything they liked, and would not take a word of it back.

**His reasons, and all three hold:** being served is a day of a boat and a day of a cart, and the loft's own book has four dates and four sums in it and the third is a woman whose papers went into the creek and were sworn out again out of her own memory; **consent is not a yes or a no, consent is a door with a person in it, and a person in a door can be moved, and the way a person gets moved is that somebody stands in the doorway being awkward**; and the real one — **one matter ends in one answer, and the guild's price for a tide is a penny and Slackwater's is a day, and one of them is going to be wrong in public, and it will not be the guild, which has nineteen years and a pane on a wall, and it will not be a man off a road, so it will be Slackwater — and afterwards Slackwater will be a body that was in a matter about the water and was wrong.**

**It is recorded in full** on the back of a fishmonger's notice, in the hand of the only person in the loft who could write it, read out twice to the room as the assembly's practice requires, and handed to the man it was refusing. **It is not in a book and cannot be come back for.** And the consequence is the finding: **a tariff on a road is not in a matter about a strait and never was. He knew it on the first day and came nine miles by water to see whether a body would let him put it in anyway. The flat is not in anything and has not been in anything for four hundred years, and there is not one body on this coast that owns nine hundred yards of shingle.**

## The batch close

**A partial, real, paid-for change: a person who guides a cart over Wether Cross on a tide is not a trespasser by that fact alone and is not counted as a cart.** The test is a small physical thing — a chain, a boy's hands, a hand-lead, a board with no line on it — and the person who resolves it is the harbourmaster, not the man off the road. **It cost an afternoon of a young man's day and an hour of a woman's bad light and nothing in money, and it was entered on paper and not in the returns on purpose, so that if it is ever wrong a man can draw a line through it, and a name was not put on it on purpose, because a name on that line would be a name the boy could be found on.** It also costs about two pounds a year in a wage out of a margin of four pounds, and the harbourmaster has entered that the council will not like it and that she has never told the council the crossing runs on four pounds a year and does not know what she would do about it. **A second change, and it is a downgrade on purpose: thirty-one years of pencil is copied into a shelf in the paper book, dated, in the stall-holder's own hand, and is deliberately not the guild's record and is not on the list for the fire, and the harbourmaster says why twice — a thing that is not the record can be wrong, and a thing that is the record has to be true.** It cost five mornings of a stall and a half-crown of custom off a crew of eleven years, and both numbers were said out loud in a room, and it changes nothing about the rate.

**The thing Volume 02 was about, in a new shape:** a duty with nobody in it goes to whoever is standing nearest, and a record is a thing only local people can keep, and on this coast the record is a pane that cannot be argued with and the people keeping records are a boy with a lead, a woman with a slate in a basket, a stall-holder with a pencil, a woman with a book of her own, an apprentice with nineteen notches on a wall, a man with a hammer counting a post, a man with a pane on his own wall that he cannot get compared with anybody's, and a man of thirty-one with a torn-out page he cannot put back.

**The ending is a decision and a question and not a verdict.** He did not ask Alma Gannock the question and he did not tell her, and he wrote the question he asked and the one he did not on the same page in the same hand, with the reason and the date and that it is his, and was told it was the most honest piece of paper she had read in six years and the least use anybody had ever made of one. **And the batch ends on the plainest question anybody on that coast has heard put, and the first one in fifteen days that is not about a person at all: WHAT DOES A BODY DO FOR A YEAR?**

## Ilyan, and the things that did not move

**Stage 2, Seam Reader, and Stage 2 at the end of Chapter 110.** No new party, no bench, no body, no office, no remedy he chose alone. **He was produced nothing and nobody sent for him** — a counsel of nineteen years named him in a lane and a woman with a stall walked eleven miles and paid a day's hire of a cart.

**The rendering, the vantage, the door, the hand, the token and the continuity of the leg are exactly where Volume 02 left them.** The left hand is a scar at the base of the left thumb and will not grip. The rendering is unchanged in kind and the vantage has not moved. The token was not touched.

**The one exception, and it is the established practice used and not advanced.** The leg cleared for about four hours on the fourth of a tide at Thornglass, carrying an iron bar out of a fire and back forty times. **The same four-hour limit; it clears inside a burden of his own; it is the same trade as the notice he carried on a road in the fifth month of Volume 02.** The paper was a duty that was not his own and the woman was in it; the bar is his own work and nobody's name is on it, and the woman the weight belongs to is four hundred miles off and is still in his leg, and it came back worse in about a minute. **He told nobody on this coast and is not going to. There was nothing here that depended on him standing or not standing, and that was the trade, and he made it with his eyes open at about the fifth turn. The cost is larger than it was in Volume 02, because the thing he traded it against is four hundred miles off and he chose that.**

**The coast worked out that he cannot read and nobody told him.** A fishwife looked at him over a boy's head and said nothing. Corrie Tallow read the tariff once, he asked for it again, she said *never mind, you have got it*, and she never mentioned it. **Bettany Rook worked it out in about four seconds in front of the counter, turned it into a question about a painted bar staff so the room would think she was testing him, and never said a word about it then or afterwards — and in Chapter 106 she watched him read her own sentence back one piece at a time and said nothing whatsoever.** **Corva Silt and no clerk is still the only person in the world who has been told, and that is still true.**

## Calendar, distances and mechanics

- **No weekday and no month is named anywhere in Chapters 101–110.** The coast's calendar is a seven-day tide, bells, and the turn, and the shore calendar is not fixed by a canon chapter in this volume and **must not be converted into one.** All four weekday slips and the one month slip caught in review were removed, and four more were removed in the post-batch repair pass — a *this month*, a *first month of a tide*, two *fourth month / seventh month* date pairs, and a *Tuesday* introduced by the repair itself and taken out again. The week count on the coast is fifteen days at the end of Chapter 110. **The only three month-dates that remain are back-references to Volume 02's shore calendar about a city four hundred miles off, and they are correct.**
- **Chapter map:** 101 sixth of a tide; 102 seventh; 103 first of the next; 104 second; 105 third; 106 fifth and then sixth; 107 sixth; 108 seventh; 109 first of the next; 110 second to sixth. **The water is over the bar on THREE days of the seven — the fourth, the sixth and the seventh — and the licence is a seven-day one, so the bad days are three and not four; no chapter and no state file may say four.**
- **No canon distance from Chapters 1–100 was moved, quoted or contradicted.** The coast's own distances: the coast to Orison four hundred miles; the road head eleven miles; Thornglass two miles; the bar road four miles; Wether Cross a mile above the harbour. **A Reach is about four miles of water and Slackwater is nine.**
- **The money closes:** a penny a tide; thirty-nine tides a week = three shillings and threepence = eight pounds nine a year; threepence a week = thirteen shillings a year; thirty-nine is thirteen times three (2,028 ÷ 156 = 13); eight pounds nine (169s) plus thirteen shillings is nine pounds two, and nine pounds two of the crossing's ninety pounds eighteen is the eleven households. The crossing takes ninety pounds eighteen, of which eighty-one sixteen is tallage, nine two is the eleven households, three two is the chain and the oil and the tarpaulin, leaving six, and about two pounds a year for a wage leaves four. **The reading man at the crossing was paid two pounds a year out of the same money four years ago, out of a year in which the crossing took ninety pounds eighteen against a hundred and six the year before, and the reader was taken off in the eleventh month; there is no figure of four hundred and forty, three hundred and eleven or fifty-one anywhere in this batch and none may be used.** Alma Gannock's toll was short thirty-two pence in eight years over two bad weeks of sixteen carts, and she decided four hundred and sixteen times. **None of these is a half-farthing, a bucket, a barrel lid, a called reading, an eight-times figure, a man who sells salt or a woman walking a long way to avoid a rate.**

## Judgement calls a later reader should know

- **Chapter 101 opens on the ground on a cart road in the rain, and the whole geography of the Nettle Straits is given by a carter sitting on a load, in the negative, in about eighty words.** It is not explained in narration anywhere in the batch, and the batch prompt forbids it in any later batch too.
- **Sera Quill's sheet is on the page in the first four paragraphs and there is no scene with her and no romance beat.** Two questions on one sheet, *Whose account are you carrying, and who will hear it*, and it is not understood until the third of a tide, and then it is an instruction rather than a question, and it is the reason the refusal of consent is understood at all.
- **The refusal is staged as a person in the open, not as a room that declines to answer**, and the room that would have been a repetition of Volume 02's bench answers is a fishmonger's notice with a refusal on the back of it.
- **The rate chapter is a tide and not a bucket, in pence, at a thirteen times multiple, found on the ground with a stick and a board, and the person who pays is paying because a cart that does not come round does not earn.** It is a want, not a concealment, and nothing in it is a restaging of a washer of sixty-one.
- **The woman with a table of tides holds the table and is not the door.** She refuses the door twice, out loud, for a reason about a stall that is open on every one of the six tides. She is not a holder of a formal term, not a reader of a district, not the person a rate is read out to a room, and she is not barred from anything: **the reason she cannot do anything about the tariff is the water and the price of a day at the counter, and the reason her thirty-one years cannot go into the record is a pane, which is a second and separate failure and is the glassmaker's as well as hers.**
- **The glassmaker is a man of sixty-one and is not the mechanism and cannot be produced.** Corva Silt was the mechanism and could not be relieved; he is the customer of the authority and not the authority, and the batch's finding is that the authority rests on the man at the fire. **He holds a record he cannot use, and the reason is that it cannot be produced: the guild's pane will not come out of its room, his own is not the record, and the only thing that could settle it is a pane in a room.** The two panes disagree in two columns out of eleven and he has told nobody until Chapter 107, and then he told a stranger. **Note: the only other pane in the world is his, on his own shed wall, made for himself three years ago. The guild's charter pane and the guild's tide table are both in the tide office at Kestrel Reach and have never been out of it, and no later batch may move them.**
- **The pattern is planted and not recognised.** Sabra Trew's nineteen notches, a four-day gap, a grandmother's book in a school a hundred and forty miles up the coast. Ilyan wrote the address down and told her the truth about himself, which is that he has not done anything about it yet and probably will about the fourth of next tide. **No panel said anything. Nobody was afraid in a way they could name. Nobody said the words.** The glassmaker's two panes that disagree are a second plant and are not the pattern and must not be read as it.
- **The Bridgeward was not re-staged.** The coast's version has no dead names, a toll that has been short twice and was paid out of her own box, no boy with a line book, a person who is alive and eighty-one and has not been asked, and a road that works and has never been shut and was not touched. **A rule with no living witness is not the same as a rule with no living person in it, and this coast has the second and not the first, and the difference is the point.**
- **Chapter 110's last beat is a man of thirty counting notches into a piece of paper because there is nobody to check him, and it will be wrong, and it will outlast the post.** It is the batch's most durable object and it is not a minute and not a bench and not a document anyone can swear to.
