# Batch 0002 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 61–70, *The City of Exceptions*

## Goal and outcome

This is the second prose batch of Volume 02 and it stages the volume's climax: **a neighborhood hearing that forces a choice between a clean ruling that saves the district temporarily and a messy shared charter that gives residents an appeal.** Both sides are argued by people who are right, the choice is made in the room in front of the people it is about, it costs a named person, and it is paid for on the ground within twenty-four hours. **The appeal is in the air and in use and is not safely secured, and the volume's resolution — the appeal surviving as a durable right, and Sera recovering a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms — is named in the last chapter and not delivered.**

The batch's structural discovery is the **ward**. The word the whole of this business turns on is found in a guild's term book, upside down on a bench, in the rain, and it means *a duty a district owes and has not got round to* and not a place anybody lives in. A duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does. The word that let a woman be paid at a gate for thirty years is a word for a job, and a job is done by whoever is standing nearest. That single finding explains, in ninety seconds and in a court of eleven benches, why a rate can be paid to a ward and cannot be paid to a person, why the ward's seats have been empty for nineteen years, why a foot-gate opens toward the people the rule leaves out, and why the word *ward* has been doing the work of a body for a hundred and ninety years without anybody noticing. It is found by a man who cannot read a clause, out of a trade's working vocabulary, and it is corroborated by a bench of three and not by him.

**The clean instrument is good and it is written by a good man who signs his name under it.** Bel Ordway wrote L-3 on the back of a weight receipt in an hour and a half and it closes a gate and it is payable and it pays four hands and nobody is unhoused, and it puts a quarter of a penny on every bucket in a lane for ever with nobody in it who can say no. The messy charter is nine sheets, four terms copied off a bridge, a fifth term that wants a person, and a cost that has to come out of the same pump, and it gives the people of one poor district a right to be appealed against and nothing else at all. **The batch does not let the choice be a choice between a good rule and a bad one. Both texts cost the water. The difference is whether there is a person at the end of it.**

**Ilyan's growth is a body and a document, not a speech.** He is asked for a burden in Chapter 62 and can give four numbers and no sentence, and says so in front of a magistrate. He argues a gate hand out of thirty years of her own protection in one sentence, and the sentence is not about rights: *the whole point of a number is that it gives a person something to be angry at somebody specific about.* He counts her hours, and **the afterimage does not clear when he rests; it clears when he is inside a burden of his own** — the discovery is that he had it backwards for eleven days, and it is a practice with a four-hour limit in it and not a cure, and the moment he stops it comes back in a minute and comes back worse. He reaches the token once, at a cost, and the third layer resolves into a **rendering**: a picture of a gate from two hundred feet, at the same height, every time, and a rendering made for somebody has a vantage, and a vantage is a place. He does not know where it is and he has traded four days of smell for the question. **He takes the smaller charter and pays for it**: the carrier's clause puts his name into nine sheets in a poor district four miles from a bridge with a duty on it and a consequence, and he is now the fifth thing in this city's record that he cannot un-enter, and it is the only one of the five somebody can call out and ask to see him. **Maren Kett tells him on a step that he has made himself findable, that it took a month, and that it required no good argument, and that the city is going to spend ten years finding out which of those two things it wants.**

**The cost is named and it lands on people.** Bryn Sallow is thirty years and cannot come to the hearing because the gate cannot be left, and the reason is entered in her own words before anybody argues; she then takes the pay she refused for thirty years and finds she is owed it by two people she buys salt off, and says so in advance and says the order is deliberate. Wenna Tace takes the page in order to hold a district's law, and cannot appeal from it, and asks for both halves of that sentence to go in the record. Nell Ambrey enters, against herself, that a bench's time given to appeals means her sanitation complaint is not heard this year. Corva Silt asks to be relieved of the print authority for the fifth time and is refused and says she will not ask a sixth. Cael Rundle pays a rate on his own lane and says *this is the right rate* before he says anything else. **Wist Anhalt is asked what he wants for the first time in a month, and what he wants is not the props and not the money.**

**The machinery works, and the discovery is what it is for.** The first appeal in the record is served in four bells and two carts with no defect anybody could name, and the finding is about the *limit* of the ninth-bridge settlement, not its expansion: *on this span* is the court's own correct drafting and will not be widened, the claimant knew it and filed anyway, and the output is a **duty to answer** — eleven benches must say in public, in eleven days, whether they hold any page a person can swear to, and the court has no power to compel the answer. Nine answer, seven say they hold nothing, and a city has established as a matter of public record that it is empty, which is the first time in nineteen years and is not a remedy.

## Key events

- **The sitting on the category (Ch 61).** The twenty-ninth, the basalt court, nineteen names and eleven places, two matters losing their places, four benches lent by a guild that is out of money, and a builder who says he lives in the ward and is in neither of the room's two words. **Ilyan says out loud, in front of eleven benches and a magistrate, that he cannot do the hearing in the law's words, and it is entered, and Odile records that it has happened four times in eleven years and is not her trade.** The finding is in a guild's term book and is corroborated in three other documents. Rell recesses with two texts promised and states that one of them is a confession and not a victory.
- **The clean instrument (Ch 62).** Form L-3, eight clauses, published at the fourth bell on the thirtieth, written and signed by a weighbridge convener with a cart on the scales. Rundle's daughter's bucket. Bryn Sallow's answer, unedited and in one line: *the rate is the right size, I should have had it thirty years ago, and I am not going to pretend I am grateful either, and both go in the same line.* Ansel Nyle, from the low fen, reads a sheet he has no standing to touch and says the rate is the right size and that it does not carry.
- **The count (Ch 63).** Taken on the low water because a category settled by paper is a category settled wrong. The return is five places and a duty nobody does. Bram refuses the line book and is right. **Bream refuses to swear a rent roll and says why.** A household column is struck out at a ratepayer's request and the loss of accuracy is entered against the district. Wenna Tace says the court has counted places, not people, and that it did it in front of her on purpose.
- **The charter's labour (Ch 64).** Nine sheets, three days, a stove that does not draw. Read aloud on a bank in the mud to about a hundred and ninety people and it fails on words twice and cannot contain the one want that matters. **Sera Quill writes the carrier's clause and tells him she chose the consequence on purpose.** Bream asks that the lane not be in it and is told what that costs the district and says *then it is a worse charter, I asked for it anyway, do not thank me.*
- **The first sharing in the body (Ch 65).** He counts four hours and the leg thins instead of going. The afterimage clears inside a burden that is his own and does not clear when he rests. The token, once. The rendering, the vantage, the fourth layer, the door. **And the cost stated by the woman it belongs to: a number on a slate that only exists when a man from a bridge is standing on that bank.**
- **The telling (Ch 66).** A district that adopted the settlement by copying is told what it adopted, and the payer is **not** the district told: Meridith Vale pays four pounds and eleven out of the ninth bridge's road fund in a clerk's entry, and puts the price in the same breath — the same fund owes the fourth bridge ninety-six pounds for four props, and there is a hundred and four in it and fifteen of it has just gone. **Perdy Sill, thirty-eight, of a coal yard, is the template's second body and wants a cart, and the instrument has made a man with a sledge four times dearer without naming her once.**
- **The appeal (Ch 67).** Served in four bells and two carts. Corva Silt's eleven minutes in a lane. **The seal was served on a desk with a stamp and no clerk and nothing came back.** Kett is the advocate and argues against his own client. The court enters the limit, then the receipt, then **a duty to answer with no power behind it**, and says a duty to answer is the smallest power it has ever given out and that it is frightened of it. Rell: a city has said out loud, in eleven hands, that it is empty.
- **The choice (Ch 68).** The gate hand's absence is entered first, in her words, with the reason. Both texts argued by people who are right. Rell votes for the clean instrument, in writing, signed, for ever, and says why at length. **Harrow asks *who in this room reads*, and the person is standing there and nobody had thought to ask her.** The holder may not be a party, and she takes the page anyway. Ilyan's contribution is nine words. The charter is made at the ninth bell with a person in the fifth term.
- **The cost on the ground (Ch 69).** Half a farthing a bucket on Weir Lane, in chalk, on a barrel lid, the first tax in nineteen years, and a ratepayer who says *this is the right rate* before he says anything else. The props go back on a cart for four pounds and no new ones are bought this year. **Anhalt is asked and answers.** **The first appeal under the charter is sworn by the gate hand against the office of gate hand, is refused on a rule that is in the charter, and gets her the rate, by name, in chalk, and the thirty years entered as owed and not paid.**
- **The close (Ch 70).** Nine answers to the eleven-day question, seven of them *no*, and one of them *yes* from a preservation authority nobody has ever asked, which is the only new fact about the standing desk in this case and it was obtained by asking. A form with EXCEPTION at the top of it, printed by nobody on order, un-stoppable. **Bram writes to Wen Lask and says the price out loud; her answer is one question nobody can answer.** **The continuity line appears for the sixth time under the fifth term, with the word changed again, and Harrow refuses for the fourth time in the same words and names nobody.** The fifth thing he entered, with its size. Sera's two things, named and not resolved. The review in five weeks.

## Panel accounting

**Three System-rendered entries across the batch, one each in Chapters 61, 64, and 67**, at most one in any chapter, and none stating a general rule. All three are formal records entered in Ilyan's open case: the sitting as convened with the category question and what is not asked, the draft charter with the fifth term showing no holder entered, and the disposition of the challenge. **They are set as plain bold lines with no blockquote marker and each is introduced by a narrative line saying the System put it in front of him**, which is the Volume 01 convention and the only thing that lets a reader tell a rendering from a piece of paper. **Every block-quoted block in Chapters 61–70 is a physical object in the fiction**: L-3, the ward's entry in the guild's term book, the count return, the three pieces of corroboration, the letters, C-4, the Coldwater minute, Vale's entry, the bench's minute of the dispositions, the rate on the barrel lid, the bench's decision on the props, the gate hand's written refusal to come, the schedule and the pay line, the dispositions, the eleven-day answers, the form with the word on it, the letter from Wen Lask, the continuity line, and the five entries.

## Relationship and power changes

Ilyan's growth is a changed decision and a body. He is the name on a document in a room he does not control for a further twenty days; he is asked for a burden and cannot give the sentence; he argues a person into her own protection and the argument is about anger and not about rights; he discovers that his own work clears what rest does not; he accepts a duty with a consequence in nine words; and he is out-argued by his own advocate for an hour after saying the only sentence anybody needed. **He is not rewarded for a good speech and he is not punished for the archive.** The clean instrument was written by a cart owner who is not a lawyer; the eleven months' arrears were extracted by a barrow; the count was taken by a hauler; and the page is held by that same hauler, and none of those things happened because of him.

Sera is opposite him more often than beside him. She writes the clause that names him and constrains him, and says out loud that she chose the consequence on purpose and that it is in the record because a clause that cannot be broken is a post, and he has been a post for a month and it made him a gate. She declines to put a name in the fifth term she has not asked, and says the entering of her own failure would cost her licence. **Her third license term is what put her in Halloway and what put the charter on a bank in the mud; her fourth term is what she says out loud in the last chapter, and her family claim is named as a thing she has not looked for in a month and still needs a page that may not exist.** No romance beat occurs.

## Deliberately left unresolved

The historical right of refusal, of which one quarter of a sentence on a facing leaf now has to be read as a duty and not a place — a consequence, not a recovery, and the next batch must not be able to top it. The authorship question and the Shale Mirror, touched once in fifty minutes and not resolved. The standing preservation authority, which has now answered *yes* to a question nobody asked, and whose C—2 chain, whose plate, and whose wage are exactly where they were. The responsible person for the continuity line, which has spoken six times and has been refused four times. The fifth mark in the count book. The Bridgeward, pushed once by a boy who said the price out loud, and answered with a question nobody can answer. **Ansa Rell, out-voted and un-resolved, and difficult.** **The afterimage, the leg, the fourth layer, the door, and the token.** **The review, in five weeks, at which the charter of one poor district will have no standing unless somebody makes it have one.**

## Review pass

An independent reviewer audited Chapters 61–70 against Chapters 55–60 and the state files and returned seventy-odd findings across ten classes. **All actionable findings were fixed in place.** Six were canon or role violations: Cael Rundle speaking as Bream, Abel Kearn given Rundle's bad chest, Wen Lask's grandfather entered as the last name on the form instead of her father, a count attributed to Bram that is Ilyan's, a repetition of a chapter-59 paragraph in Chapter 67, and a re-run of the facing-leaf gloss in Chapter 70. Eight were arithmetic: the charter's annual cost stated as one hundred and eighty pounds in four places against a fund arithmetic that assumes forty-four; a cart described as thirty-four times a barrow; a farthing described as not being a quarter of a penny; a gate hand's count described as eight, four, five and six days across three chapters; the props described as propping a bridge for forty-one days when the seat did not leave its fortnight until the thirty-fourth day; the span's first load placed on the wrong day; the ninth bridge carrying loads for nine, eleven, thirteen and fourteen days in four places; and two hundred and forty people used for five different crowds. Nine were date slips, including a seat with four days left when canon gives five, a name matter on the board for nineteen days when the board gives twenty-two, a duty to answer created on the sixteenth and dated to the eleventh, and Sera's licence and her reading of the facing leaf dated to six weeks when the ruling was eight weeks back. Six were meta leaks, one was a missing System lead-in, one was an inline bold in dialogue, and eleven were repetitions of beats already staged in Chapters 55–60, now cut or reduced to a reference: the serving of the appeal, the gate hand's wage argument, the foot-gate, the barrow-as-a-load, the wheelbarrow clause, the preservation desk, the facing leaf, Nyle's two clearances, and a dead mother given to two characters. Three were misnumbered terms and the carrier clause was moved into the second half of the fourth term so that the charter has four bridge terms and a fifth that wants a person. Two findings were rejected as misreadings: that Chapter 60's documents lack bold (**they are canon and were not touched**) and that five of ten chapters are sittings (**the cards for Chapters 61–68 are a hearing, and the batch shape is the volume's climax**).

**Word range for the batch: 3,743–5,382.** Chapter 68 is the longest and is the volume's climax and is not padded. Chapter 65 is the shortest and is a complete scene. **Do not treat either figure as a target.**

## Batch ending

A rate of half a farthing a bucket is read aloud at a pump by a woman who is not allowed to appeal, on the same board as a name in chalk for six and a half hours a day at the district's own rate, and a hundred and forty people come off a bank in the water at the ninth bell. A bench of five in another district has minuted in five hands what it adopted and did not notice one term, which was an appeal. The first appeal in the case has been heard and it produced a duty to answer and eleven written answers, seven of which are *no*, and one of which is *yes* from a desk with a stamp and nobody in it. A charter with a person in its fifth term is five days old, is working, and has no standing outside one poor district. Four props are on a cart in a yard and there will be no new ones this year. A bridge four miles up the fen road has been carrying loads for twenty-two days and its west seat has not moved. A boy of seventeen has written to a woman of seventy-one at a gate on a good road and asked her a question she has answered with one of her own, and it is the only question anybody in this case has been asked this month that none of them can answer: **does your paper say anything about a road with nobody in it.** And in a room over a pump, under the fifth term, on a page a woman with a barrow keeps and cannot appeal from, a line came out that says *continuity preserves the record against the holder*, for the sixth time, with the word changed again, and a trialwright refused to rule on it for the fourth time in the same words and named nobody, and the man whose leg it is was on the floor for a minute and a half and got himself up against a wall.
