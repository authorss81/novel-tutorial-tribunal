# Batch 0005 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 91–100, *The City of Exceptions*

## Goal and outcome

This is the fifth and final prose batch of Volume 02 and it is **the half of the volume's resolution that is about Sera Quill**: `outline/series.md`'s second resolution line, *the appeal survives, and Sera recovers a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms.* Batch 0004 delivered the other half. **This batch delivered the fragment, and it delivered it as a quarter of a sentence on a facing leaf, with the gaps marked and not supplied, entered under her licence, dated and signed, and read as a duty and not a place.** The right of refusal is not restored. The historical question is not answered. Sera's own family claim is not filed and is not resolved. The removed page and her missing duplicate remain two events on two dates.

The batch's engine was Chapter 90's question — *nobody has ever told me what this page is for* — and the batch's finding is that the fragment **does not answer it and makes it worse**, and that the answer was worked out on the ground by a hauler in about four minutes, twice a week, for four months, while a man with a bad hand held a leaf flat for four days and a clerk entered a bill against a fund four miles away.

## The six findings, each worse than anybody wanted

1. **A question is not a matter, and the only one of the six parts of a matter a recordwright can be inside of by accident is the subject.** A claim, a subject, a burden, a witness, a remedy, an exit. Five are blank and one is filled and it is a woman with a barrow. Answering a hauler's question would put Sera inside a matter whose subject is somebody else, and the fourth term of her licence says she takes no matter of her own while she holds this one. **The hauler is the resistance, and she cannot be named in a support of anything, so there is no proceeding in which an answer to her question can be given to her.**
2. **A term that can be read a second way on the day it bites is a wall with a door in it, and the person who drafted the term is the person who has to say so out loud.** Sel Harrow proposed, on the court's own motion, a narrow reading of the fourth term that would have let her answer once in writing. She refused, in three reasons, and the third was that a sentence about what a page is for, entered with a licence against it, is a thing that will be used against a person. He then entered that he drafted the term, that he has used it against her four times without knowing, and that a thing a person drafted they do not get relieved of by deciding to be bound by it. **The fourth term is not a technicality and this batch proves it: it is the reason a district can be handed ten words with a crease in them and her own family cannot be handed a page.**
3. **A shelf can tell you what a rule said and cannot tell you what a rule was for, and that is the third time in four months the same answer has come back out of the same kind of place** — a bench of three that holds nothing, the standing authority of preservation, and a room over a pump that holds a page. The honest thing to do with a shelf is stop asking it questions it has already answered, and she stops, and writes down what it cost: the only desk in the city that said yes has now said yes and no and a reason three times, and a person who needs a page sworn can no longer even point at it.
4. **A unit is not a price.** A bench of four agreed what a load is — a load is a barrow, and the four in the guild's sentence is a fact about a cart and a cart does not come — and did not agree what a load is worth, because the price of a load is the price of a tide and **there is no day in this district that is a day.** The deciding cost the hauler her own count of her own work, in a bench's own book, with her name on it, for the rest of her life: she has now said out loud, in public, that her work has no day. The two debts are in one unit for the first time in four months — one hundred and fifty loads and about six thousand loads — and neither is in any coin.
5. **A form is a habit with a seal on it, and the word at the top of it does not take the clause. It takes the paper.** The first district to print another district's charter terms on a form headed with the word is Coldwater, and its clerk ordered four hundred and sixty-three of them on a correct sum, read the word before the print came back, thought about it for the better part of a minute, and printed it anyway twice more, and entered all of it against herself. A woman of thirty-eight with a good clause in her own district's instrument and a coal yard at the bottom of a slipway is asked for her pitch in writing, in front of five people, by a man with a sledge holding up her own bench's paper, and gives it. **No court and no bench has power over a form, and every bench that has said so has said it in a room with no reader and no pump and no rule and about nobody.**
6. **A person refusing herself and a person being refused cannot be told apart afterwards, and she wrote that down and refused to write down which of the two it was.** A six-year-old family claim needs a page. She has just entered ten words off a leaf about the keeping of an entry. She did not open the book. She wrote two letters with no reason in them to two people who have kept paper for her for six years, because a reason would be a matter. **The difference that lets her do one and not the other is the whole of her licence: a record is what a person knows and a claim is what a person wants.**

## The fragment, exactly

Entered under licence, the twelfth of the fifth month, in a cold room in a building with no name on the door, four miles from the court, upon the facing leaf beside the empty slot and not upon the removed page, which is not on the shelf and has not been on it since before she was born:

> **OF THE ENTRY IS A DUTY OF THE WARD, AND**

Entered as one quarter of a sentence. **It does not restore any right of anybody to answer no. It is not the clause. It is not for anybody.** The first line of the removed page, entered on the twentieth of the second month, still stands where it stands and was not re-entered. The comparison of the twenty-fourth of the second month — the facing-leaf line and the page over a pump, side by side, not the same sentence — **stands unamended**, and she says in her own book that one quarter of a sentence more does not make a better comparison, it makes a longer one, and that a reader in twenty years may prefer the earlier one and would be right to.

**Read as a duty and not as a place** is the whole of it. A ward is a duty and not a place; a duty cannot be answered, it can only be given work; the work goes to whoever is standing nearest; and in four miles the person standing nearest is the holder of the fifth term, who cannot appeal from one word of it and who has never been told. The finding is entered as a consequence and not a remedy: **the quarter sentence does not tell the holder what her job is. It tells her that keeping the entry is a duty of the ward, and the district has no ward and has never written down a duty, and she has been doing it for four months for a pound eleven a quarter, and the oldest thing she has ever read does not know her name.**

## The cost, which falls on people and not on documents

- **Corrin Vask, forty-one, sweeps a counting-house four miles from the court, at ninepence a day, and has for nine years.** He has wanted to be asked one question about what is on the shelves since the day he started. He was asked one. He held a four-hundred-year-old leaf flat with the heel of a right hand that is six years gone at the wrist, for three hours a day over four days, and could not open it afterwards without doing it one finger at a time. The price of the day — a shilling, paid — is entered **in the building's own day book in the same column as the sweeping of its floors**, with the name of the person who paid it, and the sweeping of those days is entered as not done and is not done by anybody else. **A thing with no price in it looks like a service, and a record that a reader cannot leave until a person has paid for it is a door, and this building is not a door.**
- **Sera's own standing.** She has made herself a door again — in a room nobody in four miles can find, in a building whose door is not locked, in a district that has asked for nothing, holding a fragment of a line four hundred years old. She knew it on the ninth and did it anyway on the twelfth, and the only reason she can give is that nobody was going to ask, **which is the same reason a desk gave her**, and she is not going to write it down as a reason in the place where a reason goes.
- **Ottoline Fenn, sixty-one**, has walked four miles every morning for eighty-six days, has told one person, and on the first of the seventh said the thing nobody had said: *I did not tell the district because the district does not know.* **The lost penny still cannot be entered as a loss because nobody collects buckets, and the walk cannot be entered as a burden, and there is no instrument in this city that carries the eighty-six mornings.** It is in a page a bench of four can come back with, in a district of about two hundred and forty people, in a hand that is not a clerk's.
- **Salla Kern, forty-four**, eleven years of pay days on a slate in a man's house, walked down a lane on her own money and was neither refused nor helped.
- **Perdy Sill, thirty-eight**, a pitch at the bottom of a slipway.
- **Ilyan Vester**: produced a third time, a day and a leg, and the eighth thing in his record, which is a print.

## Ilyan's eighth thing, and the leg

**The eighth thing: his name is printed.** The form in Coldwater carries the terms of the charter of the district of Halloway, and the terms carry the second half of the fourth term, and the fourth term names him. **There is now a piece of paper in this city that cites him four hundred and sixty-three times, in one district eleven miles off, under a word that says what it cites is temporary and that is in nine other districts, and a man with a sledge has used it to take a pitch at the bottom of a slipway off a woman of thirty-eight who has four people. He may not be cited as a permit, a schedule, a levy, an acknowledgment or a bearer of labor, and he is the only person in that sentence who cannot do one single thing about any part of it.**

**And the leg, and the finding he did not expect.** Carrying a notice is a duty of his own, and for about four hours on the eighteenth of the fifth month, carrying a notice down a road, the leg cleared, and that was the only time it cleared after the twenty-sixth of the fourth. It was not a gift. **It was a trade: he has been holding a gate hand's weight out of his leg for four hours by carrying a piece of paper up a road, and the paper was the weight, and the woman was not.** The leg does not clear and has not since the twenty-sixth of the fourth month. The rendering is unchanged in kind, the vantage has not moved, the door in it is at the same height and the same distance it was at on the third of the second month, the token is not touched, and the Shale Mirror is not touched.

## The continuity line: ten appearances, seven refusals

**The tenth appearance is the first one nobody put anywhere.** A bench of three seven miles from Halloway asked for a copy of the charter — which is not the same as adopting it and binds nobody — and a clerk copied nine sheets word for word from a copy, and copied the bottom of the ninth one as well, and did not know what she was copying, and has entered that she copied it and would like it entered that she did not know. **The line is in three copies and not in the sheet. A line copied three times is three lines or one and nobody has decided which.** It was read out in the open air at the First Verdict on the first of the seventh month, at the fourth morning bell, before about ninety people, and Sel Harrow refused it for the seventh time in the same four words, and named nobody, and said he would not change his sentence for the tenth any more than he changed it for the first. **The holder has still not read it, and nobody in this case will tell her, and she reads a charter in order and a charter ends at the fifth term.**

## The Bridgeward, pushed once, and not answered

At the sixth bell on the first of the seventh, a boy of seventeen read out one question from his own line book on a mud bank, and nobody took it into a file. **A form at a gate on a good road has a line for a person to be named against each term; eleven names went in and all eleven are dead and the last of them is a woman of seventy-one's father, and she has written over it twice and does not know why.** He says he is not asking anybody to go, that the price of the first push was paid in an afternoon instead of a year, and that he is not going to make a good road a bad road for the only reason that anybody in that family would ever have heard of them. **The volume closes on his question and not on a verdict, and the question is about a person: whose name is the last one in that line, and what is a person doing on a form that nobody has to answer to, and how would anybody find out, and why does she keep writing over it.**

## Review pass

**An independent review agent audited Chapters 91–100 cold, against the canon chapters and the state record, and returned forty hard findings and sixteen soft ones. All forty hard findings are fixed in the prose and the state record. The prose record is at `reviews/volume-02/batch-0005.md`.**

*The hard findings that mattered most, and what they were:*

- **The load conversion contradicted its own unit.** The bench agreed that a load is a barrow and that the guild's four-loads-to-a-cart figure is a fact about a cart that does not come, and then used the guild's four to convert the two debts — so a hundred and fifty hauls became six hundred loads. **The unit now holds: a hundred and fifty hauls is a hundred and fifty loads, and six thousand hauls is about six thousand loads, and the guild's four is not used for either, in the chapter, in Chapter 100's reading, and in six state files.**
