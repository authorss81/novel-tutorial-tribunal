# Batch 0004 Summary — Volume 03, Chapters 131–140, *The Appeal, and the Breach*

## Goal and outcome

The fourth prose batch of Volume 03, and the batch `outline/series.md` gives the appeal and the breach to. **The appeal is used twice, and it works both times, and it is a different failure from Volume 02's: a man spends the only appeal he has on a question in the right words, is answered out loud in front of seventy people, and the answer is true and good and useless to him — and the person who answers is not a bench and not a court and is right.**

**The batch's finding is that the road works and the road is two days a week.** There is one appeal on this coast and it is a person and it is a stone, and it runs on the fourth and the sixth at the turn, in the open, and the seventh is the one day of the week the harbourmaster does not know, and **on the seventh there is nothing at all.** That is delivered as a fact and not as a speech, in a carter's mouth, in Chapter 134: *there are two days a week when the stone is there and a woman on it, and about four hundred thousand days a week when it is not, and I have never once heard anybody on this coast work that out in a sentence until just now.*

**The batch's cost is a day, and it is somebody else's day, and nobody thanks him.** The four things a measurement on this coast takes are a lead line, a boat, a man who can sound and a day, and the first three are in a shed and in a boat and in a man of fifty-eight, and the fourth one is a cart and a pair of beasts and a man's own day at a landing, and it is given away in about nine words in a lane by a man of thirty-one who says *you are not going to ask me* before anybody has. **Nobody said thank you. Not the man with the bad leg, not the man in the boat, not the three people at the yard, and not afterwards.**

**The batch's milestone is delivered and it is the beginning of the milestone and not the end of it: Ilyan and Bettany Rook.** He asks her, in the open, on a good day, in front of about nine people who can hear it, whether she would like to be the one standing on the stone. She says no, and the no is true, and the truth is not the answer he wanted, and he is wrong out loud and she is right out loud, and they walk round the far stones having disagreed about everything and both go on working. **The end of that relationship is the end of the volume and is not in this batch.**

**The batch close is a breach and a decision and not a verdict.** A bank of stone the guild built nineteen years and eight months ago, on a stretch of water where the guild was right and is right, has eleven yards of its toe gone out from under the bottom course, and on the sixth of the eighth tide at about the seventh bell the water goes out through the middle of it, in front of about six people on a wall, and nobody is in it. The guild is not wrong. **The harm is not the hole. The harm is the gravel, and the gravel is two feet, and the two feet is the whole of that point.** And the number that would have told anybody — taken the day before, by a man with a lead line, out of a boat of his own — is in a book in a shed with a date on it, and four people on this coast know what is in that book, and he told all four of them himself at the top of the lane the night before, and not one of the four can do one single thing about it.

## What the batch delivers, in order

1. **Chapter 131, the fourth of the seventh tide, a bar day, the turn. The appeal used, and it works.** About seventy people on a flat that takes nine, a cart at the head of it with stone that has been on the wrong side of the water for five days, and **Wend Garrow, thirty-three, carter since he was nine, asking the one question the stone is for: what is the water going to do.** Bettany Rook answers out loud, in the open, in about two minutes, without hedging and without asking him for anything: take the cart at the near stones and not in the channel; the ebb comes off the far side; watch for the water at the far stones going brown at about the quarter past the turn; take the middle of it and not the start of it, because at the start of it the sand is coming up and the channel is still full of the last tide; you will have the best part of two hours; you will be over in about three quarters of an hour if your beasts are used to a flat; it is true tomorrow and the day after; on the sixth the water is back over the crown of the bar; the fourth of next tide is a longer one. **Nobody asked a second question, nothing was refused twice, the song was not sung because it is not the seventh, the stone is not treated as evidence, and the answer is not written down by anybody afterwards as though it were a record.** He says thank you out loud on the shingle in front of seventy people, **and there is nowhere on this coast to put a thing that happened to a man** — and the leg goes at the turn, at that moment, and seventy people watch and none of them says a word.
2. **Chapter 132, the fifth, a good day, the flat takes six. A man decides what to spend the only appeal he has on.** Hal Bevan, fifty-four, nineteen years on the chain, has one question unspent and it expires at the turn on the sixth, and has never told anybody he has it. The dealer's copy of the merged account has been in his breast pocket for nine days and **stopped a cart six days ago.** He prices three questions while working and nobody asks him what he is doing: the water (he knows that channel to about a yard, which is not a figure and cannot be put down); the guild's table (not a question about the water; one true sentence in four seconds; no way to get it back); nothing (the only reason a cart has ever been held off that flat stays a sheet a stranger wrote in a net loft). **Dell Gannock, Cleve Gannock, Sorrel Gannock and a carter with a load of deals each do work and each says one true thing and none of them is asked to advise anybody.** Corrie Tallow, at the far stones, says the other half, to the man with the bad leg and not to Bevan: **she has asked on that stone once, and she has wanted to ask where the ninth crossing of a bad sixth goes for about eleven years, and she is not going to, because a ninth crossing is a number and the stone does not do numbers.** He keeps his nine-word promise from Chapter 130 and tells Ilyan exactly what he is going to ask. **The thing he would spend it on is not a question about the water, and he knows it, and there is no other door.** He takes out a slate at the seventh bell and puts it back: **it is a slate, and it is the only thing he has that anybody will act on, and it has nothing to say to him about a piece of paper.**
3. **Chapter 133, the sixth, a bar day, the turn. The question is spent, and it is answered truly, and about the water.** He asks: **is there anything in that paper that is wrong about the water.** She says that is a question about the water, and tells him he gets one and has got one, and **answers it in the open in front of about seventy people: there is nothing in that paper that is wrong about the water** — and then the water, in her own numbers and terms, and then the one thing she will not do, which is to say whether the table on a wall a mile up the hill is right or the paper is right, **because she has never had those two things in front of her at once and if she tried it she would be guessing about the water and she does not guess about the water.** **Nobody is refused. The answer is given, fully, and it is useless, because the paper was right about the water and that was the whole of what was wrong with his week.** She tells him, in the ordinary way and not unkindly, that he did not spend it on the water, and that there is one stone and two days a week and her on it, and that is all there is. He goes back to work. **Seth Garrick comes out of the oil shed, looks at the flat, says one word out loud to nobody in particular — "Well." — and shuts the door, and that is all he ever says about it to anybody for the rest of his life.**
4. **Chapter 134, the seventh. The day there is no appeal at all, and the day the water owns the bar.** Water, weather, and the ordinary work of a seventh. **Gell Roke, seventy-one, at the harbour mouth with a pot of paint and a boy of about ten, because the turn on a seventh is the only moment in the week a man can paint the bar staff at two feet under; and the mark is short by four hundred yards and nobody has ever said so and nobody says it now.** Nine crossings; Corrie Tallow's slate for the ninth; the boy of eleven at a penny; the post and the board, merely there, not recounted, not renailed, and inspected once a year by a man with a hammer. **Danno Ray's sentence is said out loud a second time on this coast — *a rule that cannot be changed for a year is not a rule, it is a season* — and about nine people are within earshot and not one of them answers him, and it has not been answered the first time either, and it is not the rule he is complaining about, you have had that crossing a hundred years, he is complaining about the season.** The song is sung at the stone at the turn, as it is every seventh, and it is not written out, it is not the subject, and no question about it is put to anybody on the stone. **The stone was bare behind him. Nobody stood on it that day and nobody could have.** The person it lands on is Bevan, and what he has instead of the answer is the fact that he was told he was right about the water. And the batch's finding, in a carter's mouth, as a fact: **two days a week, and five days a week with nothing, and nobody is going to be able to use it.**
5. **Chapter 135, the first of the eighth tide, a good day. The defence the guild built, and the guild is right about it.** Out of doors on a working stretch of water with people who work on it. **Halse Tarrant, fifty-eight, thirty-one years on the wall and one of the hands that laid it, says what it is for in the language of his trade and is right about every part of it.** Two hundred and sixty yards in courses, nine at the inner end falling to two at the outer, laid on the seaward side of a nine-foot hole in a gravel bar, to hold two feet of gravel against an ebb that took a hut and a boat slip two winters before it went down. **Nineteen years and eight months old, the year the guild had a thing fired in a shed two miles along this coast, and nobody on this coast has ever put the two in the same sentence.** The guild was right, the danger is real, it is there that morning, and it has not been looked at since. **What he has not got is the four things — a lead line, a boat, a man who can sound, and a day — and he has had three of them since about the sixth year and has not asked anybody, because a wall does not ask for a boat.** And the plant, said flat, with no alarm and no countdown: **the water has moved.** It is outboard of the outer end now and has been for years, and he can see where it is working and has no number for where it worked then, and the numbers are on two walls a mile up the hill and two miles along this coast, and neither wall will come to him. Ordinary business goes on around the whole of it. Nothing is threatened, nothing is alarmed, and he tells a stranger at length and in the trade's mouth **why nobody on this coast ever asks anybody for anything.**
6. **Chapter 136, the second, a good day, Thornglass, the shed, the second fire. The two columns, and the woman who reads them aloud.** The third column and the eighth column are the two that record a place and not a depth. **Sabra Trew reads them out of her master's own pane, because she is the only other person in that shed:** the outer end of the north wall at two hundred and sixty-one yards from the post, and the toe of the seaward face standing to the second course for the whole length and to the fifth for the last nine yards before the outer end. **Hollis Thrale has the guild's two figures in his head in a clerk's hand, because the harbourmaster read him two columns off her own wall in a yard on the fourth of a tide and would do nothing else and was right to refuse the other nine, and he did not go back.** The guild's third says two hundred and sixty-four. The guild's eighth says the toe stands to the fourth course for the whole length and gives no length at all. **The two columns do not agree and are not meant to. The batch's only worked piece of arithmetic on them has been recomputed: a course on that wall is about the thickness of a hand, nine courses is about six foot, four courses is two feet eight and two is a foot and four.** **Three yards in nineteen years and eight months on a wall laid in seven weeks is not three yards of nothing, and he cannot say how much gravel has gone or when, because a number about a wall is not a number about water and you have to be in a boat to get one of those.** A third pane is refused again in about eleven sentences and is not a solution. And the thing nobody has said out loud: **he has never once said it to the man who keeps the wall, because he has never been there, and the two of them have been four feet from the same argument for two years and neither of them knew it.** The order stays under the batch book with a date and no figure. The arch stays cold. Nobody on this coast is going to be asked which column is right.
7. **Chapter 137, the third, a good day. The milestone. Ilyan and the woman with the table of tides.** The stone is bare, because it is bare five days in seven, and he asks her anyway, in daylight, in the open, on a working day, at the near end of a flat that takes six, with about fourteen people within earshot and nine of them with the whole of it. **Would you like to be the one standing on it. No.** And she answers it in three parts and in the open, and **the truth is not the answer he wanted.** A stone is worth what the person on it is worth and take her off it and it is four feet of flat stone and about a hundred years of people respecting a habit, and that is the half he expected and it decides nothing. **The other half: she would not give it up, and it is not a smallness** — in nineteen years nobody in that building has asked her for one thing, and the stone is the only thing on this coast that has ever happened to her, and what happened was that nobody made her do it, and the whole of what that gives her is that nobody can ever say she agreed to it; and if she stands down then in about a month a man on a bar road finds out what happens to a road with nobody on it, the way a carter finds it out, and it will be a person and not a rule. **And the part she wants written in his own words: she does not want to be a person doing it. She wants it to keep being the coast. I would rather be a coast than a woman. That is not a noble thing to say. It is the true thing.** And: **do not write it that I stood on that stone for six years out of duty — standing in somebody's habit is not duty, duty is a thing you agree to, and I never agreed to it, and if I had agreed to it I would have had to be asked.** He is wrong out loud, in front of nine people, before he is shown: **the hundred years of habit is the strong part, it has outlasted every record anybody on this coast has ever made including three glass ones, and he had it the wrong way round — you are the person in it and the habit is the hundred years of everybody else.** And she is right about all of it and about the other half too. **They walk round the far stones and have not agreed about a single thing and both go on working.** No romance, no rescue, no warmth, the pane is not put to her, the two panes do not come out of their room, the blank wage line is not argued, and the right of refusal is not restored.
8. **Chapter 138, the fourth, a bar day. The record that is in the world and is useless.** Mostly water and weather. **Sorrel Gannock, thirty-six, crosses the shallow in front of the reader, because she is the only one of the three who will, and she has done it eleven years in her father's place and for nothing**, and the leg goes in the boat and nobody says a word. It is about the eleventh time in twenty and it holds. **A driven stake in the seaward face, with a plate on it, gone the colour of a coin, carrying a guild punch and a number and no person's name at the end of any line, recording a place and not a depth** — a hundred and thirty-nine, and Ilyan does not lean over it and asks the man to read it out loud. **And then, in the trade's mouth, true and useless: a driven mark is a man saying the water was working here this morning, and it is worth having for about three years because the next man does come and look, and after that it is a piece of iron with a number on it** — and the guild's book of chainages is in a locked room a mile up the hill and he was not in the gang that drove them, **so he can tell you what kind of number it is and not what it is a number of.** A place from nineteen years ago is not a place now. **It cannot be produced at the moment anybody needs it, which is every time anybody needs it, and it is the only kind of record on this coast that cannot be produced at all, because it is not in a room and it is not on a bench and it is not in a cart — it is in the wall.** A cart's wheel comes off and nobody turns round, and **Ostre Brann says the new true useless thing: you can get a number off a wheel** — everything in a nave above the axle line took the load and everything below it did not, so the wood above the axle line is a record of how deep the going was — **and the number is about four inches, and it is about the ground, and it is about one crossing, and it is not about the water at all.** Nobody puts the two together and nobody could, and the number is not even the same kind of thing as a column on a pane. She is not asked to solve anything and does not solve anything.
9. **Chapter 139, the fifth, a good day. What a measurement costs, in a day, given away.** A lead line, a boat, a man who can sound and a day: the whole of it, and the only instrument on this coast that is not a pane. **Nobody has the money for it and the only day that could be had is a day somebody else was going to lose, and it is Cleve Gannock's — the beasts, the cart, the day — and he offers it in the ordinary way in about nine words in a lane, and he says *you are not going to ask me* before anybody has, and nobody asks him, and nobody thanks him, and he is two hours late at a landing and tells the man there it was the beasts.** Ilyan **has no standing to accept it, cannot refuse it on the man's behalf, and gives no advice**, and the whole of what he does is write down that it was offered and by whom and on what day and that it is his. The measurement is taken: **the toe of the seaward face is gone along about eleven yards, in the middle, not at the ends, and gone to about two feet of depth, and there is no bottom holding gravel under the bottom course for eleven yards.** And it is in a book in a shed with a date on it and he will swear to it in a room, and **it is worth nothing tonight, because the only instrument anybody will act on is on a wall a mile up the hill and does not come out of its room, and the other one is three years old and is about the same wall, and none of the three of them is going to travel to meet either of the others.** The guild's own answer comes up the coast road by post: **four correct lines, refused, and the guild is right about all of it** — a record of a moving thing is a working instrument, instruments do not travel, the guild will sound the wall at its own cost and in its own season and it will not be this season, and the guild does not send a copy of a working instrument to a person who has one; and the fourth line, which costs them nothing and which Slee notices: **the guild is not in dispute that the wall is nineteen years and eight months old and that it is to be walked.** **Ansa Rell writes by post, is correct, asks for a place where a refusal can be shown to somebody else, and is refused** — a coast with a stone and no box, said once by a woman at a stall, without softening anything. And the **blank wage line, in about four hundred words, doing on its own what a blank does**: a clerk two hundred miles off reads a line with a wage on it and no person on it and chooses the option that writes nothing, and the vote of a council of nine sits in a book for a year doing nothing, and nobody asks Seth Garrick, and nobody is going to, and in about a year a clerk will go looking for the minute and will not be able to find the person.
10. **Chapter 140, the sixth, a bar day. The batch close, and the breach.** A bar day with a westerly two days old, the sea over the crown of the bar, nine crossings, seventy people, and **Alma Gannock still knitting four miles up the coast, with the boy of eight about whom nothing happens, and he had been up the lane before the light and did not go in. She was told one thing in Chapter 129 and said no to it in four minutes, and what is true at the end of this chapter is that she does not know what is under any of it, and that nobody on that road is going to tell her.** Then the wall, and the sand packing, and the bar being a road for the length of the ebb, and the ordinary end of it: the deep making its depth again. **Pell Cawley, the boat's carpenter at the hard, who has been building and mending flat-bottomed boats there for thirty-one years, sees it first, and nobody was in it.** In the middle of the seaward face, for about eleven yards, the water was going out through the wall, and a man who works the water says what it means in four words, and the man who keeps it says the two things he wants said in the open: **the harm is not the hole, the harm is the gravel** — two feet of it, the whole of that point, the ebb coming through the wall instead of past it, a shore going, less of a disaster than the last time because this time the guild is putting stone in a hole instead of taking a wall out — **and the guild was right, and I want that said too, and by me, and in the open, and not once in the next year by anybody else.** Four people carry it across the flat and the story has four versions and none of them is about the gravel. Jory Vance makes the day-book entry standing up at the chain and the System renders it and nothing else. **Then the link, and it is a link and not a monster: three people on this coast who could do something about the eleven yards and not one of them can see the other two, and nobody has asked any of them for anything** — a woman of thirty-six with a boat and a towrope who is not going to be told about the ninth bell because nobody in that yard is going to think of it; a woman of fifty-one who asks how many feet and is told nobody has measured it in front of anybody, and who decides not to send a boy up the lane; and a man of sixty-one two miles along the coast whose eighth column is the only figure anybody has in their head. The rendering comes up on nine hundred yards of wet shingle at the ninth bell and the leg goes with it and does not come back and he tells nobody. **And the decision: he is going to go and ask three people whether they would like to be the one standing there — not to tell them, not to fetch them, not to carry a sheet, not to write one — and he has no standing to ask and has been produced four times already, and his one guess is that at least one of them will say no and that the no will be right.** And there is a fourth road he is not going to go up at all.

## The relationship milestone the batch owed, and what it looks like on the page

- **Ilyan and the woman with the table of tides: adversarial colleagues who are both right, and this batch is the scene the finding was waiting for.** The batch prompt's whole risk was a second Wenna Tace, and the volume's own answer to that risk was already on the page: **the glassmaker is the coast's answer, and Bettany Rook is not a person who holds a record she cannot use. She is the water.** Her table is a column of tides she reads out of a room on a hill and she is right about it every day of her life, and what she cannot do is be produced. **She appears in three chapters and in all three she is the only person on four hundred miles a person can be answered by, and the pane is never put to her, and the guild's two panes never come out of their room, and this volume does not make her wrong about the sea once.** In Chapter 131 she gives a carter a specific, useful, tradeable answer about the ebb and the next two days. In Chapter 133 she gives a man a true answer in the wrong words about the right subject and tells him, in the ordinary way, that he did not spend it on the water. **In Chapter 137 he asks her, in front of people, whether she would like to be it, and she says no, and the no is true, and the truth is not the answer he wanted, and he is wrong out loud about which half is the fragile one, and she is right out loud that he has a record and a person the wrong way round, and they end the chapter able to disagree in public and keep working.** She is not softened, not rescued, not redeemed, and not argued into warmth. The volume's design — *no trust restored, no romance, no rescue* — is honoured and the end of the milestone, which is the end of the volume, is not in this batch. **She has never once been asked and she is asked once here, out loud, on a shingle, in daylight, and the coast treats it as a habit.**

## The appeal, in full, and what it is not

- **One appeal, one person, no bench, no court, no guild, no written thing. A person stands on four feet of flat stone at the head of the flat, on the fourth or the sixth, at the turn, in the open, and asks Bettany Rook one question, and she answers it out loud, in front of everybody on that shingle, truly, without hedging and without asking for anything. Nobody may ask a second. About a hundred years old. Written down nowhere. The guild has never interfered and never will.**
- **It is for the water.** Seth Garrick said so to a carter twice in plain words in Chapter 127, in plain words and to his face, and he was right, and the batch's failure is that everybody else now finds out what he already said.
- **The song is a different custom and it is on a different day and it belongs to a different man.** The song is on the seventh. The stone is on the fourth and the sixth. **The seventh is the one day there is no appeal at all, because the harbourmaster does not know the seventh, and nobody on that coast pretends otherwise.** That is the batch's finding in one sentence and it is said, in Chapter 134, in a carter's and a chain-man's mouths, as a fact about the shape of a week.
- **The song is sung and is not staged.** It is sung at the stone at the turn in Chapter 134 as it is every seventh, in four sentences of narration, with no lines written out, not the subject of anything, and no question about it put to anybody on the stone. Nobody put a figure out of it and nobody appealed with it.
- **Nobody merged the song and the stone and nobody was asked which is older.** About nine people know they are two customs. That is a fact about nine people and it is not a discovery and it is not a mystery, and it is restated once per chapter at most and is not a beat.

## The batch's new coast, and the four things, and the breach

- **The training wall.** Two hundred and sixty yards of stone laid in courses on the seaward side of a nine-foot hole in a gravel bar at the north end, four hundred yards up the lane from the crossing. Nine courses at the inner end falling to about two at the outer; the outer end stands out into the deep on purpose. **Built by the guild in one season of about seven weeks with eight men, and the last course went on nineteen years and eight months ago, in the same year the guild had a thing fired in a shed two miles along this coast for the room upstairs of the office at the Reach.** It is there to hold two feet of gravel against an ebb that, in the two winters before it, went back eleven yards and took a hut and a boat slip belonging to a man called Teel. **The guild was right and the danger is real and the guild is right now.**
- **The physics, and it is all given by working people and not in narration.** On a good day the deep is nine feet. On a fourth, a sixth and a seventh the sea stands on the crown of the bar, the ebb is short and hard and about an hour and a quarter, it brings the sand out of the channel and packs the middle of the bar flat, the deep fills, and the whole two hundred yards of gravel is a road — **about eleven times in twenty, and the nine is somebody's cart in nine feet of water.** The wall stands out of the deep on those three days and a flat boat can be stood over the top of it. On the other four days there is nine feet of green water over the toe of the seaward face and no way at all to get at it.
- **The four things: a lead line, a boat, a man who can sound, and a day.** The line is the guild's own, in a box in a shed since the wall went down. The boat is a man's own and has not been out in a year. The man who can sound is the man who keeps the wall and can put a number on a depth to a foot inside of forty yards. The day is the only one that cannot be bought and it is a day somebody else was going to lose. **This is the same four things the glassmaker named at a bench in Chapters 125 and 128, in the same order, in the same plain words, and a man on this coast said them out loud on a good day without knowing anybody had said them before.**
- **The driven mark.** An iron stake in the seaward face with a plate on it, gone the colour of a coin, carrying **a guild punch and a number and no person's name at the end of any line**, recording **a place and not a depth.** The guild drove one every five yards along the whole wall on the last two days of the job, off a chain, with a boy and a book, and the book went up the coast road and is in a locked room a mile up the hill, and the man who keeps the wall was on the inner courses and was not in that gang. **So he can tell you what kind of number it is and cannot tell you what it is a number of.** It reads a hundred and thirty-nine. **It is bedded about nine yards along the wall from the inner end and about two foot under the top of the ninth course, which is the full six-foot height at that end and is why it is only standable there.** It is under nine feet of water on a good day and standable from a flat boat on a fourth, a sixth and a seventh, and it has not been looked at in nineteen years. **It is the only record on this coast that cannot be produced at all, because it is not in a room and not on a bench and not in a cart — it is in the wall. And a place from nineteen years ago is not a place now.**
- **The breach.** Eleven yards of the toe of the seaward face, in the middle, not at the ends, gone to about two feet of depth, so that the bottom course of that wall is standing on nothing along that length. Found by a lead line from a boat of his own on the fifth of the eighth tide, a good day, and written in a book with a date on it, and sworn to. **On the sixth, at about the seventh bell, with the sea standing on the crown and the sand going out of the deep, the water goes out through the middle of the wall for eleven yards and makes a boil about the size of a doorway, and six people on a wall see it and nobody is in it.** The wall is not failing and the guild is not wrong. **The harm is not the hole and it is not the breach: the harm is the gravel, and the gravel is two feet, and the two feet is the whole of that point, and the first thing the water does on the far side of a breach is take gravel.** It is a shore going and it will take less than two winters this time, because this time the guild is putting stone in a hole instead of taking a wall out.

## The money, and the blank, and what this batch did not restate

- **No figure from the crossing's money is restated in this batch except the two pounds a year on the blank line, which is the line's own wage and not a sum.** The crossing's ninety pounds eighteen, the tallage of eighty-one pounds sixteen, the households' nine pounds two, the three pounds two, the six, the four and the thirteen shillings are all in the corrected record and **none of them is put on the page in Chapters 131–140.** A reader cannot add this batch's figures up because there are none to add. **The margin of four is not said aloud in a room and was not said aloud in a room.** The council's five-to-four vote is **not repeated** and no council sits and no council is built.
- **The one money-shaped thing in the batch is a blank.** A line in the guild's quarter for a person to be paid two pounds a year out of what the crossing takes, to count crossings in persons and not carts, has had no name on it since the fourth of the fourth tide. The office read it this quarter and chose the one of its two options that writes nothing. **And then the chapter does what the prompt asked and shows what a blank does on its own, in about four hundred words, in a clerk's hand two hundred miles off: the vote of a council of nine sits in a book for a year doing nothing; nobody in that building has asked the office here to fill it in and nobody here has written and asked; in about a year somebody will go looking for the minute and will not be able to find the person and will put the minute back.
