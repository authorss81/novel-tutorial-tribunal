# Batch 0001 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 51–60, *The City of Exceptions*

## Goal and outcome

This is the first movement of Volume 02 and it delivers the first three of the four things `outline/series.md` assigns to the volume: the print is put in somebody else's hands early, the power progression arrives at its price, and the volume's climax is named in the air without being staged. It does not resolve the volume.

The batch's turn is not a speech and it is not a judgment. It is that a two-hundred-year-old word meaning *registered* gets read in a poor district as *proper*, and the instrument that does the reading is **well made.** Corva Silt is thirty-four and has held the Halloway bench's print authority for six years and she filled in a form on the second day after a hearing four miles up a fen road, lawfully, without asking anybody, because a district's print authority runs to any public instrument and there is no fee and no permission. The document she produced has three good clauses and one excellent clause and it is unjust, and Ilyan's finding in the field is that this is the problem and not the excuse: *it is well made; that is the problem; somebody made it well.*

**The cost is staged rather than discovered.** Kett warned about the phrase in open court, was told it is the charter's own word, and was believed by nobody. So the batch does not surprise anybody; it makes a room in which a ratepayer uses Ilyan's own citable precedent against him, correctly, because he asked for it to be citable in a corridor at two in the morning a month ago. The ninth-bridge settlement's own door — *a person not on a register is not a trespasser by that fact alone* — turns out to be the sentence a woman with a barrow can use to get eleven months of arrears out of a district bench, **and the same sentence is the reason a clerk could shut a gate**, and the sentence right beside it in the same charter is the one that traps her, because a barrow is a load and a keeper admits a person on foot who offers no load.

**The load falls on a person and she is not the one the room can see.** Bryn Sallow, forty-four, gate hand at the low-water culvert, thirty years in the post, stands with one foot on the sill-stone and one on the bank and had asked in writing that it be recorded that no person has told her which of two things is true, for thirty years. She is the visible end of a burden whose far end is a ward that has no name, and the chapter that names that is also the chapter where the burden gets an entry, and the entry is unpaid.

**The right does not travel.** The batch's most important structural fact is that both of Volume 01's new instruments are one bridge in one ward. The appeal runs to "any person affected by a ruling **on this span**." The exit is entered in a register kept **in the ward** and sworn by **the landlord of the lower road**. Wenna Tace is four miles away. She is affected by a ruling about a culvert and the culvert is not the span. She asks who she writes to and nobody can answer her, and the answer she gets is a ratepayer with a cart.

**And when the right is finally used, it works perfectly — by the man who was already in the case.** Ilyan files the first appeal in the record on the twenty-third, on term six, before the review date, with notice on every one of fourteen named parties and one seal, and the appeal does not suspend access, housing, or work, and the whole apparatus does exactly what Volume 01 built it to do. It is the fourth thing he has entered into this city's record that he cannot un-enter, and he says so into the record with the size of each, so that a clerk in four years does not assume they are the same.

**The power arrives as a practice, a failure, a decision, and a leg.** Ilyan is now Stage 2, Seam Reader. He sees the assembly of a rule inside a hearing field — which piece is bolted to which, and what a rule rests on when it is resting on nothing with a name — and it arrives as two shadows from one body, a chain on a handbell trembling four seconds before it is struck, and a foot-gate eighteen inches wide, hinged at the bottom, opening outward toward the people the rule leaves out. He practises it deliberately on two documents about money and gets a true result in four seconds and pays for it in six. Then he uses it once in public and is **wrong**: he puts the burden on the gate hand, and the field shows him that a burden which can be paid to a person is a burden somebody has been paying for thirty years out of a gate at a rate nobody wrote down, and the word that let her be paid was *ward*. The misassignment is what he carries, which is why the failure is in the body and not in the memory. Then a letter arrives from a keeper he has never met, two lines, saying *if it binds nowhere I have a copy of nothing and a cart that cannot turn. You do not owe me the power. I want you to know that I know*, and Ilyan **withdraws his own request on the record** and takes a loss, and gets something smaller: that a citation is not an adoption, and four hours a day at a gate for eight days at a wage, in chalk, by name, at a pump.

**The new question is the worst one in the book, because the answer is *it is fine*.** On the far side of the water past the cut banks, a region with no district takes an eighth of a load at a gate, and the road is good, the toll has never been short, the loads go through, and the book carries the ninth-bridge settlement copied out term for term with **eleven dead names entered as the witnesses against it.** Nobody can challenge it, because a challenge must be served on every party named and every party named is dead, and the clerk's office that took the form closed the week it went in, and the desk is a desk. A seventeen-year-old bell-runner has written it in his line book and has put it in nobody's file, and has told Ilyan that if he goes and looks, then in about a year that road will be a bad road for the only reason anybody in that family has ever heard of.

## Key events

- **The print in Halloway's room.** The sheet on the gate board went up on the second day after the hearing, nine days before Chapter 51, and a copy went up the fen road to the ninth bridge on the fifth day, which is how a thing two days old becomes a week and a half old in a poor district. Nobody had asked Halloway for a copy of anything.
- **The reader of the first movement is a refuse hauler.** Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, is the only person on her street who reads, so she reads the notice out at the queue, and she finds the fourth paragraph on her own and reads it twice, which she has never done in eleven years, and says the word in it is the bridge word, and that it means a person in a book, and *look at the size of that book*.
- **The bench's own clerk explains the copy without defending it.** Corva Silt says before anybody else does that she printed it, that the print authority runs to any public instrument and there is no fee and no permission, that she read the fourth paragraph four times because it is the sentence she was putting her district's name into, and that she read the fifth once at night on a lamp, and the fifth said a ward rate paid to a person in chalk on a day-book line, **and this district has no ward and has a woman.**
- **The honest first use of a precedent is extraction, not obedience.** On the thirteenth day the room spends forty minutes working out, from a pay book and a bench rule, how to get eleven months of arrears out of a sentence in a charter four miles up the road, and gets it, and Wenna Tace says *eleven months* and goes out to her barrow. **The hundred and fifty hauls in the same eleven months that are in no book of the district cannot be entered by anybody in the room, because nobody there can say what a haul is where there is no cart.**
- **Instrument H-9.** Five clauses, numbered, sealed, published at the fourth bell on the fourteenth. Clause three, written by the clerk in the small hours because *a notice that says who may open a gate and does not say it is the gate hand is a notice that says anybody may open a gate*, is the best clause on it. **Clause two is struck out by the clerk herself on the fifteenth, and she insists the bench minute it formally so that it is not hers alone.**
- **The first consequence that cannot be taken back is a bell attached to nothing.** At the sixth bell on the fourteenth, with eighty people watching, Bryn Sallow rings a handbell on a chain — a handbell is attached to a hand — and a carter with four loads of district sand and lime turns around and takes it back up the hill, and the apron round the standpipe does not get its sand, and a boy of nine watches the whole thing, and the clerk enters, at Ilyan's request and not the bench's: *the gate hand was not instructed. No person instructed the gate hand.*
- **The cost of being a name is a fair question nobody can answer.** Cael Rundle moves that the ninth-bridge settlement be withdrawn from use in Halloway — not the sheet, the precedent — and asks the attached record-holder, on the record, whether his own paper makes a gate shut to the unlisted unlawful. **The question is fair and Ilyan does not have the answer and says so in front of sixty people in a room where he has no standing, no constituency, and no advocate.** The answer takes an hour to come four miles up the road, and the hour is Bryn Sallow's, standing at a gate, and **there is no line in that district on which an hour like that can be paid**, because the line that pays it is a term of the paper the ratepayer has just asked to withdraw. The clerk's hand, on the back of a slip, with the hour on it: *the office has no line.* And when the answer finally arrives, in a counter's hand off a bridge, it is that the ninth bridge's paper **is** the Halloway sheet with *span* struck out and *district* written in over it.
- **A gate is not a span.** The answer, read aloud from Ilsa Bray: *the access condition of this span is about this span. A span has a deck and a load path and a keeper. A gate on a culvert has none of those things, and the sentence is about a person and not about a list, and a district may make a list of its own.*
- **And the rule does not travel, so the two districts that had nothing now have a copy of something.** The low fen and the cut banks adopted the settlement entire as their own maintenance term. Ansel Nyle has called the deck clear twice; both loads were peat carts that could not turn; both were off in nine minutes; both were paid out of road funds to the person on a line in chalk. **The ward line does not go short on a thin week, because the ward line comes out of road funds and the toll does not, and a man who has to stop a load for nine minutes gets his nine minutes from the same place whatever the week looks like.**
- **The common-rule sitting fails on a category.** Eleven benches, nine districts' clerks, four drafts, three hours, and the argument is not about a rule. Two delegates name the ward differently and both are right about somebody. **Ansa Rell's entered finding: this city has two words for the same poor and will not pay either of them.** Draft eleven goes to the eleven benches with the ward question on its face and no answer.
- **The field at the culvert.** Harrow opens it at the fifth bell on the tide turn, at the second change, because a field which tests records behind a bench tests nothing. **The court says twice, on the face of the field, that it has no power over the gate and takes no view on whether it should be open or shut.** The disputed words are the ninth bridge's own sentence about a trespasser. Tarin holds the rope with four stakes and no order.
- **The foot-gate.** Eighteen inches, hinged at the bottom so the water cannot lift it, opening outward toward the flat, mended in the eleventh year when the bank went soft. The gate hand's account of a machine, in the past tense, given twice, with no improvement: *a man with a loaded barrow cannot turn a foot-gate on a soft bank. It was not a rule. It was a hinge.*
- **The burden schedule, and the page with the hours on it.** Four lines. The gate, first light to dark, by the gate hand, *not my hours, I have never counted them and I am not going to start now for a page.* The foot-gate. The hand-bell, bought at a market, **attached to nothing.** And no rate, because the rate is set in a book four miles up the road and this court has not amended that book. **It is the first page in the history of this city with a woman's own hours on it, and the hours are not a number, and the court says so on the face of it.**
- **A marshal's account of a practice, entered and not found on.** Tovin Dain: a burden assigned to nobody is assigned to the person with the least standing in the room, every time, without exception, in nineteen years, and she is the only one in the chain who can be made to be there without anybody having to write anything down. Four hundred and eleven loads, and nobody said yes to any of them.
- **The four hours, and the loss, and the leg.** Ilyan puts his hand out and finds the rope and holds it and Tarin says take your hand off the rope. He can say what the field did. He cannot say what he did. He sits on the step and his left leg is not his.
- **The letter from a keeper.** Two lines, in a keeper's hand, from four days away, arrived before the decision instead of after it: *if it binds nowhere I have a copy of nothing and a cart that cannot turn. You do not owe me the power. I want you to know that I know, and that I have had two days with it.* **Ilyan withdraws his request on the record and asks for the smaller thing instead, and says the true reason out loud: he is the only person in the field who would be affected by a decision he makes in nine minutes about a bridge he is not on, and he had made himself citable in a corridor a month ago and had not been frightened.**
- **The token is the same kind of thing as the afterimage.** It has never been warm. On a milestone it is warm to about the temperature of a hand that has been on a chain, and touching it takes the low water out of his nose for fifty minutes, and when it comes back it comes back with the water over the flat and a third layer under both, and he understands in the body, not in words, that the passage in his pocket and the thing in his leg are the same operation, that one was done to him without asking and the other is being done by the same somebody, and that whatever put the low water in a leg on a bank had put him on a bridge.
- **The refusal, and the woman the right does not reach.** Wenna Tace refuses the four hours and the balance of the tide. The exit is four miles up the road. **Hanne Kolm walks the four miles with the box under her arm on the twenty-fourth, refuses to let anybody carry it, and swears a schedule to her own sworn volume at Halloway** — not a new instrument, not a new column, not a new volume, and what is new about it is that nobody will take it. The court neither accepts nor refuses it. The reason is on the back of a barrel lid in a clerk's hand, at a pump, where people read it going for water.
- **The first appeal, and the arithmetic.** Ilyan asks for the arithmetic to be entered while the woman who can check it is standing up: *every line of it is about my crossing and my room and my work, and I have got the sixth line right, and the sixth line is that the appeal does not suspend anything.*
- **A child comes down a fen road and is not called.** Tamsin Ro, ten, asks a gate hand three questions about a chain, and comes back up the bank and says the difference between her rope and this one is not the machine. *The difference is that somebody looked at the pin.*
- **The batch's new question is reported, not investigated**, and Ilyan declines to chase it and says why: there is a preservation office in this city that has been keeping records on purpose for four years and he has not gone near it, and if he chased every odd thing that came up the road in a month he would be the man who made records stop coming, to everybody.

## Panel accounting

**Three System-rendered entries across the batch, one each in Chapters 56, 57, and 59**, at most one in any chapter, and none stating a general rule. All three are formal records entered in Ilyan's open case and each is introduced with a narrative line so the reader can tell it from a document: the field's opening and its disputed words, the burden schedule as entered, and the challenge as filed. **All other bold or block-quoted text in Chapters 51–60 is a physical object in the fiction**: H-9, the read-back, the bench minute, the burden schedule lines, the declaration and the withdrawal, the board at the gate and the clerk's reason on the back of it, Kolm's schedule, the docket, the four lines off the ninth bridge's frame, and the props message read back by Coll.

## Relationship and power changes

Ilyan's growth is a decision and a body, not a power. He is the name on the document in a room he does not control for eleven days. He is asked a fair question and cannot answer it and says so out loud in front of sixty people. He uses his new perception once, correctly, on purpose; and once, wrongly, in public, and the wrongness is what stays in his leg. He takes a loss on the record in order not to take a bridge from a keeper who has no other source for it. He files the first appeal in the record and it works and it does not help the person it was wanted for. **He is not rewarded for a sentence and he is not punished for the archive: Corva Silt printed the instrument and Wenna Tace found the sentence that got eleven months of arrears and both of those happened without him and would have happened without him.**

Sera is licensed, and the license is the engine of the batch rather than his consolation. Her third term — carry the terms of the matter to any other district that asks — is the reason she is in Halloway, and it turns out that **a district that has not asked has no door in it**, and she went in the front and found a wall with a kind clerk behind it. She corrected the volume's framing twice. She read the facing leaf again in Chapter 60 and found that she had been reading a place into a sentence for a fortnight because a place was what she had, and said she would rather be wrong in a lane than be wrong in a record. **No romance beat occurs.**

Wenna Tace wants money and says so. Bryn Sallow wants her thirty years on a page and will not count her own hours, because counting them would make them a wage and a wage wants a rate and a rate wants a body. Corva Silt is the mechanism and the archivist of her own mistake. Cael Rundle wanted the paper gone and got a sentence instead, and said so. Nell Ambrey is right and is losing. Oakes refused a wage in one sentence and was right, and his sentence is the ninth bridge's tenth term.

The fifth mark in the count book, unexplained. ## Review pass

An independent reviewer audited Chapters 51–60 and **forty concrete defects were found; thirty-eight were fixed and two were rejected as misreadings.** Seven were meta leaks in which the narration referred to the book as an artifact (*this volume*, *this batch*, *this novel*) — all removed. Three were canon violations: the print-shop's cold-paste page had migrated onto the Quiet Chamber's cut page, the trespasser sentence was credited to the wrong hand and conflated with Tamsin's five-terms court copy, and Tamsin's not-a-trespasser account was misattributed. A romance beat was flagged by the narrator in the last line of Chapter 51 and cut. **Five weekday names had crept in** ("Tuesday" four times, "Thursday" once) and were removed, along with a dozen bell references to a tenth, eleventh, and twelfth bell that Volume 01 never uses. **Chapter 55's common-rule sitting was failing on a missing clerk, which is the forbidden procedural failure; it was rebuilt so that it fails on a real category conflict and the magistrate's own finding is that the city has two words for the same poor.** A rule the batch had invented — that a licensed recordwright cannot read a public instrument aloud without a translator — was cut, because it contradicts Sera's third license term. **The Stage 2 practice beat was missing and was written in**: four seconds of true, useful perception on two documents about money, paid for in six seconds of a cold hip. **The three measurements were conflated in the closing image of Chapter 60 and were separated.** The four-hours wage contradicted itself four times across two chapters and now reads as four hands, four hours, eight days from the twenty-first, ending on the twenty-eighth. Kolm's schedule carried four mutually inconsistent dates and now carries one. **The batch's own timeline, tide week, bell order, load counts, cart counts, and the district clerk's refusal date were rebuilt on one anchor.**

Two findings were rejected: that Sel Harrow's *trialwright* is a new coinage (**it is the term used for him in Chapters 1–50 and in `outline/volume-01.md`**) and that Ilsa Bray's refusal to help Tamsin further is an unsourced addition to canon (**`state/character-state.md` records it exactly**). Two were accepted and rewritten: a surname collision with Lio Fenn, and a nameless seventh official deciding the batch's central refusal. A further pass on the reviewer's own edits then caught three regressions of its own and fixed them: a narrator flag in Chapter 51's last line that narrated a romance beat the prompt forbids, a slip of the speaker in Chapter 54's closing exchange, and a garbled cart count in Chapter 54's opening.

**Word range for the batch: 3,035–5,293.** Chapter 56 is the longest and is not padded; it carries the field, the practice, the first use, the failure, the cost, and the token. Chapter 59 is the shortest and is a complete scene. **Do not treat either figure as a target.**

## Batch ending

A gate on a low-water culvert four miles below the weighhouse is shut to loads by an instrument that a thirty-four-year-old clerk printed at the fourth bell, lawfully, from a charter four miles up a road that its own text says is about a bridge. The refuse is still on the flat. A woman has stood at the gate for thirty years and has now a page with her own hours on it and no rate against them, and four days of the eight were paid and four were not, and the reason is on the back of a barrel lid where people read it going for water. A sixty-one-year-old landlord has sworn a schedule four miles outside her own district because there was nowhere else to put a refusal, and the court has entered that it neither accepts nor refuses it. The first appeal in the record has been filed by the only person in this city entitled to file it, and it works. A magistrate's court sits in eleven days on a question nineteen years old: whether a district may pay a person who has no address. On a bridge that has been open and unloaded for nine days, a keeper's counter reads a light load out one and a half and back, the pale set does not move, and the bell is rung for the change by a man who has looked at the pin on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope. And past the cut banks, on a road that is good, a woman of seventy-one collects an eighth of a load at a gate where there is no district, out of a book with eleven dead names in it, and nobody living can be told that they may challenge it.
