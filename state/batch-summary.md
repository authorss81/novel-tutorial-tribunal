# Batch 0003 Summary — Volume 02, Chapters 71–80, *The City of Exceptions*

## Goal and outcome

This is the third prose batch of Volume 02 and it is the batch that **uses** the volume's machinery instead of building it. The carrier's clause, born in Chapter 68 and never once exercised, is exercised here for the first time, and it turns out that what travels is not only paper. **A word printed at the top of a form costs a working man a season. A page held by a person who cannot appeal turns that person into the door of a district and there is nothing behind the door. A debt of thirty years gets a figure and the figure is wrong and the woman it is owed to refuses it and cannot be made to accept it. A city that has said out loud that it holds nothing finds out what a public *no* costs the people who live behind it. A place of sixty-one people with no name in it finds out that the only way to give it a page is to destroy the page four miles away. And a public maintenance review in the open air decides a sum, buys four new timbers for a bridge four miles up a fen road, and takes four hours a day away from a man of twenty-two whose name nobody had entered anywhere.**

**The batch's engine is the carrier's clause, used once, and the consequence of using it is a person.** Ilyan walks eleven miles he is not obliged to walk, past the point at which the clause stops obliging him and nobody can make him go on, and the leg is his own the whole way because walking is a burden of his own, and it stops forty yards past the end of the lane. He is paid nothing. The district that asked cannot pay. The ask itself is a piece of paper that cannot be verified by anybody in the case, because the only person in the district who can read a paper is a cowman's wife of thirty-eight who cannot read a contract. **And the district that asked did not want a charter. It wanted a person to come and stand in its room, and when it was told that what it had been sent was a man with a duty and a consequence, it wrote that down and asked for him anyway.**

**The batch's discovery, in four parts, each of which is a worse answer than anybody wanted.**

1. **The word on a form is not a label. It is a claim that a rule is temporary.** A summary of a permanent instrument, in a language nobody in the district uses, is a different rule. A weighhouse foreman who had a real second reason for saying no to a man uses a printed form to say the first reason, in ninety seconds, honestly, and then says he would like somebody to send the shelf back. A barrow-hirer of thirty-four loses a season of four months and a roof.
2. **A holder is a door with no room behind it.** A holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one — and that one rule is why everybody in a district brings the holder everything, because she is the only person who can read, and she is the only person the paper will not have. She makes her own choice about it, at a cost she names on a page: she asks a bench of four for a rate for a haul, having cut the barrow out of that charter herself, and the rate she is given is two thirds short, and she must read it aloud every quarter for ever and cannot appeal from it.
3. **A public answer is a price.** A bench that has told the truth about itself in its own hand has priced a room in its own district at one answer in eleven days, and the price is being paid by a woman who has lost a fortnight's notice to a landlord who used the bench's own letter. One bench of the seven withdraws its answer in writing, because of a woman called Adela Cray. And the one *yes* in the city turns out to be a yes about paper and a no about people: an authority that holds every instrument printed here for four years says, in the same hand for the second time in fifteen days, that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person, because a sworn page is a record of a person and an office with no person in it is a shelf.
4. **A place cannot have a page, and the only candidate to be one is paid for by four hundred people.** Nine Yards: sixty-one people, nine gates, no district, paying Nethergill's rate for a bar they do not own. The machinery lands on them lawfully: a matter, a notice, service on a post. **If Ilyan holds a page, he stops being a carrier, and a carrier is a man in a room with nine sheets who is the reason an appeal four miles away is not stayed.** So the cost of a page for a place is a door closing for everybody else, and the four pounds and six shillings a man had been skimming out of a toll slot went back into the slot in eleven days — which is the only thing in the batch that got fixed, and it got fixed because a man with a cart is a person and a person can be named.

**The review is held and it decides a sum.** The ninth bridge's district road fund is four hundred and ten pounds a year. Two hands under a deck every change is two hundred and ten. The telling is fifteen. The charter is forty-four pounds and sixteen. Four new props for the fourth bridge are ninety-six. **The remainder after all four is forty-four pounds and four shillings, which is fifty-one pounds and sixteen short of the props.** The review buys the props, takes the old four out of the fourth bridge's deck, leaves that deck open in the weather until new timbers arrive, and reduces the ninth bridge's under-deck watch to one hand for the year. **T. Blore, twenty-two, eleven months under a deck, loses the second hand, and is the only person in this case's record who is named as having lost something in it.** Nobody thanked him and he was not thanked.

**Ilyan's record costs him a sixth thing.** He answers a bench of four's question — he did not need to — and enters a figure of thirty years of a gate hand's work into a district's book without her consent, in a load-carrier's figures, and says out loud before he does it that the figure is wrong and that she has refused it. **The figure is a hundred and thirteen thousand four hundred hours at threepence the hour: one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings, six years of the district's road fund and thirty-two years of the cost of the charter.** Bryn Sallow supplies the correction herself, unasked: the working tide of the turn is one day in seven and the week is about sixty-three hours and not seventy-three and a half, which takes out sixteen thousand two hundred hours and two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings. The remaining error is the rate — threepence the hour is not a rate this district has; it is a day's figure of nineteen and a halfpence divided by the six and a half hours the district does not pay for. **A refusal a court cannot compel and cannot overrule, entered on the same page as the figure it refuses.** He is now a witness in one more matter and a witness can be produced.

**The token is reached once and it takes the words.** For fifty minutes the low water, the leg, the chain and the door all clear, and the rendering resolves one step further: the vantage is a room, with a floor, a table, a lamp with the wick down, a window with weather on the wrong side of it, and four people at the table, one of them about forty-four with her back to him. It is a gate hand. He has never seen her at a table. The vantage has not moved. **The cost is that from the second of the month until the sixth he could see a line of writing and could not get sense out of it, and the terms of a charter he had walked eleven miles to carry had to be read aloud by somebody else, and he did not tell Corva Silt why, and she worked it out, and now a second person in this world knows that Ilyan Vester cannot read, and it is not on a page.**

**The continuity line appeared for the seventh time and was refused for the fifth**, in the same words, nobody named, at a review rather than in a room over a pump. The holder still has not read the line under the fifth term of the copy in her own room, and in Chapter 71 she explains why she has not — a charter ends at the fifth term and there is nothing after it — and tells him not to read it to her, and nobody in the case will.

## Key events

- **The first ask (Ch 71).** The thirtieth of the month. A folded paper from the bench of four of **Nethergill** — eleven miles from the ninth bridge, seven from Halloway — arrives on a cart that was not coming for anything, asking for the terms of the Halloway charter to be carried under the second half of the fourth term, *and for the paper itself, and it cannot pay for the paper*. Corva Silt will not certify a bench eleven miles off. The only reader is Wenna Tace, and a holder is not a party. **The four-mile line means that past four miles he is not obliged to go and nobody can make him; he goes anyway, and past the fourth mile it is a favour, and a favour cannot be entered.** He carries the nine sheets and a reader's day, and the leg is his own for eleven miles and stops forty yards past the end of the lane. **The fifth appearance of the continuity line is not a reveal: the holder has not read it, and says why.**
- **The word on a form (Ch 72).** The first. A form headed EXCEPTION, un-ordered, sealed, numbered, summarising a charter of another district, in a bench's drawer. **The finding: the word is not a label on a rule, it is a claim that the rule is temporary, and the charter it summarises has no end on it.** Hal Dreeve, thirty-four, hires out barrows, has put his name down for a season at the weighhouse, and is told he is on an exception. **Parnell Slee, the foreman, is honest about the second reason and says he took a sentence off a shelf in his own yard, and that the second reason is real and would have been given with or without the paper.** Ilyan can follow all of it and cannot do one thing about any of it, and the reason he cannot speak is a rule, and the rule is correct.
- **What they wanted (Ch 73).** Nethergill wanted a person to come and stand in its room, not a charter, and says so. **Ilyan's answer to *who are you* is the worst thing he has said in this case: he is the reason the appeal is not stayed, and that is his whole use, and a favour cannot be entered.** A stranger in the room offers to carry the terms and is refused in person. **The copy Nethergill already had — brought down the lane on a cart by a drover, with no seal and no clerk — is short of the second half of the fourth term, because a clerk does not copy a man's name into a district's paper when a district has not asked for a man. Hester Vole finds it, not him, by laying two sheets on a table.** The district swears its own copy out by candlelight in a kitchen, and the only complete copy of the charter in the world made in a kitchen binds nobody and can be come back for by nobody.
- **A burden in the body (Ch 74).** The second. He takes a barrow-load of a stone off a chapel flat in eleven loads with a woman of fifty-one who did not ask him, and the whole of the job is about two and three-quarter cart-loads, and the fund has elevenpence in it and it is promised to the candles. **The ninth bridge's tenth term says a district which pays a favour has learned to rule by habit, and it applies to him.** His left hand opens in four places and is a scar at the base of his thumb in four years, and there is no line anywhere in this city that pays a man for a hand. **The token, once. The vantage is a room. The token takes the words, and he cannot read from the second to the sixth, and Corva Silt works out why and does not enter it.**
- **The holder is used (Ch 75).** The sixth to the seventh. Five people come to a room over a pump in one day and one of them wants her named in an appeal, and she has to say no. **The finding: a holder is consulted by everybody and named by nobody, and it is one rule doing both things, and it is working.** She makes her own choice, at a cost she names on a page: she asks a bench of four for a rate for a haul, having cut the barrow out of that charter herself, and the rate will be read aloud every quarter by the person who wanted it and cannot be appealed from by anybody. **A second barrow, entered on a page for the first time in her life.**
- **Thirty years, and a rate nobody can set (Ch 76).** The ninth to the fourteenth. Oakes needs a figure to carry a debt. **A hundred and thirteen thousand four hundred hours at threepence the hour is one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings, six years of the road fund, thirty-two years of the charter** — and it is wrong three ways, and Bryn Sallow gives the first correction herself and unasked, and the second correction cannot be found in any district in this city, and the third is that thirty years of three hundred and sixty days is a number he made up. It is entered in three hands in the district's book with the refusal on the same page. **The sixth thing he entered, with its size, and nobody has thanked him and he did not ask.**
- **What it costs to have said no in public (Ch 77).** A clerk of the Orison chamber walks eleven miles and pays two days out of her own. **A bench of three at Fallowditch withdraws its answer of the eighteenth in writing because of a woman called Adela Cray, and the landlord who put the bench's own letter on a table is not a bad man with a bad week.** The standing authority of preservation is asked, for the first time in four years, to *hold a person* rather than to be read, and answers no, with eleven lines of reason, and says again that it can prove a rule was printed and cannot hold a person. **Nahl's own finding, in her own book and in no record: a public answer is a price, and one desk is a monopoly, and the leverage is real and cannot be used, and somebody will try in about a year and there will be a form about it.**
- **Twenty-one names (Ch 78).** The fourteenth. **A builder of thirty-five with lime in his apron takes his own name off a docket board with his thumb, in the open air, and does not want to be heard, because a name on a board is a name a clerk can come back with and he would rather be a man who did a thing than a name in a column.** The board goes to twenty-one. The wall is a hundred and forty-one years old and in a book in a district that says it holds nothing, and there is no clause anywhere that lets a person carry a sworn page four miles to a room. Rell answers his last question — *can I be counted as a place* — in the plainest words she has, and the answer is no.
- **Nine Yards with no name in them (Ch 79).** The fifteenth to the eighteenth. **A man with a cart has been lifting the pennies out of a toll slot into a tin under his seat for ten weeks and has four pounds and six of it, and nobody could do anything, because a bar with nobody at it is not a gate.** The Nine Yards pay Nethergill's rate for a bar they do not own, and a matter arrives because a carter is a person and a place is not. Notice is nailed to a post, lawfully. **And the only candidate to hold a page for a place of sixty-one people is the carrier, and a holder cannot carry, and a carrier who is a holder is a door that closes on four hundred people four miles away.** The four pounds and six goes back into the slot in eleven days, in a cart, with a docket in a clerk's hand.
- **What the review bought (Ch 80).** The twenty-seventh, at the fourth morning bell, at the span, in the open air, not called early, not postponable. The sum. The four new props, the deck open in the weather, the watch at one hand, **T. Blore named in the disposition.** He is produced in person with his report and the two copies of his own sequence and names what the record cost and who paid it, and puts one name in the record that nobody else had put there. **Wen Lask's question is read out and answered, and the answer is true, and the answer is entered as not being the answer, and the road is not touched.** Harrow invents a fourth procedure — how a carrier is sworn in — and says he is less frightened of it than of the duty to answer and that both are true. **The continuity line, the seventh appearance, the fifth refusal.** And on the first of the fourth month a haul rate is read out at a pump by the woman who asked for it and cannot appeal from it, and she tells him that no procedure in this city exists for what happens to a page when the holder cannot do it.

## Panel accounting

**Four System-rendered entries across the batch, one each in Chapters 71, 78, 79, and 80**, at most one in any chapter, and none stating a general rule: the second half of the fourth term as it stands in the record, and what the record does not show; the docket of the First Verdict and what a name on it is; the notice served in a matter in which no respondent is a person; and the disposition of the public maintenance review. **They are set as plain bold lines with no blockquote marker and each is introduced by a narrative line saying the System put it in front of him**, which is the Volume 01 convention and the only thing that lets a reader tell a rendering from a piece of paper. **Every block-quoted block in Chapters 71–80 is a physical object in the fiction**: Nethergill's ask, the carrier's clause, the form-with-a-word record, the painted toll board, the two page entries, Fallowditch's withdrawn answer, Nahl's entry for the two days she paid, the preservation authority's eleven lines, the docket blocks, the matter as set, the figure and its three corrections, the two sets of hours side by side, the sixth thing, the review's fund, the disposition, the carrier procedure, the continuity line, and the haul-rate entry.

## Relationship and power changes

Ilyan's growth is a changed judgment and a body, not a speech. He takes a share of a burden nobody asked him for and pays for it in his hand; he answers a bench's question he did not need to answer and enters a figure about a woman who refused it, with the wrongness stated before the sum; he offers a room over a pump as a solution and is refused by a recordwright who is expressly not his advocate, with the reason, and the reason is that he cannot be a door in a lane and a door at once. **He is not rewarded for carrying a document. He is paid nothing, the walking is a favour past the fourth mile, and the sending costs the district a day of a reader's time and a crooked sheet of paper in a kitchen. His standing has not grown: it is confined to the ninth-bridge matter, he is not a party, and the only thing he can offer a man with a season to lose is a definition and a direction and a woman with a barrow.**

Sera is opposite him twice more and is right both times. She tells him that if he holds a page anywhere he stops being a carrier, that the appeal four miles away is stayed the day he opens one, and that nobody in the city will be able to tell him he did it — and that she did not see it when she wrote the fifth term, and that a man cannot be a door in a lane and the only door at the same time. **Her third licence term is what put the terms in a district eleven miles from a bridge. Her family claim is not touched.** No romance beat occurs and none is planned.

Wenna Tace is the person this batch is about. She becomes a door for five people in a day, she finds the one thing about herself she cannot answer, she asks for the rate she cut out of her own charter, and she pays for it by becoming the author of a rate she must read aloud every quarter and cannot appeal from. **She has named a want on a page for the first time in her life: a second barrow.**

## Deliberately left unresolved

The historical right of refusal, of which one quarter of a sentence on a facing leaf has to be read as a duty and not a place. The authorship question and the Shale Mirror, touched once in fifty minutes and not resolved. **The rendering, the vantage, the room, the door, the hand, and the leg.** The responsible person for the continuity line, seven appearances, five refusals, and the holder has not read the line. The fifth mark in the count book. The Bridgeward, pushed once more at the review, answered once, not resolved, and the road not touched. **Ansa Rell, out-voted and un-resolved and difficult.** **The appeal's survival as a durable right, which this batch advanced by exactly one thing — a procedure for swearing in a carrier that nobody can use — and did not deliver.** The Quiet Chamber fragment. **And the question the batch ends on: there is no procedure in this city for what happens to a page when the holder cannot keep it, and the holder is twenty-nine and hauls two hundred loads a year.**

## Review pass

**An independent reviewer audited Chapters 71–80 against Chapters 51–70, the state files, the volume outline and the batch brief, and returned forty hard findings and nineteen soft ones. All are fixed.** They were: **a full re-cut of Chapter 77, which had restaged Chapter 70's nine-answers device and breached the brief's prohibition on another room that declines to say anything** — it is now two rooms, Fallowditch and the preservation desk, with the other six reported rather than staged, and the chapter is longer and better for it; **the continuity line counted as the fifth appearance when canon makes it the seventh appearance and the fifth refusal**; the eleven-day answers called nine-day; a district money line that matched no line of the fund; **a haul rate that contradicted itself three ways, in which the district's own rate was set as the rate for a haul, which is self-cancelling**; **the keeping of the page priced at a quarter of a pound a quarter in three places against canon's pound eleven**; a day-rate conversion that did not close and a word-count slip of twenty years in a calculation that the whole chapter rests on; a barrow-load total that did not match its rate; a sheet-price figure that did not match nine sheets at threepence; **a Nethergill sitting eleven miles from the ninth bridge described as four**; seven mutually exclusive distances in one batch, now settled at four, seven and eleven; **the nine sheets' provenance untracked across three chapters, which had the district's reader holding a copy before the carrier had arrived, had a clause written to forbid a person carrying the terms breached by a copy sitting in that district, and had the number of complete copies stated two different ways** — the copies are now the holder's, the weighhouse office's, the court's file copy, the carrier's, and the short one that came down the lane on a cart; a stray quotation mark on a System lead-in; a broken sentence in the fund argument; a non sequitur about the office of gate hand; a page's entry count; a cancelled clause about the bench's four seats; two unsupported adoption counts; a man's nineteen years read as nineteen years old; eleven weekday names, all removed; **and thirteen instances of inline bold in running prose and dialogue, which is a third typographic category the convention does not have, all stripped.**

On the soft findings: the "whole of it" construction ran eighteen times across the batch and is now eleven including one in Chapter 70; "a count and not a record" ran three times inside fifteen lines and is now two, one of them a magistrate deliberately saying a keeper's words in her own mouth; the word-count tics and the pause-measure formula were thinned; five trailing morals were cut; **the closing paragraph of Chapter 80 was a copy of Chapter 70's closing roll-call and now ends on a question in a clerk's book instead of an inventory.**

**Word range for the batch: 3,260–6,636.** Chapter 80 is the review and the batch close and is longer than the working shape; it is not padded, and Chapter 68 was longer still in Batch 0002. **Chapter 77 is the shortest at 3,260 and is a complete scene.** **Do not treat any of these figures as a target.**

## Batch ending

A barrow-load of stone comes off a chapel flat four miles up a lane and a man who wrote nine words in a room eleven miles from a bridge has a scar at the base of his thumb and cannot read for four days. A hauler of twenty-nine reads a rate she asked for, two thirds short, at a pump, to about two hundred people, on the first day of a quarter, and cannot appeal from it, and has a second barrow written on a page for the first time in her life. A gate hand's thirty years is in a district's book at one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings with her refusal on the same page and a line saying the figure is wrong by two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings. A man of twenty-two has lost four hours a day and is nineteen miles off and does not know yet. A line came out under the fifth term for the seventh time and was refused for the fifth and nobody was named, and the person it is about has not read it because a charter ends at the fifth term. **And in a clerk's own book in a chamber in Orison there is a question nobody has read: the only district in this city with a carrier is the only district that does not need one.** The appeal is in use and not secured. The Quiet Chamber's quarter sentence and the page over a pump are side by side in a record and are not the same sentence, and nothing in this batch is better than that. The Bridgeward has been asked a question twice and answered once, and the answer is entered as not being the answer.
