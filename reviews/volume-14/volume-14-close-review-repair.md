### 6.1 The three degrees of freedom the method text still did not name, and what each one costs

**A SECOND INDEPENDENT PASS OVER THIS REPAIR HAD NO EXECUTION TOOL AND RE-DERIVED NOTHING. WHAT IT DID WAS READ THE REPAIRED METHOD TEXT AND ASK WHAT IT STILL DID NOT SAY. - **`autojunk` IS A REAL DEGREE OF FREEDOM AND WAS UNNAMED.** `difflib.SequenceMatcher(None, a, b).ratio()` leaves `autojunk` at `True`, which purges from `b` every character occurring more than `len(b)//100 + 1` times, **and only when `len(b)` is 200 or over.** Thirty-three of these fifty closing blocks are 200 characters or longer and the longest is 409, so the purge is **live on the last-block window and idle on the twenty-eight-token window, whose strings never pass 151.** **Setting `autojunk=False` moves thirty-three of the fifty pairs, raises the median from 0.100 to 0.307, and puts a second pair over the 0.45 flag at `677`/`678` 0.451 — while leaving both gate verdicts at 0 and 0. THE CONCLUSION IS ROBUST UNDER IT AND THE DECIMALS ARE NOT.** Named, with its cost, in all four method statements.
- **THE `a`/`b` ORDER IS UNNAMED AND IS NOT COSMETIC EITHER.** The purge is keyed to `b` alone, so the measure is not symmetric: **swapping the two arguments moves forty-eight of the fifty pairs.** Named: `a` is the lower-numbered chapter.
- **THE JOIN IS THE LARGEST UNNAMED DEGREE OF FREEDOM AND IS THE ONE THAT PRODUCED FINDING 4.** Joining the `WORD` tokens by single spaces gives `650`/`651` 0.047 and `699`/`700` 0.426; joining them by nothing gives 0.326 and 0.176. Named, with both figures, in all four method statements.
