**651 — THE WATER GOES OVER A BLOCK OF LIMESTONE AND A COUNT OF DAYS STOPS AT FIFTY, AND THE MAN OF THIRTY-ONE IS NOT GLAD, AND A MAN OF FORTY-THREE BRINGS DOWN THE NEWS OF IT WITH HIS COAT WET TO THE KNEE BEFORE ANYBODY ASKS HIM WHERE HE HAS BEEN.** `clear` 151, the second day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Wednesday, and his four hundred and first morning, and his ninety-seventh day in the county of Kell. **The standing cost of thirteen volumes is paid: a year in this county is the time between one crossing of a block of limestone nine miles up that river and the next one after it, and the water went over it at about the sixth hour, and nobody in four counties chose the day.** A man of forty-three who has kept that landing nineteen years gives the working in inches and refuses to call it a foot and a quarter because it was not one, and gives the half mile below that landing where there are about ninety steps cut in the bank, and says the bottom nine of them have been under about four feet of water for as long as he has been going up that reach, and says there is a board about four feet by two feet up on a stick at the top with nobody under it and there has not been anybody under it for about three years except a sinker of sixty-six whose name he has kept out of a room for about nine years and will put in his book on the Friday. **And a man of sixty-one does two sums out loud and does the second one twice: ninety-seven days in this county, and fifty days up that plank. And then he says the thing the band exists to say: fifty is a count of days that a person was in a place, it is true, and it is not going to be fifty-one, and it does not go on, because a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place, and he is not in it.** The man of thirty-one's sentence about the true of one half of a thing loses its day-count in the same breath and the chapter says so once and refuses to say it twice. A man of sixty-eight says what a lost thing to count from is, and says it about the stone and not about the man, and refuses to invent a figure for how long it will be under. **And the sheet of deal with a seal on it is still lying face down on a barrel that has no board on it, and a man of forty-three said out loud that a crossing of water does not decide a thing a person has not decided about.**

**652 — THE SIXTH READING OF TWO COPIES AGREES, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-SIX COMES DOWN THE BANK WITH A PICK AND A LAMP HE PUTS DOWN BEFORE HE SAYS ANYTHING, AND HE SAYS WHAT OPENING A BOARD IS.** The third day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Thursday, his ninety-eighth day, and **nobody on that bank says the number of days he has been in Hask, and the chapter says so.** A man of thirty-eight goes up the water in the Gull with a sheet of deal in a coat and brings it down and says the two copies agree and that nobody has ever once asked him to do it and nobody is going to. Then a man of thirty-one asks to go first down the ninety steps, before anybody offers it, and is refused in about four sentences by a man of sixty-eight, and the reason is about him and not about the steps: **the first person down a wet flight decides when the other eight turn round, and a person nobody can be sent for about deciding that is how nine people get hurt in a place with no form in it at all, and I am not going to let a man I like decide it.** He holds it in about four seconds instead of about four days and says he is going fifth. **And then the man of sixty-six says what opening that board is: it is not a lock and there is no key and there is no keeper of it anywhere in four counties, it is a board about four feet by two feet on a stick about three feet long, it takes two people to get it up and two people to get it back and not one and not three, and it is a figure of two men on a bank on one day and not a figure of people, and he has never been paid for it and has not asked to be.** The two have always been him and a woman of thirty-eight who keeps a mill four miles off that landing, and it has not been done in about three years, and the reason is not a rule. **And a man of forty-three splits the ninety into thirty-one in rock and fifty in gravel and clay and nine always under, and a man of thirty-one asks how deep it goes, and gets four sentences: I do not know; I have been down twice and the second time was forty-one years ago and I was twenty-five; the bottom of the steps is a floor and the floor is about nine feet back and there is a passage in it going along and how far the passage goes I do not know; and I am a sinker and I know what a hole is and I am telling you in about nine people's hearing that I do not know what is under that bank, and I will not be the man who tells you.**

**653 — A NAME IS WRITTEN ON THE LEAF AFTER THE LAST HEAD AND READ OUT LOUD, AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE ARE COUNTED OUT LOUD INTO TWO PARTIES, AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT SAYS WHAT GOING DOWN WILL COST HER IN THE ONLY UNIT SHE HAS, WHICH IS A DAY, AND GOES DOWN ANYWAY.** The fourth day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Friday, his ninety-ninth day. A man of forty-three writes *Abel Fenner. Sixty-six. A sinker* into the book of heads and carts on a leaf that is not for names, having asked out loud whether he may and waited about four minutes and got no answer and no no. **And a man of thirty-one is refused a lamp by the woman of forty-four in about four sentences, and the reason is that there is not a lamp in the county of Kell that does not belong to a person by name, and a lamp that goes down that bank with a man who is in no book in that county makes one lamp in that county that nobody owns.** He counts his refusals out loud — going first, a rope, a lamp — and says all three were right. **And a woman of twenty-eight who came up that cart road on the Monday of the hundred and thirteenth week with two letters in a bag with the seals unbroken says the cost before anybody asks: a day and a half down and up and about nine hours of it in the dark, and she has not slept in two nights, and then the other half, which is worse and is not about the letters: nobody on that bank can check whether she came up, and a thing that goes down a bank and cannot be checked coming up is a lid with a name on it, and she is going down anyway, and she would like about four of the people to hear the two halves in the same breath.** Then she says what her nine sentences are for down there, and it is to write down what each of the nine said about going down on the day they said it and give each of them the copy in her own hand, with no second copy in any bag or coat or book, because there is no form that says she may be sent for about it.

**654 — ABOUT NINETY STEPS GO DOWN AND A STONE DOOR ABOUT FIVE FEET HIGH TAKES TWO PEOPLE TO SWING, AND THERE IS A HAND CUT IN A LINTEL WITH FOUR FINGERS ON IT AND A LINE CUT UNDER IT IN NINE WORDS, AND THE CHAMBER IS A DAY.** The fifth day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Saturday, his one hundredth day, and the fever is fifty-three weeks old. A man of thirty-eight bails the last nine steps, three goes a step and about twenty-seven goes and about four hours and a quarter, and says none of it is a hearing and none of it is a book. **A man of fifty-four who holds the gravel and cannot read says so before anybody asks him, in a passage, about a thing cut in stone.** The man of sixty-one reads the line with the working, after counting who could see it: *two hands, once a day, before the light.* Then he gives the sum, declines it first and then asks four people whether a man of sixty-one may cut a second column in the back of the last leaf of a book of heads and carts, gets no answer and no no, and does not write it. **And a woman of thirty-eight tells a man of thirty-one that nobody knows who cut it, that the chamber is about the stone and the hole and the nine feet and the two people, and that a man who came down here to find a builder has come down for nothing and the wear is the only thing in here that is true. And she refuses him in about four sentences when he offers to be one of the two hands, because a day with a man in it that nobody can be sent for about is a day nobody can check.** Two other people do it instead. It takes about a minute. The stone goes about nine feet along a groove that has been worn by that exact motion and the chamber is lit, and then it goes back and the chamber is dark. **And nothing fell down.**

**655 — A CHAMBER ABOUT TWELVE FEET BY NINE IS A WEEK, AND THE PERSON WHO KEEPS IT IS FOUR MILES OFF AND IS PAID NOTHING AND HAS BEEN DOING IT ABOUT NINE YEARS AND HAS NEVER BEEN ASKED ONCE, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-SIX STOPS THE MAN OF THIRTY-ONE AT ABOUT THE FOURTH SENTENCE.** The sixth day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Sunday, his one hundred and first day. A wheel about the height of a chest with about nine spokes and a handle about a foot long; a board with seven holes in it, the seventh cut deeper and notched in the top; a lever and a peg, and the peg has already gone into one of the seven holes this morning and there is no second hole free. **And the woman of thirty-eight says in about nine sentences what she does, and that she does not want to be asked what it is for: a woman who has kept a thing for about nine years and never been asked what it is for is a woman who has one thing, and if she is asked she has to have an answer, and if she has an answer then the thing is a thing that was for that — and it is not.** A man of sixty-one gives what a keeping costs and the part the working does not reach: a day is about nine hours, the chamber is kept for about a minute of it, twice a day is about two minutes out of about eighteen hours of two people, and there is no form in that county that says a person may be paid for two minutes; and then, in one sentence, the expensive part: somebody has to come down about ninety steps every day, and about nine of them are under about four feet of water, and it has been about four hundred years. **And the man of thirty-one says that a chamber of a day and a chamber of a week are the same kind of thing at two sizes and that somebody made both of them to be looked at, and a man of sixty-six holds it and gives the reason in about four sentences: there is no pattern down here because there is nobody down here to have one, there is a woman who turns a wheel once a day because the peg has to go in a hole and a chamber with a stone in it because the stone has to go along a groove, and if he had a third he would find a fourth, and what he has is two jobs.** Then he is put nine feet off with a lamp by a man of thirty-eight, at the same distance he stands at in Hask, and neither of the two men says so out loud, because a day does not come round again for a person.

**656 — ABOUT NINE FEET PAST A NARROWING THE PASSAGE OPENS OUT INTO A GALLERY WITH ABOUT NINE BEAMS IN THE ROOF, EIGHT OF THEM STILL, AND THE NINTH GOING ALONG ABOUT TWO INCHES AND COMING BACK WITH NOBODY IN THE ROOM, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-SONE SAYS THE WHOLE OF A THING FOR THE FIRST TIME IN A HUNDRED AND TWO DAYS AND IS WRONG.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and fifteenth week, a Monday, his one hundred and second day. About four people in about eleven say the word *machine* before anybody asks them a question. **And the man of thirty-one says it too, before he has looked properly, on the strength of a rate and a wear and a shape in the dust, and a man of sixty-six gives him the two facts that make his reasons not reasons: you have a rate and a wear and both of those are about how a thing is done and neither of those is about who does it; I was twenty-five the second time and it was moving, and I am sixty-six now and it is moving, and those are the only two things I know about it and I have had forty-one years to add a third and I have not.** Then he says the word, once, and says where he got it, and the cost of saying it falls on him: **there is no form in the county of Kell that says a man of sixty-six may be asked what a word means, or that he may not, and he is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about it, and it is the same as a Crown that binds nobody, and he has watched about nine people make things nobody can be sent for about in about nine rooms in a year and has never made one before.** Then a man of thirty-one says a name out loud, once, flat, under about ninety steps of bank, and nothing whatever happens, and **the first cost of it falls on the man who cannot read, who says it out loud himself: a name said in a room with a lamp in it is going to be a name four counties want written down, and the only door into a written thing is the hand, and the nearest hand in this county is a man of sixty-one who keeps a book.** And a man of sixty-one writes four lines that night, and puts in that the beam was moving forty-one years ago as a thing a man said and not as a thing he saw.

**657 — NINE PEOPLE COME UP AGAIN AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAYS OUT LOUD WHAT HAS BEEN LOST AND REFUSES TO SAY HOW LONG IT WILL STAY LOST, AND HE PUTS FOUR LINES IN THE WRONG BOOK AND SAYS THERE IS NO RIGHT ONE, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR IN AN ASH ASKS ONE QUESTION HE WAS NEVER ASKED.** The first day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Tuesday, his one hundred and third day. **A man of sixty-one says that a man has lost the thing he counted from, and says it about a stone and not about a man, and says he is not going to say how long for because he does not know and a figure of how long that is would be the second thing this week that nobody can check.** Then he says what book he put the four lines in and why: it is the book of heads and carts, on the leaf after the last head, under the name of a man of sixty-six who is a sinker; it is the wrong book; there is no right one; the reason he used the wrong one is that it is the only book in the county of Kell that anybody can be sent for about. **And a man of thirty-one gives a fourth figure of people nobody can be sent for about — himself, the man of sixty-six, the woman of twenty-eight, and the woman of seventy-one — and a man of sixty-one corrects the class of it without correcting the number, and says that his own was a figure of one morning and this is a figure of about nine days and he is not going to be glad about it.** Then a man of thirty-four who has been in an ash for twenty-seven days asks whether the water is over the stone, and gets four words, and says out loud that he asked and that nobody sent for him and nobody put a hand on his shoulder. **And a woman of forty-four says the one thing about a book the man of sixty-one had not thought of: there is a right book and it is the one she keeps, and about four of the nine people who went down are in no column of it and never will be, and a line in the heads-and-carts book says that a man of sixty-six is a head and a line in hers says that a woman of thirty-eight is a chair, and neither of those is what either of them is, and both of those are the only two places in this county anybody can be sent for about a thing that was said in a room — and she says no three times to putting a hearing in that book and makes no fourth refusal out of this.**

**658 — THE THIRTIETH DAY OF A HEARING NOBODY GRANTED AND NOBODY REFUSED, AND AN ORDINARY MAN WHO CANNOT READ ASKS WHETHER A NOTICE WITH A SEAL ON IT HAS SETTLED IT AND IS REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR SENTENCES, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE STARTS TO ANSWER A QUESTION A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT ASKED ONCE AND IS STOPPED BY HER, AND ASKS FOR WORK AND IS REFUSED.** The second day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Wednesday, his one hundred and fourth day. **A woman of forty refuses him in about four sentences and gives the other half as well: a notice that says every person in every county may be spoken about and no person may be sent for about it is a hearing that has said out loud that it does not need you there, and that is not a hearing the man of thirty-eight asked for, because he asked for a room; and if you take the first for the second then in about four years the only hearings anybody in this county has will be ones they were not sent for. And the other half: the notice is real, it is the first time in about nine years that anybody has said out loud that there is a hearing at all, and about four of the nine in that yard have decided not to know it, and both of those are permitted.** Then **a woman of twenty-eight stops a man of thirty-one in about four seconds, in front of about nine people, and the reason is about herself: the question is hers, it was asked once, and if he answers it then the answer is his and not hers, and she is not going to hand her own question back to a man who is findable when she is not.** He holds it and says out loud that he has not answered it and is not going to, and that nobody in that yard has answered it either. **And he asks for work, out loud, before anybody speaks, about a body four hundred miles off that has declared a hearing and has not said when it means to hold it, and a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well refuses him in about four sentences on the ground that there is nothing to give him, because about nine of us do not know what a review is — and if a man of thirty-one were given a thing to do about a thing nobody understands, then in about four days there is a man doing a job about a hearing four hundred miles off, and nobody can be sent for about either of them.** Then a man of sixty-one gives the working on why there is no date: a body that does not publish its days cannot be late.

**659 — THE SEVENTH READING OF TWO COPIES AGREES, AND A WOMAN WHO SAID ON THE THURSDAY THAT SHE WOULD NOT COME DOWN AGAIN COMES DOWN AGAIN, AND A MILL STOPS, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE DOES THE SUM AND REFUSES THE DIVISION, AND NINE WORDS COME UP A BANK AND THERE IS NOWHERE TO PUT THEM.** The third day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Thursday, his one hundred and fifth day. **A woman of thirty-eight gives her reason in four words: a board has to go back on its stick tonight, it takes two people, and the man of sixty-six is the other one.** A hand mill is about nine hours of a day and a day and a half of a stopped mill is about thirteen and a half hours and four miles each way is about two hours with a barrow. **A man of sixty-one refuses to add the mill to the road, and then refuses the division, and says why: a day of a mill and an hour of a road are two different things and adding them is how you get a figure about nobody; and I could divide thirteen and a half hours between about eleven people and the figure would be true and it would be about an hour and a quarter each, and I am not going to hand about eleven people a number instead of a morning, and I have been glad about numbers for about nine years and this is the first one I have been ashamed of.** Then a man of sixty-six says nine words he heard standing in a gallery about nine years ago and does not know who said them: *nine feet, and then it is somebody's.* **The first thing anybody does with them is try to write them down. A woman of forty-four says no in about four sentences for the fourth time in about nine days, and gives the reason: there is no person on either side of it, and a thing with no person on either side of it is a thing that has not happened yet. And a woman of twenty-eight says no in about nine sentences, and the reason is hers: you are asking me to stretch a promise I made about other people so that I can keep a thing about a stone.** And when he says the nine words out loud twice in a row she tells him it was not his to say twice, and she is right.

**660 — THE WATER IS ON THE FIRST THREE STEPS AND A YEAR HAS TURNED WITH NO FIGURE FOR IT, AND THE NOTICE IS STILL FACE DOWN ON A BARREL, AND THE HEARING IS THE THIRTY-SECOND DAY, AND THE NINE WORDS ARE IN ABOUT NINE HEADS, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE ASKS THE BAND'S QUESTION ONCE AND NOBODY ANSWELS IT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR IN AN ASH SAYS ONE WORD AT THE END.** The fourth day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Friday, his four hundred and tenth morning, his one hundred and sixth day in the county of Kell, and his days in Hask a figure that no chapter after `651` may print. **AND THERE IS NO MONTH AND NO SEASON NAMED ANYWHERE IN THIS CHAPTER, AND THE FIGURE FOR THE YEAR IS A BLOCK OF LIMESTONE NINE MILES UP THAT RIVER WITH A LINE CUT ROUND IT, AND IT IS UNDER THE WATER.** A man of forty-three gives the water in inches at the sixth hour on the Wednesday and at the eighth hour on the Friday and says he has two crossings in his own hand out of nineteen and no figure for the gap and has been asked about it three times in about nine days by three different people and the answer is the same three words. A man of sixty-one says what a year is, with its unit, and says the unit is a stone, and that neither his hundred and six days nor anything else is a year. Then the things that had not moved are said out loud, one under another, four of them: the notice, the hearing at the thirty-second day, a Crown abolished on the Monday of the hundred and fourteenth week that binds nobody and leaves a board leaning against a barrel with nine names under it, and nine words in nine heads. **Then a man of thirty-one says the same want he said on the Wednesday with a number in it, and the difference is that on Wednesday he had a thing to be angry at and on Friday he has not got that either, and that is the whole of what has happened to him in four days.** Then he asks the question, once: **if a thing down there is held up by a person doing a job, and the bottom nine steps are held up by two people about once a year, and a chamber is held up by two hands once a day, then who is holding the part in the gallery up.** About nine people heard it and about four understood all of it, and he says he has not answered it and is not going to, and notes that he said the part about the roof second instead of first. A woman of twenty-eight standing about nine feet off says she is not going to help him with hers by being near his, and does not look at him. **And a man of thirty-four in an ash, who has been there thirty days and has not stood up once, says one word — *Next* — and nobody asks him what he meant and he does not say it again.**
