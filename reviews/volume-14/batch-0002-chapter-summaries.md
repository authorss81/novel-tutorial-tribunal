**661 — A LIST OF NINE JOBS IS READ OUT LOUD BY A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WHO SAYS HE CAN DO NONE OF THEM, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-SIX IS ASKED TO GO DOWN A THIRD TIME AND SAYS NEITHER YES NOR NO.** The fifth day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Saturday, his four hundred and eleventh morning, his one hundred and seventh day in the county of Kell, and the fever fifty-four weeks old. A man of forty-three gives the water in inches, thirty-three on the Friday and thirty-nine this morning, and the difference is six inches, which is two hand's breadths, and he says he has no figure for what it is doing. **Then the man of thirty-one says the finding out loud as a question of work and not as a question of belief, and reads nine jobs, and the ninth of the nine is two people who have not been asked anything and the eighth is a job that cannot be filled because there is no form in that county that says a person may be sent for, and he stops for about as long as it takes to pole a quarter mile before he reads that one.** A woman of forty-four says a list is a column and that a column in this county is a thing people get put in inside about four years, and says she has said no three times to putting a hearing in her book and is not going to make a fourth refusal out of a list. A man of sixty-one says the list is worth what a thing read aloud once is worth, and that it is gone by the time the light goes, and that he is glad about that. **A man of sixty-six comes down the bank at about the ninth hour and is asked to go down a third time, and says no, and takes the no back in the same breath, and says neither yes nor no, and gives the reason: he is the only person in four counties anybody can be sent for about a word he said in a gallery, and a man who says yes to a third descent is a man who has been asked twice and has said yes twice, and that is how a person becomes a thing that is sent for.** A woman of forty-four says out loud that a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything and that it is not a discovery. A woman of twenty-eight says he has said a whole thing twice and the first time it was true and the second time it was his, and she is standing about nine feet off and she did not look at him. **And a man of thirty-four in an ash had not said a word on the Saturday when about nine people walked past his mouth, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that a man who has said one word and then stopped is a man who has finished and that he is not going to make him into a reason for a list of nine.**

**662 — WHAT A COALITION IS AND WHAT IT COSTS IS SAID OUT LOUD ON A BANK, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE OFFERS TO BE THE THING THAT IS SENT FOR AND IS REFUSED AGAIN.** The sixth day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Sunday, his four hundred and twelfth morning, his one hundred and eighth day. About eleven people are on the bank and it is two more than were there on the Saturday, and the man of sixty-one says the number out loud before anybody asks and says it is a figure of a Sunday morning. **The man of thirty-one says he counted the times he has said the true of one half of a thing until the twenty-third and is not going to count them any more, and gives the reason: a count of times is not a count of days, and a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place, and the two sat in one sentence for a long time and one of them has stopped.** A coalition is said to be nine people who have said in front of each other that they will do a thing on a day when it can be done, with no seal, no room and no list, and the man of sixty-one applies a man of sixty-eight's sentence about a Crown that binds nobody to it and says he is not going to stand in a room and say nine is different from one. Its cost is a day and not money, and the working is about four hours and a quarter of bailing, about nine hours of a boat, about four hours of a road, and about two minutes of a chamber twice a day. **And he refuses to say how many of them there are, in about four sentences, and the reason is that a number on a coalition makes the number the thing and the people the figure, and in about four years there will be a form with a number in it and a name at the top of it and the five people standing there become the first line of a number and nobody ever gets to be the first line of a number again.** A woman of twenty-eight is told the cost and says if she is one of nine in a county where nobody can be sent for about then a room four hundred miles off can say out loud that she was one of nine, and says she is not going to be one of the nine. A woman of forty refuses the cost in about four things for a reason about nine people and not about the man of thirty-one. **And the man of thirty-one offers to be the thing that is sent for, in front of about eleven people, and a man of about fifty-two who cannot see well refuses him in about eight sentences and says he is not going to give a new reason: a thing that is sent for is a thing people are sent for about, and in about four years there will be a form with a name at the top of it and it will be a man of thirty-one who asked for it out loud in front of witnesses, which is the worst way of all of them to be one. And he holds it in about four seconds and says he is not going to say how many times he has held one, because the man of sixty-one gave up a number this morning.**

**663 — THE WORK OF GETTING NINE PEOPLE TO A BANK AND BACK IS DONE BY PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT FAMOUS FOR IT, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT IS ASKED WHAT SHE THINKS SHE IS KEEPING AND GIVES AN ANSWER WORSE FOR EVERYBODY THAN THE NOT-KNOWING WAS.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and sixteenth week, a Monday, his four hundred and thirteenth morning, his one hundred and ninth day. The water is off the steps, and a man of forty-three gives the working and corrects the band's own standing saying out loud: the water in those steps comes out of a drain and not off the top of a river, and the bank empties in about three days, counted from one morning to the next and not from a morning to itself, and the bank has a person in it and the stone has nobody in it at all. **A man of forty-one who keeps two carts says what a road is after abolition: a road is four miles and a barrow and whoever is going that way, and the only thing the Monday of the hundred and fourteenth week changed is that now nobody in four counties can be sent for about whether they go on it, and a road is a figure of carts and not of a rule.** A boy of thirteen is counted out loud as one of the two who go and fetch, because the penny a head is on everybody over sixteen, and says he has walked four miles with a barrow since he was nine and that nobody has ever asked him and nobody is going to. A man of sixty-six says what a wet flight is for the third time anybody asked him, and the third time has a new thing in it: a passage is somewhere a person can be, a wet flight is somewhere a person goes through, and there is nowhere on it to stand, and nobody knows what the other seven of the nine steps under the water are for, and he says out loud that what is past the narrowing is not one of the seven and that if he is wrong about that on the Tuesday then he will be the one who is wrong and not the water, and he says that the four things he gives everybody are the four he gave in a gallery on the Monday of that same week and are not the four about a floor he gave on the Thursday, and the count of four is a count of four things he says and not of sentences. A woman of forty-nine comes down that road a second time and says for the third time that she does not know what a copy is and compares a written thing to a fence post. **And a man of sixty-one asks the woman of thirty-eight what she thinks she is keeping, having said out loud that she had said she did not want to be asked, and asks her in front of about nine people instead of in a doorway because a no and not knowing are two different things and he cannot tell them apart from outside. And she answers in about eleven sentences: she is not keeping a week, she is keeping the fact that the peg has a hole for it, and if she is keeping the difference between a day and a week then the difference is kept by her and it is not in a book and not in the stone and not in a hand anybody can be sent for about, and in about four years if she stops the peg goes into a plain hole on the seventh morning and a chamber that is a week is a chamber with a wheel in it, and the not-knowing had a wheel in it and the knowing has her in it.** A man of sixty-one then refuses to cut a fifth line in the back of the last leaf, and says where the fifth line is going to sit instead, and that in about four years one of them will have to say out loud what that cost. The man in the ash is at thirty-three days and the woman in the reed at thirty-four, both spoken out of `630` and `629`, and the man of sixty-one says he is not going to add them together.

**664 — THE BOARD IS LIFTED BY TWO PEOPLE NOBODY COULD NAME, THE FINDER IS NOT THE ONE WHO GOES, AND THE BOTTOM NINE STEPS ARE UNDER ABOUT FOUR FEET OF WATER AND ARE BAILED AGAIN BY A MAN WHO IS NOT THANKED FOR IT.** The first day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and fourteenth morning, his one hundred and tenth day. There is no form in that county that says who lifts a board about four feet by two feet, and a man of sixty-one says he is going to do nothing about it and that he is going to stand on the bank at the eighth hour. **A boy of thirteen goes up the track at about the fifth hour and comes back at about the seventh with two people, after being four miles and back once already, and says nobody sent him and that he is not going down there and nobody asked him to.** A man of thirty-eight goes down first with a lamp that is his and gives the working for the bailing: nine steps, about four feet, three goes a step, about twenty-seven goes, about nine minutes and a half a go, about four hours and a quarter, and says he is not going to be thanked for it again. A man of forty-one says he will do the bailing because it is the same work as a wet load and he has been doing it about nineteen years, and says if he is slow at the end he is to be told and not waited for. **A man of sixty-six goes past a narrowing he has not been in for forty-one years, and stands in the water on the other side of it for about as long as it takes to pole a quarter mile, and nobody asks him anything at all, and he says the reason he came was a narrowing and not a question.** In the gallery about nine people watch a beam go along about two inches and come back and about four of them say the word out loud before anybody asks them anything, and nobody changed their mind and nobody told them to. The day is done in the chamber of the day by two people and put back, and the wheel in the chamber that is a week is not turned. **A man of fifty-four who cannot read leads the man of thirty-one out by a hand on his coat in the dark when the lamp gutters, and says he has said so five times in about nine days and that a coat is a thing a person can be sent for about.** They come up, the man of forty-one last, and before anybody gets near him he says he had been slow at the end and that somebody should have told him to hurry and that nobody had.

**665 — THE SECOND DESCENT GOES ABOUT NINE FEET PAST THE LAST BEAM AND FINDS A PLACE WHERE THE WATER STOPS, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE TRIES THREE NAMES FOR IT AND IS STOPPED THREE TIMES.** The second day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and fifteenth morning, his one hundred and eleventh day. The man of forty-one bails for about two hours instead of about four hours and a quarter, and says out loud that about two hours is enough for nine people to stand in and is not a figure of those nine steps. **The nine feet past the last beam is given three ways and all three are true: about four and a half paces of a man of thirty-eight at about two feet a pace, about nine feet by a rope that is the man of thirty-eight's and that he did not lend to anybody, and nine hands along the wall by a man of fifty-four who cannot read and who says a hand of a man of fifty-four is not the same as a pace of a man of thirty-eight.** Then the water stops. There is a line about nine inches up the wall that goes back all the way to the bottom of the ninety steps, level, and below the line the wall is dry and the gravel is dry and about a foot deep and it is walked, not trodden, and there is no groove in the gravel and not one in the stone under it, and a person cannot walk a floor that many times without putting a groove in it. **A man of thirty-one says the true of one half of a thing and then tries to say the whole of it, three times, and is stopped three times: it is not a chamber because a chamber is a job and there is no job in it, it is not a gallery because a gallery has beams in the roof of it and there are none, and it is not a room, because a man of fifty-four who cannot read and who has not been asked anything says that three names in three sentences in a passage with a lamp in it are in about nine mouths on the bank by morning and that he is the one they will come to, because the only door into a written thing is the hand, and a man who cannot read has just kept a place out of a list.** The man of thirty-one says he is not going to say a fourth, that the three are on a bank by morning, and that he cannot get any of them back and that it is the first thing he has said out loud in about nine days that he cannot take. **A man of sixty-six is asked what the water stopping means and gives the same four things for the second time, and the count of four is a count of four things he says and not of sentences, the first having been given in a gallery on the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and he is asked twice in the same morning and answers the same way both times, and gives the same four things for a line on a wall, and says a man of sixty-six does not get better at answering a thing by being asked it in front of more people.** A man of sixty-one gives the thirty-seventh day of the hearing out of `628` and says he is not going to add anything to that number, because a count of days is only a count of days while a person is in the place and the place for that count is a room and there is no room.

**666 — A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS NO OUT LOUD, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, TO A REQUEST THAT WAS MADE PLAINLY AND FAIRLY BY A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE WHO SAID THREE THINGS BEFORE HE MADE IT, AND NOBODY ON THAT BANK ARGUES HER OUT OF IT.** The third day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Thursday, his four hundred and sixteenth morning, his one hundred and twelfth day. The man of sixty-one says he is asking and not telling, and says out loud that he is saying so before the question so that nobody in that bank can say afterwards that he did not, and nobody on that bank said the request was not reasonable, because it was reasonable, and that is the whole of the difficulty. **She says no, in nine sentences that she counts herself and out loud, and the reason is about the coalition and not about her: a coalition of nine with no seal and no list does not bind anybody, and after about four years there is one person in it that everything goes through, not because anybody decided that and because there is no column and the only place a thing can be kept is a person, and then anybody who wants to know what the nine agreed has to be able to reach that person, and the nine does not stop existing and it stops being nine.** A man of thirty-one opens his mouth to argue and does not, and says out loud that the reason is not that she is right, because he cannot find that out from where he is standing, and that the reason is that a thing that is sent for is a thing people are sent for about and she is not the instrument of anything. **A man of sixty-one says it is eight, and that he is not going to go and find a ninth, and that if he does he is doing the thing she has just described and on purpose, and he includes himself in the bar, because he is the one with the book and he is the one it would go to.** About four people on that bank say the word and the man of thirty-one says out loud that nobody on that bank is going to say it, and that he has not said it either and would have said it on the Friday of last week, and that nobody is going to thank her for saying no, and that he is not going to put this one in the count of refusals because he does not know what to do with it instead. The woman of thirty-eight is asked to go down for the fourth time and says yes in about four seconds and a man of thirty-one says he noticed the four seconds and is not going to make anything of them. **The man of thirty-one says out loud that the eighth of the nine jobs is a job that cannot be filled, that he is not going to offer it again, and that a person who cannot be sent for about is not a spare pair of hands. And eight people went down instead of nine.**

**667 — A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE DOES A SUM HE REFUSED TO DO ON THE THURSDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND SIXTEENTH WEEK AND DOES NOT REFUSE IT THIS TIME, AND THE REASON IS WORSE, AND THE FIGURE THAT COMES OUT OF IT IS A FIGURE OF NOBODY.** The fourth day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Friday, his four hundred and seventeenth morning, his one hundred and thirteenth day. **A man of forty-one says what it has cost him out loud before anybody asked: a load of stone is not going up that track on the Monday, and a Monday that somebody else does not get is the whole of it, and he does not know whose Monday, and there is no form in that county that says a man may be sent for about a Monday.** The man of sixty-one gives the sum with the working: a hand mill is about nine hours of a day, a day and a half stopped is about thirteen and a half hours, a day is about nine hours, and that mill has stopped four times that anybody counted, and thirteen and a half and thirteen and a half and nine and nine is forty-five hours, and he is not going back to count the three descents before the Thursday of the hundred and sixteenth week because he does not have the figures. **And he does the division he refused nine days earlier and says why before he gives the number: he is not taking his refusal back, and a room with a true number in it that refuses to say the number does not get a made-up one. Forty-five hours between about eleven people is about four hours each, four hours of eleven of them is forty-four, and forty-five is an hour over that, and an hour over is the same kind of thing as a quarter of an hour under.** And he says the whole of what it is a figure of: forty-five hours is a figure of a mill, and it is not a figure of the woman of thirty-eight, who came and decided to come, and it is not a figure of her mother, who is one of the eleven and was not asked and does not know and cannot be sent for about it, and there is no line in that book that will take it, and it is the first number he has had in eleven years he could not put anywhere. **A boy of thirteen does a sum of his own out loud, unasked: four miles is about two hours, two hours is eight hours in four days, eight hours is a working day, and a working day of a boy of thirteen is a figure of nobody, and he does not know what to do with it, and that is the same as the man of thirty-one and he is not going to pretend he is better.** A man of thirty-one asks for the division to be refused so that no true number about eleven people exists, and says he has watched a man say a number in a room of about nine people for about nine days and that two of the nine are in an ash and in a reed and neither knows it, and a man of sixty-one tells him that if it were about the coalition then nine people would owe a day and nobody would owe a name, and that he is the one person in that bank who cannot be given a day at all. The thirty-ninth day of the hearing is given, and a man of sixty-one says that on Monday it will not be in the thirties and that he is not going to stand on a bank on Monday and give a new figure, and gives the reason.

**668 — THE THING THAT IS TURNING IS PUT TO ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AS A QUESTION, AND THE MAN WHO HAS BEEN DOWN TWICE SAYS THE SAME FOUR THINGS HE HAS SAID SINCE THE MONDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND FIFTEENTH WEEK, AND A NAME IS SAID OUT LOUD FLAT ONCE BY A WOMAN OF FORTY.** The fifth day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Saturday, his four hundred and eighteenth morning, his one hundred and fourteenth day, and the fever fifty-five weeks old. The bank was full on the Friday and nobody went down, and a man of forty-three says that is a day gone and is not going to tell the bank it is the only one, because a man of forty-one bailed on the Tuesday and again on the Wednesday and again on the Thursday, and the Friday is a day nobody bailed at all because the water was not there to be bailed. **What turns a beam about nine feet long is put to about nine people as a question, and about four of them say the word out loud within about nine seconds of each other, as they said it on the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week and on the Tuesday, and nothing anybody has said in three days has changed it; about nine are not sure of anything, and one of the nine says out loud that not being sure of anything else is not the same as not being sure it is a machine.** The man who has been down that gallery twice is asked and gives the same four sentences, and says he is saying them a second time because a question was asked of him and a man of sixty-six does not get better at answering a thing by being asked it in front of more people. **Nobody in that bank accepted the four things and nobody rejected them, and a man of thirty-one said the whole of that out loud so that it would be in the record of the morning, and said he is not going to supply a fifth and that he has had five sentences in him since the Monday of the hundred and fifteenth week and has not said one.** Then a man of thirty-one asks the room what to call nine people who have agreed to do a thing, and says first that it is the wrong question and that he has asked it because he does not know a better one. **A woman of forty says a name out loud, flat, once, and says she is the one a room looks at after a no and that she would rather hand them a name that is about somebody else than let them make one up. Nothing whatever happened. A woman of twenty-eight says out loud that she has been listening since the Thursday for somebody to say that the two names were the same thing, that nobody had, and that she is not going to be the one who says it either, and that it is not because it is not true.** And a man of thirty-one says the cost of it out loud, in the first sentence: it did not go to a man of fifty-four and it did not go to him, and it went to the one person in that bank who has spent nine weeks asking for a name to be used against her, and that is the most exact thing that has happened in that county in about nine days and it happened by accident. **And a man of sixty-one says the coalition is called the nine, and that the nine changes every time somebody says no, and that it is not a name anybody can be sent for about, and that he is not going to write it down.**

**669 — THE QUESTION IS PUT OUT LOUD WHETHER ABOUT NINE PEOPLE CAN HOLD OPEN A THING THAT ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED YEARS OF ONE-PERSON JOBS HAVE BEEN HOLDING, AND THE WORKING IS GIVEN, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM LIKES THE ANSWER.** The sixth day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Sunday, his four hundred and nineteenth morning, his one hundred and fifteenth day. The man of thirty-one says in the first sentence that he is asking about a door and not about a beam. **The man of sixty-one gives the working out loud and mislasts it out loud and corrects it out loud: a day is about nine hours, about four hundred years is about a hundred and forty-six thousand days, nine hours of a day for a hundred and forty-six thousand days is one million three hundred and fourteen thousand hours, and one million three hundred and fourteen thousand divided by nine people is a hundred and forty-six thousand hours each.** And he says what the figure is a figure of before he says the figure: a hundred and forty-six thousand hours at nine hours a day is about sixteen thousand days, and sixteen thousand days is about forty-four years, and that is a figure of nine people who have not been born, and he is not going to hand a true number about people who do not exist to a bank of nine people who do, and that it is the first number he has given this week that he has been ashamed of. **The answer came from a man of forty-one who works and it is about ten sentences and nobody liked it: eight hands is not better than two hands for a board about four feet by two feet and nine people are not better than two people for a stone about five feet high, and it is about how many hands are on it and not about how many people agree to it. And the half nobody wants: somebody has to keep coming, not about nine people but one at a time, and about twice a day, and about ninety steps, and about nine of those steps under about four feet of water, and that is going to be the case on Monday morning whatever is done on that bank.** A man of thirty-one says the band's own question a second time and a woman of forty-four says out loud that two is the correct number of times to say one, and he says he is not going to answer it and that the answer he has been given about a door is not an answer to it. The man in the ash is at thirty-nine days and the woman in the reed at forty, each spoken with the subtraction, and neither is added to the other. **And a man of sixty-one says the door is not shut, that it has not been shut since anybody in that county has been alive, that he is not going to be a man who is glad about a door, and that about four years from now somebody is going to be glad that it has been open every day, and that the gladness is going to be the thing that is sent for about.**

**670 — THE NINE ARE COUNTED OUT LOUD BY THE PERSON WHO SAID THEY WOULD, AND ABOUT FOUR OF THE NINE SAID THEY WOULD NOT FIRST AND CAME ANYWAY, AND THE MAN OF SIXTY-ONE READS ALL FOUR OF THOSE OUT LOUD.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and seventeenth week, a Monday, his four hundred and twentieth morning, his one hundred and sixteenth day. A man of forty-three gives the working for the fourth time and says the next time those steps can be walked is about four days after that bank is full and not the next time that block of limestone comes out, and that nobody in four counties has a figure for when that stone comes out either and that he has been asked four times in about nine days by four different people and the answer is the same three words. **The man of sixty-one says he is not going to say how many of them there are and that what he is doing is reading nine mouths that said a thing, and that a mouth is not a figure of anything, and reads the nine with the reason each person gave: a man of sixty-six, a sinker, who came for a narrowing and not a question and has been down three times in three days; a woman of thirty-eight who keeps a mill four miles off, who has come down four times since the Thursday of the hundred and fifteenth week and whose mill has stopped four times; a man of thirty-eight who is the only one who may carry a board of thirty-two notches and who did not bail once in three days; a man of forty-one with two carts who bailed about eight hours and a half in three days and whose Monday is not going up on the Monday; a man of fifty-four who holds the gravel and has said he cannot read six times; the man of fifty-four, who has been under about ninety steps of bank four times without being asked a question; the boy of thirteen, who said he was not going down there and has not, and who is one of the nine on the strength of four miles twice a day and not on the strength of a descent; and the man of thirty-one, who asked to be the thing that is sent for twice and was refused twice, **and a man of sixty-one says he does not know what to do with him and that that is not a reason to take him off the list.** The three who are not in it are read out loud too, because that man has never in eleven years read out one half of a thing without the other half. **And a man of thirty-one says the four things that have not moved — the sheet of deal face down on a barrel, the hearing that is out of the thirties with nobody granting it and nobody refusing it, nine words still in the heads of about nine people, and a place about nine feet past the last beam with three names in about nine mouths and no fourth — and says he is not going to say the nine words again in front of a bank, because he said them twice in a row once and a woman of twenty-eight told him that was how a saying stops being a thing a man heard.** He says the same want he said on the Wednesday of the hundred and sixteenth week with a number in it and says there is no number in it now, and that the difference is that on the Wednesday he had a thing to be angry at and this morning he has a man of sixty-one who has given him a hundred and forty-six thousand hours each, and a woman of thirty-eight who is the reason there is a fifth line in a book that does not have one, and a boy of thirteen who has a number now and does not know what to do with it. **And the ninth of the nine is a man of thirty-one who asked to be the thing that is sent for twice in about four days, was refused twice, and is in it anyway, and about four of the people in that bank had worked that out before the count.**
