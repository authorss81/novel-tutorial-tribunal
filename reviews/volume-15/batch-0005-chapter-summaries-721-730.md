**721 — THE LAST MORNING OF A FORTNIGHT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS THE DAY OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN TO TAKE IT OFF A MAN WHO SAID HE WOULD NOT.** Second day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and seventy-first morning, a hundred and sixty-seven days in the county of Kell. The bank is full for the seventh morning running and a man of forty-three gives the reading and then says the half of the ladder he had been hiding, that a bank full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth. A man of forty-one says the last morning of his fortnight out loud, says nothing is going to happen, refuses to name the day on a bank or on a Wednesday or on a Thursday, gives the reason in a man of fifty-four's words, and says he will name it in the passage at the fifth hour before the light on one of two mornings in the next fortnight and will not say which. A woman of twenty-eight says that is a rule. A man of thirty-one says the morning out loud in the open, and the man of forty-one says that has made it about nine people who can be sent for about a morning he had not said, on a Wednesday, which is the thing he said he would not do.

**722 — THE SEVENTH TIME THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY IN THE NIGHT, AND TWO SETS OF THE SAME NINE WORDS ARE PUT IN ONE ROOM AT THE FIFTH HOUR BEFORE THE LIGHT, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME.** Third day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Thursday, a hundred and sixty-eight days. A man of about fifty-two who cannot see well says the nine words twice and gives nine reasons, the ninth being that this is the whole of what a man who cannot read can be given. A man of thirty-eight reads them off the ninth line in his own lamp and gives four reasons the two are not the same, the fourth being that the ninth line has no day against either name on it and a man of fifty-four has been asked about it. A man of fifty-four says he can check a thing against another man saying it out loud and not against the paper, and asks for it every morning while there is a reading on that counter. A man of thirty-one says out loud, before anybody asked, that he is refusing because nobody can be sent for about him to tell him when to stop, and is told in nine sentences that nine people now have a word for him that they did not have on Sunday.

**723 — A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR ASKS IN THE OPEN FOR THE NINTH LINE TO BE READ TO HIM, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE GIVES TWO COUNTS AND A DIFFERENT REASON FOR NOT ADDING THEM, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS A SHAPE ABOUT A WOMAN IN A REED.** Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Friday, a hundred and sixty-nine days. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names for the thirteenth time and gives thirteen is seven hundred and twenty-three less seven hundred and ten. A man of sixty-one gives ninety-three days for a man in an ash and ninety-four for a woman in a reed, with both subtractions spoken, and refuses to add them because if either asks he will say it to them and not to the bank. A woman of sixty-four says what she told them on the Tuesday came true in four days and that she has not worked out how she knew. A woman of twenty-eight says the count of the times she has said a sentence and not said it carries no ordinal and is not restarted. A man of thirty-one says the fifth line is the only line anybody could send somebody to, is refused in seven sentences, and is not going to be told he was half right.

**724 — THE THIRTEENTH NAME, IN INK, ON A LINE THAT HAS A DAY AGAINST IT, AND THE FIRST TIME ANYTHING ON THAT COUNTER HAS MADE ANYBODY FINDABLE.** Fifth day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Saturday, on which the fever was sixty-three weeks old, a hundred and seventy days. A man of thirty-eight finds thirteen names on nine lines and gives the working before he reads one of them, and says the thirteenth is on the seventh line and the seventh line has a day against it. A woman of forty says a day is a place a man sat in a chair, gives four days as a figure of a gap and refuses to add it to a figure of five people. A woman of sixty-four says she was right about four days and does not know how, and that nobody on that bank is going to thank the man of thirty-eight for finding it. A man of thirty-one says the thing that asked a man of fifty-four is the thing that wrote the name, and a man of sixty-one refuses him in six sentences and will not tell him whether it is the same thing.

**725 — A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR WALKS FOUR MILES DOWN A CART ROAD TO SAY SHE HAS WORKED OUT A THING SHE TOLD NINE PEOPLE SHE WOULD RATHER NOT KNOW.** Sixth day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Sunday, a hundred and seventy-one days. A man of thirty-eight says he decided again at the fifth hour at his own gate and again at the seventh hour on the bank, and has not said a name out loud since the Monday of last week. She says the nine words on the ninth line are the same nine words two men in two coats used through her own door, that one of those men was given them off that line, and that there was never anybody in this county standing in a room going to say nine words, and that she has known for two days and cannot give it back to anybody. A man of thirty-one says she is the only safe place in four counties to put it, and is told in three sentences that he has handed her a thing for the fourth time in about nine days, and a woman of forty-four says a place nobody can be sent for about is not a qualification and is not a use.

**726 — A FULL BANK, AND THE NEXT TIME ABOUT NINETY STEPS CAN BE WALKED IS A FRIDAY OF NEXT WEEK SAID OUT LOUD FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SECOND WEEK, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO SAYS HE CANNOT FIND HIMSELF ON EITHER OF TWO SHEETS HE HAS BEEN A LINE ON FOR ELEVEN YEARS.** Seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Monday, a hundred and seventy-two days. A man of about fifty-two gives eleven is seven hundred and twenty-six less seven hundred and fifteen and says he would like somebody to say out loud whether a line on a sheet is a person. A woman of forty-four says it is not, and that it is not him, and that she is not going to say what it is. A man of thirty-one says a sheet of deal and a sheet of printed paper are the same instrument, and is told in four sentences that a sheet of deal is a thing you can put your thumb on and that he has used a man to get at a thing, which the man says is true and owns himself.

**727 — THE MIDPOINT. THE THING THAT PRINTED NINE LINES FOR THE NINE OPENINGS IS A COPY AND NOT A SOURCE, IT WAS BUILT BY A DISSIDENT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WAS CARRIED OVER TO BE A MARK AND NOT TO WRITE.** First day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, a Tuesday, a hundred and seventy-three days. A man of thirty-eight stops in the middle of reading a line at the top of that sheet which he has read out loud in the open seventeen mornings running, and reads it a second time, and it says the thing is set out from a form carried over from Veyra. A woman of forty says a mirror is a thing that shows you a thing and is not the thing, gives the working first, and produces three spaces about as wide as a thumb and all three the same width, and says one of the three was cut first and the other two were cut from it. A man of sixty-one gives nine sentences about what a copy leaves out and refuses the tenth. A man of fifty-four refuses a shape in four sentences and will not say which side of the mirror the thing that asked him is on. A man of thirty-one says he is an external witness, that he was selected and not chosen, and that nobody is going to be told he was right. A woman of forty-four says none of a copy, a mark and a man who was not chosen is anybody's right of refusing. The name of the thing that is not a person is not said in any of the ten chapters.

**728 — A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ SAYS HE HAS BEEN READ A LINE HE DID NOT KNOW ABOUT EVERY MORNING FOR EIGHT MORNINGS, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS OUT LOUD THAT NONE OF IT IS AN ANSWER TO THE QUESTION ASKED ON A WEDNESDAY OF A FORTNIGHT AGO.** Second day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, a Wednesday, a hundred and seventy-four days. A man of thirty-eight says he reads everything out loud and has never left anything off, asks for a figure for how long a fourth time and is not given one, and says he will not ask a fifth. A boy of thirteen gives ninety-eight days for a man in an ash that nobody asked him for, with the working, and says he is not going to say what that man does in there. A woman of twenty-eight says she checked it once and the count carries no ordinal. A man of about fifty-two asks whether a mark can say no, in nine words, and a woman of forty-four does not answer him and gives the reason.

**729 — A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO ASKS HOW LONG A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WAS IN THE PLACE HE IS NOT IN, AND THE FIGURE IS REFUSED TWICE OUT LOUD AND PRINTED NOWHERE.** Third day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, a Thursday, a hundred and seventy-five days. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names for the nineteenth time and says he will not ask a fifth time for a figure for how long. A man of sixty-one says he has five lines on the leaf after the last head of his own book and calls it a figure of a leaf. A man of about fifty-two asks the question in nine words in the open. A man of sixty-one cuts him off in six sentences, the fourth of which is that a day with nothing to point at is not a day anybody can be sent for about, and says he will refuse it twice. The man of thirty-one refuses it himself, says he is glad of the fifth sentence and is not going to be thanked for it, gives the working for a hundred and seventy-five days, and gives the age of a cut across his right palm as forty-four days with the subtraction spoken and asks again that nobody say whether it will keep. A woman of forty-four says knowing does not put a column in that county with his name at the top of it.

**730 — THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY IN THE NIGHT, AND A MAN OF FORTY-ONE SAYS THE DAY HE NAMED IN THAT PASSAGE AT THE FIFTH HOUR BEFORE THE LIGHT, AND NOBODY PUTS THEIR HANDS ON THE STONE.** Fourth day of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, a Friday, a hundred and seventy-six days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read to him for the ninth morning and says he is still not answering the fifth thing and still waiting. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names for the twentieth time, and gives twenty is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and ten, and says the sheet will be on that counter tomorrow. About nine people go down at the fifth hour because a keeping is done before the light. A man of forty-one gives nine is seven hundred and thirty less seven hundred and twenty-one, says the day is the Friday of this week, says nobody is to write it down, and says he is going to stop being the ninth of nine at about the fifth hour before the light. Nobody puts their hands on the stone, the second pair of hands is not there, and nothing falls down. A man of thirty-eight says there is a chair in the eighth room and a lamp burning on a hook and two worn places in a floor and that one of them has not got any shallower this morning. A woman of forty refuses to say that is a fourth of the three things there are. A boy of thirteen reads twenty-nine days and twenty readings back.
