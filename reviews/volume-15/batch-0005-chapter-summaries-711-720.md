**711 — THE SECOND MORNING OF A THING WITH NO TERM ON IT, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS OUT LOUD THAT IT IS A PRACTICE AND NOT A SETTLEMENT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE ASKS TO BE SOMEBODY'S AND IS REFUSED IN SIX SENTENCES.** The sixth day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Sunday, his four hundred and fifty-seventh morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and one day, a hundred and fifty-seven days in the county of Kell, and the bank still full at about sixteen inches on the fifth morning. The man of forty-three gives the figure for the next time about ninety steps can be walked as three days, and says the working is four days after a bank is full and not four days from this morning, which is the finding he has to say out loud twice. A man of thirty-eight reads nine lines out loud for the second morning, says it took about nine minutes and the same as yesterday so that about four of them can tell he has not been learning it, and says nobody asked him and nobody is going to be thanked. A woman of forty, opening with her three words, says a practice is not a settlement, that a settlement is a thing that holds when the person doing it stops, and that nobody on that bank has stopped anything, and refuses to say what would make it one. A woman of forty-four asks what happens on a morning when the man of thirty-eight is not there, and is told that about four of them can read it, that he read it in the dark under the bank before he promised, and that a piece of paper lying on a counter with nobody keeping it is not a catastrophe. A man of thirty-one says out loud that there ought to be one person on that bank who can be stopped, and a woman of forty-four takes it off him in six sentences, the fourth of which is that he has been in this county a hundred and fifty-seven days and nobody has asked him for anything in one of them. He agrees with her, gives the working, and says he is not going to stand on that bank on Wednesday as a man who was right about it on Sunday.

**712 — FIVE OF THE ELEVEN NAMES ARE PEOPLE NOBODY IN FOUR COUNTIES CAN BE SENT FOR ABOUT, THREE OF THOSE FIVE HAVE A DAY AGAINST THEM, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ SAYS THAT TO LOOK AT A PLACE IS TO GO AND STAND OVER A WOMAN IN IT.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-third week, a Monday, his four hundred and fifty-eighth morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and two days, and the bank still full on the sixth morning. A man of sixty-one gives three counts with the subtraction spoken and then refuses, out loud, the figure of the fourth count he stopped himself on the Wednesday of the hundred and fifteenth week, and says it does not go on and nobody is going to get it out of him in about nine weeks. A man of thirty-eight reads eleven names out loud for the third morning. A woman of forty reads eleven names against nine days and gives five that are in no book in this county, says two of those five have no day and that leaves three with a day, and stops in the middle of her own arithmetic — because she did not go and look, nobody made her look, and the fourth figure came out of the three she already had, and she had just made it true about three people who were safe on Saturday morning. A man of thirty-one says somebody ought to write the five down before he has thought about it, and a man of sixty-one refuses him in six sentences and puts a figure for why in the sixth, and the man of thirty-one then says the true of it: he wanted for a hundred and fifty-eight days to be on a piece of paper somewhere. A boy of thirteen says the fifth line is one of the three and does not say her name again, gives eighty-three days with the subtraction, and says a day is a place and a man can be sent to a place. A man of fifty-four takes it off him in nine sentences: to look at that place you go along the edge of that drain about a hundred yards and stand about twenty feet off a reed and look into it at a woman lying on her side, and that is what being sent for about somebody is, not the asking but the going.

**713 — ONE COLUMN AND NOT TWO, A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT WORK OUT WHETHER TWO MEN IN TWO COATS ASKED HER ONE QUESTION OR TWO, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE WHO WANTED AN OFFICE BECAUSE A BUILDING CAN BE REFUSED AND A MAN CAN STAND IN FRONT OF ONE.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and fifty-ninth morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and three days, and the bank still full on the seventh morning. The man of forty-three says the Wednesday is one day off and that he will not promise a river. A man of thirty-eight reads the sheet out loud for the fourth morning. A man of sixty-one says there is one column and not two, that a day in it has meant the day a head came through a landing for eleven years and has meant the day a person was asked to come at first light for four days, gives two reasons for not saying which is real and stops at two on purpose. A woman of forty says a column that means two things is one column in two places, that no form says which one you are reading, and that every day she has asked for since before any of them came up that plank has meant the first one. About nine people then walk four miles up the cart road to a bakehouse, and a woman of forty-four gives the one thing she has worked out in four days: both men used the same nine words, the same two hands, the same first light, and neither knew about the other, so if it was one question then something counted her once and if it was two then nothing counted her at all, and she cannot get at either from a bakehouse four miles up a cart road. She will not say which cost more. A man of thirty-one says there is one office with two kinds of paper in it and is refused in three sentences, and says out loud that he wanted one, because one office can be asked a question, a building can be refused, a man can go and stand in front of one, and he cannot go and stand in front of anything in this county because there is nothing here to stand in front of.

**714 — THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY STEPS FOR THE SIXTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK, A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT GIVES THE SAME FIGURE TWICE FOR THE FIRST TIME IN SEVEN WEEKS, AND A MAN OF FORTY-ONE SAYS IN THE DARK THAT HE HAS GOT A WEDNESDAY AND NO DAY.** The second day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Wednesday, his four hundred and sixtieth morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and four days, and the water off all ninety at the sixth hour, four days after a full bank on the Saturday of last week. About nine people go down at the fifth hour, because a keeping is done before the light. A man of thirty-eight puts his lamp on the floor of that gallery and says the light under the eighth door-stone is the width of his own hand at the knuckles, that it was a thumb eleven days ago, that it has been a hand for eight days and has not gone back and has not got wider, and he will not say what that means and will not say what it does not. A man of forty-one says the Wednesday of next week is the last day of his fortnight, tells about nine people about it in the dark and asks nothing, and says he has a fortnight and a Wednesday and no day because he said a week ago he would not do it on a Wednesday, and that he worked the date out nine days before and told nobody. A man of thirty-one offers to be one of the two hands and is refused in nine sentences by the man of forty-one, whose ninth sentence is that he does not want the last thing his two hands do to have a man of thirty-one in it that nobody can be sent for about. Two people then push a stone about as big as a door nine feet along a groove and push it back, and it takes about a minute. A man of fifty-four puts his own hand under the ninth door-stone and says an inch and about the thickness of his own finger, three mornings running and none of them a different figure, and says nobody is going to be thanked for it.

**715 — A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR COMES UP A BANK TO ASK WHETHER HER NAME IS ON A SHEET OF PRINTED PAPER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE TELLS HER IT IS NOT BEFORE ANYBODY HAS READ IT, AND IS WRONG, AND SAYS HE HAD NOT LOOKED.** The third day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Thursday, his four hundred and sixty-first morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and five days, and the water about four inches over the top step, one day since it came off all ninety. The man of forty-three says he is going to stop giving figures for how long while he is ahead. A man of thirty-eight reads the sheet out loud for the fifth morning and the count is still eleven. A woman of sixty-four who has been out of every book in this county for nineteen years says four things: that nobody has been able to find her by writing her name down in any of them; that she is not on that piece of paper and nobody has told her either way; that she came up this bank to be told and not to be given a thing; and that nobody is going to ask her what it was like to be asked. A man of thirty-one says out loud that she is not on it, that he has read the thing five times this week, and then says he has not looked this morning and said it because it is what he wanted to be true, and refuses to say he is sorry, because he made a decision and not a mistake. A woman of twenty-eight says in four sentences that this is the first time in this county he has been wrong about a fact instead of wrong about a shape, and that it is not a lie and it is worse than a lie, because a lie has somebody in it who decided something. He agrees, gives the age of a cut across his right palm out loud as thirty days, and asks that nobody say whether it will keep. She then says what it cost and that it is hers: she came up to ask whether she was on it, a man of thirty-one told her she was not and was wrong, and now she knows there is a place on that thing where her name could go and yesterday morning she did not.

**716 — TWELVE NAMES AND NOT ELEVEN, THE TWELFTH IN INK ON THE NINTH LINE WITH NO DAY AGAINST IT, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR WHO HEARS HER OWN NAME READ OUT AND SAYS THAT BEING A NAME ON A PIECE OF PRINTED PAPER COST NOBODY ANYTHING AT ALL IN SIX DAYS.** The fourth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Friday, his four hundred and sixty-second morning, the fever sixty-one weeks and six days, and the water about eight inches over the top step. A man of thirty-eight goes down four feet, picks the sheet up and counts, and says nine lines and twelve names before he reads one of them, gives the working, says it was eleven on the Friday, the Saturday, the Sunday and the Thursday of this week, that there are seven mornings from the Saturday of last week to this one and there is a reading on six of them, that he is not going to say which one has not got a reading on it because he was under that bank with his own lamp at the fifth hour, and that he is not going to say that was the reason and is not going to say it was a coincidence. He reads the twelfth name aloud. A woman of sixty-four gives four things in order: she said yes and is not going to be asked about the asking today, in nine days or in four years; she is still a name nobody can be sent for about because there is no day against her; she is nevertheless a name on a thing, and that took six days and cost nobody anything against nineteen years and one thing a day; and nobody in this county can be sent for about who wrote it. A woman of forty gives the count: nine lines, twelve names, two the same name twice, three with no day, and one name added in six days, which is a figure of a gap and not of a person, and she will not add it to the five she worked out on Monday. A man of thirty-eight says he is not going to carry it into a room and is not going to stop reading it, and puts those two next to each other on purpose. A man of thirty-one says he will find out who wrote it and is refused in nine sentences, the first of which is that a person who cannot be sent for about is not a person who gets found.

**717 — TWO BOARDS AND A SHEET ON ONE COUNTER AND NONE OF THE THREE READ, FOUR OF THE NINE BOARDS CARRY A JOB WITH A THUMB'S WIDTH OF SPACE WHERE A NAME GOES AND FIVE OF THE NINE JOBS WERE NEVER PUT BACK TOGETHER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT ASKS ABOUT NINE PEOPLE NOT TO JOIN THEM UP.** The fifth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Saturday, his four hundred and sixty-third morning, the fever sixty-two weeks old, and the water about twelve inches over the top step, three days since it came off all ninety. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names out loud for the seventh morning reading. A man of fifty-four who cannot read takes the eighth of nine boards out of the inside of his own coat, puts it down about a foot from the paper, and says before he does it that he is not asking anybody to read it to him; and gives the figure of the nine boards — four with a job and a space about as wide as a thumb between two hands where a name goes, five with no job at all because five of the nine jobs were never put back together. A man of thirty-eight puts down the board a man of thirty-one gave him at the end of the hundred and twenty-first week and does not read it either, and asks about nine of them to notice if they are about to make two same-sized things into one thing before they do it. A woman of forty says the difference in three sentences: a board is a job with a space in it where a name goes and the space is empty and waiting for somebody, and a sheet is a question with a name already in it, twelve names and no spaces and nothing waiting for anybody. A man of thirty-eight gives the working for both marks, the thumb-sized square cut in the paper and the thumb-wide space between two hands over the ninth opening, says he has had both in his own hands, refuses to say they are the same thing, and refuses to say they are not the same thing either. A man of thirty-one says they are the same nine and a man of forty-three takes it off him in seven sentences, the seventh of which turns out to be true and is the only shape he ever got right, and is about nine lines and nothing else. A boy of thirteen gives sixteen days and seven readings and keeps both.

**718 — SOMETHING THAT IS NOT A PERSON SAYS A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR'S NAME OUT LOUD AND ASKS HIM THE SAME QUESTION IN THE SAME NINE WORDS, A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR REFUSES IT IN NINE SENTENCES AND GIVES NOBODY THE RIGHT OF REFUSING, AND HE SAYS THAT WHAT HE SAID WAS THE REASON HE HAD NOT GOT AN ANSWER AND NOT AN ANSWER.** The sixth day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Sunday, his four hundred and sixty-fourth morning, the fever sixty-two weeks and one day, and the bank full again at about sixteen inches, with the next time the ninety steps can be walked in four days. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names out loud for the eighth morning. At about the ninth hour something that is not a person says Barnaby Crove and asks him, and does not ask anybody else anything. A woman of forty-four refuses it in nine sentences before he opens his mouth — he is now the fifth thing anybody can be sent for about, a man who cannot read cannot refuse, he can only be spoken for, a thing that asks a person nobody can be sent for about is not asking a person, there is nothing to appeal it to, and she is not going to give him the right of refusing and is not going to take it off him. He says he is not going to answer it and says that what he says is not a refusal: he cannot read, there are nine words on that thing and about four of them are about his own two hands, and he cannot tell whether the nine words he has been given are the nine words on it, and that is the reason he has not got an answer and not an answer. A woman of forty says there are three of those things now — a door that will not open, a door that is shut and held, and a man who cannot read — and refuses to pick one. A man of thirty-one offers to go and ask him the same question himself, about nine seconds after something that is not a person did it, and is refused in nine sentences by the man of fifty-four, the fifth of which is that if he goes up that road then in about four days somebody is going to come up it and ask a man of thirty-six and then a man of thirty-four in an ash, and every one of them knows where that ends.

**719 — A MAN OF FORTY-ONE GIVES THREE WAYS HE HAS TRIED TO GET HIS DAY WRITTEN DOWN AND NONE OF THEM IS A FORM IN THIS COUNTY, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS A NAME OUT LOUD TO THE GROUND AND IT DOES NOT ANSWER, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR GIVES WHAT THAT COST HER AND IT IS HERS.** The seventh and last day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week, a Monday, his four hundred and sixty-fifth morning, the fever sixty-two weeks and two days, and the bank still full on the fifth morning. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names out loud for the ninth morning. A man of forty-one gives three ways: the sheet, and there is no column on it that gets asked that; a book, and a man who writes it is a man who knows it alone; one of the nine boards, and eight of the nine are in four counties and none is going to be found in about four days. He is not going to put his day on the sheet, and gives a man of thirty-eight's reason in his words: he would be asking it once, and a column that gets asked once is not a column, it is a favour, and he has been the ninth of nine for nineteen years and has had enough of favours. A woman of forty-four says the third thing she has worked out about two men in two coats, which is that she does not want to know, and says she came down the road to say a thing nobody asked her for. Then a man of thirty-one says a name out loud, once, to the ground, in plain words, and nobody shouts back and nothing whatever answers, and it is the first time in this county a person who was asked something by it has said it out loud. A woman of sixty-four says she said that name herself on the Thursday of last week because she was not going to let the first person to say it be a man who was not there, and that he was there; and gives what it cost — about nine people on that bank now have that name in their mouths who did not have it on Monday, and one of the nine is a man of thirty-eight who has to stand on that bank before anybody goes anywhere and decide every morning whether he says it out loud — and says it is a figure of mornings she has not got, that she is not going to be thanked for the nineteen years, that she is not going to be told it was worth it, and that she is not relieved and nobody there is relieved. A man of thirty-one says he said it to find out whether it would answer, and that he used about nine people on a bank to find out, and a woman of twenty-eight refuses to stop it a third time and says she said on the Saturday of last week that she is not going to be the person who holds it.

**720 — A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAYS THAT A DAY ABOUT NINE PEOPLE HAVE HEARD READ OUT IS NOT A DAY IN A BOOK AND NOT A DAY ON THAT SHEET AND NOT A DAY ANYBODY CAN BE SENT FOR ABOUT, AND IT CUTS BOTH WAYS, AND A WOMAN OF TWENTY-EIGHT REFUSES TO HOLD A STANDING SILENCE FOR A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE A THIRD TIME.** The first day of the hundred and twenty-fifth week, a Tuesday, his four hundred and seventieth morning, the fever sixty-two weeks and three days, and the bank still full on the sixth morning, with the next time the ninety steps can be walked in two days. A man of thirty-eight reads twelve names out loud for the tenth morning reading, says he is going to keep reading the twelfth with the other eleven, and asks for a figure for how long a fourth time and is refused. A man of sixty-one gives nine sentences: a day about nine people have heard read out is not a day in his book, not a day on that sheet, not a day anybody in four counties can be sent for about, and not a day anybody can point at either, and those last two are different things; there is no form anywhere that says a person may be sent for about a day and no form that says a person may not; the only thing a man of forty-one has got is about nine people who heard it; and a thing about nine people have is a thing about nine people can be sent for about, which cuts both ways and is a sentence of a man of fifty-four who cannot read, quoted back and not taken off him. He refuses the tenth sentence and refuses to write any of the nine down. A woman of sixty-four says somebody is going to write another name on that sheet in about four days, four days early on purpose, that nobody is going to be sent for about it, that she is not going to say it will be her and not going to say it will not be her, and that she is not going to be thanked for saying it out loud a week early on purpose. A man of fifty-four says he has had it two days and has not answered it and is not going to this week and nobody has asked him since and he said he would wait to be asked and is still waiting. A man of forty-one says he has not moved his day, is not going to do it on a Wednesday and is not going to do it on a Thursday because a Wednesday is easier, and that that bank is going to have about nine people on it on Wednesday morning, and nobody on it is going to say anything to him about it. A man of thirty-one says he will be standing on that bank on Wednesday and a woman of forty-four takes it off him in six sentences, the sixth of them being that he has not been asked again and that if he stands there he will be standing in front of about nine people waiting for something to happen to him and everybody on that bank will be able to tell; and he says nothing has asked him anything since the Wednesday of a fortnight ago, that he is not going to arrange to be asked, and that he is not going to say the shape of his answer a second time because a rule is a thing that works without a person.
