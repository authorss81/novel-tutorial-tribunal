## VOLUME 12, CHAPTERS 571 TO 580 — *the harvest and the private season*. **THE MIDPOINT IS SPENT AT `578`, ONCE, ON THE HOUSEHOLD OF GARR RYLEY, AND BANDS 0004 AND 0005 CARRY THE CONSEQUENCE AND THE SETTLEMENT AND MAY NOT RE-SPEND IT. IT RESOLVES NOTHING.**

**571 — A dyker walks nine hundred yards of standing water to ask what fifty-eight people are doing, and counts himself a day late.** `clear` 71, the sixth day of the hundred and third week, a Sunday. Hoare Ryley, fifty-one, four acres of his own and eleven between his two brothers, is named for the first time and walks off the drove across the flats to the rise about eleven feet wide. He asks Bett Searle what fifty-eight people on ground that belongs to nobody are doing on a Sunday morning and whether anybody in Kell can be sent for about it, and is told they are doing nothing at all, which is the only ground in the district where a person cannot be sent for. He works two counts out loud on the mud — five days from the rising to the Sunday, two days after it to the Thursday water — and nine days off the Monday that was the seventh and last of the hundred and first, which lands Wednesday against a Thursday water, and says himself he is a day late. Reuben Searle, nineteen, is back on the water with a pole, says his four words to a stranger and will not say them twice. Closing: two hundred and twenty feet on a wall that is about ground and not people.

**572 — A nine-year question in a parlour, and twenty-one acres nobody can check.** `clear` 72, the seventh and last of the hundred and third, a Monday, morrow first of the hundred and fourth. Aldis Crane, about fifty, a holding, a seat, a skep behind his gate, asks Iven Ashcombe whether the low meadow has ever gone under, and is answered in a number: about thirty-one acres, about two thirds under, about ten standing, about twenty-one not to be mown this year, a fortnight with a scythe and about nine men beginning on the morrow. He then asks what a number is for when one man can be asked anything and has nine lines against himself and a wall anybody can correct in four seconds and nobody may be asked about afterwards. The innkeeper says the number back wrong fourteen minutes later. Toby Henshaw, fourteen, is sent for with nine feet of knotted line and no line to give him: thirty-one acres cannot be walked. Ilyan, eighteen days in the county, says out loud he will not walk four hundred yards to look. Closing: a number in a bar that cannot be taken back.

**573 — A barrow of sallow, four shillings and twopence, and a lane walked twice.** `clear` 73, the first of the hundred and fourth, a Tuesday; the hay does not get cut. Garr Ryley, thirty-three, six acres of the sally bed with nothing in the square eight days after giving it to a room, barrows sallow out of a hundred and forty yards of ditch alone with the wrong tool: about nine loads a day, about sixteen days, twelve shillings and sixpence a year and nobody paying it. The innkeeper of four beds pays four shillings and twopence on two acres she does not work, the first rate on the bar in two years not from seven hundred sheep, and forbids her name on the wall. Sabra Lyne, thirty-eight, four acres taken in April, walks the lane twice to ask for a parcel against her own challenge at Saturday reading where nine can be checked. First load out of the yard all day comes in a barrow; the cart does not go out. Closing: mud, money, and a request.

**574 — Five lines on the back of a remedy, and a letter up a drain.** `clear` 74, the second of the hundred and fourth, a Wednesday. The remedy of three hundred words has lain five days; Sabra Lyne takes it off the bar and writes five lines in another hand, the fifth that the ground should be put on it. Ilyan, twenty days in the county, argues for his own paper in about nine people’s hearing and is told he is the worst man in Kell to do it, because he wrote it and cannot be sent for, and stops. Bray comes to the bar, says he has mowed, says a number was said at ten gates by a stranger and a season given to a room because of a door, says nobody has asked him about the boards in eight days. A flat boat brings four lines that are a refusal of the same kind as the first and not an invitation; they are not read out to anybody. Closing: an unread letter and a stopped argument.

**575 — The ninth day from the rising, and three things done.** `clear` 75, the third of the hundred and fourth, a Thursday. Hester Nunn, forty-one, has been to the common and back and seen it herself; has read the nine things with foot or fifty-eight on them, two in the green book (the rising day, the empty square); and the third thing is done by the man, not her — Ilyan asks in a room who a hundred of thirty men asks when it asks, about four answer wrong in four seconds, about nine do not answer, nobody is bound. She decides about the paper: the second close off the dyke, six acres, parcel and acres and day, no name top or bottom, twelve shillings and sixpence checkable where a woman of forty-one is not. Closing: the first line in two hundred and thirty years a rate can be checked against.

**576 — A market seven days on, and twelve shillings and sixpence refused.** `clear` 76, the fourth of the hundred and fourth, a Friday; water off the green in seven days. Toby reads the five lines; the remedy is worth less, nine of three hundred having been somewhere. The sixteen-acre brother lays twelve shillings and sixpence (sixteen at twenty-five pence is four hundred pence, one pound thirteen and four) for his brother’s six, and the clerk refuses in nine sentences: no form lets him hold it or give it. Ilyan, twenty-two days, says why he will not lay it either: the hand that pays the rate holds the season, and a holder cannot say a true thing about a man in that room. The barrow woman’s nine words are owned out loud. Closing: about nine can repeat it, two hundred and ninety-one cannot be asked.

**577 — A Saturday reading with six challenges, and nine parcels on the morning list.** `clear` 77, the fifth of the hundred and fourth, a Saturday. Perrin Voss, sixty-two, holds the slate; nine have read the six hay challenges before he begins. He reads hay, mown on four acres top side; nine say the reading is wrong. Sabra Lyne says her parcel — four acres above the lane, willow stump, hay standing — and gets it written, the first challenge with a parcel. Eight more follow until nine parcels stand on the list, Hester’s among them with no name. The remedy front and back are read; nine say the nine-men-fortnight-scythe cannot be got where five challenges name no ground. Ilyan, twenty-three days, carries twenty-one from parlour to door and says it twice. Closing: a figure with no column for the difference.

**578 — THE MIDPOINT. He can put six acres back and speak for the harvest, and cannot do both.** `clear` 78, the sixth of the hundred and fourth, a Sunday. Thirty-one men in sixty feet. Hoare Ryley says the two true things: six acres empty at one hundred and fifty pence with his brother five days into sixteen alone; forty-four acres sixth year, nine acres new sward, in season to Bray who paid £21 17s 6d, fortnight begun five days ago. Ilyan, twenty-four days, under two pounds, says the sentence: he can put six acres back in season and speak for the harvest and cannot do both, because the hand that pays the rate holds the season and a holder cannot say a true thing about a man in that room. One panel (CLAIM, EVIDENCE, BURDEN, CHOICE). Bray, asked nothing for twelve days, says he is not answering today. By the ninth hour Garr Ryley, thirty-three, says the sentence back. It is not Hoare: he holds four acres in season and has lost nothing. Closing: the sentence in somebody else’s mouth.

**579 — Two of ten acres cut, and a thirteen-day question asked.** `clear` 79, the seventh and last of the hundred and fourth, a Monday, morrow first of the hundred and fifth. Nine men, four holders and five not, scythes to ten acres six days late; first swath down at the ninth hour and it cannot be put back; two acres down by the fourth, no barn takes wet hay. Ilyan, twenty-five days, stands further off as told: watching is the second of his two true things. At the field edge the clerk counts thirteen days aloud and asks Bray whether he meant the boards; Bray stands nine minutes, gives no answer that day, does not say no. Garr watches with the stranger: first of the consequence, not the whole. Closing: a cut that cannot be put back.

**580 — A decision in a yard, and a man asked who has not said no.** `clear` 80, the first of the hundred and fifth, a Tuesday. Ilyan, twenty-six days, walks the mud to six acres and decides aloud to Garr: stay nobody’s until the year turns; one hundred and fifty pence stays in the purse; the voice stays in the room. Garr says the cost back plain: six empty into the year, nine more days for a year of ditch, twenty-one gone this year, the meadow gone in two. At the wall the empty square is read as gone; Hester reads it: still nothing, not hers to mend. Three boards lie at Coldcombe; asked yesterday at the field edge, fourteen days after said, Bray has not said no. Closing: a person asked for something who has not said no — the thing Band 0004 spends, visible, unspent, unnamed as a choice.
