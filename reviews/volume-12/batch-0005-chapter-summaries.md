## VOLUME 12, CHAPTERS 581 TO 590 — *the year turns, and the thing the water does*. **THE MIDPOINT IS NOT RE-SPENT AND THE PANEL AT `578` IS SPENT AND GONE. IT RESOLVES NOTHING. THE BAND'S ONE PRICED FIGURE, FOUR POUNDS ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND THREEPENCE, BELONGS TO A WOMAN WHO KEEPS FOUR BEDS AND NOT TO HIM, AND THE BOARDS ARE ANSWERED BY A CONDITION THAT CANNOT BE MET.**

**581 — A decision in about nine mouths, and a man with a barrow who says the money is on the bar.** `clear` 81, the second day of the hundred and fifth week, a Wednesday. Ilyan, twenty-seven days in the county, goes to a pump to find out what his Tuesday decision arrived as, which is the carrier habit for the fifth time in twenty-eight days: a man of about thirty-four with a barrow tells him the money is on the bar, and a man of about fifty with a holding tells about four people on a corner that a stranger paid the rate on six acres on Tuesday, and neither has lied. Toby Henshaw, fourteen, stands at the north wall at the fifth hour with a rag and nothing to rub out. Then Garr Ryley's yard: a nine-foot plank across the ditch-mouth, about nine yards of sallow, about nine loads a day, about eight days left. He holds the low end for about five hours. Garr's terms: take nothing, write nothing, do not talk; a man who cannot be sent for cannot be sent back either. Closing: an empty square and nothing to take a rag to.

**582 — A load of wet hay that does not fit where it is hauled to.** `clear` 82, the third of the hundred and fifth week, a Thursday. About nine men, about three of ten acres of a low meadow of about thirty-one down, the hay nine days late, water standing between the lines, and a fourth pass being a hay of the following year. A boy of fourteen walks the ends of the swaths and counts eleven with his feet because he cannot do it with nine feet of knotted line. A cart comes up the lane to a house with four beds in it and stands in the only way in. Ilyan's mistake: he says the true of the woman's answer out loud about nine seconds before she gives it, the fifth time he has made a person the worse for something he did in the open, and she says it her own way afterwards. Her reason is a line on a wall about a woman of thirty-eight and a crop of hay she never had. Closing: the load and the woman who would not take it, and the load goes down the lane in the night to a yard nobody had asked.

**583 — The third market since the rising, and a condition that is not a no.** `clear` 83, the fourth of the hundred and fifth week, a Friday. About three hundred people. The sally bed goes on the step for the third week running and nobody puts money on the bar. A man of about thirty with sixteen acres says out loud, at the bar, why no one has gone round the back of it since. Owain Bray, fifty-four, is asked at the step in front of about three hundred people — seventeen days in the carrying — and ANSWERS: he has the money, and he will put three boards in that gate when the hay is off that meadow, because in the same week he would be asking nine men for a fortnight and putting a foot of water on four closes, and he will not hand three hundred people a reason they cannot check. He also says the true of the gate out loud for the second time: the foot of water that went over the common did not come through his gate, and nobody has worked out what three boards hold off. He says he is not saying no. Ilyan's mistake: at the step, publicly, he says he is standing behind the harvest and not behind six acres, and about four people have it as a man who has picked a side. Closing: about thirty heard it and about two hundred and seventy cannot be asked.

**584 — A boy of fourteen holds the slate, and a parcel is taken off a wall in about four seconds.** `clear` 84, the fifth of the hundred and fifth week, a Saturday, the reading at the sixth hour. Perrin Voss has not held it for three readings; Toby Henshaw has, and this is the fifth thing he has been asked to write and the first he has not said he did not know what to do with. He reads the eleven lines, the square with nothing in it for the third week running, the four lines, the smithy notice, the remedy front and back; he corrects seven hundred sheep out of nine hundred with Bray in the room. One of the nine parcels put on the morning list last Saturday is wrong and the man who put it there says so and the boy rubs it off in about four seconds. The sixth of six challenges has nothing against it and nobody stands up. Ilyan's mistake, and it breaks his own word: he names a parcel out loud for that sixth challenge, out of something a man said at a bar on Wednesday, and the man with sixteen acres says it is his brother's six acres and not his — six acres at two shillings and a penny the acre, a hundred and fifty pence, twelve shillings and sixpence, the rate canon gives that parcel — and the clerk declines to write it, not because the acres are wrong, which they are not any more, but because the man who gave them has said the ground is his brother's. At the ninth hour Hoare Ryley comes down the lane, says it is his, and the boy takes the rag to it in about four seconds. Closing: the challenge with nothing against it, and a line with nothing against it is a sentence.

**585 — No room, no slate, no bar, and a rise with nothing on it.** `clear` 85, the sixth of the hundred and fifth week, a Sunday. Ilyan, thirty-one days in, walks out at the seventh hour with a crust in his coat. At the six-board gate on the main dyke he puts his hand flat through a space between two sill blocks where sallow has grown up and gone hard, and there is moving water on the other side of his wrist at a level that is not the level in the channel, and there are two lines on the timber above the frame and he cannot get the upper one. On the rise: eleven lines of peg holes, eleven fire pits, a churn hoop, a boat with her pole across her, no sack under the churn because there is no churn, and bare grass the whole nine hundred yards. Perrin Voss asks how many. His mistake: he counts and writes nothing down, and says why — a stranger's chalk would be a fifth hand and a figure in one head is the worst of the two. Toby Henshaw asks him for the rise and he will not say. Closing: fifty-eight is not a figure of tents, and nobody has said the number out loud anywhere but on a wall.

**586 — The band's one priced figure, and it belongs to another mouth.** `clear` 86, the seventh and last day of the hundred and fifth week, a Monday; the morrow is the first of the hundred and sixth, a Tuesday. Nine people in the parlour of the Green Paw. Ilyan, thirty-two days in, asks a woman of about fifty who keeps four beds and has never given him her name to price a year of a man of thirty-one in front of about nine people, having decided not to say it himself because when a man says what a thing costs him in a room it is a number he has chosen. She takes three pence a day, not two, because he eats three; three hundred and sixty-five days, because a year is the time between one crossing of the tally stone and the next; a thousand and ninety-five pence; four pounds is nine hundred and sixty of them and a hundred and thirty-five over; a hundred and thirty-five pence is eleven shillings and threepence. **Four pounds eleven shillings and threepence**, and about four in the room think it worth more and about nine less. Ashcombe adds the other half: one, being one morning, one room, one seat in about thirty. She then gives him a parcel — two acres of the strip behind that yard — instead of her name, because two acres at four shillings and twopence cannot be argued with at a wall.

**587 — The water comes back to the line and does not go over the cut.** `clear` 87, the first day of the hundred and sixth week, a Tuesday. A water-clerk of fifty-eight said the Monday and is a day out, and says so at the seventh hour with a rod before anybody works it out. The water stands at the cut for about four hours and does not cross it; one crossing makes a year and they have had one. At the tenth hour the top of the three boards in the gate goes under and the channel runs over them, and about four feet on the low side a ditch is wet to the top of its bank. Ilyan's mistake: he says out loud, in about nine people's hearing, that the water is coming through a gap under the sill — which is what his hand found on the Sunday — and Hoare Ryley, who has cut ditch for thirty years, tells him it is the meadow drain and the old line, two feet higher, which does not touch that gate. Two men who have both put their hands on that gate do not agree, and about four people have Ilyan's shape of it and are wrong. Also: nine yards of Garr's cut ditch running clear and nine yards still choked, about nine days left. Closing: the line on the stone wet all the way round and drying at the ends, and not over.

**588 — A hundred and forty yards out of the ground in sixteen days, and two pairs of hands.** `clear` 88, the second of the hundred and sixth week, a Wednesday. About nine loads a day, about a hundred and forty-four loads, about sixteen days, and a boy of fourteen with about nine feet of knotted line who works out that a hundred and forty yards is four hundred and twenty feet and about forty-seven of his line, seven hours of walking, and would do it twice before he would say a number. Ilyan's mistake: he says the total out loud before the man who dug it can, out of a figure Garr gave him at a gate on the Tuesday of the fourth week. Garr's correction is his own: there was never a foot of sallow in the whole of it, only a foot in the first about a hundred and ten yards and about a hand's depth in the last thirty. Then the half nobody had said out loud in thirty-four days: the rate on six acres is one hundred and fifty pence a year and nobody is paying it, a ditch fills in about two years cut and about one uncut, and on the morning the water comes over the tally stone the square will be exactly as empty as it is this morning. Closing: two men on a plank, one of whom can be sent for.

**589 — The carried question, asked in a new mouth, in a room with the door shut.** `clear` 89, the third day of the hundred and sixth week, a Thursday. About thirty people, nobody summoned them, the door shut because thirty is not nine. Aldis Crane reports the rise: nothing on it, in front of about thirty. Bett Searle, twenty-eight, daughter of six on her hip, asks it — who is it this hundred asks, when it asks — a fortnight after a man of thirty-one asked it in the same room. About four answer wrong in about four seconds, about nine do not answer, and no form in the county of Kell makes anybody answer. Ilyan answers it once, with the wall, and she tells him in about four seconds in front of all of them that a wall cannot be asked anything at all, and he does not defend it. Ashcombe writes a second line under fifty-eight in the taking column: the parcel, the day he stood on the rise, nothing above it, nothing below it, and says he is not writing fifty-eight off and is not permitted to be asked the second thing. Closing: nobody bound, about nine mouths by the fourth hour, and the rest of eleven hundred never.

**590 — The fourth market since the rising, and a woman asked who does not say no.** `clear` 90, the fourth day of the hundred and sixth week, a Friday. About three hundred people and standing water on the low side again. About nine men from the meadow put the state of the hay on the step: six of ten acres down, and the first of them lying in the wet since the ninth hour on the Monday of the hundred and fourth week, which is eleven days, and four standing and three days past the fortnight. Aldis Crane decides in the open that the four will be cut this afternoon and that no form lets him send for about four more men; a woman of about seventy asks where the hay will go and nobody answers her. Then the clerk says, for the first time out loud, that a condition a man of fifty-four set himself on that same green seven days ago cannot be met — twenty-four days in the carrying off the rising, and the hay is not off this morning and what comes off it this afternoon is not a hay either. Ilyan says no to Crane at the step in front of about thirty people and then asks for the nine seconds back, because a no said in a market reaches about nine who can repeat it and a yes reaches two hundred and ninety-one who cannot be asked about it, and there is a third thing and he has not got one for a green. Closing: Hester Nunn, forty-one, asked in the open whether she will put her name on the paper under the bar in order to take six acres back before the year turns — and she does not say no.
