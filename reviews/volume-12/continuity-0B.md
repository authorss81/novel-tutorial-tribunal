## 0B. WHAT CHAPTERS 561–570 ADDED TO THE GROUND

**THE HUNDRED** met at midday on the rising of `566` with forty-one people in the building, and on eleven lines of the thirty the name in the first column is the same name, and there is no form anywhere in that county that says whether that is one seat or eleven, and one of those seats is a man four miles south who has never been in the building in nineteen years. `562`, `566`

**THE SEASON AND THE SEASON-PENNY AT THE RISING.** Eleven parcels, two hundred and ten acres, at two shillings and a penny the acre, which is twenty-one pounds and seventeen shillings and sixpence a year, of which four pounds is the water-clerk's wage. Six acres owe twelve shillings and sixpence, which is one hundred and fifty pence, and that figure is the price of the ditch between the sally bed and the low meadow for a year. `565`, `566`

**AND A LINE WITH NOTHING IN THE SQUARE,** for the first time in about two hundred and thirty years, and one hundred and fifty pence a year of ditch gone out of it. `565`, `566`

**THE NUMBER AGAINST THE COMMON, AND IT IS TWO.** Two hundred and twenty feet, written on `566` in the square at the end of the line, derived on the page out of about nine hundred yards of usable edge at about twenty feet a tent for the tent, the fire, the churn and the line. **THE CLERK SAID OUT LOUD, BEFORE HE WROTE IT, THAT IT WAS A FIGURE ABOUT A WIDTH OF GROUND AND A NUMBER OF TENTS AND NOT ABOUT PEOPLE, AND THAT HE KNEW IT WOULD BE USELESS IN THE SAME BREATH. AT `593` A WOMAN PICKED IT UP AND USED IT AS A PLACE FOR FIFTY-EIGHT PEOPLE AND IT WAS THE FIRST THING THE SETTLEMENT GOT WRONG.** Second: fifty-eight, in the taking column, written on `567` at the request of a woman of twenty-eight with a daughter of six on her hip, with the day on it and no name on it. `566`, `567`, `593`

**THE WEEKLY READING.** A district reads its wall on the sixth hour of a Saturday morning, out loud, by whoever holds the slate, under the lamp on the bracket, with the door open. About two hundred and thirty years old. **THE WATER-CLERK WILL NOT READ IT AND GAVE THE REASON TWICE.** `563`

**THE PROHIBITION.** Nine lines, chalked by a boy of fourteen on a Sunday morning outside the water-clerk's own door: that no person in this hundred is to be asked what they remember, in any venue, of anybody, including him, including a stranger, including a man with a slate, and including his wife. **It was entered by a person and not by an office, and about nine people want it taken down and about nine do not, and the two nines have not asked each other.** `564`, `570`

**THE MARKET** is on a Friday. The Friday of `562` was called off by a man with no right to call it off. The Friday of `569` and the Fridays of `576`, `583`, `590` and **`597`, the fifth held since the rising**, were held, with about three hundred people each time. `562`, `569`, `597`

**THE REMEDY.** **Three hundred words with nothing at the top of them, in the hand of a man of thirty-one, written standing up in a room over a stable on a cracked steel pen on `569`.** It now lies under the oak bar at the north end of the green, and it is announced on the wall with the day on it, and any person in the hundred may read it, take it away and write on the back of it. **NO PERSON IN THAT COUNTY IS BOUND BY A WORD OF IT.** `569`, `570`, `597`

**THE WEEK'S SHEET.** A boy of fourteen writes it in chalk on the end wall of the smithy on a Friday night for a penny, with about thirty names nobody asked for and a stroke beside the ones that are not of this hundred, which means they came. `570`

**THE DITCH, THE MEADOW AND WHY THE COMMON WENT UNDER.** The water that went over the low common on `568` came off the low meadow, about thirty-one acres, which had been under since Friday because the rate on eleven parcels had not been paid for two years and the ditch that takes the meadow off the pasture had a foot of sallow growing in it. **THE RISE IS ABOUT NINE HUNDRED YARDS LONG AND BELONGS TO NOBODY. THERE IS NO HILL IN THAT COUNTRY.** Two of eleven tents were still standing at the ninth hour on `568`. Nobody was drowned. `565`, `568`

**THE COME-OVERS' LICENCE HAS NOW LAPSED.** It said *for the common, in season*, and that people might stay **until the water turned**, which is the rising. **The water turned on `566`. The licence lapsed on the same morning as every other season in the hundred, and there is no form anywhere that renews it, and there is no copy of it in the county, and the clerk has never seen it.** `566`, `568`

**AND TWO FIGURES THAT ARE A COUNT OF HOW LONG AGO AND NOT A CLOCK.** `558` ten days, `561` eight days, both corrected in a doorway with the subtraction spoken; both come out at `568`. Neither was converted to a week-count and the rising itself is not given a number. `561`, `568`

**AND HE ARRIVED ON THE EVENING OF `553`. THEREFORE EVERY COUNT OF HOW LONG HE HAS BEEN IN THE COUNTY IS `chapter − 554`: `591` THIRTY-SEVEN THROUGH `600` FORTY-SIX, one a day, each in a mouth or silently kept.** `554`, `555`, `558`

**AND THE SIX ACRES ARE TWO PARCELS AND NOT ONE.** **The woman of forty-one holds six acres of the second close off the dyke, and the man of thirty-three holds six acres of the sally bed on the top side. The two are the same rate and the same one hundred and fifty pence and are not the same ground.** `555`, `561`, `565`
