## 0. WHAT THE REVIEW ACTUALLY WAS, AND WHY THIS FILE EXISTS AT ALL

**`logs/volume-close.review.log` IS A TRANSCRIPT OF A REVIEW SESSION THAT CRASHED BEFORE IT WROTE A REPORT.** Its last recorded action is a tool call the harness rejected, and there is no verdict in it and no finding list. **A transcript that ends mid-check is not a review, and this file does not pretend one happened.** What the transcript does contain is a long series of probes, and three of them were aimed at the six defects the close had itself recorded and refused to repair in §5.6 — the eleven days at `523:133`, the twenty-five days at `544:41`, the smithy at `547:31`, the fifth at `538:13`, and the *hundred and forty-one days* at `550`.

**AND THE TRANSCRIPT'S OWN DATE-LINE CHECKER WAS BROKEN, WHICH IS WHY THE REPAIRS BELOW WERE DONE BY HAND AND BY A PARSER WRITTEN FROM THE CHAPTERS RATHER THAN FROM THAT SCRIPT.** The session's word-to-number map was missing `hundred`, the round tens, and the ordinal forms, so it printed two hundred and six *mismatches* on fifty date lines that are correct, and a reader who had trusted it would have "repaired" a volume that was not broken. **It is the same failure as finding eleven wearing a different hat: a checker whose map is wrong reports a defect that is not there, and the repair is worse than the disease.** The date lines were therefore re-verified by a separate parser written for this pass, and the result is at §3.

**THE SIX DEFECTS IN §5.6 WERE THE CLOSE'S OWN AND NOT THE REVIEW'S, AND THE CLOSE'S REASON FOR NOT REPAIRING THEM WAS, ON FIVE OF THE SIX, TOO STRICT.** The close held that repairing them would be "a choice between two readings, and choosing is a re-plan." For five of the six that is not true: there is one available value, it is derived, and leaving a figure that points at a chapter with no room in it is worse than the small edit. **The sixth is the exception and it is left exactly as the close left it, for the close's reason, which holds.**

## 2. THE ONE THAT WAS NOT REPAIRED, AND WHY THE CLOSE WAS RIGHT

**DEFECT 1 IS STILL OWED AND IS STILL UNREPAIRED, AND IT IS THE SAME DEFECT THE CLOSE RECORDED.**

`550` title, `:15`, `:137`, `:167` and `:169` all say **a hundred and forty-one days**. `550 − 400 = 150`, and `550`'s own date line says *One hundred and fifty days after the settlement*. The figure 141 is **the age of the leaf in years** and it has migrated into the days slot: it is right at `541` (`541 − 400 = 141`) and frozen at that value at `544` (144) and at `550` (150).

**AND THERE ARE TWO REPAIRS AND THEY MEAN DIFFERENT THINGS, WHICH IS THE CLOSE'S REASON AND IT HOLDS:**

1. **One hundred and fifty days** — a span of this town's recorded week, and the chapter's own date line says it.
2. **A hundred and forty-one years** — the first time in a hundred and forty-one years that anything has been written at the back of that book, which is the better sentence and reads as the deliberate echo of the leaf.

**THIS ONE IS NOT REPAIRED, FOR THREE REASONS THAT ARE STRONGER THAN THE REASON THE OTHER FIVE WERE.** It is in **the title of the volume's last chapter and in its last sentence at the same time**, so a repair is a change to how the volume ends and not a correction of a figure. It is **a choice between two readings, and choosing is a re-plan** — which is what the close said and what this repair agrees with. And **a repair in the fifth band would move a title that four instruments and one act have already read**, for a figure whose correct value depends on which of two true sentences the volume meant.

**IT IS OWED TO THE VOLUME 12 PLANNER, WHICH IS THE FIRST PHASE WITH THE VOLUME OPEN AND A READING TO SPEND ON IT, AND IT IS RECORDED IN `outline/volume-12-handoff.md` AS THE ONE FIGURE THAT IS STILL OWED.**

**AND THE CLOSE'S §5.7 CLAIM IS CONFIRMED RATHER THAN SOFTENED BY THIS REPAIR: the five defects that could be repaired were repaired in bodies and closing lines, and the one that survives is the one in the title, which is exactly what that failure says happens to a figure that lives in a title.**
