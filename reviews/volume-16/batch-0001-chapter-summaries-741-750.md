**741 — A TUESDAY, THE FEVER SIXTY-FIVE WEEKS AND THREE DAYS OLD, AND A DAY APPEARS AGAINST THE NINTH LINE OF A SHEET OF PRINTED PAPER BETWEEN THE SEVENTH HOUR YESTERDAY AND THE SEVENTH HOUR THIS MORNING.** The water is about twelve inches over the top step at the seventh hour, three days, and the man of forty-three gives five days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, which is the Sunday of this week, and says he has given you that Sunday every morning since Friday. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read to him in the open for the twentieth morning and says he has been listening to the water since the second hour and is not going to stand on a bank and be believed about it. A man of thirty-eight reads that line, gets about nine words in, and stops, because there is a day against that line and there was not one yesterday morning and he has read it every morning since the first day of the hundred and twenty-fourth week. He turns the sheet over and finds nine words printed on the back of it in the same small type as the line at the top of the other side: not there on a day is agreeing to it. He counts them out loud, and the count is nine. A woman of forty gives four things and refuses to make a total. A woman of sixty-four says, before anybody has asked her, that she is not going to be asked about that day, and that if she agrees to it then it goes against the other name on the same line, and she is not going to be the way that day gets to be true. A woman of forty-four says in four sentences that this is not a refusing, because a shut door is not the right of refusing and a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything, and that the day is going to be there in about four minutes either way. A man of thirty-eight says there is a second side to that thing and about nine people have had the first side in their hands thirty-one mornings running. A boy of thirteen gives four counts and says he does not know what the fourth one is yet. And a man of thirty-one says out loud that he wants the ninth line to have no day on it again by tomorrow morning, gives the working for a hundred and eighty-seven days first, says he has not got a way of doing it, and says that if he did not know what his wanting was until a man of fifty-four told nine people on a bank then nobody is going to be told he was right.

**742 — A WEDNESDAY, A FULL BANK FOR THE FIRST MORNING SINCE THE SUNDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOURTH WEEK, AND A BOY OF THIRTEEN SAYS OUT LOUD THAT A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR IN AN ASH HAS BEEN THERE A HUNDRED AND TWELVE DAYS AND HAS A DAY AGAINST HIS NAME AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ANYTHING IN ANY OF THEM.** The water is about sixteen inches, four days, and the man of forty-three gives four days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, which is the Sunday of this week. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-first morning and says that a man of thirty-eight read it yesterday morning with no day on it and read it this morning with one, and that those are two men saying one thing out loud and they do not agree, and that he has no way of choosing between them. A man of thirty-eight reads the line, the fourteen names and the nine words on the back, and says the rule is about the nine lines and not about the fourteen names, and that the ninth line has had a day against it since yesterday morning and that it is one day for two names. A boy of thirteen gives the figure nobody on that bank had: that a man of thirty-four has been in an ash a hundred and twelve days and the day against his name is not one of the hundred and twelve, it is a day and a half and it is not his. A man of sixty-one tells him in four sentences not to do that again this week, and says it is not because the boy is wrong, and gives no reason. A woman of sixty-four gives nine sentences and refuses the tenth, and the sixth is the finding: a day on a line does not want anybody to agree to it, and a day is a place, and a place can be written down whether anybody sat in it or not, and that is why she said on Monday she was not going to be asked about it, because if she agreed it would go against the other name too and neither of the other two is going to be asked. A man of thirty-one says he is right about all nine, and that he said on Monday he wanted a day taken off a line, and that it has got one, and that he has not got a way of taking it off and is not going to tell you he has.

**743 — A THURSDAY, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ SAYS OUT LOUD IN FOUR SENTENCES THAT HE HAS BEEN ASKED FOR A DAY HE CANNOT COME ON AND HAS NEVER BEEN ASKED FOR ONE HE CAN, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS HE IS NOT GOING TO READ THAT SHEET AGAIN THIS WEEK.** The bank holds at about sixteen inches for the fifth day and the man of forty-three gives three days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-second morning and gives his four sentences: one, that he has been asked for a day he cannot come on and has not been asked for one he can; two, that he does not know what day it is and is not going to become a man who gets told; three, that about nine of you have asked him the same question every morning for three weeks and not one of you has asked him whether he could come; and four, that this is not a complaint and nobody is going to be thanked. A woman of twenty-eight, who is not one of the nine, says the true of it in four sentences and that she has been in the hearing of all four of his sentences and of that every morning since Wednesday and has said nothing, and that she has been thinking about the chair for two days and is not going to say what she has been thinking. And a man of thirty-one says he is not going to read that sheet again this week, not in the open and not in the dark, and gives the shape of it before the want: he is a man who reads things to people, that is the whole of what he has got, about nine people have said out loud that he is the best instrument in four counties for being the one who asks, and if he reads that sheet at the ninth hour then about nine people on that bank have a man who reads things to them, and that man works whether he is standing there or not. He also says the want, which is not the want of Monday morning: he wants to be asked about one thing in this county. Not to be the one who asks. Asked.

**744 — A FRIDAY, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR WHO CANNOT READ PUTS A CASE IN FRONT OF AN ARGUMENT AND THEN SAYS THE ARGUMENT IN THREE SENTENCES, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR PUTS FOUR THINGS THAT ARE ABOUT A BANK AND NOT ABOUT A FIGURE.** The bank holds for the sixth day and the man of forty-three gives two days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, and says he is the only one of the nine who can be checked against yesterday and has not said why. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-third morning and says the case first: a man of thirty-four has been in an ash a hundred and thirteen days, his name is on a line with a day against it, he was not there on that day, there was nobody there to see it, and nobody has been able to ask him anything in any of the hundred and thirteen, and that line says he agreed. Then the argument, in three sentences, because he is fifty-four and has not got nine: that he has been asked for a day he cannot come on for twenty-three mornings and not one of the nine has asked him whether he could come, and a man of thirty-four has not been asked anything at all and a line has filled itself in, and a thing with a form under it stops being a person refusing and becomes a field somebody fills in. He says he is telling it by hand because he cannot read, and that he put the case before the argument on purpose. A woman of forty gives three names and one day and refuses to add it to anything. A woman of forty-four says in five sentences, and refuses the fifth, that a man of fifty-four has said the argument out loud and not one of the nine has asked him whether he would be a fifth thing about a room, which he asked for once and was given nothing, and that a shut door is not the right of refusing and a man who cannot read is not it and a person who cannot be asked has not refused anything. And a man of thirty-one says he is not going to fill anything in, not a line, not a day, not a name, not a space about as wide as a thumb, and that he wanted a day taken off a line on Monday and did not know that was what it was until a man of fifty-four told nine people on a bank.

**745 — A SATURDAY, ON WHICH THE FEVER WAS SIXTY-SIX WEEKS OLD, AND A SHEET OF PRINTED PAPER IS ON THAT COUNTER WITH THREE COLUMNS ON IT AND TWELVE WORDS AT THE TOP OF THE THIRD, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS THE WORD AMENDMENT OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE'S HEARING AND SAYS HE IS NOT GOING TO FILL IN ANY OF IT.** The bank holds for the seventh day and the man of forty-three gives one day to the next time about ninety steps can be walked and says he is not going to say the seventh day-name of the drift. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-fourth morning. A boy of thirteen gives three counts and refuses the fourth, and a man of sixty-one does not tell him to stop this week and does not tell him to go on either. Then about nine people see a sheet of printed paper on that counter which was not there on Thursday morning, and a man of thirty-eight who has read that sheet thirty-five times has never seen a third thing on it: three columns, and at the top of the third one, in the same small type, twelve words — a person who is not there on a day agrees to it — and he says out loud that the twelve are the nine words from the back of the sheet with three words in front of them and one of the nine printed *agrees* where the back of that sheet says *is agreeing*, and that there is still no column on that thing for a person stopping. A man of fifty-four says in four sentences that he cannot come on any day of any week and has held the gravel at the head of that drain for nineteen years and is not going to stop holding it so that a piece of printed paper can have him on a day, and that if he does not come he has agreed to all nine, and that a thing which cannot tell the difference between a person who agreed and a person who was not there will get it wrong about everybody on that line. **746 — A SUNDAY, THE FEVER SIXTY-SIX WEEKS AND ONE DAY OLD, AND THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY OF THE STEPS IN THE NIGHT FOR THE TENTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK, AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE GO DOWN ABOUT NINETY STEPS AT THE FIFTH HOUR BECAUSE A KEEPING IS DONE BEFORE THE LIGHT.** A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-fifth morning and says that water came off in the night and he has been listening to it since the second hour because he does not sleep through it. A man of thirty-eight reads the line, the names and the twelve words, and says there is still no fourth column and nothing in any of the three after two mornings, and that he is not going to say that means anything. Then about nine people go down, and a man of thirty-four bailed for about an hour and said nothing about it, and a man of fifty-four went down at his own pace and nobody helped him. And a man of thirty-eight said what he had found, which was not what anybody went down for: the ninth room is not being kept and has not been kept since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, there are two worn places in the floor of that room and one of them is his and the other is about as deep as the thickness of his own finger, and there is about a thumb's width of light under the eighth door-stone, and he does not know what it is. A woman of sixty-four says a man of thirty-one refusing a piece of paper is not what kept that room and is not what unkept it. A woman of sixty-four says again that nothing that has happened this week pays back one thing a day for nineteen years, and that she is not going to be thanked for it. And a man of thirty-one says what his refusing a piece of paper did, which was nothing: it did not keep the room, it did not put a second pair of hands in that passage, it did not ask a man of thirty-four whether he came on his day, and it did not ask a man of fifty-four whether he could come on any of the nine.

**747 — THE SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHTH WEEK, A MONDAY, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR SAYS OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE'S HEARING THAT THERE IS NO RULE IN THIS COUNTY THAT SAYS A THING WHICH CANNOT BE ASKED CAN STOP BEING ASKED ABOUT.** The water is about four inches, one day, and the man of forty-three gives seven days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, which is the Monday of the hundred and twenty-ninth week, and says he said five sentences this morning and is not going to say the extra one out loud. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the twenty-sixth morning and says that the sheet has been on that counter three mornings and nothing has gone into any of the three, and that what he has to say about it is a figure of hands and not of columns because he cannot read. A man of thirty-eight reads the line, the names and the twelve words, and says a man of fifty-four has told nine people what he has seen at a thing he cannot read and that his figure for it is better than his own. And a boy of thirteen says a figure of three out loud for the first time in a week, having been told on Wednesday not to, and a man of sixty-one does not tell him to stop and does not tell him to go on, and says that he has now said the same sentence twice.

**748 — THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, A TUESDAY, AND ABOUT NINE HOUSES IN A VILLAGE FOUR MILES UP A CART ROAD ARE DOING THREE THINGS WITHOUT BEING ASKED TO BY ANYBODY.** The water is about eight inches, two days, and the man of forty-three says the next time about ninety steps can be walked is in six days, the Monday of this week. A woman of forty says a covenant is two people who have both said a thing out loud and can both be asked about it, and that a man of fifty-four asking the same question every morning for twenty-seven mornings while nobody asks him one back is not that, and refuses to make one figure of a morning and a morning and nine houses. **749 — THE SECOND DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, A WEDNESDAY, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAYS OUT LOUD THAT THERE ARE NINE OF THESE THINGS IN THIS COUNTY AND NONE OF THEM IS BEING KEPT THIS WEEK AND THAT A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYING A WORD OUT LOUD ON SATURDAY DID NOTHING TO ANY OF THE FIVE HE NAMED.** The water is about twelve inches, three days, and the man of forty-three gives five days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, the Monday of this week. **750 — THE THIRD DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, A THURSDAY, HIS FIVE HUNDREDTH MORNING, A FULL BANK AGAIN AT ABOUT SIXTEEN INCHES OVER THE TOP STEP, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR ASKS OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE'S HEARING WHETHER THE PEOPLE OF THIS COUNTY CAN WRITE A RULE THAT DOES NOT NEED ONE OF THEM TO BE THE ONE WHO WROTE IT, AND NOBODY ANSWERS.** The man of forty-three gives four days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked, the Monday of this week, and says a bank that is full on the fourth day comes off on the eighth and that he said it on Sunday of last week and is going to leave it there.
