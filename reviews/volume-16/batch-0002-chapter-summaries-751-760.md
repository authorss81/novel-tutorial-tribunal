**751 — A FRIDAY, THE FOURTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND FIRST MORNING, AND A FULL BANK ON THE FIFTH MORNING OF IT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT DOES NOT READ THE SHEET OUT LOUD FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ABOUT NINE DAYS.** The water is about sixteen inches, five days, and the man of forty-three gives three days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read to him for the thirtieth morning and says nobody on that bank has asked him a question in about nine days and neither has he. **A man of thirty-eight does not read the sheet and gives five sentences for it: he will read it again when somebody on that bank asks him to in front of about nine people, nobody has, and he is not going to ask.** Then four leaves of paper are on that counter that were not there on Thursday morning, carried down that cart road on the bread, addressed to nobody. A woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs says she was not asked to bring them, that there is no column on them and nothing to fill in, that it is not a form, and that she cannot tell anybody what a hearing is. A woman of forty says she is not going to add the sheet and the leaves together. A man of about fifty-two reads four lines and refuses the fifth because he has asked four times in a fortnight what a mark is worth as a figure and nobody answered him. **A woman of twenty-eight says in four sentences that a form asks a person and a finding is a figure of an argument and not a figure of anybody, and that nobody in four counties can be sent for about what nine people said in a room they were not in, and that this is the first morning in about two weeks that her own sentence about a second time being a rule is about something other than that counter.** A man of thirty-one says he wants somebody on that bank asked whether they were in that room and not him, and says the word want out loud for the first time, and says he has been standing in one place about two weeks and calling it work.

**752 — A SATURDAY, ON WHICH THE FEVER WAS SIXTY-SEVEN WEEKS OLD, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT READS FOUR LEAVES OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN BECAUSE ABOUT NINE PEOPLE SAID NOTHING AT ALL.** The water is about sixteen inches, six days, and the man of forty-three gives two days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-first morning and says out loud that the reason it was read was not him, because about nine people said nothing. The man of thirty-eight reads all four leaves and reads the fifth thing on them, which is that nine people heard about a line and did not object, and says there is no name on any of the nine and no room on any of the four leaves. **A man of sixty-one says what a minute is and what it is not in six sentences, and the sixth is that he is not going to write nine on the leaf after the last head because nine is not a thing he saw, and that a minute about nine people who did not object is a figure of an argument the nine were not in the middle of.** Then a woman of twenty-nine says one word from a reed about twenty feet off that drain and it is the first anybody has heard from her in about eighty days, and nobody repeated it back. A boy of thirteen carries four counts and says he is not going to carry a fifth because he does not know what one word out of a reed is a count of. A man of thirty-one says he wants the woman who said that word asked what word it was and not him, and says he is not going to walk a hundred yards and stand over her, and says the mistake is that a room four hundred miles off became a room a hundred yards off on the same morning and about nine people asked her a question by not asking her anything. A woman of forty-four gives four things that have not happened this week and gives them as four.

**753 — A SUNDAY, THE SIXTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR COMES DOWN THAT BANK FROM ABOUT TWO DAYS OFF A ROAD.** The water is about sixteen inches, seven days and the man of forty-three gives one day. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-second morning. **A man of thirty-eight says in three sentences that he carries a board in the inside of his coat since the last day of the hundred and twenty-first week, that he is not going to carry a fifth thing out of a coat and open it in front of about nine people, and that the reason is that a thing only about four of you are waiting for is a thing about four people and not about nine.** Then a woman of thirty-four who walked about two days and cannot be sent for about says she was one of the nine, that six were in that room and three came in at the end and did not sit down, that nobody asked her anything on that day and the one thing she said was about a road and is not on those four leaves, and that she is not going to say what it was about. **She says the minute is not false and is not a true account of what happened in that room.** Then a man of sixty-one writes six on the leaf after the last head, which is the second number he has written in eleven years, and says he does not believe anybody and has written the number down, and refuses the working for it three times and gives a fourth reason. A man of thirty-one says the want is that the three who came in at the end and did not sit down be asked, and says the same sentence he said on Friday, and says the cost is that a man of sixty-one gave up eleven years on the word of a stranger.

**754 — THE SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-NINTH WEEK, A MONDAY, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTH MORNING, AND THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY OF THE STEPS IN THE NIGHT FOR THE ELEVENTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK.** The man of forty-three gives the reading at about the sixth hour and then gives the day it was on, and says it is one day later every cycle and the cycles are eight days apart, and that he is not going to work out in front of about nine people why they are eight days apart because he has not found out. About nine people go down that bank at about the fifth hour before the light because a keeping is done before the light, and a man of thirty-eight carries the lamp and it is his. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-third morning and says it is the last one this week. **A woman of sixty-four says out loud that she was in that room, on the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week, that she was asked whether she could attend and said she would and was told she could not, and refuses to say the reason and asks not to be asked twice, and gives the working both ways.** A woman of forty says she is not going to add the three instruments on that counter together. A man of thirty-one says he wants a man of thirty-eight to ask her why and says he is not going to ask him to do it, and says that wanting to arrange for about nine people to be asked is not a want but a job he has made up and nobody in this county can be sent for about it. A woman of twenty-eight says in five sentences that the last four of his wants have been about other people and not about this county.

**755 — THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, A TUESDAY, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTH MORNING, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT IS ASKED FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ABOUT THREE WEEKS TO READ SOMETHING OUT LOUD AND SAYS YES.** The water is about four inches, one day, and the man of forty-three gives seven days to the next time about ninety steps can be walked. A man of fifty-four asks for the ninth line and a man of thirty-eight reads it and says a man of fifty-four told him about four of the nine had not thought about the asking and about four had, and that three weeks of asking is not the same thing as being asked. **A man of sixty-one says he wants that hearing to stand and gives the count first, and says it is about a road and about four houses that cannot come on a day and is not about the woman in the reed or the man in the ash or the woman of sixty-four.** A man of thirty-one says he wants a man of sixty-one asked whether he believes a woman of thirty-four, and says out loud that this is the same sentence he said on Friday morning, and says the last four of his wants have been about other people. A boy of thirteen carries five counts and gives back two of them that were wrong, and says a man in an ash had been there a hundred and twenty-two days and he had said a hundred and twenty-four, and that a woman in a reed had been there a hundred and twenty-four and he had said a hundred and twenty-six, and that the second one is worse because he rounded up and he does not round anything.

**756 — A WEDNESDAY, THE SECOND DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR IS STILL STANDING AT THE END OF THAT BANK AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING.** The water is about eight inches, two days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-fifth morning and a man of thirty-eight reads it because he was asked. **The woman of thirty-four says in four sentences that she has been standing there about an hour and nobody has asked her a question and nobody is going to, and that she said on Sunday she would not be here tomorrow because the water was coming off and the water came off and she is here, and that the last of the four is what she would like said about her.** Then a woman of forty-four comes down four miles and gives three things out of nine and refuses the other six, and says **about four houses in that village have stopped sending the second of their two carts down that road since Friday because of a day nobody on that bank has seen written down**, and that one of the nine put its own name on its own paper with a day against it in its own hand. A man of thirty-one says he wants to walk those four miles and read the paper to about four houses and says he is not going to, and gives the reason: about nine people in four counties would then have a man of thirty-one going up a road saying a thing about a day nobody has seen written down. A boy of thirteen says he is going to carry six on Friday and tells about nine of them that morning so that about four cannot say they did not know.

**757 — A THURSDAY, THE THIRD DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY READS THE FIFTH LINE OF A HEARING OUT LOUD IN THE OPEN FOR THE FIRST TIME IN HER LIFE AND IS BAD AT IT AND SAYS SO FIRST.** The water is about twelve inches, three days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-sixth morning and says he is not going to call asking three days running a habit. **A woman of forty reads three things off the fourth line of that paper: that a finding is made in the absence of the persons heard about, that their absence is not to be read as consent, and that it is held open for nine days for any person heard about to come forward — and says before she starts that she is going to be bad at it, and stops twice, and refuses to read the rest.** Then a man of thirty-one says he wants the four leaves to stop being on that counter, all four of them, and says he is not going to move them and is not going to ask anybody to move them, and says out loud so that a woman of thirty-four at the end of that bank can hear him say it, and says there is one woman in this county on that line and she is about twenty feet off the edge of that drain. A man of sixty-one says a figure of nine days is a figure of a paper four hundred miles off and not a figure of a woman who has not got the paper, and says a man of sixty-one is not going to help with taking them off that counter either. A boy of thirteen says he is not going to carry a figure that is not a day-count.

**758 — A FRIDAY, THE FOURTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, AND ABOUT FOUR OF THE NINE WHO COME UP THAT BANK CAME UP IT TO SAY WHAT A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE IS.** The bank is full again at about sixteen inches, four days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-seventh morning and says about four of the people up there this morning came to say what a man of thirty-one is and not to say what he is. **A man of sixty-one counts four things said at him at once from four directions before he lets any of them finish — you are the one who wrote it, you are the one who should have refused it, you should say yes to the thing asked on the Wednesday, you should go up that cart road — and says he is not going to write any of the four on the leaf after the last head, and that he has written two numbers in eleven years and neither of those two was a person.** Then a man of thirty-one says he is not going to decide which of about four of them he is going to answer first, and gives the reason that picking is the instrument, and says **that what he called a cost on the Friday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week was not a cost, it was a trade, and he has been calling it a cost for about four days because a cost is a word he knows how to use.** A woman of twenty-eight says in six sentences that people who came up a bank to say what a man is did not come up a bank to ask a man anything, and that about four of them want him to go up four miles and about four want him to say yes, and that neither of those is a want about this county and both are a want about who gets to use him, and refuses a seventh sentence.

**759 — A SATURDAY, ON WHICH THE FEVER WAS SIXTY-EIGHT WEEKS OLD, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO ASKED FOR THE FIFTH TIME WHAT A MARK IS WORTH AS A FIGURE.** The bank is still full at about sixteen inches, five days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-eighth morning and says he is going to stop putting a figure on how long he has been waiting because about four of them would want one. A man of thirty-eight says he is not going to read anything while a woman walks down that bank. **A man of about fifty-two says he asked it first, and says out loud that a mark is a thing a person says, and that he has been the fifth line of two sheets of deal for eleven years and there is no fourth thing that comes before a fifth thing, and that he has been able to be checked against the four lines under him every morning for eleven years, and that that is the whole of what a mark is for.** A woman of thirty-four gives him a figure of nothing with the working, and refuses it as an answer in the next sentence, because a woman of thirty-four standing on that bank is not a clerk and she did not come two days to be one. Then a woman of twenty-nine in a reed says two words, and they are that he is right, and nobody repeated them back. A man of thirty-one says he wants to be able to say what he is out loud on that bank and has not been able to since the Friday of the hundred and twenty-eighth week, and says being asked what you are is not a request and there is no column for it. A woman of forty says she is not going to repeat either of those words back to either of the people who said them.

**760 — A SUNDAY, THE SIXTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND TENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WALKS DOWN THAT BANK TO THE WATER AND IS NOT GOING TO BE ASKED ANYTHING ON HER WAY.** The bank is still full at about sixteen inches, six days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the thirty-ninth morning and says he is going to be here Monday and it will be forty. A man of thirty-eight says a woman is going to walk down that bank and about nine of them are going to watch her do it, and that he is not going to read anything out loud while she does it. **The woman of thirty-four says from about four feet into the water that nine of them watched her walk about two hundred yards and nobody asked her one thing, and that three men and a woman have all said what they want about this county in about six days while she has said what she wants about a room and about two hundred yards of water.** Then a man of thirty-one says he is not going to say what he is, and gives the reason in his own mouth: he has been on that bank for two hundred and six days being the thing about four people come up to, and there is a woman standing in about four feet of water who came two days and has been looked at for six and asked nothing, and if he says a thing about what he is this morning then about nine of them will have it and she will not. **And he says that the reason he gave on Friday for not picking which of about four people to answer first was also that he did not know how to say what he is, and that he put a sentence in front of it that about four of them could carry and called it a principle.** A woman of forty-four puts two things on that counter as two and refuses to give a third. The four leaves were still on that counter on that Sunday morning with nothing in any column of any of them.
