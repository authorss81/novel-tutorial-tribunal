**761 — A MONDAY, THE SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTIETH WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR IS NOT AT THE END OF THAT BANK, AND NOBODY ON IT SAYS SO FOR ABOUT A MINUTE.** The water is about sixteen inches over the top step on the seventh morning of it and the man of forty-three gives three things and one day to the next time about ninety steps can be walked. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read to him for the fortieth morning and says he said on Friday that he was going to stop putting a figure on how long he has waited and he has not stopped. **The man of thirty-eight gives the count of that sheet, fifty-one, with the working, and says about four of you know the difference between being asked to read and not being asked and about nine of you do not, and that he has carried a board in the inside of this coat since the Monday of the hundred and twenty-first week and is not going to open it on a Monday morning when one of the about four of you is not standing on that bank at all.** A woman of forty-four who keeps a book of chairs gives the two counts off that counter, a printed sheet seventeen mornings and four leaves eleven, and refuses a third thing about it because a woman was not at the end of that bank and that is not a thing about paper. **A woman of forty says she has asked for a day against a name for eleven years and has never once asked for a day against nothing, and that there are about nine days on that paper against nothing, and that she noticed it on Friday and has not worked out what she noticed.** A man of sixty-one gives four counts and adds none of them to any other. **A man of thirty-one says he wants about nine of them to go back to whatever they were doing before Friday, and says the mistake is his own: he said a sentence about a woman out loud before he had found out she was not there to hear it. He says the figure of about four of them did not change when one of them was not there and that he cannot use it for anything.**

**762 — A TUESDAY, THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND TWELFTH MORNING, AND THE WATER COMES OFF ALL NINETY OF THE STEPS FOR THE TWELFTH TIME SINCE THE SATURDAY OF THE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH WEEK, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO ASKS THE ONE QUESTION NOBODY ON THAT BANK HAS ASKED.** The man of forty-three gives the hour and the count and the day of the week, and says he said the whole of the day-names out loud eight mornings ago and is not going to say it again, and that he gave a figure for why the cycles run eight days apart and took it back and has not found out. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-first morning and says a man of thirty-eight read it because he was asked and that is not the reason. **The man of thirty-eight says the reason is that if he has to be asked every morning then the asking is a job a man of fifty-four is doing for him, and he is not doing a job for him.** Then **a man of about fifty-two reads the fourth line of that hearing out loud twice, badly, and asks: NINE DAYS FROM WHAT. He gives the reason out of his own house and it is the first time in eleven years: there is no fourth thing that comes before a fifth thing, and every morning he can be checked against the four lines under him, and a fifth thing that does not say where it starts from is not a fifth thing he knows what to do with. He says he is not going to make this the sixth time he has asked what a mark is worth.** A man of sixty-one gives the four counts and says out loud, twice, that he wants that hearing to stand, and says it is about a road four miles up and about four houses and not about the woman in the reed or the man in the ash or the woman of sixty-four. **A man of thirty-one says the mistake is nine mornings old: the day of that hearing has been on that counter since Friday morning and he has never once put it against a figure of nine days.**

**763 — A WEDNESDAY, THE SECOND DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTEENTH MORNING, AND A BOY OF THIRTEEN DOES A SUBTRACTION OUT LOUD ON A BANK AND GETS TWO ANSWERS A DAY APART AND GIVES BOTH OF THEM.** The water is about four inches, one day, and the man of forty-three gives seven days. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-second morning and says he has stopped saying that he is going to stop putting a figure on the waiting and that is not the same thing as stopping. **A boy of thirteen gives his own count, a hundred and ten days, with the working, says he wants a piece of chalk, and counts nine days out loud twice from the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. Counting the day it was held as the first, the ninth day is the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week. Counting from the day after, it is the Friday of it. He says he has done it twice on his fingers and both are right, and that he is not going to sit there and look like he knows which one it is, and that about four of them are going to want him to say one of them out loud so that the number stops moving.** **A man of sixty-one says he is not going to write either of them down, and gives the reason: a day is neither a head nor a cart, and if he writes one of the two down then in about half a year somebody in this county will say that is the day and he will not be able to say it is not.** The man of thirty-eight gives his count and says he is not going to be the one who says which of the two it is. A woman of forty says she is not going to add any of the three things on that bank together. **A man of thirty-one says he wanted to be the man who said those two days out loud and gave it up to a boy of thirteen with a piece of chalk, and says he is not going to come in now and say he would have got it right.**

**764 — A THURSDAY, THE THIRD DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND FOURTEENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR SAYS SHE HAS BEEN ASKED NOTHING FOR THREE WEEKS AND HAS NOT ASKED, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS WHAT SHE WANTS.** The water is about eight inches, two days, and the man of forty-three gives the next off the other way round from the morning before and says he is not going to go back and tidy it up for about nine of them. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-third morning and says that this morning he asked out loud for the first time, and that about four of you heard it and about nine of you were standing somewhere else, and that this is not brave. **A woman of twenty-nine in a reed about twenty feet off that edge says three words, and they are that somebody should ask a clerk, and nobody repeated them back to her and nobody asked her for a fourth and nobody asked her what a clerk is.** A woman of sixty-four says a boy of thirteen used a day she gave, says she is not going to say whether he got it right, and says she has been asked nothing about that room for about three weeks and wants it said out loud in about nine people's hearing that she has been asked nothing, by her and not by about four of them, and that asking not to be asked twice is not the same as not asking for anything. **A woman of forty says what she wants for the first time in eleven years: a day with nothing against it, not a name and not a house and not a cart, because she has never once been asked what she would do if a day turned up with nothing on it. She refuses to be the person who goes and finds out which of two days a man of sixty-one will not write down.** A man of thirty-one says the mistake is about a minute long, which is the smallest he has made in two hundred and ten days: he watched about nine of them hold a question he asked for, and a boy of thirteen answered it.

**765 — A FRIDAY, THE FOURTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTEENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE BAKEHOUSE FOUR MILES UP THAT CART ROAD COMES DOWN ON THE BREAD CART AND GIVES FIFTEEN OR FOURTEEN.** The water is about twelve inches, three days, and the man of forty-three says he is not going to stop giving the four-inches-a-day figure on the morning it comes out right. **A woman of forty-four says she has come down because the bread comes down on a Friday and she is on the bread cart, gives that as the whole of the reason before anybody can say she came about a man of thirty-one, and gives a figure of fifteen or fourteen, worked both ways from the day the four leaves came down on her bread. She says she counts days the way she counts loaves and both are right and she cannot get at which one it is, and that nobody in this county can be sent for about it.** **She then names the one new place of the band and its distance out loud: the second cart of about four houses turns off at a low gate about two hundred yards below her oven, and that gate is three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road from this landing, and it has never been said out loud in about nine people's hearing in nineteen years. She says those four houses did not stop because a hearing went against them, and they stopped because there is a day and nobody will tell them what day it is, and a cart cannot turn on a day nobody has said.** A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-fourth morning. A man of thirty-eight says he cannot check fifteen or fourteen against the four lines under him and is not going to stand up and pick one. **A man of thirty-one says he wants to walk four miles up that road and stand at that gate and say a day out loud to about four houses, and says he is not going to, and says the reason he gave about nine days ago was about about nine of them and was not the reason. The reason is that if he says a day then the thing that has happened is that a man of thirty-one said a day, and it is a day he got off a piece of bread.**

**766 — A SATURDAY, THE FIFTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND SIXTEENTH MORNING, ON WHICH THE FEVER WAS SIXTY-NINE WEEKS OLD, AND THE BANK IS FULL AGAIN, AND A WOMAN OF FORTY SAYS WHAT JOB A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE HAS BEEN STANDING IN.** The water is about sixteen inches, four days, and the man of forty-three gives the next off in four days, which is the Wednesday of next week, and says he is not going to say it a fourth time to about four of you who have it written down wrong. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-fifth morning and the man of thirty-eight says yes out loud before he is asked, because a man of fifty-four has been asking out loud for four mornings and about nine of them have got used to it, and if he has to be asked every morning then the asking is a job. **A woman of forty says, in about nine people's hearing, that a man of thirty-one standing on a bank for two hundred and twelve days being the thing about four people come up to is a job and not a position, and that about four of them know it and about nine of them have been treating it as a position, and that he told her himself a word said nine times in nine days in front of about nine people is not a want any more but a habit, and went on doing it.** A woman of forty-four gives the fourth thing about that counter for the first time in six mornings: the two ninth days are the Thursday of the hundred and twenty-seventh week or the Friday of it, and she has written both on the back of her own hand and neither is in a book of chairs. A man of sixty-one says a day in a book is a day that stops being a day and becomes a line. **A man of thirty-one says the want is about himself for the first time in about eleven days: he wants somebody on that bank to ask him for a thing. A woman of forty tells him what shape that is before he has finished the sentence and he says it back, because it is the shape of the thing on that counter, and about nine of them can be here for it.**

**767 — A SUNDAY, THE SIXTH DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO WALKED TWO DAYS WALKS UP ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YARDS OF THAT BANK AGAIN AND SAYS SHE DID NOT KNOW THE DAYS WERE BEING COUNTED.** The water is about sixteen inches, five days, and the man of forty-three gives three things and says he is not going to add a fourth because a woman was on that bank on Friday and was not on it yesterday and is on it today. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-sixth morning. **Nobody walked down with her and nobody had been asked to and nobody offered, and about two hundred yards of that bank was walked by one person from the water at the bottom of it on a Sunday morning with a full water. She says she did it in one go this time and is not going to tell anybody what it cost, that she was down there six mornings, that she did not go home and did not go off up that water and did not ask anybody for anything. Then the one thing: I DID NOT KNOW THE DAYS WERE BEING COUNTED. Nobody told her there were about nine days on that paper, nobody told her there was a figure of days at all, and she came two days to say one sentence about a room and said it on the Sunday of a fortnight ago and nobody asked her a thing for six days after that. She says she is not going to say anybody in this county should have told her, because she does not know who would have.** A woman of forty-four asks her one question, which is not about the room, and says out loud why she had not asked one for six mornings, and says about four of them were waiting for her to ask her something about that room and she did not want to be a second person doing that about a thing she had already said. **A woman of forty says she is not going to take what she said as the day she asked for on Thursday, because a thing that was done to her is not a thing she asked for, and is not withdrawing the want either.** The man of thirty-eight will not open the coat because there is a woman standing on that bank who has just said a thing about herself. **A man of thirty-one says he wanted to be on that bank when she came up, and says it out loud first, before he had decided to, and says the woman who walked two days was never one of the about four and he did not notice that until Friday morning.**

**768 — A MONDAY, THE SEVENTH AND LAST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTEENTH MORNING, AND A WOMAN OF SIXTY-FOUR WHO WAS IN THAT ROOM AND WAS TOLD SHE COULD NOT ATTEND ASKS FOR ONE THING, WHICH IS A DAY TO BE SAID OUT LOUD EVERY MORNING THE WAY THE WATER IS.** The water is about sixteen inches, six days, and the man of forty-three gives one day and says about four of you have written Tuesday beside it twice and he is not going to say it a fourth time. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-seventh morning and says he was not thanked for asking out loud because nobody has thanked him for asking at all. **A woman of sixty-four says everybody on that bank stopped and nobody had stopped for her in about three weeks, and asks for one thing: that the day of that hearing be said out loud on that bank every morning. The Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. She gives the reason: she has been in this county nineteen years and has watched about nine of them get up every morning about one inch of water, and a day is worth an inch, and she is not asking for a column and not asking for a name and not asking anybody to write it down anywhere.** A man of thirty-eight says he will read a day out loud every morning if he is asked and will not say why if nobody asks, and that thirty-two notches cut in the edge of a board is a bigger thing than a day. A woman of forty-four gives the counts and the fourth thing again. A man of sixty-one says he will not write a day down for her. **A man of thirty-one says the day, and says he is not going to promise tomorrow because he cannot be sent for about tomorrow and neither can about nine of them, and that a thing one man promises on a Monday morning is a thing about one man and not about the Wednesday of the hundred and twenty-sixth week. He says the cost is that he was one of the about four who had been keeping off her and did not know he was until Saturday morning.**

**769 — A TUESDAY, THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SECOND WEEK, HIS FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH MORNING, AND A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAYS HE IS GOING UP THAT CART ROAD TOMORROW, AND A WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE BAKEHOUSE SAYS SHE IS GOING TOO, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE SAYS HE WORKED OUT WHICH OF ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE IS WHICH AND IS NOT GOING TO SAY IT.** The water is about sixteen inches, seven days, and the man of forty-three gives one day and says that will be the thirteenth time since the Saturday of the hundred and nineteenth week, and that the last eight of the thirteen are eight days apart one from the next and he is not going to work out the first five in front of about nine of you because he has not found out. A man of fifty-four has the ninth line read for the forty-eighth morning and says about four of you have started asking for the day and about nine of you have not. **A man of sixty-one says he is going up tomorrow on the bread cart to stand at a low gate about two hundred yards below an oven, at three miles and six hundred yards up that cart road, and to ask about four houses which day their second cart can come down, and that he has not decided what he is going to say when he gets there. He says a man of thirty-one saying he is not going is not an answer to him.** **A woman of forty-four says she is going up as well and it is her third time and a day, and that a fortnight is up and she is not going to pretend she kept to it out of manners, and that she is going because it is her oven and her road and the bread goes up on a Wednesday. She says a man of sixty-one is going up on the same cart because a man of thirty-one would not, and that about four of you have been standing there about nine days waiting for somebody to go, and that she has not gone up because he did not.** A woman of forty-four refuses to ask either of them to bring her back a day, because then it is a thing about a man of sixty-one and not about a day. **A man of thirty-one says he worked out on Saturday which of about four people who came up on Friday is which and is not going to say it, and gives the reason: saying it would make about four of them start being the kind he said and about nine of them start being the kind he did not say, and that is two figures of people made out of one mouth before breakfast.**
