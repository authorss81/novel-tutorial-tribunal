# Review repair — volume 21 band 4 (`1031`–`1040`)

Source: `logs/batch-0004.review.log`. This is the record of what was changed in the
chapters and in state after that review. Band 4 was not restarted. No plot was
changed. Nothing was resolved that the review said was not ours to resolve.

## Fixed in the chapters

### A paragraph was byte-identical in nine chapters — fixed

The paragraph beginning *"Nothing on that landing was pulled, turned over, weighed in
the hand…"* was byte-identical in `1032`–`1040` and differed by two clauses in
`1031`, so it read across eleven consecutive chapters. `outline/series.md` and
`AGENTS.md` both forbid duplicated paragraphs. All ten were rewritten in their own
words, keeping the facts each one asserts: nothing pulled, turned, weighed, read out,
carried inside, put back or used; no hand laid on the ash to take anything off it, then
or afterwards; nothing named; nothing said out loud about what the wood is.

Two details changed deliberately while rewriting:

- The clause *"and nobody counted anything"* was dropped from `1032` and `1037`,
  because a man of thirty-eight counts in `1032` and a boy of thirteen counts in
  `1037`, and an absolute figure in the middle of a chapter that counts is a
  contradiction a careful reader will find.
- `1035`'s version names what was *not* named — the chair, the two pieces of ash, the
  iron in the fortieth step — because that chapter is the one about a chair nobody
  owns.

A paragraph-boundary sweep is too coarse for its own finding, and a second pass found
two longer verbatim runs *inside* paragraphs that the first sweep could not see:

- the description of the two pieces of ash, about 253 characters, word for word in all
  ten chapters;
- the core of Corvin Tace's account of the sweep — the fortieth step gone round and
  not stood on, the chair gone round and not sat on, the iron untouched, the broom not
  put near the ash — about 60 words, in all ten.

Both were rewritten in each of the ten chapters, keeping every fact: a forearm long,
split down the middle the way wood splits when it is meant to, thick end left as it
came apart, a saw mark on the square end of the second, two shallow notches in the
flat with one of the two run out of the side. What changes between chapters is the eye
doing the describing, not the objects. `1035` had kept the old stock opening for its
first two sentences and was rewritten in that pass.

Swept again: no byte-identical paragraph of 120 characters or more remains anywhere in
`1031`–`1040`. Two remain elsewhere in the volume and are outside this band — the same
stock opening across `1001`–`1009`, and a sweep paragraph at `1012:101`/`1028:91`.

### The lead was never named in his own volume — fixed

`Ilyan` appeared on exactly one line in each of the ten chapters, always the
third-person descriptor line. In the prose he was *the man of thirty-one*, thirty times
across the band, and never a speaker tag. `outline/volume-21.md` §7 requires that
"Ilyan is named in every chapter." He is now named in the prose of all ten, three to
five times each: in the arrival transition, in another character's line where the
scene allows it — Simon Rook's *"He said it to Ilyan and not to the landing"*
(`1032`), Sena Dorr's *"She said it to Ilyan and not to the landing"* (`1033`) — and
in the closing lines where the man acts.

Wenna Pyle still does not say his name. The name that travelled is his, she says so
by implication, and she has said she will not say it back to the man who said it or to
the man it is for. That refusal is intact.

### Three logic breaks in the order of the mornings — fixed

The house convention of this landing is that the day's figures open the chapter and
the day's visitors follow in the order the scene needs, which puts a sixth-hour
visitor after a seventh-hour report. That convention is forty chapters old and was
left alone. Three breaks inside it were not convention, they were impossibility, and
they were repaired:

- `1032`: Simon Rook, arriving at the tenth hour, said *"He said it to the man of
  thirty-one"* to a man who had not yet come up the bank. Ilyan's block was moved
  above Rook's.
- `1035`: the same, in the transition paragraph. Ilyan's block was moved above Rook's.
- `1033`: Ilyan's arrival was narrated twice, once at line 23 and again at line 67
  with the same figures in it. The second was rewritten so that it is the same man
  still standing on the second step where she asked him, and Rook's tenth hour now
  falls after Ilyan's ninth.

### Every paragraph over a thousand characters — split

The longest ran to 1,338 characters. Eighteen paragraphs in the band were over a
thousand; all eighteen were split at a sentence or speech beat. The longest paragraph
in the band is now 986.

The best material in the band was not touched for content. Simon Rook's refusal to put
his hand on that ash is intact word for word, and *"my hand cannot be asked to stop"*
survives the split.

### Descriptor paragraphs that restated the speech beneath them — trimmed

The house form is a third-person line that says who a person is, what they can and
cannot do, and what they said that morning. Where that line repeated the speech that
follows it — *"and said on the fourth day of the hundred and sixty-ninth week that a
voice had come down that road in the wind…"* followed immediately by the woman saying
that — the redundant half was cut. Seven were trimmed, in `1031`, `1032`, `1033`,
`1034`, `1035`, `1036` and `1038`. The identity half of each is unchanged, including
the ages, the trades, the disabilities and the three hundred yards.

### A break of the same class in the chapter beside them — fixed

`1034:27`: Barnaby Crove, at the sixth hour, reported that a man who cannot see well
*had just* put his hand out over the strip and had not put it on. That act is narrated
three hundred words later, at the tenth hour. Same defect as the three above, and it
survived the first pass. Barnaby now says the man is going to do it before the light is
off it, because he has done it every morning of that week, and that he has not heard a
sound of it any of the times — which keeps his function in the chapter, which is that
he does not ask.

## A second pass, after a review of this repair

A reviewer was sent over the first pass and found real defects. They are recorded here
rather than quietly absorbed, because the first version of this file published figures
that were wrong:

- the sweep in the section above was paragraph-level and so could not see the two
  verbatim runs *inside* paragraphs. Both are now rewritten, and `1035` had in fact
  never been rewritten at all.
- a `nobody` figure quoted here was measured over eleven chapters rather than ten.
- the list of split paragraphs named thirteen chapters and omitted `1036`, while the
  heading said sixteen.
- `state/continuity.md` claimed three of the ten pages carry three `four hundred and …`
  mentions. The honest statement is the one now there: each page carries the Kell
  figure twice, and the other `four hundred and …` phrases are his count of wrong
  things.
- `state/batch-summary.md` called `1038` the last morning of the band, said a question
  about the name is asked in words on five of the ten when it is nine, and put the
  ordinary form on four mornings when two of its four trades fall on the same one.
- `state/character-state.md` said Simon Rook asked a question on every morning of the
  band; he asks on nine. It said Sena Dorr came up three times when she comes up four,
  and that she was refused by the shape of the morning on `1033`, when she is refused
  by a person in a mouth.
- `state/open-threads.md` gave Barnaby Crove four hundred and eighty-six mornings of
  asking. That is Ilyan's figure in the county of Kell. Barnaby's own count is three
  hundred and nineteenth.

The reviewer also found the `1034:27` continuity break described above, and two older
duplicates outside this band, which were left alone.

## Not fixed, and why

- **The manuscript is 190 chapters past the ending its own outline specifies.**
  `outline/series.md` gives approximately 850 chapters; `outline/ending.md` ends at 850
  and that chapter is finished on disk. Volumes 18–21 rest on no authority but
  repetition, and `outline/volume-21.md` §0 records the disagreement with the owner as
  *nobody written down*. This is a controller or human decision. It is recorded in
  `state/current.md`, `state/open-threads.md` and section 10 of the band-5 prompt, and
  it is not resolved here, by extension, or by writing another ten chapters.
- **The primary relationship and the System have been absent for 150 chapters.**
  `outline/series.md` names Ilyan and Sera Quill as the primary relationship and calls
  the System the premise; the last *Sera* is in volume 18 and *System* stands at zero
  in volumes 18 through 21. It cannot be repaired from inside band 5: §9 of the volume
  outline puts `System` at zero and forbids introducing it for the first time, and §6
  forbids naming a person not already named in a chapter. Recorded as an obligation on
  the outline's owner; not retro-fitted into finished chapters; not written around.
