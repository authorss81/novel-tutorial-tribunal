- **`1001`** — *A Wednesday.* Opens on a broom that stops short, not on a name. Corvin Tace sweeps along the front of the dry strip, off its end, round its back and up to where he started, so the grit moves and the wood does not, then says out loud, for the first time in nineteen years, that what he wants is a stone with nothing on it that he could walk a line down the middle of. Nobody calls that large or small. Barnaby Crove asks at the sixth hour whether there is one thing in this county a man who cannot read is wanted for, once, with nothing kept against it, is answered on none, and puts three refusals into the air unasked — how long a name has stood, how many times one has been said, who has heard one — and asks for none of them noticed. The boy of thirteen says a true thing about a cup in the shed that is not her cup and would not be hers with her name cut into it, and asks nobody to connect it to the landing. Ilyan Vester gives the **figure** of the standing offer at three hundred and one days with the subtraction out loud, and says out loud that it is unanswered and that he has not asked it, and says he was not one of the people who went up on the morning all ninety steps were walkable and is not going to be thanked for it. Sixteen inches, about sixty steps under, one day — three figures and never a fifth. Nothing was touched.
- **`1002`** — *A Thursday, and the water came off that bank in the night.* Corvin Tace gives **no** figure of inches, **no** figure of steps under and **no** figure of days, says all three absences out loud so they cannot be unsaid, and nobody supplies an ordinal. All ninety steps are walkable and the two pieces of wood have not moved. Barnaby Crove is answered on none and looks down at the iron in the seam of the fortieth step without putting a hand or a foot in it. Wenna Pyle refuses to say a second time what that wood is, and says instead that a thing I cut is not anybody's because I cut it, and that a name in wood is a nail you cannot take out without splitting it. Ilyan Vester comes all the way to the top for the first time in this county, keeps his hands behind his back, says he is not going to touch it and is not going to say why, and says that if somebody stands at that strip this morning and puts the right name on it, they will be doing it for those people and not for the wood. Nobody stood at the strip.
- **`1003`** — *A Friday.* Four inches come back into the joints of ninety steps in the night and the boy of thirteen is the only one awake for it. Corvin Tace gives seven days and says a big figure of days is not a good week; Barnaby Crove says a number of days does not care how big it is, and is not going to praise him for it, because being praised is another thing to carry down the bank. The boy says the same true thing a second time, this time about a pail, notices nobody has joined the two together, and does not join them himself. Simon Rook's hand goes past the end of the dry strip without stopping on it, and he says what a name said over a thing does: it does not make the thing the name, a thing a man has said belongs to the man who said it, it is not a fact about the wood, not a gift to the man it names and not a debt — and says it is the last time he will say it out loud, and that he will not hand a man of thirty-one a sentence to say about himself. Ilyan Vester heard all of it, came up after it, and took none of it.
- **`1004`** — *A Saturday, and the fever comes out whole.* No figure of days stands beside Barnaby Crove's whole figure of weeks and he says so, and says that if he is asked what a whole figure of weeks is for he will answer that he does not know, and that this will be the answer that stops the next person asking. He says he has stopped asking what the name is on his own account, and that if he is asked he will put his hands on the wall and say nothing. Corvin Tace says a shape has been going round this county in more mouths than one, has made his morning easier, and is not a gift, not offered, not given, not left, not provided, not kept and not meant. Ilyan Vester says he will agree with all seven and gives his reason: a man who can say those seven words has decided what the thing is, and he has not decided. Nobody agreed out loud.
- **`1005`** — *A Sunday.* Sena Dorr does not look at the strip once, and uses the shape against the people who want that name to be right, saying a right name is a finished thing and that when it comes out right they will be the same people with a nicer thing to hold. She says nobody is to tell her what she has done and that she is not going to be told she has any right to it. Corvin Tace, who stood with his broom and said nothing while it happened, then says out loud what it cost her: she took a hope off a room, and it cost her the right to be a comfort to anybody for as long as she stands on that landing, and he will not give it as a number because a number of what it cost her would be a figure of her. Barnaby Crove says somebody will get that name right one day and his question will not change. Nobody was thanked.
- **`1006`** — *The last day of the hundred and sixty-fifth week, and a full bank.* Corvin Tace gives the three figures, gives no fourth, says he will say no word after that, and then does his whole morning without anybody having to say why it mattered — sweeping the thirty steps that were out along the top of the water on the sixty that were under, with nobody speaking to him once. Barnaby Crove says a high morning and a dry morning have both been asked on and his question has been the same shape on both, says he knows which morning the water was off all ninety steps and will not say, because a figure of a morning is a figure of him being on that landing, and says he had nothing in his hands but a barrow, which is not a promise and not a virtue and not a refusal of anybody. Simon Rook says nobody has asked him to put his hand on that wood and is not going to offer. Ilyan Vester puts his face to the wall with the strip behind him, says the shape out loud, and will not turn round and finish it, because a man who says that about a piece of wood behind him has made himself a figure.
- **`1007`** — *The first morning of the hundred and sixty-sixth week.* Corvin Tace says the week has turned and nothing else on that stone has turned. The boy of thirteen gives his own count, says it is four more than this time last week and he has not worked out why, and says it is a number of times and will stay a number of times. Garrin Tolley comes up a road he said he would not come up and says a shape that was in other mouths is his, that he did not make it up and could not have, compares it to a haft that becomes the man's only when the man names what he wants off it, and asks that nobody tell him he has taken it or come into it. The woman of forty-four stands at the wall, says a shape is going round about a name and she is not in it and is not going to be asked whether she is. Nobody went up the road.
- **`1008`** — *A Wednesday, and two days, the shortest figure Corvin Tace gives.* Barnaby Crove says two days is the number he had been waiting for without knowing it, and that there is no version of being used to a name on that strip that does not have him in it. Wenna Pyle comes up with nothing in her hands, which nobody on that landing has done before, and uses the shape about her own trade and not about the strip, because using it about the strip would make her the person who put a name there; and says she has waited all week for somebody to tell her she should not have said what she said, nobody came, and the not-coming does not make the saying smaller. Ilyan Vester, who had been at the bottom of the steps, comes up and does not say what he saw and everybody waits for him to; then says nobody on that landing was told what to say, and that a mouth took a shape and nobody handed it over, and that he is not going to make a figure of how many mornings he has wanted one. He also says out loud that what he worked out on that landing was worked out once and is not to be treated as found this week.
- **`1009`** — *A Thursday, and one day.* Sena Dorr says she knows where the shape came from and is not going to say it, that she would say *I do not know* in the flat voice she uses on a page if she were asked, and then says it once herself — that a name said out loud over a thing is not the thing and is not the man it names either — and says she is not saying it because it came to her that morning but because it costs the person who says it nothing, and she has spent the morning watching what it costs. She says there is no age in that shape and nobody is to say how many weeks it has been going round. Ilyan Vester says there is work at the low end of that drain a person cannot do alone, that a woman has asked for a person to stand with her and has named nobody, that he is not going to go down there, and that a man who goes because it would be nice of him has turned a woman's asking into an audience; and asks that nobody give him a word for what he has declined. Simon Rook says he could count the boots that went down that bank and is not going to.
- **`1010`** — *A Friday, and the last morning of the band, with no figure of water on it.* Corvin Tace is the first one up the bank, which he had not been since the bank filled, and gives the three absences again in words he has not used before and will not use a third time. Barnaby Crove says it is the same words at the same hour, that the only thing that has changed on that landing in a fortnight is what people say when they pass each other, and that his question will be it on the first morning of the next week whatever is standing on that strip by then. Garrin Tolley asks, out loud, whether anybody has got a figure of how long that name has stood on that piece of wood, says he is not asking because he wants the figure, and then says that not one of them said a word is the answer. Ilyan Vester gives the **figure** of the standing offer at three hundred and ten days with the subtraction out loud, and says out loud that it is unanswered and that he has not asked it on any morning this week, and says he is neither relieved nor unrelieved and will not be either quietly, and says there is a thing he wants that he has not wanted before this week and is not going to give out on a morning when every one of those ninety steps is walkable. Simon Rook says his list is where it was. Nothing was touched, and the dry strip came out of the morning as the only clean bit of stone on the whole landing.
