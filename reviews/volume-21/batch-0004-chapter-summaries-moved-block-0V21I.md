## 0V21I. ⚠ CHAPTER SUMMARIES FOR VOLUME 21 BAND 3, CHAPTERS `1021`–`1030`, ⚹ **TEN ENTRIES, ONE A CHAPTER, ⚹ AND EVERY FIGURE IN EVERY ONE OF THEM WAS READ OFF THE PAGE AND **NOT** INHERITED FROM ANY TABLE OR FROM BAND 2** ⚛⚾ ⚠ **NO FIGURE APPEARS HERE THAT IS NOT ON THE PAGE, ⚠ AND NO FIGURE OF A HEAD COUNT, OF HOW MANY THINGS ARE LEFT ON THAT LANDING, OF HOW MANY TIMES ANYTHING WAS SAID, OF HOW LONG A NAME HAS STOOD, OF AN OFF-MORNING'S PLACE IN A RUN, OF HOW MANY NEW NAMES WERE PUT ON ANYTHING, OR OF HOW MANY HEARD THE MIDPOINT APPEARS HERE AT ALL — **INCLUDING AS ZERO.** ⚛⚾**

- **`1021` · 168/1 · Tuesday · TWELVE INCHES · ABOUT SEVENTY UNDER · FIVE DAYS.** **A sentence the man of thirty-one said on that stone the week before comes out of another mouth on that stone the first morning of the next week**, in front of him, and he will not say it again so that the man who carried it up the road can hear whether he has got it right, because a man who says it with the asker in the room is saying it for the asker. He gives the working: the man will have it right or wrong on his own and it will sit on him. **The landing carries the sentence across a week boundary and does one thing with it, which is to keep on being a landing.**
- **`1022` · 168/2 · Wednesday · SIXTEEN INCHES AND IT HOLDS, A FULL BANK · ABOUT SIXTY UNDER · FOUR DAYS.** A man with a broom asks a woman who saws lengths who has been up her road before the light and is told she does not know because she is paid by the cut and not by the mile, and that noticing who went past would turn her into a woman who keeps a book about her road. **She then gives the working out of her own trade, a cut face and not a tape, and says out loud that a thing cut for a person is not that person's**, and it is in her mouth about a length before anybody on that landing argues about it. Nobody thanks her. She carries her length down the road at the end.
- **`1023` · 168/3 · Thursday · SIXTEEN INCHES AND IT HOLDS · ABOUT SIXTY UNDER · THREE DAYS.** **A man of thirty-eight comes up having decided to stop saying the name for the rest of his life, and asks a man with a broom for the figure of how that name got onto that strip.** The broom refuses the figure and gives the working, and **refuses to say whether the name put him in that strip or not, because either answer would have done it by arithmetic.** He is told neither that he is right to stop nor that he was wrong to start.
- **`1024` · 168/4 · Friday · SIXTEEN INCHES AND IT HOLDS · ABOUT SIXTY UNDER · TWO DAYS.** **A boy of thirteen works out that a wrong thing said first in a room gives the right thing somebody to answer to and stops it going anywhere**, and goes down the cart road to say one. **A woman of forty-four puts her hand on his yoke and stops him before the name in it**, with the working out of a chair standing in the wrong place in a room nobody stops using, and does not tell him it is wrong. **A man of thirty-one says out loud that he wants the boy to do it and will not say whether he should.** The boy goes down the lane and does not say it, and the name he intended was never finished in the air and is on no page.
- **`1025` · 168/5 · Saturday · SIXTEEN INCHES AND IT HOLDS · ABOUT SEVENTY UNDER · ONE DAY. ⚠ THE FEVER IS A WHOLE FIGURE OF WEEKS AND CARRIES NO FIGURE OF DAYS.** A man with a broom says in advance that he will not speak about a whole figure and keeps to it. **A whole figure of weeks comes out of the wall again with nothing beside it and doing it again is not easier.** **A man who cannot see well asks the man who cannot read for his count and is refused it so that no figure of a person leaves the mouth it was counted in, and then a man of thirty-one offers his own count to the man who cannot read and is refused by him on the ground that a figure handed to a man who cannot read is a figure nobody will ever check.** Both wanted it and neither took it.
- **`1026` · 168/6 · Sunday · NONE · NONE · NONE.** A man with a broom gives the three absences in a fourth set of words and gives a fourth thing about himself: **in nineteen years he did not know what the next morning would be like.** A whole figure of weeks does not come out of anybody's wall, because there is a day with the fever this morning and the man who cannot read keeps his figure and says so. A man who cannot see well walks up ninety dry steps, turns round at the fortieth and comes back down, because loose grit on a dry step is a step that moves. **A man of thirty-one asks the woman who reads for a living to read him an account that has never been on anything, and is refused on the ground that a thing with no writing on it is not a page.**
- **`1027` · 168/7 · Monday, THE LAST MORNING OF THAT WEEK · FOUR INCHES · ABOUT NINETY UNDER · SEVEN DAYS.** **A man who cannot see well works out in front of everybody what a week is — the time a wall takes to go from warm to cold and come back round — and is told by a man who cannot read that what he has described is an afternoon.** He is then given a week, and the week is when the gravel comes and how far up the wall his hands get. **A man of thirty-one asks a man with a broom to tell him in the morning whether the week has turned, and is told that he is asking the one man who admitted yesterday that he did not know**, and the chapter does not say the week has turned, because it turns in the night, between this morning and the next.
- **`1028` · 169/1 · Tuesday, AND THE WEEK BOUNDARY FALLS BETWEEN IT AND THE MORNING BEFORE · EIGHT INCHES · ABOUT EIGHTY UNDER · SIX DAYS.** **A man with a broom asks a man of thirty-one to let him go down the lane to the work at the low end of that drain, where a woman has asked out loud for a person to stand with her and has not named anybody, and says he wants one morning of a road after nineteen years of a stone.** He is refused, with two workings, one of them that a man who is sent down a lane is not standing with a woman but standing in the way of her doing it alone. **A woman who saws lengths refuses to carry the asking down that road in her mouth, because nobody has told the man what he would say when he got there.** Nobody goes down that lane and the work is not done.
- **`1029` · 169/2 · Wednesday · TWELVE INCHES · ABOUT SEVENTY UNDER · FIVE DAYS.** **A man of thirty-one goes from one to another on that landing asking what he is owed for a sentence he said out loud about a piece of wood.** A woman who saws lengths refuses him a figure and gives him the working out of a table with the plate taken off it. **A man who keeps a list of things he cannot check tells him that a thing which cannot be checked does not grow**, asks for a line on that list and is refused his own request. **The man of thirty-one finds out he is owed nothing for the sentence and says he is not relieved, and says asking about it cost him what asking has always cost him, which is nothing.**
- **`1030` · 169/3 · Thursday, THE LAST MORNING OF THIS BAND · SIXTEEN INCHES AND IT HOLDS, A FULL BANK · ABOUT SIXTY UNDER · FOUR DAYS.** **A boy of thirteen asks the man of thirty-one to go down that lane with him and is refused, with the working that a thing you did on your own stops being yours the moment a man is put in it**, and the boy goes down the lane with the yoke and does not say the name. A woman who saws lengths asks the landing whether anybody has been told what that piece of wood is and is given the landing, not a person. **The man of thirty-one's own count comes out as a whole figure and he says nobody is to do arithmetic at him. And a man with a broom finds a lip of grit on the top step that is holding water back in a bowl of its own making, and spends the whole afternoon walking it down off the step on his own, load by load.**
