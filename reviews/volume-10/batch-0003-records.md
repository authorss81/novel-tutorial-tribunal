## 2d. THE TENTH LOGGED FINDING, AND IT IS NOT A DAY-COUNT AND IT IS THE FIRST ONE THIS FILE DID NOT EXPECT

**WHAT THE THREE EARLIER FINDINGS IN THIS FILE HAVE IN COMMON AND WHAT THIS ONE ADDS. The seventh was four state files carrying 1,073 where the chapters said 1,074. **THE STANDING INSTRUCTION, AND IT IS TWO CLAUSES AND THE SECOND IS THE ONE THAT IS NEW. **AND THE BY-PRODUCT, which is in the refrain row above and is worth more than the repair: fourteen of the `Say the rest of that` prompts in this band were rewritten as `Go on and say the rest of that` while the long sentences were being split, and that single change is the whole of the difference between 39.5 per 10k and the 38.3 of the band before it, and it is the reason the *do not stop* family is at 1 in this band against 14 in the last two. ## 2e. THE ELEVENTH LOGGED FINDING, AND IT IS THE SAME CLASS AS THE SEVENTH, AND IT WAS FOUND WHILE DERIVING THE NEXT BAND'S ARITHMETIC AND NOT WHILE REVIEWING ANYTHING

**Four state files and four chapter summaries carried, as a standing figure of this volume, that the roll of Halloway is wrong by 112 pence a year. That is true of one thousand and sixty households and false of one thousand and fifty-two, and the roll has stood at one thousand and fifty-two since the thirtieth day after the division, which is three bands ago.**

**The working, which is the whole of the finding. At 1,060 the error is 1,074 − 1,060 = 14 households at 8d = 112d a year, and 112d was made of 24d on three doors and 88d on eleven rows, and that is the derivation `state/current.md` §3 has printed since the repair that produced it, correctly, at the time. At 1,052 the error is 1,074 − 1,052 = 22 households at 8d = 176d a year = 44d a quarter. And 44 is checkable against a figure already in the chapters without doing any of this: the quarter is 56d short of the heel at 1,052 and a shilling, that is 12d, short at 1,074, and 56 − 12 = 44.** The 176 pence a year is fourteen shillings and eightpence. **All four state files and the four chapter summaries repaired this phase; no chapter was touched, because no chapter printed 112.**

**AND THE SAME SLIP IS AVAILABLE IN THE OTHER DIRECTION AND NOBODY HAS MADE IT YET, WHICH IS WHY IT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN ONLY FIXED: the year's money at 1,074 is 8,592d = £35 16s 0d, and £35 14s 0d is 8,568d, which is 1,071.** A band that reaches for the printed figure of £35 14s 0d in order to describe 1,074 will be wrong by two shillings and will be wrong in a way that looks entirely right.

**WHAT ADDS TO THE OTHER TEN.** The seventh was four state files carrying 1,073 where the chapters said 1,074. That is a figure that was never right. **This one is a figure that WAS right, for about one band, against a roll that stood at 1,060, and then the roll changed and the figure did not, and nothing about it looks wrong.** A stale figure is better dressed than a false one. **The defence is the cheapest one in this repository and it is the same defence as finding seven: put the figure next to the thing it describes and do the division, and 1,074 − 1,052 is twenty-two and not fourteen, and the twenty-two is the number of households behind nineteen doors, which is in `473` and is the number everybody in this town knows.**
