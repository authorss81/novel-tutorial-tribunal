# Volume 10, Batch 0003 — the four measurement findings and the two repair passes, moved out of `state/batch-summary.md`

**These four blocks were moved out of `state/batch-summary.md` at the VOLUME CLOSE, not at the end of Batch 0003, because the close had to re-measure both of the last two bands into the same file and the hard cap is 60,000 bytes.** They are the tenth and eleventh logged findings and the two repair passes that produced them. They are whole, in order, and they were the first time this repository recorded that a band can be arithmetically perfect and structurally unreadable at once, and that a repair pass is a writer who agrees with itself exactly the way a self-review does. **Findings twelve through seventeen are in `state/index.md` and `reviews/volume-10/`, and the two that came at the close are the ones a later band most needs: *a contradiction between two adjacent chapters is invisible to every instrument here*, and *a title is written first and re-read least*.**

## 2d. THE TENTH LOGGED FINDING, AND IT IS NOT A DAY-COUNT AND IT IS THE FIRST ONE THIS FILE DID NOT EXPECT

**The first draft of 481–490 was delivered to this file with 21.22 per cent of its sentences over sixty words, on a gate of ten, and with a paragraph of 170 words on a cap of about 120. Its band median was 21 and passed. Every clock, every money figure, every day-count and every date line in it was correct while it was in that state, and would have gone to a reviewer and come back clean.**

**Ninety-one sentences and six paragraphs were broken up by hand into two, three or four sentences each. The prose was not otherwise touched, and no date, no figure, no clock and no day-count changed as a result. **THE FIGURES THAT CAME BACK FROM THAT REPAIR DID NOT REPRODUCE TWELVE PHASES LATER AND THE SENTENCE AS IT STANDS NAMED 28,368 WORDS, 1,165 SENTENCES AND 1.37 PER CENT OVER SIXTY. The band is 28,951 words, 1,178 sentences, median 21 and 1.87 per cent over sixty, and the difference is `490`, which is 586 words longer than the certificate said and which carries 6.30 per cent over sixty on its own. See §1 and finding twelve.****

**WHAT THE THREE EARLIER FINDINGS IN THIS FILE HAVE IN COMMON AND WHAT THIS ONE ADDS. The seventh was four state files carrying 1,073 where the chapters said 1,074. The eighth was eleven defects in 471–480, all of one class, and the instrument passed on all of them. The ninth was seven more, four of them in lines the repair pass had itself edited. All three are the same lesson: a record that cannot be checked against the thing it describes is worse than a record nowhere. This one is the shape of that lesson seen from the other side, and it is the first finding here that the tables would never have caught and the *only* one that the instrument caught immediately and a writer ignored, because a band with a median of 21 looks like a band that has been measured.**

**THE STANDING INSTRUCTION, AND IT IS TWO CLAUSES AND THE SECOND IS THE ONE THAT IS NEW. First: after a repair pass, re-open the file and grep the finding's own figure, and check a figure in a state file back against the chapter it describes. Second, and new: RUN THE INSTRUMENT BEFORE THE HAND-CHECK AND PRINT BOTH, AND DO NOT LET A PASSING MEDIAN STAND IN FOR A PASSING BAND. A median is one number. A band is a distribution, and the gate that catches the distribution is the over-sixty line, and the over-sixty line is the one a writer reads past when the median is where it should be.**

**AND THE BY-PRODUCT, which is in the refrain row above and is worth more than the repair: fourteen of the `Say the rest of that` prompts in this band were rewritten as `Go on and say the rest of that` while the long sentences were being split, and that single change is the whole of the difference between 39.5 per 10k and the 38.3 of the band before it, and it is the reason the *do not stop* family is at 1 in this band against 14 in the last two. The cheapest repair in this file took a decision and not a tool.**



## 2b. THE REPAIR PASS AT THE END OF THIS PHASE, AND IT FOUND ELEVEN DEFECTS IN TEN CHAPTERS THAT HAD BEEN IN THE BAND SINCE IT WAS WRITTEN

**A writer phase was dispatched for this batch after the band had already been written, self-reviewed and delivered. It did not rewrite anything. It re-ran the published instrument on the files, read the ten chapters against the four clocks and the money, and found eleven defects, all of them of one class: a figure or a day-count in a chapter that could not be checked against the thing it described. Every one is repaired. The prose was not touched except where a figure was wrong, and no paragraph was replaced.**

**WHAT THE INSTRUMENT SAID AND WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG. The published instrument passed on the first run and had passed on the run before it. It measures sentence length, paragraph length and the tics, and it cannot ask whether the sixth hour of the thirtieth is the sixth hour of the thirty-first. All eleven defects were found by hand, by putting every day-count in the band next to the calendar table in `outline/volume-10.md` §1 and every money figure next to the four household counts. This is the same finding the file has now made four times and it is worth stating plainly: THE INSTRUMENT IS NECESSARY AND IT IS NOT SUFFICIENT, and a band that reports only the instrument has reported half of what happened.**

1. **`477` WAS A CHAPTER DATED THURSDAY THAT NARRATED FRIDAY MORNING, ALL DAY.** Its date line said the thirty-first day after the division, which the table makes a Thursday, and its body had the bar unwound on the Friday, the stone room at the ninth hour on the Friday, and the square at the eleventh hour on the Friday, and its closing paragraph called it the second day. Five `Friday`s repaired to `Thursday`, the third missed winding cut, the closing count made the first day. **This is the worst of the eleven, because it breaks the one spine the whole volume stands on — a chapter a day — and no length gate and no tic count can see it.**
2. **THE FIRST MISSED WINDING OF THE BAR WAS THE THIRTY-FIRST DAY AND FOUR STATE FILES AND THE BATCH 0004 PROMPT ALL SAID THE THIRTIETH.** `476:199` has always said the sixth hour of the Thursday morning, and the Thursday of that week is the thirty-first. `477`, `478` and `480` now agree with it: not turned on the thirty-first, the thirty-second, the thirty-third or the thirty-fourth; the wheel slow since that Thursday; the first, second, third and fourth days counted from it. `479`'s wheel count and the Batch 0004 card header were made to match. **A band inherits its arithmetic from the state files, and one wrong anchor in a state file propagates into the next prompt's card titles.**
3. **`475:121` SAID THE TRUE FIGURE WAS *TWENTY-TWO PENCE A YEAR* AWAY FROM THE FIGURE ON THE WALL.** The figure on the wall is 1,071 and the true figure is 1,074, which is three households, and three households at 8d is **twenty-four pence a year**. Twenty-two is the number of households behind nineteen doors. **A number of houses had been printed as a number of pence, in the one chapter whose whole subject is that a correction reaches the people who were not its subject.**
4. **`472:87` SAID THE SECOND OF THE THREE SENTENCES WAS *OUT OF DATE BY ELEVEN POUNDS AND EIGHT PENCE*.** The money in that sentence is £34 14s 0d a year and the money on the twenty-sixth day is £35 6s 8d. The difference is 12s 8d a year, which is the whole of the nineteen houses at 8d. Repaired, with the unit in the sentence.
5. **`472` PLACED THE THREE SENTENCES ON THE TENTH DAY, AND ITS OWN TITLE SAID THE THIRTEENTH.** They are of the **nineteenth**, at the sitting brought forward six days, and the vote there was **nine to nothing** and not six to three; the six to three is the vote on the scouring of the tenth day, which is a different meeting and is still in `472:91` where it belongs. Title, two speeches and the narrator's summary at `472:121` all repaired, and `472:121` also said three of the nine men had the sentences in the same words where `465:45` and `472:93` both say four.
6. **`472:111` CALLED THE POSITION THIRTEEN DAYS OLD.** The nineteenth day to the twenty-sixth is seven.
7. **`473:75` PUT THE COUNTY MAN AT THE HEAD OF THE SLUICE ON A THURSDAY.** The gates were shut on the Friday of the eleventh day and he was there; `457:5` and `457:85` both say Friday.
8. **`475:19` CITED *THE FINDING OF NINETEEN DOORS ON THE FIFTEENTH DAY*.** The finding is the twenty-first (`467`), and the fifteenth is the day of the miller's nineteen lines, which is a different thing and is in `461`.
9. **`473:115` PUT THE COUNT ON THE BILL *SINCE THE FIFTEENTH DAY*,** and `473:69` had Ordwin Cape reporting that the steward told him about the form *nine days ago* when he had been told it on the eighth and nine days before the eighth day is before the division. Repaired to the twenty-first and the nineteenth.
10. **`479` AND `480` BOTH PUT THE READING OF THE NOTICE *ON THE FOURTH DAY AFTER THE DIVISION*.** He came into the town at about the fourth hour of the **sixth** day after the division and read it out at the ninth hour of the same day (`452`). `480`'s ninth line said *nineteen days in Halloway* on that day, which is the count for the thirty-first day and not for the day he arrived. Both repaired, and the ninth line now names the hour he came in, so that a reader can check it.
11. **`477`'S TITLE AND THREE SPEECHES SAID EIGHT DAYS AND FOUR DAYS** where the letter is of the twenty-second day and the chapter is of the thirty-first, which is nine, and where the three uses of one form run from the twentieth day to the thirtieth, which is ten. `480:115` had the county man's page-is-twenty-two-miles-off speech, which is `470:117`, on the twenty-eighth day instead of the twenty-fourth. And `480:121` gave the cause to the thirty-one ratepayers and dated the flood *nine years ago*, when the cause is Ordwin Cape's, written on a Sunday for nobody, and when `476` had made a man of fifty-four say that flood as a day-count in a room of thirty people so that it could go in a form.

**AND TWO THINGS IN THE RECORD ITSELF, BOTH OF THE CLASS THE FILE KEEPS FINDING.** `state/current.md` printed `£35 6s 8s 8d` for 1,060 × 8d, which is a duplicated *s* in a money line in the one file a later band reads first. And §1 of this file carried a **per-chapter distribution of both refrains that was wrong in eleven of twenty places** while certifying the totals, which were right; the row had been carried from an earlier band and nobody re-derived it. **That is the eighth logged-and-unmade repair in this repository's record and the second one in a certified measurement line in this file.**

**WHAT WAS NOT REPAIRED AND WHY.** `476:41` locates the sentence *a household is a house* in a market square in the rain on the twelfth day after the division, and that sentence is not in any printed chapter. It may be an off-page event and it may be a gap, and there is nothing in the manuscript to decide it, so it was left alone rather than invented. `472:123` reaches twenty-five days forward to his understanding of his own lie, which lands in the last band of this volume and is a promise and not a contradiction. And the self-review's refusal to cut the two places where the band opens and closes on an inventory stands.



## 2c. A REVIEW OF THE REPAIR PASS IN §2b, WHICH FOUND SEVEN MORE, FOUR OF THEM IN LINES THE PASS ITSELF HAD EDITED

**An external review ran on the three commits of Batch 0003 and on §2b above. It edited nothing. It found seven defects, all seven repaired, and two of them are in chapters and five of them are in state files.**

1. **THE REPAIR PASS'S OWN EDIT TO `state/index.md` RULE 11 HAD A BROKEN `**` PAIR.** The edit split one bold run into two and left `and the closing fever of a chapter must agree with its own date line.**` as a stray bolded fragment, thirteen markers on the line. **A literal-markup defect, in the file a later band reads first, introduced by the pass that was sweeping the repository for unchecked figures.**
2. **§2 OF THIS FILE PUBLISHED THREE STALE BYTE COUNTS AND A FOURTH FOR THE SAME FILE IN THE SAME SENTENCE.** It claimed `batch-summary 26366 · current 16766 · index 10653` and, in the same breath, that the move had brought this file "to 17,468". `state/index.md` was updated to the true figures in the same commit and §2 was not, so the two files published different sizes for the same files, in a sentence that said *re-measured after the repair pass*. **All seven re-stat'ed and the sentence rewritten; the sizes are now the last thing written in this file, and the rule is index rule 11's: a file that certifies a measurement is measured last.**
3. **A HAZARD NUMBER WAS INSERTED OUT OF ORDER IN `state/open-threads.md` §0** — the eighth logged-and-unmade repair sat between 17 and 18. Renumbered, no prose changed.
4. **§1'S PER-CHAPTER ROW WAS ONE WORD OUT.** `477` was printed 3,119 and is 3,120, so the row summed to 30,792 against a total of 30,793 printed in the same sentence. **The row and the total and the per-10k figures behind them are re-derived; `480` also moved, by the fourteen words of repair 5 below.**
5. **`480:127` COUNTED THE BAR'S MISSED DAYS ONE HIGHER THAN `480:99` ENUMERATED THEM.** Line 99 listed Thursday, Friday and Saturday and said the wheel had been slow three days; line 127 said the bar was not turned *for the fourth day*. **The Sunday's sixth hour is now in the list — `480:99` ends *and the sixth hour on this Sunday morning is gone past the same way* — and the count is then carried by the chapter's own enumeration, which is what the Batch 0004 card at `481` already assumed when it opened on the fifth day of the run.** The wheel stays at three days and is not the same count as the bar: the wheel is counted in days completed, the bar in days on which an hour came and went. **That distinction is now written into `state/current.md` §6, `state/open-threads.md` §1 and index rule 11's neighbourhood, because three state files and one chapter summary were each counting the wheel their own way.**
6. **`472:125` SAID *FOUR DAYS AGO* WHERE THE REFUSAL WAS TWO.** The board refused nine pounds in the words of the request that caused it at `470:111`, which is the twenty-fourth day, and `472` is the twenty-sixth. **This is the same class as the eleventh defect of §2b, which repaired the identical error at `480:115`, and the repair pass fixed one instance of it and left the other.**
7. **TWO RELATIVE CLAIMS IN THE STATE FILES THAT WERE SIMPLY FALSE, FOUND WHILE LOOKING.** `state/index.md` said of this file that it "is now the SMALLEST of the seven" — it was the second largest, and `index.md` itself was the smallest — and that "five are over the ~12,000 soft cap" when six of the seven were over it and `index.md` was the only one under. **A relative claim about a file — smaller than, biggest, the only one — is a claim about that file and about every other file at once, and it is the only figure in this repository that goes stale the moment a byte is added rather than the moment a day-count is wrong.** **AND THIS REPAIR DEMONSTRATED IT IN ITS OWN ACT: §2b of this file grew by 3,340 bytes and passed both its neighbours, so the very sentence being written about a stale ranking became stale while it was written, and `index.md` crossed the soft cap and the count of files over it went from six to seven. There is now no relative claim about a file size in `state/index.md` or in §2 above, only the seven numbers and the sentence that says they were taken last.**

**WHAT THIS ADDS TO §2b's LESSON, and it is the same lesson with a second half. §2b established that the instrument is for shape and the tables are for truth. This establishes that a repair pass is a writer: it agrees with itself exactly the way a self-review does, and four of these seven were in lines it had just written. The cheapest defence in this repository is free: re-open every file the pass edited, and `stat` them, before the pass is called done.**



## 2e. THE ELEVENTH LOGGED FINDING, AND IT IS THE SAME CLASS AS THE SEVENTH, AND IT WAS FOUND WHILE DERIVING THE NEXT BAND'S ARITHMETIC AND NOT WHILE REVIEWING ANYTHING

**Four state files and four chapter summaries carried, as a standing figure of this volume, that the roll of Halloway is wrong by 112 pence a year. That is true of one thousand and sixty households and false of one thousand and fifty-two, and the roll has stood at one thousand and fifty-two since the thirtieth day after the division, which is three bands ago.**

**The working, which is the whole of the finding. At 1,060 the error is 1,074 − 1,060 = 14 households at 8d = 112d a year, and 112d was made of 24d on three doors and 88d on eleven rows, and that is the derivation `state/current.md` §3 has printed since the repair that produced it, correctly, at the time. At 1,052 the error is 1,074 − 1,052 = 22 households at 8d = 176d a year = 44d a quarter. And 44 is checkable against a figure already in the chapters without doing any of this: the quarter is 56d short of the heel at 1,052 and a shilling, that is 12d, short at 1,074, and 56 − 12 = 44.** The 176 pence a year is fourteen shillings and eightpence. **All four state files and the four chapter summaries repaired this phase; no chapter was touched, because no chapter printed 112.**

**AND THE SAME SLIP IS AVAILABLE IN THE OTHER DIRECTION AND NOBODY HAS MADE IT YET, WHICH IS WHY IT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN ONLY FIXED: the year's money at 1,074 is 8,592d = £35 16s 0d, and £35 14s 0d is 8,568d, which is 1,071.** A band that reaches for the printed figure of £35 14s 0d in order to describe 1,074 will be wrong by two shillings and will be wrong in a way that looks entirely right.

**WHAT ADDS TO THE OTHER TEN.** The seventh was four state files carrying 1,073 where the chapters said 1,074. That is a figure that was never right. **This one is a figure that WAS right, for about one band, against a roll that stood at 1,060, and then the roll changed and the figure did not, and nothing about it looks wrong.** A stale figure is better dressed than a false one. **The defence is the cheapest one in this repository and it is the same defence as finding seven: put the figure next to the thing it describes and do the division, and 1,074 − 1,052 is twenty-two and not fourteen, and the twenty-two is the number of households behind nineteen doors, which is in `473` and is the number everybody in this town knows.**
