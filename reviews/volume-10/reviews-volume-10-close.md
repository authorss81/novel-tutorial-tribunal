## 3. Three new findings for the log

**FINDING FIFTEEN — a contradiction between two adjacent chapters is invisible to every gate in this repository, and the careful gate is not the one that catches it.** `493:93` said the season was **nine weeks gone** on the day before `494:87` said **the season has come**. Both date lines were correct. Both had been certified. Every length gate passed. The instrument passed. The money passed. The money is not supposed to know anything about a season. **The only thing that found it was putting the last line of one chapter against the first line of the next, which is thirty seconds of work and which nothing in this repository asks anybody to do.** The cheap gate is a *read* and it is not a gate at all, which is why it is the one that keeps being skipped: a gate is a number and this is an act of attention. **At the end of every band, once, read the closing lines of two consecutive chapters against each other before the instrument runs.** → `state/open-threads.md` §0.28

**FINDING SIXTEEN — a title is a claim about the body and is written first.** `496`'s title said the **sixth** heading was objected to; the body objects to the **fourth**; and the six came off **the sixth ward**, twenty lines earlier, in the same chapter. Index rule 10 already says a title may not state a number the body contradicts and already says a title is a date line. **What it does not say is that a title is the first thing written and the last thing anyone re-reads, so an error in one is the error most likely to survive a review, because nine state files agreeing with the body do not make the title right — they make the title look more wrong.** Titles are written **last**, against the body, and a title containing a number is checked against the body the way a date line is checked against the table. → `state/open-threads.md` §0.29

**FINDING SEVENTEEN — a stricter sweep can return a worse-looking number than the certificate, and the prose can still be right.** The sixteen-wording silence sweep is certified at **0** and returns **5**. All five are a sluice frame, a walk home, a page, a copyist and a man in a room — physical, or one named person, never a chorus. **This is the first time this repository has had to certify that a repair was not needed, and it needed the same discipline as the nineteen times a repair was: put the five next to the thing they describe before touching a word.** Finding seven and finding twelve are the same lesson from the side where the certificate is the thing that is wrong. **A gate that gets stricter is not thereby evidence of a defect, and the answer to a sweep that returns a worse number is to read the five, not to cut them.** → `state/open-threads.md` §0.30
