### 1e. What 591–600 changed

**ILYAN VESTER, THIRTY-ONE, NAMED IN ALL TEN, WITH A WANT, A MISTAKE AND A COST OF HIS OWN IN ALL TEN, INCLUDING THE CHAPTER WHERE HE IS RIGHT AND GETS NOTHING.**

- **Wants the last reading before the settlement to have nothing of his on it, and says four things at a stone step in about nine seconds instead.** `591`
- **Wants one thing off a man’s own mouth about the standing hay, and gives a figure away on a headland before the man who mown it can, and is told the true of it, and says out loud that he will not take the next one.** `592`
- **Wants the place on the rise to be a number anybody can check, and asks a room for a number of feet that fifty-eight people need, and is told on the ground that nobody in that hundred knows it and that about nine mouths will be measuring children by Wednesday.** `593`
- **Wants the sheet finished and himself in the room and not the author of it, and says out loud in about nine people’s hearing that a settlement cannot give fifty-eight people a place, and is told in the same hour that he has said half of it.** `594`
- **Wants the three boards settled, and stands on a bank while three people read them in the open and one of them is a man of fifty-four who says the true of it twice and does not agree with a man of fifty-one.** `595`
- **Wants somebody else to price him, asks a room, and then puts a year of his own future down himself, out loud, with the working, and is told the price of it in a woman’s mouth that is not his.** `596`
- **Wants the settlement read by somebody who is not its author, and stands nine feet off a stone step for nineteen minutes and is not asked anything.** `597`
- **Wants a settlement that can be checked, and says out loud in about nine people’s hearing that a document is not an answer because it is on a wall, and names what has been on that wall for two hundred and thirty years.** `598`
- **Wants to hear somebody else say what it cost, and says his own half out loud in about nine people’s hearing, and is not the carrier of it.** `599`
- **Wants the mark at the bottom of the back identified and put right, and says out loud what it is in about nine people’s hearing, and then cannot answer a question asked out loud four seconds after it, and nobody answers it for him.** `600`

**HIS MISTAKE IS STILL THE SAME ONE AND IT IS NOW SIX VARIATIONS IN FIFTY CHAPTERS: HE MAKES HIMSELF THE CARRIER.** A paragraph at a lamp; four sentences at a step; a figure on a headland; a request for a number of feet; a sentence about what a settlement cannot do; a sentence about a wall. **AT `600` HE SAYS A NAME OUT LOUD IN A ROOM FOR THE FIRST TIME IN FIFTY CHAPTERS AND IT IS THE RIGHT THING TO DO, AND ABOUT NINE MOUTHS WILL HAVE IT BY THE FOURTH HOUR AND ABOUT TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-ONE WILL NOT HAVE IT AND CANNOT BE ASKED ABOUT IT.**

**HIS COSTS IN THIS BAND, and every one of them is a thing that stays: forty-six days in the county on a clock from the evening of `553`; under two pounds in a purse; a number of feet for fifty-eight people in about nine mouths that will be measuring children by Wednesday; about four people in a hundred carrying the wrong half of a true sentence; a name said once in a room; and the standing cost, which is A YEAR OF HIS FUTURE OPTIONS FROM THE CROSSING OF THE TALLY STONE TO THE CROSSING AFTER IT, AND FROM THAT MORNING A PERMANENT COUNT FROM THE WATER WITH NO FIGURE FOR IT AND NOBODY ABLE TO CHECK IT.** STAGE UNCHANGED: **CO-WITNESS. HE IS CO-WITNESS AT `501` AND HE IS CO-WITNESS AT `600`.**

**WHAT HE CAN NO LONGER DO. HE CANNOT LEAVE THIS COUNTY BEFORE THE WATER GOES OVER THAT BLOCK OF LIMESTONE AGAIN. HE CANNOT STOP KNOWING WHERE THAT RIVER IS. HE CANNOT PUT A THIRD HAND ON A WALL. AND HE CANNOT ANSWER, IN A ROOM, THE ONE QUESTION THAT MATTERS, WHICH IS WHAT A THING THAT NAMES ITSELF CALLS ITSELF, AND HE SAID IN FOUR SECONDS THAT HE COULD NOT ANSWER IT AND HE HAS BEEN UNABLE TO ANSWER IT FOR FORTY-SIX DAYS AND FOR EVERY DAY BEFORE THOSE.**

**OWAIN BRAY, FIFTY-FOUR. NOT A VILLAIN AND NOT A SAINT, AND HE ASKED TO BE ASKED.** On `595` he came four miles on his own legs into a room over a stable and said for the third time that he is not saying no, said he had had thirty days to find a way to say no that he could live with and had not found one, and said out loud that **he is asking to be asked — not as a favour, not as a thing anybody owes him, not on a bar — and that any person in that hundred may go and ask him on his own ground and he may say yes or no, and that if he says no nobody in that hundred is to be blame.** On `599` he said he had said yes on Friday afternoon, on his own ground, to about four people and not to nine, **and that his hay is in because he said it was and that nobody in that hundred may now say he was made to say it. HE PUT NO BOARDS IN AND HE IS NOT PUNISHED FOR THAT AND HE IS NOT EXCUSED FROM IT. HE READ THE THREE BOARDS AT A GATE IN THE OPEN AND SAID THE TRUE OF THEM, WHICH IS THAT THREE BOARDS HOLD A LEVEL AND THAT NOBODY IN THAT HUNDRED KNOWS WHAT THEY HOLD OFF, AND HE SAID IT TWICE IN A MARKET BEFORE THAT.** `578`, `583`, `590`, `595`, `599`

**BETT SEARLE, TWENTY-EIGHT, OF THE COME-OVERS.** **AT `593` SHE PUT ELEVEN TENTS ON THE TOP OF A RISE BEFORE THE LIGHT WITH NOBODY HAVING ASKED HER, AND IT WAS THE FIRST MOVE OF THE SETTLEMENT AND IT WAS DONE BY SOMEBODY OTHER THAN A STRANGER.** She said out loud herself, before anybody got near it, that two hundred and twenty feet is a figure about a tent and a fire and a churn and a line and was never a figure for fifty-eight people. She walked four miles a day for five days. **AT `595` SHE SAID IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS THAT NOTHING WAS STANDING ON THAT MEADOW AND WAS TOLD IN FRONT OF ALL OF THEM THAT SHE DID NOT KNOW SHE WAS SAYING IT FOR A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR. AT `600` SHE ASKED, OUT LOUD, WHAT IT CALLS ITSELF, AND NOBODY ANSWERED HER.** Her count is the only count and no person in that county may add to it or take from it.

**HESTER NUNN, FORTY-ONE.** **AT `594` SHE PUT SIX ACRES OF THE SECOND CLOSE OFF THE DYKE ON THE SETTLEMENT WITH THE DAY ON IT AND NO NAME AT THE TOP OF IT AND NO NAME AT THE BOTTOM OF IT, and said out loud why, and it is the reason she has now given three times: a paper with a name at the top of it in a county where about nine people read that wall every morning is a paper nine people can be argued with, and she would rather somebody argued with a parcel. AT `597` SHE READ THE WHOLE OF THE SETTLEMENT ALOUD AT A STONE STEP IN FRONT OF ABOUT THREE HUNDRED PEOPLE IN ABOUT NINETEEN MINUTES WITHOUT HAVING WRITTEN ONE WORD OF IT.** **The question asked of her at `590` — whether she will put her name on a piece of paper in order to take six acres back — is STILL UNANSWERED, and about two hundred and seventy people who heard the asking cannot be asked about it, and she has not been asked again and did not volunteer it.** She is not the midpoint, she is not a symbol, and she has four rooms, two children, a water-rate bill and a strong opinion.

**THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO KEEPS FOUR BEDS, WHO HAS NEVER GIVEN HIM HER NAME.** **AT `596` SHE PRICED A YEAR OF HIM OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE’S HEARING WITH THE WORKING SPOKEN IN NINE SENTENCES, AND THE FIGURE IS FOUR POUNDS ELEVEN SHILLINGS AND THREEPENCE AND IT IS HERS AND NOT HIS, AND THE OTHER HALF OF THE PRICE IS ONE, BEING ONE MORNING, ONE ROOM AND ONE SEAT IN ABOUT THIRTY.** She then said what he had bought with it: **the right to be wrong in front of about nine people and to be asked about it on a Saturday morning for a year, and that it is not a place, there is no column for it, there is no form that renews it and there is no rate on it, and that a man who pays it is not one of about thirty men and is not anybody’s and is not nobody’s either.** **At `597` she said out loud that a settlement is not a promise, it is a list of what a person could not do and did anyway.** She wrote on the back of the settlement and there is no name at the foot of her hand.

**IVEN ASHCOMBE, FIFTY-EIGHT, THE WATER-CLERK.** **He did not read his own wall for a fifth time.** He wrote the second line under fifty-eight at `589` and said out loud that he was not writing fifty-eight off. **At `591` he said out loud in front of about nine people that a settlement cannot go on that wall, and the reason he gave was that a wall in this county binds nobody and a settlement on that wall would be read by about nine people every morning of a year and that about two hundred and ninety-one people in that town would have it and could not afterwards be asked about it — AND THE SETTLEMENT WENT ON THAT WALL ANYWAY AT `598` AND HE SAID OUT LOUD BEFORE ANYBODY BEGAN THAT HE DID NOT WRITE IT AND THAT HE HAS BEEN NINETEEN YEARS THE MAN WHOSE HAND THAT IS AND THAT HE KNOWS IT.** He is right about the water and he is not comfortable and he is not a villain and he has a wife who will be told about a story at a pump.

**TOBY HENSHAW, FOURTEEN.** **He held the slate at the sixth hour on a Saturday for the second time at `591` and got NOTHING WRONG and told about four people to say so out loud in about four seconds, and for the third time at `598` and read a settlement off the front of that wall along with two hundred and thirty little figures going left, which he read as a run of them and said so out loud before he started.** Nine feet of knotted line is about forty-seven times against a hundred and forty yards of about four hundred and twenty feet. He had been asked to write a thing five times in his life and had said he did not know what to do with it four times.

**GARR RYLEY, THIRTY-THREE, THE MIDPOINT HOUSEHOLD.** Still out of season, still owns nothing, asked for nothing and was promised nothing. His ditch was finished at `588` and **his hundred and forty yards is on the settlement, in his hand, with the rate unpaid and the statement that the square at the right-hand end of that line will be exactly as empty on the morning the water comes over the tally stone as it is now, and that nothing can be taken off a wall with a rag because there is nothing on it to take the rag to.** He is named in `591` and `593` and on the settlement and appears in no scene of his own in this band.

**HOARE RYLEY, FIFTY-ONE.** Thirty years cutting ditch. **On `595` he put his hand on the six-board gate in the same nine people’s hearing as a man of fifty-four and said the true of it and they did not agree, and the settlement carries the disagreement.** He is the one who asks a man of thirty-one whether he wants a number of feet for fifty-eight people and hears the answer on the ground.

**ALDIS CRANE, ABOUT FIFTY, HOLDING, SEAT, SKEP.** The one who asks the other half every time in this band. He asked the fourth thing on the sheet into the open at `596`, he asked what a settlement can give at `594` before the fourth hour, he asked that the two hundred and twenty feet and the fifty-eight be carried as two numbers and not one, and he said on `592` that about four passes was wrong and it was five and the fifth was the one that was not a hay. He is not out of season; he is not the midpoint; he is not a device.

**A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WITH A BARROW AND A STROKE BESIDE HIS NAME IN CHALK.** Nine acres, no seat. **He stood in the two acres behind the Green Paw before the light on `591` and found a foot of sallow in the ditch along the top side of them and said out loud that it is the same as the one on the sally bed. On `592` he said out loud to about nine people on a meadow that two loads of wet hay went into his barn and that he had never told anybody and that nobody in that hundred can be sent for about him. On `597` he asked at the back of a stone step in front of about three hundred people where a load of wet hay in his own barn goes, and it went in on the settlement with the day and the number of loads and no name of the yard.** He is not in the hundred's room and has never been in it.

**REUBEN SEARLE, NINETEEN.** Poled the last of the camp's boats. Appears once, at `593`, carrying tent poles four miles in the flat, saying nothing.

**SERA QUILL, TWENTY-EIGHT, FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF. DOES NOT APPEAR.** Her two letters are unread and unmentioned and nothing has changed. **THE PAGE THAT CAME BACK FOUR HUNDRED MILES IS STILL IN THE INSIDE POCKET OF HIS COAT AND IS NOT MENTIONED IN ANY CHAPTER OF 551–600.**

**NADIA KESTREL. FIRST MENTIONED IN THE MANUSCRIPT IN TWELVE CHAPTERS, AT `600`, AND NOT APPEARING.** Two letters and a number, at the bottom of about nine hundred records, in a building with a bad coffee machine, in a country that is not this one. **She is not in this county, has not been in it, and nobody in that room has ever seen her face.**

**THE FIRST WITNESS IS NAMED AT `600` AND NOT BEFORE, AND IT IS NOT ADDRESSED, AND IT IS NOT ANSWED.** It was not asked anything. It said two sentences and then four words and about four people in a room heard it and about nine heard nothing at all. **NOBODY IN THAT HUNDRED IS BOUND BY A WORD OF IT AND NOBODY ANSWERED THE QUESTION IT WAS ASKED.**
