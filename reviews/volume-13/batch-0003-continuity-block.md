**THE RULE AS IT WORKS HAS FOUR CONSEQUENCES AND EVERY ONE OF THEM IS A CONSEQUENCE A PERSON HAD TO SAY OUT LOUD.** A thing anybody can be sent for about **goes where it goes** and the rule does not say it may not, and a man of thirty-eight cut a third copy off the after thwart of his own boat at about the second hour on the Tuesday of the eleventh week and said so out loud before he did it, and the reason he gave is that he is not going to be a sheet, and he has a child of nine. · The rule makes the record a week behind, because a line written at about the seventh hour on a Tuesday is not on the copy nine miles up the water, and **under the rule a woman of seventy-one who has kept a house that is not hers for eleven years and was never asked is not true for four days**, and a woman of thirty worked that out twice in a room to be sure of it and it was the same both times. · The rule can be used to make a record wrong in public, and it is, and eleven copies of a thing that is not true this week were made on a Sunday anyway by people who said out loud that it was not anybody’s fault. · And the rule **cannot be stopped by about nine people**, because if nine people can stop a copy then a person can be sent for about having stopped it, and the man of sixty-eight said that out loud on the Monday of the eleventh week and would not take his own rule back, and the rule is his. `622`, `627`, `628`

**WHAT THE FIRST WEEKLY READING FOUND, IN A MOUTH, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE.** The two originals had **never once been in a room.** They were read against each other once a week by a man of thirty-eight poling nine miles up and nine miles down and standing about in two yards and remembering it, and about four people in that town had worked that out and about nine had not. **A NO IN THE OPEN IS THE ONLY THING IN THAT TOWN THAT DOES NOT TRAVEL, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE FOUND THAT OUT BY SAYING ONE.** He said no, out loud, in front of about nine people, on the Tuesday of the eleventh week, about a chair nobody had offered him, having said before he said it that there was no chair and that he knew there was no chair. **Nothing whatever happened.** The book was not written in, the count did not move, nobody said anything, and a woman of forty-four who keeps it said out loud that in nineteen years there has never once been a day in that book for a thing a person said out loud and did not take, and the reason is that for nineteen years every no in that county was a person standing up out of a chair, and a person standing up out of a chair is in the book already on the day they sat down. **AND THE FINDING IS THAT A NO IS THE ONLY DOOR OUT OF A CHAIR AND IT LEAVES NO MARK, WHICH IS WHY THE COUNT OF CHAIRS IS NOT A COUNT OF PEOPLE.** `622`

**A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR ANSWERED ONE THIRD OF A QUESTION AND REFUSED THE OTHER TWO THIRDS, AND THE THIRD WAS THAT NOBODY DECIDED.** She was asked, in the open, in front of about nine people, on the Tuesday, the question a woman of thirty-four asked out loud on the Sunday of the tenth week: whether the saying of no has to be written down the way the saying of yes is written down, and if it is not, who decided that, and when, and on what day. She refused the whole of it in four sentences, because an answer said by her in the open is not an answer, it is the position of the record, and this record has never had a position, it has had a woman in it. **THE ANSWER SHE GAVE WAS THE THIRD OF IT: nobody decided it. The book has a column for a yes because a yes has to be written down or a chair is not a chair. And there is no column for a no, and she did not leave it out; a no was never needed by the book, a no is needed by the person, and the book was never for the person. That is not a decision. There was nobody in a room deciding it. She was nineteen and she had a book and she did not go and ask, because you do not go and ask the person you keep a book for.** The other two thirds are owed forward and are not answered. `622`

**THE PERSON THE RELEASE IS OVER IS PERDY LOUND, THIRTY, AND SHE IS AGAINST THE COPIES AND SHE IS RIGHT AND SHE WENT FOUR DAYS UP THE WATER ON THE MONDAY OF THE ELEVENTH WEEK.** She was named out loud in front of about nine people on the Wednesday of the eleventh week **by a woman of forty and not by the man of thirty-one**, and the reason for the naming is that he had spent nineteen days asking people somebody can be sent for about, and she is not a person anybody can be sent for about, and about nine of the nine he asked cannot be sent for about either. **HER REASON IN FOUR SENTENCES:** a copy of that sheet is going to be read out in rooms and a thing that gets read out in rooms is a thing people go looking with, and when they go looking for the six they are not asking her, they are asking about a list, and one of the six that have not come is her brother and he is four days off and she has not been able to say out loud in three weeks whether he is ill, and **she does not want him found, she wants to be the one who tells him.** She was asked a question in the open on the Friday of the eleventh week by a woman of forty-one with two carts and said out loud that she was not ready, and gave the reason, which is that the only thing about those copies in her hands is whether the copying is done well, and she cannot know that until Saturday and cannot know the answer to the question until she knows that. `623`, `625`, `628`

**THERE IS NO SECOND CALENDAR.
