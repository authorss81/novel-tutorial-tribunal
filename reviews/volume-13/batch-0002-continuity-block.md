## 0G. WHAT CHAPTERS 611–620 ADDED TO THE GROUND, AND EVERY FIGURE IN IT IS ONE A PERSON SAID OUT LOUD

**THE SUBSTANCE OF THE BAND IS WORK AND THE NAME OF A THING, AND IT IS THE SAME GROUND AS `601`–`610` — A TOWN OF ABOUT ELEVEN HUNDRED PEOPLE, TWENTY-NINE CHAIRS, A PLANK ELEVEN FEET LONG, AND A LIGHTER CALLED THE GULL. NOTHING WAS BORROWED AND NOBODY WENT ANYWHERE.** `611`–`620`

**THE COUNT IS A COUNT OF A CHAIR AND NOT OF A PERSON, AND IT WAS FOUND OUT IN THE OPEN, AND IT IS NOW A FIGURE WITH A HISTORY THAT CAN BE SPOKEN.** Eleven on the Tuesday of the hundred and eighth week · **ten** from the Thursday of that week, when a woman of twenty-nine sat down in the reed in about nine seconds · **nine** from the Saturday of the hundred and ninth week, when a woman of sixty-eight came back from the fair at the crossroads and sat in the fourth chair from the after end and did not know she was doing it · **ten** since the Wednesday of the hundred and tenth week, when a woman of twenty-nine stood up out of the reed in the open and took it back. **It is ten, it has moved four times in twenty days, and every one of the four has a day against it and a subtraction said out loud.** `603`, `611`, `612`, `616`, `620`

**AND THERE IS A SECOND COUNT, AND IT IS A COUNT OF PEOPLE AND NOT OF CHAIRS, AND NOBODY KEEPS IT IN A BOOK.** Nine of the ten standing up on that boat on the Friday of the hundred and ninth week were people this town had not been able to ask, **because the tenth was a woman of sixty-eight at the fair at a crossroads four miles off, which comes twice a year, and who could be asked.** The count of chairs is ten and the count of people that cannot be asked is nine, and both are right, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that he has kept the penny a head and the penny a cart for eleven years and has never once had to know which of the two a number meant. `611`

**THE TOWN HAD BEEN TOLD TEN EMPTY CHAIRS AND HAD HEARD IT AS TEN DEAD MEN, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE HAD SAID THE SENTENCE ON A BANK BEFORE HE KNEW THE TOWN HAD IT.** It is **two dead men and two dead women**, one gone up the water in a lighter about eleven years ago and not heard of since, six that have not come, and one at a fair. He had said the phrase *ten dead men* **about four times on that deck in ten days and about nine times in the town behind him in the same ten days, and every one of the first four was his**, and a woman of about sixty-eight told him it was not his and told him not to go taking it. `611`

**A COALITION WAS NAMED OUT LOUD BY THE MAN OF THIRTY-ONE, TOLD IT WAS NOT THEIRS BY A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE, AND OWNED BY THE MAN OF THIRTY-ONE IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE.** The nine who come up that plank are the man of thirty-eight, the woman of forty, the man of sixty-one, the woman of forty-one, the man of sixty-eight, the man of fifty-four, the woman of thirty, the boy of eleven, and the woman of twenty-six, and **not one of the nine reasons was about any of the other eight.** About four have a reason about money and about nine have a reason about themselves. `613`

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-ONE HAS BEEN THE INSTRUMENT FOR ELEVEN YEARS AND WAS NEVER ASKED, AND SHE TOLD ONE PERSON AND ASKED HIM NOT TO CARRY IT ABOUT.** She keeps a house that is not hers for a man who is not in it and has not been in it for nine years and is not dead. **She would not have it put in a book. She said that there is a difference between saying a thing to a person who will keep it and writing it where anybody can be sent for about it, and that the difference is the only thing she has left.** `612`

**A THING THAT WAS SAID IN A WEIGH-HOUSE NINE MILES UP THIS WATER BY SOMETHING THAT WAS NOT A PERSON HAS COME DOWN TO HASK AS A THING A MAN SAYS IN A ROOM, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THAT ANYBODY WHO HAS BEEN TOLD IT CAN SAY IT AGAIN.** A man said a name, four words, in the front room of that house on the Thursday of the hundred and ninth week, and the woman of seventy-one said it back to ask whether she had heard it right. **Nine people came through that door that day. She has not guessed which of the nine and is not going to, and the name was not addressed in any of the ten chapters and was not answered in any of the ten.** `612`, `619`

**THE WORK, AND IT TOOK FOUR DAYS AND NINE PEOPLE AND NOBODY WAS PAID.** Withy hurdles at the head of the plank, about thirty feet, about nine hundred lengths off the hedge above the landing at about two inches at a time, pegs sharpened with a knife, **a barrow and a half of gravel out of the head of the drain given by the near party on a Saturday and not mentioned until the Monday**, and sand out of a barrel in the two low places, argued about for about nine minutes and settled by putting some in each. **A bed and a crust is two pence a day · thirty-six days of work at two pence a day is seventy-two pence, which is six shillings · the woman who keeps the chairs is paid a hundred and sixty pence a year, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence, and six shillings is a little under half a year of hers.** **The cost of the four days is not the six shillings. It is that a count of nine people and four days cannot tell a gift from a day’s work, and there is no column in this county that can.** `615`

**THE MONEY OF A CAUSEWAY, SPOKEN BY A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE IN A MOUTH WITH THE WORKING.** A penny a head and a penny a cart · about eight hundred heads and about two hundred and forty carts · a thousand and forty pence, which is four pounds six shillings and eightpence · three pounds of that is the book, which is ruling, binding, lamp oil and copying it out · what is left is three hundred and twenty pence, which is one pound six shillings and eightpence, between two people, a hundred and sixty pence each, which is thirteen shillings and fourpence. A cart at a crossroads four miles off is **four shillings there and four shillings back with a load, which is eight shillings a load, which is ninety-six pence · about nine loads to a quarter mile, which is eight hundred and sixty-four pence, which is three pounds and twelve shillings · AND THE GRAVEL ALONE IS MORE THAN THE WHOLE OF THAT LANDING FOR A YEAR.** A man of thirty-one had a pound and fifteen shillings and eightpence, said so out loud, and was told no about a sum. `613`, `614`, `615`, `618`

**A SHEET OF DEAL IS THE FIRST THING IN THAT TOWN THAT ANYBODY CAN BE SENT FOR ABOUT, AND THE RULE FOR IT IS A MAN OF SIXTY-EIGHT’S.** **Twenty-nine lines, one for a chair, and the ground is said before the name.** One copy is **nailed inside the after thwart of the Gull** and one is **kept at a landing nine miles up the water**, and they are **read against each other once a week in the open, and if the two do not agree then the thing is not true.** It was written by the man of thirty-one and copied by a boy of eleven on a board on a barrel, because there is no other table in Hask. **Ten of the twenty-nine have a name against them and nobody in them. A second line, the eleventh thing of its kind in that town and never read, was asked for by the man of sixty-eight for the last quarter mile and the plank, because the plank has no chair and the plank is why nine of them came.** `617`

**THE ONE NAME ON THE SHEET THAT NOBODY IN THAT TOWN CAN BE CHECKED AGAINST.** **Wren Sayer**, the last line, the gravel and withy at the top of the drain above the head, gone up the water in a lighter about eleven years ago and not heard of since, the brother of the man who held the reed, **and one of the other three of the four people who were in that weigh-house.** A man of thirty-eight said his name out loud on that deck at `619` for the first time in nineteen years, to a woman from another county and not to the man of thirty-one, and did not know whether saying it was a thing he was doing for his brother or a thing he was doing for himself. `617`, `619`

**A MAN OF FIFTY-FOUR SAID YES TO A SECOND CHAIR, FOR A PIECE OF GROUND HE HAD NEVER WALKED, AND IT COST SOMEBODY ELSE.** He has held the gravel at the head of the drain for nine years and took the plank as well, **and said one true thing in it about the gravel and not one word about the quarter mile, and about nine people in that town had it as being about the road by the Monday.** A man of sixty-eight, who keeps the quarter mile, cannot correct it, **because he is not in a chair and there is no form in this county that lets a man ask to be corrected in one.** `614`

**A WOMAN OF THIRTY-FOUR CAME NINE MILES DOWN THE WATER TO BE ASKED WHETHER SHE WANTED A CHAIR SHE HAS HAD SINCE SHE WAS NINETEEN, AND WHAT SHE ASKED FOR WAS NOT A CHAIR AND NOT OUT OF ONE.** **Fifteen years.** She wanted to be asked. She came down with a man of forty-one from a town two days off who has his own name against a chair in another county and has not taken it off. `619`

**AND THE QUESTION ASKED ONCE AT `620` AND NOT ANSWERED, AND IT IS NOT THE RIGHT OF REFUSAL, NOT THE VOLUME 11 QUESTION, NOT THE QUESTION OF `610`, AND NOT THE VOLUME’S NEW QUESTION:** *whether the saying of no in the open has to be written down the way the saying of yes is written down, and if it is not, who decided that, and when, and on what day, and out loud in front of about nine people the way everything in that town is done.* A woman of thirty-four asked it on a Sunday. **Nobody in that town answered it. The woman who could answer it best has not come up that plank since the sixth day of the hundred and ninth week, and she is forty feet off at a landing, and nobody went and got her.** `620`

**THE SECOND SPLIT IS INSIDE THE NINE AND IS NOT THE FIRST ONE.** The split from the sixth day was between people who were on a deck and people who were not. **This one is between about nine people who think a count is a fact and about four who think a count is a thing that happens to you, and it is about a day written against a name.** A woman of forty-one with two carts said that if the day went on a sheet she would have to say what the load was in the same morning, and a man of fifty-four would have had to say his barrow and a half on the Saturday instead of the Monday. **A woman of forty-one stopped coming up the plank on the Friday, nobody sent for her, and the man of thirty-one said out loud that he was not going to go and ask her why.** `618`

**A MAN OF SIXTY-ONE SAID NO TO THE PENNY IN THE OPEN AND GAVE THE TRUE REASON, WHICH IS THAT HE DOES NOT WANT A ROOM.** Eleven years without a room with the woman who keeps the chairs, two of them, and one of them has to be able to say no to the other about something that is not the book. **About four people in a town of about eleven hundred people now know that the man who takes the money would rather nobody counted it, and he said so himself before anybody else could, and he is not a better man for it.** `618`

HE IS STILL NOBODY’S — there is no column in that county with his name at the top of it, no form that renews him, no rate on him, and he is not one of about thirty men, because there is no hundred in that county either, AND A SHEET OF DEAL WITH TWENTY-NINE LINES ON IT IS NOT A COLUMN WITH HIS NAME AT THE TOP OF IT, WHICH HE SAID OUT LOUD AT `617`.** `611`–`620`
