**`921` — Six hundred and seventy-first morning · 153 / 6 · a Sunday · settlement 521 · fever 91w 1d · since the division 475 · since a page was read 421.**
**WATER: SIXTEEN INCHES, HOLDING, ONE DAY OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER.** Corvin Tace gives three figures and adds that he will not make anything of one day. **THE CHAPTER DID:** opened the promise Pol Dunnage made on `919` — the oven went out on the Sunday he named, he is on a fire in the far corner of his own bakehouse, the loaf is grey and heavy, and the village has said so; a woman at the end of his lane has told his wife the far corner is not even a bakehouse and he will not go and ask her what she meant; **and NEVIN TRASK, SIXTY-ONE, KEEPS A BOOK OF HEADS AND CARTS, WAS ON THE PAGE OF VOLUME 19 FOR THE FIRST TIME** — he asks the baker once about the fifth day of the hundred and fifty-third week, gets an ordinary account of shutting an oven down, **writes nothing down, and gives the reason in a mouth**, which is that a cart goes up a road and what the baker did was in a room with a door on it and a man in it. Barnaby Crowe says his **two hundredth** and says he did not ask anybody and gives the reason that there was water to come off that step and he wanted to see it come off. Wat Marshe says **two hundred and sixty-eight** and says he has not asked anybody and nobody has asked him and that those are two different things. Ilyan Vester says **three hundred and sixty-seven** in the county and the cut is **two hundred and thirty-six** days old and the standing offer is **two hundred and twenty-one** days old, and makes his **sixty-first** wrong thing, which is that a man with a head full of a thing he cannot put anywhere will go and look for a use for it. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** write anything down, ask anybody twice, print a figure of people, name a weekday, name a day, or answer the question that has been standing open on that step since `919`. **IT ENDED ON:** a man with a book walking up a road with a Saturday in his head and nowhere to put it.

**`922` — Six hundred and seventy-second morning · 153 / 7 · a Monday · settlement 522 · fever 91w 2d · since the division 476 · since a page was read 422.**
**WATER: NONE. OFF ALL NINETY. THE THIRTY-SECOND TIME, AND THE ABSENCE OF INCHES, OF STEPS UNDER AND OF DAYS SAID OUT LOUD IN A MOUTH.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the band engine in a mouth. On an off-morning the whole county is out on the road; Trask has been up the cart road since the fifth hour and has asked the man who sets Pol Dunnage's bread **once**, and the answer is **the same words in the same order** as the baker's, which he did not expect. A man at the top of the steps says *that is a check*. Trask refuses it with the working: a book is a thing a man can read who was not there; what he has is two accounts of a morning, both of them of what a man did, and **neither one of them is the morning**; and the only thing that would put them against the morning is the morning, and there is no third person — **and there is no third thing.** A woman begins to say a figure and is stopped before the number is out, and Trask says he would not have that said on that step and gives the reason, and Barnaby Crowe says out loud that for twenty years he has been the man on that step who wants to know how many people were in a room and that he has never once wanted to hear the same thing again out of a different mouth. Barnaby's figure is **two hundred and first**; Wat's is **two hundred and sixty-nine**; Ilyan gives **three hundred and sixty-eight**, the cut at **two hundred and thirty-seven**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-two**, and his **sixty-second** wrong thing, which is that he went to get a second account because he wanted a second one. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** print a figure of people, write anything down, print the count of asking, or repeat a third morning. **IT ENDED ON:** a man with a book letting a week go out of itself without putting anything in it.

**`923` — Six hundred and seventy-third morning · 154 / 1 · a Tuesday · settlement 523 · fever 91w 3d · since the division 477 · since a page was read 423.**
**WATER: FOUR INCHES, SEVEN DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT NINETY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the case that required no argument about the world. The man at the bench comes up the cart road **of his own accord**, nobody having asked him, and gives the morning **before** the Saturday — that on the fourth day of the hundred and fifty-third week he told the baker the damp was not the wall, and that the baker shut the oven down some hours later, and that none of that came out of what he said. And he says the thing the band exists for, unasked, before anybody argues about it: **nobody on that step has asked him about any day except one day, and he gave it once, and he would rather the man did not come up and ask him a second time to see whether he says it different** — because then it is in two places, and two places is a thing you can check, and if it can be checked somebody will do it, and then they will come and ask him again, and he will give them the same words, and then there will be three. Wat Marshe says **two hundred and seventy** and says he has had a thing for a fortnight and has not come up with it, and why. Barnaby Crowe says **two hundred and second** and says he is not going to be the man who asks the new one anything and gives **no reason at all**. Ilyan gives **three hundred and sixty-nine**, the cut at **two hundred and thirty-eight**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-three**, and his **sixty-third** wrong thing, which is that a man who gives a thing up unasked has found the way to do it properly — refused by the man at the bench, who says a thing everybody does becomes a rule in a fortnight and then a man asks him a second time because a rule is a thing that has to be kept. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** print a figure of people, write anything down, discover what the nine positions are, or ask the man at the bench anything. **IT ENDED ON:** a man going down the cart road with a morning in his mouth that he had handed to a step and had been told would not be held by anybody.

**`924` — Six hundred and seventy-fourth morning · 154 / 2 · a Wednesday · settlement 524 · fever 91w 4d · since the division 478 · since a page was read 424.**
**WATER: EIGHT INCHES, SIX DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT EIGHTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the ordinary case, and the reason it is easy. Ned Farrow comes down off the turn with the smell of the byre on him and puts the easy version in front of the step: he has asked at his bench once for nine years, has never asked anybody else, and has never had to — and it is not because he has worked anything out. Barnaby Crowe says **two hundred and third** and supplies the reason nobody had: it is not the asking once, it is the eleven days afterwards, and a man with two hundred mornings of nothing but words has never once wanted to hear the same thing again out of a different mouth. Wil Cairns comes along the wall above the ash on his way back to the parish line and gives the other half: he has cut that hedge for nine years and has never had a morning anybody asked him about, and there is a morning every year in that hedge when his man and he are the only ones in the parish who could say what was in it, and if they all stopped the parish would be a different parish in a year and **nobody could say when**, because nobody in this county was there the year before the one he is in. Wat says **two hundred and seventy-one** and says a byre is the only place he has ever stood where nobody asked him anything, and that a quiet place is not the same as a place where nobody is going to come. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy**, the cut at **two hundred and thirty-nine**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-four**, and his **sixty-fourth** wrong thing — refused by Ned Farrow, who says the being there is what is keeping it working, and that if you take the asking off this step in ten years there will not be a step to take it off, **and you have not made anything easy; you have made a hole with a shape in it.** **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** print a figure of people, count anybody's stakes, name a weekday, or print a run-length for anything. **IT ENDED ON:** a man going back up past the turn without stopping at the low wall.

**`925` — Six hundred and seventy-fifth morning · 154 / 3 · a Thursday · settlement 525 · fever 91w 5d · since the division 479 · since a page was read 425.**
**WATER: TWELVE INCHES, FIVE DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SEVENTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the cost of the ordinary case, and the clerk's refusal. Pol Dunnage comes down the cart road with a loaf out of the temporary fire, cuts it, and refuses to let anybody say anything about it for a minute; then says the wall has cost him the **shape** of the bread and not yet the bread, and says he has thought about going down the lane about nine times to ask the woman what she meant and has not gone and is not going to, because she is not wrong and there is nothing to be gained by standing in a woman's lane and being right at her about bread. **SENA DORR IS AT SIXTY-EIGHTH AND DECLINES, IN A MOUTH, TO ENTER WHAT TWO MEN SAID ABOUT ONE SATURDAY**, giving the reason twice — a run takes what came in and what went out and who stood there while it did, and a bread did not come in and did not go out — and then the worse reason: if she entered it, in ten years somebody could stand in front of that page and say *well, that settles it*, **and that is all it would be good for, and there is no end on that page.** Wat says **two hundred and seventy-two** and says a thing in a head changes shape whether you are guarding it or not. Barnaby says **two hundred and fourth** and says he gave a reason on Tuesday and it turned out to be about him. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-one**, the cut at **two hundred and forty**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-five**, and his **sixty-fifth** wrong thing — refused by the baker in nine words, with the working. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** write either Saturday into a book, ask the woman in the lane, put a figure on anybody, or thank anybody. **IT ENDED ON:** a second loaf put down on the low wall for whoever came up next, and a man walking back up the road at the pace he came down at.

**`926` — Six hundred and seventy-sixth morning · 154 / 4 · a Friday · settlement 526 · fever 91w 6d · since the division 480 · since a page was read 426.**
**WATER: SIXTEEN INCHES AND A FULL BANK, FOUR DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER — AND THE WORD A FULL BANK SAID OUT LOUD.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the band engine's second refusal, given to a different man and not in the same sentence. Garrin Tolley comes up the cart road and asks a whole step, in front of everybody, whether the same morning said twice is a check. Corvin Tace comes down one step — which he does not do — and gives the working: a check is when a thing is put against the thing itself; a gate and a gate and a gate, and the gates are the same gate; **two mouths agreeing is not that**, and it is worth exactly what a man's word is worth; and **what would put two accounts against the Saturday is the Saturday**, and there is nobody in this county who can be asked what was in that room and **nobody who could be asked again either, because they were asked once and there was nothing wrong with it.** And he adds, which is the only time in the band the house permits itself not to know: *I am not going to tell you that it is the wrong answer, because I do not know that it is the wrong answer.* Garrin then says the real reason for his own asking — that he went up a road twice because a man who asks is a man doing a thing while he waits — **and refuses to describe it**, and says he is going to stop and **is not naming a day and is not asking anybody to hold him to it.** Barnaby says **two hundred and fifth** and says he asked nobody and that if somebody comes and says there is a day against somebody's name in this county it will not be the day he wants and will not make his want smaller. Wat says **two hundred and seventy-three** and says a reason is a thing you can say twice without getting tired and the thing underneath it comes out once. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-two**, the cut at **two hundred and forty-one**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-six**, and his **sixty-sixth** wrong thing — that a man should be able to be checked once in his life — refused in nine words by Garrin Tolley: you cannot be checked once and put it down, there is no shelf for it, and a man carrying a checked morning is a man carrying it. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** tell him he was wrong, tell him he was right, name a day for him, count the notches on his board, or give the figure of his board's age. **IT ENDED ON:** nobody on that step saying anything at all, which was the first morning in a fortnight that had happened.

**`927` — Six hundred and seventy-seventh morning · 154 / 5 · a Saturday · settlement 527 · fever NINETY-TWO WEEKS OLD · since the division 481 · since a page was read 427.**
**WATER: SIXTEEN INCHES, HOLDING, THREE DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER — AND THE ANCHOR.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** the band's only anchor, and it says the figure. Corvin Tace gives three figures and then says the fever is **ninety-two weeks old, with no figure of days beside it and not going to be one**, and says he has said it once and is not going to say it a second time that day — **and says nothing whatever about whether the figure could be checked against another one, because a figure that has no neighbour to be compared with is still a figure and still has to be spoken.** Nevin Trask says he has **not opened the book in six days** and gives the reason, and then the worse reason: if he writes it down it is a thing a man can come back to, and that is a reason to come back to, and he has wanted one for about two days and has not gone. Wat Marshe says **two hundred and seventy-four** and says he has begun to be afraid — not of anybody asking him, but of the not-asking being the thing that happens to him, and that if nobody asks him then he will be the boy nobody asked, and that is going to be how he gets talked about — **and says he does not want that asked, because then it is a thing he did to make a story and he has not got a story.** Barnaby says **two hundred and sixth** and says he has not asked the man of thirty-eight one thing and that there are six mornings coming and the not-asking is going to be the hardest week of his life. Sena Dorr is at **seventieth** and says a Saturday which is a whole number of weeks old does not get entered any differently from a Wednesday, and that the arrangement is four hundred miles off and still answering and did not answer this week and she has put no day on it **and is not going to put one on it because it is a whole number of weeks old and that is a good enough reason to do nothing.** Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-three**, the cut at **two hundred and forty-two**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-seven**, and his **sixty-seventh** wrong thing — that saying an unanswered offer every morning is the same as asking twice — refused by Corvin Tace with the working: **an asking puts something to somebody, and a figure said every morning puts it to nobody at all**, and there is not a man in this county being made to stand there and decide whether you have caught him in something. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** put a figure of days beside the weeks, editorialise about the anchor, print the count of asking, or name the day the man of thirty-eight said he would stop. **IT ENDED ON:** the fever being ninety-two weeks old with nothing standing beside it.

**`928` — Six hundred and seventy-eighth morning · 154 / 6 · a Sunday · settlement 528 · fever 92w 1d · since the division 482 · since a page was read 428.**
**WATER: SIXTEEN INCHES, HOLDING, TWO DAYS OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** a woman who is owed being asked once and is not asked, and says so before anybody does. Orla Fennimore comes up the cart road past the low wall for the first time in eleven days and says that eleven days is a long time to have nobody ask her anything, that she noticed on the fourth day of the hundred and fifty-fourth week and has said nothing for four mornings about it, and that she is saying it now **because if she says it once and then stops, this landing will have a new story about her in a week and she would rather it had the true one** — and that her question is still open, has not been improved on in eleven days and is not going to be. **Aldis Rook comes up on foot with his hands behind him, says the thing he says every time she comes to the back of that passage, will not give the words, and was asked by nobody for them** — and says he has been on his own this morning for two reasons and one of them is that he is not going to be the man on that step who finally does it for her this afternoon, because then for the rest of nineteen years that is what she will have. Wat says **two hundred and seventy-five** and says a man who moves chairs said **nineteen years** and that is longer than everything else on that step. Barnaby says **two hundred and seventh** and says he walked past a man at the head of his own gate with it in him and did not stop, because he could not have said what he wanted. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-four**, the cut at **two hundred and forty-three**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-eight**, and his **sixty-eighth** wrong thing — that a man who knows a passage every morning is a man who knows — refused by the woman of forty-four: **a man who moves a chair through a room knows where the chairs go**, he has never once said a word about what was said in that house in nineteen years and that is not a failure of memory, it is the only thing about that passage anybody has been able to keep, and if you put a question to him you will have got a sentence and **the sentence will not be worth the chairs.** **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** ask her, prepare her to be asked, ask him what he did, or relieve anybody. **IT ENDED ON:** the woman of forty-four going home past the low wall at a time when nobody was asking her anything at all.

**`929` — Six hundred and seventy-ninth morning · 154 / 7 · a Monday · settlement 529 · fever 92w 2d · since the division 483 · since a page was read 429.**
**WATER: SIXTEEN INCHES, HOLDING, ONE DAY OF THE COMING BACK, ABOUT SIXTY OF THOSE NINETY UNDER — AND THE WEEK'S SHAPE, WHICH HE REFUSES TO GIVE IN FIGURES.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** a man with a day against his name, and Crowe's want not relieved by it. Ivo Kellow, thirty-four, keeps nothing, holds no stone and no seat and no run, comes up from the low side of the ash and says out loud, because he has heard on the low side that a man on that step has been asking this county for a thing he has got, **that he has a day against his own name and has had it since the second day of the hundred and fifty-fourth week and has never been asked about it and has never wanted to be.** Barnaby asks one question — *what name* — and is told *mine*, and is told, in front of everybody, that a day against a name in this county is not the day he wants and that Ivo would not give it to him if he had it, and that if he did give it, Barnaby would have two days which would not be two of the same thing and not two of a different thing, **but a man on a landing holding a piece of paper and a man on a landing holding a want, and neither of them able to put the other one down.** Ivo names **Marda Vell** once, in a mouth, refuses to give her answer away, and says if either of them goes and asks her what he said he will know. Barnaby says **two hundred and eighth** and gives the figure **without a sentence on it**, which is the first morning in a fortnight he has done that. Wat says **two hundred and seventy-six** and says he has a list of the three times in one week that somebody said a thing out loud unasked and is not going to give it to anybody. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-five**, the cut at **two hundred and forty-four**, the offer at **two hundred and twenty-nine**, and his **sixty-ninth** wrong thing — that the landing owes a man something for being refused in the open — refused by Sena Dorr at **seventy-second**: **he has not been doing it alone, he has been doing it in front of us and we have not helped, and those two things are not the same**, and nobody is going to do anything about that man this week, and it is not because he is unloved, **it is because there is no third thing.** Trask comes up for the last time this week and says he is not opening the book again and is not going to give the reason a second time, and then gives the band's own verdict in a mouth: **this week has not made anybody better off.** **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** relieve Crowe's want, print a figure of anybody in that room, print the count of asking, or say what three of the nine positions are. **IT ENDED ON:** a week going out of itself at midnight without one thing on it having been settled.

**`930` — Six hundred and eightieth morning · 155 / 1 · a Tuesday · settlement 530 · fever 92w 3d · since the division 484 · since a page was read 430.**
**WATER: NONE. OFF ALL NINETY. THE THIRTY-THIRD TIME, AND THE ABSENCE OF INCHES, OF STEPS UNDER AND OF DAYS SAID OUT LOUD IN A MOUTH BEFORE ANYBODY ASKS — AND THE MORNING IS ALSO THE FIRST DAY OF THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH WEEK, WRITTEN ONCE, IN ONE PASSAGE, WITH THE MAN ON THE STEP SAYING HE IS NOT GOING TO SAY THOSE TWO THINGS TOGETHER.** **THE CHAPTER DID:** nothing at all, for about an hour and a half, and then said so. On an off-morning the whole county is out on the road and **the first thing that happens on that step is that nobody asks anybody anything.** Wat Marshe says **two hundred and seventy-seven** and says the thing that was on his mind: this was the morning it would have been easiest for anybody in this county to ask him one thing out loud and walk away, and nobody did, and he wanted it on the step that he would have taken it from anybody who offered. **Barnaby Crowe comes up with the barrow still on his shoulder — which he has not done once in three weeks — says his two hundred and ninth, and says he walked up from the bottom of the bank with a question in his mouth and took it out of it on the way, and why: he worked out on the road that the man would say it in the same words he would have said it on Wednesday, and that two accounts that fit together is not a check and is not what he has been asking for either.** Sena Dorr is at **seventy-third** and says a clerk's week turns over at midnight and that she entered the morning of the hundred and fifty-fifth week before anybody else on that step had said a word about it. Ilyan gives **three hundred and seventy-six**, the cut at **two hundred and forty-five**, the offer at **two hundred and thirty**, and his **seventieth** wrong thing — that a man who has one thing in his mouth should be given a second thing to carry — refused by the woman who sweeps the steps, who has swept ninety steps for eleven years: **a man who says a thing out loud every morning for two hundred and thirty days is not a man short of something to say; he is a man with one thing that is not finished**, and you cannot hurry the middle of a thing by handing somebody a second one to carry. **THE CHAPTER DID NOT:** answer a question, prepare a person to be asked, hand `931` its opening, print the coincidence of the off-morning and the week twice, print a figure of people, print the count of asking, or set up the morning after. **IT ENDED ON:** a man going back down the bank with a question he had taken out of it still in his pocket, and ninety steps dry and walkable.
