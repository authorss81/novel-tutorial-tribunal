# Batch 0005 Review — Volume 01, Chapters 41–50

An independent reviewer subagent audited the finished batch against `AGENTS.md`, `outline/volume-01.md`, the batch prompt, `bible/power-system.md`, and the canon state files, and against Chapters 38–40 for voice and continuity. Its findings were applied as a fix pass, and a second reviewer audited the first pass and found that the date rebuild had left the repository behind. **The prose was preserved in both, no chapter was restarted, and no planned beat, ruling term, or ending was changed.** Fourteen concrete defects were fixed in the first pass and eleven more in the second, listed below. Volume 01 is complete at Chapter 50 and the plan for Volume 02 is intact.

## Verdict

The batch is sound. All ten chapters exist as finished scenes that each end on a completed beat. There are no duplicate paragraphs, no meta markers, no chapter numbers inside prose, no `Ilya`, and no real software jargon. The batch's two required turns both land and both are structural rather than rhetorical. The volume's concrete resolution is delivered in full.

## The word *exception*, reconsidered

The first pass recorded that the ruling never uses *exception* and left it out, on the ground that the phrase and its absence are both load-bearing. A second pass disagreed with the reasoning and the survey behind it. The word is not absent from Volume 01: it is a Bench term in Chapters 4 through 26, including a **MAINTENANCE EXCEPTION** panel in Chapter 5, Dain arguing a "named exception" in Chapter 15, and a Chapter 15 titled *A Good Exception, a Bad Road*. `outline/volume-01.md` requires the span to remain open "under a narrow, renewable exception" and Ilyan's name to be "attached to the exception," and `outline/series.md` gives Volume 01's power progression as ending "with a limited local exception." What was genuinely missing was the closing settlement never being named, in the room, for what a district is about to call it.

Sera now says it, once, in Chapter 49, in the daylight Harrow has just demanded for the precedent: in this room it is an **amendment**, and in a few days it will be called an **exception**, because that is the word this city uses for a thing one bridge is allowed to do that the charter does not allow, and a word costs a clerk less than an argument costs a district. Nothing in the ten terms will stop a form being printed with the word at the top of it, and when it is, the paper in the clerk's hand is still the paper she verified and the word is not on it, and somebody will come to her and ask which of the two documents is the real one. Dain objects that it is a prediction and not an objection.

**No term of the ruling was touched, and the word was deliberately not put in the instrument.** The value is that Volume 02's engine now has its origin in the room where the settlement was made rather than arriving by implication: the reader has watched a recordwright name the mechanism, and four days later a district printed a form. The volume is called *The City of Exceptions* and this is the moment it earns the name.
