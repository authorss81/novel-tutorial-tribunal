## Volume 02: The City of Exceptions (Chapters 51–100)

### Central pressure

Orison's neighborhoods compete for limited access to the Bench while a seemingly fair rule produces displacement. A ruling made about one bridge becomes a document other districts print, quote, and copy; every copy is lawful; and the burden of each copy lands on the person standing nearest to it.

### Starting state

**CANON.** Ilyan is thirty-one, Stage 1, Unlicensed Eye, with limited standing restored by the ninth-bridge ruling and confined to that matter. He holds a spent blackglass token and a private sequence he cannot un-enter. Sera Quill is a licensed recordwright of a public record, four terms, expressly not his advocate, and she is carrying her own six-year family claim. Tarin Keel is a field route-arbiter who holds the neutral boundary and disagrees on method. Sel Harrow is the trialwright. The ninth-bridge charter has ten terms plus a continuity line beneath it that has now appeared four times and been refused four times.

### Starting relationships

- **Ilyan and Sera:** licensed collaborators in a contract with teeth. She answers for the record and not for the outcome. He does not argue with her. This is not intimacy and is not to become intimacy in this volume.
- **Ilyan and Tarin:** cautious working alliance. Tarin writes the true sentence about Ilyan's condition first and is right, and will still refuse Ilyan when he thinks refusal is correct.
- **Ilyan and the Bench:** a man whose name is on documents in rooms he does not control, produced at a review he did not choose.
- **The districts and their benches:** not a public. They copy each other, adopt what they have not read, and cannot serve each other because no instrument says how to serve a district.

### Starting power level

**CANON.** Stage 1, Unlicensed Eye. He can find seams in a stated rule inside a live hearing field and cannot act on them without a party, a bench, or a body. His local language is a bounded work vocabulary of roughly two hundred guild terms. **Delivered in this volume: Stage 2, Seam Reader, arrived Chapter 56, failed in the same chapter, paid for in Chapter 58, and by Chapter 65 become a practice rather than a power.** See `state/continuity.md` for the exact terms and costs.

### Major locations

**CANON, all four miles and less from the basalt block.** The ninth bridge and its workroom, keeper, counter, and bell. The fourth bridge, four square holes, and Wist Anhalt. Halloway: the low-water culvert, the oak gate on its frame, the standpipe, the room over the pump, Weir Lane, the flat, and the two unnamed yards a hauler called *the bit by the wall* and *by the pump*. Coldwater, the weighhouse, and the Slipway Lane coal yard. The First Verdict, which is a basalt block standing in the open air with eleven places in a morning. The standing authority of preservation: a desk with a stamp, no clerk, and an incoming index that stopped four years ago. The Bridgeward, where a region has no district, and a road that works.

### Major factions

- **The Bench and its trialwrights:** procedurally careful, publicly honest, and unable to compel anything they have not been given power over.
- **The district benches of Orison:** nine of them, short of hands and short of money, and now on public record as holding nothing a poor person can swear to.
- **The bridge trade:** a guild out of money, four benches lent to a court it has no standing in, six crews that have voted for nothing, and a term book that is the only document in the city that says what its own words mean.
- **The copied:** districts that adopt a settlement by copying it out of a book because a sealed letter told them to and nobody told them the cost. They are the mechanism and they are not villains.
- **The preservation authority:** an office that holds everything printed in this city for four years and has never been asked.

### Midpoint reversal

`outline/series.md`: *a different chamber copies "lawful traveler" from a maintenance ruling to close a poor district during a sanitation dispute.*

**CANON. Delivered, Chapters 51–53.** Halloway's clerk printed an extract of the ninth-bridge settlement on the second day after the ruling and put it on a gate board with four nails and no glass; its fourth paragraph turns the charter's own two-hundred-year-old word for *registered* into a reason to shut a gate, against a traveller book a hundred years out of date. **The reversal acquired a second and third body.** Coldwater's Form C-4 closed a slipway lane and made a man with a sledge sixteen shillings a load where he was four, correctly, and a woman with a coal yard is four times poorer with no word she can take hold of and shake. And the appeal worked perfectly and reached nobody who needed it, because the woman it was for was not eligible and the word that excluded her was written by the court in a week when the whole case was about whether a man could stop a cart.

### Escalation sequence

1. **Chapters 51–60 — the copy, Batch 0001. CANON.** A print on a gate, a rate with no person in it, a gate hand owed for thirty years, a count that returns five places instead of a ward, the first appeal in the record filed by the only person entitled to file it, and Stage 2 arrived and paid for. The volume's question is reported by two people and not investigated. **The neighborhood hearing is set for the twenty-ninth and not held.**
2. **Chapters 61–70 — the category and the choice, Batch 0002. CANON.** A magistrate's written question: may a district pay a person who has no address? The answer is that *ward* is a guild's word for a duty and not a place, and the entered finding is that **a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does.** Two texts are put in front of a room and both cost the water; a magistrate who is right votes for the clean one and is out-voted four to one. The charter is made with a person in its fifth term and with a carrier's clause that names Ilyan Vester and stays the district's appeal if he refuses. The first appeal is heard, works perfectly, and produces a duty to answer with no power behind it; nine benches answer, seven say they hold nothing, and one preservation authority nobody had asked says yes. **The volume's climax is staged and paid for. Its resolution is not delivered.**
3. **Chapters 71–80 — the first ask, Batch 0003. PLANNED, and the batch prompt exists.** The carrier's clause has never been used. A district that never asked for the charter asks for the terms and cannot pay for the printing, so the paper is what is asked for. A word printed at the top of a form costs a working man something small and irreversible. The holder is used. Somebody has to try to set a rate for thirty years and get a number that is wrong in a way that matters. **The public maintenance review falls on the twenty-seventh of the third month, inside this batch, and the batch must reach it.**
4. **Chapters 81–90 — the survival of the appeal. PLANNED.** The appeal has to stop depending on one poor district's willingness to keep paying half a farthing on a pump. The pressure is that a right which costs money and has no standing outside one bench is a favour with a seal on it. **This is the half of the volume's resolution that is not about Sera.** It must be paid for in somebody's water, and the person who pays must not be Ilyan.
5. **Chapters 91–100 — the fragment. PLANNED.** `outline/series.md` requires that **Sera recovers a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms.** It must arrive as work, in a room, at a cost, and it must be a **fragment**: one quarter of a sentence on a facing leaf, read as a duty and not a place, and it is already on the page in Chapter 70 sitting beside a district's page over a pump with the statement that they are not the same sentence. **Chapter 70 has put the two side by side and the batch that delivers the fragment must not be able to top that comparison.** Her own family claim stays unfiled and unresolved; the removed page and her missing duplicate stay two events on two dates.

### Volume climax

**CANON, staged at Chapter 68 and paid for in Chapter 69.** `outline/series.md`: *a neighborhood hearing forces him to choose between a clean ruling that saves the district temporarily and a messy shared charter that gives residents an appeal.*

Form L-3, eight clauses, written and signed by a weighbridge convener with a cart on the scales, closes a gate, is payable, pays four hands by name in chalk, unhouses nobody, and puts a quarter of a penny on every bucket in Weir Lane for ever with nobody in it able to say no. The charter of the district of Halloway is nine sheets, four terms copied off a bridge and a fifth that wants a person, and costs half a farthing a bucket — smaller only because the gate is shut. **Ansa Rell votes her own weight for the clean instrument, in writing, signed, for ever, says the whole of the case out loud, and is out-voted four to one, and is not defeated and is not redeemed.** Ilyan's entire contribution is nine words: *I am for the charter, and the reason is that a gate is not the problem.* Maren Kett, his own advocate, argues against him for the rest of the hour and says on the record that he has been out-argued.

The immediate promise of the volume is delivered. A poor district has an appeal, a pay line, a page, a rate, and a person to send for. **The larger question is not.**

### Concrete resolution

**PLANNED for Chapters 71–100. Nothing in this list is delivered yet.**

- **The appeal survives as a durable right, and it survives by costing somebody.** The survival is not a ruling that the charter is good. It is the charter still being enforceable after the district that made it has been squeezed, and the mechanism by which it survives must be visible, ordinary, and unpaid by the person it protects.
- **Sera recovers a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms.** A fragment, not the clause. The right of refusal is **not** restored, and the historical question is **not** answered.
- **Ilyan produces himself at the public maintenance review** with his report and the two copies of his own sequence, and says out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and may not send somebody with his name on it.
- **Ansa Rell is not redeemed.** Her defeat produced a difficult ally and that is what it produced.
- **No one gets a new power.** Stage 2 stays Stage 2. The token is spent further, at a higher cost, and the rendering, the vantage, and the door are touched and not resolved.
- **The continuity line is refused a seventh time, in the same words, and nobody is named.**

### New question for the next volume

**CANON as asked, PLANNED as answered.** `outline/series.md`: *which region has accepted a rule with no living witness at all?* The Bridgeward. Wen Lask, seventy-one, collecting a toll her father and grandfather collected, on a good road where the toll has never been short and the loads go through, returned one question on the twenty-ninth of the second month: **DOES YOUR PAPER SAY ANYTHING ABOUT A ROAD WITH NOBODY IN IT.** Nobody can answer it, the court has no power over that road, and the honest answer from every door in Orison is that the paper says something about a *person*. **The volume's last movement must push this question at most twice, must not answer it, and must not damage a road that works.**

### Final chapter image

**PLANNED.** The shape is fixed by `outline/ending.md` at the series level and is not to be anticipated: the final image is an ordinary person refusing a specific rule with visible consequences and remaining part of the community, with no System panel declaring the choice correct. For this volume, the image that should be carrying the weight is smaller: **a person in one poor district reading a rate aloud at a pump at the sixth bell, out of a page she is allowed to keep and not allowed to appeal from, to about two hundred people who have been told all year what it costs them and have not once been asked.**

## Volume guardrails

- **Do not restore the right of refusal in this volume.** The Quiet Chamber page is missing, the facing leaf yields one line, and one quarter of a sentence now has to be read as a duty and not a place. That is a consequence, not a recovery, and `outline/series.md` assigns the recovery of a *fragment* to this volume and the answer to a later one.
- **Do not resolve the Bridgeward.** It is the volume's new question and it is one step further along and no further.
- **Do not name who writes the continuity line.** Nine appearances, six refusals, no name, after Chapters 81–90. The seventh refusal is reserved for Chapters 91–100 and is a volume-level promise.
- **Do not give Ilyan a party, a bench, a body, a remedy he chooses alone, or the right to assign another person's burden.** His standing is confined to the ninth-bridge matter and his carrier's clause is a duty with a consequence, not an office.
- **Do not introduce a new final enemy.** The First Witness remains the distant institutional voice already established in Volume 01, and Sel Harrow, the district benches, the copied instruments, the forms, and the preservation authority are immediate pressures. The antagonist for this volume is **a category that cannot say who is in it**, and it has no villain.
- **Do not turn the word *exception* into law.** It is a Bench term, it is at the top of a printed form, and it must never appear in any instrument in this case. Let it travel as a word and let it cost somebody.
- **Do not resolve the authorship question or the Shale Mirror.** The token may be reached, at a cost, and the vantage touched. The rendering has a place he cannot find and the fourth layer is a door.
- **Preserve ordinary scale.** Every promise in this volume is carried by a gate hand, a hauler with a barrow, a landlord who takes a shilling a week, a clerk who printed the thing, a coal-yard woman who wanted a cart, and a keeper of a bridge that has wanted four props for twenty-nine years.
