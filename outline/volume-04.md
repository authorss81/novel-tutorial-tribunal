## Central pressure

**CANON, verbatim from `outline/series.md`:** *A food and debt crisis in the Ninefold Shelf becomes profitable to turn temporary exceptions into lifelong obligations.*

**PLANNED, in this volume's own words, and it is the sentence every chapter in the volume has to be able to survive:** **the Harvest is good and the harvest is being taken.** There is no blight and no famine and nothing has gone wrong anywhere that anybody could name. What has gone wrong is that a bad year, three years ago, was made permanent by a paper, and the paper is a piece of stationery, and it is priced the same for everybody, and the price of the paper is two shillings and eightpence.

**PLANNED, the volume's thesis, and it is not a thesis anybody is going to be allowed to say out loud in a room.** On the Ninefold Shelf a temporary exception is not a promise to a person. It is a **permission**, and a permission is *exercised by anybody the holder points at it*, and nothing in the Bench's form asks who exercises it, because the form asks **who carries the burden** and the burden is on a district and the district is not the party who moves the grain. **So the exception can be spent, and it will be spent on the only thing that can spend it, which is carriage; and carriage belongs to somebody else; and the district's protection is now being used by the owner of the carts; and the district pays interest on a loan it took out to have had its interest suspended.** No rule is broken. No document is forged. No person lies to anybody. The Bench's own slogan — *no burden without a name* — is what makes it transferable, because the name is the district's and the name is not the user.

**PLANNED, the sentence the trade will say by the forty-fourth week, and it must be said in an ordinary mouth and by a person who is not him:** *Nobody in this district is being cheated. Nobody in this district is being told anything.*

**PLANNED, the antagonist is a class and the class has one honest member and the honest member is right.** There is no villain in this volume's first movement. **Ors Halm, the Chancellor**, is the author of no forgery, no threat and no lie, and his system put nine thousand people into a winter meal last year, and the four lines he says about it in Chapter 160 are every part of them true, and the volume does not put him in the wrong once. Per the series outline, **his first scheme is exposed at the volume's resolution and his financial network survives**, and per `bible/characters.md` he is removed from one office and not from the network, which is a shape this volume's later batches must honour. **He is already rung 3 of the ladder and is not a new enemy.** Nothing in this volume makes the world bigger.

## Beginning state

**CANON.** Ilyan is thirty-one. **Stage 3, Witness Broker, arrived in Chapter 147 of Volume 03, late, by a practice, a failure, a relationship and a permanent cost.** Being Stage 3 is a duty with a consequence and not a power: he is responsible for the safety of the people he has linked and for keeping their accounts apart, and **the permanent cost is a permanent liability on his own record** — three names in a bundle with his own at the foot of the third sheet, not sealed, not locked, and any clerk two hundred miles up the Saltglass coast can ask for it, copy it, and carry the copy to a shore he has never stood on. **He cannot join accounts, cannot compel a person, cannot read, and cannot produce a record.** He has no constituency, no party, no bench, no body, no office and no standing on any coast or in any city. He holds a spent blackglass token he has used four times, and the fourth use took his words, and the token is not touched again. He has an afterimage with a four-hour limit, a left hand that is a scar at the base of the thumb in four years, a rendering at the same height and the same distance, a vantage that has not moved, and a door in a picture at the same height and the same distance since the third of the second month. **Corva Silt and no clerk are the only two people in the world who know he cannot read.**

**CANON, and it is the thing this volume must not soften: the leg.** It has cleared exactly once, on the fourth of the third tide at Thornglass, for about four hours, and it has not been his own on any day since. It was attacked twelve times on that coast, in twelve named chapters, and he told nobody in any of them. **A carried notice cleared it for four hours once, in Volume 01, and the finding written down at the time is that the paper was the weight and the woman was not.**

**PLANNED, made concrete, and it is this volume's language limit and not a plot:** **he arrives in the Ninefold Shelf with about two hundred guild terms of a bridge-and-weighhouse trade, a finger he can move along a line, and a working inability to read a clerk's sentence, a ledger, a register, a lease, a scale's ticket, or the words on a form.** **In this volume he never learns to read a lease, a warrant, a register or an account, and the chapters are written so that the things he cannot read are the things the plot turns on.** He states it out loud at least twice in Batch 0001, and one person on the Shelf works it out and says one sentence about it, and she is a clerk who has read a legal instrument aloud to a room for eleven years.

**PLANNED, the shape of the first batch's arrival, and it is a change of shape and not a bigger one.** Volume 03's practice was asking three people, out loud, whether they would like to be the one standing there. **Volume 04 opens with the same sentence arriving from the other end: a bench of four asks a stranger, in its own room, in public, on a day the bench appoints, whether he will help — and he says yes and says the price, which is that his name goes in another book in a district he has never been in, and he says out loud, before they agree to anything, that he has been produced four times and every one of them was by a thing that was simply nearest to him.** He is not produced a fifth time in this volume. **Being asked and being produced are the same outcome and they are not the same act, and the difference is a room with a door in it and one day of a stranger's work, and the volume's first movement is him learning that the difference is real and worth less than he thought.**

**PLANNED, how it is staged, and the shape of it is his flaw exactly.** In Chapter 156 a clerk of a Bench counter tells him that a district which sends nobody is carried as uncooperative. **The clerk is right.** Ilyan says that a district which sends nobody is a district that has decided it has nothing to say, and that a decision is a thing a person makes, and that he will not pretend otherwise in a room — and the clerk is satisfied, and the reader is satisfied, and nobody remarks on it. **It is a true sentence, it is about him, and it is said by a man who has been produced four times by whatever was nearest to him and is bracing in advance.** In Batch 0002 the sentence is in a deposition, and the words *has decided* are read as *has refused*, and a district of six hundred people who cannot afford to lose four days of work is carried as a district that will not help itself. **The cost of it is not that he was wrong. It is that he was right, early, about a category, in order to spare a clerk the unpleasantness of an exception, and the district paid for it.**

**PLANNED, and the words must be his and must be exact, because the batch that delivers the reversal will need them verbatim:** *A district that sends nobody has decided it has nothing to say, and a decision is a thing a person makes. I will not stand in a room and call that anything else.* — **Chapter 156, said to a clerk at a counter, in the ordinary way, about a category in the abstract, and not about Ryefold, and he did not know he was talking about Ryefold and will not be permitted to have said so afterwards.**

## THE MONEY, LOCKED. EVERY FIGURE BELOW IS FIXED AND THE PROSE MUST MATCH IT EXACTLY.

**This section exists because this project has spent three volumes being bitten by figures, and because the Volume 03 close's standing instruction is that a count with no line number beside it is unverified. Every figure here is arithmetic-checked and the working is shown so a reader can check it instead of believing it.**

- **Ryefold's account with the Shelf granary account: nine thousand two hundred and forty pounds (£9,240)** when the forbearance was granted. Advanced in the shape of subsistence and seed, **with a lien on the district's next three good years.**
- **The account's charge is one shilling in the pound a year. £9,240 at one shilling in the pound is £462 a year exactly.** Check: 9,240 shillings ÷ 20 = 462 pounds. **This is the figure that has to be right, because every other figure in this section is a multiple or a fraction of it.**
- **at one shilling in the pound and nothing on the principal, the principal is the same figure in ten years and in fifty. A district that pays the interest and nothing else does not have a debt it is paying off. It has a debt it is attending.**
- **The forbearance** was granted in the **thirty-ninth week of last year for one grain year, fifty-two weeks**, and suspended **£462**, and **it expired in the thirty-ninth week of this year — the week BEFORE the volume opens.** Nobody in Ryefold has ever been told the date, because the date is in a record in Orison, **and the district is six days past the end of its own protection and does not know.** This is the volume's engine in one fact and a reader should be able to reconstruct it from the two weeks alone.
- **What the district spent the exception on, and this is the whole of the volume:** the district was under the forbearance for a whole grain year and therefore did not pay four hundred and sixty-two pounds of interest, and it spent the money on getting its harvest in. **The granary holds about eleven thousand loads. The cut moves about three thousand four hundred. The district owns four carts and they move about three hundred. About three thousand loads are hired at three shillings a load, which is four hundred and fifty pounds, and the weigh and loading fee is about ten pounds, and the bill is four hundred and sixty pounds.** Check: 3,000 × 3s = 9,000s = £450. **THE EXCEPTION WAS WORTH FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-TWO POUNDS AND THE CARTS COST FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS TWO POUNDS AND THREEPENCE, AND THE CARTS BELONG TO OTHER PEOPLE.** Check: 462 − 460 = £2 3d.
- **The hire went on the account, because the account is the only credit a tenant district has.
