# Volume 01 Outline

## Volume 01: The First Hearing

### Central pressure

Ilyan must answer a bridge case in Orison while the Bench decides whether his arrival is a breach. A collapsed public bridge has produced a live covenant conflict: its charter promises safe passage to registered lawful travelers, and an unlisted child is initially trapped beneath it. After the immediate rescue, the case remains a fight over the child’s household’s legal access, housing, and right to appeal. Every proposed remedy changes who bears the burden, and the Bench records Ilyan’s choices as evidence about Veyra’s authorship.

### Starting state

- **Ilyan:** On Earth, he has just filed a defect report about a refusal route in *The Sovereign’s Wake*. A legacy null passage pulls him across without consent, and he lands at Orison’s ninth bridge as the First Defendant with a spent blackglass null token, no local standing, no useful local language, and no supernatural protection.
- **Immediate want:** Survive the first hearing, prove he is not Veyra’s author, and find a route home.
- **Power:** Stage 0, Unlicensed Eye. He can identify contradictions and sequence behavior, but cannot open a formal challenge without a provisional advocate or an affected community.
- **Relationship state:** Sera Quill is a recordwright forced to serve as provisional advocate because she needs the case for a family appeal. She distrusts Ilyan. Tarin Keel is a field route-arbiter assigned to contain the bridge. Sel Harrow is the Trialwright who controls formal access.
- **World state:** Veyra is functioning unevenly but is not openly collapsing. The current Bench is trusted by many people because it ended a worse crisis. Quiet Chamber records concerning withdrawal are incomplete.

### Starting relationships

- Ilyan and Sera: adversarial collaborators; she sponsors procedure, not him.
- Ilyan and Tarin: suspect observer and cautious guard; neither trusts the other’s methods.
- Ilyan and the Bench: defendant and institution; the interface is technically valid but morally suspect because Ilyan did not give informed consent to crossing.
- The trapped child and her household: potential witnesses whom Ilyan initially treats as case data rather than people with immediate danger.

### Starting power level

No formal Bench ability. Ilyan has practical testing knowledge, paper notes, a cracked pen, and an outsider’s habit of finding seams in stated rules. The System can present a claim, evidence, burden, and choice; it cannot read his mind or make the bridge safe by command.

### Major locations

- Morrowlight’s closed test room on Earth, shown only in the opening movement.
- The lower passages and public approach of Orison’s ninth bridge.
- The Chamber of Passage, where a challenge must be named and entered.
- The bridgekeeper’s workroom, a practical repair space beneath the span.
- The Quiet Chamber archive, where the old withdrawal language is missing.
- The public hearing hall where the temporary settlement is delivered.

### Major factions

- **The Bench and its Trialwrights:** institutionally protective, procedurally conservative, and capable of genuine public service.
- **The bridgekeeper’s guild:** responsible for maintenance and frightened by the cost of admitting an unlisted traveler.
- **The affected household and district:** people who need the bridge, fear the next collapse, and do not agree on what counts as a lawful crossing.
- **Local recordwrights and witnesses:** the people who must decide whether Ilyan’s challenge can be heard without becoming another rule imposed from above.

### Midpoint reversal

By the end of Batch 0001, Ilyan has won only a provisional stay, not a final settlement. The bridge remains open for one review cycle, and his proposed wording has been copied into another district’s petition. The apparent proof that a good exception can help becomes evidence that a limited remedy can become a weapon. Sera discovers that the Quiet Chamber’s copy of the right to withdraw was removed before Ilyan arrived.

### Escalation sequence

1. **Chapters 1–10 — first hearing:** Ilyan crosses from Earth, rescues the child, files a challenge through Sera, and receives a provisional stay after a public but incomplete hearing.
2. **Chapters 11–20 — the copied precedent:** The neighboring district uses the same provisional wording to keep a guild road open while shifting its maintenance burden onto an unregistered ward. Ilyan learns that a remedy copied beyond its original place can create a different injustice, while Sera pursues the missing Quiet Chamber page.
3. **Chapters 21–30 — local institutions:** The bridgekeeper’s guild, the affected household, and the district assembly disagree about who should maintain the bridge. Ilyan’s first public testimony is used against him, and his relationship with Sera becomes a working conflict rather than a rescue partnership.
4. **Chapters 31–40 — the record under pressure:** The Bench attempts to close the hearing under an emergency continuity rule. Tarin’s field action and Bram’s early route knowledge help preserve evidence, but the missing refusal clause remains only partially readable.
5. **Chapters 41–50 — full hearing:** The bridge field opens under public observation. The three remedies return, now with the copied precedent and the Quiet Chamber fragment before the court. Ilyan must propose a settlement that can survive review and be appealed, not merely save one child.

### Volume climax

At the full hearing in Chapters 41–50, the Bench offers three remedies: amend the bridge charter, break the span, or hold the hearing open. The child and household are already out of immediate danger, but the quick amendment would grant them access on paper while placing an undefined burden on nearby districts; breaking the bridge would preserve the charter and cut off their only lawful route to housing and work; holding it open risks losing the district’s shelter and supply line before dawn. Ilyan names the missing condition, accepts a personal continuing burden, and requests a public, time-limited settlement. Sera sponsors the filing, the child chooses to testify about the warning bell, and the bridgekeeper supplies the physical witness. The Bench rules that the charter must include an appeal and a visible exit, while the bridge remains open under a narrow, renewable exception.

### Concrete resolution

- The child and household are physically safe after the opening rescue and receive a legally recognized route to housing, work, and the bridge.
- Orison’s bridge receives a temporary settlement with a public review date, a named maintenance burden, and an appeal route.
- Ilyan receives provisional standing, not full citizenship or immunity, and his name is attached to the exception.
- Sera becomes formally licensed to sponsor his next case only after accepting independent responsibility for the record.
- The Quiet Chamber’s missing page remains unresolved, but a phrase from it gives Ilyan a new clue about the original refusal clause.
- Tarin and Ilyan establish a wary working arrangement; neither controls the other.
- The next-volume question is raised by a distant ruling that uses Ilyan’s private Earth sequence, proving that the bridge case was only the first visible use of a larger authorship dispute.

### Next-volume question

A ruling from a distant district uses the exact wording of Ilyan’s private Earth bug report. The Bench did not invent that language, and the district’s charter contains a missing witness. Who has been feeding Ilyan’s private words into Veyran law, and why does the answer point toward a larger citywide case?

### Final chapter image

At the end of Chapter 50, the repaired bridge carries a small new line of names beside the old register. Ilyan watches the first unlisted traveler cross under the full, time-limited settlement while Sera closes the case ledger and leaves the appeal date visible. Far away, another bridge answers with a sound like a page turning in a room no one has entered.

## Volume pressure rotation

- Chapters 1–10: action, orientation, physical rescue, witness consent, and provisional procedure
- Chapters 11–20: investigation, neighborhood politics, precedent risk, and relationship work
- Chapters 21–30: guild labor, household testimony, procedural setback, and recovery
- Chapters 31–40: field action, emergency continuity pressure, archive work, and political negotiation
- Chapters 41–50: full public hearing, moral choice, concrete settlement, and a new mystery hook

## Volume guardrails

- Do not let Ilyan solve the bridge case by simply naming a hidden bug. The missing condition must be found in local records, the bridge’s physical behavior, and a living witness.
- Do not let Sera become an unconditional ally in Volume 1. Her family appeal and professional caution justify help, not trust.
- Do not reveal the full First Compact, the First Witness’s identity, or the final constitutional answer here. Reveal only that the right of refusal was once recorded and has been deliberately removed from the current public version.
- Do not introduce a new final enemy. Sel Harrow and the bridgekeeper’s guild are immediate pressures; the First Witness remains the distant institutional voice already established in the opening.
- Preserve ordinary scale: the bridge case must matter to families, bridgekeepers, and neighboring districts even if the world is not yet ending.
