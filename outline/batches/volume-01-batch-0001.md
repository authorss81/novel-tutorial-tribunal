# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

## Batch number and range

- **Batch:** 0001
- **Chapters:** 1–10
- **Volume:** 01, The First Hearing

## Batch goal

Give Ilyan a complete first mini-arc: he arrives in Orison, survives a physical rescue, enters the Bench through a provisional advocate, investigates the bridge charter, and wins a narrow provisional stay that costs him his anonymity as a neutral outsider. The batch must prove the legal-fantasy promise without answering the larger mystery.

## Starting condition

Ilyan has just filed an Earth defect report when its tester signature activates a separate, one-use null passage and pulls him across without consent. He lands at Orison’s ninth bridge as the First Defendant moments after the passage consumes the anchor’s emergency reserve. The bridge was already failing under a crowded crossing; losing the reserve turns a survivable fault into an immediate collapse. Tamsin Ro, an unlisted child, is trapped below, and the current charter protects only registered lawful travelers. Ilyan remains at Stage 0, can understand only formal Bench records translated in his open case, has no local speech, no licensed advocate, and no automatic return route.

## Starting relationships

- Ilyan and Sera: hostile, provisional collaboration once she accepts the case file.
- Ilyan and Tarin: guard and suspect outsider; Tarin supplies narrow human interpretation before he trusts Ilyan.
- Ilyan and Sel Harrow: defendant and procedural gatekeeper.
- Ilyan and the Ro household: potential witnesses he has not yet treated as people with agency.

## Named opening witnesses

- **Tamsin Ro, age ten:** the trapped child. Tamsin notices warning-bell timing, wants to return to Neris rather than become the center of a public case, and must retain a voice in every later choice.
- **Neris Ro, age thirty-four:** Tamsin’s mother and a dye-yard tally worker. She needs a safe route to work and housing, distrusts the court because a local petition removed the household from the lawful-traveler register, and may refuse any use of her private flood account.
- **Oren Sedge, age fifty-seven:** ninth-bridge keeper and guild repairer. He understands the physical terms the System does not, wants the span safe and open, and fears that recognizing an omitted condition will make the guild liable for every future crossing.

## Translation, burden, and record guardrails

- The System translates only records and notices formally entered into Ilyan’s open case, plus the visible boundary of a hearing field. It does not translate ambient conversation, trade shorthand, tone, or local custom; Tarin, Oren, and Sera supply those human translations.
- The null passage assigns Ilyan no burden. His first voluntary burden is not offered until Chapter 4, after the affected parties are named, its source, cost, automatic end, and limits are public.
- A **bell** is a marked interval between court-bell strikes, not a single peal. The Chapter 5 maintenance exception lasts one such interval; the separate night hearing in Chapter 7 has its own announced one-bell window.
- A hearing field tests and activates disputed public records; it does not silently rewrite archival documents. Any changed or replaced working plate must have a visible physical or human cause.
- **Review** means a scheduled re-examination, **appeal** means a new challenge to a ruling, and **sunset or exit** means a clause through which a rule ends or a person may refuse it. A **provisional stay** preserves a named access for one review cycle and expires unless the Bench issues a new ruling at that review; it does not amend the charter or create an appeal. The batch grants this temporary stay, not the volume-climax appeal and exit settlement.

## Batch midpoint

Chapter 5 is the midpoint. After Ilyan and Oren keep Tamsin alive, Oren obtains a one-bell maintenance exception for a repair crew and one load path, not permission for Tamsin to cross. Their repeatable test holds only while the guild performs named labor, then exposes maintenance-review language missing from the current charter. A physical petition from a neighboring district uses the same old wording to treat refusal as a danger to everyone else. Ilyan’s Chapter 4 challenge is formally recognized, but his access exception remains ungranted. At the deadline, Ilyan attaches Neris’s private flood account to the live emergency docket without first asking whether it may become public. Sera halts the access request and immediately suspends her provisional representation, but the docket has already preserved the copied words, so Chapter 6 must address the harm before Chapter 8 permits limited representation to resume.

## Batch climax

The preliminary hearing formally places amendment, destruction, and delay before the Bench. Ilyan names a bounded burden himself, asks Tamsin, Neris, and Oren to choose their own testimony, and secures only a provisional stay: Tamsin’s household may use the bridge for one review cycle, a public review date is recorded, and the stay expires unless the Bench issues a new ruling at that review. The full case continues into the rest of Volume 1. The current charter is not amended; an enforceable appeal, right of refusal, and lasting housing or work protection remain for the full hearing. The Bench records Ilyan’s name in the stay.

## Required character changes

- Ilyan shifts from treating the case as a puzzle to accepting a public, continuing burden.
- Sera shifts from reluctant sponsor to a provisional advocate who files a licensing recommendation; she remains provisional until the full Volume 1 hearing.
- Tarin shifts from containment guard to cautious working ally.
- Ilyan and the Ro household establish a non-romantic witness relationship built on consent.
- Sel Harrow becomes a recurring institutional obstacle rather than a one-scene villain.

## Required power changes

- Ilyan remains Stage 0 Unlicensed Eye through Chapter 9. Chapter 5’s doubled vibration is ordinary physical strain from touching an active plate during a load shift, not an early Seam Reader effect.
- Ilyan learns to formulate a challenge with Sera: claimant, affected parties, disputed condition, burden, and remedy. Recognition in Chapter 4 permits a request; it does not grant an exception or complete his stage growth.
- The one-bell maintenance exception belongs to Oren’s repair crew and load path. Ilyan’s separate, narrow access exception arrives only with the Chapter 10 stay.
- Ilyan reaches early Stage 1, Case Opener, in Chapter 10 after fulfilling his bounded Chapter 4 duty with a willing advocate; Tamsin, Neris, and Oren choose their own testimony rather than carrying his burden.
- The public name attached to the case becomes a resource and a liability.

## Required world reveals

- Covenants have subject, condition, burden, witness, remedy, and sunset or exit; a review date, appeal, and exit are not interchangeable.
- The Bench can create a bounded hearing field that tests active records under heightened observation, but neither the field nor the System can rewrite an archival plate or guarantee agreement.
- The bridge’s old charter contains a removed or unrecorded maintenance-review condition, and that language already has a copied local precedent.
- The right of refusal was once recognized in a public record, though the current Quiet Chamber copy is incomplete.
- A continuity voice briefly intrudes on the System’s ordinary translations, preserving the tester record “before remedy.” Ilyan cannot yet tell whether this is a separate institution, an older layer of the Bench, or something else; the First Witness is seeded, not identified.

## Open questions after the batch

- Who removed the bridge’s missing condition?
- Why does a distant ruling echo Ilyan’s private Earth report?
- What does the Bench mean by “playable”?
- Is Sera’s family appeal connected to the same missing clause?
- What personal cost will Ilyan’s name attachment create in later cases?

- **Continuity fact:** Ilyan is a former game tester, not a fighter or lawyer. *The Sovereign’s Wake* is cancelled, and the report is never merged. The null passage worsens an existing partial failure rather than creating Tamsin’s legal exclusion.

- **Next-chapter pull:** The bridge’s work lights die in sequence, its anchor rods lose tension, and Tamsin’s hand moves beneath the span just as Ilyan reaches the lower approach.
