## 1. THE STATE OF VOLUME 11'S QUESTION, WHICH IS THE FIRST THING A VOLUME 12 PLANNER NEEDS AND THE LAST THING IT MAY NOT SETTLE

**VOLUME 11 ASKED ITS QUESTION OUT LOUD AT `550` AND DID NOT ANSWER IT. IT IS OWED TO VOLUME 12 UNANSWERED.**

> *Can an external person become a citizen of a county without being taken into the shape of the thing the county tells about itself?*

**HOW IT WAS ASKED AND WHY IT WAS NOT ANSWERED, from the chapter and not from a state file.** A man of eighty-one who kept the book before his daughter asked it standing in a room with the door open in front of about nine people, at `550:97`, and said out loud beforehand that there is no form in the county of Aldwick that lets anybody ask it. The keeper of the book **said it out loud in the voice she uses for minutes** so that about nine people would know it had been asked, at `550:107`, and then **refused to answer it, at `550:111`: *I am not going to answer it. I am the only person in this county anybody can be sent for, and I have been asked one question this morning, and it was not that one.*** And the narration closes it: *Nobody in that room answered it then and nobody answered it afterwards.*

**THE PROOF IT WAS NOT ANSWERED ANYWHERE: `citizen` occurs twice in all fifty chapters of Volume 11 and both are at `550`.** `preferred story` 0. `absorbed` 0. `First Witness` 0 case-insensitively as a string.

**AND THE FACT THAT MAKES THE QUESTION AND THE MAN THE SAME QUESTION, WHICH A PLANNER CANNOT GET FROM A SUMMARY: the man who is the record of the refusal is himself the first person in the manuscript who cannot be sent for.** Nobody in the county of Aldwick can ask him anything; there is no form in that county that says what a man has to do to stop being a person who can be asked about; and a man of fifty-four said out loud in a porch in front of about nine people that the difference between those two is *the whole of what has happened to you in a hundred and forty-one days*. He is Co-Witness at `550` and he was Co-Witness at `501`, and the stage did not advance in any of the five bands, and there is no panel, no form, no office and no keeper anywhere in the volume.

**WHAT THIS MEANS FOR VOLUME 12, STATED AS A CONSTRAINT AND NOT AS A DIRECTION: the question is open, the man is in the open, and the first time a planner writes a scene in which anybody answers it, a volume of a chalk town has spent the series' question. The series' question is not whether he belongs. It is whether belonging can be had without being taken into the shape of what the place tells about itself.**

## 4. `state/continuity.md` IN FULL, WHICH IS THE GROUND OF A CHALK TOWN AND THE DISTANCES IN IT

**THE WHOLE FILE IS CARRIED, NOT SUMMARISED, AND IT IS AT `state/continuity.md`. Its header already reads *Volume 11 closed, Chapter 550*. The five things a planner must not lose out of it, and the standing rule that catches the most expensive mistake in this repository:**

**THE STANDING RULE: A NEW PLACE GETS A NEW DISTANCE AND THE DISTANCE GOES INTO `state/continuity.md` ON THE DAY IT IS INVENTED — not on the day it changes, and not when a checker notices. This is index rule 15 and it has caught a real error: the state file said the town two days off was three days away for the whole of the first band, and thirty-nine consecutive date lines said two, and the file was wrong and was corrected at the repair of `batch-0002`. A distance is a figure like any other and a figure nobody checks goes stale, and a stale distance is a whole book built on a wrong map.**

**THE GROUND.** Bramblefold is a market town of about two thousand four hundred people on the chalk slope at the head of a river, in the county of Aldwick, in a country district, **not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench of any kind; the nearest counter is thirty-one miles off at Saltcaster on a road.** A market on a **Thursday**; a lime kiln burning nine days in ten; a smithy; a chandler's; a saddler's; about eleven streets with a pump at the top; a millpond with no mill on it. **Not a stone room with a door open, not a hearth, not a beam, not a board nine feet up over a fire, no water board, no sluice, no mill.** **A new volume is a new substance: a chalk town with a meeting-house, a chancel, a lime kiln, a wall, a fold and an orchard, and the only instrument in it is a book that nobody can be sent for.**

- **The meeting-house** — a long low flint-and-chalk building, a square tower at the west end that has never had a bell in it, about two hundred and thirty years old. The chancel is off the south-east corner, about eight feet by eleven, one window, and **the door has the only lock in the building, because the chancel holds the vestry cupboard and the vestry cupboard holds the book.** **A room where a book is read is a room where people say things they are going to be held to, and a person not of the town is not in it, and the reason is not distrust: there is no form in the county of Aldwick that will make a stranger a neighbour.** The porch is a stone bench about six feet long with a mark worn into the stone about two-thirds along it.
- **The fold, the wall, the orchard** — about sixty households keep a beast on a hundred and forty acres of down inside a common wall about eleven feet high and four miles long, with a chalk coping a foot wide set on edge. The orchard is about eleven acres and about nine hundred trees inside the wall, eleven households work it, and the lower corner is a frost pocket where the fruit is a fortnight later.
- **Kiln Lane** — four feet of garden wall on the lane side in places, built out of the white the kiln makes, and about nine of those stones carry four letters and a stroke cut into the face of them, and **nobody in this county can say whose they are.**
- **The distances in the volume, all of them** — Coldcombe is a market town **twenty-one miles** off. **Aldwick is nine miles**, and the county offices are there, and the office keeps one book of returns and one book of orders and can make a return and nothing else. **Saltcaster is thirty-one miles.** **Ostrey is four days' cart east** and the cart road does not come near this town. **Halloway is two days from here** — a day on foot to Coldcombe and a cart the second day as far as the Four Gates, a toll bar four miles short of Bramblefold, and the last four miles on foot; the Four Gates took a penny at each of four gates ninety years back, the toll was taken off, and the list of the four gates is in the town's charter and nobody in that town can name which four. **A town four days off**, invented at `531` and entered in the continuity file the day it was invented, where a man of about thirty-four keeps a shelf, and **the town is not named in any chapter and must not be named without a distance entered in that file on the day it is named.**
- **The Descent Book** — bound in calf, four hundred and eleven years old, the size of a large family Bible, a lead tag at the foot gone the colour of a wet stone, four hands, the spine a third original and two-thirds a repair by W. Hask at a charge of four pounds. **THE FOUR COLUMNS ARE the descent; holdings, with the number and what it is; of the fold, the number of acres of a hundred and forty a household may put sheep on; and of the ground behind the meeting-house, in feet and inches, going back about two hundred and twenty years. THERE IS NO FIFTH COLUMN AND THERE NEVER HAS BEEN ONE, AND THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR DEATH, which is the finding the whole of Volume 11 was built on: a man who dies does not stop holding anything, so the book has about four hundred men a year in it and every one of them is alive in the book.** The struck leaf: ruled in the same four columns, filled to the foot, **forty-three names**, a single stroke through all of them, four words on the inner margin — **struck by order** — **no day, no name of the striker, no name of the person who ordered it, no seal, no counter, no office, no county, no number.** The household on it was the household of **Kiln Row**, it was asked about the common above the town, it answered, the answer was made use of, and **it was not lying.** Nine entries at the back on separate unruled leaves, and there are **nine and not ten**, and only the ninth has a name on it, and the ninth is *a person not of this town, name Ilyan Vester, whose record is not findable.*

## 5. THE ONE THING ABOUT BRAMBLEFOLD A PLANNER CANNOT GET FROM A SUMMARY

**This town has four columns and no fifth. It has no column for death. It has five leaves at the back of the book with nothing at the top of any of them. About nine people in it can say what a heading is a promise about, and about nine cannot. And the man at the end of the room is a person of the record and not of the place, and nobody in that room can be sent for about him, and that is the first time in eleven volumes that the record has a class over a man in it.**

**And the reason this is a floor and not a ceiling: about nine people in that room understand what a heading is a promise about and about nine do not, and that ratio is not a measurement, it is a room. It is a room in which a man of eighty-one has said the same question to himself twice and said nothing both times, and a woman of about fifty-one has stood against a wall and said one sentence, and a man of thirty-four has counted nine lines twice, and a boy of fourteen has written his own name at the foot of a thing with nothing at the top of it, and a woman of about seventy has said no twice and given a reason each time, and a weaver of sixty-two has said a name out loud and it went in at the back of a book on a fresh leaf, and a man of thirty-one has said four words and had them read back to him in a doorway before the cupboard was shut.**

**Volume 11 ended on that room and on a question nobody answered, and a planner is handed the room and the question and the twenty-six threads and nothing else. There is no instrument in this repository that can tell a planner which of those twenty-six a Volume 12 scene should touch, and the answer to that is a card and a mouth and not a number, and the two acts that have caught the most defects in eleven volumes are still free: put the figure next to the thing it describes, and read two consecutive chapters against each other.**
