## 1. The seven threads, in a planner's order, each checked against the chapters

### 1. The page that came back, with two names in a witness line
**`500:41`–`69`, `500:101`–`121`.** One page from a register in a building of four hundred people on a road four hundred miles off, delivered at the eleventh hour of the hundredth day after the settlement — a Saturday. It carries the register's printed head, its ruled columns, and **one entry: a refusal given six years ago in another county by a household dead except one person.** At the foot is a witness line with **two names.** One is a name of this town: **four letters and a stroke, in a hand not of this town, which nobody in Halloway can read.** The other is a hand Ilyan has seen twice in six weeks, once of them at `500:65` on the fortieth day, and it is the small upright hand of a recordwright — **a hand he put there himself, on a form about a town of ninety thousand people a hundred and forty miles from a city he cannot get to, before he came to this town.**

**Checked and confirmed in the chapters, and these four things are the load-bearing facts a planner must not soften.** The band **does not say** whether the woman four hundred miles off opened the range of about nine hundred refusals; it **does not say that she did not**; it **does not say the page is in answer to anything**; and it **does not name the First Witness, address it, answer it, or let anybody in Halloway read the four letters and a stroke aloud.** Wenna Crale says out loud, in the room, that **there is no way of telling from this town which of the two witnessed the refusal, or whether the hand that wrote it in this county is the hand whose name is on the line, or whether that is a different thing entirely** (`500:105`). That sentence is the volume's last word on the question and it is a refusal to know, delivered by a copyist, in public.

**Also confirmed:** the page went into the press against the west wall of the Stone House with a copy beside the door, and **any person of the town may read both** (`500:117`). It is not secret. He told nobody (`500:69`), and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says he shall and none that says he may be asked. **The volume ends with him unable to be asked about the only thing in the book that concerns him.**

**The volume's new question, verbatim in intent: why does a former game tester's signature stand beside a Veyran refusal witness, four hundred miles from anything he has been in ten volumes?** Left open. Left open on purpose.

### 2. Whether the open witness line is ever filled
**`499:63`–`77`, and `state/open-threads.md` §5 records this as nine words; the chapters say nine words and about twenty-two.** The request is a head and two lines, **about twenty-two words**, asking that a refusal given in a lane in this town **on the eleventh day after the division, by Sefa Lund, who had no right of refusal to give**, be entered upon the register of refusals. **The witness line is the other nine words, and it stands open: no name is put to it, no hand is set to it, and a copyist of this town does not set her hand to a witness line that is empty** (`499:69`).

**Checked and one correction made here: the reason a refusal cannot be entered is that *a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it, and there was nobody in that lane whose job it was to be one* (`499:75`, Perdy Sallow).** Sefa Lund put her own name to the refusal, read the two lines back over the copyist's shoulder, and **put her finger on the name and said that it was spelt right** (`499:81`). She has nothing to refuse with and nobody gave her anything for it. **The carrier's wage of one shilling and fourpence came out of the copyist's own two pounds a year and was entered under the head with the day on it** (`499:117`) — the first time in this manuscript that a cost somebody bore personally has been written into a document instead of standing in a room.

**Nothing in the county of Ostrey can say whether it will be answered and nothing in the county can ask.** A man came eleven miles and asked what happens when it gets there, and nobody in that room could tell him, and Wenna said it was a fair question and she was not going to pretend it had one.

### 3. The forty-one kitchens
**`495:45`–`75`, `495:97`–`105`, and `state/open-threads.md` §5 has this slightly wrong — corrected here.** Forty-one men, one elected head of household in each of nine wards, were called for the first time in nine years on the single question of what forty-one men can do. The answer is one sentence: **forty-one men can find out what a thousand people think and say it out loud in forty-one kitchens for a year.** (The state file said *four hundred people*. It does not: four hundred is Sera Quill's number — her building, her road, and Sera alone — and a band of this volume used it as a crowd count twice and had to be repaired. **A planner must not reintroduce it.**) They are not to decide anything; they are to ask, come back, and **say the answers out loud where about two hundred people in the square can hear them, and put them in a press any person of the town may read.**

**The last man of the ninth ward said the half that the volume is about** (`495:55`, `495:67`, `495:75`): *a body which can only find out what people think is a body that somebody else has to answer*; and then the other half — *a House which can do that and nothing else is a thing this town has built so that it does not have to answer anybody.* **He is a person who has been in this business for six weeks and is not a stranger, elected on a roll three weeks old, and he said he was not asking anybody to be wicked, he was asking them to notice the shape, and a shape is not a man.** He refused the word *wicked* out loud and would not use it.

**A man of the seventh ward discovered, on the way out of the door, that asking and telling are different** — that the charter with the exit in it is not a question, it is a thing to say yes or no to — and **nobody in this town has ever asked him which of the two he had been doing for nine years.** He was elected on a roll three weeks old; the roll he would be elected on has now been made, and **nobody in Halloway has worked out what the autumn is.**

**Nobody has been asked yet. The answers are not in.**

### 4. The collection, thirteen weeks off
**`493:33`–`45`, `493:73`–`93`, `500:33`, and every money figure in this volume.** The roll for the autumn was made on the **fortieth-seventh** day after the division, in an office over a shop, by a steward of sixty-one **working alone** from the seventh hour to noon, and it stands at **one thousand and seventy-four.** A woman selling bread came out of her own shop and told a man of thirty-one that he could stand on the step as long as he liked and it would be the same at the twelfth hour as it was now. **Nobody lied to him. The figure was not withheld; it never occurred to the man counting it that the man in the square was a person the figure was owed to, and there is no form in this county that says a roll is read out to a square and none that says it is not.**

**The whole working is printed in the chapter, in pence as well as pounds, and it is this:** 1,074 × 8d = **£35 16s 0d** a year; 1,074 × 2d = **£8 19s 0d** a quarter; the heel of the frame is **£9**; a levy of **3½d on each of the eighty lots is 280d a year = £1 3s 4d**; **a board in this county has always counted the whole of that 280d against the quarter it holds in its hand** (`493:83`, said out loud in a chapter for the first time, and it is not a thing anybody in that town had ever said aloud in a room); 2,148d + 280d = **2,428d = £10 2s 4d**, which is **268d** over the heel; 1,052 × 2d + 280d = 2,384d = **£9 18s 8d**, which is **224d** over; and **2,428d − 2,384d = 44d a quarter, which is the whole of what this volume was about and is twenty-two houses at 8d a year.** The held roll of 1,052 is wrong by 176d a year. **Nobody in Halloway will feel any of it for thirteen weeks, and the season the levy was for went on the forty-eighth day with a sound and not a finding.**

**A planner must not divide the 280d by four.** It would be tidier and `488`, `490`, `494` and `500` would all be wrong.

### 5. The unturned bar, the open gate, and a tail bar that will not hold a frame up
**`490:161`, `494`, `492`, `500:135`.** A bar four feet long, two people, an hour, at the sixth hour and the twelfth hour, for nine years whether the gates were shut or open. **The first missed winding is the sixth hour of the thirty-first day after the division, and it was not turned again on any of the twenty-four days to `500`.** The wheel at the mill is a different count and is twenty-three days slow; the bar is counted in days on which an hour came and went, the wheel in days completed, and the wheel is one behind. The gates have stood open on two hinges since the sixth hour of the fourteenth day, and the hinge is not locked, and there is no form anywhere in this county that says who may shut it and who may open it. The frame went at the seventh hour of the forty-eighth day, half a hand to the north, and a board of nine entered the fact with a day on it and **called it nothing**; a man asked whether the minute ought to call it a failure and was told to write that at the foot of it with his name against it, and did not. A man of the county came back with a nail, knocked it into the frame at a new place, wrote three figures on the back of his own hand, wiped them off, and wrote them again.

**And a second winding bar is being cut at the tail of the race**, out of the ordinary fund, out of timber this town can cut, resolved on the forty-sixth day, and **it will not hold a frame up.** A miller of thirty-nine said a thing in a room that a man of the county had to hear: *the frame is what the frame is; a second bar is a thing a town does so that it can go on not mending the first.* And a man who owns **five acres** — lot nine, two pounds, a bill of sale in his coat — came down two steps and said the other half: *a town that will not mend the thing it cannot afford will mend the thing it can, and that is not nothing.*

### 6. Ilyan on somebody else's book
**`497:83`–`93`, `497:115`–`121`, `498:75`–`103`, `500:77`–`95`.** The sheet in the press against the west wall carries **a sentence of his own, struck out with a line through the middle of it, and an objection entered at the foot with his name against it.** He had said in a market square on the Sunday of the twentieth day that a right a person has to knock on nineteen doors for is a favour and not a right, and he had been saying it in rooms for six weeks because it was the best sentence he had and because it did not cost anybody anything. **A copyist of thirty-four — the only person in Halloway who can say whether a man may be asked about a sentence he said in a square — said no in front of nine people, in about two seconds, and said it would stay not in the sheet, and then did exactly what she said she would do, in the order she said she would do it.**

**And he offered to be a party to a compact of six lines with four towns not upon a road of the Shelf, and was refused in about four sentences, correctly:** *a man who cannot be sent for cannot be obliged by this and cannot oblige anybody by it.* He said *then I will not be a party; put me down as a man who was in the room*, and was told that is the whole of what he is in five lines and it is not less than it sounds and it is not a favour. **And on the last page he asked for the second of two names to be read more slowly, and was asked why, and said *because the hand matters*, which is true and is not the whole of it, and about four people in that room heard the place where the answer stopped.** He is on nobody's book and he is on everybody else's.

### 7. Sera Quill's family record, and the volume's last question
**`477`, `485`, `489`, `499`, `500`, and the whole of the thread in `state/open-threads.md` §1 item 12 and §3.** A second sheet, folded in four, nothing on the outside, out of a bag in a market town, in the small upright hand, **about six years**, and a refusal given by a household **dead except one person**, and a claim that **has not been entered** because a refusal is entered by the name of the person who witnessed it and that person is not findable. A boy of fifteen asked, in a market square, who had witnessed the refusal in it, and the man reading it out could not answer.

**It cannot be un-read and it cannot be given back.** It was read out in about four minutes to about thirty people on the thirty-first day and to about two hundred on the thirty-ninth. **Four pages in a round hand, addressed to a water board, signed with no name at all**, came into the town on the forty-third day and say that a shelf can be built and a lane can be built and neither of the two people can build the other one's thing. They carry the six headings — a subject, a condition, a burden, a witness, a remedy, and an exit — and the seventh, the exit, is the one nobody in that county has ever had.

**Her building has sent a page. That is the first document in ten volumes that has come back.** The range of about nine hundred refusals stands on a shelf eleven inches wide in a room eleven inches from where a woman of twenty-eight sits, **and she has written down eleven times in six years that she has not opened it**, and the eleventh of those eleven was in a letter read out in a market square in this town to about thirty people and then to about two hundred, and a second letter has gone out of that town in her own hand with nothing on the outside of it, and a page has come back, **and nothing has been said about whether the range was opened.**

## 2. The eleven things 491–500 did not do, checked against the chapters

1. **No right of refusal restored, and nobody given one.** `497` says in a room, in front of about nine people, that the ninth clause is a local holding of a right that had been taken out of the law about a hundred and forty years before it was written, and the volume does not repair it. The clause is a hundred and thirty-eight years old, in a fifth hand and a different ink, on the back of a board nine feet up over a fire.
2. **The out-of-roll book not used, and not opened on the day the roll was made** — and there was nobody in that office to open it. One line, a name, a day; the last entry anywhere in this county was a man at Ostrey with no roof on him, four years back.
3. **The authorship question, the historical right of refusal, the Bridgeward, the responsible person for the C—2 chain and the rendering all untouched.**
4. **One System panel, at `497`, and one thing chosen out of it, by him, out loud, in a room, in about eleven seconds: a right that a person has to ask a body for is a favour, and the difference between a favour and a right is the whole of what a handle on the inside of a door is.**
5. **Nobody relieved, forgiven, redeemed or thanked.** A man of thirty-nine said so in a room on the thirty-eighth day, said it again on the fifty-second in front of four people who had walked a long way, and meant it both times, and there is no form in the county of Ostrey that says a man must be sorry. `grateful` is 1 in the band and it is a negation; `thanked` is 7 and all seven are negations.
6. **Nothing resolved.** The charter binds nobody. The compact binds nobody. The instrument of the fortieth day decides nothing.
7. **No new final enemy. The First Witness is not named, not addressed and not answered — the string does not occur once in fifty chapters.**
8. **The page that came back is not explained, and the band was forbidden from explaining it, and the one hand on it that can be read has not been spoken about.**
9. **No document turned out to be the answer to anything, including the one that came back four hundred miles. Nothing on a bar road is offered as the answer to anything.**
10. **No seat was scaled with a household.** The false version — that the fen is nineteen more seats — is in about forty mouths in this town, `495:9` records that nobody corrected it that day and nobody was in a position to, and **no narrator in the volume corrects it.**
11. **The four letters and a stroke are not read out.** A woman of about thirty stood at the press on the hundredth day for about six minutes and could not read the second name and **did not ask anybody to read it to her.** **No character in the band counted the households of this town.** **The forty-one kitchens have not been asked yet.** **Nobody in Halloway has been sent for and nobody in Halloway can be.**

## 3. What the volume close changed, so a planner does not plan against a version that no longer exists

**Eleven defects, all repaired, no chapter restarted, no card re-planned, no plot moved. Full record: `reviews/volume-10/reviews-volume-10-close.md`.** A planner needs four of them.

1. **The season's approach was printed wrong in seven places and at `493` it said the season was *nine weeks gone* on the day before `494` said it had come.** It arrives on the **forty-eighth** day after the division, a Sunday. **It is a day-count in every chapter now: six days at `488`, four at `490`, two at `492`, gone by `493`. A week-count for the season is always wrong in this volume.**
2. **Lot nine had become nine acres.** Eighty lots **of five acres at two pounds**; Ivo Cray holds lot nine and paid two pounds for it. A chapter had him priced four pounds below what he gave. **A name and a number in the same breath is the most dangerous place in this manuscript to put a figure, and there is no table for it.**
3. **`495` had the House ask *four hundred people*.** It is **a thousand**, which is the household count of the roll. **Four hundred is Sera Quill's — her building, her road, four hundred miles — and nobody else's.** Four hundred *years* is the covenant. Four hundred *yards* is the lane. Four hundred *pence* is arithmetic.
4. **`496`'s title said the sixth heading; the body objects to the fourth**, which is a witness, and the six had come off *the sixth ward*. The state files all had it right, which is exactly why the title survived two reviews: **a lone dissenter in a file of ten is read as the mistake.**

## 4. The three things a planner is most likely to get wrong

**These are the three figures and the three shapes in this volume that have cost a band each, and every one of them is in this note because the close found it and a planner will not find it again without being told.**

- **Do not convert a count of how long ago into a week-count, and do not convert it into a month.** There is no month anywhere in this volume and `month` is at 0 in all five bands. The band printed *six weeks* about twenty times for a stay of thirty-nine to forty-eight days, and that is a recorded convention meaning the run of the matter, not elapsed days, and the chapter that argues for it in dialogue is `476`. **A convention that has been examined is not a figure that has not been checked, and this repository has been wrong about the second kind fourteen times.**
- **The House of Halloway does not scale.** Nine wards, one elected head of household in each, forty-one in all, five in the ninth ward, elected for a year. **The false version is in about forty mouths and cannot be recalled, and no narrator in the volume corrects it.** A planner may use the false version as a thing characters say; a planner may not build on it, and no card and no sentence may rest on *seats scale with households*.
- **The two counts of the sluice are two counts.** The bar is days on which an hour came and went; the wheel is days completed; the wheel is one behind and it is a different number and not the same number wrong.

## 5. What a planner must not do with this handoff

- **Do not open Volume 11 with the answer to the page.** `outline/series.md:217` puts the Archive of Unmade Names and restored names at 501–550, and the new question at the end of Volume 11 is whether an external person can become a citizen without being absorbed into the world's preferred story. **The question this handoff hands over — a game tester's signature beside a Veyran refusal witness — is not Volume 11's question, and nothing in Volume 11 is required to answer it.** Leaving it unexplained is a decision the volume made twice, in a chapter and in a prompt, and reversing it is a planner's move and not a repair's.
- **Do not have anybody in Halloway read the four letters and a stroke aloud.** It has not been read in this volume and the woman at the press chose not to ask.
- **Do not scale the House, divide the 280d levy by four, restore the right of refusal, name the First Witness, thank anybody, or put a month in a title.**
- **Do not treat any figure in this note as checked because it is here.** It was checked against the chapters on the day it was written, and the certificate expires the moment somebody edits a chapter. **`reviews/volume-10/reviews-volume-10-close.md` §2 is the measurement; this note is the reading of it.**
