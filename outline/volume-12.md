## 3. The place, and why it is a different substance

**BRAMBLEFOLD IS NOT IN THIS VOLUME AFTER `551` AND NOTHING IN WITHERMERE MAY BE MADE INTO IT.** No meeting-house, no chancel, no vestry cupboard, no book on a key on a string, no leaf of descent, no strike by order, no fold, no lime kiln, no chalk, no smiting of a wall. **THE INSTRUMENT TURNS UPSIDE DOWN AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF THE NEW SUBSTANCE: in Bramblefold the record was in a locked cupboard and nine people could be sent for about it; in Withermere the record is a sheet of parchment about four feet wide hanging on the wall of a public weigh-house and EVERY PERSON IN THAT TOWN MAY WALK UP AND READ IT, AND IT IS THE THING A MAN IS ALLOWED TO WALK UP AND READ THAT IS USED AGAINST HIM.**

**WITHERMERE.** A market town of about **one thousand one hundred people** on the north bank of a slow river at the eastern edge of a fen, in the hundred of Withermere, in the **county of Kell**, which is a different county and a different hundred and a different kind of ground. **Not upon a road of the Shelf, with no register, no counter, no appointed person and no Bench of any kind; the nearest counter is twenty-two miles off at Kell.** **A market on a FRIDAY**, which is not Bramblefold's Thursday and is not a joke about it. A weigh-house, about sixty feet, sodden brick and tarred oak. A chandler, a smithy, a brewing house, an inn called the **Green Paw** with four beds and a parlour. **About eleven streets and a green with a pump at the top.** A lime-free ground: **peat, sallow, willow, sedge, and water-meadow hay cut in a fortnight.**

**THE SEASON BOOK.** A sheet of parchment about four feet wide and six feet long, ruled in **three columns** and hung on the north wall of the weigh-house where the wool is weighed. **The three columns are the ground, with a name of a parcel and a number of acres; the taking, with what goes on it and how many; and the year, with one figure, the year the season runs to.** **THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR A PERSON'S NAME AND THERE NEVER HAS BEEN ONE, AND IT IS THE EXACT INVERSE OF BRAMBLEFOLD'S FOUR COLUMNS, AND THE VOLUME SAYS THAT OUT LOUD IN A MOUTH IN BAND 0001. A season is not attached to a household. A season is attached to a parcel of ground, and any person who pays the rate on a parcel may hold the season on it for a year, and the holder's name is written in the column that is the ground, because there is nowhere else to write it and it is not a fault of anybody.**

**THE WATER-RATE.** Two shillings the acre a year, out of which the water-clerk is paid **four pounds a year** and the ditches are cut. The season-penny is a penny the acre on top. **THE TOTAL IN THE BAND: two hundred and ten acres in eleven holdings, at two shillings and a penny the acre, which is four pounds four shillings and ten pence a year, and the smallest single holding owes four shillings and two pence of it in a year when nothing has been cut.**

**THE RISING.** Once a year the river comes over the **tally stone**, a block of limestone with a line cut round it at the level of a full water, put there about two hundred and thirty years ago by a man whose name is on it and whose name is still legible. **The day the water comes over the tally stone is the first day of the year, and every season in the hundred lapses on that morning and is re-let on that morning, and the re-letting is done in the weigh-house with about thirty people in it and a clerk and a book on a wall.** **It is a day of a river and not a day of a calendar, and a year in Withermere is the time between one crossing of that stone and the next, and there is no month and no quarter and no reckoning from a frost.**

**THE THREE BOARDS OF THE SLUICE.** The main sluice at Withermere holds three of six boards, and the level is a foot below full. **Putting in the other three raises the water a foot.** It holds it off seven holdings and the cut on the low meadow; it takes it into four holdings and into the common on the low side where the come-overs are camped. **THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE SEVEN AND THE FOUR IS NOT MONEY AND IT IS NOT LAND WORTH MORE: the four are not richer than the seven, and the water-clerk says both halves of that out loud, and no arithmetic settles it, and that is the reason the volume exists.**

**THE COME-OVERS.** About sixty-one people off a fire on the high ground four days east, in eleven households, living on the common on the low side under a licence to stay **until the water turns**, which is the rising. They cut, they graze, and they work the ditches, and their licence is a season, and it lapses on the same morning as everything else in the hundred.
