# Power System: Benchcraft, Covenants, and the System

## The governing rule

A power in Veyra is a rule that has been made visible enough to act on. The world does not reward raw talent by itself. It responds to declared conditions, accepted witnesses, carried burdens, and consequences that remain after a ruling ends.

The **Bench** can judge and temporarily shape these rules. It does not grant unlimited spells, read thoughts, restore the dead, or guarantee that a result is morally correct. Magic and civic law use the same underlying structure: a promise changes the world when the people affected can feel the change and the change is recorded.

## Core objects

### Covenant

A binding agreement, law, or inherited public practice. A complete covenant has six parts:

1. **Subject:** the people, place, creature, or thing affected.
2. **Condition:** the event or practice that makes the rule active.
3. **Burden:** the cost, who carries it, and when it is paid.
4. **Witness:** the person, community, object, or place able to test the claim.
5. **Remedy:** what happens when the claim succeeds or fails.
6. **Sunset or exit:** how the rule ends, is amended, or may be refused.

A missing part does not make the rule harmless. It makes the rule **loose**: it can spread, change shape, or fail unpredictably. A loose covenant often produces a monster, a repeating accident, or a social practice that everyone obeys and nobody can explain.

### Challenge

A formal request to the Bench to settle a live conflict. The request must identify the claimant, the affected parties, the disputed condition, the burden, and the remedy sought. A challenge creates a **hearing field**: a bounded area in which the disputed rule can be tested under heightened observation.

The field does not freeze everything else. People can enter, leave, prepare, or break the field, but doing so may create new consequences. A challenge cannot be filed for a purely private wish unless the wish affects an existing covenant or produces immediate public danger.

### Witness

A witness is anything that can verify an event in terms the Bench can compare across places and times. A person is a witness, but so are a bridge, a bell, a well, a family heirloom, a road, a dead person’s preserved testimony, or a place that has endured a particular rule.

Witnesses are not truth detectors. They report what happened to them. Memory can be incomplete, coerced, mistaken, or worn away. A witness may refuse to testify. A witness that lies can be exposed; it cannot be tortured into a universally reliable answer.

### Burden

The price of opening, testing, or enforcing a rule. Burden can be time, labor, memory, identity, property, bodily harm, political responsibility, or the loss of a privilege. A burden is not automatically paid by the person who invokes the System. The parties must identify who can carry it and whether the proposed remedy gives that person a real choice.

A Bench ruling, translated through the System, cannot simply confiscate an unwilling person’s life. It can attach a burden to a person or object that has already accepted the hearing, and it can impose consequences for breaking an accepted ruling. This is why consent, evidence, and procedure matter.

### Ruling

A bounded decision. A ruling names its place, subject, remedy, duration, review date, and the burden it assigns. It may do something as small as permit a ferry to cross at low tide for seven days, or as large as suspend a city’s inheritance chamber while a public review occurs.

A ruling can be:

- **accepted:** people comply and the condition remains active;
- **broken:** people violate it and face the declared consequence;
- **appealed:** a new challenge is filed before the review date;
- **expired:** the rule ends and the Bench records what changed;
- **contested:** affected people refuse to recognize its authority, beginning a political crisis.

The Bench can enforce an accepted ruling through the physical anchors and records that support it, while the System displays the result. It cannot make every person agree. Noncompliance is often a separate story rather than an automatic reset.

### Exception

A temporary permission to do something the current rule forbids. Exceptions are narrow. A ruling might permit Ilyan to cross a sealed bridge, release a trapped witness, or speak in a Chamber of Names. It does not grant general access, immunity, or the ability to rewrite a person’s mind.

An exception ends when its stated condition, time, or place ends. If its burden is unpaid, it closes and the original rule returns. Some exceptions can be renewed, but renewal is a new hearing.

### Null passage

A null passage is a one-use breach between an external witness and an already open hearing field. It is a legacy emergency clause from the First Compact, preserved outside ordinary consent rules so an omitted condition cannot become irreversible while the Bench is unreachable. It can carry one living person, but not an army, arbitrary cargo, or a second traveler. A public advocate slot, a compatible anchor, and a recorded unresolved refusal are required. The advocate may accept or refuse after the crossing; the slot itself is not informed consent.

The passage does not attach a future burden to the traveler before the traveler can answer. Instead, it consumes the anchor’s emergency reserve and leaves the local public work unstable until the first hearing is settled. The traveler must be named in the opening case and may refuse testimony or a remedy. The anchor bears the immediate cost; the Bench bears the duty to provide a safe field and a named advocate. The null token is the spent seal of this breach, and the passage closes permanently after use, leaving no automatic return route. The later civic crossings are slower public mechanisms with named hosts, exit guarantees, and no summoning power.

### Final Exception

The **Final Exception** is a named, one-time constitutional procedure, not a normal exception or a general spell. It can be used only when a continuity hearing has exposed a failed foundational covenant and all nine anchor positions have been identified. It offers one named defendant a temporary authority to select one complete version of the foundational rules and seal the anchor network. If the seal succeeds, it also opens a one-time route home for that defendant as part of the offer. It cannot rewrite a person, resurrect the dead, create a private world, or bind a local covenant without a later public hearing. Its danger is scale: the selected version could become the only version the First Witness recognizes.

A Final Exception is valid for one hearing and one anchor-sealing cycle. If the authority refuses, the nine positions remain unstable. If the seal succeeds, the selected foundational version becomes the single version recognized by the old Bench and the First Witness; that permanence is the danger. The procedure is designed to prevent another Great Silence by concentrating responsibility, which is why accepting it is the final moral and political trap.

### Unspooling

When a loose covenant, broken ruling, or unstable witness produces a physical contradiction, the local world begins to **unspool**. Objects lose a stable past, routes change, weather follows an old grievance, or a creature appears wherever the rule was never completed. Unspooling is not a generic monster generator; it is a legible consequence that can be investigated and sometimes repaired.

A person can die during an unspooling. There is no automatic return from death. A person can also lose recognition—being treated as a stranger, having their name removed, or having their legal history rewritten—but that is a social and magical injury, not a convenient respawn.

## The System

The System is the translated interface of the Bench. Its messages are short because the underlying Loom is old and damaged, not because it is hiding a larger, more elaborate user manual.

A typical System panel may combine these short fields in one message; it is still one panel and should present only the immediate decision:

```text
CLAIM: A public promise does not cover an unlisted traveler.
EVIDENCE: The bridge failed after a crowd; the charter names a lawful crossing.
BURDEN: Someone must accept responsibility for the next crossing.
CHOICE: Amend the charter, break the bridge, or hold the hearing open.
```

The System may also display a warning or the public consequence of an existing Bench ruling. It should not produce item lists, experience bars, hidden objectives, or a complete map of the world.

### What the translated interface can display or request

In this list, “display” means that the System presents a Bench record or asks the affected parties to take a permitted procedural step. The Bench remains the institution that gathers testimony, opens a hearing field, and preserves a record.

- show a challenge and its named parties after the Bench accepts it for filing;
- present testimony already offered by participants;
- display the disputed words of a covenant;
- show the boundary of a hearing field after the Bench opens it;
- display a temporary exception or ruling after the required evidence is present;
- show a burden’s source and present due date;
- mark a ruling for appeal, expiry, or contestation;
- display a public record after the Bench has preserved it.

### What the System cannot do

- read a person’s mind;
- know the moral truth of a claim;
- force a witness to testify;
- restore a dead person;
- guarantee that a ruling will be accepted;
- give Ilyan a skill without practice and consequence;
- make a person’s consent genuine;
- prevent a community from breaking a ruling;
- prove that Veyra is a game merely because it uses rules.

The System is biased by omission. It emphasizes the current valid text and may hide superseded terms unless a party can produce a witness. Its central mistake is treating disagreement as a condition to be corrected rather than a form of life to be governed fairly.

## Ilyan’s special ability: casecraft

Ilyan is not born with a giant combat class. His advantage is that he sees a system as a sequence of claims, actions, and consequences. His experience in game testing gives him three useful instincts:

1. He can identify when the stated rule and the actual rule differ.
2. He can build a repeatable sequence that exposes that difference.
3. He can separate a temporary exception from a permanent change.

Before he has formal power, these are ordinary reasoning skills. As the Bench recognizes him, they become powers with costs.

## Power stages

The stage names are author shorthand, not a visible level system. The System may describe a stage as a changed capacity, but it does not award a number. Growth requires practice, a meaningful failure, a relationship or changed decision, and payment of a lasting cost.

### Stage 0: Unlicensed Eye

Ilyan can notice a contradiction but cannot formally open a hearing without a licensed advocate or an affected community. He can use physical tools, observation, and his testing notes. He has no supernatural protection.

**Limit:** A clever argument cannot save a person from an immediate physical danger.
**Cost:** Every time he insists on proof, he risks losing the window in which evidence can be collected.
**Likely volume:** 1.

### Stage 1: Case Opener

With a named advocate, Ilyan can phrase a live conflict as a challenge and request one narrow exception. The Bench recognizes his request only when the claimant, affected parties, disputed condition, burden, and remedy are named.

**Limit:** He cannot challenge a rule that has no current conflict, and he cannot choose another person’s burden.
**Cost:** His own name enters the case record. A failed case gives the opposing party a legitimate claim against him or his community.
**Likely volume:** 1–2.

### Stage 2: Seam Reader

During a hearing field, Ilyan can perceive the dependencies and exclusions in an active covenant. The effect appears as physical mismatches—two shadows cast from one body, a bell vibrating before it is struck, a door that opens toward the person who is absent.

**Limit:** He sees how the rule is assembled, not why its authors wanted it. A coherent rule can still be unjust.
**Cost:** The rule’s burden leaves an afterimage in his body or senses. Persistent effects accumulate until he rests, changes decisions, or shares the burden.
**Likely volume:** 2–3.

### Stage 3: Witness Broker

Ilyan can connect compatible cases and invite a witness from one site to testify in another if the witness and receiving community consent. He can cross-examine a witness through the case structure: ask what happened, when, and under which term. He cannot force a truthful answer.

**Limit:** Every link must have a physical or social bridge. He cannot summon a dead person, an absent witness, or a place that has no surviving record.
**Cost:** He becomes responsible for keeping the linked witnesses safe and for preserving the distinction between their accounts. A false merge can harm people who never met.
**Likely volume:** 3–5.

### Stage 4: Burden Bearer

Ilyan can voluntarily carry a ruling’s burden across time and distance, keeping a rule from expiring while the people who made it negotiate a replacement. He cannot carry a burden for an unwilling person and cannot transfer it by hiding the cost.

**Limit:** One carried burden at a time; carrying it prevents him from opening a separate case.
**Cost:** The burden can be a physical scar, lost memory, revoked privilege, political liability, or a lost future option. The most expensive stages take a named future possibility rather than a generic pain token.
**Likely volume:** 4–7.

### Stage 5: Remedy Drafter

Ilyan can draft a concrete alternative covenant after a hearing has established the conflict. A draft needs witnesses, a public cost, and acceptance by the people who will live under it. He can propose but not impose.

**Limit:** No draft can solve more than one primary conflict without new participation. Drafting a perfect rule is harder than defeating an opponent.
**Cost:** Once a draft is filed, its visible flaws become his public responsibility. A failed draft can weaken the people who trusted him and give a harsher ruling more credibility.
**Likely volume:** 8–10.

### Stage 6: Co-Witness

Ilyan can form a compact with willing allies who share observation, limited power, or a burden. Each participant keeps agency and can withdraw before the compact is sealed. The group is stronger than its members, but not controlled by Ilyan.

**Limit:** Consent must be renewed at major turns. A co-witness cannot be used as a disposable battery or made to carry an undisclosed share.
**Cost:** Ilyan becomes liable for injuries and broken obligations caused by the shared action, and the allies acquire a claim on his decisions that he cannot erase.
**Likely volume:** 9–12.

### Stage 7: Foundation Challenger

During a continuity hearing, Ilyan can question one foundational premise of the Codification and open a **blank interval** in which the current protections are suspended while the premise is tested. This is the most dangerous non-combat power in the series.

**Limit:** He can question one premise at a time, and only inside a defined field. Before opening it, he must give the affected assemblies notice of the danger, map the full predicted spill zone, and obtain consent from the people who will remain in that zone. A person may withdraw through a marked safe margin before the interval begins. If consent cannot be obtained, the challenge fails; Ilyan cannot impose a blank interval on an unwilling population.
**Cost:** Natural guarantees and legal protections may fail for people who knowingly remain in the full spill zone, and an unforeseen path through a broken anchor can extend the danger beyond the prediction. If that happens, Ilyan must personally anchor the field with a named burden and the failed boundary becomes public evidence against his claim. The final use costs him the lived memory of filing the *Sovereign’s Wake* refusal report; he retains the report and its facts, but not the original choice that made the work matter. The dead may not return, identities may be misrecognized, and the chosen future can be physically closed.
**Likely volume:** 13–17.

### Stage 8: Free Witness

After the First Witness loses unilateral authority, Ilyan can speak and act outside Bench recognition. He has no special immunity, no guaranteed access to magic, and no right to compel belief. His final influence comes from public argument, relationships, and ordinary civic work.

**Limit:** Every act is subject to the laws of the place where it occurs. The System can no longer display a binding ruling to him, and people can still oppose him.
**Cost:** He loses the right to a personal return route and becomes fully subject to the new civic order.
**Likely volume:** 17.

## Failure and recovery

- A failed challenge does not rewind the world. It leaves a public record, damaged relationships, exhausted witnesses, or an active consequence.
- A broken exception returns the original rule with its unpaid burden, often making the next case harder.
- A mistaken ruling can be appealed, but the remedy must account for people who relied on the mistaken order.
- Ilyan’s recoveries are practice, rest, delegation, and changed decisions. He does not heal through a new System panel.
- Death is permanent. Identity damage and lost memory can be partially addressed through witness work, but restoration may change relationships and leave contradictions.

## Other practitioners

The world has people who work with covenants without Ilyan’s casecraft:

- **Anchor-smiths** shape the physical structures that hold a rule.
- **Recordwrights** preserve exact language and trace chains of testimony.
- **Healers** repair bodies and the social obligations that made a wound meaningful.
- **Bell keepers** interrupt dangerous action through recognized warning customs.
- **Guild engineers** build roads, bridges, water systems, and temporary wards under practical constraints.
- **Children and households** learn to notice contradictions, though adults often dismiss their observations until it is useful.

Ilyan’s power is valuable because it connects these practices. It is not a substitute for them.

## System-panel discipline

A panel should appear at most once in an ordinary chapter and should state only the immediate rule, evidence, or consequence. Ilyan’s interpretation and action carry the drama. The System may be incomplete, biased, or unable to display a local concept; the story must never treat a missing explanation as proof that a rule is harmless.
