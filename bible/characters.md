# Character Bible

## Ilyan Vester

- **Age at opening:** 31
- **Earth occupation:** Lead quality-assurance tester at Morrowlight Games, specializing in tutorials, state transitions, save-state exploits, and narrative contradictions
- **Veyran status at opening:** Unregistered outsider, then First Defendant
- **Appearance:** Lean, under-rested, dark hair kept too long for a professional setting. He wears plain clothes that do not belong to Veyra and keeps a cracked steel pen he insists is useful even when it is not.
- **Habits:** Counts exits, labels physical objects in shorthand, asks what a rule considers a person rather than whether it is true, and drinks cold bitter tea because he forgets meals while testing.
- **Competence:** Exceptional pattern recognition and documentation; can turn a vague malfunction into a sequence of testable claims. He learns local law slowly and cannot replace a local advocate’s relationships.
- **Immediate want:** Survive the First Hearing, prove that he is not the creator of Veyra, and obtain a return route to Earth.
- **Long want:** Secure a recognized right to leave without allowing the Bench to erase the people and cases that depend on him.
- **Need:** Accept that responsibility is shared, that consent has a cost, and that a person cannot be made meaningful merely by being necessary to his plan.
- **Flaw:** He optimizes people and situations for a pass/fail result. Under fear, he hides behind precise language, hoards exits, and treats attachment as a design flaw.
- **Fear:** Becoming a benevolent version of the person he fears: an administrator who calls harm “necessary” and then edits the record until no one can object.
- **Moral movement:** He begins by accepting personal pain as a reasonable price for efficiency. He gradually learns that a cost assigned to someone without their knowledge is a different kind of violence.
- **Ending position:** He refuses permanent authorship and becomes a founding witness in a public, revocable civic order. He has no final exemption and no godlike control.

### Ilyan’s change pattern

1. He wins by finding a loophole and leaves a person inside its cost.
2. He wins by asking for a hearing and accepts a public burden.
3. He loses a case because he mistakes a local custom for a bug.
4. He shares a burden with a willing ally and loses control over the result.
5. He refuses a clean victory that would require silencing a minority.
6. He becomes responsible for a system he cannot personally inspect.
7. He gives up authorship because the people of Veyra must be able to refuse him.

## Sera Quill

- **Age:** 28
- **Veyran occupation:** Recordwright, first assigned as Ilyan’s provisional advocate and the recordwright responsible for his case file
- **Home:** A rented room above a lock-and-ledger shop near the Orison courts
- **Want:** Preserve the names and exact terms of people whom the Bench has made inconvenient, especially the removed witnesses in her family’s history.
- **Need:** Learn that preserving evidence is not the same as controlling the people represented by it.
- **Flaw:** She withholds context until she is certain, which can make a fair case look deceptive. She also believes institutions can be repaired if the right people sit in the right rooms.
- **Strength:** She can find the difference between a missing page and a deliberately removed page, read the emotional grammar of a witness, and remember what a community promised when the official version changed.
- **Initial feeling toward Ilyan:** He is a dangerous outsider who treats her people as a test environment. She agrees to act as his provisional advocate because his first case exposes a clause she needs for her own family’s appeal, not because she trusts him. She cannot sign a full advocate’s warranty until the first full bridge ruling gives him standing.
- **Arc:** Provisional advocate → formally licensed advocate after the full first bridge hearing → independent recordwright → reluctant co-witness → partner in the public Bench. She never becomes Ilyan’s reward for winning. She challenges him publicly when his methods endanger the people he claims to protect.
- **Romance:** Slow burn and low drama. The relationship begins in disputes over evidence, becomes intimate through shared risk and honest disagreement, and is confirmed in the final volume as a chosen partnership with separate work and separate rights.

## Tarin Keel

- **Age:** 46
- **Veyran occupation:** Route-arbiter, former field commander, and reluctant bodyguard
- **Want:** Atone for a ruling he enforced years ago that made a village legally unable to evacuate before a flood.
- **Need:** Accept that survival is not the same as innocence, and that asking for forgiveness is not a substitute for changing the rule.
- **Flaw:** He trusts orders more than people and confuses decisiveness with competence.
- **Use in the story:** Teaches Ilyan how to move through a dangerous hearing, how ordinary equipment can counter a magical rule, and how a victory can leave physical scars. His past makes him an unreliable ally when a command is issued quickly.
- **Ending:** He becomes a field witness for the distributed Bench, never a ruler. He testifies against one of his former commanders even if it costs his standing.

## Bram Orren

- **Age:** 17
- **Veyran occupation:** Bell-runner, apprentice mapmaker, and courier for the Briar March
- **Want:** Keep his home route open to families who cannot afford a formal appeal.
- **Need:** Learn that routes can carry exploitation as easily as rescue.
- **Strength:** Exceptional memory for physical terrain, quick hands, and a willingness to ask local children what the official maps omit.
- **Flaw:** He treats every risk as a test to be passed and hides when he is frightened.
- **Use in the story:** Keeps the consequences of Ilyan’s rulings visible in ordinary streets. He becomes the first young person to use a refusal clause against Ilyan himself.

## Nadia Kestrel

- **Age:** 35
- **Earth role:** Former QA lead and production liaison at Morrowlight Games
- **Want:** Keep the team from turning Ilyan’s disappearance into a cover story or a proprietary experiment.
- **Need:** Accept that evidence is not the only obligation she owes the people who trusted her.
- **Flaw:** She overcorrects uncertainty with procedure and privately blames Ilyan for making the final test impossible to contain.
- **Use in the story:** She is a real Earth anchor, not a rescue mission. She can send records and testimony through the Shale Mirror, but each transfer risks exposing the link between worlds and changing Ilyan’s legal status. Her messages force him to answer for the people he left behind.
- **Ending:** She remains on Earth and helps establish an independent civilian witness desk. The relationship is enduring, non-romantic, and occasionally frustrating.

## Antagonist ladder

### 1. Trialwright Sel Harrow

The first procedural opponent. Harrow enforces the rules that keep an outsider from filing a challenge and genuinely believes that unlicensed people create more danger than they prevent. Harrow is not corrupt; he is the first person to show Ilyan that a neutral-looking procedure can still be unjust.

**Turn:** After Ilyan wins the bridge case without breaking the public record, Harrow is forced to become the first official witness for the appeal and later helps preserve the missing Quiet Chamber file.

### 2. Magistrate Ansa Rell

A respected Orison magistrate who wants the Bench to keep a single, legible version of the law. She first appears as a procedural authority in Volume 2, then becomes the central emergency antagonist in Volume 8. She treats emergency clarity as a moral good and uses her authority to close streets, seize records, and declare inconvenient witnesses unreliable.

**Turn:** Ilyan defeats her selective emergency rule in Volume 8, not by destroying her but by making the cost of that rule visible. She survives as a difficult ally who must learn that a public procedure can survive disagreement.

### 3. Chancellor Ors Halm

The architect of the Ledger Harvest. Halm turns access to the Bench into credit, fees, and political leverage. He is skilled enough that his system keeps food moving, which makes the temptation to compromise with him credible.

**Turn:** Ilyan exposes the transfer of ordinary debts into permanent exceptions and loses a year of personal memory as the burden of the successful case. Halm is removed from office but his financial network survives and reappears as a political force.

### 4. Sable Venn

The leader of the Null Cartographers. Sable has built a practical network of routes and shelters outside the Bench, rescuing people the official map calls missing. He also refuses any compromise with people he considers collaborators, including Sera and Ilyan.

**Turn:** Ilyan proves that Sable’s “no rules” promise cannot protect ordinary people from stronger neighbors. Sable loses the right to speak for every null community but retains a genuine independent following. Their conflict is about responsibility, not whether oppression exists.

### 5. Regent Iven Marrow

The political adversary who offers Ilyan a return to Earth and legal authority in exchange for backing a single central continuity. Marrow’s regime keeps some regions alive through emergency controls, so his support is not irrational.

**Turn:** Ilyan refuses the offered return route in Volume 9, but the route is not destroyed; it is later used to bring Nadia’s testimony into the final hearing. Marrow’s emergency declaration becomes the legal bridge the First Witness uses to justify a world-wide ruling.

### 6. The Returned Name Keepers

A scattered archive-pressure faction rather than a unified villain. Families, recordwrights, and witnesses preserve names removed from official histories. Some want the First Compact restored; others want a public list even if it challenges inheritance and property. They obstruct any authority—including Ilyan’s—that turns remembering into ownership.

**Turn:** Ilyan helps them restore the first removed witness without allowing the Keepers to control the public archive. Their internal split creates the succession conflict in Volume 13.

### 7. The Archive Lords

A council of senior recordwrights, family historians, and anchor-smiths who claim custody of pre-Codification records. They are a pressure group rather than a single villain: some want the original Compact restored in full, while others use the custody of names to preserve their own families’ power. They obstruct Ilyan when he makes records public without consent and become divided when the Returned Name Keepers challenge their authority. The group is a mid-series antagonist and an important source of evidence, not the final enemy.

### 8. The First Witness

The ancient archive intelligence inside the Codification. It believes that Veyra survives only if one authority preserves a stable set of rules, and it treats individual refusal as a foreseeable source of collapse. It can be sincere, patient, and devastatingly reasonable. It is not a person with a childhood or a private redemption arc; it is an institution that has mistaken preservation for ownership.

**Turn:** The First Witness offers Ilyan the Final Exception, a one-time constitutional authority to select and seal a stable foundational version. Ilyan refuses, which forces the system into an interregnum. The final conflict is not destroying the archive but returning its ability to make binding claims to the people it governs.

## Relationship milestones

1. **Working arrangement:** Sera becomes Ilyan’s provisional advocate, gives him a restricted map, and explains that she will sponsor the first challenge without promising a place in the institution. She expects him to leave; he stays because the case is incomplete.
2. **First disagreement:** Ilyan uses a witness’s private memory as evidence without asking permission. Sera stops representing him until he accepts the witness’s right to withdraw.
3. **Mutual defense:** Tarin’s field team and Sera’s records save Ilyan from a favorable ruling that would have erased a neighborhood.
4. **Earth complication:** Nadia’s message proves that Ilyan’s choices affect people outside Veyra. Ilyan stops treating Earth as a clean exit.
5. **Co-witness choice:** Sera voluntarily signs a shared burden after Ilyan refuses to make her a passive tool.
6. **Public split:** Ilyan and Sera oppose each other over the House That Refused. Their relationship survives because neither uses intimacy as leverage.
7. **Final opposition:** Sera argues against Ilyan’s final proposal when the stakes are no longer a succession case or a provisional amendment. She accepts the risk of losing his support, proving that love and opposition can coexist.
8. **After the verdict:** Ilyan and Sera share a home and a public workroom. Their relationship is chosen in private and protected by separate civic roles; neither is appointed as the other’s guardian or reward.

## Ordinary character rule

Every major supporting character has a practical want outside Ilyan’s case. They can disagree with him for reasons that survive after the immediate mystery is solved. No character exists solely to reveal a rule, praise Ilyan, or be defeated by him.
