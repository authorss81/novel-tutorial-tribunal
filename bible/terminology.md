# Terminology

Capitalized terms below are stable canon terms. Ordinary words such as “law,” “magic,” “rule,” and “world” remain ordinary unless a scene explicitly makes them covenant terms.

## Veyran institutions and history

### Veyra

The living world. Its people do not use the word “game” to describe reality, though the Bench uses “playable” as a technical term for a rule set that permits predictable action.

### The Bench

The public judgment network that hears challenges, records covenants, issues bounded rulings, and preserves precedents. It is an institution with local agents and a hidden ancient infrastructure, not a single ruler.

### The System

The translated interface that Ilyan sees when the Bench addresses him directly. It can display a claim, evidence, burden, warning, or consequence. It is not omniscient, does not issue binding authority on its own, and does not expose a complete database of reality. The Bench is the institution that records and enforces a ruling; the System is how that authority appears to Ilyan.

### The Loom

The distributed civic infrastructure built beneath Veyra by the First Compact. The Loom is made from anchor-stones, records, living practices, and the promises of many communities. It predates the current Bench.

### First Compact

The original agreement that created the Loom. It was built collectively over generations, not authored by one person.

### Great Silence

The historical cascade in which old covenants began contradicting one another. Roads reversed, names vanished from records, and people lost recognition across thresholds. The current Bench was created in response.

### Warden’s Codification

The emergency reconstruction of the Loom that froze disputed rules, restricted appeals, and removed the public right to withdraw. It saved Veyra but created the conditions for a centralized prison.

### The First Witness

The title of the ancient archive intelligence that administers the Codification. It has absorbed many names and presents itself as a neutral custodian of continuity. It is a planned final adversary and must be seeded early; it is not a new cosmic enemy revealed at the climax.

### Shale Mirror

A fragment of black shale that connected Morrowlight’s unreleased game to Veyra. It is a translation layer, not the source of Veyra.

### Loom relay

A one-way, bounded transmission between compatible anchor-stones. It can carry a translation record or reflected rule, not a living person, a portal, or independent power. The Shale Mirror arrived on Earth through a relay.

### Null passage

A one-use breach that carries one living external witness into an already open hearing field. It requires a recorded refusal, an open public advocate slot, and a compatible anchor; it closes permanently after use. The null token Ilyan carries is the spent seal of that passage. It is not a return portal.

### Final Exception

A one-time constitutional procedure that can let one named defendant select and seal one version of Veyra’s foundational rules after all nine anchor positions are identified. It is not a normal temporary exception, cannot rewrite a person, and expires if the foundational hearing fails; if completed, the selected version becomes permanent under the old Bench and the defendant receives the promised one-time route home.

### Civic crossing

A post-verdict public route for consenting travelers between Veyra and Earth. It uses surviving Veyran anchor functions paired with the inert Shale Mirror’s Earth endpoint under civilian supervision, plus a named traveler, host, route, and exit guarantee. It can move people, but it is slow, costly, auditable, and never a summoning power.

### *The Sovereign’s Wake*

The cancelled Earth game tested by Ilyan. Its title is a game-within-the-bootstrap record and should be typeset in italics in prose.

## Legal and magical terms

### Covenant

A binding rule or public promise. A complete covenant names its subject, condition, burden, witness, remedy, and sunset or exit. An incomplete covenant is loose and can produce unspooling.

### Challenge

A formal request for the Bench to settle a live conflict. It names the claimant, affected parties, disputed condition, burden, and remedy, and creates a bounded hearing field. It is not a quest marker and does not guarantee a fight.

### Hearing field

The limited area in which a disputed covenant can be observed and tested under heightened rule tension. A field may include bystanders and does not make everyone inside it an ally.

### Witness

A person, community, object, place, or preserved testimony able to verify an event. Witnesses report what happened to them; they are not truth detectors.

### Burden

The cost of opening, testing, or enforcing a covenant. A burden can be time, labor, memory, identity, property, bodily harm, political responsibility, or a lost privilege.

### Ruling

A bounded decision. It names a place, remedy, duration, burden, and review date. It can be accepted, broken, appealed, expired, or contested.

### Provisional stay

A temporary ruling that preserves a named access, practice, or protection for a fixed number of review cycles. It does not amend the underlying covenant or create an appeal. Unless the Bench issues a new ruling at the stated review, the stay expires and the prior rule returns.

### Exception

A temporary permission to cross or act against a current rule. An exception has a limit, an expiry, and a burden; it is not a permanent rewrite.

### Unspooling

The physical and social contradiction produced by a loose covenant or broken ruling. It can change roads, weather, names, rooms, or inherited obligations. It is not a generic monster generator.

### Null clause

A deliberately blank condition that preserves room for refusal. The original Compact used several null clauses; the current Codification conceals them.

### Right of refusal

The civic practice of rejecting a specific burden or ruling without losing citizenship, provided the person makes the consequences visible and does not conceal harm from those who depend on them. The current Bench treats this right as a defect.

### Playable

A Bench term for a rule set that allows predictable action and a meaningful response. The System uses it as though it means “fit to continue.” Ilyan initially treats it as proof of a simulation; the novel gradually separates the technical word from the ethical claim.

### Continuity

A world’s capacity to support life, memory, and change without arbitrary reversions. It is not a numerical stat and cannot be proven by a single successful ruling.

## Places

### Orison

The central city on the Ninefold Shelf, built around nine bridges and the First Verdict. The First Verdict is a basalt court, not a palace.

### Ninefold Shelf

The central river plain containing most of Veyra’s grain, trade, archives, and official Bench infrastructure.

### Saltglass Coast

Western ports and glassworks where exact charters are preserved in saltglass.

### Briar March

Eastern villages and bell-right communities outside strong Bench authority.

### Mossward

Northern reed villages and root-covenant healers.

### Red Basin

Southern red-clay territory whose repeated earthquakes and contradictory deeds expose accumulated legal failures.

### The Underloom

The changing, pre-Bench network of anchor-stones and records beneath Veyra. It is a lived civic space, not a disposable dungeon.

## People and relationship terms

### First Defendant

Ilyan’s legal status after the null passage pulls him into a Veyran hearing. It means that his choices are evidence in a case about Veyra’s authorship and continuity. It does not mean he is guilty or that the status is a prophecy.

### Ilyan Vester

Male protagonist; former Earth game tester and First Defendant. See `bible/characters.md`.

### Sera Quill

Recordwright, Ilyan’s first provisional advocate, and the single slow-burn romantic partner. She becomes formally licensed after the Volume 1 hearing.

### Tarin Keel

Route-arbiter and former commander; field ally and recurring source of practical consequence.

### Bram Orren

Bell-runner and apprentice mapmaker from the Briar March; young witness and courier.

### Nadia Kestrel

Earthside former QA lead; Ilyan’s non-romantic anchor and external witness.

### Sel Harrow

Trialwright and first procedural antagonist.

### Ansa Rell

Orison magistrate; institutional antagonist who becomes a difficult ally.

### Ors Halm

Ledger chancellor who commodifies access to the Bench.

### Sable Venn

Leader of the Null Cartographers; insurgent opponent whose refusal of compromise is both protective and dangerous.

### Iven Marrow

Regent who offers central authority and a return route to Earth.

### The Null Cartographers

A network of routes, shelters, and mapmakers that helps people leave or revise hostile covenants. They are a movement, not a secret army.

### The Archive Lords

A council of senior recordwrights, family historians, and anchor-smiths who claim custody of pre-Codification records. Their internal disagreements shape the archive and succession volumes; they are not the final enemy.

## Usage rules

- Use **Bench** for the institution and **System** for the interface. Do not call them the same thing without making the distinction relevant.
- Use **challenge** for a legal request and **case** for the people, evidence, and consequences that grow around it.
- Use **ruling** for a decision, **exception** for a temporary permission, and **covenant** for the underlying rule or promise.
- Do not use “level,” “stat,” “skill tree,” “respawn,” or “NPC” as Veyran magic terminology. Game vocabulary may appear in Ilyan’s private thoughts or in an Earth document, with context.
- “Monster” means a living or persistent threat with agency or consequence. An unspooling is a broken rule, not automatically a monster.
- “Witness” never means a disposable test subject. A witness is a person with interests and limits whenever the person is living.
- Capitalize named places, institutions, formal rulings, and titles only as listed here. Generic “bridge,” “river,” and “magistrate” remain lowercase.
- Avoid real software terms in System messages. Use “record,” “rule,” “term,” “burden,” “review,” “witness,” and “ruling.”
- A System panel contains one immediate decision at most. It may omit a detail, but the omission must be a character or institutional limitation, not random teasing.
