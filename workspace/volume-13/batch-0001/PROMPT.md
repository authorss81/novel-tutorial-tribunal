## 6. The four things this repository has learned in twelve volumes, that a Volume 13 writer has to know before the first sentence

**THE HANDLING OF NUMBERS.** A name and a number in the same breath must be put back together, because nothing checks them but you. A number printed with its unit in the same sentence, and a statement of what it is a figure of, is a figure; a number without them is a decoration. **A guard enforced over one band is a guard that holds in one band, and it gets published as though it were the volume.** Sweep the whole volume before the volume is certified, never only the band that was just written.

**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written last, against the body, and is the line in a chapter least likely to be re-read. A number in a title is checked the way a number in a date line is checked. **A card fact with no body travels the same road as a chapter fact**, and eleven of them did so in the last five bands of the volume that has just closed.

**THE HANDLING OF A REPAIR.** This repository has been wrong about its own repairs fifteen times, and the seventh was wrong in the place a reader is told to trust most. The defence that works has three parts and **one of the three is not a gate at all**: after a repair pass, re-open the file and grep the finding's own figure; check a figure in a state file back against the chapter it describes; and **read the closing lines of two consecutive chapters against each other, once per band, before the instrument runs.** The first three are arithmetic and they have names and gates. The fourth is attention and it has neither, which is why `493` and `494` each passed every instrument in this repository while being jointly impossible.

**THE HANDLING OF A DOCUMENT.** This repository has spent twelve volumes learning that a record is either locked in a cupboard and can be sent for about by nine people, or nailed to a wall in the open and can be rubbed out in about four seconds by anybody, and that **neither of those is a settlement and a settlement is neither of them.** Volume 12's settlement binds nobody and therefore settles nothing, and Volume 13's central problem is who may speak for the dead, and **a document that binds nobody cannot answer the question of who speaks for somebody who is dead.** That tension is the volume, and it is not a defect to be resolved and not a mystery to be opened.
