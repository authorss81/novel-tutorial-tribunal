**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written LAST, against the body, and is checked against **the body of its own chapter and not against the band.** The last band wrote *On Friday* over a body in which the lifting happened on the Tuesday, in the title and again in the closing line.

**THE HANDLING OF A DOCUMENT.** Thirteen volumes have learned that a record is a cupboard, then a wall, then a row of chairs, then two copies read against each other, then a record that goes where it goes, then a board on a barrel, then a column cut into the back of a leaf, then nothing. **VOLUME 14'S SEVENTH THING IS A KEEPING, AND IT IS A PERSON DOING A JOB, AND WHEN THE JOB STOPS THE DOOR STOPS AND NOTHING FALLS DOWN. THE MIDPOINT IS WHERE THAT BECOMES A WHOLE VALLEY OF THEM AND IT MUST STILL BE PEOPLE.**

**THE HANDLING OF THE PROSE ITSELF.** Sentence median under twenty-five and long sentences under one in ten, checked before the second paragraph of each chapter and not after the tenth. **Two to six sentences to a paragraph, varied rhythm, and a one-line paragraph only when the beat is dramatic.** Physical detail that is load-bearing: cold stone, a lamp that gutters, a rope, a beam, wet gravel, the smell of a room that has had a hole in its ceiling for four hundred years. **And the thing that makes this manuscript readable: every figure is said out loud by a person to other people, and the saying is the scene, and nobody is thanked for it.**
