## 7. The five things a writer needs before the first sentence

**THE HANDLING OF NUMBERS.** A number printed with its unit in the same sentence, and a statement of what it is a figure of, is a figure; a number without them is a decoration. **A COUNT OF DAYS WHILE A PERSON WAS IN A PLACE IS A CLOCK, A COUNT OF TIMES A THING WAS SAID IS A COUNT OF TIMES, AND ONE OF THE FIRST HAS ALREADY STOPPED.** **AND A COUNT HAS A LABEL AND THE LABEL IS PART OF THE COUNT.**

**THE HANDLING OF A POINTER.** A week-ordinal is a figure. It reads like a date, and it looks right, and it can only be checked against the table, **and the table it is checked against is the one computed from the formula and not the one copied out of a summary row, because five figures in the summary rows of this volume's own calendar were wrong and four of them were wrong by enough to break a date line.** **AND THE DAY-NAME FOR AN ORDINAL IS A SEPARATE FIGURE, BECAUSE A REVIEWER RE-DERIVED `653` AS A THURSDAY WHEN THE FORMULA GIVES A FRIDAY.**

**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written LAST, against the body, and is checked against **the body of its own chapter and not against the band.** The last band wrote *On Friday* over a body in which the lifting happened on the Tuesday, in the title and again in the closing line.

**THE HANDLING OF A COST.** This band pays one and it is the band the volume's power stage is paid in. **THE SCAR IS PHYSICAL, IT IS ON A PERSON, IT IS PAID IN THE CHAPTER AND NOT AFTER IT, IT IS NOT REFUNDED, AND IT IS NOT A MORAL.** `outline/volume-14.md` §5.4, `series.md:253`. **A cost that arrives at the end of a chapter and is mourned at the start of the next one is a cost the reader was told about and not a cost that happened. And the person who pays it says so in their own mouth before anybody else describes it.**

**THE HANDLING OF THE PROSE ITSELF.** Sentence median under twenty-five and long sentences under one in ten, checked before the second paragraph of each chapter and not after the tenth. **AND THE PER-CHAPTER FLOOR OF `state/index.md` RULE 4 IS 2,000–3,400 WORDS AND IS NOT TO BE TREATED AS NOT A GATE BY A STATE FILE, WHICH IS EXACTLY WHAT THE LAST BAND'S RECEIPT DID: THREE CHAPTERS CAME IN UNDER IT, IT DECLARED THE FLOOR IRRELEVANT, AND A REVIEW WAS RIGHT, AND THE HONEST MOVE IS TO PRINT THE ROW AND NAME THE SHORT CHAPTERS RATHER THAN TO PAD THEM OR TO RE-DESCRIBE THE RULE. THE HAZARD IS CARRIED AT `state/open-threads.md` §0A.1.** Two to six sentences to a paragraph, varied rhythm, and a one-line paragraph only when the beat is dramatic. Physical detail that is load-bearing: cold stone, a lamp that gutters, a rope, a beam, wet gravel, the smell of a room that has had a hole in its ceiling for four hundred years, **and the body that paid the price, described once and not twice.** **And the thing that makes this manuscript readable: every figure is said out loud by a person to other people, and the saying is the scene, and nobody is thanked for it.**
