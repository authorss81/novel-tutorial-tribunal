## 7. The six things a writer needs before the first sentence

**THE HANDLING OF NUMBERS.** A number printed with its unit in the same sentence, and a statement of what it is a figure of, is a figure; a number without them is a decoration. **A COUNT OF DAYS WHILE A PERSON WAS IN A PLACE IS A CLOCK, A COUNT OF TIMES A THING WAS SAID IS A COUNT OF TIMES, AND ONE OF THE FIRST HAS ALREADY STOPPED. AND A COUNT HAS A LABEL AND THE LABEL IS PART OF THE COUNT.**

**THE HANDLING OF A POINTER.** A week-ordinal is a figure. It reads like a date, and it looks right, and it can only be checked against the table. **AND A POINTER FORWARD IS A PREDICTION AND NOT A FACT, AND THE DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE OF RULE 2 ABOVE.**

**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written LAST, against the body, and is checked against **the body of its own chapter and not against the band.** A title that says a bank is full, or that a Thursday was right, is checked against the figures in its own chapter.

**THE HANDLING OF A COST.** This band pays three more: the last four of the nine terms, the chamber that is sealed, and whatever the copies do to the woman of twenty-eight. **EVERY COST IS PAID IN ITS CHAPTER AND NOT AFTER IT, IS NOT REFUNDED, IS NOT A MORAL, AND IS SAID BY THE PERSON WHO PAID IT IN THEIR OWN MOUTH BEFORE ANYBODY ELSE DESCRIBES IT.** **AND A COPY IS THE MOST EXPENSIVE THING IN THIS BAND AND NOBODY MAY SAY IT IS A SMALL ONE.**

**THE HANDLING OF A SEAL.** `series.md:252` requires the First Witness to seal the central chamber. **A SEAL IS A KEEPING THAT HAS BEEN STOPPED, AND NOTHING FALLS DOWN WHEN A KEEPING STOPS, AND THAT IS THE HORROR OF IT AND IT MAY NOT BE WRITTEN AS A COLLAPSE.** Nobody in four counties may be sent for about it, and a man of fifty-four who cannot read is the only person who can be sent for about what is written, and the chapter knows that before it happens.

**THE HANDLING OF THE PROSE ITSELF.** Sentence median under twenty-five and long sentences under one in ten, checked before the second paragraph of each chapter and not after the tenth. **AND THE PER-CHAPTER FLOOR OF `state/index.md` RULE 4 IS 2,000–3,400 WORDS AND IS NOT TO BE TREATED AS NOT A GATE BY A STATE FILE. THE HAZARD IS CARRIED AT `state/open-threads.md` §0A.1.** Two to six sentences to a paragraph, varied rhythm, and a one-line paragraph only when the beat is dramatic. Physical detail that is load-bearing: cold stone, a lamp that gutters, oil in a hook-cut bowl, chalk, paper that has been folded and unfolded, the smell of a room with a hole in its ceiling for four hundred years, **and the body that paid the price, described once and not twice.** **And the thing that makes this manuscript readable: every figure is said out loud by a person to other people, and the saying is the scene, and nobody is thanked for it.**
