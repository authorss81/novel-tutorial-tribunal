## 7. The five things a writer needs before the first sentence

**THE HANDLING OF NUMBERS.** A number printed with its unit in the same sentence, and a statement of what it is a figure of, is a figure; a number without them is a decoration. **A COUNT OF DAYS WHILE A PERSON WAS IN A PLACE IS A CLOCK, A COUNT OF TIMES A THING WAS SAID IS A COUNT OF TIMES, AND ONE OF THE FIRST HAS ALREADY STOPPED.** The interval is the count less the thing counted to, worked out in a room with the subtraction spoken, and **a figure that is right about one thing and wrong about another is a figure nobody can check by reading it.** And **a figure that fails on its own arithmetic inside its own paragraph is a decoration and not a figure.**

**THE HANDLING OF A POINTER.** A week-ordinal is a figure. It reads like a date, and it looks right, and it can only be checked against the table. **Twenty of the twenty-one defects in the last band were pointers or counts and three of them were right on every figure on the line except the day.**

**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written LAST, against the body, and is checked against **the body of its own chapter and not against the band.** The last volume wrote a title that asserted a journey a two-day road cannot make in the time the body gave it, and another that asserted *four* where the body had been repaired to *three*. **A title is the first thing the instrument reads and the last thing anybody re-reads.**

**THE HANDLING OF A DOCUMENT.** Thirteen volumes have learned that a record is a cupboard, then a wall, then a row of chairs, then two copies read against each other, then a record that goes where it goes, then a board on a barrel, then a column cut into the back of a leaf, then nothing. **VOLUME 14'S SEVENTH THING IS A KEEPING, AND IT IS THE FIRST ONE THAT IS NOT A THING AT ALL: IT IS A PERSON DOING A JOB, AND WHEN THE JOB STOPS THE DOOR STOPS AND NOTHING FALLS DOWN.** **THIS BAND IS ABOUT THE PEOPLE WHO DO THE JOBS AND THE COST ON EACH OF THEM WHEN THERE ARE ABOUT NINE OF THEM INSTEAD OF ONE, AND THE COST MUST LAND ON AN ORDINARY PERSON'S ORDINARY WEEK AND NOT ON A SPEECH.**

**THE HANDLING OF THE PROSE ITSELF.** Sentence median under twenty-five and long sentences under one in ten, checked before the second paragraph of each chapter and not after the tenth. **Two to six sentences to a paragraph, varied rhythm, and a one-line paragraph only when the beat is dramatic.** Physical detail that is load-bearing: cold stone, a lamp that gutters, a rope, a beam, wet gravel, the smell of a room that has had a hole in its ceiling for four hundred years. **And the thing that makes this manuscript readable: every figure is said out loud by a person to other people, and the saying is the scene, and nobody is thanked for it.**
