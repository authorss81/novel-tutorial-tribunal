## 6. The five things a writer needs before the first sentence

**THE HANDLING OF NUMBERS.** A number printed with its unit in the same sentence, and a statement of what it is a figure of, is a figure; a number without them is a decoration. **A COUNT OF DAYS WHILE A PERSON WAS IN A PLACE IS A CLOCK, AND A COUNT OF TIMES A THING WAS SAID IS A COUNT OF TIMES, AND THIS BAND IS WHERE THE FIRST OF THOSE STOPS.** The interval is the count less the thing counted to, worked out in a room with the subtraction spoken, and a figure that is right about one thing and wrong about another is a figure nobody can check by reading it.

**THE HANDLING OF A TITLE.** A title is a date line, is written LAST, against the body, and is checked against **the body of its own chapter and not against the band.** Band 0001 of the last volume wrote three titles that asserted a fact their own chapter did not carry; the last band wrote one at `631` that asserted a journey a two-day road cannot make in the time the body gave it; and the tenth volume wrote one that asserted *four* where the body had been repaired to *three*. A title is the first thing the instrument reads and the last thing anybody re-reads.

**THE HANDLING OF A REPAIR.** Re-open the file and grep the finding's own figure. Check a figure in a state file back against the chapter it describes. Run the structural sweep after any mechanical prose edit, because a sentence split that looks correct in a diff can leave a doubled closing mark or a lost space.

**THE HANDLING OF A DOCUMENT.** Thirteen volumes have learned that a record is a cupboard, then a wall, then a row of chairs, then two copies read against each other, then a record that goes where it goes, then a board on a barrel, then a column cut into the back of a leaf, and then nothing. **VOLUME 14'S SEVENTH THING IS A KEEPING, AND IT IS THE FIRST ONE THAT IS NOT A THING AT ALL: IT IS A PERSON DOING A JOB, AND WHEN THE JOB STOPS THE DOOR STOPS AND NOTHING FALLS DOWN.** The distance between a thing a person is sent for about and a job a person does about nine times a year is the whole of fourteen volumes and it is not resolved in this one.

**THE HANDLING OF THE PROSE ITSELF.** Sentence median under twenty-five and long sentences under one in ten, checked before the second paragraph of each chapter and not after the tenth. **Two to six sentences to a paragraph, varied rhythm, and a one-line paragraph only when the beat is dramatic.** Physical detail that is load-bearing: cold stone, a lamp that gutters, a rope, a beam, the smell of a room that has had a hole in its ceiling for four hundred years. **And the thing that makes this manuscript readable: every figure is said out loud by a person to other people, and the saying is the scene, and nobody is thanked for it.**
