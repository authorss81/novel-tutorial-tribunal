## WHAT A COLD REVIEWER WILL LOOK FOR, BECAUSE IT HAS LOOKED FOR EVERY ONE OF THESE IN THIS PROJECT

- **A distance attached to a walk, a document or a person.** The towpath is six miles each way. The Orison court is **four miles** from Ryefold, and **eleven miles is the ninth bridge and is not the court** — a Batch 0002 card got this wrong and it is recorded. The counting-house with no name on the door is about six miles. **The account's own office is six miles up the road that goes north out of Ryefold.** A document's distance is the one that is always wrong.
- **A career written as two periods that add up to the wrong total.** Nineteen years in a bench's book, and nothing before it. Nine years in a building. Twenty-two years a reeve. Two years in the account's office and eleven months entitled. One night on a towpath and nine days in a cut. **Six plus nineteen is not twenty-five, and twenty-five is not anywhere in this district.**
- **An object in three places across two chapters.** The lease sheet is on a nail. The minute is in a file. The ration list is in a reeve's hand and a copy of a finding is in a drawer. **It is one nail, and Perrin Gant said so out loud in Chapter 161 before anybody said anything else, and a later pass must not find two nails.**
- **A count in one chapter that has two frames.** The forbearance's age is the standing example: **fourteen days on day thirteen, which is how old the debt is; the tenth day before the page, which is how long the district had known; four weeks to the cut; and *about a year*, which is how long the carts have been on the account. All four are correct about different things and may never be quoted together as one figure.** **And the one hundred loads and the four hundred loads are two different figures about two different things and must never be joined: one is a shortage of paid hire in this district and the other is what will rot on a quay if a carter's second cart does not come.**
- **A figure in a state file treated as a fact.** This project has been bitten twelve times and the twelfth was a subtraction that no chapter printed. **Every count in your state record carries a line number or it does not go in, and every subtraction you print is recomputed by a script and not by a tired reader at one in the morning.**
- **A bold word in running prose or dialogue.** Scan for it. **The standard is two lines in a whole batch and both of them a panel.**
- **A duplicate paragraph and a reused phrase across the batch.** The words *about nine people*, *at the fourth bell*, *in the ordinary way*, *about four seconds* and *a thing that is not a complaint* have all become this volume's tics. **Each of them may appear often and none of them may be load-bearing in two chapters running.** **The *about four seconds* tic was reduced from five instances in one chapter in Batch 0002 to one; hold that.**
- **A scene attributed to a person who was not in it.** This class has been found in four separate batches. **If you attribute a sentence to a person, check they were there, and check the place exists.**
- **A person produced.** The volume's proudest moment so far is a bench that asked instead of sending. **A boy sent up a lane with a number is a production, and on this shelf the mechanism of production is a Bench form four hundred years old and is in nobody's book.**
- **A narrator that knows it is in a book, and a narrator doing arithmetic on the reader's behalf.** **Batch 0002 repaired fourteen of the first kind in ten chapters and miscounted them as seventeen, and a review pass then found three more of a kind the scan could not see: two that were still in the files and one that was on the list of repairs and had never been repaired. The three are at 164:35 (*the point of the chapter … in Volume 04*), 167:27 (*Check: 3,060 + 940 = 4,000.*) and 163:29 (*Check it however you like … it comes to the same figure in three ways*).** Scan for *this volume*, *this chapter*, *this batch*, *the reader*, *the author*, *narration*, *a later pass*, *a chapter* used as a container for a thing, **and the word *Check*, and any sentence in which a sum is done for the reader's benefit by a voice that is not in the room.** The last of those is the one that got past two passes: 163:29 reads like a *character* checking a figure and is a checker who is not in the room. **Note that *the reader of a district* and *a bench's own book* are not this and will show up in a naive scan.**
