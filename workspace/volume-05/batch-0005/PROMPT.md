Do not re-read a list.** A list in this project has been checked against the previous list instead of against the chapters six times in two hundred chapters, three separate lists were each short once, the Volume 04 close found one short by ten, and the Volume 04 audit found a twenty-second day-count instance that four passes had missed and a weekday that the project's own instrument was **structurally incapable of seeing**, because the pattern recorded as plural-safe was `\b(monday|tuesdays?|…)\b` and its Monday alternative has no plural. **Run `\b(mondays?|tuesdays?|wednesdays?|thursdays?|fridays?|saturdays?|sundays?)\b`, and run it after the last save.** When you print a number, print what the number is a count of; when you print a duplicate count, print the threshold and the unit beside it. **When you take a number from a state file, measure it again** — and know that a Batch 0003 writer took a recurring figure out of a table, added a wages figure to it, and got £171 16s 8d for a thing that is £178 13s 4d, and caught it by printing the working, and the state layer had to be corrected at the same time.

**Print the unit price in the sentence, find it, and multiply.** And print the subtraction where the subtraction is the point, because **`206:45` carried an entire flood in one subtraction and had the sign the wrong way round through five passes, and the reason nobody caught it is that its working was the sentence itself and a sentence cannot be checked against itself.**

**And do not resolve a thread in order to make your batch land.** The new question, the carrying, the blank in the third line, Nesh Arrad's clock, the unasked fourth hand, the clerk of thirty-four's unopened letter, the claim of forty-five pounds two shillings and eightpence, the dead man in the fourth line, the box of forms nobody can reach, the four hundred and eighty-five pounds, the ninety-one numbered sheets, the minute, the woman of about seventy-six and the two women who have been in that room, the chair a dead woman sat in, and the seed in a cellar — **all of them come out of this batch exactly as they went in, except the column, which this batch opens because the outline says Chapter 245 or 246 and it is the volume's climax and not a thing a batch may postpone for tidiness. The carrying is off nothing. The blank is a blank. Nobody is redeemed. Nobody is forgiven. The name stays off the eleven-hand sheet and the space at its foot stays empty and undashed. The eleven feet goes into a third document in three copies and not onto the settlement. And the column is opened, and it costs a good name, and nobody is thanked.**
