## Where the case stands on the morning Chapter 51 opens

**Volume 01 ended ten days after the ruling, on the eleventh day of a twenty-day unloaded period.** The ninth bridge stands on four props over a channel with the west seat bedded the day after the span closed — the second day of the unloaded period, out of a three weeks' cure Puck priced at the hearing — and still curing. The count runs every change with two hands under the deck. The bell is rung at every change and its wheel and pin are inspected on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope. A strip of board about eighteen inches long is nailed beside the lawful-traveler register on the eastern approach, carrying Hanne Kolm's sworn column heading, the line *CARRY ON FROM HERE. THESE ARE NAMES OF PERSONS WHO CROSSED. THEY ARE NOT A REGISTER AND THEY REPLACE NOTHING*, and, in a child's careful hand, *AND NAMES*. Twenty-three names were on it when the board went up, and the old register's last line, scratched by an unknown hand nine years ago, reads *and after this nobody*, which the strip is a deliberate and earned answer to.

The **public maintenance review** is the fourth morning bell of the third month after the ruling. It may be called early by any party on a named reading outside the band the keeper has kept, and it may not be postponed. Ilyan must be produced at it with his report and the two copies of his own sequence, to say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and may not send somebody with his name on it.

## The four things `outline/series.md` assigns to Volume 02, and where they land in these ten cards

Volume 02's central pressure is that Orison's neighborhoods compete for limited access to the Bench while a seemingly fair rule produces displacement. Its midpoint reversal is that a different chamber copies *lawful traveler* from a maintenance ruling to close a poor district during a sanitation dispute, proving that administrative language can become a weapon even when the original precedent is not formally cited. Its power progression is **Seam Reader** — Ilyan learns to read exclusions and sensory afterimages. Its volume climax forces a choice between a clean ruling that saves the district temporarily and a messy shared charter that gives residents an appeal. Its resolution is that the appeal survives and Sera recovers a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms. Its new question is which region has accepted a rule with no living witness at all.

**All of that is Volume 02's arc across Chapters 51–100. Chapters 51–60 must deliver the first movement of it and must not resolve the volume.** Specifically, these ten cards must:

1. **Put the Halloway print in a room, early, in somebody else's hands.** Kett has gone to stand in a poor district's sanitation room under protest. The print was made on the second day after the ruling and brought up off the fen road on the fifth, four days later, so it was **two days old at the moment it arrived** — those are the same figure, not a contradiction, and do not move either date to make them look like one. The same words in the same order in a different column, printed by a district that had been sent nothing and had asked nobody for a copy, and *lawful traveler* — the ninth bridge's own two-hundred-year-old word for *registered* — is doing the opposite work at a low-water culvert. **This is the midpoint reversal and it is already in motion; Chapters 51–53 should show its consequences spreading and should let Ilyan's name be the first thing the room puts on the table against him.** Do not stage the reversal itself as a surprise: the reader knows. Stage the *cost* of it.
2. **Deliver the power progression honestly and at the right price.** Ilyan reaches **Stage 2, Seam Reader**: during a hearing field he begins to perceive the dependencies and exclusions in an active covenant, and the effect appears as physical mismatch — two shadows from one body, a bell vibrating before it is struck, a door that opens toward the person who is absent. **The limit is that he sees how the rule is assembled, not why its authors wanted it, and a coherent rule can still be unjust. The cost is that the rule's burden leaves an afterimage in his body or senses, and persistent effects accumulate until he rests, changes a decision, or shares the burden.** Do not let him use the new perception in Chapter 51. Earn it across the batch: a practice, a failure, a changed decision, and a named physical cost. **He does not gain the power by being right about a speech.** Consider making the accumulation visible and unpleasant by Chapter 58, and consider having it change a decision rather than simply hurt him.
3. **Set up the volume's climax without staging it.** The neighborhood hearing that forces a clean ruling versus a messy shared charter must be *in the air* — Kett's experience in Halloway's room, the districts' reaction to a citable precedent, the arrival of at least one Orison chamber's own clerk — but the hearing itself belongs to Chapters 61–70. The batch's own resolution should be partial, real, and paid for: something changes on the ground in Halloway, somebody's burden is named, and Ilyan's own record costs him something he did not choose.
4. **Leave the Quiet Chamber fragment for later.** Sera's recovery of a fragment of the original terms is the volume's resolution, not this batch's. What this batch may do is show the *consequence* of the one line she entered under her license — that a refusal is entered against a burden and not against a person — being read, copied, misunderstood, or attempted, and let the archive's silence about the rest of the page stand.

## Canon from Volume 01 that must be preserved

**The amended charter's ten terms.** Read them in `state/continuity.md` before drafting. The load-bearing ones for Volume 02:

- **The maintenance-review condition is restored as a power to call the deck clear.** A named keeper, having read the joint on a named inspection, may call the deck clear, and the deck being clear is the whole of the power. **The original wording is expressly not recovered and is not supplied by the ruling.** A keeper may not stop a load.
- **A pause lasts as long as the loads standing on the deck and no longer.** If the deck is not clear within that time the bell is a warning and the span is released, and no keeper has held anything. A load that does not come off is a load that has not been stopped by anybody.
- **The ward rate is paid at the moment of the work to the person performing it, by name, on a day-book line, in chalk, and not to a body and not to a place and not to an address.** An empty line is a record. A full line is a wage. The district pays the stopped hour, the under-deck watch, the pump's shortage, and the guild's hands for the unloaded works, out of the district's own road funds. The guild's four props came off the fourth bridge, whose keeper Wist Anhalt is not a party, is not represented, has objected in four words, and whose objection is preserved and answered.
- **The appeal.** Any person affected may challenge the ruling before the review date, in a public sitting, on notice to every named party. **The appeal does not suspend access, housing, or work, on the court's own seventh-morning-bell ground, now written into the charter so nobody has to ask for it again.**
- **The exit.** The charter may be refused as to a specific burden. The refusal is entered in a sworn column kept in the ward by Hanne Kolm, which records the burden refused, the person who carried it instead, the date, and what it did to the span, and which records no consent and no address. **No person is removed from any register by refusal, and no relief already entered is withdrawn by refusal.** The right to refuse may not be used so as to conceal a harm from a person who depends on the span.
- **Access, housing, and work.** A participating party may cross at a named hour, on the repaired load path, without registration, at the ward rate and not at a toll. The lower-road tenancy is protected until an appeal or a review says otherwise and is not ended by a refusal. The work record is portable. The landlord is bound. **A person not entered in the lawful-traveler register is not a trespasser by that fact alone, and a keeper admits any person on foot, or with a handcart, or with a wheelbarrow, who offers no load and does not enter the load path.** The protection is not conditional on the ninth-bridge record and may be ended only by a named finding and not by the lapse of any instrument.
- **The access condition of the span continues for a lawful traveler of this span.** That is the phrase. It is two hundred years old. It means registered. Kett objected to it in open court on the last day of the hearing, was told it is the charter's own word and cannot be rewritten at the end of a hearing, and was believed by nobody. **This is the engine of the volume. Do not let anyone in this batch discover that it is dangerous; they already know, and it is being done.**
- **The word *exception* was spoken once in the hearing and refused as a description, and the settlement is one.** Sera Quill said in the open, in Chapter 49, that in the room it is an **amendment** — which is what the ten terms say was done to a charter — and that in a few days it will be called an **exception**, because that is the word this city uses for a thing one bridge is allowed to do that the charter does not allow, and a word costs a clerk less than an argument costs a district. She said that nothing in the ten terms would stop a form being printed with that word at the top of it, and that when it is, the paper in the clerk's hand will still be the paper she verified and the word will not be on it, and somebody will come to her and ask which of the two documents is the real one. Dain objected that it was a prediction and not an objection. **The word is a Bench term throughout Volume 01 and is not in the instrument. Do not put it in the charter. Let it travel as a word, which is what Volume 02 is about, and note that the print has already done the first half of what she described.**

## Unspent pressures to carry

- **The appeal is unused.** The charter gives every person affected the right to challenge the ruling, in a public sitting, on notice to every named party, without suspending access, housing, or work. Nobody has appealed anything. Four districts may adopt the settlement or not. **An appeal nobody has ever used is a right on paper until the first person needs it, and the first person who needs it in Volume 02 should not be a district.**
- **The court has no rule for what happens to a ruling nobody appeals.** Ten terms, a review date, an appeal route, an exit, and a citable precedent, and no answer to the question of who enforces term four in a district that adopted the document and then stopped paying.
- **What a precedent is and what a template is.** Sera named the difference in a corridor and told Ilyan to be frightened of it. He was not, and a rider arrived four days later. The batch's job is to show the difference acquiring a body: an office, a form, a queue, a person who believes they are applying the ninth bridge's ruling.
- **The refusal column is a template too.** A sworn column that records a burden refused, who carried it instead, the date, and what it did to the span — copied by one ward's choice, readable by anybody, and capable of being turned into a list of people who have refused. **Hanne Kolm's whole argument was that a name on a page about a burden is not a name on a page about a family. Volume 02 should find out whether she was right.**
- **The two measurements and the third.** A bridge that keeps two numbers, plus a third that only exists under a standing load, is a span whose readings will be quoted differently by four people who were all in the room.
- **Orison's nine bridges and a first Verdict that is a basalt court, not a palace.** The city has been scenery for fifty chapters. This is the first batch in which it is a place with neighborhoods competing for limited access to the Bench.
- **Nadia Kestrel** remains an independent Earth witness on the other side. Every exchange must carry a cost and there is no rescue shortcut. **Stage 2's afterimage cost is a good place to reach for the Shale Mirror question without resolving it.**

## The ten cards

Use these as chapter plans, not as prose. Each card must become a complete scene with its own physical ground, its own resistance, and its own consequence.

**Chapter 0051 — the print in Halloway's room.** Open on the ground, not in a hall: the low-water culvert, the smell, the standpipe queue, the district that printed the ninth bridge's settlement two days after the ruling. **Maren Kett is in the room and it is going badly, and he has his written instruction produced and read, as he asked.** Ilyan is not there and hears about it second-hand, and the second-hand is worse. A named local person in Halloway — a woman or a man with an ordinary job and a real want — is the reader of this volume's first movement. End on a decision: somebody in Orison decides to send a record-holder into Halloway, and Ilyan is the record-holder, and Sera says plainly that she will come and that coming is not the same as being on his side.

**Chapter 0052 — the terms in a strange room.** Sera carries the settlement's read-back — the sentences in the guild's words — into a room in Halloway and does the job she licensed for: she says what the ninth bridge decided, who was standing behind each sentence, and what it cost. **She is not a witness for Ilyan and she says so in the first minute.** The first thing the room does with the text is not obey it; it is find the sentence that helps somebody local, and that is how precedents get their first honest use. Physical work early: the culvert, the gate, the water, the queue.

**Chapter 0053 — Ilyan in the room, and the room's first question.** He goes as a witness to his own attached observation and is asked, in the first ten minutes, what he did about this. He has no standing outside the ninth-bridge matter and cannot represent anybody. He does the thing he has learned to do: he reads the numbers and says what he cannot read. A district clerk in Halloway produces a form that is not the ninth bridge's settlement and is built out of it, and somebody has already used the phrase *lawful traveler* in a notice. **The turn is not a speech; it is a form.** End with the first consequence that cannot be taken back.

**Chapter 0054 — the cost of being a name.** Ilyan's precedent citation is used against him in a room where he has no constituency, and the district is right to use it, because he made it citable. **The loss is not the price of anything and it is not the archive's fault and it must not be presented as a punishment for the Volume 01 win.** Somebody in Halloway asks him a fair question he cannot answer, and the answer costs an hour of somebody's work that is not bought. Physical: a load, a queue, a gate, a bell that is not connected to anything.

**Chapter 0055 — the first attempt at a common answer, refused, in a way that is not procedural.** Orison's chambers want a city-wide maintenance and access rule because one legible rule is what Kett asked for and it is what everybody now expects. **The room fails on a real conflict, not on a missing document and not on a refusal to be universal.** Someone is right about a common rule and someone is right about what a common rule does to a ward that has no address, and neither of them is a villain. Do not make this a fifth local arrangement. End with the problem named more precisely than it was.

**Chapter 0056 — the first use of the afterimage, and the first failure.** Stage 2 arrives as a physical mismatch inside a hearing field: a covenant's exclusions becoming visible, and the burden leaving an afterimage in Ilyan's body. **He uses it once, gets something wrong with it, and it costs him.** The limit: he sees how the rule is assembled, not why its authors wanted it, and a coherent rule is still unjust. Do not resolve the accumulation problem; make it a thing he is now carrying.

**Chapter 0057 — somebody else pays.** A named person in Halloway or on the low water takes a burden the room has assigned to nobody, and it is visible, and it is not noble, and it is not free. **This is the chapter's whole work: a cost, paid by a person, on the page.** A named local voice; not a district, not a ward, not a constituency.

**Chapter 0058 — Ilyan changes a decision instead of winning an argument.** He takes a loss on the record to stop something he could have won with his name, and the something is smaller than he wanted. The afterimage accumulation reaches the point where it changes what he is able to do, and he rests or changes a decision or shares a burden — **pick one and pay for it in the body, not in a speech.**

**Chapter 0059 — the first appeal, or the first refusal of one.** Somebody affected actually uses the route Volume 01 wrote: a challenge to a ruling on the ninth bridge, or a refusal under term seven entered in Hanne Kolm's sworn column, and the machinery either works or visibly does not. **The point is to find out what a right looks like when somebody who was not in the room when it was written needs it.** Do not resolve Volume 02's climax here.

**Chapter 0060 — the batch close.** A partial, real, paid-for change on the ground, a burden named and visible, Ilyan's own record having cost him something, the afterimage unresolved, and a new question that the next batch can carry. The volume's climax — a neighborhood hearing forcing a clean ruling against a messy shared charter — must be **in the air and named, not staged.**
