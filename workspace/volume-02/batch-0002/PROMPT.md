## Where the case stands on the morning Chapter 61 opens

**Chapter 60 ends on the twenty-ninth day of the unloaded period, at the last bell, on a bridge that has been open and unloaded for nine days.** The props came out on the twentieth day; the west seat has **five days of Puck's fortnight still to sit in** before anybody may put a load on it. The count runs every change with two hands under the deck and the bell is rung at every change, and the bell's pin has been looked at on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope.

**The ninth-bridge charter's public review is the fourth morning bell of the third month after the ruling, roughly seventy days out from the end of Chapter 60, and it is not forty.** It may be called early by any party on a named reading outside the band the keeper has kept, and it may not be postponed. **The runway is load-bearing: Ilyan's appeal must be heard or disposed of before the review date.** Ilyan must be produced at it with his report and the two copies of his own sequence, to say out loud what the record cost and who paid it, and may not send somebody with his name on it.

**On the twenty-ninth, in the basalt court, the adjourned common-rule sitting is held on Ansa Rell's own question, put by Mr Vale in writing: whether a district may pay a person who has no address.** The court's own first note is that a clean instrument can be written in one afternoon and will close the gate and be payable and unhouse nobody, and that the other thing cannot be written at all, and that four benches have tried it in nineteen years and produced eleven valid instruments and none that was affordable, and that the court is afraid. The court's second note: the missing thing is not a rule and not a remedy; **it is a piece of paper that a person can swear to in a room with no address in it, and no bench in this city holds one that travels.**

**That is the volume's climax, and this batch is where it is staged.** A neighborhood hearing that forces a choice between a clean ruling that saves the district temporarily and a messy shared charter that gives residents an appeal. Deliver the choice, make both sides real, and **do not resolve the appeal's survival or Sera's recovery of a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms in this batch** — those are the volume's resolution, in Chapters 71–100. What this batch must end with is the choice made, costed, and paid for, and the appeal *in the air and in use*, not safely secured.

**Ilyan's first appeal is pending.** Filed on the twenty-third under term six, before the review date, notice served on every named party and one seal, the appeal not suspending access, housing, or work. The condition disputed is whether a review held at one span may be received in a bench that has not adopted the instrument. It must be heard or disposed of in this batch, and the hearing of it is the best available vehicle for the neighborhood hearing, because the challenge *is* the neighborhood hearing: the people of a low-water district are asking a court whether a ruling made about a bridge governs a gate four miles away.

## Canon from Batch 0001 that must be preserved

**The amended charter's ten terms.** Read them in `state/continuity.md` before drafting. The load-bearing ones for this batch:

- **The maintenance-review condition is restored as a power to call the deck clear. A keeper may not stop a load. The original wording is expressly not recovered and is not supplied by the ruling.**
- **A pause lasts as long as the loads standing on the deck and no longer.** If the deck is not clear the bell is a warning and the span is released, and no keeper has held anything.
- **The ward rate is paid at the moment of the work to the person performing it, by name, on a day-book line, in chalk, and not to a body and not to a place and not to an address. An empty line is a record. A full line is a wage.** The district pays the stopped hour, the under-deck watch, the pump's shortage, and the guild's hands for the unloaded works, out of its own road funds. The guild's four props came off the fourth bridge, whose keeper Wist Anhalt is not a party, is not represented, has objected in four words, and whose objection is preserved and answered. **The props go back on the same cart before the review, or the district pays.**
- **The appeal.** Any person affected may challenge the ruling before the review date, in a public sitting, on notice to every named party. **The appeal does not suspend access, housing, or work**, on the court's own seventh-morning-bell ground, now written into the charter.
- **The exit.** The charter may be refused as to a specific burden. The refusal is entered in a sworn register kept in **the ward** and sworn by **the landlord of the lower road**, recording the burden refused, who carried it instead, the date, and what it did to the span, and recording no consent and no address. No person is removed from any register by refusal and no relief already entered is withdrawn by refusal. The right to refuse may not be used so as to conceal a harm from a person who depends on the span. **Neither the appeal nor the exit reaches a person four miles away, and this batch is where that fact stops being an observation and becomes a hearing.**
- **Access, housing, and work**, including that a person not entered in the lawful-traveler register is not a trespasser by that fact alone, that a keeper admits any person on foot, or with a handcart, or with a wheelbarrow, who offers no load and does not enter the load path, and **that the protection is not conditional on the ninth-bridge record and may be ended only by a named finding and not by the lapse of any instrument.**

## The ten cards

Use these as chapter plans, not as prose. Each card must become a complete scene with its own physical ground, its own resistance, and its own consequence.

**Chapter 0061 — the sitting on the ward, and the first thing it finds.** Open on the ground in the basalt court: the docket, the eleven places, the nineteen names, the furniture nobody has. The twenty-ninth is the day Vale's question is heard and it is a category question, and the first thing the sitting finds is that the ward is not only unnamed but **cannot be described without somebody saying who lives in it, and that both of the words the room has for it are wrong in front of witnesses.** Ilyan's language fails him here in the worst place yet: this is a hearing about a *category* and the category is made of legal words he does not have. Physical work early. **Do not let the clean instrument be written yet.**

**Chapter 0062 — the clean instrument, and what it closes.** Somebody competent and right writes the rule that can be written in an afternoon: it closes the gate to loads, it is payable, it pays four hands, and nobody is unhoused. **It is good, and every person who wanted something in Halloway gets less of it, and the room is asked to choose.** Give the clean instrument a real advocate and a real motive. The point is not that it is a trap. **The point is that it works and it is smaller than the truth, and that a person can be relieved by it and also be diminished by it, and both of those are true on the same afternoon.**

**Chapter 0063 — who the ward is, on the record.** The hearing has to stop arguing about a category and start finding out who is in one, and this is a census problem in a city with no addresses: forty-one at the standpipe, four hundred and eleven loads, nine on the traveller book, two hundred and forty people outside a rope. **Bram's line book is the only record of who actually crosses and it is not a constituency, and the room will want it to be one.** A named person is counted. The count changes what is possible and it also names people who did not ask to be named, and that is a real cost and it must land.

**Chapter 0064 — the messy charter begins, and it is worse to write than the clean one.** A shared charter that gives residents an appeal: who writes it, on what paper, sworn by whom, and what it costs the people who are in it. **This chapter should be about the actual labour of drafting and about the first person who asks not to be in it.** The charter must be visibly worse to make and visibly better to live in, and the reader should be able to see both.

**Chapter 0065 — the first sharing of a burden, in the body.** The afterimage accumulation has been building since the nineteenth. Ilyan **shares or takes a share of a burden that is his to take** — not another person's — and pays for it in the body, not in a speech, and it is not a cure. The token is reached once, at a cost. **The Shale Mirror question can be touched here and not resolved.**

**Chapter 0066 — a second room, a second district, and the same sentence.** The districts' reaction to a citable precedent, and the arrival of more than one Orison chamber's own clerk. A district that adopted the settlement by copying needs to be told what it adopted and what it did not. **Someone must pay for the telling, and the payer is not the district.** This is the template acquiring a second body, and the second body must have its own person in it with their own want.

**Chapter 0067 — the appeal is heard, and the city finds out what the machinery is for.** Ilyan's challenge on the twenty-third comes up. Notice was served on fourteen named parties and one seal and the appeal does not suspend access, housing, or work. **The hearing works. That is the discovery: a right nobody had used turns out to be the only thing in the case that runs.** And it produces a finding that is inconvenient for everybody, including Ilyan, and the finding is about the *limit* of the ninth-bridge settlement, not its expansion.

**Chapter 0068 — the choice, made in the room, in front of the people it is about.** The volume's climax. A clean ruling that saves the district temporarily, or a messy shared charter that gives residents an appeal. **Both are argued by people who are right. The clean one has a date on it and the district's money behind it. The charter has four terms that are about a bridge and a fifth that nobody can pay.** Ilyan must choose, and the choice must cost somebody, and the somebody must be named. **Do not let it be settled by a speech.**

**Chapter 0069 — what the choice costs, on the ground, within twenty-four hours.** The consequence chapter. Somebody's burden is visible, in chalk or in a body or in a shut gate. The appeal machinery is in use and it is not clean and it is not a triumph. **A character from Batch 0001 who has not yet been paid for what they carried must get their consequence, or the choice was free.**

**Chapter 0070 — the batch close.** A partial, real, paid-for change; a burden named and visible; Ilyan's record having cost him something; the afterimage unresolved. **The volume's resolution — the appeal surviving as a durable right, and Sera recovering a fragment of the original Quiet Chamber terms — must be in the air and named, not delivered.** The Bridgeward may be pushed once and must not be resolved. End on a question the next batch can carry.
