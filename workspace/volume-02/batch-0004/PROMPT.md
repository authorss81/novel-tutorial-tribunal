## Where the case stands on the morning Chapter 81 opens

**The public maintenance review was held on the twenty-seventh of the third month, at the fourth morning bell, at the span, in the open air, and it was not called early and it is not postponable. It is over. Chapter 80 ends on the first of the fourth month, at the sixth bell, at a low-water standpipe, with about two hundred people going up a mud bank.**

**What the review bought, in the ninth bridge's district's own arithmetic, in Meridith Vale's hand.** The road fund is four hundred and ten pounds a year. Two hands under a deck every change at the ward rate is two hundred and ten. The telling of Coldwater is fifteen. The charter of the district of Halloway is forty-four pounds and sixteen a year. The fourth bridge's four props are ninety-six. **The remainder after all four is forty-four pounds and four shillings, which is fifty-one pounds and sixteen short of the props.** One hand under a deck every change is a hundred and five pounds a year.

**So: four new props are bought, at ninety-six pounds, out of the ninth bridge's district road fund. The four old props come out of the fourth bridge's deck and go into a yard, and that deck is open in the weather until the new timbers arrive, and nobody in the court has any power to shorten that.** The fourth bridge has been propped for thirty-nine days by four old timbers that had already stood under the ninth bridge's west seat for a fortnight. **The under-deck watch at the ninth bridge is reduced from two hands to one hand for this year, and the count goes on at every change and is entered as what it is.** The fifth-bell reading stays. The bell's wheel and pin are span maintenance, inspected on a named day by two people, one of whom has pulled the rope. **If the band is ever read outside the band the keeper has kept, by a name and with a date, the review is callable on the day it is read and is not waitable.** The pale permanent set has not moved. The span has carried a load at every change for fifty-four days, every figure taken with a moving cart. **There is no figure of thirty-four days and no figure of forty-four days anywhere in this case. Do not use either.** **There is also no figure of five weeks and no figure of seventy days *as a run to the review* — both were runway figures and both were wrong, and the corrected runways are 49 days from the eighth of the second month, six weeks from the fourteenth, and four weeks from the twenty-ninth of the second month.** **That ban is narrow and it is only about that distance. "Five weeks" is live elsewhere in the manuscript as ordinary elapsed time since Ilyan came over — the token in his pocket in Chapter 56, Nahl's gallery in Chapter 66, the bank in Chapters 69 and 70 — and those are canon. Do not use either figure to date the review, and do not "correct" the elapsed-time uses into some other figure.**

**T. Blore, twenty-two, eleven months at the west end under a deck, lost the second hand at that review, and is nineteen miles off working a deck at the cut banks, and does not know yet.** Oren Sedge is the man who will tell him and is not the man who took it. He is the only person in this case's record who is named as having lost something in it, and it took a defendant with limited standing to put him there. **The guild is out of money, the district has forty-four pounds and four shillings left in the year, neither of those bodies is a person, and neither has said anything to him yet. An apprenticeship in that trade wants two seasons of a span and one is not two.** Nobody thanked him and nobody has written to him.

**The charter of the district of Halloway is working, is three months old, and is not secure.** Nine sheets, five terms, four of them a bridge's and a fifth with a person in it. It costs half a farthing a bucket at a low-water standpipe — the first tax in nineteen years, set by a bench of four because the cartage that feeds the district's road fund has been nil since the fourteenth of the month before last, and set at exactly eleven pounds four in the quarter and not a unit more. **It has no standing outside one poor district, no rate it can pay out of anything but a pump, and no second sitting of the quarter has yet produced an argument about whether the rate can go up.** Cael Rundle pays it on his own lane and said *this is the right rate* before anybody said anything.

**What is in the page the holder keeps, and who is holding it.** Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, refuse hauler, about two hundred loads a year, paid a third of a haul rate and **about a pound and eleven a quarter for keeping nine sheets and a page**. She may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one. **The page has four entries.** The thirty years is entered as owed and not paid, and has been entered *as a figure for the first time in its existence*: **a hundred and thirteen thousand four hundred hours at threepence the hour is one thousand four hundred and seventeen pounds and ten shillings**, entered over Bryn Sallow's refusal, in three hands, on the same page, with a line saying the figure is wrong by at least two hundred and two pounds and ten shillings and by an amount nobody can name. **The hundred and fifty hauls of the last eleven months are entered as owed and not paid, and the rate for a haul has been set at the district's own rate, which is a third of a haul rate, so a haul in Halloway is two thirds short of a haul** — and the rate was set by a bench of four, and asked for by the holder, and cannot be appealed by anybody, and is read aloud by the holder at the pump on the first of every quarter. **A bench of four in a district that has never had a matter before a court has two debts in its book and has paid neither.**

**The question Chapter 80 ends on, in the holder's own words, and it is the engine of this batch: there is no procedure in this city for what happens to a page when the holder cannot keep it.** The fifth term wants a person in the district who can read. There is one, and she is twenty-nine, and she hauls two hundred loads a year, and everybody in that district knows she is doing it, and the whole of the district's law now depends on whether a hauler's back holds out until somebody who has never done it tries. **A court in Orison is going to sit down in about four years and ask how an appeal survives in a district that is being squeezed, and the honest answer is *as long as a woman of twenty-nine can still read and haul at the same time*, and that is an answer about a person, and this city cannot write a person down.**

**What the batch before last left in the air, and what this batch can and cannot use.** The first appeal in the record was heard and worked perfectly and produced a **duty to answer** — a bench that has received the instrument shall answer, in writing, in public, within **eleven days**, whether it holds any page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected may swear to in its own room at its own counter without leave, **and the court has no power to compel the answer and says so on the face of it.** Nine of eleven answered; seven said they hold nothing; two named a hundred-and-sixty-year-old book neither of which anybody can swear to; one bench did not answer at all, which is not a breach of anything. **The seventh *no* costs what it costs:** a bench that has told the truth about itself in its own hand has priced a room in its own district at one answer in eleven days, and three of the seven have already been asked by a person in their own district to produce a page and have had to say, from their own letter, that there was nothing in their room.

**The eleventh-day answers, in one place, and they are canon.** Seven *no*, from seven named-or-unnamed benches. Two *no* with a hundred-and-sixty-year-old book. One *yes*, **a standing authority of preservation that holds every instrument printed in this city for four years in a series running by plate, where any person may read any of it at a counter on a day, and which no person has ever asked for anything.** One bench that did not answer.

**And the second question that was put to it, in Batch 0003, and the answer.** Asked for the first time in four years — will this authority *hold a page* sworn by a person in a district, for that person, and produce it on request — it answered **no**, in eleven lines, in the same office hand, and said for the second time in fifteen days that it can show that a rule was printed and cannot hold a person: *a sworn page is a record of a person, and an authority that keeps records of persons is a bench, and this is not a bench, and it decides nothing — an office with no person in it is not a door, it is a shelf.* **The authority was never confronted. It was asked a question nobody had thought of asking, in writing, and it answered, and the answer was a reason and not a silence.**

**One bench of the seven has withdrawn its answer in writing and can hold a page from the fifteenth of the third month.** The district of **Fallowditch**, a bench of three, eleven miles from the fen road, clerk **Perditta Nye, twenty-nine, three years in the post**, withdrew the answer of the eighteenth *because of a woman called Adela Cray who asked the bench for a place to put an agreement she holds and was told there was none and went home.* It held the agreement on the eleventh in its own room over a shop that was a chandler's, and could do nothing whatever with it, and the landlord was not there and was not a party. **A fortnight's notice is between a woman and a man and no bench in this city hears that.**

**The places with no name in them.** The **Nine Yards**, four miles beyond Lowgill bar: sixty-one people, nine gates, one well with a bucket on a chain, no district, no bench, no clerk, no keeper, paying **Nethergill's** rate for a hundred and forty-one years for a bar they do not own, because a rate followed a road and a boundary followed a hedge. **Merrin Coke, twenty-nine, keeps the count in charcoal marks on a shutter.** A matter came before the First Verdict against nobody; the respondents were a carter, a bench of four, and *the keeper of Lowgill bar*, there being no keeper and none ever sworn in in this city. **The place is a fact in the pleadings and is not a party, because a place is not a person.** Service was made by nailing the notice to the post, in the open air, and the court accepted it, and **there is no clause in any instrument in this case for notice to a place.**

**And the finding of the last batch, in three hands in three places, which is this batch's spine: a place can be given a page only by making a person a body, and the only person available is the carrier, and a holder is not a carrier, so the day he holds a page anywhere the appeal in the district of Halloway is stayed for about four hundred people.** A holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one. There is no instrument in this city that says how a second carrier is sworn in.

**The carrier's clause has been used once, and here is its whole text, because this batch must not contradict it.** The terms of the charter are carried, at the carrier's own cost and without pay, to any district that asks for them; the carrier is named and it is one person and nobody else may carry them; if he refuses, or is unable, or is not within four miles, the terms are not carried and the appeal is stayed until another carrier is sworn in that district. **Past four miles the clause makes it a favour and a favour cannot be entered. It obliges a carrier to four miles.** He walked eleven miles to Nethergill. He is a party to nothing, is not a bench and is not a body, and is owed nothing by anybody.

**Harrow's fourth procedure, invented in public at the review, is the whole of what the city has invented about succession in this case:** where an instrument refers to another carrier being sworn in a district and there is no person sworn in that district, any bench in that district may swear in a carrier on the application of a person of that district, in its own room, in public, on a day the bench appoints, and the name shall be entered in the bench's own book with a date, and swearing in a carrier is not an adoption of the instrument in any other district. **The court has no power over any such bench. It is not precedent. It cannot be used by anybody, because the only district with a carrier is the only district that does not need one — and that sentence is in a clerk's own book in a chamber in Orison with a date on it, and nobody in this case has read it.**

**The two forms, and the finding about the word.** A form summarising the charter with **EXCEPTION at the top of it in a bigger letter than any other word on the page** arrived un-ordered, sealed and numbered, printed in Orison in a district's typeface by a shop filling a space in a docket. **It is in every clerk's drawer in nine districts and in no instrument in this case, and a court has no power over a form.** **The finding of Chapter 72: the word is not a label on a rule, it is a claim that the rule is temporary — and the charter of the district of Halloway has no end on it, because it was made out of a bridge and a bridge does not end.** It cost Hal Dreeve, thirty-four, a barrow-hirer, a season of four months at a weighhouse and a roof on his mother's two rooms, and Parnell Slee, the foreman, said out loud that the form gave him a sentence instead of the sentence he had, and asked for the shelf to be sent back. **The first district to print the charter's terms on a form headed with the word is still not found, and this batch may find it once.**

**Do not put the word *exception* in any instrument.** Let it travel as a word, and let it cost somebody.

**The word *ward* is canon and may not be re-derived.** It is a guild's word for a duty a district owes and has not got round to, not a place anybody lives in. It is in the ninth bridge's term book under a bridge heading, *duties carried, not discharged*, with a star against it; it is corroborated in the standing of the wards, a hundred and ninety years old, in which **eleven of fifty-five duties have ever been performed**; in the traveller book's own front leaf at Halloway, one ward, five duties, none performed, the last performer dead thirty-one years; and in **Hanne Kolm's sworn volume, the one document in this case that does not use the word at all.** The entered finding: **a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does.** A rate may be paid to a ward only if the ward is a body; the ward is not a body because it is not a person; a duty cannot be paid anything, it can only be given work; and **the work goes to whoever is standing nearest.** A count taken on the low water returned **five places**: the bank above the standpipe, the flat, Weir Lane behind the pump with the room over the pump inside it, the upper street, and the two unnamed yards a hauler called *the bit by the wall* and *by the pump*. **A place is the category the court came to get rid of, and in Chapter 78 a builder asked to be counted as one and was refused.**

**The four hundred people and five places are the volume's problem and this batch is where the court has to say something about them.** Tarin Keel's count has no line in it: **there is no line anywhere in this city on which the number of people who are not at the pump goes.** A bench of four in one district struck out its household column at a ratepayer's request and entered the loss of accuracy against itself, and a child of fourteen is a number in a record and is not named in any page of this court, and must not be.

**Ilyan's record has six things in it that he cannot un-enter**, named with their sizes in his own hand on the fourteenth of the third month: two copies of a private sequence from *The Sovereign's Wake*; the precedent he asked for in a corridor; the first appeal, which worked perfectly and reached nobody who needed it; the carrier's clause, the fifth, which cost a district a day and a half of a cart and is the only one of the five somebody can call out and ask to see him; and the sixth, **a figure of thirty years' work entered into a district's book without the consent of the person it is owed to, who refused it, and which is wrong, and which nobody has paid him for and nobody will.** **He is now a witness in one more matter, and a witness can be produced.** He said out loud in the same entry that the only thing he can offer against the wrongness is that he said it was wrong before he did it, and that he would answer a bench's question again.

**The two things nobody can file, and one of them is the batch's engine.** First: **a rate in a poor district can be set and read out and cannot be appealed, because the person who asked for it may not appeal and may not be named in an appeal and is the only person who can read.** Second: **a page has no successor, and the court has no power over the charter, and the only bench in this city that has ever held a page for a poor person is a bench of three eleven miles away that did it for one woman and one agreement and cannot do anything with either.** Neither of these is a rule and neither is a remedy, and this batch is where somebody has to try.

## Canon from Batch 0003 that must be preserved

**The word on a form is a claim of temporariness.** The charter has no end on it. The clean instrument that lost, Form L-3, expires at the third sitting of next quarter, and the word is a correct Bench word for a temporary permission with a limit, an expiry and a burden, and it is old, and it is on the top of a form and in no instrument.

**A holder is a door with no room behind it.** One rule keeps her out of appeals and puts her in front of everybody. She reads the charter to about four hundred people and the rate aloud every quarter and the notice a woman of twenty-six cannot read and a man of fifty needs her to say.

**A public answer is a price.** Seven benches said *no* in their own hands in eleven days and the price of a room in those districts is that answer. A bench that has said *no* cannot begin holding a page without making its own letter false, and cannot change its answer quietly, because a letter can be found.

**The one *yes* is a yes about paper.** An office with no person in it is a shelf. It can prove a rule was printed for four years and it cannot hold a person, and it has now said so twice, in the same hand, and the honest answer from every door in Orison is that the paper says something about a person.

**A copy of a term stops where a person begins.** A clerk does not copy a man's name into a district's paper when a district has not asked for a man. Four complete copies of the charter exist and the only one made in a kitchen binds nobody and cannot be come back for, and a short one is already in circulation.

**The thirty years is a figure and the figure is wrong and it is entered over a refusal.** Two sets of hours, both honest, both wrong a little, four minutes apart in a four-hour day, side by side on a page, with the line down the middle of the slate still there. A person is not a measuring instrument, and that goes on a page now.

**The distances are fixed: the ninth bridge to Halloway four miles; the ninth bridge to Nethergill eleven; Halloway to Nethergill seven; the Nine Yards about fifteen from the bridge. One hand under a deck every change is a hundred and five pounds a year. A barrow is four loads to a cart and a haul is a barrow's load. A working tide at the low-water gate is ten and a half hours by the gate hand's own count, and the turn is one day in seven, and a week comes to about sixty-three. The sixth bell is the turn at Halloway: the flat is walkable, the gate is half up, and the water is coming.**

**The three measurements stay apart.** The pale line on the deck is the permanent set. The band is one and a half fingers on a light load and up to three on a heavy one, never observed to accumulate, and every figure in the guild's count book was taken with a moving cart. The third number exists only under a standing load. **Any scene that reports drift must say which kind of load produced the figure.** The ninth bridge has been carrying loads at every change since the third of the second month and the count now runs with one hand, and the pale permanent set has not moved. **The flat's load count is one sequence and it is his, and any scene that reports it must report one of the four figures — four hundred and six on the fourteenth, four hundred and nine on the seventeenth, four hundred and nine again on the nineteenth, four hundred and eleven on the twentieth — or say when and by whom it was counted.** The last count was four hundred and eleven, counted from the flat by I. Vester alone on the twentieth of the first month and stated by him to be wrong by about forty, and nobody has counted it since.
