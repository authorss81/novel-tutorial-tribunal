## Where the case stands on the morning Chapter 71 opens

**The charter of the district of Halloway is five days old.** Nine sheets. Five terms: the first four are marked copies of terms of the ninth-bridge instrument; the fifth is original and is about a person. **The second half of the fourth term is the carrier's clause and it names Ilyan Vester**: the terms are carried to any district that asks, at his own cost and without pay, and if he refuses, or is unable, or is not within four miles, the terms are not carried and **the appeal is stayed until another carrier is sworn in that district, and nobody else may carry them**. **The fifth term is the holder** — a person in the district who keeps the page and swears an entry in it and does not hold it for a district — and **a holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one. Wenna Tace, twenty-nine, a refuse hauler, the only person on her street who can read, holds it, in a room over a pump, and cannot appeal from it. She has two entries. She asked for the whole of that to be entered, including that she did not volunteer.**

**A rate of half a farthing a bucket at the low-water standpipe** is chalked on a barrel lid nailed to the gate-side board, the first tax in nineteen years, set by a bench of four because the cartage that feeds the district's road fund has been nil since the fourteenth. **The fund stands at eighty-nine pounds; the whole of the charter costs forty-four pounds and sixteen a year, which is eleven pounds four in the quarter, of which the paper is two pounds seven shillings and threepence and is paid once, the keeping of the page is about a pound eleven a quarter, and the serving of the notices is the largest of the three because no instrument in this city says how to serve a district; the rate is set at exactly eleven pounds four in the quarter and not a unit more.** Cael Rundle said *this is the right rate* before he said anything else, and then said his daughter is fourteen and walks to that pump every morning.

**The four props of the fourth bridge went back on the same cart on the eighteenth for four pounds** and no new ones will be bought this year. They came off that deck on the twentieth of the first month, nine days before the hearing, propped the ninth bridge's west seat for the fortnight that seat was given, and stood in a yard for the other fortnight, so the deck stood open for **twenty-eight days**. The account is carried with a deficiency of eleven pounds four in the quarter entered against the district and not against the keeper of any span. **Wist Anhalt, forty-nine, twenty-nine years in the post, was asked what he wants for the first time in a month and answered**: not a page; the account carried; the four words taken off the frame and into a book on his desk, with the frame to read *fourth bridge, four words, asked and answered* with a date and nothing else; and that he has been the man who was not on the bridge for twenty-eight days and would like that on a page in a book and not on a nail. **There is no figure of thirty-four days anywhere in this case and no figure of forty-four days for the holes; on the fourteenth the holes were twenty-five days old. Do not reintroduce either.**

**Bryn Sallow, forty-four, gate hand, thirty years, is now owed** six and a half hours a day at the district's own rate — a third of a haul rate — by name, in chalk, at the moment of the work, from the eighteenth. **The thirty years before that is entered as owed by the district and not paid, and the district never kept hours and cannot pay it because it has no rate for it and no hours for it.** She refused to have the thirty years counted; the number is over a hundred thousand and the court entered that a debt which has never been counted is not a small debt. **She knows that six days of a gate hand's tide exist only because a man from a bridge stands on that bank and counts them, and that there is no line in this city that pays a man for having a leg, and she does not know that the leg is hers.**

**The appeal machinery is in use and is not clean.** The first appeal under the charter was sworn by the gate hand against the office of gate hand, heard on the twenty-first at the sixth bell on the bank, **refused on the correct rule that a person may not refuse an office and keep it**, and partly won, because four of the charter's five terms are a bridge's. **There is still no clause for serving a district**; nine hours in a clerk's office found that you serve the person who keeps the district's book, which in Halloway is the clerk who printed Instrument H-9, and she served it and minuted it and entered that she asked to be relieved of the print authority and was refused again and is not relieved and will not ask a sixth time.

**Bream, fifty-four, landlord of Weir Lane, swore a serving list of forty names on the eighteenth and is now a body.** He is the only person in this case who has come from *no* to *yes* without being persuaded, and the lever was a distinction he made himself: **a roll says who owes him a shilling a week, and a roll is a page with a seal on it, and a page with a seal on it is a thing a bench can come back with; a serving list says where a notice can be left, and nobody had ever asked him for that, and he is the only person in the city who knows where thirty-one of the doors on one lane in that district are.** He read the forty out himself into a page the clerk held up and did not copy into the bench's book, got one of them wrong, apologised to a man of nineteen who has paid a shilling a week since he was fourteen, and asked for the correction to be entered. It is the first thing he has ever sworn. **The cost: forty people in a lane found out that their landlord's mouth had turned them into a page; he is a body now; if Weir Lane is ever contested he is the first name anybody will ask for and he will have to answer for a list he wrote in his own hand. Nobody offered him a shilling of the money that never comes back, he did not ask for one, and he asked for that to be entered too. He is not thanked. He has never been thanked.** His earlier refusals stand and are not reversed by this: he still will not swear the rent roll, he still asked that the lane not be in the charter, and he still says he would rather be a man nobody can produce.

**The ninth-bridge appeal has been heard and disposed of in three parts.** The limit: *on this span* is the court's own correct drafting and is not widened. The receipt: a bench that receives the instrument may not enforce any part of it against any person unless it has adopted that part by its own act and its own minute. **The duty to answer: a bench that has received the instrument shall answer, in writing, in public, within eleven days, whether it holds any page, book, column, register, schedule, or sworn instrument which a person affected by a received record may swear to, in that bench's own room, at its own counter, without leave — and the court has no power to compel the answer and says so on the face of it.** Nine of eleven answered: seven *no*, two *no* with a hundred-and-sixty-year-old book, **one *yes* — a standing authority of preservation that holds every instrument printed in this city for four years in a series running by plate, at a counter, on request, and no person has ever asked** — and one bench that did not answer at all.

**The bench of four at Halloway is holding its third sitting on the twenty-eighth of the month and nobody knows what is on its agenda and the convenor has said in writing that she does not either.** Her sanitation complaint, entered eleven weeks before Chapter 51, is not heard this year and she entered that against herself in open court.

**The word is on a form.** A printed form summarising the charter arrived in Halloway on the twenty-second, un-ordered, sealed and numbered, with **EXCEPTION at the top of it in a bigger letter than any other word on the page.** The court has no power over a form. A form is a habit with a seal on it. The word is in every clerk's drawer in nine districts within eleven days and it is in no instrument anywhere in this case.

**The continuity line appeared for the sixth time on the twenty-second**, under the fifth term of the copy kept in the holder's page, and the word changed again: *CONTINUITY PRESERVES THE RECORD AGAINST THE HOLDER.* **Sel Harrow refused for the fourth time, in the same words — *I will not rule on a line I did not enter* — entered it as an unentered line, named nobody, and said he will refuse on the seventh time.** The holder does not know.

**The Bridgeward was pushed once, by Bram Orren, seventeen, on his own initiative**, who sent the charter's terms to Wen Lask, seventy-one, toll collector at Carrisand, told a magistrate before anybody could stop him, and said the price out loud: in about a year that gate is a gate a man from a city has written to about a rule, and that is the only reason anybody in that family would ever have heard of us. **Her letter came back on the twenty-ninth with one question and no answer: *DOES YOUR PAPER SAY ANYTHING ABOUT A ROAD WITH NOBODY IN IT.*** Nobody can answer it. The road is good, the toll has never been short, the loads go through, the last name in the witness line is her father's, and she has written over it twice.

**Ilyan's record has five things in it that he cannot un-enter**, named with their sizes in his own hand: two copies of a private sequence from *The Sovereign's Wake*; the precedent he asked for in a corridor; the first appeal, which worked perfectly and reached nobody who needed it; and the carrier's clause, the fifth, which cost a district a day and a half of a cart. **Maren Kett told him on a step that he has made himself findable, that it took a month, and that it required no good argument, and that this city is going to spend ten years finding out which of those two things it wants.**

**The public maintenance review is the fourth morning bell of the twenty-seventh of the third month after the ruling, counting the month of the ruling as the first of the three.** The second month of the case has thirty days, and the three distances that are true at once are 49 days from the eighth of the second month, six weeks from the fourteenth, and **four weeks — twenty-eight days — from the twenty-ninth of the second month, which is where Chapter 70 ended.** Callable early by any party on a named reading outside the band the keeper has kept, and not postponable. **It falls inside this batch and this batch must reach it. ## Canon from Batch 0002 that must be preserved

**The word *ward*.** It is a guild's word for a duty a district owes and has not got round to, not a place anybody lives in. The nine bridge's term book says so under a bridge heading, *duties carried, not discharged*, with a star against it. It is corroborated in the standing of the wards, a hundred and ninety years old, in which **eleven of fifty-five duties have ever been performed**; in the traveller book's own front leaf at Halloway, one ward, five duties, none performed, the last performer dead thirty-one years; and in **Hanne Kolm's sworn volume, the one document in this case that does not use the word at all**, because the duties of a lower road are the ones a landlord actually does. **The entered finding: a duty with nobody in it is not a duty nobody does. A rate may be paid to a ward only if the ward is a body; the ward is not a body because it is not a person; a duty cannot be paid anything, it can only be given work; and the work goes to whoever is standing nearest.** The court has no power to make a seat, a name, or a body out of a piece of work, and Harrow said so twice on the face of the record.

**The two words this city has for the same poor** — *the poor people* and *the carters, hauliers and gate hands* — are both wrong in front of witnesses, and Abel Kearn, thirty-five, a builder, stood up and said he lives in the ward and is in neither of them. A count taken on the low water returned **five places**: the bank above the standpipe, the flat, Weir Lane behind the pump with the room over the pump inside it, the upper street, and the two unnamed yards a hauler called *the bit by the wall* and *by the pump*. **A place is the category the court came to get rid of. Nobody has yet asked whether a duty can be refused by the person doing it, and that is the question the word *ward* now makes obvious.**

**The count return is canon and its figures are dated:** forty-one at the standpipe at the sixth bell on the twenty-eighth, nine at the ninth, nineteen by the seventh, **nine names entered in a hundred years in a traveller book, three of them crossed**, two hundred and forty behind a rope, and **four hundred and eleven loads on the flat, counted from the flat by I. Vester alone on the twentieth and stated by him to be wrong by about forty.** Tarin's finding stands: there is no line anywhere in this city on which the number of people who are not at the pump goes.

## The ten cards

Use these as chapter plans, not as prose. Each card must become a complete scene with its own physical ground, its own resistance, and its own consequence.

**Chapter 0071 — the first ask.** A district asks for the terms, and the ask is a piece of paper, and the paper is not the charter and cannot carry it. **A district with a bench of four and a road fund of nothing, that never asked for the charter and cannot pay for the printing, asks for the terms because a man with a sheet of paper came to a pump and read one out.** That is the mechanic the carrier's clause exists for and it has never been used. Ilyan must walk it himself, four miles or eleven, and the walking is his work and the leg is his while he does it and is not the moment it stops. **The resistance is not a gate. It is that a person who cannot read cannot tell whether the paper he has been given is the paper that was asked for**, and the whole of Chapter 70's form-with-a-word-on-it has made that lethal. The reader of that district has to be a person and is not Ilyan and is not anybody in the room.

**Chapter 0072 — a word on a form, and what it does to a real person.** Somebody in a district prints the charter's terms on a form headed with the word, and the form is a summary, and a summary of a rule in a language nobody in that district uses is a different rule. **A working man has to decide today whether the form in his hand is the rule he is living inside.** Give him a want of his own, not a grievance, and let the form cost him something specific and small and irreversible. **The discovery of the batch's engine: the word is not a label on a rule. The word is a claim that a rule is temporary, and a form that says so can be used against the person who relies on the charter.** Nobody in this city can stop the printing and one person can be ruined by it.

**Chapter 0073 — what the district that asked for the terms actually wanted.** Follow the ask to its floor. The district did not want a charter. It wanted a person to come and stand in its room, and it will say so. **The carrier's clause carries terms and a carrier, and a carrier is a man who stands in a room, and a district that has been given nine sheets and a stranger is going to ask what the stranger is for.** Ilyan's standing is confined to the ninth-bridge matter and a man with a carrier's clause is not a party, a bench, or a body, and a bench of four that did not adopt the ninth-bridge settlement has to decide what it is looking at. **Do not let him win this by explaining.**

**Chapter 0074 — the burden of being a body, taken in the body.** The second half of the batch's growth. Ilyan's practice holds or it does not, and this chapter should put it under a load it was not built for. **He takes a share of a burden that is his to take and pays for it in the body and it is not a cure** — and the sharing should be with a person who did not ask him to, in a place where being the man who carries is visible, and the cost should be a thing that shows on him within a day and cannot be entered anywhere. **The token is reached once, at a cost, and the vantage is touched and not resolved.** The Shale Mirror question is on the table and this is its last safe place to be reached.

**Chapter 0075 — the holder is used.** Wenna Tace's page, her two entries, her room over a pump, and a person who comes to her because she is the only door. **The holder may not be a party, may not appeal, may not appear in support of one, and may not be named in one — and the batch's engine is that a person in that position becomes the person everybody brings their problem to, and she has no power except that she can read.** Her want is the hundred and fifty hauls and a rate for them, and the pressure of being the door is what stands between her and asking for it. **She must make her own choice about the page and it must cost her something she can name, and it must not be resolved by Ilyan.**

**Chapter 0076 — the thirty years, and a rate nobody can set.** The debt of over a hundred thousand hours is owed by a district that never kept hours, at a rate two of the gate hand's neighbours set, and it cannot be paid and cannot be discharged. **Somebody must try to set a rate for thirty years and find out what thirty years is worth, and the answer must be a number that is wrong in a way that matters, and it must be entered in somebody's hand.** Bryn Sallow has refused to have it counted. **The chapter is about a refusal that a court cannot compel and cannot overrule, and about the person who has to live on both sides of it.** Do not resolve the debt. It can be valued, provisionally, wrongly, and the wrongness must be visible and must hurt.

**Chapter 0077 — the second room, and a city that has said out loud it is empty.** A district that answered the duty to answer with *no* is asked something it did not expect, and a district that answered *yes* is asked for the first time in four years, and **the seven that said no find out what it costs to have said it in public.** The Bench's scarcity — twenty-two names, eleven places — is the pressure, and this batch should use it hard, because a city that has publicly established it holds nothing is a city where a district that holds something has leverage it has not asked for. **Someone must pay for the asking and the payer is not the district asked.**

**Chapter 0078 — a decision somebody cannot take back.** A chapter with a real, irreversible, small consequence at its centre, made by an ordinary person in a room, with no speech. **A person refuses, or accepts, or withdraws, and a document changes shape because of it, and the change cannot be undone and is not a ruling.** Whoever does it must be someone who has a want of their own and who is not a party and not a lawyer. The batch's best material for this is a ratepayer, a builder, a landlord, a keeper, a coal-yard woman, or a refuse hauler, and it should not be any of the four people the batch has already used to decide something.

**Chapter 0079 — what it is going to cost, in a place that has not been on the board.** A district that has never had a matter before the Basalt now has one, and the machinery of a court lands on a place with no clerk, no keeper, no bench and no name, and the cost is not a gate and not a wage. **The court's own second note is the spine: it is a piece of paper that a person can swear to in a room with no address in it.** This chapter should be the batch's answer to its own question and it should be a worse answer than anybody wanted.

**Chapter 0080 — the batch close.** A partial, real, paid-for change; a burden named and visible; Ilyan's record having cost him something more; the afterimage unresolved; the carrier clause used at least once and its consequence visible. **The maintenance review falls on the twenty-seven of the third month, inside this batch. No chapter may pass that date without saying what happened on it, and the batch's close must be downstream of it, not upstream.** **The appeal's survival as a durable right may be advanced and must not be delivered. The Quiet Chamber recovery must not be delivered. The Bridgeward may be pushed once more and must not be resolved.** End on a question the next batch can carry, and do not manufacture suspense by cutting away mid-sentence or mid-action.
