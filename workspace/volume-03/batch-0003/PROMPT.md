## THE COAST'S FIXED CUSTOMS, WHICH ARE CANON AND WHICH NOBODY ON THAT COAST HAS WRITTEN DOWN

Write these into your working notes before you start, because four of the ten cards turn on them and every one of them is a thing a working person says in dialogue.

- The **strait is shut, and the water is on the bar, on the fourth, the sixth and the seventh** of a seven-day tide. Those are the three days the guild's seven-day licence cannot be used at all, and the seventh is the one day the harbourmaster does not know.
- The **flat takes six crossings on the first, the second, the third and the fifth, and nine on the fourth, the sixth and the seventh.** It takes nine because on those three days every cart that has to come round this coast comes over nine hundred yards of shingle instead of going through. There is more of it, not less.
- A **bar day** is a day the strait is shut. A body of nine on this coast **meets on a bar day**, because a body that meets on a day it cannot travel is a body nobody can be got hold of on. That is practice and not charter, and it is about four hundred years old, and it is the only piece of administration on that coast that has ever improved anything.
- The **tide stone**: anybody standing on it may ask the harbourmaster **one** question, on the **fourth and the sixth**, **at the turn**, in the open, and nobody may ask a second, and it is written down nowhere. The guild has never interfered with it. The stone is **for the water**. About nine people on that coast have noticed in the last year that the song and the stone-custom are **two customs and not one**.
- The **crossing's song** is sung at the stone on the **seventh** at the turn by Seth Garrick, and it is a tariff in four lines, and it is not written down. A **third** song exists at Sallow Bight, a hundred and forty miles up, sung on the second morning of the turn by whoever gets to the top first, and it says nine, and nine is three in a dry turn and eleven in a wet one, and a song cannot hold three numbers so it holds the worst one.
- The **tally post** at Wether Cross is inspected at about the turn on the **seventh**, once a year, by a man with a hammer. They cut in fives: a notch for each crossing, a hole for each ten, a bar cut across the face for each hundred. The old notches are filled flat and beaten level and the new ones are cut. It took **two hours and eleven minutes** last time and it is the only count of that post that has ever been made, and it is **four thousand and sixty-eight**, and about fifty notches on the underside are cut twice by a man in a hurry.
- The **tide office** is a mile and a flight of stairs up the hill from Wether Cross. The **harbour mouth** and the **bar staff** are two hundred yards down that road. The **lamp-oil shed** is at the head of the lane. The **glassworks** is two miles along the coast at Thornglass.

## THE MECHANISM OF PRODUCTION, AND WHY IT IS RIGHT

**Ilyan is produced a fourth time, and it is not a tray, a seal, a duty, a cart, a bench, a court, a summons, a notice served, a door, or a slip of paper with words on it. All seven of those are used and closed.**

**It is a physical requirement, and it is four hundred years old, and it is in nobody's book: a figure that bears a name has to be spoken by the person it bears, in person, at a counter, because the guild's own custom is that no person on this coast answers for another person about a crossing.** It is right. It is the same shape as consent and as a person not being used by proxy, **nobody on that coast has ever written it down, and two people on it could not have told you why it is right.**

**What produces him is that he is holding the paper.** He has picked up a thing and the thing is a production. He cannot refuse it, because refusing stops nothing, which is the finding from Chapter 113 and which is his and not this batch's invention — **a marked echo of a sentence he has already earned is permitted; the slip of paper with five words on it is not to be re-staged and he does not write one.** Nobody sends for him. Nobody has standing to send for him. **He has been on that coast thirty-nine days and not one person has asked him for one thing, and the fourth production is the shape of that sentence turned the other way round: everything he has ever been brought forward for, he brought himself.**

**Let the mechanism work perfectly.** It is right, it is fast, and it costs somebody else a day. **And let the person it would protect not want to be protected, and be kind to him about it, and the whole shape of the failure be that a correct instrument on a coast with no consent in it produces the person standing nearest instead of the person the instrument is about.**

**A false merge is not a lie and not a mystery and not a betrayal by a villain. It is a competent man doing a competent thing to a set of true statements.** Write it that way and it will be the worst thing in the volume so far.
