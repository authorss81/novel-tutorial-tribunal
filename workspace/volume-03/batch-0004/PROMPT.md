## THE COAST'S FIXED CUSTOMS, WHICH ARE CANON AND WHICH NOBODY ON THAT COAST HAS WRITTEN DOWN

Write these into your working notes before you start, because five of the ten cards turn on them and every one of them is a thing a working person says in dialogue.

- **The strait is shut, and the water is on the bar, on the fourth, the sixth and the seventh** of a seven-day tide. Those are the three days the guild's seven-day licence cannot be used at all, and **the seventh is the one day the harbourmaster does not know.**
- **The flat takes six crossings on the first, the second, the third and the fifth, and nine on the fourth, the sixth and the seventh.** Nine because on those three days every cart that has to come round this coast comes over nine hundred yards of shingle instead of going through. There is more of it, not less.
- A **bar day** is a day the strait is shut. A body of nine on this coast **meets on a bar day**, because a body that meets on a day it cannot travel is a body nobody can be got hold of on. Practice, not charter, about four hundred years old, and the only piece of administration on that coast that has ever improved anything.
- **The tide stone**: anybody standing on it may ask the harbourmaster **one** question, on the **fourth and the sixth**, **at the turn**, in the open, and nobody may ask a second, and it is written down nowhere. The guild has never interfered with it. The stone is **for the water**. About nine people on that coast have noticed in the last year that the song and the stone-custom are **two customs and not one**.
- **The crossing's song** is sung at the stone on the **seventh** at the turn by Seth Garrick, and it is a tariff in four lines, and it is not written down. A **third** song exists at Sallow Bight, a hundred and forty miles up, sung on the second morning of the turn by whoever gets to the top first, and it says nine, and nine is three in a dry turn and eleven in a wet one, and a song cannot hold three numbers so it holds the worst one.
- **The tally post** at Wether Cross is inspected at about the turn on the **seventh**, once a year, by a man with a hammer. They cut in fives: a notch for each crossing, a hole for each ten, a bar across the face for each hundred. Old notches are filled flat and beaten level and the new ones are cut. It took **two hours and eleven minutes** last time and it is the only count of that post that has ever been made, and it is **four thousand and sixty-eight**, and about fifty notches on the underside are cut twice by a man in a hurry. **The post is not recounted in this batch and no second figure is put beside the first.**
- **The tide office** is a mile and a flight of stairs up the hill from Wether Cross. The **harbour mouth** and the **bar staff** are two hundred yards down that road. The **lamp-oil shed** is at the head of the lane at the north end of the flat, about two hundred yards above the far stones. The **glassworks** is two miles along the coast at Thornglass. **The bar road** is four miles along the coast above the north end. **The far stones** are at the far end of the flat and the far side of that water is where the guild's own sea wall is.

## THE APPEAL, AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS, AND THE TWO THINGS IT IS NOT

**This is the batch. Read all of it before you write.**

- **There is one appeal on this coast and it is a person, not a bench, not a court, not a guild, and not a written thing.** It is: a person stands on four feet of flat stone at the head of the flat, **on the fourth or the sixth, at the turn, in the open**, and asks Bettany Rook **one** question, and she answers it out loud, in front of everybody on that shingle, truly, without hedging and without asking for anything. Nobody may ask a second. It is about a hundred years old. It is written down nowhere. **The guild has never interfered with it and never will.**
- **It is for the water.** That is the whole of its size, and Seth Garrick told a carter so twice in plain words in Chapter 127 — *you already know what the answer is going to be. It is going to be about the water* — and he was right, and the batch's failure is that everybody else now finds out what he already said.
- **The song is a different custom and it is on a different day and it belongs to a different man.** The song is on the **seventh**. The stone's question is on the **fourth and the sixth**. **The seventh is the one day there is no appeal at all, because the harbourmaster does not know the seventh, and nobody on that coast pretends otherwise.** That is the batch's finding in one sentence and it must arrive as a fact and not as a speech: **the road exists on two days a week and on the third one the water owns the bar and there is nothing.**
- **The song may be sung and may not be staged.** It is sung every seventh at the turn whether or not a chapter is written about it. **Write it out and it is a violation. Make it the subject of a chapter and it is a violation. Use it as evidence, as a record, as a thing anybody quotes a figure out of, or as a thing anybody may appeal with, and it is a violation. It is a tariff in four lines that a man sings to people who already know what it means.**
- **Nobody may merge the song and the stone, and nobody on that coast is going to be asked which one they think is older.** Nine people know they are two customs. That is the fact about nine people and it is not a discovery and it is not a mystery.
