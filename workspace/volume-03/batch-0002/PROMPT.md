**How it is staged, and these are the rules and they are not negotiable:**

- **It is put on the page by somebody who is not Ilyan** — Sabra Trew, or a ferryman, or Efa Lund, or Corrie Tallow, or Seth Garrick — **and that person is afraid of it before Ilyan sees it**, and cannot say what they are afraid of, and is not wrong to be.
- **Ilyan's contribution is one question and it is the same question he asked in Chapter 108: *what are the marks for.* The answer is *for counting*.** He has already been told that twice, by a man of thirty who used to be the crossing's reader. He may ask it again and he may be answered again and he may be wrong to ask and be told so.
- **No System panel may confirm that the pattern is a map. No panel may say anything about it at all.** The System translates formal records, notices and hearing-field boundaries. Nineteen notches on a kiln wall is none of those.
- **Nobody may name *The Sovereign's Wake* in dialogue as anything but a thing a man from a city once played.** No one on this coast has heard of it. It may be named in narration once if it is necessary and not more, and it must be the thing Ilyan played, not the thing he is looking for.
- **Nothing is confirmed by anything. Nobody says it is a map. The recognition, when it comes, is four batches later and it is the System, not a person.**
- **The batch must not be a mystery batch.** The pattern is a discovery about the coast's own record-keeping, made by a person with a want, and the want is ordinary: somebody wants a row of notches to come out right, or somebody wants to know why their grandmother's book and a kiln wall agree, or somebody wants a number they can swear to. The pattern is a by-product of a working person's problem and it must stay a by-product.

**Where the address takes him.** A hundred and forty miles up the coast is about four days' walk with a cart, or two by water if the tide allows it, and the school's room is a schoolroom and the book is a grandmother's book and the grandmother is dead or alive, pick one and state it. **The trip is optional. What is not optional is that somebody local goes and looks, and that the looking costs somebody a day, and that what comes back is not an answer.** Ilyan's leg, his hand, his money and his four-day leg-clearing are all live constraints on whether he can go at all. If he cannot go, somebody else must, and that is better.
